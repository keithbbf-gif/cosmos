# When to seek a speech evaluation for your toddler

Parents often wonder whether a child is “just a late talker” or needs a speech-language evaluation. There is no single age that fits every child, but **patterns over time** matter more than one hard day.

## What many families notice

- Fewer words than peers by **18–24 months**, or no new words over several months [CITE NEEDED: ASHA or CDC milestone reference with date]
- Hard to understand even by familiar listeners past age 3
- Frustration, biting, or meltdowns when trying to communicate
- Difficulty following simple routines (“get your shoes,” “give me the cup”)
- Loss of words or skills that were previously present — always worth prompt medical discussion

## What an evaluation adds

A licensed speech-language pathologist looks at speech sounds, language comprehension, play, and social communication together. The goal is a **clear picture**, not a label, so you know whether therapy, monitoring, or other supports (hearing test, early intervention) makes sense.
