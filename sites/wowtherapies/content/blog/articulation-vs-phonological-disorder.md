# Articulation vs. phonological disorder: what parents hear

When a child is hard to understand, families often say “they’re missing sounds.” Speech-language pathologists listen for **which** sounds and **whether there is a pattern**.

## Articulation (often single-sound focus)

A child may substitute, omit, or distort specific sounds — for example always saying “wabbit” for “rabbit.” Therapy may target how that sound is made in isolation and in words.

## Phonological disorder (pattern across words)

Some children simplify many words the same way — leaving off final consonants (“ca” for “cat”) or replacing whole classes of sounds. That pattern can make speech very difficult to understand even when the child seems to try hard.

## At home

- Model clear, slow speech without demanding “say it right” every time
- Repeat the correct word naturally: “Yes, **cat** — you want the cat”
- Celebrate successful messages, not perfect sounds
