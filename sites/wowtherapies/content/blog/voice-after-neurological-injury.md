# Voice changes after neurological injury: what SLPs address

Voice is more than volume. After stroke, Parkinson’s disease, or other neurological conditions, people may notice **hoarseness**, breathiness, pitch changes, or fatigue when talking.

## Common clinical labels (evaluation determines which apply)

- **Dysarthria** — muscle weakness affecting speech clarity
- **Apraxia of speech** — planning difficulty for speech movements
- **Vocal fold paralysis or paresis** — medical ENT evaluation may be needed [CITE NEEDED: ASHA voice evaluation scope vs. ENT referral criteria]

## What voice therapy may include

- Breath support and easy onset exercises
- Vocal hygiene (hydration, reducing irritants)
- Loudness training programs when appropriate for neurological voice [CITE NEEDED: LSVT LOUD or SPEAK OUT! efficacy citation if claiming outcomes]
- Strategies for social participation when voice tires quickly

## Educational disclaimer

New hoarseness lasting more than two weeks warrants medical evaluation to rule out structural causes. This article is not a substitute for ENT or SLP assessment.
