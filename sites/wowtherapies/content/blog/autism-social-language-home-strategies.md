# Social language at home: supporting autistic communicators

Autistic children and teens may communicate effectively in their own ways while still needing support for conversation, friendship, or classroom participation. Speech-language therapy for **social communication** should honor neurodiversity — building skills without shame.

## Goals that matter

- Understanding others’ perspectives in everyday situations (not trick questions)
- Joining and staying in interactions the child **wants**
- Using AAC (picture boards, apps, devices) without penalty when speech is hard [CITE NEEDED: ASHA AAC position statement]

## Home strategies

- Offer choices instead of quizzes (“pizza or tacos?” vs. “what do you want?” when overloaded)
- Narrate routines with simple language
- Play games with clear turns and visible rules
- Protect downtime — social effort is real effort
