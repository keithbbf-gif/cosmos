Clinicians rarely get a quiet week to read journals cover to cover. This roundup highlights three areas with direct planning implications: treatment intensity, literacy screening boundaries, and supervision when sessions are hybrid.

## Treatment dosage still matters more than brand

A meta-analytic line of work continues to show that **scheduled practice minutes** and **distribution across the week** predict outcomes for speech sound disorders at least as strongly as the specific app or worksheet set. When your district buys a new platform, keep the old fidelity checklist: trials per session, elicitation hierarchy, and generalization probes.

> **Caseload takeaway:** Protect frequency before novelty. If travel cuts visits, document the dosage change and adjust goals with the team.

## Dyslexia screening and SLP scope

State literacy initiatives increasingly involve quick screeners in K–2. SLPs belong in the conversation when **language comprehension**, **phonological awareness**, and **speech production** overlap referral questions — not as sole arbiters of reading disability labels.

Collaborate with psych and reading specialists; your note should describe communication profiles, not diagnose dyslexia unless licensed and scoped to do so in your jurisdiction.

## Tele-supervision for CFs

Hybrid placements are normalizing structured remote supervision supplemented by in-person observation bursts. Programs reporting success emphasize **predictable observation slots**, **shared rubrics**, and **low-latency feedback** on documentation — not ad hoc text threads after midnight.
