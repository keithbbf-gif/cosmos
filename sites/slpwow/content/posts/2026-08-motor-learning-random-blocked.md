Blocked practice (many repetitions of one target) feels efficient. Randomized practice (mixing targets) feels messy and sometimes frustrates students. Motor learning research often finds **randomized schedules support retention and transfer** once initial acquisition occurs — but young children and highly fragile learners may still need blocked introduction.

## Apply with clinical judgement

Use blocked trials when establishing a new motor pattern or cuing hierarchy step. Shift toward variable practice when accuracy stabilizes above your preset criterion and you are probing generalization.

Document the shift in your session note: “Moved from blocked /r/ sets to mixed initial-medial words per motor learning retention protocol discussed with team.”

## Avoid binary preaching

Studies differ by age, disorder severity, and feedback type. A school SLP with 55-minute groups cannot always run pure random schedules; **partial randomization within the last third of a session** is a realistic compromise.
