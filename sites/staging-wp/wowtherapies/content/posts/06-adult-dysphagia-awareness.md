Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) can follow stroke, neurologic disease, head and neck cancer treatment, or progressive illness. It is not only a “normal part of aging,” though some changes warrant medical review.

## Signs to report to your doctor or SLP

- Coughing or choking during meals
- Wet or gurgly voice after eating or drinking
- Food sticking in the throat
- Unexplained weight loss or dehydration
- Recurrent pneumonia

## Role of speech-language pathology

SLPs evaluate swallow function—often with imaging studies ordered by a physician—and recommend texture modifications, strategies, and exercises when appropriate. Treatment is coordinated with your medical team.

Seek urgent care for sudden breathing difficulty or complete inability to swallow. This post is for education, not emergency guidance.
