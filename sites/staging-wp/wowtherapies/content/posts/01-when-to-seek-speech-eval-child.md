Many parents wonder whether their child is “just a late talker” or needs professional support. There is no single milestone that fits every child, but patterns that persist or cause frustration at home or school are worth a conversation with a speech-language pathologist (SLP).

## Possible signs to watch

- Limited words or phrases compared with peers by age 2–3
- Speech that is hard to understand after age 4
- Frequent repetition or “getting stuck” on words (stuttering)
- Difficulty following directions or answering questions
- Trouble interacting with peers or staying in back-and-forth play

## What an evaluation includes

An SLP gathers history from caregivers, observes communication during play or conversation, and uses standardized tools when appropriate. You should leave with a clear summary of strengths, concerns, and recommended next steps—whether that is therapy, monitoring, or referral to another specialist.
