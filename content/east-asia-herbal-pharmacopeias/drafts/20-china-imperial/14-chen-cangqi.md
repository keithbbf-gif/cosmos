The official book of 659 was not the only Tang *bencao*. It was the one a court could point at. Beside it, and soon after it, a man named Chen Cangqi 陳藏器 wrote a supplement that later people could not stop quoting. The title is *Bencao shiyi* 本草拾遺 — picking up what was left. The date you will see most often is Kaiyuan 27, 739. I will use it as the conventional date, not as a colophon I have read.

Chen, in the biographical crumbs, is a man from Siming (later writers say today’s Ningbo) who served as a county captain at Sanyuan in the capital region. That is not a palace medical post. It is the kind of job from which you might see what the official book did not bother to name: local things, ugly things, things a village already used, things a tribute list had no room for. The *Shiyi* is usually described as a preface-and-rules *juan*, six *juan* of supplements, three of disputes or explanations. The *New Tang History* bibliographic note is the ordinary source for the ten-*juan* shape. The book as a book is lost.

Why it matters anyway:

**It names growth as a method.** *Shiyi* is not “a new system.” It is an admission that the state list is incomplete. After Chen, incompleteness is a genre. Zhao Xuemin will still be writing *shiyi* a thousand years later (draft 22), and he will mean it.

**It is a quotation mine.** *Ishinpō*, the *Zhenglei* books, the *Gangmu* — they keep Chen’s sentences when they cannot keep Chen’s volume. Shang Zhijun’s modern collation (the 1983 Wannan mimeograph that later reprints depend on) is the working text I see cited. I have not checked Shang against the tanks item by item. A draft that says “Chen lists 692 drugs” is repeating a reconstruction’s count. Treat it that way.

**It experiments with function.** Handbook accounts say Chen grouped substances by what they were *for* — dispersing, attacking warmth, and so on — in a way later writers treat as an ancestor of functional classification. That is a big genealogical claim. I will say only: later people *used* him that way. Whether he “founded” a classification I leave open.

**It is the unofficial twin of the official book.** The Tang century now has a pair: a court text that wants one voice, a private text that wants the leftovers. Song official editors will eat both. That is the pattern. The state does not only sponsor. It later metabolizes what it did not sponsor.

I like Chen more than I trust my liking. He is easy to cast as the folk hero against the court — the county man who heard the village. The quotations we have were kept by later encyclopedists, not by villagers. Whatever local world he saw, we meet it already lifted into the same literary ocean as Su Jing. That is still a different shoreline. It is not a tape recording of the shoreline.

When Japanese and Korean books quote “Bencao” and the sentence cannot be found in the reconstructed *Xinxiu* or the *Jing*, Chen is one of the first places a responsible editor looks. He is also one of the first places a sloppy editor dumps a mystery sentence. A second pass should watch for that dump.

## Open questions

- How stable is the 739 date across early bibliographies?
- What portion of “Chen” quotations in the *Gangmu* are really from *Zhenglei* quoting Chen, and how much wording shifted in the hop?

## Sources used

Yaozi and standard handbook entries on Chen and the *Shiyi* (Kaiyuan 27, ten-*juan* shape, lost status). Shang Zhijun collation as the modern working text named in those entries. Mayanagi on *Ishinpō* as a quotation witness. He Bian 2017 as the later analogue for *shiyi* as a social form (draft 22).

## Claims I am not making

- That I have read a Tang manuscript of the *Shiyi*.
- That Chen is “more empirical” than the court book. That is a modern prize.
