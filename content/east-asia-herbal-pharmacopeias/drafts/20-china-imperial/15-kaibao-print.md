# Kaibao: print arrives

The Song does not find the Tang book and reprint it. The Song does something more arrogant and more useful: it orders a new official *bencao* and sends it to woodblocks. The reign-title in the name is Kaibao. The years in the usual story are 973 for the first cut and 974 for a revision. The men named around the work include the imperial physician Liu Han, the Daoist Ma Zhi, and a cluster of medical officials; a second-year pass adds more drugs and more reviewing names (Lu Duosun, Li Fang, and others in the handbook list). I am repeating a preface-tradition, not a payroll I have audited.

Two claims about this book are worth keeping, and one is worth strangling.

Keep: **it is the first printed official *bencao* in the Chinese series as we tell that series.** Print is not a neutral upgrade. It changes what “promulgation” can mean. A Tang standard is a manuscript family. A Song standard is a set of blocks — still not infinite, still not cheap, but repeatable in a new way. The Kaibao preface, as later quoted, talks about the old red and black having drifted. Print will drift too (a block wears, a recut adds a mistake), but it drifts differently. You can now be wrong *the same way* in two cities.

Keep: **it metabolizes the Tang pair.** The editors are described as collating the *Xinxiu* and folding in Chen Cangqi, cleaning aliases, marking the old classic in one graphic style and the new attachments in another. The official book has learned to eat the supplement. That meal becomes the Song habit.

Strangle: **that we have a Song Kaibao we can open.** I have not found, in the catalogs I used, an identified original Song printing that you can put on a stand and photograph as *the* Kaibao. What we have is the book as it was eaten by later official and private compilations, especially the *Zhenglei* line, and as it is described by prefaces. “The Kaibao says” should usually be “the Kaibao, as later recensions and prefaces represent it, says.”

The count that travels is 984 substances after the revision. Again: a preface number. Useful as a scale. Not a flora.

Print also changes pictures. Tang illustrations, if we believe the descriptions, were color and unique. Song illustrations can be line, copyable, worse and more common at the same time. The great picture-event is not Kaibao but Su Song’s *Tujing* a century later (draft 16). Kaibao is the event of **the official page becoming a printed page**. Once that happens, a private Sichuan physician can build a bigger book on official bones and expect printers to care (draft 17). Li Shizhen can, much later, expect that a book the size of a cabinet might still be carved. None of that is imaginable in the same way in 659.

I should say a word about the Song state’s broader medical publishing — the *Taiping shenghui fang* and other enormous formularies — without letting this file become a history of Song medicine. The court of the 980s is collecting recipes on a scale that makes a *bencao* look small. The *bencao* is the identity book. The formulary is the action book. They feed each other. A printed identity book is how a printed action book knows what string it is naming.

Japan will meet Song print as objects on ships. Korea will meet it as a problem of import and as a model of what a state can sponsor. Neither meeting is “reception of Kaibao” in a clean line. By the time the blocks matter abroad, the *Zhenglei* and then the *Gangmu* have already become the louder titles.

## Open questions

- Is there any fragment or later facsimile that a specialist would still call “Kaibao text” rather than “Zhenglei-preserving-Kaibao”? I want that distinction made by someone who has done the collation.
- How many official Song *bencao* recuts sit between 974 and the Jiayou project, and how much did they actually change?

## Sources used

Kaibao preface narrative as given in modern introductions (Yaozi; herbal-history surveys). Unschuld’s official-series account. Asaf Goldschmidt’s Song medical-publishing context (*The Evolution of Chinese Medicine* and related work) for the formulary boom. The 984-count as a transmitted figure.

## Claims I am not making

- That I have handled a 973/974 printing.
- That print immediately produced a nationally uniform drug supply.
