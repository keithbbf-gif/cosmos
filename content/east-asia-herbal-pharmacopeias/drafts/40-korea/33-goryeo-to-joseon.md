# From Goryeo lists to early Joseon compilations

A single emergency book does not make a policy. What happens after *Hyangyak gugeupbang*, in the late Goryeo and the first Joseon generation, is the claim that local drugs can carry a kingdom. I am reconstructing this from later historians (Shin, Suh, Ahn as cited in handbook chapters), not from a run of Goryeo manuscripts I have read. Several of the Goryeo titles survive only as names and as quotations inside early Joseon books. That is a *shiyi* situation. We meet the leftovers in the book that ate them.

Titles you will see in the Korean encyclopedia’s wake: *Samhwaja hyangyakbang*, *Hyangyak gobang*, *Hyangyak hyeminbang*, and others. I will not fake tables of contents for books I cannot open. Their shared first word is the evidence: *hyangyak* has become a way to name a medical book.

The hinge title for the dynastic turn is *Hyangyak jesaeng jipseongbang* 鄕藥濟生集成方, 1399, associated with the Jesaengwon in the first years of Joseon. Later accounts tie Bang Saryang to a policy of restraining Chinese luxury imports and stabilizing a new state’s medical and fiscal life by leaning on local drugs. I like the tightness of that story and I flag the tightness. Founding dynasties attract founding policies. Maybe Bang said exactly that. Maybe later Joseon writers needed him to have said it. Either way, **1399 is an official local-herb compilation at the birth of a dynasty**, and that is already a stronger state-fact than a Goryeo emergency pamphlet.

What I think is happening, claims-guarded:

Goryeo’s late decades live in a Yuan world. Yuan goods and Yuan fashions are expensive. Local-herb writing becomes a way to be medical without being a luxury importer. Joseon inherits the habit and professionalizes it: more volumes, more offices, eventually Sejong’s enormous 1433 book (draft 34) and, in parallel, a giant that does the opposite job — vacuuming the Chinese formulary world (draft 35). The new dynasty does not choose local *or* oceanic. It staffs both.

This is the place to say that “Korean medicine” as a later brand is being assembled out of these office decisions, not discovered under a rock. The brand will later want Heo Jun as the face (draft 36). Heo Jun has a Goryeo and early-Joseon basement. The basement is *hyangyak* policy plus Chinese textual appetite.

Japan, in the same late-fourteenth / fifteenth centuries, is not running this pair of official projects. I will not call that backwardness. I will call it a different state and a different print-and-office ecology. Comparisons that start with “Korea was first to…” should have to name the class: first *surviving* emergency local-herb book, first *dynasty-founding* official local compilation, and so on. Draft 02 is watching.

A thin-file warning, as in the Japanese medieval draft: several Goryeo titles here are ghosts. A second pass that only inflates the ghosts into a “Goryeo pharmacopeia tradition” will have built a tradition out of a bibliography. I would rather keep the ghosts labeled.

## Open questions

- What fragments of the named Goryeo *hyangyak* books actually remain, and where?
- How much of Bang Saryang’s policy is contemporary documentation versus later Joseon historiography?

## Sources used

Handbook chapter “History and characteristics of Korean Medicine” (1236 *gugeupbang*, 1399 *jesaeng jipseongbang*, Bang Saryang, Yuan-luxury context). Encyclopedia of Korean Culture on later use of Goryeo prescriptions inside the 1433 book. Suh on the local as layered.

## Claims I am not making

- That I have reconstructed Goryeo pharmacy as a system.
- That Joseon “completed” a Goryeo project. Joseon used Goryeo leftovers for Joseon reasons.
