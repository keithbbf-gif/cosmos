# After Heo Jun: vernacular, war, reprint

A famous encyclopedia can freeze a timeline. I want the century after 1613 to stay a century. I do not have, in this pass, a second Korean *bencao* of *Gangmu* scale to put on the table. What I have is a set of pressures that later books and later politics will have to live with.

**Reprint.** Official and commercial reprints keep *Donguibogam* in the room. The 1633 recut of the 1433 *hyangyak* book (draft 34) sits in the same postwar decades. War eats books and then makes them valuable. I do not have a census of seventeenth-century medical print runs. I have the obvious: a kingdom that has been invaded reprints its medical standards when it can.

**Vernacular.** Heo Jun already cared about names people could read. Later Joseon writing, and later hangul medical books, push further. I am not opening a history of hangul medicine here. I am saying that **the graph-and-mouth problem does not end when the royal encyclopedia is printed**. A farmer who cannot live in literary Chinese is still the person who has to find the plant. Suh’s *Naming the Local* is the long book on that problem into modernity. This draft only keeps the door open.

**Other faces.** Yi Jema’s *Dongui suse bowon* (1894 in the usual dating) and the Sasang typology will get pulled into “Korean medicine” surveys as a third founder beside Heo and Saam. Sasang is a late-Joseon intellectual system about persons, not a pharmacopeia. I name it so that no one thinks I forgot it, and I refuse to make it a drug-list. Saam’s acupuncture lineage is likewise out of scope. A pharmacopeia history that swallows every famous doctor has lost its object.

**Qing goods and new leftovers.** The same centuries Zhao Xuemin is writing *shiyi* in China, Korean markets are dealing with Qing and maritime things too. I have not done the Korean *shiyi* work. A second pass should ask whether late-Joseon pharmacists wrote supplements to Heo the way Zhao wrote supplements to Li, or whether reprint-plus-vernacular was the Korean form of leftover-management.

**The clinic and the shop.** Official encyclopedias are not the only drug lists. Peddlers, women, Buddhist and village specialists — draft 42 will say this for the whole region. Late Joseon is not an exception. If the paper trail is official, the practice was not only official.

This file is intentionally not a tour of titles I have not read. It is a brace against the UNESCO effect: the 1613 book as the end of Korean pharmaceutical history, after which one only awaits the modern KP. Between Heo Jun and 1958 there is colonial law, wartime herbal policy, Republican and empire codes next door, and a lot of unnamed shop practice. Draft 38 is the cut. This draft is the reminder that the cut does not land on a still surface.

## Open questions

- What is the first substantial hangul-script herbal or *bencao*-like book, and does it live inside Heo Jun’s rooms or leave them?
- Is there a late-Joseon *shiyi* I have missed because I followed the famous titles?

## Claims I am not making

- That I have surveyed late-Joseon medical publishing.
- That Sasang medicine is a pharmacopeia.
- That 1613 closed the Korean drug-book tradition.
