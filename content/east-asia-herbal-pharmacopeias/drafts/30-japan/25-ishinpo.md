In 984 Tanba Yasuyori 丹波康頼 (you will also see Tamba) presented a thirty-*kan* medical encyclopedia. The title is *Ishinpō* 醫心方 — prescriptions from the heart of medicine, in the museum English. It is the oldest surviving Japanese medical book in the usual account. A Heian-period copy associated with the Nakarai (Nakarai/Nasei) family sits in the Tokyo National Museum and is a National Treasure. I have seen the museum description. I have not turned the scrolls.

If this were a history of Japanese medicine, the file would be about etiology, acupuncture, the sexual *juan* that English Wikipedia likes to visit, the Buddhist pregnancy sutra, the dependence on Chao Yuanfang’s *Zhubing yuanhou lun*. This is a pharmacopeia collection, so I am here for a narrower reason: **Ishinpō is one of the best fridges of lost Chinese drug writing, and it is a Japanese editorial act, not a fridge with legs.**

Mayanagi Makoto’s work on *juan* 30 is the door I used. His point, as I understand it: the book’s value is systematic compilation with sources named, in wording that has not yet been through later Chinese recuts. The *Shennong* layer, Tao, the *Xinxiu*, *Shiliao*, and a cloud of Six Dynasties and Tang titles that China later lost — they sit here in a Heian hand. *Juan* 30 is the *honzō* volume in that reading. *Juan* 1 carries Japanese names that quote *Honzō wamyō*.

So the book *looks* like a medical encyclopedia, and it *is* one, and it is also an archive with a compiler’s initials on the box.

Two cautions.

**Chinese sources, Japanese use.** A common sentence says *Ishinpō* preserves more than two hundred Chinese documents and no Japanese ones. That sentence, even if the count is roughly right, can be used to make Tanba a copyist. He is a copyist the way Tang Shenwei is a copyist: the selection, the arrangement, the decision to put a *honzō* at the end, the *kana* and reading marks later hands will add — those are Japanese facts. The Nakarai copy’s decorated paper and later supplements are Japanese facts. An archive is a use.

**The sexual and Daoist material is not a sideshow I will moralize.** It is evidence that “medical encyclopedia” in 984 does not mean what a ministry of health means. I will not excerpt it. I will not use it to spice this file. I mention it once so that someone who knows the book does not think I pretended *juan* 28 was not there.

For Korea, *Ishinpō* is not a source I have seen treated as central. For China, it is a witness Chinese editors now need. For this collection, it is the moment Japanese medical writing becomes indispensable to anyone who wants to talk about Tang *bencao* without lying about survival (draft 13).

Tanba’s dates are commonly given as 912–995. The 984 presentation is the date I will keep in a timeline. The Nakarai copy is mostly Heian transcription with later patches — museum notes say twenty-seven Heian scrolls, one Kamakura, Edo supplements. “The book” is a family of times. You already knew I would say that.

If you take one habit from this file: when a modern reconstruction of a lost Chinese pharmaceutical text cites *Ishinpō*, ask whether the citation is to Tanba’s labeled quotation or to a later editor’s guess that a bare sentence “feels like” the *Xinxiu*. Mayanagi is careful. Not everyone who cites Mayanagi is.

## Open questions

- Is *juan* 30’s “Bencao” consistently the *Xinxiu* large-character layer, or does the identification vary by passage? Mayanagi argues toward the former; I want the exceptions listed.
- How should the Edo supplements be marked so that no one treats them as 984?

## Sources used

Tokyo National Museum / e-Museum notes on the Nakarai *Ishinpō* (984 compilation, Heian copy, National Treasure). Mayanagi, study of *juan* 30’s *bencao* value. Encyclopedia statements on Chinese-source counts — treated as order-of-magnitude. *Honzō wamyō* in *juan* 1 as per Japanese reference works.

## Claims I am not making

- That I have read all thirty *kan*.
- That *Ishinpō* is a Japanese *bencao* in the sense of a new classification of substances. It is an encyclopedia that archives one.
- That the sexual *juan* is “the point” of the book. It is a *juan*.
