# Why a court needed a book

A private editor can live with a corrupt classic. A court that runs an Imperial Medical Office, a Palace Drug Service, and a tribute pipeline cannot — or tells itself it cannot. The road to the *Xinxiu bencao* of 659 starts before Su Jing’s memorial. It starts in the northern and southern dynasties’ habit of treating medicine as something a state already staffs.

I am working here from secondary reconstructions of medical statutes and from the way later codes remember earlier ones. The *Tiansheng ling* medical articles, a Song-period window onto older law, have been used by historians to argue that Tang administration already imagined a loop: the palace needs a substance, someone looks up where the *bencao* says it grows, a budget office issues the demand, the prefecture sends it. If that loop is even roughly right, the book is not a library ornament. It is a procurement document with literary ancestors.

That does not mean every county magistrate slept with Tao Hongjing under his pillow. It means the court had a reason to want *one* arranged text: so that the word “the drug” in an order pointed at a comparable thing in the provinces. Identity is a fiscal problem. Substitute is a fiscal problem. A look-alike that is the wrong plant is a fiscal problem and a palace problem. You do not need modern chemistry to feel those.

There are other reasons, less pretty.

**Examination.** If medical students and palace doctors are to be tested, they need a set text. A set text is a political choice about which layer of the tradition counts.

**Display.** A new dynasty likes a new book. The early Tang is a dynasty that wants the northern legalist inheritance *and* a southern literary one. A jointly staffed *bencao* — specialists from the medical office, men from the secretariat, a famous name or two at the top of the list — looks like the dynasty in miniature. Historians who have read the contributor list (Su Jing, who is not only a physician; Zhangsun Wuji’s shadow in some reconstructions; Li Chunfeng; twenty-two names in the Japanese-transmitted account) have argued that the mix is the message. I find that persuasive as a reading of a list. I cannot see the meetings.

**Gardens and harvest.** Tang institutional history talks about medicinal gardens and about harvesters. A book that describes appearance is a book you can walk into a garden with. Whether the 659 illustrations were actually used that way, we do not know. The idea that they *could* be is already a reason to pay for painters.

**Loss.** Tao’s book, however good, is a manuscript family. Manuscript families drift. The Kaibao preface, centuries later, will say the red and the black no longer match. The Tang project is an earlier attempt to stop the drift by restaffing it. Stopping drift by making one more manuscript family is a joke the print era will get. In 657 it is still the available tool.

I do not want to make the court look like a modern regulator. There is no evidence in what I have read for a Tang inspector with an assay. There is evidence for a court that believed drug knowledge was the sort of thing an empire should speak in one voice about. The difference matters. One voice is a charter. An assay is a method. The *Xinxiu* is a charter with descriptions attached.

Japan will receive that charter as a textbook. Korea will receive pieces of the same ocean through other books and then write *hyangyak* against the cost of the ocean. The court’s need, in other words, becomes other people’s problem. That is the only sense in which I will say the Tang book “spreads.”

## Open questions

- How much of the procurement-loop reconstruction depends on reading Song law as a faithful mirror of Tang practice? I flag this as a known methodological risk.
- What did “promulgated as the standard for drug use” mean in a world of copies? A decree, a school text, a palace copy, a provincial expectation? I do not have a map of surviving Tang-period *bencao* fragments that would let me answer.

## Claims I am not making

- That the Tang state achieved uniform drug identity across the empire.
- That a legal reconstruction from a later code is the Tang code itself.
