# What official books leave out

An official drug book is a portrait of a state’s willingness to speak. It is not a portrait of a country’s substances. I have been implying this since the *Bielu* (the annex as admission) and Chen Cangqi (the leftover as genre). Here I want the omissions named as omissions, without filling them with invented archives.

**Women’s labor.** Midwives, abortion, lactation, the messy pharmacopeia of the birth room — these appear in official encyclopedias in pieces (*Ishinpō* has pregnancy material; the Korean emergency book has women’s sections; *Donguibogam* has rooms). They also live in oral and household practice that a court compilation is not designed to credit. A history of pharmacopeias that only follows named male editors will call the official piece the whole.

**Unlicensed specialists.** Peddlers, monks, village experts, the *muyou* and the shop clerk Zhao Xuemin could hear. Official books sometimes eat their sentences and strip their names. Sometimes they never hear them.

**The wrong animals.** Later law and later extinction sit on top of older lists: rhinoceros, tiger, pangolin, and other bodies that a *bencao* can name in a calm voice. I will not inventory them. I will say that **repeating a historical list without a legal and ethical note is a kind of omission of the present**. This collection will not serve as a shopping list.

**The wrong people.** Enslaved and bonded gatherers, corvée harvesters, the prefectural painter who was not a pharmacist, the Korean commoner who was supposed to use *hyangyak* because the import was a luxury — the books are not written to keep their hours.

**Failure.** The *Pinhui* that did not print. The *Xinxiu* that became a ghost at home. The Goryeo titles that are now names. The colonial drafts whose legal force I cannot even certify. Official memory prefers finished, inscribed, UNESCO-ready objects. Staging this folder is a small refusal of that preference.

**Doubt’s losers.** Li’s *bianyi* slot keeps some arguments. Many more arguments died as unmarked deletions in a recut. Jiangxi can move a phrase and make a new truth. A clean official page may be a page that lost.

**Food that stayed food.** Draft 11. The kitchen leaked in; it also stayed out. A lot of ordinary plant knowledge never became a monograph.

**Japan’s medieval stretch; Korea’s ghosts; China’s lost pictures.** I have already marked these as holes. An omission file that did not mention the historian’s own omissions would be a joke.

Why this is not a confession booth: because **the official book’s job is to omit**. One voice is made by leaving other voices off the page. The Tang team of twenty-two is already a selection. Sejong’s two big medical projects are a selection (local *and* oceanic, but not everything). The 1886 JP is a ferocious selection. Once you see selection as the job, you stop asking official books to be complete, and you start asking *whom the selection was for*.

A second pass could be crueler and more useful: pick one official title and list, from contemporary sources, the drug-worlds it did not include. That would be a real omission study. This file is only the charter for one.
