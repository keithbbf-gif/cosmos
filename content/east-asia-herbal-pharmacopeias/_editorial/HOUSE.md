# House rules for this collection

These are working rules, not a style cult.

## Voice

Write as if a careful reader is in the room and will catch a swagger. Prefer the short sentence after a long one. Prefer a named book, a named office, a named copy, over a civilization talking to itself. First person is allowed when it marks a limit (“I have not seen the 1417 print”). It is not allowed as a brand.

Do not open with “In the rich tapestry,” “Since ancient times,” or “It is important to note.” Do not close with a TED-talk uplift. If the file ends, let it end on a problem.

## Claims

Every strong verb needs a source class:

- **Catalog / archive**: a holding, a colophon, a nomination file.
- **Scholarly consensus**: more than one specialist, named if possible.
- **Single reconstruction**: say so.
- **National or UNESCO prose**: treat as a claim about prestige, not as a measurement.

When dates conflict, put the conflict in the body, not in a footnote you hope nobody reads.

## Medical line

Do not say a substance treats, cures, prevents, or is safe. You may say a *text claims* a use, or that a later clinic *inherited* a name. You may say aconite is poisonous. You may not write a dose.

## Names

First mention: original script if useful, a stable romanization, and a short English gloss. After that, pick one form and keep it. Pinyin for Chinese book titles unless a well-known English title is already in the scholarly bloodstream (*Compendium of Materia Medica* is allowed, *Bencao gangmu* is better). Modified Hepburn for Japanese. Revised romanization for Korean, with McCune–Reischauer in parentheses when a catalog still uses it.

## Length

A draft is done when it has made one argument and shown its joints. Padding to look complete is a claims violation.

## Staging

`status: draft` stays until someone has checked the file against the primary book or a diplomatic edition. Promotion is a later human act. Do not auto-promote.
