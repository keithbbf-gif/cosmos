Once the puddle test has voted no, people still want a trick that lets
them plant in the same dish. The trick is height. Not a secret
amendment. Not a variety that “likes wet feet.” I have not met that
variety. I have met people who wanted one.

Plant the crown above the grade that floods. Let the native clay be the
wall that sheds, not the cup that holds. If you amend, amend at the
surface and out, not a secret pot underground. A raised bed of imported
mix *on top of* clay is a different sentence — the water can run off
the bed. A buried cylinder of mix cannot.

UC IPM: plant in raised beds, or on a ridge or mound several inches
high and several feet across where drainage is poor. Plants settle as
organic matter decomposes. That last line is why I build a little
higher than my pride wants. A six-inch berm in March is a three-inch
berm in October. The fig sinks. The bowl comes back.

How high is enough? High enough that the morning after a storm the
crown is not in a lake. On our clay that is often a wide, lazy mound —
several feet across, not a volcano you piled against the trunk. A
narrow volcano sheds water *away* from the roots you wanted to drink
and still wets the bark. Wide and low is the shape. Think fried egg,
not Hershey kiss.

I backfill with the dirt that came out of the hole, plus a little
surface compost once the tree is in. I do not refill with a Promix
shaft. UC IPM says refill with the original soil and avoid adding
organic amendments and fertilizers in the hole. That matches the scar
I am trying to spare you. The pretty hole is a later draft. This one
is the grade.

Zone 7 needs the height for winter as much as for rain. A crown in a
dish plus a freeze is a collar of ice. Zone 9 needs the height for the
week the Gulf sits on the yard. 8a needs it for the thunderstorm that
arrives the night after you planted because that is when it always
arrives. Stake a young tree if the mound is still loose. UC mentions
wind and wet soil for a reason. A lean in May is a lean in July.

If you cannot raise the hole, you do not have a fig hole. You have a
pot site. That is an honest outcome. Hundreds of our names live in
cans because the ground was a bowl or a nematode hotel or both. Height
is the in-ground ticket. Buy it with dirt you pile up, not with a
prayer you pile in.

How I build the fried egg: scrape the sod, pile the native
dirt wide, rough any glazed shovel marks, set the ball so
the nursery line sits a little under the new surface and
the whole yolk sits above last year’s flood mark. Water
to settle. Add a little more dirt where it sinks. Mulch
the white of the egg, not the flare. If the first storm
puts the yolk underwater, I did not build it wide enough
or I picked a swale. I do not argue with that storm. I
add dirt or I move the tree. Height is the ticket. The
clay will be the wall if you let it shed.
