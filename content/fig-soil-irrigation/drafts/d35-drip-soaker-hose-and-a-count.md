The tool follows the count. One tree: a hose you set
and forget until you are bored. Ten trees: a soaker
ring or a drip loop you can see leak. A hundred trees:
you are in our problem, and our problem used
sprinklers and a mixer.

Drip is the cleanest near fruit. Emitters at the
dripline, not at the trunk. Pressure that matches the
bag. A filter if your well laughs at emitters. I have
watched a drip line clog and a row go quiet while the
owner trusted the timer. Walk it. Look for the wet
spots. A drip system you never walk is a Tuesday with
extra plastic.

Soaker hose is honest and ugly. It weeps. It wants to
sit under chips so the sun does not cook it. It wants
a level run so the low end does not drown and the high
end does not lie. I like soaker on a berm because the
water stays in the fried-egg instead of jetting off.

A hose with a thumb over the end is how you compact
the ring and water the lawn. A hose on a breaker, left
to trickle in a basin, is a year-one tool I trust. The
thumb is a personality. The trickle is a drink.

Ollas and tree-watering bags I will not mock if you
have two trees and a vacation. I will not run them on
a library. A bag against a trunk is a wet collar if
you leave it all season. Use it, take it off, look at
the bark.

Zone 7 drip lines freeze if you leave water in them.
Blow them out or drain them like you mean it. Zone 9
drip lines grow algae and ants. 8a drip lines silt if
your well is a clay story. None of the zones forgive a
system you installed and then admired from the porch.

I do not need you to buy our brass siphon. I need you
to match the tool to the number of plants you will
actually walk. Four trees and a mixer is theater. Four
hundred trees and a watering can is a fantasy that
kills the back row. Pick the honest tool. Then walk
it. The walk is the irrigation system. The plastic is
a helper.

Walk the wet spots after a cycle. A drip line you
never inspect is how a quiet row dies while the
timer still clicks. Soaker wants to sit under chips
so the sun does not cook it. A thumb over the end
compacts the ring; a trickle in a basin does not.
Zone 7 lines freeze if you leave water in them —
blow them out. Zone 9 lines clog. 8a lines silt.
None of that is a reason to skip the walk. I carry
a paint pen for emitters that died quietly. The
plastic is a helper. The walk is still the system.
