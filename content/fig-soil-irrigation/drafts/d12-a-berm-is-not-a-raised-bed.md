People use the words like they are the same pile. They are not. A berm
is native dirt you shaped so the crown sits above the flood. A raised
bed is a box or a row you filled, often with dirt you brought, so the
whole root zone lives above the county. Both beat a dish. They fail in
different ways.

A berm is cheap and honest. You use what you have. You make it wide.
You plant high. The roots will eventually find the native clay and
that is fine if the clay is a pantry. The failure mode is settling,
and mower blight, and a berm you built too steep so every drink runs
off before it soaks. I have built those. The tree looked droughted in
a wet month because I had made a hill for water to refuse.

A raised bed is a decision to import a climate. We have used one as a
nursery row — yard dirt, shade, cuttings a few inches apart — already
on the [Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) page. That is a
production bed, not an orchard. Dig the keepers out before the roots
write a knot you will hate next March. A *keeper* raised bed wants
width. A fig mat is not a tomato. A 4×4 box is a pot made of lumber.
It will dry like a pot, freeze like a pot, and still sit wet in the
corners if you lined it.

Do not line a fig bed with plastic. I have smelled that funeral. If
you need a liner you do not have a bed, you have a tank. Hardware
cloth against voles is a different sentence. Plastic is a bowl.

Wood sides rot. That is not a moral failure. Plan for it. Untreated
boards in South Arkansas last until they do not. I will not start a
treated-lumber argument here. I will say I care more about the dirt
inside and whether water can leave the bottom into the county. A bed
on a grade that already sheds is a bed. A bed in a swale is a box in
a ditch.

Zone 7 raised beds freeze harder than in-ground because they are a
pot with manners. The cold-hardiness pack can fight that fight. This
pack says: if you chose a bed in 7, you chose more winter work. Zone
9 beds are for nematodes and bad fill more than for frost. 8a beds
are how clay people keep figs without pretending the subsoil changed.
I use both. I do not call them the same thing when I am talking to
myself, because the hose and the freeze do not.

If you only remember one difference: a berm asks the county to
cooperate. A raised bed asks you to keep filling a climate you
bought. Pick the one you will actually maintain. An abandoned box
full of weeds and a collapsed berm are both bowls with extra steps.
