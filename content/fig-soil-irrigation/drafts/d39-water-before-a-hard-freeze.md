Texas A&M: to minimize freeze injury in a dry fall, water
the figs a few days before a hard freeze. Wet soil holds
heat better than dry soil. A bone-dry root ball freezes
harder. I have believed that on the ground and in a can.

This is not an order to flood a bowl. If the hole already
holds a lake, you do not have a dry-fall problem. You have
a drainage problem that a freeze will make into ice. If
the fall was dry and the finger dusts, I give a deep drink
and then I stop. I do not sprinkle for a week into a chip
pile so the collar stays wet into the night that matters.

Pots are a cliff in January. The hardiness pack can have
the garage and the wrap. This pack says: a slightly moist
ball, like a cake that is two days old, not a swamp, not a
brick. Virginia Tech’s backyard-fig talk of monthly winter
water until just moist matches what I do, except I poke a
finger if the shop is dry. Rain barrels. Little or no
feed. They are not a jungle in January.

In-ground 8a most winters the earth and a drink before the
ugly night are enough for a variety that has earned the
zone. Young plants do not get that deal. Neither do pots.
Neither does a fig in a wind tunnel.

Zone 7 should treat this as religion. Their dry cold is a
different knife than our wet cold, and a dry ball in 7 is
how you lose roots you thought were hardy. Zone 9 should
treat this as a cold-snap tool, not a lifestyle. Watering
every week in a 9 winter because a 7 person scared you is
how you keep a plant moving when it should be still. 8a
takes the TAMU drink when the forecast is ugly and the
fall was proud and dry. Some years I skip it because the
sky already paid. The finger, then the forecast.

I do not wrap a tree I just drowned. The wrap-and-water
combo is a steam room. Drink, wait a day, then if you
wrap, wrap a plant that is moist, not dripping. The
hardiness set can argue materials. I will argue the
timing: water is a few days before, not the hour before,
and not every day after.

Drink a few days before the ugly night, not the hour
before, and not every day after. Wrap a moist plant,
not a dripping one. Pots want a two-day-old cake, not
a swamp, not a brick. Young plants and cans do not
get the established-in-ground deal. The finger, then
the forecast. Some years the sky already paid. I
take the gift.

Zone 7 should treat the pre-freeze drink as religion
when the fall was dry. Zone 9 should treat it as a
snap tool, not a weekly winter habit. 8a takes TAMU’s
sentence when the finger dusts and the night looks
ugly.
