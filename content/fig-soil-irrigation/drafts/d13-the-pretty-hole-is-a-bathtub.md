The pretty hole is the scar I want to spare you.

You dig a clay pit. You fill it with Promix and perlite. The tree looks
happy for a month. Then a three-inch rain, and the mix is a cup sitting
in a clay bowl. The water has nowhere to go. The crown sits wet. The
leaves yellow. You add fertilizer because yellow means hungry in every
video.

Yellow is often wet feet. Dig before you dump nitrogen on a plant that
cannot breathe.

UC IPM tells you to refill with the original soil and skip the organic
amendments in the hole. That is not stinginess. That is hydraulics.
Water moves from loose mix into tight clay the way it moves from a pot
into a saucer — until the clay will not take any more, and then it
stops. The interface is a wall. You built a container without holes
and you buried it. I have tipped trees out of those holes. The root
ball was still the shape of the Promix shaft. It never left. It
soured in place.

If you want imported mix, put it *above* grade in a bed the water can
leave. Do not put it in a shaft. A little compost worked into the
backfill is not the same crime as a fifty-dollar bag poured into a
sump. I can tell the difference when I dig. You will tell the
difference when it rains.

People do this because the native dirt looks mean. Red, slick, not
Instagram. Figs take mean dirt. They do not take a sump. Texas A&M
will grow a fig in relatively heavy clay. They did not ask you to
replace the profile. They asked you to drain.

The other pretty hole is the one you dug twice as deep as the ball
and filled the bottom with gravel “for drainage.” Gravel in the
bottom of a clay pit is a perched water table with extra rocks. Water
hangs at the interface. You spent money to make a worse bowl. I want
the bottom of the hole to be native dirt you did not glaze with the
shovel. Rough the sides. Set the ball. Backfill with what came out.
Water to settle. Walk away from the gravel idea.

Zone 7 pretty holes freeze into a slush pot. Zone 9 pretty holes stay
sweet and anaerobic through a warm wet week. 8a pretty holes do both
in one year if you are unlucky. I have been unlucky. Now I plant
high, I backfill ugly, and I spend the Promix money on the cans that
can actually use it.

If the tree is already in a pretty hole and yellowing after every
storm, you do not have a fertilizer problem. You have a sump. The
honest fixes are raise it — lift and reset on a berm — or knock it
and put the name back in a pot. Leaving it in the shaft and “cutting
back on water” does not empty a clay cup. The sky will fill it
whether you like it or not.
