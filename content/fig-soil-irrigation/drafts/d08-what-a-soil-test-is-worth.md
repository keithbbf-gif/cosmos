A county soil test will not tell you the hole holds a lake. I have watched
people wait on a lab sheet while a thunderstorm wrote the only number that
mattered. Send the bag anyway. Just do not let the bag replace the morning
after.

What I want from UAEX or your own extension lab: pH, phosphorus, potassium,
and a salt or conductivity line if they offer one. Organic matter if they
print it. I want a second opinion before I lime a keeper hole or dump a
triple-sixteen on a young fig. I do not want a fertility religion. Texas
A&M wants small, frequent nitrogen on figs, and no fertilizer in the
planting hole. The first flush comes from the wood. A lab that says “low N”
in March is not an order to bury a handful of 13-13-13 against the trunk.

What the sheet will not say: whether the clay is a pantry or a bowl.
Whether last year’s tomatoes left nematodes. Whether you compacted the
ring with a mower. Whether the well is a boron story. Those are other
tests, or a shovel, or a knock-out morning on a tool plant.

Sample the dirt you will plant in. Not the pretty bag. Not the compost
pile. Six to eight inches in the actual hole, a few cores mixed in a
clean bucket, the form filled out like you mean it. If you have two
spots — a sand pocket and a clay ridge — send two bags. One average of
two counties is how you lime the wrong one.

I do not test every #3. Pots are a moving target. You fertigate, you
leach, you change bags. The lift test and the leaf tell me more than a
$15 sheet on a can I will empty in October. In-ground keepers are the
ones I will pay for. A tree you want for twenty years is worth one
conversation with the county.

Zone 7 people sometimes test because winter feels like a science fair.
Zone 9 people sometimes never test because the Gulf grows weeds. 8a can
steal both habits. Test a keeper hole once. Read the sheet standing next
to the puddle test, not instead of it. If the lab says lime and the hole
still holds water in the morning, you have a drainage job, not a lime
job. Do the drainage job. The lime can wait until the tree can breathe.

**[VERIFY]** the current UAEX kit and address before you mail dirt in a
mayonnaise jar you thought was funny. The form changes. The hole does
not care about the joke.

I keep the sheet in the same folder as the variety list so
I do not lime twice. That is the other worth: memory. Next
March you will remember the figs and forget the phosphorus
line. The paper does not forget. If the agent writes a
rate, I still keep it off the trunk and I still water it
in. A dry dump of lime against bark is a collar burn with
a lab’s blessing. The sheet is a conversation. The hole is
the other speaker. Listen to both before you shop a bag
that promises to change the county overnight.
