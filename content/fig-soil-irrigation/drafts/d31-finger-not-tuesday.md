I do not trust a watering day. I trust a knuckle.

In-ground: through the chips, into the dirt, second knuckle.
Smear means wait. Dust means drink. A crust over soup means
you have a bowl and a schedule problem. In a pot: lift first,
then poke if the lift is a maybe. A #3 that is light gets a
drink. A #3 that is heavy does not get a drink because the
top looks pale. The top lies. The weight does not.

Moisture meters in bark and perlite lie unless you calibrated
them in *that* mix and you still believe them after they
have sat in a drawer. I would rather teach a kid the squeeze
and the lift than hand them a probe they will not use. A
meter is fine if you use it. A meter in a drawer is a
paperweight.

Timers are for a collection you cannot walk, and even then
I want a rain skip and a heat bump, not a Tuesday that runs
through a thunderstorm. We run sprinklers on the pot block
on days it does not rain. That sentence already has the
skip in it. A timer that does not know about rain is how
you sour a row.

People ask for ounces and minutes. I will not print a
minute count as a law. Clay, sand, pot size, mix, wind,
and whether the can is on gravel change the minute. The
finger is the translator. If you cannot tell smear from
dust, water a tool plant on purpose until you can. That
is cheaper than a dessert name you drowned on a chart.

Zone 7 Tuesdays in October are how you go into a freeze
with a swamp. Zone 9 Tuesdays in August are how you miss
a day the pot actually needed two. 8a Tuesdays in April
are how you water a hole that the sky already filled.
Look at the sky. Then look at the dirt. Then the clock
can have an opinion.

I changed after I killed a plant on a good calendar. The
calendar was pretty. The hole was a lake. The finger
would have been rude and correct. I prefer rude.

A collection needs a skip rule, not a better day-name.
We skip when it rained. We bump when the gravel is a
griddle. A timer that cannot hear the sky is a leak.
If you must have a day, make it a walk day: lift five
cans, poke two berms, then water what asked. The walk
is the clock. Tuesday is only a nickname for the walk.

Teach a kid smear versus dust on a tool plant before you
hand them a dessert name and a chart. The knuckle is
rude. The chart is polite. I bury fewer plants with
rude. I keep a cheap notebook on the shop bench for
the weeks I do not trust my memory: rain, lift, skip.
The notebook is uglier than an app and it still works
when the phone is in the house.
