A saucer full of water is a pond you can see. Dump
it. The fig does not want a basement. Mosquitoes do.
In winter a saucer hides a freeze puck. In August it
hides a sour. I will use a tray under a shop row so
the floor stays civil, and I will empty the tray. A
cache pot that hides a nursery can is a cache of
stale water unless you lift the inner can every
drink.

Self-watering pots and reservoirs are a vacation
tool I do not trust as a lifestyle for figs. The
reservoir keeps the bottom wet while the top lies.
Figs want a dry-back. A wick that never lets the
bottom breathe is a pretty hole in plastic. If you
use one for a week you are gone, use it, then take
the plant out and let the mix cycle again. Do not
leave a dessert name on a reservoir all summer
because the bag said easy.

Ollas in a #5 are the same idea with clay. They can
even out a weekend. They can also keep a sour zone
in the middle of a can you stopped lifting. I would
rather a neighbor with a hose than a device I stop
questioning.

Mix collapses. That is not a failure of character.
Peat shrinks. Compost disappears. Roots take the
volume. The can that drained in May ponds in
September. Pot up, or slice the roots and refresh,
or both. Do it before June if you can, so you are
not shocking a plant into our heat. Late pot-ups
sulk. The Introduction already said that.

When I refresh, I throw out the sour cake. I do not
reuse a smell. I do not pick the old mix off a
knotted root and put it under a new name. Wood
travels. Galls stay in the trash.

Zone 7 self-watering in a garage is a way to keep a
bottom wet into a cold snap. Zone 9 self-watering is
a way to keep a bottom wet into a rust week. 8a
self-watering is a way to skip the lift until the
smell finds you. I have skipped. I have also dumped
a can I skipped. The lift is cheaper.

This is the last draft in the set on purpose. Soil,
hole, blanket, hose, can. If you only keep three
habits: plant high, mulch the mat, lift the pot. The
rest of the pack is those three habits talking. The
fig does not need a perfect recipe. It needs air,
a drink that can leave, and a person who will look.
Even is the word. Hero is the split. Height is the
ticket. The can is honest if you pick it up.

Empty the tray. A reservoir is a weekend, not a
summer. When the mix collapses, throw out the sour
cake. Wood travels. Galls stay in the trash. If you
only keep three habits from this whole set: plant
high, mulch the mat, lift the pot. The rest of these
drafts are those three habits talking in different
weather. A perfect recipe was never the point. A
person who will look is.
