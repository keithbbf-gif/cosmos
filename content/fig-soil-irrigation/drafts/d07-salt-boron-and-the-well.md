UC IPM will grow a fig on lime. They will not grow a fig on a sodium jag.
Tipburn. Yield off. That sentence sits in a California page and still
belongs in an Arkansas well conversation, because the hose is part of the
soil. Whatever you pour, the pot and the hole have to live with.

I have used rain barrels in winter and well water in summer. That is already
in our public notes. I will not lecture you about municipal chlorine. I
will say: know what you have. A water softener that dumps salt into the
irrigation line is not a kindness. Soft water is for a washing machine. A
fig does not need its calcium swapped for sodium. If the softener is on the
house, take the hose from before the tank or from a rain barrel. I have seen
leaf edges burn all summer on a “good” mix because the mixer was drinking
softener water. The mix got blamed. The tank did it.

Boron is the one people do not test until the tips look scorched and the
tree looks thirsty when the pot is heavy. Some wells carry it. Some
fertilizer programs add it because a forum said micronutrients. A little
boron is a plant nutrient. A lot is a leaf-edge event you will not undo with
a chelate. **[VERIFY]** with a water test if you are on a new well and the
tips keep burning after you have already leached and backed off the
Miracle-Gro. I will not invent a ppm I did not measure.

White crust on a #3 rim is the pot talking. Salt from feed, salt from the
well, salt from coir that was not rinsed. A clear-water watering now and
then helps. I have not run a leach trial I will quote. I will say I have
tipped a can after a long fertigation summer and the runoff tasted like a
battery. That can got a rest on rainwater.

In-ground is slower to confess. The clay pantry buffers. Then one dry July
you pile on soluble feed and the next storm does not come, and the leaf
margins crisp. Small, frequent nitrogen as Texas A&M says — not a lawn dump.
If you are on a salty well, I would lean harder on compost at the surface
and lighter on the hose cocktail. The tree can eat from the mulch ring
without you salting the profile every Tuesday.

Zone 9 irrigation districts with reused water are a different church. They
already talk salt. Zone 7 wells can be iron and sulfur and still be kinder
than a softener. 8a South Arkansas wells vary by ridge. Test the water you
will actually use in July, not the kitchen tap in January. The fig drinks
the July hose.

I do not chase perfect water. I chase a hose that does not add a second
injury on top of a wet hole. If the well is ugly and the hole is a bowl, fix
the bowl first. Pretty water in a bathtub is still a drowning.
