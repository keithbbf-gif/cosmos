The [Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) page uses yard
dirt for bulk starts of wood we can replace. That is free
rootstock logic. That is not a rare-name logic.
[An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/)
already said do not root in mystery local soil. I am
saying it again because the brick is tempting. The dirt is
free. The pot is right there. The name is $40.

Yard dirt in a can compacts. It imports whatever the
tomato row was carrying. It does not drain like a mix that
has rocks in it. You water. The water sits. Or it runs
around a cake and out the hole. Either way the fig is in a
county you already decided was a bowl or a hotel, only now
the county is the size of a #3.

NC State and TAMU both put root-knot on sand country as a
fig limit. Clay yard dirt is a brick. Sand yard dirt is a
hotel. Neither belongs under a dessert name. Bulk wood we
can replace can take the gamble in a bed. A rare stick
cannot.

I have built mix with expander perlite, topsoil, castings,
leaf compost, aged chicken manure. Topsoil is a fraction
in that sentence, and it is *my* topsoil I have a feel
for, sitting next to rocks I can see. It is not a coffee
can from the fence line. If you cannot see air, the fig
cannot either.

Box-store “garden soil” is the same brick with a barcode.
Moisture-control garden soil is a drowning kit. The Intro
page already warned about box-store dirt crossing state
lines for other reasons. Nematodes are one more. I would
not save money on a rare stick by filling a cup from the
tomato row. LSU 1802 already named that ground. The
savings are a gall.

Zone 7 yard dirt in a can is a freeze puck waiting to
happen. Zone 9 yard dirt in a can is a nematode starter
kit. 8a yard dirt in a can is how you take a clay bowl
with you onto the porch. Leave the yard in the yard.
Buy a bag that already has air, or build a mix that
shows its rocks. The brick was free. The name was not.

Bulk wood in a bed of yard dirt is a gamble we take
on purpose. A dessert name in a coffee can from the
fence line is a donation. Box-store garden soil is
the same brick with a barcode. Buy air, or build
rocks you can see. LSU already named the tomato row.
I will not restage that as a new trial. The savings
were never savings. They were a gall you imported
into a can you now have to dump.

If you cannot see air in the cup, the fig cannot
either. Topsoil as a fraction next to expander
perlite is a sentence I will own. A coffee can from
the fence is not that sentence.
