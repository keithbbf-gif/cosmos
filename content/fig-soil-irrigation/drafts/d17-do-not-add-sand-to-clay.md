This is the Saturday I am trying to talk you out of. The clay is
slick. The forum said sand. The trailer has a yard of it. By
evening you have a hole full of something that looks lighter and
will set up like a cheap brick the first time it wets and dries
in place.

Sand plus clay plus water plus time is how adobe happens. You
need a *lot* of sand to change clay into something that behaves
like loam — more than a trailer, more than a weekend, more than
the hole you are standing in. The amount people actually add is
the amount that makes a binder. I have dug those holes. The
shovel rings. The roots turn.

If you want to change clay, you change the grade and you add
organic matter at the surface for years. Compost, leaves, chips
that rot in place, manure that has aged enough not to burn.
Those things make crumbs. Crumbs make pores. Pores make air.
Sand in a clay matrix makes a filled pore that still does not
drain the bowl.

Perlite in a clay hole is a milder version of the same wish.
A bag of perlite disappears into a county. It does not tile a
pan. Save the perlite for the cans, where you can see it and
where it is most of the volume. In the ground I want height
and surface litter, not a white speck that made you feel
modern.

People also add gypsum because the bag said clay. Gypsum can
help a *sodic* clay in a lab story. Most South Arkansas clay
is not a sodium clay. It is a wet clay. Gypsum will not punch
a hole in a bowl. I would rather spend that money on chips
and a soil test that actually asked about salt. If the test
says sodic, **[VERIFY]** with the agent before you gypsum a
religion. If the test says clay and wet, build a berm.

Zone 7 sand-into-clay is a winter brick. Zone 9 sand-into-clay
is a summer brick. 8a gets the brick and then a thunderstorm
that perches on top of it. I have been the person with the
trailer. The fig did not care that I was tired. It cared that
I had made a worse hole.

Leave the sand on the road. Raise the planting. Mulch the mat.
Let the clay be a pantry. The Saturday you save is the one
where you are not digging a brick back out in October.

If you already dumped the trailer, you do not have to
confess to the internet. You do have to decide whether
the hole still drains. Run the second-fill test. If it
sits, you built a brick bowl. Lift the tree if you can
still get it, shake the brick off, and reset on a berm
of native dirt. If the tree is too far gone in the brick,
knock the name and put the wood in mix you trust. The
sand Saturday is expensive either way. The second
Saturday can be shorter if you stop adding more sand
to fix the first load.
