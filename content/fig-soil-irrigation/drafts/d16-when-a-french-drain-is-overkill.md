Once a person has watched a puddle sit until morning, the next video
is a French drain. Trench, pipe, gravel, fabric, a weekend, a
receipt. Sometimes that is the right weekend. Often it is a way to
avoid saying the fig does not belong in that swale.

A French drain moves water *along a grade* to a place that can take
it. If your yard has no place that can take it, you have built a
gravel museum. I want to know where the pipe ends before I care
where it starts. Into a ditch that already runs? Fine. Into the
neighbor’s fence? Not fine. Into a low spot you will then plant with
a second fig? You moved the bowl.

For one tree, I would rather raise the planting than tile the yard.
A berm or a bed is a day’s dirt. A drain is a project that fails in
the one joint you hurried. For a row you intend to keep, and a
yard that already has a place for water to go, a drain *uphill of
the row* can keep the county from delivering a storm into your
crowns. That is a farm sentence. Most collections are not a farm.

Do not put the pipe under the fig and call it love. You want the
root zone to shed, not to sit on a wet gravel vein. The drain
belongs where the water is coming from, or at the edge of a bed
that must stay above a seep. Under the trunk is how you keep a
collar wet.

I have seen landscape fabric wrapped around a pipe so tight the
silt sealed it in two seasons. I have seen gravel without a sleeve
turn into a silt log. I am not writing an engineering spec.
**[VERIFY]** with someone who drains yards for a living if the
water is a seep you cannot berm out of. I will write the grower
rule: try height first. Try another spot second. Tile third. Pots
are still legal.

Zone 7 drains freeze and heave. Zone 9 drains silt shut in a
hurry. 8a clay silts a pipe if you let the trench become a river
of fines. None of those zones forgives a drain that ends in
nowhere.

If you already own a wet swale and you love it, plant something
that wants a swale. Do not spend a Saturday making a fig
comfortable in a ditch. The fig will not thank you. The ditch
was not confused. You were.

If you already have a downspout writing a river into the
fig bed, move the spout before you tile. A cheap extension
and a splash block have saved more of my plantings than a
pipe. Look up. The roof is an irrigation system you did
not count. I have planted a perfect berm under a gutter
joint and then wondered why the puddle test passed in
March and failed in June. The sky found a new hose. Fix
the hose that is already paid for. Then talk about
gravel. Then, if you still need a drain, know where it
ends. Nowhere is not an outlet.
