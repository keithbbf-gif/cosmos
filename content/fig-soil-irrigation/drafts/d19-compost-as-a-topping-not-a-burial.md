Figs prefer regular composting instead of a fertility circus. The
Austin Master Gardeners said that in their fig handout for Central
Texas, and it matches how a shallow mat actually eats: from the
top. A 2 to 3 inch layer of finished compost over the root zone in
spring, and again in fall if the tree is one you want to keep, is
a meal the roots can find. A shaft of the same compost in the
planting hole is a wet cake.

I want compost that looks like dirt and smells like a woods floor.
I do not want steam, and I do not want a pile that still looks
like the kitchen. Raw manure against a crown is a burn you will
call disease. We have used aged chicken manure from the place in
mix and as a light top-dress. Aged is the word. Fresh is a dare.

Spread it wide. The mat is wide. A doughnut of compost against the
trunk is a wet collar plus a hungry dripline. Pull it back from
the bark the same way you pull mulch. The tree does not eat
through the collar. It eats where the rain hits the wide ring.

Do not till it in around an established fig. That is the hoe
again. Lay it. Let worms and rain take it down. If you are
building a new berm, a little compost mixed into the *surface*
of the mound is fine. The bottom of the mound can be the ugly
clay you already have. The fig will write roots up, not down
into a feast you hid.

In-ground trees in clay that drains do not want a weekly cocktail
forever. Small, frequent nitrogen in spring as Texas A&M says, or
the compost layer and a walk-away, is enough for a tree that is
already putting wood. A weak tree that dropped fruit may have
been dry, not hungry. UAEX lists drought, storms, and cool
weather after set as drop causes. Fix the hose before you buy a
bloom booster. There is no bloom booster that replaces a root
system.

Pots are not a compost pile. A #3 of straight compost is a wet
cake with a label. Compost is a fraction. Perlite is the argument
that keeps it from becoming soup. The media drafts are that
fight. This one is the in-ground topping.

Zone 7 compost in fall is also a little insulation if you keep it
off the trunk. Zone 9 compost in a wet summer is slug and sour
if you pile it. 8a can do spring and fall if the pile is
finished. I would rather skip a feeding than bury a tree in a
wish. The topping is a habit. The burial is a scar.

Chicken-yard compost that is still hot will burn a flare
faster than a fertilizer spill. I have done that kindness.
Aged means it looks like dirt and you cannot name the
feather. Mushroom compost can be salty and alkaline; I
use it light if I use it, and I watch the edges.
**[VERIFY]** your pile. If it smells like ammonia, it is
not a topping yet. It is a dare. Let it sit. The fig can
wait a month. The burn will not wait.
