A bare clay ring and a chipped ring are not the same plant.
The calendar you copied from a desert video was written on
bare dirt. Chips hold the night’s cool and yesterday’s
drink. If you water both rings on Tuesday because Tuesday
is Tuesday, the chipped tree sits wet and the bare tree
sits proud. Then you blame varieties.

I water the finger, not the day. After I mulch a new berm I
expect to wait longer. The top inch of chips will lie to
you — they look dry while the dirt under them is still a
cake. Poke through. If the dirt smears, wait. If the dirt
dusts, drink. The chips are a hat. The head is underneath.

Pots with a chip lid lie the other way. The lid looks wet
from a sprinkle that never entered the mix. Lift the can.
The lift does not care about the lid. A #3 on gravel in
July will still need more drinks than an in-ground ring.
Mulch on a pot is not in-ground mulch. It is a sun hat,
not a county.

When you first lay chips on dry dirt, water *through* them
once so you do not create a thatch over dust. A dry thatch
sheds the first storm and you think you mulched. You
roofed. After that, the clock slows. That is the point.

In fruit swell I still want even moisture under the hat.
Mulch helps me not swing. It does not let me disappear for
three weeks in a 100°F stretch and call it Mediterranean.
Established in-ground, maybe. First two summers, no. Pots,
no.

Zone 7 mulch can keep a ring too wet into a freeze if you
water on the old bare-dirt clock in November. Ease off.
TAMU still wants a drink before a hard freeze if the fall
was dry — one deep drink, not a week of sprinkles into a
chip pile. Zone 9 mulch plus daily sprinklers is a rust
spa. 8a is the in-between: the hat saves July and will
sour August if you never poke through.

I changed my clock the year I mulched and kept watering
like I had not. Yellow leaves. Heavy ground. I had built a
good blanket and then drowned it on a schedule. The blanket
was not the mistake. The Tuesday was.

New chips on a dry berm need one drink through the
thatch or you built a roof. After that I wait longer
than I used to. The Tuesday that drowned my first
mulched ring was a leftover from bare clay. I kept
the leftover. The tree kept the swamp. Now I poke
through before I open the hose. The hat is dry. The
head decides.

Pots with a chip lid still get lifted. The lid is a sun
hat, not a county. A #3 on gravel will not inherit the
in-ground clock just because you dressed it. Hat, then
weight, then hose.
