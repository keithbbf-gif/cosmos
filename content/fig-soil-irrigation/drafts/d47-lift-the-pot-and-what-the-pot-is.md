A #3 on gravel in July will lie if you poke the top
inch. Lift it. Light means water. Heavy means wait.
That sentence is in our pot-versus-ground notes and
it is still the only meter I trust when I am tired.

The can itself changes the lift. Fabric pots drain
and freeze faster than thick plastic. They tell the
truth early. They also dry out on a windy 8a day
while you were in the house. I like fabric for a
plant I will walk. I do not like fabric for a plant
I will forget.

Black plastic is ugly and useful. It moves. It takes
a paint-pen line. On gravel at 2 p.m. it is an oven.
Afternoon shade. A board under it. A shuffle off the
griddle. We like black plastic because the library
moves. We do not like it because it is pretty.

Terracotta looks good, dries out, and cracks. I
would not collect in terracotta in zone 8a. Glazed
ceramic looks like a gift and then it has one sad
hole and then it is a vase. I have been given those
gifts. The fig went back into plastic.

Air pots and prune pots are a conversation if you
like the hardware. They dry faster. They make a
nicer root if you water them like they asked. They
are not a substitute for mix that drains. Fancy
walls around a moisture-control cake are still a
cake.

Size: a cutting in a cup, then a trade gallon, then
about a 3-gallon before June so you are not shocking
a plant into midsummer. That timing is already on
the Introduction page. A #3 is not a forever home.
Most of our library has lived in 3- and 5-gallon
cans. The Fig Jam interview already put the honest
count in public: hundreds of trees, most in those
sizes, a couple dozen in the ground.

Zone 7 pots are the method, not a hobby. They go
into a shop. Fabric in 7 is a faster freeze. Zone 9
pots are for decks and nematodes; winter is a snap
you can roll away from. 8a pots are a choice you
keep making every November and every July. If you
will not lift them in July, plant in the ground or
give the fig to someone who will.

The lift is the habit. The material is the accent.
I can grow a fig in an ugly can I lift. I have
killed a fig in a pretty can I admired.

Fabric tells the truth early and dries while you are
in the house. Black plastic moves and cooks on
gravel. Terracotta cracks here. Ceramic is a vase
with one sad hole. A #3 is not forever. Pot up
before June. Zone 7 pots are the method. 8a pots are
