Jack’s high-casting mix works because of the perlite
beside it. Castings feed. Castings also hold water.
Castings alone in a #3 are a wet cake. If you copy
the Fig Jam interview, copy the rocks too.

I have used castings heavy. I have also built mix
with expander perlite, topsoil, leaf compost, aged
chicken manure from the place. That is practice, not
a bagged SKU I will rank. I will not publish a
percentage I did not measure. **[VERIFY]** on a lift
test: wet versus dry should be obvious when you pick
the can up. If you cannot tell, the mix is too much
like clay.

Perlite you can see. If you cannot see air, the fig
cannot either. Cheap perlite is dusty. Wet it before
you mix if you like having lungs. I am not writing a
safety lecture. I am saying I have coughed through a
bag and it was dumb.

Pumice is heavier and stays put. I like it when I can
get it. Rice hulls disappear in a season. Fine. They
are a year, not a religion. Bark fines give structure
and they rot. Fine. Refresh when the can collapses.
Vermiculite holds. I use it sparingly or not, because
I already have castings and compost trying to hold.
The argument I will not drop is visible air.

Fertigate after the plant is growing, not a lawn dump
into a wet cake. A swamp plus fertilizer is how you
write a sour smell. We saw about one to three feet of
new growth on most trees in a spring we were watching
the mixer. I will say that because we saw it. I will
not turn it into a guarantee for your water and your
mix.

Zone 7 mix can lean a little more hold because cans
dry in the wind and the season is short. Do not lean
so far you freeze a cake. Zone 9 mix should lean
air because the rain and the hose will both try to
drown you. 8a mix is the in-between: air you can
see, a meal you can afford, a lift you can feel.
I have chased fancy recipes. The cans that lived
were the ones I could pick up and read.

Copy the rocks. The castings were the part people
remembered from the interview. The rocks were the
part that kept the castings from becoming a pond.

Wet dusty perlite before you mix if you like lungs.
Pumice stays put. Hulls disappear. Bark rots —
refresh when the can collapses. Vermiculite holds;
I already have compost trying to hold. The argument
I will not drop is visible air. Copy the rocks from
the interview, not just the castings. The rocks kept
the meal from becoming a pond.
