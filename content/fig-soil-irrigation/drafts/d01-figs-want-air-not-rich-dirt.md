People show up with a truck of “garden soil” like they are doing the fig a
favor. The bag says rich. The bag says moisture. The hole looks dark and
expensive. Then a three-inch rain sits in that hole overnight, and the leaves
go yellow, and somebody fertilizes a plant that cannot breathe.

Figs did not invent themselves in a raised bed of peat. Wild *Ficus carica*
lives on rocky, fast-draining limestone hills around the Mediterranean. The
roots evolved to dry a little between events. That is not a romance. That is
why a fig will take hard ground — clay, rock, a thin county — and still throw
fruit, and why the same fig will sulk in a compost cup you buried because a
video said “amend generously.”

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/)
already said it in a bullet: most figs can take harder soil like clay and
rock. You want airflow. They hate being drowned. That is the whole soil
chapter. This pack is the rest of the chapter.

Rich is not the same as open. Compost holds water. Castings hold water. Peat
holds water. A fig will eat those things at the surface, in a thin layer, on a
bed that sheds. A fig will not thank you for a two-foot shaft of the same
stuff sitting in a clay bowl. That shaft is a pot with no holes. I have
watched it. The tree looks happy for a month. Then August storm, then sour
smell when you finally dig.

I do not starve a fig on purpose. I do not plant into a gravel pile and call
it Mediterranean authenticity. I want a hole that drains, a crown that sits
above the grade that floods, and organic matter where the shallow roots
actually live — the top few inches — not a secret feast at eighteen inches
that turns into soup. Texas A&M is plain: well-drained soil, sand to heavy
clay, and the damage they name on sand is root-knot, not “not enough
compost.” Clay is not the villain. A sealed clay bowl is.

Zone 7 people sometimes over-amend because they are already fighting winter
and they want the plant to “get a good start.” The start they get is a wet
crown in a hole that will not empty before the next freeze. Zone 9 people
sometimes under-amend because the dirt is already warm and they assume figs
are weeds. They still need air. A Gulf thunderstorm does not care that your
county never sees 15°F.

8a in the humid South is the in-between again. We get the rain. We get the
clay. We get the week in July when the same clay turns to pottery. The move
is not a truck of mix. The move is to name the dirt, raise the planting if
the hole holds a lake, mulch the mat, and stop treating “rich” as a moral
good.

If you only change one habit this year, change this one: do not buy dirt
until you have watched water leave a hole. A $40 name in a lake is a
donation. The variety is the second question. The hole is the climate.
