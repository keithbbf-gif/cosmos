The fabric is sold as a weed stop that still “lets the soil
breathe.” Then you come back in three years and the chips have
become a dirt layer on top of a plastic tablecloth, the weeds
are in the dirt layer, and the fig mat is trying to live in
that dirt layer because the county under the fabric is a
foreign country.

I have pulled that tablecloth. It comes out in wet rags with
roots woven through the slits. The soil underneath is pale and
slick. Worms gave up. The hose water ran sideways on the
fabric and came out at the edge like a spring. That is not
breathing. That is a roof.

Figs want organic matter to reach the top inches. Fabric is a
vote against that. If weeds are the fear, I would rather a
thicker chip layer I refresh than a product I will hate in
year four. Spot weeds. Do not sheet a mat.

The one fabric I will tolerate is a temporary square to keep
a new berm from washing while grass takes the slope — and then
I want it out. Permanent fabric under a keeper fig is a
decision to farm the mulch layer forever. You will fertilize
the mulch layer. You will water the mulch layer. The county
you planted in will sit there unused, or sit there wet if the
fabric perches.

Weed mat in a pot is a joke I have seen. A disc of fabric over
the mix “to stop fungus gnats” that also stops you from
feeling the mix. Lift the pot. Poke the mix. Do not put a lid
on a can.

Zone 7 fabric plus freeze heaves and tears and then weeds
anyway. Zone 9 fabric plus sun cooks and shreds. 8a fabric
plus clay is a perched table. I have paid for that table. I
do not pay twice.

If you inherited fabric, you do not have to rip the yard
apart in July. Peel what you can when you are already
refreshing chips. Cut out to the dripline over a couple of
seasons. Do not fertilize the dirt-on-fabric layer and call
it a bed. That layer is a pot with no holes, sitting on a
roof, on a county. The fig deserved better than a product
aisle.

Cardboard under chips for a season to kill grass is a
temporary sheet I can live with. It fails. That is the
feature. Fabric does not fail on a schedule you like.
If you inherited fabric, peel on a cool day when you
are already moving chips. Cut to the dripline. Do not
fertilize the dirt-on-fabric layer and call it soil.
That layer is a pot on a roof. The fig deserved the
county you planted it in.

I have paid for that table once. I do not pay twice. A
product aisle is not a drain, and it is not a mulch. It
is a roof you will peel later, on a cooler day, with
chips already in the wheelbarrow.
