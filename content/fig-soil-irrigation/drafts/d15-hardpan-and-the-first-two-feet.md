UC IPM says figs grow best in four feet of soil and also survive in
shallower dirt with hardpan or claypan. That sentence has comforted
people into planting on a plate. Survival is not a crop. A plate
that perches water is still a bowl. A plate that sheds can carry a
fig if you are honest about size and water.

I find hardpan with a rod or a shovel that suddenly rings. In some
counties it is a claypan. In some it is a plow sole somebody’s
grandfather ironed in. In some it is rock. The fig’s fibrous mat
does not need four feet the way a pecan does. It does need the first
foot to be a place oxygen can live. If the pan is at eight inches
and the hole perches, you have a dish on a table. Raise the planting
or pick another spot.

Blasting a hole through the pan and filling it with mix is the
pretty-hole trick again. You made a well. The well fills. I would
rather break the pan in a few spots with a bar so water can *leave*,
then plant high, than excavate a shaft I cannot drain. If I cannot
punch through, I assume the water sits, and I build above it.

Shallow dirt over rock is a pot the county made. It dries like a
pot. It freezes like a pot. It can still fruit if you mulch and you
do not miss drinks in July. I have seen old figs on thin soil
against a barn wall outlive a collection in deep clay bowls. The
wall was the climate. The thin soil drained because there was
nowhere for a lake to hide.

Do not confuse “figs are tough on poor ground” with “figs like a
pan.” Poor ground that sheds is Mediterranean. A pan that holds a
lens of water is a sump with a geology degree.

Zone 7 hardpan plus winter is a shallow freeze-through. Treat it
like a pot if the dirt is thin. Zone 9 hardpan plus a Gulf rain is
a week of perched water you will blame on the variety. 8a hardpan
shows up after you have already dug the pretty hole and wondered
why the second fill would not leave. The rod would have told you
before the bag of Promix.

I will not give you a depth chart by county. **[VERIFY]** with a
rod in the actual hole. Two feet of open dirt that sheds beats four
feet of romance on a pan. The first two feet are the ones the fig
will write a life in. Make those two feet a pantry, not a plate.

A rod test takes five minutes and saves a bag of Promix.
I push until it rings or it does not. If it rings at a
foot, I assume perched water after a storm unless I can
see a daylight outlet. Planting on a slope above the pan
is kinder than blasting a well. If I am already committed
to the spot, I plant high and I treat the tree like a
wide pot the county made. More mulch. More first-summer
hose. Less fantasy about taproots. Figs do not need four
feet. They need the first two to be honest.
