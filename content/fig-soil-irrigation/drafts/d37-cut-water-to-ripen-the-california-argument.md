Commercial fig talk, especially dried-fig talk, will tell
you to ease water as the crop finishes so sugar concentrates
and the fruit does not split. UC IPM: reduce water in late
summer and fall. The 2013 California deficit paper put a
percent on it for two varieties in an orchard. That is a
real argument. It is also an argument that breaks pots.

I will ease an *established in-ground* tree in 8a if the
rain is already doing the job and the fruit is softening. I
will not starve a #3 in July to chase brix. Starving an
Arkansas pot is drop, then a binge when you panic, then a
split. Even is still the word. The California percent stays
in California.

What I will steal: do not add a hose binge on a softening
crop the night before a storm. Do not fertigate late
nitrogen onto a ripening tree to “push.” NC State: late
nitrogen makes tender wood, light fruiting, splitting,
souring in their writeup. Leaves, not jam.

Dried-fig weather is 90 to 100°F days and no late rain. We
do not have that weather as a lifestyle. We have
thunderstorms and humidity. Fresh figs in a humid South
are a pick-every-day job, not a deficit job. If you want
dried figs here, you are fighting the sky. I have not won
that fight by turning off a pot.

Zone 7 can steal the late-season ease more safely because
the heat is already leaving and the crop is done or done-
for. Zone 9 can steal it only if they are in-ground and
the Gulf is quiet — a rare week. 8a can steal it on a
keeper berm in a dry September. That week happens. I take
it. I do not schedule it.

If a grower on a forum swears cutting water turned their
fruit to candy, ask whether they were in a pot, whether
they were in a rain shadow, and whether they counted the
figs they dropped. Candy on three figs and a dump of the
rest is not a method I will copy onto a library.

I would rather pick a ripe fig that is merely excellent
than perform a deficit that makes a story. The story is
cheap. The crop is not.

An established berm in a dry September can ease when the
county tank is already falling. A #3 on a porch cannot
ease the same way. The pot is a fast tank. The county is
a slow one. Treat them like different climates even when
they share a zip code.

Dried-fig weather is heat without a late storm. We do not
have that lifestyle. We have radar. I pick. I do not
perform a percent that was measured in an orchard I do not
own. The pick is the ripening move here. Even beats hero.
