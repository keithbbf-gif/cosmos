A fig in a lawn is a fig in a fight. The grass wants the same
top inches. The mower wants a clean pass. The weed-and-feed
wants a shallow mat to land on. I have watched a ten-year
Celeste go pale in a Bermuda sea because somebody loved a
yard more than they loved a ring.

Living mulch is a phrase that sounds kind. Clover under an
apple can be a conversation. Grass under a fig is usually
just grass. It takes water. It takes nitrogen. It hides the
flare so you hit it. If you want something green under a fig,
I want it farther out than the dripline, and I want a chip
ring the mower is not invited into.

How wide: as wide as you can stand. A three-foot doughnut is
better than a collar. A dripline ring is better than a
doughnut. The pretty clean edge you string-trim weekly is
how you scalp roots and hit bark. I would rather look at
chips than look at a trimmer scar that sours in August.

Keep the lawn fight off the ring. A weed-and-feed on shallow
fig roots is a way to write a bad year. If the grass creeps
in, I pull or I smother with cardboard *under* fresh chips
for a season — cardboard I can live with as a temporary
sheet, not as a forever fabric. Then I pick the cardboard
bits out as they fail. I do not spray a lawn product into a
mat I want to keep.

Dogs will choose the ring. So will kids. So will the hose
bib path. Compaction and grass come together. A stepping
stone to the bib is uglier than a path in the chips and
kinder than a mud trail.

Zone 7 lawns go dormant and people think the fight ended.
The grass still owns the spring flush. Zone 9 lawns never
blink. 8a Bermuda is a religion. I am not here to convert
your yard. I am here to say the fig needs a parish of its
own. If you cannot give it a ring, give it a pot. Hundreds
of our names live in cans for worse reasons than a lawn.
A lawn is a reason.

I like a messy ring. Guests do not. Guests do not pick the
figs. I do. The chips stay.

String-trimmer blight is how a lot of old Southern figs
get a sour scar at the flare. I would rather the ring
look messy than look edged. If you must edge, edge the
chips, not the bark. A physical border — rocks, a board
— reminds the trimmer. It does not replace the ring.
Bermuda will still come. Pull it or smother it. Do not
spray a lawn story into a mat you want to keep. The
parish stays chips.

Guests can have a lawn. The fig can have a messy parish.
I pick the fruit. The chips stay. That is the whole
argument, and I have already lost it to Bermuda once.
