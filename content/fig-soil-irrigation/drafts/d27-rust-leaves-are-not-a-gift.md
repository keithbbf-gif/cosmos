Fig rust loves a rainy season. Texas A&M says so. Infected
leaves go brown, orange on the underside, then they drop
and sit in the ring like a blanket you did not order. People
call that “leaving the nutrients.” I call it leaving the
inoculum.

I do not compost rust leaves against the trunk and call it
mulch. I do not run them through a pile I will put back on
figs the same year. I rake. I bag or I burn if the county
allows it. I do not need a perfect sanitation religion. I
need those leaves off the mat before the next wet week
writes the next cycle.

The pest-and-disease pack can have the copper and the
timing. This pack has the ring. A chip layer that stays
clean is easier to rake than a hay mat that swallowed the
leaves. That is one more reason I like chips. The rust
pages fall on top. I can see them. I can take them.

Do not blow the leaves into the fence and think you won.
You moved the pile to a place you will forget. Off the
property or into a hot pile you will not put back on figs
is the bar. **[VERIFY]** your town’s yard-waste rules
before you make a smoke column. I am not starting a fire
argument. I am starting a “not on the ring” argument.

Overhead water at dusk is a gift to a fungus. Morning is
saner. Leaves can dry before night. We still use
sprinklers on the pot block because hand-watering a
collection is how you start skipping rows. I know what
that costs near harvest. Drip is cleaner. If I had ten
trees I would drip. We have not published a trial that
says sprinklers cost us X percent of a crop. **[VERIFY]**
on your fruit in a wet August. The rust draft is why dusk
sprinklers annoy me even when I run them.

Zone 7 rust is milder some years because the season is
shorter. Zone 9 rust is a lifestyle. 8a humid summers are
why we talk tight-eye fruit and morning sun. The ring is
part of that talk. A dirty ring under a tight-eye fig is
still a dirty ring.

I have left a mess because July was loud. I have also
picked a cleaner August because I raked in June. The
leaves were not a gift. They were last year’s weather
trying to stay.

Morning sun on an east wall is sanitation you plant
once. Raking is sanitation you do every wet year. I
want both. A dirty ring under a tight-eye fig is still
a dirty ring. I have left a mess because July was loud.
I have also picked a cleaner August because I raked in
June. The orange underside is not compost. It is last
year trying to stay. Bag it.
