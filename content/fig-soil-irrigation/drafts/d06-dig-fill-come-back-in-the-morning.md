This is the only soil test I will not let you skip. Dig the hole. Fill it
with water. If it is still there hours later — if it is still a puddle the
next morning — do not put a fig in it.

I want that test before you buy the plant, not after you have a leafy stick
in a bucket and a Saturday of optimism. The hole is the climate. The variety
is the second question. A $40 name in a lake is a donation.

How I run it: a hole about the size I would actually plant — call it a
foot and a half wide and as deep as the root ball, not a post hole to China.
Fill it. If the county is already wet, you will learn faster. If the county
is in a three-week dry, the first fill will disappear into dust and lie to
you. Fill it a second time. The second fill is the one I believe. Come back
in the morning. Standing water is a no. A damp wall that has shed is a yes.
A hole that empty-sips in twenty minutes on clay is a yes I still mulch,
because that clay will bake later.

You can cheat the hole by making it bigger and backfilling with fluffy mix.
That cheat is how you build an underground bathtub: water leaves the pretty
mix and hits the clay wall, then sits. The plant is in a pot with no
drainage holes, buried. I would rather plant **high**. A berm. A raised bed.
A mound. Stick the tree so the crown is above the grade that floods. UC IPM
says the same thing in their language: plant on a ridge or mound several
inches high and several feet across where drainage is poor. We have rooted
cuttings in a raised bed with yard dirt — shade, not wet — already on the
[Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) page. Same physics for a finished
tree. Height is drainage you do not have to explain.

I will not give you a recipe of “X inches of sand plus Y of peat” as if I
soil-tested your county. **[VERIFY]** with a local agent if you want lime
and numbers. I will give you the grower move: **up, not deeper into the
bowl.**

Zone 7 people need this test twice — once in March mud and once after a
slow winter rain — because a hole that drains in May can sit until March.
Zone 9 people need it after the Gulf dump, not after a pretty January. 8a
needs it after the thunderstorm we actually get. Do not run the test on a
Tuesday you picked because you were free. Run it when the sky has already
done the first fill.

If the puddle is still there in the morning, you have three honest options:
raise the planting, pick another spot, or keep the fig in a pot. You do not
have a fourth option called “maybe this variety is tougher.” Tough is not
gills.
