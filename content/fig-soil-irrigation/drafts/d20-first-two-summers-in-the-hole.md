An established fig in a hole that drains will outgrow your
interest in watering. A first-year fig will not. The mat has not
left the planting circle. There is no creek in the county that
plant can find yet. People plant in March, get rain in April, and
disappear until the fruit drops in July. They call the variety
tender. The variety was dry.

Texas A&M: fibrous, shallow, relatively sensitive to drought
stress. UAEX: dry trees drop fruit. UC IPM: young trees may need
3 to 5 gallons a week, more in heat, and frequent light
sprinkles make a lazy shallow root. I want a deep drink when the
top of the root zone is actually dry, not a daily sprinkle that
teaches the mat to live in the top inch and then die there.

How I water a new in-ground fig in 8a: a basin or a ring of
mulch that holds a slow gallon count — not a blast that runs
off the berm — until the ball and the native dirt around it are
wet. Then I leave it alone until the finger says so. In a clay
week that may be seven to ten days. In a sand week that may be
three. In a July that forgot how to cloud over, I do not argue
with the calendar I wrote in April.

The first two summers I treat the tree like it is still in a
pot that happens to be made of county. Year three, if the
growth is a foot or more and the fruit holds, I start trusting
the rain more. I still water a crunchy county. I do not water a
week that already sat in the hole overnight.

Do not fertilize at planting. Do not chase a first-year crop
with nitrogen. The job is roots. A few figs on a baby are a
bonus you can pick or take off if the plant is struggling. I
would rather see wood than a heroic plate the tree paid for
with next year’s roots.

Zone 7 first summers are shorter and the winter is the second
exam. Do not let the tree go into dormancy bone-dry. TAMU:
water before a hard freeze. Zone 9 first summers are a hose
religion because the heat does not blink. 8a is the in-between
again: spring rain that lies, then a July that tells the truth.
Stay for the truth.

A 25-foot fig you never watered after planting is a tree you
gambled. Some gamble well. I have also watched a $40 name
become a stick because June was proud and dry. The hole was
fine. The hose was in the shed. That is not a soil problem. It
is a two-summer problem that starts in the soil conversation,
which is why it lives in this stage. You fixed the hole. Now
you keep the plant alive long enough to use it.
