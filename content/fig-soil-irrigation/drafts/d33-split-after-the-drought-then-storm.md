The split fig is a water story before it is a variety
story. A dry week, a binge, a storm, a fruit that was
almost jam and then opened along the side like a mouth.
People blame the name. The name was the last thing in
the room.

Kong, Lampinen, Shackel, and Crisosto (2013) put
numbers on side cracks and ostiole-end splits, and
regulated deficit irrigation at 55% ETc helped Brown
Turkey and Sierra in that California orchard. That is
not an Arkansas pot recipe. I will not cargo-cult 55%
ETc onto a #3 in zone 8a. Starving an Arkansas pot in
July is how you get drop, then a binge, then a split.
The number stays in the paper it came from.

What I steal is the mechanism: a swing. Dry, then wet,
then the skin cannot keep up. Our version of the swing
is a pot you forgot on gravel, then a thunderstorm, or
a hose you ran the night before the storm because you
felt guilty. Guilt is a binge. Turn it off when the
sky is already committed.

Open-eye figs sour when rain sits in the eye. That is
a different mouth. Split is a crack. Sour is a ferment.
Both show up after weather. Both get worse if you add
a hose to the weather. Tight-eye names in a humid
South are how we dodge some of the sour. They still
split if you swing them.

What I do in a week that looks like a split week: keep
the pots even *before* the front arrives. Do not catch
up the night of. Skip the sprinklers if the fruit is
soft. Pick what is ready before the rain if I can walk
the row. After the rain I do not “make up” a drink.
The sky paid.

In-ground trees on a berm split less than pots on
gravel because the county is a slower tank. They still
split if you drought them and then the Gulf arrives.
Mulch slows the swing. That is one of its jobs.

Zone 7 splits in a short season hurt more because you
do not get a second chance. Zone 9 splits and sours in
the same week. 8a gets the afternoon storm on a
softening crop every year we have a crop. I have
stopped trying to be the hero with a hose at 6 p.m.
when the radar is already green. The radar is the
irrigation system. Get out of its way.

Pick what is ready before the front if you can walk the
row. After the rain I do not “make up” a drink. The
sky paid. Open-eye names I pick harder. Tight-eye names
I still skip the sprinklers. Guilt is a binge. The
55 percent paper can stay in California. Our swing is
a pot on gravel and a thunderstorm. Even before the
front, then skip. If you already binge-watered, pick
what will crack and stop adding more story to the
radar.
