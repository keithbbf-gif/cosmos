August rain. Hot morning. Two kinds of trash on the same tree, and people use one word for both.

**Split** is physics. The fig took up water faster than the skin could grow. Side cracks. Ostiole-end tears. UC Davis postharvest work put numbers on how those cracks shorten life and open decay. Late irrigation swings make it worse. You can split a tight-eye fig. The screen door was shut. The balloon still popped.

**Sour** is biology. Rain, juice, yeast, bacteria. Dried-fruit beetles taxi the crew through an **open eye**. Texas A&M: plant closed eyes if you can. UAEX: Celeste’s tight eye helps; a wet August still sours when water gets in, and heat after rain makes it worse. Vinegar smell. Soft mess. Not a flavor note.

A splitter that sits a day becomes sour. That does not mean split and sour were the same at 7 a.m.

## What I do with each

Split this morning, still smells like a fig: eat it or cook it now. Do not save it for Thursday. Birds and wasps have a vote.

Sour: trash. Compost far from the canopy or the can with a lid. Do not leave a necklace of vinegar under the tree. UAEX is right about wasps and ants showing up for the next pick.

Open-eye varieties in a wet week: pick **early** in the ripe window. You are farming a schedule. The tight-eye draft is the variety fix. This draft is the week-of fix.

## Water in the week they soften

Even moisture while they size. Mulch so the shallow roots do not see every cloud. If you flood a dry pot the night before a storm, you helped the split.

Overhead sprinklers wet fruit. We use them on a collection anyway. I would drip a small row near harvest if I had the hours. I have not published a split-count trial on sprinklers vs drip. [VERIFY] on your pad.

Do not dry them out on purpose the last week because a California paper mentioned deficit irrigation. That work was a specific trial on specific cultivars. Starving an Arkansas pot in July is how you get drop, then a binge, then a split. Read the paper if you want. Do not cargo-cult it.

## Variety still matters

Jack’s notes: NSDC splits if there is much rain, large eye. Syrian Dark #2, Jack Lilly — tight, small. That is fruit we ate, not a catalog. Your clone may differ.

Celeste and Texas Everbearing / Brown Turkey are the county-agent closed-eye pair. I am not a fan of them for dessert. I still understand why they exist here.

A giant thin-skinned fig in a steam-bath August is a hobby. Enjoy it the hour it ripens. Do not plant a row of it as the family tree.

## One walk, two buckets

I pick with two hands in a bad week. Good ripe. Trash. The trash bucket is the one that keeps next week edible.

Do not spray a souring fig with hope. TAMU said chemicals have not been fully effective. The beetle is the taxi. The hole is the driveway. The split is the hose and the sky.

## Thin skin, big fruit, wet week

A large fig with thin skin is a balloon. Jack’s NSDC note — large eye, splits if there is much rain — is the pattern. Small tight figs still split if you binge them. They just give you fewer square inches of failure.

I would not plant a row of thin-skinned giants as the family tree in 8a. I would plant one as a hobby and pick it like a job.

## Birds open the rest

A peck is a split you did not make. It sours by afternoon. Net or pick earlier. The bird draft is the rest. Do not blame only the eye when a mockingbird started it.

## After you pull the trash

Do not leave juice on the pot rim and think you are clean. Rinse your hands. Latex plus sour juice is a bad afternoon. Gloves if you are the person UAEX warned.

Walk the same tree tomorrow. A wet week is not a one-pick event.

Name it right. Split: water and skin. Sour: eye and microbes. Pull both. Then go look at the ostiole on the next variety you are about to buy. If the seller cannot talk about rain, they have not fruited it here. Buy a smaller, tighter fig first. Eat the balloon on a dry week if you must.

A wet August will still take a tax on a good tree. That tax is smaller if you pick in the morning and you do not flood a dry pot the night before a storm. I cannot stop the sky. I can stop helping it. The hose is the part of the storm you own. Turn it off when the fruit is softening, or run it even all month so there is no binge. Even is the word. Hero is the split.

## Two buckets, again

I will keep saying it because one bucket is how sour fruit rides home with the good ones. The good ones then taste like the trash. Separate them in the yard, not on the counter.

A sour fig in a bowl will teach a guest the wrong lesson. Throw it before anyone tastes 'fig' from it.
