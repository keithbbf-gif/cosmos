August rain. Hot morning. Two kinds of trash on the same tree, and people use one word for both.

**Split** is physics. The fig took up water faster than the skin could grow. Side cracks. Ostiole-end tears. You can split a tight-eye fig. The screen door was shut. The balloon still popped.

**Sour** is biology. Rain, juice, yeast, bacteria. Dried-fruit beetles taxi the crew through an **open eye**. Vinegar smell. Soft mess. Not a flavor note.

A splitter that sits a day becomes sour. That does not mean split and sour were the same at 7 a.m. If you treat them as one disease you will keep the wrong fruit and leave the right tax on the tree.

## Split is physics

Kong, Lampinen, Shackel, and Crisosto (2013), in *Postharvest Biology and Technology*, put numbers on how **side cracks** and **ostiole-end splits** shorten postharvest life and open the fruit to decay. That paper is California work on **Brown Turkey** and **Sierra**. Late irrigation swings made cracking worse in that trial. Regulated deficit irrigation at **55% ETc** helped those two cultivars **in that California orchard**.

That is not an Arkansas pot recipe. I will not cargo-cult 55% ETc onto a #3 in zone **8a**. Starving an Arkansas pot in July is how you get drop, then a binge, then a split. Read the paper if you want the California numbers. Do not run a South Arkansas collection like a San Joaquin irrigation block.

Even moisture while they size. Mulch so the shallow roots do not see every cloud. If you flood a dry pot the night before a storm, you helped the split. UAEX already said dry trees drop fruit. The hose is the part of the storm you own. Turn it off when the fruit is softening, or run it even all month so there is no binge. Even is the word. Hero is the split.

I have done the hero version. A #3 that sat light for two hot days, then a long drink because the radar was green, then a popped shoulder the next morning on a fig that would have been jam. The storm got the blame. The binge did the work. In-ground trees hide it a day longer. Pots do not.

Overhead sprinklers wet fruit. We use them on a collection anyway. I would drip a small row near harvest if I had the hours. We have not published a split-count trial on sprinklers versus drip. [VERIFY] on your pad.

A large fig with thin skin is a balloon. Small tight figs still split if you binge them. They just give you fewer square inches of failure.

## Sour is biology

Texas A&M’s plant disease handbook: souring plus the dried-fruit beetle. Plant closed eyes if you can — they name Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma. Magnolia and Kadota take more open-eye trouble. UAEX: Celeste’s tight eye helps; a wet August still sours when water gets in, and heat after rain makes it worse. Caldis, in *Hilgardia* in the 1930s, is the historical “beetles through the eye” story. The taxi has not changed.

Do not spray a souring fig with hope. TAMU said chemicals have not been fully effective. The beetle is the taxi. The hole is the driveway. The split is the hose and the sky.

Open-eye varieties in a wet week: pick **early** in the ripe window. You are farming a schedule. The tight-eye draft in this set is the variety fix. This page is the week-of fix. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already told humid-climate growers to look for tight eyes and smaller figs. I am still not a fan of Celeste and Brown Turkey as dessert. I still understand why the county agent keeps saying their names.

A giant thin-skinned fig in a steam-bath August is a hobby. Enjoy it the hour it ripens. Do not plant a row of it as the family tree.

## A wet-week pick: two buckets, once

This is the morning after the storm. Not a second walk to “sort it later.”

The night dumped rain on fruit that had started to soften. Dawn is already warm. I take two buckets to the first pantry tree — the one we actually eat — before I walk the library. One bucket for fruit that still smells like a fig. One for vinegar. I pick with both hands. I do not put a cracked fig in my palm “to decide later.” The nose decides now.

Split this morning, still a fig: eat it or cook it now. Do not save it for Thursday. Birds and wasps have a vote. A side crack is not a disease. It is a clock. Kong’s paper is why I do not pretend that crack will store. Sour: trash. Compost far from the canopy, or the can with a lid. Do not leave a necklace of vinegar under the tree. UAEX is right about wasps and ants showing up for the next pick.

I do this once. Two buckets. One walk. One bucket is how sour fruit rides home with the good ones. The good ones then taste like the trash. Separate them in the yard, not on the counter. A sour fig in a bowl will teach a guest the wrong lesson. Throw it before anyone tastes “fig” from it.

Walk the same tree tomorrow. A wet week is not a one-pick event. Rinse your hands. Latex plus sour juice is a bad afternoon. Gloves if you are the person UAEX warned about sap.

A peck is a split you did not make. It sours by afternoon. Net or pick earlier. The bird draft is the rest. Do not blame only the eye when a mockingbird started it.

The daily-picking draft in this set is the ripe window. This morning is the tax collector. Pull both. Then go look at the ostiole on the next variety you are about to buy. If the seller cannot talk about rain, they have not fruited it here.

## Jack’s notes, not a catalog

Jack’s tasting notes from [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith): **NSDC splits if there is much rain**, large eye. **Syrian Dark #2** and **Jack Lilly** — tight, small. That is fruit we ate, not a catalog. Your clone may differ. [VERIFY] on your plate.

I would not run a week of rain as a referendum on a tight-eye name I already like. I would run it as a bucket problem. NSDC taught us the large-eye tax. Jack Lilly did not. That is enough variety advice for a wet Monday. Celeste and Texas Everbearing / Brown Turkey are the county-agent closed-eye pair. Tight eye is not a magic roof. It is a better door. Physics can still pop the balloon. Biology still needs an opening, a beetle, and a wet warm morning.

I would not plant a row of thin-skinned giants as the family tree in 8a. I would plant one as a hobby and pick it like a job. Buy a smaller, tighter fig first. Eat the balloon on a dry week if you must.

## The hose is the storm you own

A wet August will still take a tax on a good tree. That tax is smaller if you pick in the morning and you do not flood a dry pot the night before a storm. I cannot stop the sky. I can stop helping it.

Name it right. Split: water and skin. Sour: eye and microbes. Pull both, once, into two buckets. Then leave the California 55% ETc number in the paper it came from. Our pots need even water and a walk at 7 a.m., not a deficit recipe written for a different orchard, a different cultivar pair, and a different sky.
