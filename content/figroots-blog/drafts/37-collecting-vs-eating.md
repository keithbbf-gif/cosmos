We have lived both jobs in the same week. A collection of a few hundred trees and a few hundred names. A much smaller in-ground set. Jack’s least-favorite chore is fertilizing that many pots. My least-favorite morning is inventory dead-outs.

None of that is a pantry.

A pantry is two or six trees that finish in your climate, tight enough for August, picked daily, fruit you will eat standing up. Celeste and a Turkey type are pantry candidates even if I do not love them. A berry type that has proven here is pantry. A row of unfruited celebrities is a library.

## Why the jobs fight

The library wants space, mix, winter shop, labels, patience, money in sticks. The pantry wants water, morning walks, a net if birds are heavy, and a variety you already believe.

If you spend July shuffling #3s, you will miss the one in-ground tree that is actually ripe. I have done it.

If you only plant pantry trees, you will not try the fig that converts a person who “doesn’t like figs.” I have also done that, years ago, before I knew berry types existed.

Do both if you must. Do not lie about which one a pot is.

## How I would start a house that wants fruit

One tool tree that lives here. One honey or gold if you like that. One berry from a person who has eaten it in the South. In-ground if the hole drains. Pots if nematodes or a hard freeze is the life.

Then stop buying names until you have eaten those three.

## How I would run a collection without going broke on shame

Pots. Labels. True-to-type clock. Graft the duds. Ground the keepers. Accept dead-outs. I still cringe. I try to look at what lived.

Do not ship a name you have not fruited as confirmed. Do not plant the whole library in the only good hole.

Jack ranks fruit he has eaten. That list is pantry research. It is not a shopping list for twenty more pots this week.

## The sentence

What do you want on the table in August? If the answer is fruit, plant fewer names and pick them. If the answer is names, keep the shop open and buy jam at the store without guilt.

## Dead-outs

Inventory still stings. New names especially. I try not to make that the whole story. The successes outweigh if you let them. That is what I told the Fig Jam. It is still true. It is also still a sting.

A pantry tree that dies is a different sting. You lost food. Rebuild with a tool name first.

## Money

Cuttings are cheap compared to two years of a wrong tree. They are not free if you count pots, mix, shop space, and July water. A collection has a bill. A pantry has a smaller bill and a bowl.

## How a library steals a morning

You walk the pot block for wilt. You miss the in-ground tree that is actually ripe. Birds do not miss it. I have done this in July. The fix is a pick list on the fridge: pantry trees first, then the library. Not the other way around.

If the library is in a hoop and the pantry is at the house, walk the house first. That sounds dumb until you count pecks.

## How a pantry steals a collection

You stop labeling because “we know the brown one.” Then you take cuttings and you have a bag of brown. The library dies of honesty. Keep the paint pen even if you only eat two names.

We want both. The ground is how the pantry wins a little more every year. The pots are how the library stays alive. Call them by their jobs. The week will make more sense. If you only have time for one job this year, pick the pantry. The library will wait. The ripe fig will not. A name in a notebook does not split. A fig on the tree does.

## Inventory day

We count dead-outs. It is ugly. It is also how you stop lying about the size of the library. A pantry does not need an inventory of ghosts. A collection does. Write the number. Then go pick something ripe if it is summer. That is the correction.

If a guest asks how many varieties you have, and you answer with a number that makes them blink, you are in the library. Offer them a fig from the pantry tree, not a tour of cups. The tour does not convert anyone. The fruit does.

## Two shopping lists

Pantry: tool fig, one berry you believe, hose, net. Library: labels, mix, shop space, patience. If you mix the lists at the checkout, you will come home with twelve names and no net. The birds will explain the rest.

A pantry tree without a net in a bird year is a library of pecks. Buy the net for the trees you eat. Let the library take its chances, or roll those pots to the door the week they ripen.

Count bowls, not just names, at the end of July. The number that matters is what you ate. The other number is a hobby.

A library can become a pantry one hole at a time. That is the only conversion I trust. Not a shopping cart.
