Somebody always swears water is easier. They show a jar on a windowsill and a white nubble. Then they pot it, the roots snap, and they come back asking why pops are “more complicated.”

Water is a tool. It is not the Fig Pop method. The [Introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said you can root in water in a pinch. The [cuttings post](https://figroots.com/2025/12/23/lets-talk-about-fig-cuttings/) said green wood may belong there because it will not store. I agree with both. I do not agree with turning every dormant 6–8 inch stick into aquarium decoration.

## When water earns a jar

**Green wood you got today.** Soft, leaking sap, cannot go in the crisper. A jar is faster than building a tray if you have one stick.

**A dehydrated mailer.** Wrinkled bark, still a green scratch. A soak, then a jar or a proper damp mix. I have seen people skip the dry-the-bark step and grow slime. Water is not a week-long bath for a moldy cane.

**You have no mix and you refuse to wait.** Better a jar than a dry cup of mystery dirt from a ditch that might have nematodes.

I would not water-root a box of fifty dormant cuttings because a video said roots look cool in glass. Coir or a drainy mix, squeeze test, heat mat on a **thermostat**. That is the work we already published.

## What water actually does

The stick sits in wet. Callus forms. Roots that grow in water are fat, brittle, and used to drinking from a lake. The day you move them to Promix they have to learn soil. That transplant is where people lose the plant they were so proud of.

Change the water. If it goes cloudy, you are growing bacteria, not figs. I would change it often enough that it never smells. I will not give you a fake “every 48 hours or 92% succeed.” [VERIFY] against your jar. Warm rooms go sour faster.

Keep the nodes you want for roots *in* the water and a node *out* for the shoot. Polarity still matters. A whole stick submerged is a rot project.

Low light. A hot south window cooks a jar like a pot on gravel. We already fight that with cups.

No fertilizer in the jar. There is nothing to feed yet. You will burn a cutting that cannot eat.

## How I would pot a water root if I had one

Treat it like a pop, only worse. The roots are even more snap-happy. Do not rinse them under a tap for a photo. Slide the stick into a hole in damp mix. Shade. Do not fertigate the first week. Do not put it on a bare heat mat.

If the roots are two inches of glass-grown noodles, a small cup is enough. A #3 of wet mix is a drowning.

Some people keep the jar until the roots look “strong.” Strong in water is not strong in soil. I would move them when there are a few roots you can see, not a wig.

## The honest comparison

Fig Pops: mix you can squeeze, gap under the stick, 75–78°F on a controller, leave it alone. Our [coir vs DE](https://figroots.com/2026/02/10/rooting-fig-cuttings-coco-coir-vs-diatomaceous-earth-de-lessons-from-my-a-b-test/) work was in cups, not jars.

Outdoor dirt: shade, weekly water, free wood, lower hit rate, no jar.

Water: emergency lane for green or lonely sticks.

If your whole system is jars on a kitchen counter next to bananas, you will get fungus and fruit flies and a story. I would spend the money on a bag of coir and a cheap thermostat before I bought a third mason jar.

## What I will not claim

I will not tell you water is sterile. I will not tell you it is “more natural.” I will not tell you it avoids rot — rot loves standing water.

I will not tell you a water root is a better tree. It is the same clone if the wood was true. The method is just how you got it alive.

## Glass vs a cup of water

A clear jar lets you stare. Staring is how people lift the stick every day and break the nubble. A cup of water with a cardboard collar works. I still change it.

Charcoal in the jar, a bubbler, a fish tank — I have heard all of it. I have not run those as a trial I will quote. [VERIFY] if you like gadgets. I would rather spend the gadget money on coir and a thermostat.

A dirty jar from last year’s flowers is how you start behind. Wash it.

## When the roots are brown in the water

That is not a good brown. That is rot. Recut above it if there is length, fresh water, or switch to damp mix. If there is no length, you are done.

White, firm, a few millimeters to a couple of inches: pot. Waiting for a wig of six-inch water roots is waiting for a transplant nightmare.

## South window

I said low light. I will say it again. A jar on a July sill in Arkansas is soup by Thursday. A north counter is enough. Once it is potted, then you can talk about morning sun.

Green stick, today, no mix: jar, change the water, pot like the roots will break. Dormant bundle, winter, you have coir: skip the jar. That is the whole argument. If someone tells you water is always easier, ask them how many they potted through July. The easy part is the photo. The hard part is the week after.
