I have lost more good figs to “I’ll get them after supper” than I have lost to a bad winter.

A fig has to ripen on the tree. The Intro page said it. UAEX said they are perishable. Soft neck, a little give, maybe a crack — that fruit is done arguing. It will not turn into jam on your windowsill if you picked it hard to beat the birds. You beat the birds to a sour marble.

## What ripe looks like here

Dark figs you can grade from the porch. Color, sag, a droop at the neck. Green-ripe figs you have to touch. That is the trade in the bird draft: fewer pecks, more walking.

A drop of nectar at the eye is a rumor, not a rule. Some tight eyes never weep. Some open eyes weep and then sour. Taste one. If it is still latex-bitter, wait. If it is sweet and jammy, pick the rest that feel the same.

Morning is when I want to be there. Heat climbs. Birds eat breakfast. Wasps clock in. A fig that was perfect at 7 is a husk at 2.

## Daily in a flush, not “when I remember”

UAEX: when they are on, you may need to pick every day. That is not a sales line. A productive berry type in a good week will bury you. Jack likes Red Sicilian for a reason — it makes fruit. Fruit that sits becomes trash and then a beetle hotel.

I would rather have a bowl on the counter and a jar started than a heroic Saturday and a carpet of vinegar.

If you work in town, pick before the truck or after, not “Sunday.” A pot you can roll closer to the back door the week it ripens is a harvest tool. The shuffle is not only for winter.

## Latex

White sap. It will itch some people. UAEX says gloves if you are one of them. I am not writing a medical pamphlet. If your wrists burn, wear sleeves and stop wiping your eyes. Wash.

The sap on an unripe pick is also why those figs taste like a rubber band. Wait.

## What to do with a glut

Eat the best ones standing up. That is the point of a berry fig. Cook the rest the same day — preserves, a sheet in the oven, whatever your house already does. I will not drop a recipe card here. I will say: do not refrigerate a split fig and hope. Cold slows it. It does not reverse a sour.

Give fruit away while it is still a compliment. A bag of tired figs is how you lose neighbors.

## Missed fruit

Pull it. Ground figs are raccoon dessert and a fly farm. A tree you “let go” for wildlife is a choice. A tree you forgot is how next week’s ripe fruit gets found faster by everything with a mouth.

Net the two trees you actually put in jars if birds are heavy. Ugly. Effective.

## A short walk

Hands. Two buckets if the week is wet — good and trash. Feel the green-skinned ones. Do not shake the tree like an apple. You will put tomorrow’s fruit on the ground.

Write the date a variety started. That is your GDD notebook without the math. Next year you will know when to take a morning off.

## Cold storage is a pause, not a plan

A whole, sound fig can sit in a fridge for a little while. A split one is a countdown. UC Davis work on cracks and shelf life is the science footnote. The grower footnote is: eat the cracked ones first.

I do not pick hard “for later.” Later is a different fruit. Grocery figs are picked for later. That is why people think they do not like figs.

## Kids and guests

If you want someone to like figs, hand them a ripe one in the yard. Not a bag you forgot in the truck. Jack got into this because of his grandpa’s trees and the fruit. That is the whole marketing department.

A glut week is when you learn who your friends are. Send fruit that is still a compliment.

## The walk is the method

Same path. Same trees. Morning. Feel the green ones. Two buckets in a wet week. Write the start date.

If you only have Saturday, you will lose Friday’s ripe fruit. A pot by the back door the week it starts is how a working person cheats the clock. Roll it back out after the flush if it needs sun.

Ripe is now. The window in a humid hot week is short on purpose. Show up. The tree will not hold your calendar. It holds sugar until something with a mouth arrives. Be first.

## If you work away from the trees

Pick at dawn. Put a bowl in the fridge for lunch. Do not leave fruit in a hot cab. A ripe fig in a truck is jam on the seat and a wasted tree.

If a flush is on and you will be gone two days, pick everything that is close. Close is better than a pecked hollow. You will eat a few that wanted one more morning. You will not eat the ones the birds take.

If the tree is tall, pick what you can reach first. The high fruit is bird food unless you have a ladder you will actually use.
