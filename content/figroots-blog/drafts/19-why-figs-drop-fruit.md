You wait for the first figs. They show up in the axils. Then you walk out after a hot wind and they are on the mulch, still hard, still green, as if the tree changed its mind.

UAEX lists the fathers: **dry conditions, storms, cool weather soon after fruit set, a weak tree**. Texas A&M adds a botanical insult: common figs in our part of the country make mule flowers — no real seed, no seed hormones to help the fruit hang on. So they drop easier than a seeded fruit when the week goes ugly.

That is not a variety “failing to pollinate.” We do not run wasp figs in Arkansas. It is a tree that had a bad week.

## Drought is the one you can fix

Shallow, fibrous roots. They sit in the top of the hole. A #3 dries by Tuesday. An in-ground tree on a clay ridge dries and you do not see it until the fruit lets go.

Water before the wilt at dawn. Midday wilt that recovers can be heat. Dawn wilt is late. The shade-and-water draft is the July manual. This is the fruit chapter of the same story.

A binge after a drought is how you get **split** fruit on the ones that stayed. Even water. Mulch. Do not play hero with a hose on Friday after ignoring it since Sunday.

## Storms and a cold snap

A thunderstorm will knock baby figs off. So will hail. You cannot spray your way out of physics. You can pick up the mess so it does not become a beetle bar.

Cool weather after set is a spring problem. A pretty warm week, fruit sets, then a cold rain. The tree dumps. I have not counted a percentage. I have watched a flush disappear. [VERIFY] against your own spring. Do not rip the variety out because April was rude.

## Weak tree

A pot that spent winter as a brick, a nematode gall farm, a drowned hole, a late pot-up into August — those trees set fruit they cannot finish. The drop is mercy.

Feed a growing plant, not a corpse. Fix roots before you buy a bloom booster. There is no bloom booster that replaces a root system.

Young trees set little green buttons and drop them. LSU notes that LSU Purple can carry a small crop earlier than some. Most names make you wait. The Intro page said year one maybe, year two more, year three the tree is a tree. I still get greedy and then I get a carpet of buttons.

## Not every drop is a crisis

A tree will abort fruit it cannot ripen at the tip of a late shoot when nights fall. That is GDD talking. A late variety in a short year will dump the last wave. That is information. Plant an earlier fig for the house.

A tree that drops everything in a wet August may be souring, not “drop.” Smell the fruit. Vinegar is the eye draft. Split skins are the water-binge draft. Use the right name so you apply the right fix.

## What I do the week I see drop

Lift pots. Water the light ones. Check the hole after rain — bathtub or not. Look at leaves for rust or a plant that has been hungry since May. Do not prune the tree in a sulk. Do not dunk it in fertilizer.

Write the date. If the same variety drops every year in the same week, you have a climate mismatch or a water habit. If it drops once after a storm, you have weather.

## Buttons on a baby tree

The first year a cutting is a plant, it will set little green beads and drop them. LSU has said even young trees show fruit at the leaf joint and most of it fails. LSU Purple can be an exception with a small early crop. Most names make you wait.

I still get greedy. I still get a carpet. I do not rip the tree out. I do not spray a “fruit set” miracle. I water and I wait for year two and three. The Intro page already timed that.

If a mature tree drops *everything* every year, that is a different sentence: water, nematodes, a Smyrna type you should not have planted, or a variety that cannot finish. Type first. Then culture.

## Wind

A gust will throw fruit that was barely hung. A pot that blows over dumps a year’s work. I would rather have a #3 on the ground than a tall sail in a storm. Stake an in-ground teenager if your ridge blows.

After a storm, pick up the beads so they do not become a beetle bar. Then look at what is still on the tree. That is the crop. Do not count the mulch.

## Cool nights after a warm set

Spring in Arkansas does this. Fruit sets, then a cold rain. The tree dumps. I write the date. I do not rewrite the orchard plan on one April.

If it happens every year on the same early variety, you may be asking for breba and spring fruit the climate will not let you have. Main crop later is the honest crop.

Figs drop. Healthy trees drop less. Even water, a root system, and a variety that can finish here will not make you immune. They will make the carpet smaller. If you need a number, I do not have one I measured. I have a habit: lift the pot, check the hole, smell the fruit, wait on a baby. That habit has saved more names than a product.
