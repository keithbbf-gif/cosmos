You wait for the first figs. They show up in the axils. Then you walk out after a hot wind and they are on the mulch, still hard, still green, as if the tree changed its mind.

That is not a curse. It is not a variety “failing to pollinate” if you planted a common fig. It is a tree that had a bad week, or a baby that set buttons it cannot finish, or — rarely — a wasp type you should not have planted in Arkansas.

Name it right or you will apply the wrong fix.

## UAEX already named the fathers

University of Arkansas Extension lists them: **dry conditions, storms, cool weather soon after fruit set, a weak tree**. Shallow, fibrous roots. If the tree gets too dry, it can drop fruit.

Drought is the one you can do something about. A #3 dries by Tuesday. An in-ground tree on a clay ridge dries and you do not see it until the fruit lets go. Water before the wilt at dawn. Midday wilt that recovers can be heat. Dawn wilt is late. The shade-and-water draft is the July manual. This is the fruit chapter of the same story.

A binge after a drought is how you get **split** fruit on the ones that stayed. Even water. Mulch. Do not play hero with a hose on Friday after ignoring it since Sunday. The split-versus-sour draft is that balloon. This page is the beads on the mulch.

A thunderstorm will knock baby figs off. So will hail. You cannot spray your way out of physics. You can pick up the mess so it does not become a beetle bar.

Cool weather after set is a spring problem. A pretty warm week, fruit sets, then a cold rain. The tree dumps. I have not counted a percentage. I have watched a flush disappear. **[VERIFY]** against your own spring. Do not rip the variety out because April was rude. If it happens every year on the same early variety, you may be asking for breba and spring fruit the climate will not let you have. UAEX already said breba rarely overwinters here. Main crop later is the honest crop.

## TAMU: water stress, and nematodes

Texas A&M’s 2015 figs PDF: shallow roots, relatively sensitive to **drought stress**. Root-knot nematodes **contribute to premature fruit drop**. Galls. A tired top. You fertilize and nothing happens because the roots cannot work. The nematode draft is the knock-out. This page is why a knotted tree dumps beads.

The plant disease handbook adds a botanical insult: common figs in our part of the country make mule flowers — no real seed, no seed hormones to help the fruit hang on. So they drop easier than a seeded fruit when the week goes ugly. That is not a wasp story. That is why a dry week, a storm, or a weak root system writes a carpet.

NC State: if trees **occasionally** drop mature fruit, look at extra nitrogen, shade, winter injury, drought, or poor drainage. If they drop **every year and none of the fruit matures**, that is the Smyrna test. Hold that for the next heading.

A drowned hole drops too. Wet feet look like hunger. The clay draft is the puddle test. Do not add nitrogen to a bathtub.

## Buttons, a drought dump, and a wasp-type drop

Three carpets. Do not mash them.

**Buttons on a baby.** The first year a cutting is a plant, it will set little green beads and drop them. LSU has said even young trees show fruit at the leaf joint and most of it fails. LSU Purple can be an exception with a small early crop in their writeups. Most names make you wait. The Intro page said year one maybe, year two more, year three the tree is a tree. I still get greedy. I still get a carpet. I do not rip the tree out. I do not spray a “fruit set” miracle. I water and I wait.

**Drought dump on a tree that can finish.** Hard green figs on the mulch after a dry week, then maybe a binge and splitters on what stayed. Hose. Mulch. Even water. Not a new variety.

**Wasp-type drop.** A healthy, watered tree that sets and dumps **everything, every year**, with nothing that matures. NC State: probably a **Smyrna**. The wasp is not a backyard tool in Arkansas. UAEX: in our climate only the common figs can be grown. The common-figs draft in this set is the type filter. Do not add a wasp house to a hose problem. Most dumps I see are hose and weather. A true Smyrna here is rarer than a dry pot. It is also the one dump a wasp story actually fits.

A tree that drops the last wave on a late shoot when nights fall is GDD talking. A late variety in a short year will dump fruit it cannot ripen. That is information. Plant an earlier fig for the house. I will not invent a degree-day table. The GDD draft is the heat count.

A tree that drops everything in a wet August may be souring, not “drop.” Smell the fruit. Vinegar is the eye draft. Split skins are the water-binge draft.

## The week I see drop

Lift pots. Water the light ones. Check the hole after rain — bathtub or not. Look at leaves for rust or a plant that has been hungry since May. Do not prune the tree in a sulk. Do not dunk it in fertilizer.

Write the date. If the same variety drops every year in the same week, you have a climate mismatch or a water habit. If it drops once after a storm, you have weather.

A gust will throw fruit that was barely hung. A pot that blows over dumps a year’s work. I would rather have a #3 on the ground than a tall sail in a storm. Stake an in-ground teenager if your ridge blows. After a storm, pick up the beads so they do not become a beetle bar. Then look at what is still on the tree. That is the crop. Do not count the mulch.

A weak tree — a pot that spent winter as a brick, a nematode gall farm, a drowned hole, a late pot-up into August — those trees set fruit they cannot finish. The drop is mercy. Feed a growing plant, not a corpse. Fix roots before you buy a bloom booster. TAMU said no fertilizer at planting. A drop week is not a feed week. Water. Then look at roots. Then, if the plant is actually growing, the fertigation draft starts.

## It was probably not a curse

Figs drop. Healthy trees drop less. Even water, a root system, and a variety that can finish here will not make you immune. They will make the carpet smaller.

If you need a number, I do not have one I measured. I have a habit: lift the pot, check the hole, smell the fruit, wait on a baby, check type if *everything* fails *every* year. That habit has saved more names than a product.

I would not rip a year-two pot because June dumped buttons after a dry week. I would not keep watering a bathtub because the beads made me sad. I would not plant a Smyrna because a drying video looked romantic.

The figs fell off. Walk the list. Drought. Storm. Cool snap. Weak tree. Nematodes. Buttons. Type. Then pick up the beads and water the tree that is still a tree. The curse was a story. The mulch is evidence.
