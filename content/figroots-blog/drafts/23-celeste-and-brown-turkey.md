I have said out loud that I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste for eating, so far. We still have them. We give a long trial. We would graft over before we would throw a live root system in a pile.

That is not hypocrisy. That is two jobs. **Climate tool** and **dessert**. County extension is in the tool business. I am in both.

## What UAEX actually said

Celeste: most cold-hardy of the common yard figs they talk about. Small brown fruit. Earlier than Brown Turkey. **Tight eye.** Helps with beetles and souring. Does **not** bounce from a hard late-winter prune. Chop it and you may delete the crop.

Brown Turkey, often sold as Texas Everbearing: a bit less hardy, a bit later, a longer pick, and it **fruits after freeze or a severe prune**. That is why people call it everbearing. A lot of that is main crop on new wood, not a breba you can bank.

Fruit souring in a wet August still happens. Tight eye is a screen, not a vault.

If you want one tree that lives through an Arkansas winter and feeds a kid who does not care about berry notes, these two names are why the co-op stocks them.

## Name pile

Brown Turkey is not one clone. UAEX warned about name confusion. Your neighbor’s “Turkey” may not be your box-store “Turkey.” Taste it. Watch the eye. Watch the season. Then decide if you even care what the tag said.

Celeste has aliases too. Small, early, tight, sugar-leaning — that pattern matters more than a spelling.

## How I would use them

**Celeste** if you want earlier fruit and a closed eye and you will **not** hat-rack it in February. Protect last year’s wood if you also want a shot at breba (you probably will not get it here). Main crop on unpruned-enough wood.

**Brown Turkey type** if your winters bite and you still want fruit on the resprout. Accept a later, longer season and a fruit I find boring.

I would plant one tool tree before I planted twelve internet names. Eat the tool fruit in a year the celebrities are still sticks. Then spend money.

## Flavor

Sugar. Useful. Not a conversation. The flavor-family draft is where berry and honey live. Do not expect a Celeste to taste like Negra d’Agde. That is not its job.

If a guest says “I don’t like figs” and you hand them a watery grocery turkey, you have not tested the guest. Hand them a ripe berry type if you have one. Keep the tool tree for the week the berry type sours.

## Graft, do not preach

A healthy boring tree is rootstock. We have said we would graft over. That is a later draft. It is also permission to keep the hole.

Do not sneer at a county-agent fig if your Black Madeira is still a cup. Do not pretend a tool fig is a dessert fig to win a forum argument.

## What “Texas Everbearing” is doing on a tag

It is usually a Brown Turkey type that fruits over a long stretch on new wood. It is not a machine that fruits in January in a shop. It is not a promise of breba. Read the breba draft.

If two neighbors have “Everbearing” and the fruit does not match, believe the fruit. Name pile.

## Kids and county trees

A child who wants figs should get a tree that lives. Celeste and a Turkey type are how a lot of us started — or how a grandpa started, which is how Jack started. You can add the berry conversation later. You cannot add it if the first tree died and the kid decided figs are a hassle.

## Graft day

When I am done being fair to a dull fruit, the hole stays. Scion from a name I trust. Relabel. Suckers off. That is the graft draft. The tool tree did its job: it taught the hole, it fed somebody, it waited.

## What I tell a neighbor who wants “just one fig”

I tell them Celeste or a Turkey type from a person who has fruit, or from a nursery that is not a parking lot. I tell them tight eye, water, east wall if they have it. I do not tell them to start with a $40 late berry name and a YouTube prune.

If they already have a grandpa tree, I tell them to take cuttings of *that* and eat it before they shop. That tree already proved the county.

Plant the tool. Trial the rest. Tell the truth on the pot. That is how a yard feeds people while a collection learns names. I can not be a fan of the flavor and still be a fan of the tree that lived. Those are different compliments. The county agent is in the survival business. I am in the eating business too. I need both businesses on the place.

## Taste them ripe

A grocery turkey is not the test. A tree-ripe Celeste in a dry week can be a fine sugar fig. I still prefer berry types. I will not pretend a hard store fig is the county tree’s fault.

A hard February prune on Celeste is how a useful tree becomes a leaf bush. Leave the wood if you want the fruit.
