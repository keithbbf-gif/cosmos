The [Fig Pop](https://figroots.com/fig-pops/) page is not shy. Set the mat to about **78/75°F**. Use a **thermostat**. Otherwise you will roast the cuttings.

I have seen trays go soft and sour on a mat that ran like a griddle. The mix was fine on Monday. The controller was “I’ll watch it.” Nobody watched it.

## What the mat is for

Bottom heat for a **stick**. Roots like a warm floor. Leaves do not need a tanning bed. Low light. You are not growing lettuce.

Once there are roots and a plant, take it off the mat. A leafy pop on a hot mat dries from the top and cooks from the bottom. That is how you get the collapse in the failure draft.

Outdoor dirt starts do not get a mat. Shade and time. Different method.

## Measure, do not believe the dial

Cheap mats lie. Rooms change. A probe in the **mix**, not on the plastic, tells you what the cutting feels. If you do not have a probe, you are guessing.

75–78°F in the cup is the neighborhood we already print. I will not pretend 90°F is “faster.” Faster to rot, maybe.

A basement that stayed steady is part of why the [coir vs DE](https://figroots.com/2026/02/10/rooting-fig-cuttings-coco-coir-vs-diatomaceous-earth-de-lessons-from-my-a-b-test/) tray was clean. A sunroom that hits 95 at 2 p.m. is a different machine. The mat on top of that is how you make stew.

## Moisture plus heat is the rot pair

Warm and wet is biology class. Squeeze the coir. Gap under the stick. Do not add heat to a dripping bag and call it a method.

If a cup goes light, water. If a cup stays heavy and warm for a week, you may already be late. Smell it.

## No mat

Plenty of people root without one. Our outdoor beds did. Slower, dirtier, free wood. If your house is already 70°F and you refuse to buy a controller, I would skip the bare mat rather than gamble.

I would buy the controller before I bought a third hormone.

## Winter mats, summer mats

A cold garage needs the mat more. A July porch does not. I would not run a mat in a hot shop “because the guide said 78.” The guide assumed you were not already at 85.

Read the wood. Read the room. Then plug the mat into something that can turn it off.

## Covers and heat

A dome on a hot mat is a steam oven. The Pop method likes humidity in the bag of mix, not a tropical terrarium you never open. If you cover cups, watch the temperature *inside*. I have seen condensation and soup.

DE cups under a cover in our test got help when they were dry on top. That was a hydration move, not a heat move. Do not copy a cover onto a mat that is already running hot.

## Power outages

A mat that dies in a cold garage for two days is a stall. A mat that sticks ON in a heat wave is a funeral. The controller should fail in a way you can notice. Walk the tray. Smell it. Touch the cup.

## Varieties that sit

Heat will not turn a slow name into a ten-day rooter. Our A/B tray had fast and slow in the same basement. If you crank the mat to “hurry,” you will hurry rot. Leave the slow ones in the right moisture and wait. The failure draft is callus-only. Heat is not the only knob.

## Outdoor dirt does not get a mat

I will say it again because people drag a mat onto a porch in June. The porch is already hot. The Outdoor page is shade and weekly water. Different method.

Thermostat. Probe if you can. 75–78 in the mix. Off when it is a plant. That is the whole heat chapter. If you skip one purchase, skip the third hormone. Do not skip the controller. The Pop page already told you. I am only repeating it because the roast trays keep happening. A cheap probe in one cup will teach you more than a week of guessing. Put it in the mix, not on the plastic. The plastic lies the same way a black pot lies on gravel.

## Where the mat sits

A mat on insulation behaves differently than a mat on a cold concrete floor. Measure the cup, not the room. If the floor is stealing heat, the dial that says 78 may be 68 in the mix. If the floor is a heater, the mix may be 90. The probe ends the argument.

I would unplug a mat I cannot measure before I would leave it on overnight in a hot shop. A cold tray is a delay. A hot tray is a smell. I know which one I can fix in the morning.

## Unplug when you leave town

A tray on a mat with no one home is a bet. If the controller sticks, you come back to soup. If you must leave, I would rather a cooler room and no mat than a hot shop and a prayer. Delay beats a funeral.

A thermometer that only lives in a kitchen drawer will not save a tray. Put one in the cup. Leave it there until you believe the controller. Then you can put it back.
