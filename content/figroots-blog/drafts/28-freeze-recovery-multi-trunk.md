UAEX’s comfort line: even if they freeze to the soil, they often resprout and may bear that year because main crop is on current wood. TAMU’s picture: a crowd of shoots, then you keep **five or six** as new trunks, thin over **two or three weeks** so you do not strip the leaf factory in one afternoon.

That is the Southern fig after a bad winter. It is a bush that you turn back into a bush on purpose.

## First: are the roots alive?

A pot that froze solid may be dead below as well as above. Scratch wood. Wait for budbreak. If nothing moves and the pot smells sour, you have a corpse. Cuttings from that plant should have been taken *before* the freeze if the wood was good. Too late now.

In-ground roots in clay that did not heave have a better chance. Wait. I have watched people dig up a “dead” fig in February that would have moved in April.

## Then: do not sculpt in January

Dead and live are easier at budbreak. Celeste still does not want a nervous hat-rack if any old wood lived — you may delete fruit. A Turkey type is more forgiving. Read the tool-tree draft.

If everything above ground is meat, you are in TAMU’s resprout program. Let the shoots come. When they are about two feet, pick the strong ones with decent spacing. Remove the rest in passes. Five or six trunks is their number. I have kept fewer on a tight hole. I have kept a thicket when I was lazy and then I paid for it with a spray of weak wood.

## Fruit that year

Maybe. Main crop on new shoots needs time and heat. A late freeze plus a late start plus a late variety is a GDD miss. An early tool fig has a better shot. Do not fertilize like you can buy back April.

Water the first summer. Those new trunks have a small root system relative to the leaf they will make. Mulch. Afternoon shade on a black pot of a survivor.

## Rebuild vs replace

If the variety was a tool tree, rebuild. The hole is already paid for.

If the variety was a mislabeled dud, this is graft-over season once you have a shoot as thick as a pencil. If it was a name you never liked, same.

If the roots are knotted or the hole is a bathtub, do not rebuild. Move.

## Cuttings from the thicket

You can take wood when it lignifies. You will delay the tree again. I would rebuild first on a one-tree yard. I would take extras on a donor that always suckers.

Frozen-back wood that is still brown and dry is not a cutting. Green suckers are not fridge wood.

## Pots that froze

A #3 that sat out can be dead in the roots while the stick still looks brown. Wait for bud. Smell the mix. If it is sour and nothing moves by the time the rest of the block is leafing, you are done. Take the label off so you do not water a ghost.

If it moves from the base, pot-up rules still apply. Fragile. Shade. Do not celebrate with fertilizer.

## In-ground thicket, year two

The trunks you kept will want to be a thicket again. Thin. Do not let twenty weak stems share one hole forever. Five or six was TAMU’s number. I have kept three on a tight yard. I have kept a mess and then I cut it in July when I was already hot and mad. Spring passes are kinder.

## Fruit expectations

An early tool fig may give you something. A late celebrity may give you leaves. That is GDD plus a late start. Do not rip the name out in September of a freeze year. Write the year down.

## Labels on a thicket

Every sucker is the same variety if the tree was one clone. If you had a graft, suckers are the old name. I have said that. A freeze year is when people forget and then they have a mixed bush. Paint the kept trunks if you need a reminder.

## Water the resprout

New shoots, small effective root relative to the leaf they will make. July will cook them. Mulch. Hose. Afternoon shade on a pot of a survivor. Do not treat a freeze year like an established orchard.

A freeze is a reset, not a funeral, if the roots lived. Pick a handful of shoots. Grow this year’s wood. Eat what the season allows. That is recovery without a ceremony. The ceremony is a saw in January when you cannot tell live from dead. Skip it. April will show you the green. Then you earn the right to cut.

## Do not fertilize the thicket the week it sprouts

Those shoots are using stored food. Water. Shade if they are in a pot. Feed when they are a plant, not a pale spear. The fertigation draft is the same sentence.

A thicket you never thin becomes a pile of weak stems and rust. Five trunks is enough work. Twenty trunks is a hedge you will hate in July.

## Year-after wood

The trunks you kept this year are next year’s prune decision. Do not treat a recovered bush like a California single-trunk orchard. Multi-trunk is how we live with 8a. Own the shape. Thin it. Do not pretend it is a lollipop.
