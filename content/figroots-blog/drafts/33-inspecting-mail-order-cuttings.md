We already posted [damaged cutting signs](https://figroots.com/) for refunds. People still leave the box in a hot truck because they are “busy.”

I open it the day it lands. Shade. Hands clean enough to not add dirt. Inventory. Then I decide: stick, fridge, or a photo to the seller.

## What I look for

**Count.** What they said vs what I got. Names on bags.

**Wood.** Lignified? Green? Sap in the plastic? The cuttings post is the read: brown/gray, no milk on a fresh cut if they sold you dormant.

**Damage.** Crush. Mold. Wrinkle. Frozen mush if it rode a northern truck in January. Photograph on a plate with the label in the frame. That is evidence. A later “it looked fine” email is not.

**Smell.** Sour bag is a clock. Wash the firm ones. Trash the slime.

A week on a kitchen counter is not storage. Warm, dark, forgotten — that is how a good box becomes a Damaged Cuttings folder.

## Then the fork

Green or moving: root now. Water if you have nothing else. No crisper.

Dormant, clean, tray ready: wash, stick. 6–8 inches, three nodes if they sent that. Recut tired faces.

Dormant, clean, tray full: dry the bark, parafilm if it is a long hold, double-bag, crisper. Weekly look. Storage draft.

Dormant, dirty, wrinkled: wash, maybe a soak, dry the bark, stick. I would not file dehydrated wood for three months and hope.

## Heat and cold in the mail

I would not ship green wood across the country in July. I would not leave a January box on a porch that hits 10°F overnight if I could help it. The calendar draft is the seller’s job and the buyer’s job.

If it cooked, say so. If it froze, say so. If you waited five days to open it, say that too — to yourself, at least. The seller cannot refund a counter you ignored.

## Trusted wood still gets inspected

A good name can still have a bad week in a truck. Inspection is not an insult. It is how you keep a collection honest.

If the wood is junk and the seller is real, they want the photo. If the seller vanishes, you learned the Intro lesson the expensive way.

## What I photograph

The closed box if it looks cooked. The open bundle with the label. A close-up of mold or a crush. A ruler or a known object if the length is the fight — we sell and buy 6–8 inch, three-node wood as the standard. A two-inch chip is not that standard.

Send the photos the same day if you need a replacement. A week later is a different story.

## What I do not argue

I do not argue that a green cutting should have stored. I do not argue that a box I left in the truck for four days is the seller’s winter. I do argue a bundle that was slime when I opened it the afternoon it arrived.

## Wash the same day if you are keeping it

Do not put a dirty, sap-smeared bundle in the crisper “to deal with later.” Later is mold. Wash, dry the bark, then fridge or stick. The storage draft is the long hold. Inspection day is the fork.

If you are too tired to wash, you are not too tired to put the box in a cool room and set an alarm. You are too tired to do it right at midnight. Morning is fine. Saturday next week is not.

## Length and nodes

If they sold you the industry cutting and you got a two-node whip, that is a count problem. Photograph it. You can still try to root a short stick. You should not pretend it was the standard.

A 14-inch cane is a gift. You can make two cuttings if nodes allow. Write both cups the same name. Do not invent a second variety because you split a stick.

Open it. Shade. Photo. Decide. The box is not a storage method. The porch is not a rooting room. The stick in your hand is the whole shipment. Treat it like it can still die. Because it can. The seller’s reputation is the next shipment. Your counter is this one.

## Refunds without a fight

Our damaged-cuttings page exists so you know the signs. Use them. Be specific: mold on three of eight, crush on the labeled bag, no sap vs sap everywhere. A vague “they look sad” email is how you get a vague answer.

If the seller makes it right, remember that when you talk. If they vanish, remember that too. The Intro already told you where not to buy.

A frozen bundle looks fine until it warms and collapses. If the box rode a northern truck in January, watch it as it thaws. Do not fridge a mush you have not thawed and judged. Mush is trash. Firm wood after a thaw can still go in a cup.

## Night arrivals

If the box hits the porch at 8 p.m. in July, open it. Do not let it sit in a still-hot box until morning. Morning on a porch in Arkansas is still a cook. Shade, kitchen counter, decide. Sleep after the sticks are out of the cardboard oven.

If you ordered ten names, make a list before you open the box. Check them off. A missing name is easier to see on paper than in a pile of brown sticks that all look related.
