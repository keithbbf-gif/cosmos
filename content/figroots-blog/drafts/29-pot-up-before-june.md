The [Introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) said pot into a **3-gallon** before the plant is a knot, and do it **before June** because a late pot-up can shock them. I have watched that sentence come true. The leaves look insulted. The pot sits wet because you panicked. Then it sits dry because you got scared of rot. Then it is August.

This is not a new method. It is a calendar. The other pot-up draft in this set is the **root check**: roots on the cup wall, do not shake the mix, leaves lie. This page is the **deadline**. If the plant is already growing in spring, move it while it still wants to grow. After June in South Arkansas, you are asking a wounded root system to drink as fast as July evaporates a **#3** on gravel.

## This page is the date. The other page is the roots.

The when-to-pot-up draft in this set is whether the cup is ready. Callus is not roots. A leafy stick with an empty cup is a battery. Outdoor bulk starts run a different clock: the [Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) page waits for **7–8 nice leaves** in a pot, or **9–10** in a bed, about **60–90 days**, or you wait until fall dormancy and dig then. Do not mash those calendars into this one.

This page assumes you already have a real root system. Now the question is the week on the wall.

If the cutting rooted in March, you have no excuse to still have it in a cup in July “to be safe.” Safe was a real root system in April and a move in May.

If the cutting rooted in July, I would not force a #3 on a gravel pad. Shade, a modest pot, or wait for dormancy. The before-June rule is for plants that are **already growing in spring**. It is not a dare to transplant into a heat wave.

A tray that rooted late on purpose — stuck in May, roots in June — is a tension, not a law. I would pot a June-rooted plant into a modest pot, shade, and skip the gravel pad. I would not skip the move if the cup is a knot. A knot in July drinks and dries twice a day. If I can wait until a cooler week, I wait. If the cup is cooking, I move it in the morning and I live with a sulk that is smaller than death.

## What I move into

A trade gallon is fine from a cup. A **#3** is what the Intro named as the nursery pot after the cup is root-bound. Then a **#5** if we keep it. Most of our library lives there. The Fig Jam interview already put the honest count in public: hundreds of trees, most in 3- and 5-gallon cans.

A **#5** is not a spring requirement. Cup to gallon to #3 is enough for year one. A jump to a barrel in May is a lot of wet mix around a small root. I would rather step up than drown a baby in a tub it cannot drink. If the only pot you have is a barrel, do not fill it with a cup of roots and a lake of mix. Use a smaller can inside the story you wanted. The barrel can wait a year.

Mix that drains. Promix HP if you do not want to think. Water to settle. Shade a few days.

**Fragile pop roots.** [Fig Pops](https://figroots.com/fig-pops/) already said be careful. Support the mix. Do not dump it like a tomato start. I already broke a plant by treating it like a weed. I will not pretend I didn’t. The first time I did that, the leaves looked great. The roots came off in my hand. The plant was done.

Write the name on the new pot **before** you dump the cup. I have mixed two varieties in a June rush. Two years later that is a true-to-type fight you started with dirty hands. Paint pen on black plastic is what Dad uses because it stays. A paper tag in a hose storm is how a name walks off.

Write the pot-up date on the pot too. Next year you will see which names you moved late and sulked. That is a better teacher than a rule you only read.

## Shade after. Do not hero-fertilize.

Water once to settle. Then leave it alone for a few days in **shade**. A pop that lived under a bench does not want a gravel pad on day one. Black plastic on gravel at 2 p.m. is a different climate. The shade-and-water draft is the July manual. This morning is the move.

No fertilizer the day of. Roots are broken even if you were careful. TAMU’s 2015 figs PDF says **do not fertilize at planting**. A pot-up is a kind of planting. The first flush comes from the wood. I listen.

Then fertigate when it stands up — new growth that is not the old pale leaves it already had. The feed draft in this set is that hose. Miracle-Gro tea in the hole the hour you break roots is how you burn a leak. I will say it with Dad’s page: feed the plant, not the stick, and not the hour you moved it.

If you broke the roots and you know it, keep it shadier longer. You are nursing a leak. A heavy pot and yellowing means you drowned the move. Lift the pot. Light means water. Heavy means wait.

Morning. Shade cloth ready. Water ready. Labels written *before* the dance. If those three are missing, wait a day. A late morning move is still better than a 3 p.m. move. I have done the 3 p.m. version. The plant told me about it for two weeks.

## What I will not do after June

I do not bare-root a fig “to check for nematodes” on a 98° afternoon unless I already suspect galls and I am ready to lose it. The nematode draft is a knock-out at pot-up or dormancy, not a curiosity in July.

I do not move an in-ground tree in July because a video said transplant anytime.

I do not pot up a tray at noon on black asphalt.

I do not hero-fertilize a sulk. More nitrogen will not talk insulted roots into a flush. NC State’s fig page already warned that extra nitrogen late makes succulent wood heading into winter. A shocked August #3 does not need a lawn program. It needs shade and even water.

Outdoor bed starts: the Outdoor page lets you wait for dormancy to dig. That is a legal way to miss June on purpose. Summer digs work. They also break more roots. Shade, water, do not fertilize the day you rip it out.

## A #3 in May versus a #3 in August

May: the plant wants to grow. You fertigate after it stands up. You get wood. In a season we were paying attention we watched **one to three feet** of spring push on a lot of pots. That is a conversation from the Fig Jam, not a guarantee for a plant you shocked in July. **[VERIFY]** on your own flush.

August: the plant wants to not die. You shade. You water. You get survival. Survival is not the same as a season.

I would rather have a slightly bound cup in August than a shocked #3. I would rather have a #3 in May than a bound cup in May. The date changes the right answer.

Missed the window: hold the plant. Do not make it worse. A bound cup in August gets shade and water and a promise for fall or March. A fall pot-up into a #3 can work if nights are kinder. **[VERIFY]** on your own late moves. Ours in high summer were the sulky ones.

Hold is not neglect. A knot in a fist of coir on a heat mat you forgot to turn off will cook. Take it off the mat when it has roots. The mat was for the stick. If the cup is a knot and it is cooking, a modest morning move still beats death. That is the only July exception I will own.

Spring is for moving. Summer is for watering what you already moved. If you only remember one date from the Intro, remember June. If you remember two dates, remember “not at 2 p.m. on gravel.” The plant will not thank you for your calendar. It will thank you for roots that stayed on. A sulk you caused in August is a sulk you will water until September. Cause fewer of those.
