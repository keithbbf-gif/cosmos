The [Introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) said pot into a 3-gallon before the plant is a knot, and do it **before June** because a late pot-up can shock them. I have watched that sentence come true. The leaves look insulted. The pot sits wet because you panicked. Then it sits dry because you got scared of rot. Then it is August.

This is not a new method. It is a calendar for the pot-up draft.

## Why June is the line here

South Arkansas heat. A plant that just lost roots cannot drink as fast as July evaporates a #3 on gravel. You also lose weeks of the push we fertigate for.

If the cutting rooted in March, you have no excuse to still have it in a cup in July “to be safe.” Safe was a real root system in April and a move in May.

If the cutting rooted in July, I would not force a #3 on a gravel pad. Shade, a modest pot, or wait for dormancy. The before-June rule is for plants that are **already growing in spring**. It is not a dare to transplant into a heat wave.

## What I move into

A trade gallon is fine from a cup. A **#3** is what the Intro named as the nursery pot after the cup is root-bound. Then a #5 if we keep it. Most of our library lives there.

Mix that drains. Promix HP if you do not want to think. Water to settle. Shade a few days. No fertilizer the day of. Then fertigate when it stands up.

Fragile pop roots. Support the mix. I already broke a plant by treating it like a weed. I will not pretend I didn’t.

## What I do not do in July

I do not bare-root a fig “to check for nematodes” on a 98° afternoon unless I already suspect galls and I am ready to lose it.

I do not move an in-ground tree in July because a video said transplant anytime.

I do not pot up a tray at noon on black asphalt.

Morning. Shade. Water ready. Labels written *before* the dance.

## Missed the window

Hold the plant. Do not make it worse. A bound cup in August gets shade and water and a promise for fall or March. A fall pot-up into a #3 can work if nights are kinder. [VERIFY] on your own late moves. Ours in high summer were the sulky ones.

Outdoor bed starts: the Outdoor page lets you wait for dormancy to dig. That is a legal way to miss June on purpose.

## A #3 in May vs a #3 in August

May: the plant wants to grow. You fertigate after it stands up. You get wood.

August: the plant wants to not die. You shade. You water. You get survival. Survival is not the same as a season.

I would rather have a slightly bound cup in August than a shocked #3. I would rather have a #3 in May than a bound cup in May. The date changes the right answer.

## Labels, again

Write the name on the new pot before you dump the cup. I have mixed two varieties in a June rush. Two years later that is a true-to-type fight you started with dirty hands.

## A tray that rooted late on purpose

Sometimes you stuck wood in May. Roots in June. The Intro’s June line is then a tension, not a law. I would pot a June-rooted plant into a modest pot, shade, and skip the gravel pad. I would not skip the move if the cup is a knot. A knot in July drinks and dries twice a day.

If I can wait until a cooler week, I wait. If the cup is cooking, I move it in the morning and I live with a sulk that is smaller than death.

## Fertigate after, not during

I will say it with the feed draft: no Miracle-Gro tea in the hole the hour you break roots. Water. Shade. New growth. Then feed.

Spring is for moving. Summer is for watering what you already moved. If you only remember one date from the Intro, remember June. If you remember two dates, remember “not at 2 p.m. on gravel.” The plant will not thank you for your calendar. It will thank you for roots that stayed on. A sulk you caused in August is a sulk you will water until September. Cause fewer of those.

## A #5 is not a spring requirement

Cup to gallon to #3 is enough for year one. A jump to a barrel in May is a lot of wet mix around a small root. I would rather step up than drown a baby in a tub it cannot drink.

Write the pot-up date on the pot. Next year you will see which names you moved late and sulked. That is a better teacher than a rule you only read.

## Heat the day you move

Morning. Shade cloth ready. Water ready. If those three are missing, wait a day. A late morning move is still better than a 3 p.m. move. I have done the 3 p.m. version. The plant told me about it for two weeks.

If the only pot you have is a barrel, do not fill it with a cup of roots and a lake of mix. Use a smaller can inside the story you wanted. The barrel can wait a year.
