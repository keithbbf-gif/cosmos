Jack’s tasting list had **Navid’s Unk Dark Greek** on it. The “Unk” stayed. That is more honest than a lot of tags I have seen on a marketplace.

UAEX said name confusion is normal. Fifty names in stores, hundreds on lists, the same plant wearing two hats. The Intro said wait a year or three to prove type.

If you do not know, write **unknown**. Then add the source and the year. “Unknown, neighbor, 2024, small brown, tight eye” is a working identity. “Black Madeira?” is a wish.

## How unknowns get famous

Someone ate a good fig. Someone mailed a stick. Someone guessed a celebrity. Someone sold the guess. Now there are three plants with one famous name and three fruits.

We have names in the collection that came as unknowns and stayed that way until they earned a local tag. We have names I like — Navid’s Unk, BGM Unk — that never needed a fake baptism.

If your unknown is good, keep it. Share it as unknown-plus-notes. Do not promote it to a European estate name because the pulp is red.

## How famous names get unknown

A swap table. A lost label. A sucker from below a graft. A box-store cart. The plant is still a fig. The name is now a rumor.

Relabel. The true-to-type draft is the patience. This draft is the vocabulary.

I would rather eat an unknown berry fig that finishes here than argue about a sticker.

## What I write on the pot

Received name, in quotes if I doubt it. Source. Date. After fruit: eye, size, family, season. If it matches a known clone I trust, I may add “looks like X, not confirmed.” I do not skip to confirmed because I want to sound sure on a forum.

Photos on the tree and on a plate. Year stamped. Memory cheats.

## Trading

If I trade an unknown, I say unknown. If I trade “as received,” I say that. If I have not fruited it, I say that. The people who get mad are the people who wanted a story.

If I receive a famous name and the fruit is a turkey, I do not pass the famous name along. I pass the fruit description.

## Local names

“Grandpa’s fig” is a name. It is a better name than a guessed Italian estate. Write who, where, what it does in rain. That is data. A forum can help you match it later. A forum cannot do that if you already renamed it Black Madeira.

## Kids’ names

Jack Lilly is on his tasting list — a real fruit, a real eye, a real note. A name you put on a plant you fruited is different from a name you put on a hope. I will not police what a family calls a tree. I will police what we ship to a stranger.

## What I will not do on a forum

I will not ID a variety from one leaf photo. I will not upgrade an unknown to a celebrity because two people said “looks like.” I will ask for fruit on the tree, fruit cut, eye, season, and where it grew. Even then I would say “looks like,” not “is.”

If someone wants a name for a sale, and they do not have fruit yet, the honest listing is the source plus unknown. The dishonest listing is a word they liked.

## Keeping two names on one pot

“As received: X. Fruiting as: unknown, tight, small, berry.” That pot is useful. “X” alone after the fruit disagreed is a lie.

When I am sure enough to drop the received name, I still keep it in the notebook. Clone piles are real. You may want that breadcrumb later.

Honest names are how a community stays useful. Unknown is not a stain. A fake name is. If you are unsure, the word unknown is the most professional word you have. Use it. Then eat the fig. The plate will write the next line. A sticker will not.

## Selling an unknown

Price it as unknown. Describe the fruit. Do not hide the word in the fifth line. The person who wants a name will walk. The person who wants a fig will stay. I would rather sell to the second person.

A local unknown that holds in rain and tastes like berry is a better ship than a famous name you cannot stand behind. Ship the notes. Ship a photo. Do not ship a guess. The person who gets it can rename it after they eat it. That is their plate, not yours.

## Kids naming trees

A family name on a fruited tree is honest. A family name on an unfruited cup is still unknown plus a nickname. Write both. The nickname can stay. The unknown stays until the plate.

If two unknowns fruit alike, you may have one clone. Keep both notes until you are sure. Combining names too early is how a real difference disappears. Splitting names too early is how a forum starts a fight.

An unknown that splits in rain gets that note on the pot. Future-you will thank present-you more than a guessed Italian name would.

Write unknown in the same size letters as a famous name. Hiding it in a corner of the tag is how it becomes a lie at a swap table.
A famous sticker on a mutt costs two summers. An honest unknown costs one word.
