Jack’s tasting list on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) had **Navid’s Unk Dark Greek** on it. The “Unk” stayed. That is more honest than a lot of tags I have seen on a marketplace.

University of Arkansas Extension said name confusion is normal. Fifty names in stores, hundreds on lists, the same plant wearing two hats. [An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) said wait a year or three to prove type.

If you do not know, write **unknown**. Then add the source and the year. “Unknown, neighbor, 2024, small brown, tight eye” is a working identity. “Black Madeira?” is a wish.

## Unknown is a working name

Unknown is not a stain. A fake name is.

I would rather eat an unknown berry fig that finishes here than argue about a sticker. Flavor, eye, season, rain behavior — those are data. A celebrity estate name on a mutt is a two-summer tax. The true-to-type draft in this set is the patience. This draft is the vocabulary.

UAEX already told you Brown Turkey is a crowd and Celeste wears more than one hat. Common figs only in Arkansas, and we still cannot keep the common names straight. Adding a guessed Italian name to that pile does not make you a collector. It makes you a rumor mill with pots.

Write unknown in the same size letters as a famous name. Hiding it in a corner of the tag is how it becomes a lie at a swap table. Paint pen. Paper dies in the shuffle. The label draft is the hardware. This page is what the hardware is allowed to say.

## Navid’s Unk — the case we actually have

This is the full case I will stand on, because it is on the record. I will not invent a new named unknown to make a prettier story.

Jack ranked fruit we ate. **Syrian Dark #2** — extremely tight, small, berry, productive. **Jack Lilly** — tight eye, small. **Nuestra Señora del Carmen** — large eye, splits if there is much rain. **Red Sicilian** — a producer. And **Navid’s Unk Dark Greek** — a little tighter and smaller than Red Sicilian. That last one kept Unk in the working name. He did not baptize it into a European estate because the pulp was dark. He wrote what it did on the plate, next to the other names, with Unk still showing.

That is unknown → local working name. The local name is still honest: source nickname plus Unk plus what the fruit did in South Arkansas. It is not a confirmation. It is not a listing upgrade. [VERIFY] any clone you received under that string on *your* plate. Your stick may differ. Ours was fruit we ate, with the uncertainty left in the name.

Jack Lilly is the other honest move. A family name on a tree they fruited. Real eye, real note. That is not a guess shipped to a stranger. I will not police what a family calls a tree they have eaten. I will police what we ship.

I will not upgrade Navid’s Unk to a celebrity because two people on a forum said “looks like.” I will not ID a variety from one leaf photo. I will ask for fruit on the tree, fruit cut, eye, season, and where it grew. Even then I would say “looks like,” not “is.”

If your unknown is good, keep it. Share it as unknown-plus-notes. Do not promote it to a European estate name because the pulp is red.

## How famous names go unknown

A swap table. A lost label. A sucker from below a graft. A box-store cart. The plant is still a fig. The name is now a rumor.

Relabel. Received name, in quotes if I doubt it. Source. Date. After fruit: eye, size, family, season. If it matches a known clone I trust, I may add “looks like X, not confirmed.” I do not skip to confirmed because I want to sound sure on a forum.

Photos on the tree and on a plate. Year stamped. Memory cheats.

“As received: X. Fruiting as: unknown, tight, small, berry.” That pot is useful. “X” alone after the fruit disagreed is a lie. When I am sure enough to drop the received name, I still keep it in the notebook. Clone piles are real. You may want that breadcrumb later.

If I receive a famous name and the fruit is a turkey, I do not pass the famous name along. I pass the fruit description. I am still not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste as dessert so far. That does not give me permission to leave a celebrity sticker on a turkey-type plate so the pot sells.

A freeze year is when graft suckers rewrite the collection. Suckers below a union are the old name. Paint the trunks. The freeze-recovery draft already said that. An unknown that starts as a graft mix is two plants. Write both.

## Paint pen, source, year

I write on the pot the day the plant is mine. Not later. Later is how a tray becomes a mystery.

Received name. Source. Year. Then the word **unknown** if the fruit has not spoken, or if the fruit disagreed. A **#3** before June still needs a name that is true. Pot-up is not a baptism.

If the pen is dry, stop potting. A dry pen is how a row of “brown fig” is born. I keep a spare in the shop for that reason.

Kids and help are not the enemy. A system with two writers and no scribe is. Say it, write it, then stick. A family nickname on an unfruited cup is still unknown plus a nickname. Write both. The nickname can stay. The unknown stays until the plate.

If two unknowns fruit alike, you may have one clone. Keep both notes until you are sure. Combining names too early is how a real difference disappears. Splitting names too early is how a forum starts a fight.

An unknown that splits in rain gets that note on the pot. Future-you will thank present-you more than a guessed Italian name would. NSDC taught us the large-eye tax on the record. An unnamed neighbor fig can teach you the same tax if you write “splits in rain” instead of guessing a celebrity that also splits.

## Do not ship a guess

If I trade an unknown, I say unknown. If I trade “as received,” I say that. If I have not fruited it, I say that. The people who get mad are the people who wanted a story.

Price it as unknown. Describe the fruit. Do not hide the word in the fifth line. The person who wants a name will walk. The person who wants a fig will stay. I would rather sell to the second person.

A local unknown that holds in rain and tastes like berry is a better ship than a famous name you cannot stand behind. Ship the notes. Ship a photo. Do not ship a guess. The person who gets it can rename it after they eat it. That is their plate, not yours.

If someone wants a name for a sale, and they do not have fruit yet, the honest listing is the source plus unknown. The dishonest listing is a word they liked. Marketplace reviews close before anyone has had a fig. The Intro already yelled that. I will not soften it.

Wood you send should still read like a cutting: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, lignified if you can. A guess does not get more true because the stick rooted on a heat mat at **75–78°F**. Roots are not type. Fruit is type.

“Grandpa’s fig” is a name. It is a better name than a guessed Italian estate. Write who, where, what it does in rain. That is data. A forum can help you match it later. A forum cannot do that if you already renamed it Black Madeira.

A famous sticker on a mutt costs two summers. An honest unknown costs one word. Use it. Then eat the fig. The plate will write the next line. A sticker will not.
