UAEX: plant on the **east** side of a house, or with rock or brick at their back, to block wind and throw heat in winter. You still need **6–8 hours** of sun. TAMU likes south or east of a home or barn for the same winter logic, and so morning sun dries fruit and leaves after a night rain.

That is not landscaping talk. That is a cheaper hoop house made of a wall you already paid for.

## What the east wall gives you

Morning light. Fruit that dries. A little less of the west hammer in July. A pocket that can be kinder on a still, cold night.

What it does not give you: a license for a late fig that needs a California fall. GDD is still GDD. A wall is a nudge.

## What a west wall gives you

Winter heat, sometimes. Summer death, often. Reflected sun on a pot is the gravel-pad problem standing up. I would plant a fig on the west side if that is the only side, and I would not pretend it is free ripening units. Shade cloth in July. Water.

A south wall in a northern short-season yard is a gift. A south wall in south Arkansas can be a fryer. Context. The shade draft is the summer half of this sentence.

## Wind

A fig on a ridge with a north wind will burn wood you wanted for breba. A wall or a barn that knocks that wind down is winter protection you do not have to wrap every year. Young trunks still want a cage some years.

Do not plant in the shade of the same barn. 6–8 hours. A warm alley with three hours of sun is a leaf museum.

## Pots and walls

A #3 against an east wall in April is a smart pad. The same pot in January still wants the shop if a hard freeze is coming. Walls do not replace the shuffle for thin plastic.

I have used buildings because we have them. I would not pour a slab to “make a microclimate” and then cook pots on it.

## How I would site one in-ground tree

East or southeast of the house if I can. Morning sun on the fruit. Afternoon relief from a tree line that does not steal the morning. Soil that drains. Hose that reaches. Room for a 15–25 foot plant if winter never kills it — UAEX’s mature size is a well-fed, unfrozen tree. Most of ours never get that speech.

Walk the spot in July at 2 p.m. and in January at dawn. Both walks count. If you only site a fig from a spring Saturday, you sited a hope.

## Rain and the wall

Morning sun after a night rain is why TAMU likes east/south of a building. Fruit dries. Leaves dry. Rust and souring like wet that stays. A wall that shades the morning is the wrong wall even if it is warm at night.

A roof drip line that dumps on the trunk is also the wrong spot. Move the hole over. Plant high if the eave is a waterfall.

## Neighbors and lawn

A fig against a wall will want to be 15–25 feet if winter never kills it. UAEX’s size. Most of ours never get that speech. Still: do not plant a foot from the siding and then act surprised when the trunk rubs paint. Leave a walk. Leave air.

The wall is a tool. Use the east one if you have it. Still water. Still pick. Still choose a variety that can finish. A brick is not a miracle. It is a better neighbor than a west tin shed. Walk the spot at 2 p.m. in July before you dig. If you cannot stand there, the pot you set there will not enjoy it either.

## North walls

A north wall in the South is still a shade problem for fruit. Warmth without hours of sun is a green bush. I would put a shade-tolerant ornamental there, not a fig I wanted to eat.

A north wall in a hot courtyard that still gets sky is a maybe. Walk it in July. If the fig would be a houseplant, pick another wall.

A wall also throws heat at a pot in July. An east wall in the morning is kind. The same pot shoved against west brick at 3 p.m. is the gravel problem standing up. Slide it out a foot if you can. Air behind a pot is part of the wall trick.

## Snow and brick

A brick wall that soaks sun in January can save a young trunk on a still night. It can also wake buds early and then a late freeze writes the next draft. Walls are a nudge in both directions. Watch the buds in a warm February. The wagon still matters.

Plant a foot or more off the siding so you can walk, so air moves, so the trunk does not rub paint. A fig glued to a wall is a maintenance problem you built. The wall is a neighbor, not a pot.

If the only wall is west, plant anyway if you want a fig, and cloth the afternoon. Do not wait years for a perfect east wall you do not have.

Morning sun on wet fruit is the wall's real summer job. Winter heat is the bonus. Do not flip those and fry a pot on west brick.
