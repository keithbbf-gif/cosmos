If you live in steam, a fig released by LSU is not magic. It is a clue. Those plants were selected where summers are wet and rust is not a rumor.

LSU AgCenter writes about Celeste as the old reliable — small to medium, resists split and sour relative to the open-eye crowd — and then their own names: **LSU Purple** (1991), **LSU Gold**, **O’Rourke**, **Champagne**, **Tiger**. Purple shows up in grower talk as a closed eye and a plant that will fruit younger than some. AgCenter has said Purple can throw a small crop a year or two after planting, and a heavy July main crop with a later tail. “Into December” is a Gulf sentence. I would not copy it onto an Ozark ridge. [VERIFY] the tail in your yard.

## What I treat as solid

They are **common figs**. No wasp. That is the type we grow.

They were watched in Louisiana humidity. That is closer to our August than a California dessert list.

Purple has a reputation for foliage disease resistance in LSU’s own writeups. Gold is a yellow fig with light red to pink pulp in their description. I will not invent a flavor score. I will say: if you want a gold fig in humidity, an LSU name is a better first bet than a thin-skinned Mediterranean postcard.

TAMU’s older closed-eye list was Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma. LSU Purple often gets tossed into humid-South closed-eye talk. Look at **your** ostiole. Photo it. Do not cite a forum memory as the fruit on your plate.

## What I will not do

I will not tell you to rip out a good berry type because it does not say LSU on the tag.

I will not tell you every LSU fig is tight-eyed and rust-proof. Releases differ. Years differ. A wet August still cracks fruit.

I will not pretend a Baton Rouge finish date is a Little Rock finish date. GDD and nights. We have a draft for that.

## How I would trial one

Buy from a source that has fruited it, or a nursery that actually holds the release. Box-store “LSU Purple” can be a shrug. Wait for fruit. Year one is a rumor. Year two is a conversation.

Plant it where you would plant any humid-South fig: morning sun, afternoon relief, water even, pick daily in the flush. Tight-eye habits still apply.

If it sours, you now know that clone in your week. Relabel your expectations. If it holds and you like the taste, it can go in the ground.

## Beside the tool trees

Celeste and a Turkey type keep the freezer honest. An LSU name is the next ring: still a climate-aware fig, a little more interest on the plate if the fruit is good.

The celebrity berry list is the third ring. Those we trial in pots. We do not apologize for that order.

## Where I would not start

I would not start with a rare LSU numbered seedling I cannot trace. I would start with a named release a Southern nursery actually holds. Purple is the one people can find. Gold if you want a light fig. The others when the source is real.

A Facebook “LSU Tiger” from a stranger is the true-to-type problem with a prettier acronym.

## Rust and Purple

LSU’s own talk about foliage disease on Purple is a reason to trial it where rust strips trees. It is not a promise your August stays green. Clean up leaves. Give it morning sun. The rust draft is the rest.

If Purple rusts on you anyway, you have a year, not a scandal. Write it down.

## Finish dates

July main crop in south Louisiana is not a promise for a cool spring in the Ozarks. GDD. Late freeze. Water. I would plant Purple as a humid-South common fig, not as a calendar you can set a watch to.

The “later tail” is extra fruit if the fall stays kind. I would not count on December figs in central Arkansas. [VERIFY] on your tree, your year.

LSU did the humid homework. Use it. Then taste. Then decide. A release paper is not a ripe fig. If the fruit is good and the eye holds in a wet week, it can leave the pot and take a hole. That is the only promotion that matters.

## Beside Alma and the Texas closed-eye list

TAMU’s old souring table liked Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma. LSU names sit in the same humid conversation. I would trial one LSU release next to one tool tree and write the eye and the week it soured, if it soured. That notebook is worth more than a brochure paragraph.

If I cannot find a true release, I do not buy a shrug with LSU in the title. The letters are not the fruit.

I would rather have one fruited LSU Purple from a known yard than three unfruited 'LSU' tags from a swap. The release is a clue. The plate is the proof. Wait for the plate. If Gold is what you want, say gold on the plate, not gold on a hope.

## How I would plant one this spring

Morning sun. Afternoon relief. Mix or hole that drains. Label the pot with the source, not just LSU. Pick daily in the flush. Write the eye when the first fruit opens. If it holds in a wet week and I like the taste, it can take a hole next spring. If it sours, it stays a pot or it becomes rootstock. That is the same rule as any other name. The LSU letters do not buy a skip.
