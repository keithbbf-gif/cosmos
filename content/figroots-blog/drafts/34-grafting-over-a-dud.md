I said on the Fig Jam interview we have not cut some trees we are not fans of yet. Long trial. If they stay boring, we graft over. They live on as rootstock.

That is the polite version of: I will not throw a drained hole and a winter-surviving root system in a pile because the fruit was sugar and nothing else.

## What is worth grafting

A healthy common fig. Celeste, a Turkey type, a vigorous unknown that fruited dull. Roots that are not a nematode necklace. A hole that is not a bathtub.

What is not worth grafting: a corpse, a knotted wreck you plan to blame later, a pot the size of a coffee mug.

The scion should be a name you have a reason to trust. Grafting a marketplace mystery onto a good tree is how you multiply a lie.

## Timing I will stand on

Dormant or waking wood, spring, when the bark slips if you are doing a bark graft. I am not writing a full grafting class here. We have a Grafting folder of stills and we use a planer blade Jack called Ol’ Rusty as a knife. Clean cuts. Match cambium. Wrap it so it cannot dry.

Summer green grafts exist. They also dry out in our heat if you get cute. [VERIFY] your method on a tool tree before you graft the only LSU Purple you own.

## After it takes

Relabel the same day you believe it. Paint pen. The old name is retired. If the graft fails, the old top may still be there — then the old name is still true. Do not get poetic.

Suckers from below the graft are the old variety. They will try to take the tree back. I have watched a “new” tree fruit the old fig because somebody let a sucker run. Cut them.

Fruit is still a year-plus on a new scion sometimes. Patience. The true-to-type clock resets for the top.

## Why not just plant a new hole

You can. If the ground is good, plant the new name and leave the dud as a donor for cuttings or as a kid’s tree.

If space is tight, or the dud is the only in-ground trunk that lived, graft. We have more names than holes. That is the collection talking.

## I will not claim

A 100% take rate. A week-by-week photo diary I did not keep. A “best graft for beginners” list that sounds like a thumbnail.

## Tool trees make good understock

A Turkey type that always comes back is a engine. A Celeste that lived through winter is a engine. I would rather graft a berry name onto that than plant a $40 pot into a hole I have not watched for two years.

Do not graft onto a knotted, drowning wreck and then blame the scion.

## Failure

The wrap dries. The cambium misses. The summer cooks it. The understock still lives. Try again next spring. I will not give you a percentage. I will give you permission to fail on a tool tree before you fail on the only trunk you have.

## When I would not graft

The tree is sick. The hole holds water. The roots are beads. The only scion I have is another unknown. Then I would plant a new pot of a name I trust, or I would eat the dull fig and stop talking.

Grafting is not a way to skip true-to-type. The new top still takes a year or more to prove. You just skipped digging a second hole.

## Knife

Clean enough not to smear dirt into the cut. Sharp enough not to crush. Jack’s Ol’ Rusty is a planer blade. I will not tell you a brand you must buy. I will tell you a dull kitchen knife makes a chew, not a graft.

I will claim this: keep the root that works. Change the top you do not want to eat. Tell the truth on the label. That is improvement without a funeral for the hole. The funeral is for the name, not the plant. People mix those up and then they have neither. A live turkey-type trunk is not a failure. It is a platform. Use it when the fruit has had a fair trial — not after one ugly week.

## Scion wood

Dormant, lignified, a name you trust. The same read as a cutting. A green summer scion can work and can dry. I would practice on a tool tree in spring before I use the only stick of a name I waited a year to get.

Tie a ribbon below the graft so winter-you remembers where the old tree starts. Suckers below the ribbon are the old name. They will look healthy. They will steal the new top if you get sentimental.

## One scion, one trunk

Do not put five names on one tool tree unless you like a puzzle. A puzzle that suckers is a mess. One good scion, one label, one year of wait. That is enough story for a hole.

Wait until you have disliked the fruit for a fair season, not a fair weekend. A long trial is how we still have trees I am not a fan of. Grafting is the end of the trial, not the start of a mood.

If the graft takes and the fruit is good, the hole was the right investment. If it fails, you still have a tree that lives. That is why we graft instead of dig.
