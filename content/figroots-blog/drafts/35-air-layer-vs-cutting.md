We already have an [air-layer how-to](https://figroots.com/air-layering/) and a [fall timing page for zones 6–9](https://figroots.com/2025/09/18/setting-fall-airlayers-in-zones-6-9/). I am not rewriting the foil wrap. I am telling you when I would peel a ring instead of filling a bag with sticks.

## Cutting

Cheap. Many. 6–8 inches, three nodes. Fridge if dormant. Failures: rot, callus-only, dry. A tray of names. A year to a small tree.

Best when the mother has wood to spare and you want volume or you want to mail a stick.

## Air layer

The branch stays on the tree. Water and food still come up. You scrape bark and cambium, pack **coco or long-fiber sphagnum** at field capacity, foil, check weekly. Roots form in the wrap. You cut it off and pot the ball **without shaking it**.

You get a bigger plant faster if it works. You risk a branch. You risk mold in a wet wrap late in the year. The fall-timing page said in-ground trees need an earlier start than pots you can walk into a greenhouse. Pots can have a longer root window if the space is warm. A wet wrap in a cold swing can go nasty.

Dad likes coco for layers. Sphagnum is second because of cost and because those roots can be brittle — the timing page already said that. I would not pack a layer with a lot of DE or perlite. It dries.

## When I pick which

**I want twenty plants:** cuttings.

**I want this one branch to be a tree this year:** layer.

**The wood is green and I am in July:** layer can still work. A green cutting is a now drill. I would not fridge either.

**I am late in the fall on an in-ground tree:** read the zone table we already posted. Maybe do not start. A half-rooted wrap in a freeze is a sad balloon.

**The tree is the only trunk I have:** I would not layer the only leader. I would take a side stick.

## Potting the layer

Same as a pop, only the ball is bigger and people get rough. Do not strip the sphagnum off to “see roots.” Shade. Water. No hero fertilizer.

If it is late, the timing page said keep it attached or overwinter protected rather than a panic plant-out. I agree.

## Honest overlap

Both clone the tree. Both fail wet. Both fail dry. The layer has a mother for backup until you cut. The cutting is on its own from minute one.

I wash cuttings. I still check a layer for mold. Different rituals. Same plant if the name was true.

## Fall, again

The zone table on the live site is the clock. In-ground: start earlier, take off sooner, less root time. Pots you can walk inside: more time, more mold risk if you leave a wet wrap in a swinging temperature.

I would rather take dormant cuttings in December than start a late layer on an in-ground tree in October in a zone that is already cooling. The layer page already said that. I am repeating it because people start layers when they get inspired, not when the wood has time.

## Size of the branch

The live how-to said do not pick a branch bigger than the root ball you will make. A four-foot limb with a tennis-ball wrap is a sail with a postage stamp of roots. I would layer something I can pot in a #3 or #5 without a crane.

Sticks for a tray. A wrap for a plant you can already picture in a #5. Read the two live pages for the hands. Use this page to pick the job. If you want both, layer one branch and cut six sticks. That is a Sunday. That is not a personality.

## Check the wrap

Weekly. If it dried, spray. If it soured, you waited too long or you packed it wet. The live how-to already said foil and a moisture check. I will add: a wrap you never open is a surprise, and surprises on a mother tree are expensive.

When you cut it off, have the pot ready. A layer on a tailgate in July while you look for a bag of mix is how you cook a root ball you spent six weeks making.

I would not layer the only trunk on a one-tree yard. I would take a side stick and live with a smaller plant. A dead leader is a harder story than a failed cup. The mother has to stay a tree.

## Sphagnum vs coco, again

The live page already ranked them. Coco for cost and feel. Sphagnum holds and can make brittle roots. I pack what I have that holds together wet. I do not pack a dry peat dust and hope. Field capacity is the same word as a pop. If it drips, you packed a swamp on a branch.

If you only remember one difference, remember this: a cutting is on its own from the first minute. A layer still drinks from the mother until you cut. That drink is why layers feel safer. It is also why a bad wrap can still cost a branch.

I would layer a spare branch and cut sticks from the rest. One Sunday, two methods, one mother still standing. That is plenty.
