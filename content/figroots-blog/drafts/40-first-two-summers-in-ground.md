The Intro page said plant in-ground in zone 8 or higher in spring, water in the heat, do not count on fruit, wait for the tree to become a tree about year three. UAEX’s planting window is late winter through spring so it can settle before the next winter.

People hear “figs are easy” and skip the hose. Then the fruit drops, the leaves crisp, and the variety is “no good here.”

The hole is a pot you cannot lift. Treat it that way for two summers.

## What I plant

A plant with a real root system, not a two-week pop. A variety I have eaten or a tool tree I am willing to own. A hole that **drains**. High if the clay holds a lake. East wall if I have one. 6–8 hours of sun.

TAMU: water in, no fertilizer at planting, you can cut a dormant plant back some to match lost roots. I would not strip a leafy July plant to plant it. Wait.

## Water

Shallow roots. New ones. They do not go find the creek. A dry week in July will dump fruit and stall wood.

Mulch. Pulled off the trunk. The clay draft. Even water, not a Friday flood after a drought. That Friday flood splits whatever hung on.

A soaker or a slow hose beat a five-second sprinkle that wets dust. I would rather water deep and less often than mist daily, once the plant is in — but in the first August I will not let the hole go bone dry to prove a point.

Pots you left on the pad still need water too. I have killed a leftover #3 the week I celebrated an in-ground planting.

## What I do not expect

A pantry year. Buttons that drop. A late variety that finishes. Breba. Those come later, or not.

I expect wood. A foot or more of honest shoot if we watered and the winter did not steal it. Fertigate lightly once it is growing, not a lawn dump.

## Winter after year one

Protect a young trunk. Cage, leaves, a wrap you will actually install. North counties especially. Year-two tree is still a teenager.

If it dies to the ground and the roots live, you are in the freeze-recovery draft. You have not failed the species. You have paid the 8a tax.

## Year three

Now we talk about a crop like a grown-up. Now we talk about grafting over if the fruit is dull. Now we talk about whether the hole earned a better name.

Until then, the job is water, mulch, and not mowing the trunk off. Easy is the third summer. The first two are how you get there.

## Mowers

The trunk is not a weed. A string trimmer will girdle a teenager in one Saturday. Mulch ring. A guard if you have to. I have seen more “mystery decline” that was a whip than a disease.

## Lawn fertilizer

Do not treat the fig as part of the fescue program. A weed-and-feed on shallow fig roots is a way to write a bad year. Keep the lawn fight away from the ring.

## The leftover pot

The #3 you slipped off the plant still has mix and maybe a piece of root. Do not leave it on the pad to die “empty.” That pot is still a plant if you tore a start. And the hole still needs the hose you just used to celebrate.

Plant it. Water it like it cannot walk. Pick the few figs if they come. Do not write the variety’s obituary in August of year one. The hole is still learning the county. So is the plant. Year three is when I listen to the fruit. Year one I listen to the hose.

## Stakes and wind

A teenager in a new hole will rock. A rock that never stops is a broken new root. Stake it loose enough not to girdle, tight enough not to saw the hole empty. Remove the stake when the trunk stands. A forgotten tie is a hidden girdle in year three.

Water is still the main job. The stake is just so the water is not wasted on a plant that loosened itself.

Do not plant a first-year fig in a hole you will not walk to with a hose. If the hose does not reach, the tree is a wish. Move the hole or move the hose. I have watched a 'set it and forget it' planting crisp in August ten yards from a faucet. The faucet was not the mystery.

## Fruit in year one

If it comes, eat it. Do not assume the tree is established because it gave you six figs. Keep watering. Keep the mower off the trunk. Year one fruit is a bonus, not a graduation. Graduation is a live trunk in March of year three and a hose you still use in August of year two.

If you plant in spring and then leave for two weeks of heat, you planted a wish. A neighbor with a hose is part of the method. So is a timer. So is not planting the week you leave.

A tree that lives through two Augusts in a hole you watered is a tree. A tree you forgot in August is a stick. The difference is the hose.
