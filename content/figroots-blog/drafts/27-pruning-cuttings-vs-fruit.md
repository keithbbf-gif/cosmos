Jack’s job on a prune day is cuttings: **6–8 inches, three nodes**, lignified if we can get it. My job is to remember the tree has to eat next year.

Those jobs fight.

Breba lives on last year’s wood. Main crop lives on this year’s shoot. Celeste, UAEX said, will punish a hard late-winter chop. A Turkey type will fruit on the resprout. If you treat every tree like a Turkey, you will starve a Celeste and then call it a bad variety.

## Ask what this tree is for this year

**I need wood.** Take what you need from the ends. Do not hat-rack a dessert variety you wanted fruit from. Take extra from the tool trees and the ones you will graft over.

**I need fruit.** Tip if you must. Thin a thicket. Leave last year’s shoots if you are hunting breba (rare here, still real in a pot you protected). Leave enough buds for a main-crop factory.

**I need the plant small enough to roll.** You will prune. You will lose crop. Own it. That is the pot life.

Do not “clean it up” because January is boring. Dead wood at budbreak is a better mark than a nervous saw in December.

## Cuttings from what you already took

Polarity. Three nodes. Wash. Stick or fridge. That is Jack’s drafts. The tree does not care that the stick became a pop. The tree cares that the shoot is gone.

A 14-inch cane can be two cuttings. That is two figs you will not eat on that branch. Fine, if the plant is a donor. Not fine if it was your only early tree.

Label the bag with the tree’s true name, not “the brown one.” Prune day is how mislabels are born.

## After a freeze

The winter draft and the breba draft already said it. Let it show live wood. Thin suckers to a handful of trunks over a couple of weeks. You are building next year’s main crop, not filling a shipping box.

If you need cuttings from a frozen-back tree, take them from wood that is actually alive. Brown dry sticks are firewood. Scratch.

## Summer cutting

Green wood is a now problem. You also remove leaf area in the heat. I would rather take dormant wood. If I take summer wood, I treat it like produce and I do not strip a tree that is trying to ripen fruit.

Air layers are a different clock. We already posted fall timing by zone. An air layer is a committed branch, not a handful of sticks. Read that page before you peel a ring on your only trunk.

## The sentence I want on the shed wall

What is this tree supposed to do in July? If the answer is “feed us,” put the loppers down until you have a reason. If the answer is “fill a tray,” take the wood and do not cry in June.

## Tool trees vs dessert trees

I take more wood off a Turkey type I will not cry over. I take less off a berry name I have not fruited yet. That is not favoritism. That is knowing which plant is a donor.

A donor can look ugly and still be useful. A dessert tree that looks “tidy” in February can look empty in July. UAEX already told you about Celeste and a hard chop.

## How many cuttings is enough

A tray you will actually stick. Not a trash bag you will forget in the shop until it molds. The storage draft is for extras, not for a personality.

If you only need four pops, take four good sticks plus two. Leave the rest on the tree. The tree is allowed to be a tree.

## Summer “cleanup”

I do not like a heavy summer prune on a fruiting fig in our heat. You remove leaf that is pulling the fruit. You also invite sunburn on bark that was shaded. Tip a wild whip if it is in the path. Do not sculpture in July.

Both answers are honest. Mixing them on the same plant in the same week is how people get mad at figs. Write the job on a card if you have to: “wood” or “fruit.” Hand Jack the card before the loppers come out. I have skipped that step and then stared at a pile of sticks that used to be a crop.

## Dead wood is a different job

Winter-killed twigs can come off when you can tell. That is sanitation. TAMU said dead wood is a seat for other fungi. That is not the same as taking last year’s fruiting wood for a tray.

I would rather leave a live twig I do not need than cut a live twig I needed. The tray can be smaller.

If Jack needs a tray and I need fruit, we take wood from the donor trees first. The dessert names keep their shoots. That is the only peace treaty that works on a prune day.

## Count the sticks before you cut

If the tray holds twenty cups, take twenty-four sticks, not a grocery bag. Extra wood molds in a shop you forgot. The tree keeps the rest. A smaller tray that gets stuck is more plants than a bag that becomes a Damaged Cuttings photo.

A prune day that starts with a card — wood or fruit — ends with fewer regrets. I have skipped the card. I have regretted the pile.
