A paper tag in a hose storm is how Red Sicilian becomes the green pot by the door, and you will not know until the first plate two summers later.

True-to-type takes a year or three. A missing label takes one afternoon. I like black nursery pots because a **paint pen** (Posca is what I have used) stays on plastic the hose actually hits. I have watched paper tags pulp, fade, and migrate to the next #3. I have watched two cups swap on a pot-up table and a shrug that says we will remember. That shrug is a lie you will tell a buyer later if you are not careful.

[An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said it takes **a year or more, sometimes two or three**, to prove type. The notebook in the true-to-type draft is that clock. This page is the pen that keeps the clock pointed at a pot. Hundreds of pots. Winter shuffle. I will not keep identity in my head. I will lie to myself.

## Write the name three times

**On the bag the cutting arrived in.** Date. Source. Claimed name. If there is no name, write that. “Unlabeled box 3” is honest. “Probably Black Madeira” is how a guess becomes a trade.

**On the cup before the stick goes in.** Not after. After is how you mix two varieties on a crowded table. Tape on the rim beats a map of the tray. A map gets wet.

**On the #3 before the plant goes in.** The cup label dies in the trash if you are sloppy. The pot is the plant’s name for the next two summers. Write it before your hands are dirty.

If you are extra, a notebook: source, date, first fruit. The label is so the notebook still points at a pot. I do not upgrade a tag to “confirmed” until I have eaten fruit that matches. I will not put confirmed on a stick.

What I write: the name as received. A question mark if I do not trust it. A source code — “neighbor,” “so-and-so, 2024,” not “internet.” Future-you will want to know who to blame or thank.

## Paint pen, both sides

Pencil fades. Marker fades. Paint pen stays better on black plastic. I still have pots where I can read a name from two summers ago. I also have pots where I cannot. Those are the ones I do not trade as a named variety.

Even a good pen fades on the south face of a black pot in South Arkansas. Write it twice — **opposite sides** — or write it lower where the hose does not blast. I have lost a name to one summer of sun and one winter of shuffle. Twice is cheaper than a guess.

A second label in a notebook is a backup. A photo of the block with names visible is a backup. One paper tag is not a backup.

Heat-mat paper tags die. I have pulled a tray off a mat set to **75–78°F** on a thermostat — the same range we already use on the [Fig Pop](https://figroots.com/fig-pops/) page — and found a wetted scrap of paper glued to the cup with the name bled into a gray smear. The mix was fine. The stick was fine. The name was gone. Write on the cup. Tape on the rim. Do not tuck a scrap under the cup and call it labeled. That scrap is compost by week two. The heat does not “help the ink dry.” It cooks the paper in a wet microclimate.

I do not use only a map of the bench. Benches get rearranged. I do not throw a mystery plant in the “I’ll remember” corner. That corner becomes five plants. Winter shuffle changes neighbors. If your identity was “third from the left,” it died in November. Write on the pot. Paint-pen names face the aisle after a move, or you water the brown one twice and skip the rare one. The shuffle draft in this set is the wagon. This page is why the wagon is not allowed to erase a name.

## One scribe on pot-up day

This is the morning that actually keeps names.

Two people at the table. Cups, mix, a pile of rooted sticks, a dry paint pen in somebody’s pocket. If both of us write, we will invent two spellings and swap two cups. One person is the scribe. The other pots. Say the name out loud. Write it on the **#3**, both sides. Then the plant goes in. Not the other way around.

A silent pot-up is how Red Sicilian becomes “red?” I have done the silent version. I do not recommend it. The notebook cannot save a pot you never marked.

If the pen is dry, stop potting. A dry pen is how a tray becomes a mystery. I keep a spare in the shop for that reason. Pot up **before June** so you are not shocking a plant into midsummer — that timing is already on the Introduction page — but do not rush the names to make the calendar. A #3 with no name is an unknown with extra mix.

Jack labels too. Cuttings in a bag without a card are unknowns. I would rather take fewer sticks and keep the names than fill a tray with “brown fig.” Prune day is how mislabels are born if the bag has no card.

When two cups look alike, they will swap. I pot one name at a time when I can. When I cannot, I tape first, stick second.

## A photo of the block

Once a season I would photograph the pot rows so the names are readable. When a pen fades, the photo is a backup. It is not as good as a pen on two sides. It is better than a shrug in February.

Stand in the aisle. Get the paint-pen faces. Date the file. If you shuffle for winter, take another photo after the pots change neighbors. “Third from the left” is not in the picture. The name on the plastic is. A faded south face with a still-legible north face is why I write both sides in the first place. The photo catches the day both are still readable. February is too late to start that habit.

I have used paint pen, tape, and a notebook. The combination is what survives. One method alone will fail on a bad week. Two methods is how a name lives until fruit.

## Faded name, unknown fruit

When labels conflict, the pot says one name and the fruit says another. The fruit wins. Relabel. Do not ship the old name. That is how the mess spreads.

Two pots, one name, two fruits: you have a mix-up or a clone pile. Keep both notes. Do not pick the famous photo and throw the other away until you are sure. The unknown-figs draft in this set is the vocabulary. Use the word **unknown** when it is true.

A faded name is an unknown. Treat it that way on a trade. Do not guess it back to a celebrity because the pot is near a famous row. A famous name you cannot prove is an unknown with better marketing.

After fruit, add a year and a note on the pot or in the book: “2025, tight, berry.” The received name can stay. The fruit note is what you will trust when you take wood next winter. If the plate does not match the tag, the fruit wins. Relabel the pot the same day, both sides, while the taste is still in your mouth. Waiting until “later” is how the old name ships.

Kids and help are not the enemy. A system with two writers and no scribe is. Say it, write it, then stick. The variety you wanted is the one you can still point at in two years. A faded name is an unknown. The pen is cheaper than a wrong row in the ground.
