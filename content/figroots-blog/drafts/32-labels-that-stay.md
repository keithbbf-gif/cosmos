True-to-type takes a year or three. A missing label takes one afternoon.

I like black nursery pots because a **paint pen** (Posca is what I have used) stays. I have watched paper tags pulp, fade, and migrate to a different pot in a storm. I have watched people move two cups and shrug. That shrug is a lie you will tell a buyer later if you are not careful.

## Write it three times

**On the bag the cutting arrived in.** Date. Source. Claimed name. If there is no name, write that. “Unlabeled box 3” is honest.

**On the cup before the stick goes in.** Not after. After is how you mix two varieties on a crowded table.

**On the #3 before the plant goes in.** The cup label dies in the trash if you are sloppy.

If you are extra, a notebook: source, date, first fruit. The true-to-type draft is that notebook. The label is so the notebook still points at a pot.

## What I write

The name as received. A question mark if I do not trust it. I do not upgrade a tag to “confirmed” until I have eaten fruit that matches. I have said that. I will not put confirmed on a stick.

A source code. “Neighbor,” “so-and-so, 2024,” not “internet.” Future-you will want to know who to blame or thank.

## What I do not do

I do not keep the identity in my head. Hundreds of pots. Winter shuffle. I will lie to myself.

I do not use only a map of the bench. Benches get rearranged.

I do not throw a mystery plant in the “I’ll remember” corner. That corner becomes five plants.

## When labels conflict

The pot says one name. The fruit says another. The fruit wins. Relabel. Do not ship the old name. That is how the mess spreads.

Two pots, one name, two fruits: you have a mix-up or a clone pile. Keep both notes. Do not pick the famous photo and throw the other away until you are sure.

## Jack’s prune day

Cuttings in a bag without a card are unknowns. I would rather take fewer sticks and keep the names than fill a tray with “brown fig.”

## Pencil on plastic

Pencil fades. Marker fades. Paint pen stays better on black pots. I still have pots where I can read a name from two summers ago. I also have pots where I cannot. Those are the ones I do not trade as a named variety.

A second label in a notebook is the backup. A photo of the block with names visible is a backup. One paper tag is not a backup.

## Winter shuffle

Pots change neighbors. If your identity was “third from the left,” it died in November. Write on the pot.

## Kids and help

Jack labels too. If two people pot, one person writes. A pot-up day with two writers and no system is how Red Sicilian becomes “red?” 

## Cups in a tray

A piece of tape on the rim beats a map. A map gets wet. If two cups look alike, they will swap. I pot one name at a time when I can. When I cannot, I tape first, stick second.

A heat mat tray is where names go to die if you use scraps of paper under cups. The paper wicks. The name bleeds. Write on the cup.

## After fruit

Add a year and a note on the pot or in the book: “2025, tight, berry.” The received name can stay. The fruit note is what you will trust when you take wood next winter.

Paint the pot. Write the bag. Do it before your hands are dirty. The variety you wanted is the one you can still point at in two years. A famous name you cannot prove is an unknown with better marketing. Use the word unknown when it is true. The unknown draft is that vocabulary. This draft is the pen. The pen is cheaper than a wrong row in the ground.

## Paint pen and sun

Even a good pen fades on the south face of a black pot. Write it twice — opposite sides — or write it lower where the hose does not blast. I have lost a name to one summer of sun and one winter of shuffle. Twice is cheaper than a guess.

If a helper pots with you, one person is the scribe. Say the name out loud, write it, then stick. A silent pot-up is how two cups swap. I have done the silent version. I do not recommend it. The notebook cannot save a pot you never marked.

## A photo of the block

Once a season I would photograph the pot rows so the names are readable. When a pen fades, the photo is a backup. It is not as good as a pen on two sides. It is better than a shrug in February.

I have used paint pen, tape, and a notebook. The combination is what survives. One method alone will fail on a bad week. Two methods is how a name lives until fruit.

A faded name is an unknown. Treat it that way on a trade. Do not guess it back to a celebrity because the pot is near a famous row.

If the pen is dry, stop potting. A dry pen is how a tray becomes a mystery. I have a spare in the shop for that reason.
