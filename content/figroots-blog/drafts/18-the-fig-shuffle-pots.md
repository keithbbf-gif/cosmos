We have more pots than a reasonable person should admit. The Fig Jam interview put the number in the hundreds, most in **3- and 5-gallon** cans, a couple dozen in the ground. The ground is the keepers. The pots are the library. The library has to **move**.

The Winter page calls it relocation. I call it the shuffle. July has a shuffle too. People only talk about the freeze.

## Winter move

Before the night that will freeze a thin pot solid, the cans come in. Shop. Glass frontage. Hoop. Somewhere the roots are not at 15°F.

I label with a paint pen because a paper tag dies in the move. I group by “needs a drink” and “leave it alone” if I can, but honesty says a big shuffle is messy. You will miss a pot on the north side of a bench. That pot will be the rare one.

Barely moist. No fertigation. No 70°F den. I have already said this. I will keep saying it because the first winter people bring a fig into the house they grow scale and a sad shoot.

A wagon beats a hero back. Black plastic nursery pots are what I like because they move and they take a Posca label. Pretty terracotta looks good and dries out and cracks in a freeze. I would not collect in terracotta in 8a.

## July move

The same pot that needed a shop in January needs **afternoon shade** in July. Gravel, concrete, a tin wall — those are ovens. We wrote the shade-and-water draft for the climate. The shuffle is the hardware: slide the block under a bench, a cloth, an east wall, a tree line that still gives morning sun.

If you cannot move them, cloth. If you cannot cloth, water like you mean it and accept cooked roots. I would move them.

Sprinklers on the summer pad are how we keep from walking ourselves into the ground. Overhead wets fruit. I know. Ten trees: drip. A collection: you pick your compromise.

## What a shuffle costs

Time. Space. A shop that smells like wet Promix. A wife who has opinions about the glass frontage. We were crowding a woodshop and building more covered space for a reason.

It also costs mistakes. A pot you shuffle into a dark corner and forget will etiolate or dry out. A pot you shuffle onto a new pad and do not water the same day will wilt and you will blame the variety.

Walk the block after you move it. Lift a few. Light means water. Heavy means you panicked.

## When I would stop shuffling a variety

When it has fruited true, I like the fruit, the hole drains, and I am done playing musical chairs. In-ground. First two summers I still water. After that the shuffle is over for that name.

If I cannot give a name a hole, it stays in the library and it keeps rolling. That is collecting. Admit it.

## How we actually move a block

Wagon. Two people if the #5s are wet. Do not drag a pot on its rim across gravel — you will crack it and dump mix. I have.

Group by water need if you can: the ones that just got rain vs the ones under a bench. After a move, the dry ones hide in the wet-looking crowd.

Paint-pen names face the aisle. A label on the back of a pot against a wall is how you water “the brown one” twice and skip the rare one.

A pot you cannot lift in July is a pot that is too wet or too big for the life you are living. Either open the mix or admit it wants a hole.

## Summer shuffle is not a vacation

I have moved a block under cloth and then forgotten the cloth in a week of rain. The cloth became a steam bag. Peel it back when the heat breaks. Morning sun still matters.

West-pad pots come off the gravel first. East-pad pots can wait a week. Walk at 2 p.m. The ones that are cooked will tell you.

## Winter shuffle is not a greenhouse fantasy

We crowded a woodshop. We built more cover. That is inventory pressure, not a catalog. If you have six pots, a garage corner that stays above a hard freeze is enough. If you have six hundred, you are in our math. Do not copy our square footage. Copy the idea: roots not at 15°F, trees not at 70°F with a heater.

A fig you cannot move and cannot protect is a fig you will replace. The shuffle is not extra. In Arkansas, for pots, it is the method. Put the wagon where you will trip over it in October. That is how the method happens.

If you only remember one move, remember the night before the hard freeze. If you remember two, remember the first 100° week. Those two moves save more plants than a new fertilizer. The rest of the year the pots can sit, as long as you still water. Sitting is allowed. Forgetting the forecast is not.

## A forecast I actually use

I look at the night low, not the daytime slogan. A 28°F night is a move night even if tomorrow is 60. A 102°F afternoon is a shade night even if the morning was kind.

I would rather move a block a day early than a day late. Late is ice in a #3 or a cooked root. Early is an extra trip. I can live with a trip.
