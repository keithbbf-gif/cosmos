UAEX talks rollercoaster winters. The mean version is not the January 15°. It is the March night after a week of 75.

Buds move. You relax. Then 24°F. The tips go black. Breba, if you had any, is often done. The GDD toys that let you reset after a freeze are trying to model this. A warm February does not count if March took the wood.

## Pots

This is why the shuffle exists. One ugly night: roll them in. A #3 is easy. A 15-gallon is a decision you should have made in the afternoon, not at 10 p.m. with a flashlight.

If you cannot move them, cover. Cloth, a tote, a barrel. Perfect is less important than done.

Barely moist. A wet pot in a freeze is ice around roots.

## In-ground

A sheet, lights in a teepee, a smudge of hope. Young trees first. The east wall helps and does not finish the job.

I would not irrigate the canopy at dusk so it freezes wet unless you know what you are doing. I would rather cover.

Afterward, wait. Black tips are not always dead to the ground. Scratch down the shoot. Green means wait for a new push. The freeze-recovery draft is the thicket chapter if you lost the top.

Do not fertilize the next morning to “help it.” Do not prune every tip the next morning to feel useful. You will cut live wood you cannot see yet.

## Crop math

Main crop can still happen. New shoots, current season, heat. An early tool fig has a better chance than a late berry type that just lost three weeks.

Breba is the loser. That is the Arkansas deal most years anyway.

If you take cuttings the week after, take them from wood that is still alive. Black mush is not a node.

## What I write down

Date of budbreak. Date of freeze. What died. What fruited anyway. That is a better notebook than a generic “zone 8a” sticker on the pot.

## GDD reset

The hobby calculators that zero after a freeze are trying to be honest. A warm February is not a season if March took the tips. Start the count when the tree is actually growing again. The GDD draft is the math. This is the night that breaks the math.

## Pots you left out “because 8a”

8a still has these nights. A #3 does not care about a zone map. Roll it. If you gambled and lost, wait for the base. If the base is dead, the label comes off.

## A night-of kit that is not cute

Wagon in the path. Cloth in a crate. A few totes that fit a #3. A headlamp. If the kit is in the attic, you will not use it at 9 p.m.

I would rather cover ten pots badly than cover two pots perfectly and leave the rare one out. The rare one is always the one in the back.

In-ground: a sheet on a young tree beats a plan you read. Lights in a teepee if you already own them. I would not run a space heater under a dry leaf pile. That is a fire, not a fig tip.

## Next morning

Do not photograph every black tip and declare the season over. Water if the wind dried the pots. Leave the covers on if another night is coming. Then wait days, not hours, to saw.

When new buds push below the burn, you have a plant. When nothing pushes and the rest of the block is leafing, you have a decision. Cuttings should have been taken in winter. Too late to be clever.

The late freeze is a tax on hope. Move what you can. Cover what you cannot. Wait before you saw. Then grow this year’s wood like the winter draft told you. Hope is allowed in February. A wagon in the afternoon is how hope survives the night. Zone 8a is not a promise. It is an average. Averages do not cover a pot.

## Breba after that night

Assume it is gone. If nubs survive, that is extra. Do not prune for breba in a year the tips already burned. Grow main-crop wood. That is the crop 8a actually pays.

If you have lights in a teepee, test them in daylight. A dead string on a 24° night is a prop. I would rather have a blanket I know than a wire I have not plugged in since Christmas.

## Pots against glass

A sun-warmed shop that wakes tips in January is the same tax with a roof. Vent. Pull tender pots off the glass. The late freeze can happen inside a structure if the structure taught the plant it was April.

After the night, walk pots first. They freeze through. In-ground can wait an hour. The #3 on the porch cannot. That order has saved more names than a pretty cover I installed on the wrong plant.

Write the freeze date on the shop board. Next February you will remember why you do not cheer early green tips.

If two nights are coming, leave the covers on through the day if you can stand it, or be ready to put them back. One night is not always the only night.
I would rather cover a pot I did not need to cover than explain a cooked rare name. Extra cloth is cheap.
