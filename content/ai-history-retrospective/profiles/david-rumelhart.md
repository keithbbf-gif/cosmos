David E. Rumelhart was born in 1942 and died in 2011. The photograph is from IJCNN in Seattle, 8 July 1991, taken by Rolf Kickuth: a man at a neural-network meeting, which is to say, at a meeting his 1986 work had helped make crowded.

## PDP

With James L. McClelland and the PDP Research Group he edited *Parallel Distributed Processing* (MIT Press, 1986). The volumes treated mental processes as interactions among simple units, not as a central Lisp interpreter. Past tense, reading, schemata — psychology’s objects — sit beside the algorithms. Rumelhart was a psychologist by training (Stanford, then UCSD, later Stanford again). The book is a psychologist’s raid on a computer-science winter.

## The *Nature* letter

With Geoffrey Hinton and Ronald Williams he published “Learning Representations by Back-Propagating Errors” on 9 October 1986. The letter is the object engineers kept on the desk. Priority is shared with earlier writings, notably Paul Werbos’s 1974 thesis. Shared priority does not erase the 1986 public event.

## Illness, and a prize with his name

Rumelhart developed a neurodegenerative illness and left the research front. The David E. Rumelhart Prize, awarded by the Cognitive Science Society, is the community’s way of keeping the 1986 bet attached to a person. He died in 2011.

If Hinton became the public face of deep learning, Rumelhart remains the reason the 1986 moment was a psychology moment as well as an engineering one. The units were about minds, not only about digits.
