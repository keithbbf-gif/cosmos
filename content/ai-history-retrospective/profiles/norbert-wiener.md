Norbert Wiener was a child prodigy in the old American newspaper sense — A.B. from Tufts as an adolescent, Harvard Ph.D. in 1913, a career that looks, on a CV, like restlessness until MIT holds him. He was born in 1894 in Columbia, Missouri, and died in 1964 in Stockholm. Between those dates he wrote the book that made “cybernetics” a public word.

## Prediction and fire

Wiener’s wartime work on predicting flight paths for anti-aircraft fire is the earthly origin of his later metaphysics. A gun and a plane form a loop with delay. Filter the noise; estimate the future; do not pretend the operator is outside the system. With Julian Bigelow and Arturo Rosenblueth he pushed that engineering into a 1943 paper on purpose and teleology.

*Cybernetics* (1948) gathered the mathematics — Fourier analysis, correlation, entropy in a sense neighboring Shannon’s — and then refused to stay in the mathematics. Prosthetic control, psychiatric metaphor, a theory of messages. Scientists still argue about which chapters are wheat. Readers in 1948 bought the whole loaf.

## Refusal

After the war Wiener made a public point of limiting his military consulting. *The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950) is the citizen’s version of the 1948 treatise: automation, unemployment, the risk that communication systems would be used to manage people as if they were servos. The prose is uneven. The stance is not.

He sat among the Macy Conference regulars without becoming John McCarthy’s kind of AI man. When McCarthy needed a name in 1955, he avoided Wiener’s. That slight is a historical fact about branding, not a claim that the two programs were strangers. Feedback, noise, and communication theory leaked everywhere.

## The afterlife of a word

“Cybernetics” went into Soviet philosophy debates, into management fads, into the prefix that now sits in front of crime and space. Wiener cannot be blamed for every prefix. He can be read, in the 1948 and 1950 books, as someone who saw that information machines would be political machines and then said so in trade cloth, while he still had the authority of MIT and a bestseller.

The portrait in this file is a later photograph. The man in 1948 was already arguing with a future that had not yet purchased a transistor radio.
