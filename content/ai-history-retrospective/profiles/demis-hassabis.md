Demis Hassabis was born in London in 1976. He was a chess player and a video-game designer before he was a doctoral student in cognitive neuroscience (University College London) and, in 2010, a co-founder of DeepMind. The 2018 Royal Society photograph is a licensed public image from the years between AlphaGo and AlphaFold.

## A board

The 28 January 2016 *Nature* paper on AlphaGo, with David Silver, Aja Huang, and a long DeepMind list, is the first instrument. Policy network, value network, Monte Carlo tree search, a match against Fan Hui already won, a match against Lee Sedol still to come. The paper is specific. The television was not. This profile stays with the paper.

## A molecule

“Highly Accurate Protein Structure Prediction with AlphaFold,” Jumper, Hassabis, and colleagues, *Nature*, 2021, is the second instrument. The CASP assessments are the public score. The 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, awarded to David Baker and, jointly, to Hassabis and John M. Jumper, is the official sentence about computational protein design and structure prediction. Baker’s work is a separate laboratory story that the same prize bound into one week in Stockholm.

## How to write this without a brochure

DeepMind’s corporate history, its acquisition by Google, and its later product announcements are public and easy to over-tell. The historical objects that earn a portrait in this series are a 2016 game paper, a 2021 biology paper, and a 2024 prize citation. Games and molecules are different worlds. The methodological rhyme — large learned models, a search or a diffusion around them, a public benchmark — is real. The rhyme is not a theory of everything.

Hassabis’s own interviews often mention chess first. That is biography. The bibliography mentions *Nature* twice.
