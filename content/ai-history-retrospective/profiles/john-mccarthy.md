John McCarthy was born in 1927 in Boston, died in 2011 in Stanford, and spent a remarkable fraction of the years between inventing furniture the field still sits on: a name, a language, a laboratory, and a style of asking for common sense as if it were an engineering specification.

## The noun

He is the first author on the 31 August 1955 Dartmouth proposal. The word “artificial intelligence” is his bet that a research program could be funded under a new heading rather than under Wiener’s. After a brief early Stanford appointment, he taught at Dartmouth and then MIT. He returned to Stanford in 1962. The Stanford Artificial Intelligence Project began in 1963; the foothills laboratory later called SAIL took shape in the mid-1960s with Lester Earnest. The Stanford Cart is that laboratory’s vehicle. Shakey, often filmed in the same era, was an SRI machine.

## The language

“Recursive Functions of Symbolic Expressions and Their Computation by Machine, Part I” (*CACM*, April 1960) is the LISP paper. CONS, CAR, CDR, garbage collection as a necessity, recursion as the ordinary way to walk a list. The language escaped the paper and became a civilization: MIT’s, then Stanford’s, then a commercial winter of Lisp machines.

He presented “Programs with Common Sense” at the National Physical Laboratory symposium *Mechanisation of Thought Processes*, Teddington, 24–27 November 1958. The HMSO proceedings are commonly cited as 1959. The Advice Taker would accept declarative sentences and deduce actions. The paper is short. The debt it created is long. Circumscription, later, was one of his attempts to make “unless there is a reason not to” into mathematics.

## Time-sharing and a prize

McCarthy was an advocate of time-sharing when a computer was still a room. The politics of who gets a keystroke are not separate from the politics of who gets to define a field. The ACM Turing Award in 1971 cites him “for his major contributions to the field of artificial intelligence, including his work on the theory of computation and the invention of the LISP programming language, as well as his work in the development of time-sharing systems.”

He could be impatient in print with connectionists, with philosophers, and with journalists. The impatience is documented. So is the generosity of the basic tools. People who never liked his philosophy still live inside his parentheses.

The 2006 photograph in this file is late. The man who wrote the 1955 conjecture was younger, and sure enough to put the conjecture in a Rockefeller proposal as if it were almost a method.
