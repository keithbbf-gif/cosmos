John McCarthy was born in 1927 in Boston, died in 2011 at Stanford, and spent a remarkable fraction of the intervening years inventing the furniture the field still sits on: a name, a language, a laboratory, and a style of asking for common sense as if it were an engineering specification.

## The noun

He is the first author on the 31 August 1955 Dartmouth proposal. The word “artificial intelligence” is his bet that a research program could be funded under a new heading rather than under Wiener’s. He later taught at Dartmouth, MIT, and, from 1962, Stanford, where SAIL became a place with a mountain, a film of Shakey’s cousins, and a lot of LISP.

## The language

“Recursive Functions of Symbolic Expressions and Their Computation by Machine, Part I” (*CACM*, April 1960) is the LISP paper. CONS, CAR, CDR, garbage collection as a necessity, recursion as the ordinary way to walk a list. The language escaped the paper and became a civilization: MIT’s, then Stanford’s, then a commercial winter of Lisp machines.

He also wrote “Programs with Common Sense” for the 1958–1959 Teddington symposium, published in 1959. The Advice Taker would accept declarative sentences and deduce actions. The paper is short. The debt it created is long. Circumscription, later, was one of his attempts to make “unless there is a reason not to” into mathematics.

## Time-sharing and a prize

McCarthy was an advocate of time-sharing when a computer was still a room. The politics of who gets a keystroke are not separate from the politics of who gets to define a field. The ACM Turing Award in 1971 cites him for the advice-taker paper’s subject matter and for LISP, in the Award’s official language of the time.

He could be impatient in print with connectionists, with philosophers, and with journalists. The impatience is documented. So is the generosity of the basic tools. People who never liked his philosophy still live inside his parentheses.

The 2006 photograph in this file is late. The man who wrote the 1955 conjecture was younger, and sure enough to put the conjecture in a Rockefeller proposal as if it were almost a method.
