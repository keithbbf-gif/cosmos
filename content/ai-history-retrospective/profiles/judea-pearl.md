Judea Pearl was born in 1936 in Tel Aviv and has long been at UCLA. The photograph is from NIPS in 2013: a man at a machine-learning meeting whose 1988 book the meeting had spent twenty-five years partly absorbing and partly postponing.

![Judea Pearl at NIPS, 6 December 2013.](../assets/portraits/judea-pearl.jpg)

## 1988

*Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems* made Bayesian networks a working technology: directed acyclic graphs, local conditional probabilities, algorithms for belief updating that did not require a joint table the size of the world. Expert systems had been inventing arithmetics of “certainty.” Pearl offered an arithmetic that already had a name.

## 2000

*Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference* is a different ambition. Interventions, counterfactuals, the do-operator, a claim that correlation’s culture was leaving science’s questions unanswered. Economists and epidemiologists had their own causal languages. Pearl’s graphs became, in the 2010s, a lingua franca that machine-learning people could no longer entirely ignore, especially once “explainability” became a grant word.

He came to the United States after an Israeli engineering education, took a doctorate at Brooklyn Polytechnic, and spent years on pattern recognition and on heuristics before the 1988 book made him, for a generation, “the Bayesian-network person.” That reduction annoyed him, and the 2000 book is the correction he wrote himself.

Machine-learning culture in the 2010s often treated causality as a luxury. Pearl’s public essays from those years — some collected later for a general audience — are impatient in a way that is useful to quote only when the page is in front of you. The impatience has a technical content: an interventional query is not a predictive query with better branding.

## A prize, and a public grief

The 2011 ACM Turing Award cites him for fundamental contributions to artificial intelligence through the development of a calculus for probabilistic and causal reasoning. The citation is accurate.

Pearl’s son Daniel was murdered in 2002; the public work of the Daniel Pearl Foundation is part of the later biography. A scientific profile can note the fact without turning it into a keynote. The books remain the books.

If the statistical turn of the 1990s had a conscience about what a number is a number of, Pearl is that conscience with a notation.
