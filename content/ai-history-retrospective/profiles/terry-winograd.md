Terry Winograd was born in 1946. His doctoral work at MIT, published as MAC-TR-84 in 1971 and as *Understanding Natural Language* in 1972, is still the demo people mean when they say “the program that understood a room full of blocks.” SHRDLU could move a red cube, answer a “why,” and look, for a visitor, like a conversation.

## What the demo cost

The universe was small. Every noun had a procedure. The fluency was purchased by a closed ontology and a parser tuned to that ontology. Winograd knew this earlier than the visitors. The later literature’s use of SHRDLU as either a miracle or a fraud is a failure to read the 1972 book, which is more careful than either.

## 1986

*Understanding Computers and Cognition*, with Fernando Flores, refuses the earlier picture. Language as action, not as transmission of internal symbols; a debt to speech-act theory and to Heidegger that made some computer scientists change the subject. Winograd moved his career toward human–computer interaction at Stanford. Google’s early years, in the public record, include him as a doctoral advisor to Larry Page. That fact is often used as trivia. It is more useful as a pointer: the man who had tried to make a machine understand a room later helped students think about making a machine retrieve the web.

## Why a placeholder
