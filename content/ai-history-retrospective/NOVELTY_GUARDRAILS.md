# Novelty and IP guardrails

Hard constraints for this staged series. They override clever framing.

## Out of scope — do not mention

- COSMOS, KMesh, ModelRater, or any house operating system, mesh, rater, or dashboard
- Keith’s private systems, grants, leases, pens, or session machinery
- Unpublished product roadmaps, internal tickets, or work-order queues
- Patent dockets, claim charts, unfiled ideas, or “preload” caches
- Live runtime trees, ledgers, tokens, or install paths
- Any system that exists only as private correspondence or an unpublished specification

If a sentence would only make sense to someone who has seen a private tree, delete the sentence.

## In scope

- Published papers, books, dissertations, and technical reports with public bibliographic identity
- Conference programs and published proceedings (Dartmouth proposal; IJCAI; NIPS/NeurIPS; ICML; ACL; CVPR; ICLR)
- Contemporaneous newspapers, magazines, and later scholarly histories
- Museum, university archive, and government-photograph catalogs
- Official prize citations (Turing Award, Nobel, Japan Prize, etc.) after they were announced
- Public product launches and papers through about 2026 (ChatGPT, GPT-4, LLaMA, Gemini, Claude, AlphaFold, EU AI Act) as *historical events*, not as vendor copy

## Invention ban

Do not invent:

- paper titles
- awards or prize years
- quotations
- lab affiliations
- birth or death dates
- “lost” meetings that do not appear in the public record

If you cannot cite it, leave it out. Uncertainty is allowed: “the minutes are thin,” “the date is usually given as…”

## Novelty of the series itself

These essays are original magazine prose about a public record. They are not a dump of encyclopedia articles and not a paraphrase of a single secondary book. Where a standard history is used (Crevier; McCorduck; Nilsson; Boden; Wooldridge; Russell & Norvig’s historical notes), name it in `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` and do not lift distinctive phrasing.

## Dual use and harm

This series does not include methods for weapons, pathogens, or offensive cyber operations. Historical mention of military funding (ONR, DARPA, the Manhattan Project’s computing context) stays at the level of institutions and published programs.

## Review checklist before any later import

1. Search the folder for the out-of-scope names above. Any hit is a fail.
2. Search for banned style words in `STYLE_GUIDE.md`.
3. Confirm every `portrait:` path exists or is `null`.
4. Confirm every profile either credits a Commons/museum file or uses a labeled placeholder.
5. Confirm no article claims a paper, prize, or quotation that is not in `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` or a standard public catalog.
