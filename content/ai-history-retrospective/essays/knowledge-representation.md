Marvin Minsky’s 1974 MIT memo “A Framework for Representing Knowledge” offered the frame: a chunk of stereotyped situations, slots, defaults. Roger Schank and Robert Abelson’s *Scripts, Plans, Goals, and Understanding* (1977) offered the script: the restaurant, the visit, the sequence you do not want to re-derive from first principles every time someone mentions a waiter.

These objects are literary. They are also engineering. They try to make common sense addressable by name.

## From SHRDLU to a recantation of sorts

Terry Winograd’s block-world procedures (1971–1972) were a high-water mark of the idea that understanding is a program with a closed ontology. *Understanding Computers and Cognition*, with Fernando Flores (1986), is a different book: speech acts, Heidegger, a refusal of the earlier optimism. Winograd did not vanish. He moved toward human–computer interaction at Stanford. The move is a data point. Some of the best critics of symbolic KR had written the systems.

## Description logics, OWL, a web that wanted types

While CYC, Douglas Lenat’s long project begun in the 1980s, tried to encode a mountain of common-sense assertions, a quieter line in Europe and the U.S. developed description logics — formal fragments of first-order logic with decidable inference. The Web Ontology Language (OWL), as a W3C recommendation in 2004, is that line meeting the web. Knowledge here is a graph you can publish, not a script you perform.

CYC’s public papers and demonstrations are part of the record; so is the recurring report that common sense is larger than a project plan. This series will not invent a verdict on CYC’s internal knowledge base. It will say that the ambition was explicit and that the field, after 2012, largely stopped trying to win that way.

## What KR left on the table

Databases, knowledge graphs at search companies, medical ontologies (SNOMED and its kin), and the semantic web’s remaining corners are the unglamorous afterlife. Large language models now emit sentences that look like frames. They do not, by virtue of fluency, give you a slot-filling contract you can audit like OWL.

The knowledge-representation decades were an attempt to make the world’s regularities *writable*. The statistical decades were an attempt to make them *fittable*. A history that treats the first attempt as merely failed has not looked at a hospital coding system, or at the 1986 Winograd and Flores book, which failed at a higher resolution.
