On 14 February 2019, OpenAI posted a technical report by Alec Radford, Jeffrey Wu, Rewon Child, David Luan, Dario Amodei, and Ilya Sutskever: “Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners.” GPT-2 was a transformer language model trained on a web scrape the authors called WebText. The company staged the release, citing misuse worries. The staging itself became the news. The model, when it later circulated more fully, was impressive and also ordinary in the way a new ceiling is ordinary: it failed in public, it was funny in public, and it made next-token prediction look like a general interface.

## Few-shot as a product idea

“Language Models are Few-Shot Learners,” the 2020 NeurIPS paper on GPT-3 by Tom B. Brown and a long OpenAI list, put a number on scale (175 billion parameters, in the paper) and showed that prompting could replace some fine-tuning. The API that followed turned a research graph into a billing meter. This series is not a corporate chronology. The public fact is that completion — a cursor in a box — became a way to ship software.

ChatGPT launched on 30 November 2022 as a research preview of a dialogue-tuned model. The weeks after are already over-described. What matters for a history is the social shift: a language model left the specialist demo and entered offices, schools, and joke accounts. GPT-4’s technical report (arXiv:2303.08774, March 2023) described a multimodal system and declined to publish the usual full architecture card. That refusal is also a historical datum.

## Other stacks, other papers

Google’s BERT had already industrialized the encoder. The company’s later Bard/Gemini products belong to the same public product category, with papers and blog posts that should be cited as such, not as folklore. Meta’s LLaMA paper (Touvron et al., February 2023) and the subsequent Llama 2 release made weights a political object: who may download a model, under what acceptable-use text.

Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Mistral, and Chinese laboratories (the public names include Baichuan, Zhipu, Alibaba’s Qwen line, and others as their papers appeared) turned 2023–2025 into a crowded literature of dense and mixture-of-experts models. Exact parameter counts in press releases are not always the same object as counts in papers. A retrospective should prefer the paper.

## Law and prizes, 2024

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Artificial Intelligence Act, entered into force on 1 August 2024, with staged obligations after that date. It is a legal text, not a scientific one. It belongs here because the public history of language models is now partly a history of compliance calendars.

The 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics went to John Hopfield and Geoffrey Hinton for foundational work on neural networks. The 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to David Baker and, jointly, to Demis Hassabis and John Jumper for computational protein design and AlphaFold. Those citations are official. They do not mean “AI won science.” They mean a prize committee wrote a particular sentence about a particular body of work.

## What 2025–2026 can be said without invention

By 2025–2026, dialogue models were ordinary infrastructure in many workplaces, reasoning-themed training regimes were a research fashion with public papers, and open-weight releases continued beside closed APIs. A history that stops here is not complete. It is caught, on purpose, at the edge of the public record. No unpublished roadmap belongs on this page.

The 1950 imitation game is still the cheap reference. The expensive reference is the 2017 architecture, the 2020 few-shot paper, and the November 2022 interface that made next-token prediction a mass habit.
