In 1843, in a set of notes attached to a translation, Ada Lovelace wrote out a method for the unbuilt Analytical Engine to compute Bernoulli numbers. The engine existed as drawings and as Babbage’s quarrel with the British government. The note existed as print. That gap — a program for a machine that had not yet been paid for — is the first useful fact in this history.

This series does not begin at Dartmouth in 1956. The summer in Hanover named a field and wrote a funding dialect. It did not invent the wish that a mechanism might carry an argument.

## Engines that already knew a pattern

Jacquard’s punched cards, early in the nineteenth century, told a loom which warp threads to lift. The cloth was not “thinking.” It was a memory you could stack. Babbage saw those cards and wanted them for numbers. The Difference Engine was a specialist: polynomial tables, cranked into being for navigators and gunners. The Analytical Engine, as he described it, was a generalist — mill and store, operation cards and variable cards. Historians still argue how much of the later stored-program computer is genuinely latent in those plates and how much we read backward from 1945. What is not in dispute is the public text: Menabrea’s 1842 sketch in French, Lovelace’s 1843 English notes, Note G.

Lovelace’s caution is as important as her optimism. She argued that the Engine could compose music or pictures if those things could be reduced to operations on symbols — and that it had no power of originating ideas. Later generations would quote both halves, usually one at a time.

## Logic as hardware, before hardware

George Boole’s *The Laws of Thought* (1854) treated AND, OR, and NOT as algebra. Gottlob Frege’s *Begriffsschrift* (1879) made quantification a notation. Russell and Whitehead’s *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913) tried to grind mathematics down to types and proofs so long that almost no one read them through. The work mattered anyway: it made “proof” look like a finite manipulation of marks.

In 1936 Alan Turing, answering David Hilbert’s Entscheidungsproblem in the *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society*, defined a machine by what it could write on a tape. Alonzo Church, in the same season, used the λ-calculus. Emil Post had his own production systems. Independently they were boxing the same animal: effective calculability. Turing’s paper also showed a limit. There is no general mechanical test for whether an arbitrary machine will halt.

Claude Shannon’s 1937 MIT master’s thesis, published in the *AIEE Transactions* in 1938, did something blunter. He showed that telephone relays could instantiate Boolean algebra. Logic left the page and entered the switching frame.

## War as an accelerator, not a birth

The Second World War paid for machines that would have been eccentric toys in 1935. At Bletchley Park, Turing and others attacked Naval Enigma; the Colossus machines at Dollis Hill and Bletchley, designed by Tommy Flowers, ran against Lorenz. In the United States, the Harvard Mark I (IBM Automatic Sequence Controlled Calculator) and then ENIAC at the Moore School ate ballistics tables. John von Neumann’s *First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC*, dated 30 June 1945, circulated the stored-program idea so widely that later patent fights would trip over it.

None of this was “artificial intelligence” as a brand. It was computation under military time. The brand comes later. The machines, the logic, and the habit of treating thought as a writable procedure were already on the table.

Grace Hopper, programming the Mark I and then UNIVAC, would spend the next decades insisting that a compiler was not a luxury. Konrad Zuse, in Germany, had already built programmable electromechanical machines and drafted Plankalkül. In Moscow and Kyiv, as Slava Gerovitch has shown, the word “cybernetics” would soon be a political weather system of its own.

The prehistory is not a cute prologue. It is the set of objects later AI would inherit: a tape, a relay, a punched card, a proof, a wartime deadline, and a woman writing a program for an engine that Parliament would not buy.
