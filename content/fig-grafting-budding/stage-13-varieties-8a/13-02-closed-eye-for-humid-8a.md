# Closed-eye figs for humid 8a

The eye is the hole at the bottom of the fruit. A
closed eye is a door that stays mostly shut. An open
eye is a door for rain, vinegar flies, and the beetle
that turns an August fig into a wet brown argument.

Humid 8a — the mid-South, the Gulf edge, any yard where
your shirt sticks at 9 a.m. — punishes open eyes. You
can still grow them. You pick earlier, you net, you
accept losses after a storm. Or you spend your graft
slots on closed-eye types and stop fighting the air.

*Celeste* is the classroom example. *LSU* releases were
bred with this weather in the room. Some *Hardy
Chicago* fruit stays reasonably tight. A lot of
*Turkey* / *Mission* / *Kadota* conversation in dry-west
books assumes an air that is not ours. Read those books
as desert neighbors, not as local law.

Closed-eye is not a religion that saves split fruit
after a two-inch rain on a dry week. It is a religion
that saves you from the souring beetle most years.
That is enough to change which scion I tape on a tree
I have to walk past every morning.

When someone mails you a mystery purple, ask about the
eye and about their August. A dry-west grower raving
about an open-eye fig is telling the truth *there*.
Your job is to translate.

I still keep one open-eye on a dry-ish wall for the
years it behaves, because some of those figs taste like
the reason people bother. I do not convert the main
stool to that fig. The main stool is food. Food in
humid 8a is a closed door.

If you already grafted an open-eye onto the main stool
and August was a mess, you do not have to live with
it forever. That is what the knife is for. Next March
you can put *Celeste* or an LSU back on the stubs and
call it a correction, not a failure. The climate was
the editor. You are allowed to accept the edit.

Dry 8a — west Texas edges, rain-shadow yards, a
courtyard that never stays wet — can run open-eye
figs that would sour two counties east. Do not
export your success as a rule. Ask about August
rain before you take advice, and before you give
it. The eye is a climate tool, not a moral rank.

When you taste a closed-eye fig that is merely
sweet and not interesting, you are still allowed
to keep it as the food rung and put the interesting
open-eye on a pot you can net. That is a ladder
with a spare, not a failure of taste.
