# A multi-variety ripening ladder

If you are going to build a cocktail tree, build it as
a calendar, not as a trophy shelf. Three names that
ripen in a line beat seven names that ripen in the
same week and then fight.

Early. *Celeste* or another closed-eye that colors
when the rest of the yard is still talking about
tomatoes — here that is often late June, not the
catalog week from a dry coast. This is the head you
protect from late frost if you want that first bowl.
It is also the head you do not shade with a vigorous
midseason bully.

Mid. An LSU or a workhorse that likes heat. This is
the volume rung in July and August, when 8a humidity
is already doing half the work. It will try to be the
whole tree. Pinch.

Late. Only if your first frost usually waits. A late
fig in an 8a pocket that frosts in late October is a
bowl you lose some years — I mark that rung on the
tag with the month I actually picked last year, not
the label date. I will still keep a late head on a
warm wall. I will not keep three late heads and then
act surprised.

Vigor match along the ladder. Early-polite plus
mid-bully plus late-polite is a typical mess. The
mid eats the light. Either pick a mid that you will
prune like a job, or pick a mid that is not a rocket.

Breba note. A breba rung only exists in years the
old wood lives. Do not design the whole tree around
June brebas in a frost pocket. Design it around the
main crop and treat brebas as a bonus on the wall
that keeps wood.

Label the rungs. Metal tags. A map. When you pick a
fig in July you want to know which scaffold earned
it, or you cannot decide what to pinch next year.

Two-year build. Early and mid the first spring, late
the second, after you have eaten the first two and
confirmed you still want a third walk in October.

The ladder fails when every rung is a flavor you
wanted and none is a flavor that ripens here. Taste
before you tape. A ripening ladder made from catalog
weeks written in someone else's climate is a fiction.
Write your weeks. Then spend the slots.

Leave a hole on purpose. A three-rung tree with a
spare scaffold of the original hardy variety is a
ladder that can lose a rung in January and still
feed you. A four-rung tree with no hardy spare is
a menu that winter can blank.

Pick the early rung for the east side if you can,
so it gets morning light and less west cook. Put
the vigorous mid where you can reach it with
loppers without a speech. These are boring
placements. Boring placements are how a ladder
stays a ladder instead of a thicket with fruit
hidden in it.
