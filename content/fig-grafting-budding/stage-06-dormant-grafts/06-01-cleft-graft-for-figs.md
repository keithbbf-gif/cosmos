# Cleft graft for figs

Cleft is the conversion graft. Stock thicker than the scion, still
in the range where you are willing to split it — say an inch to a
bit over two. Bigger than that, I walk away and wait for bark slip.

Head the stock square with a saw. Split it down the center a couple
of inches. A knife and a twist, or a cheap cleft tool. Do not
explode the stub into three plates. You want a single crack you can
hold open with a screwdriver while you set wood.

Cut the scion into a wedge, an inch and a half or so, thicker on
the outer face. The outer face is where the cambium must meet the
stock cambium. The inner face can show pith. If both faces are pith,
you carved a sponge. Two or three buds above the wedge. Seal the
tip.

Set one scion at each edge of a wider stub. They lean in a little.
You will keep the better one. On a narrow stub, one scion is enough
if the alignment is honest.

The wrap has to close the split. Figs dry down that crack like a
chimney. Tape firm, then wax or paint the stub face and any crack
that still grins. I have lost clefts that were aligned and pretty
because I admired the wedge and under-wrapped the chimney.

In 8a I do this when the stock is swelling and the scion is still
fridge-cold. Bag the scions if the wind is out. Shade the west
face. Stake later — a cleft union on fig is a hinge until the
second summer.

Do not cleft a frozen stub on a 30°F morning because you have the
day off. The split will be a shatter and the bark will not forgive
you. Do not cleft a stub you have not tested for life. Dead wood
splits beautifully.

Aftercare is sucker patrol. Every bud below the cleft will try to
become the tree. They are faster than your scions. Rub them while
they are soft. If you miss a week in May, bring the loppers and
try not to swear at a plant that is only doing its job.

Cleft is not elegant. It is strong when it takes. On figs it takes
when the rim meets the rim and the chimney is closed. Everything
else is theater with a knife.

If the split runs too far down the trunk, stop and wrap the run
before you set wood. A split that keeps going is a wound you did
not mean. Tape it. Next year use bark graft on that diameter. I
have chased a split with extra tape and extra swear words and
still lost the stub to summer drought through the crack.

Two scions on a stub is not greed. It is a spare. When both take,
keep the better angle and cut the other after it has helped feed
the stub for a few weeks. Cutting the spare the same week both
break is how you stress a stub that was just getting used to
having leaves again.
