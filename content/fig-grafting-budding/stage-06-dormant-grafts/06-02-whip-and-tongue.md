# Whip and tongue

When the two pieces are the same size, whip-and-tongue is the graft
that feels like carpentry. Long sloping cuts, a tongue on each
face, they lock, you wrap, you go drink water. On figs it also
makes a union that thickens without the knuckle a cleft leaves.

The slope wants to be long. A stubby slant is a short cambium line
and a hinge. I aim for at least an inch on pencil wood, more if
my hands are behaving. The tongue is a downward slit on the stock
face and an upward slit on the scion face so they interlock. If
the tongues tear, your knife is dull or you are rushing. A simple
whip without tongues still works. The tongues are extra grip and
extra contact, not a religion.

Pith will show. That is fine if the rims meet. I line up one side
perfectly and wrap toward that side. Chasing both sides on a
slightly oval fig cutting is how you undo the first alignment.

This is my bench-graft in February and my in-ground graft on young
pots in March. It is not my four-inch stump graft. If you are
forcing a whip onto a fat stub, you are using the wrong chapter.

Wrap the whole seam. Cover the buds if you are in wind; leave a
bud able to punch through stretched parafilm. Seal the scion tip.
On the bench I often slip the whole plant into a tall bag for a
week in bright shade, then vent.

Failure mode: the slope was short and the first wind folded it.
Failure mode two: diameters were not the same and you centered
the mismatch so no cambiums touched. Failure mode three: you
loved the tongues so much you handled the faces with your thumbs
and left skin oil and crushed cells. Hold wood by the bark.

In 8a heat, a whip on a thin pot dries fast. Water the pot the
day before, not the hour after in a panic. A soggy pot plus a
bag is a fungus house.

I use whip when I can choose both diameters. It is the graft I
trust on a rooted cutting and a matching scion. It is also the
graft that teaches your hands what a long, clean face feels like.
If you can cut this, the other methods get easier. If you cannot
yet, chip-bud until you can. There is no prize for a tongue that
looks like a bite mark.
