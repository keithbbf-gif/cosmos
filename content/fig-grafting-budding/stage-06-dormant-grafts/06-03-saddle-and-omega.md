# Saddle and omega

Saddle is two V cuts that sit on each other like a rider and a
horse. Stock gets the point or the notch, depending on which
school you learned; I notch the stock and point the scion so
rain does not sit in a cup. The faces need to be the same length
and the diameters need to be the same. When they are, saddle is
fast and the wrap is easy.

I like it on bench plants. I can see all the cambium. I can
correct a face. I can do ten in an hour once the knife is
behaving. In the field, on a windy March day, I still reach for
a whip or a cleft. Saddle wants two hands and no audience.

Omega tools punch a matching cartoon in both pieces. They work
when the wood is round, the diameters match, and the tool is
sharp. Fig wood is often oval and pithy. The punch then gives
you a pretty puzzle with cambiums that kiss in two spots and
miss in the rest. Takes happen. Misses happen more than the
tool aisle promised.

If you own the tool, use it on the cuttings that actually fit
the dies. Do not squeeze a fat stick through a small die and
call it close enough. The crushed rim is not cambium anymore.

Seal still matters. An omega joint looks closed and still leaks
through the pith. Wrap the whole thing. Seal the tip. Shade.

Grower notes from the fig boards: saddle was the easy tight fit
for people who did not want to practice tongues; desiccation
still killed plants that were not sealed. That matches my sink.
The geometry is not the enemy. Air is the enemy.

In 8a I treat omega as a bench toy for matching pots, not as a
conversion tool for a freeze-killed trunk. The trunk does not
fit the die. Your hand and a cleft knife do.

If saddle scares you, skip it. Chip-bud and whip will carry a
home orchard. Saddle is for when you want a straight, strong
union on twins and you enjoy cutting a V. There is no extra
fruit in it. There is only a nicer knuckle in year three.

A 8a note on the tool drawer: the omega punch is a winter bench
item. In the yard the dies clog with latex and grit, and you
will force a cut you should have walked away from. If you bring
the tool outside, bring a rag and a second method. The first
oval stick is your cue to change methods, not to squeeze harder.

Practice saddles on *Brown Turkey* prunings in a bucket of water
before you spend the rare stick. The V either sits or it rocks.
If it rocks on the practice wood, it will rock on the good wood
and then dry along the gap.
