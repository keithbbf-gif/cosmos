# Knife, tape, wax — what actually matters

You do not need a grafting station. You need a sharp blade, something
that holds, something that seals, and the sense to stop when the blade
is dull.

The knife. A cheap grafting knife that you keep sharp beats an expensive
one you are afraid to use. Single bevel is nice for bark work. A utility
blade is fine for chip-buds if you snap to a fresh edge every few cuts.
Fig wood is soft and stringy. A dull edge crushes the cambium into a wet
smear. That smear does not knit. I hone before the session and I stop
the session when I start tearing.

Alcohol on the blade between trees. Not between every cut on the same
clean stick — you will go mad — but between trees, and always after a
mottled one. Mosaic is not supposed to ride the knife the way a bacterium
does. Hygiene is still cheaper than a yard of matching yellow patterns.

Tape. Parafilm is the default for figs. Stretch it. Unstretched parafilm
is a ribbon. Stretched it becomes a clingy skin. On chip-buds I cover the
bud. That is the fig-specific habit. Apple people leave the bud naked.
Fig buds dry out like raisins if you leave them blinking at 8a wind.
Buddy tape works. PVC grafting tape works if you remember to cut it
before it girdles a summer flush.

Wax or seal. Doc Farwell's, beeswax mixes, even interior latex paint on
big stub faces. The job is to stop the stock from drying back and to
keep rain out of a cleft. Do not glob wax onto a bud you wanted to grow.
Do not use a seal that stays wet and sour in our humidity. I have seen
mayonnaise-thick goo hold water against a stub until the bark slipped
for the wrong reason.

Bags. A small zip bag or a perforated produce bag over the whole scion
for the first ten to twenty days on outdoor dormant grafts. Shade. A
bag in full April sun is a greenhouse that tops 120°F. White paper bag
on the outside of the plastic is an old trick that still works.

Labels. Metal or UV-rated tape, wired on *below* the union as well as
above. You will lose the above-union tag when you unwrap. You will not
remember. I have a whole row of "probably *Olympian*."

What I do not buy: omega tools before I can cut a saddle by hand, extra
hormones, colored waxes as a personality, a $40 "grafting station." The
union cares about cambium contact, moisture, and heat in a narrow band.
Everything else is a hobby inside the hobby.

Sharpen, stretch, shade, label. If the kit is bigger than a shoebox, you
are collecting, not grafting.
