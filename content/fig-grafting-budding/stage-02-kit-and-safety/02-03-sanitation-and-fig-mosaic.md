# Sanitation and fig mosaic

Fig mosaic is already in most collections. That is the unromantic
sentence. Leaves with yellow islands, rusted margins later in summer,
fruit that comes in small on a bad year. UC IPM: the virus moves with
grafts and cuttings, and with the fig mite *Aceria fici*. It is not
seed-borne. It is not a kitchen-knife virus the way tobacco mosaic can
be. You still do not use a mottled tree as your scion mother if you
have a choice.

Choice is the word. A lot of good figs show some mosaic and still feed
a family. *Black Mission* is a known complainer. *Kadota* often shrugs.
In 8a heat the pattern gets louder. I do not torch a tree for a few
yellow islands. I also do not copy a tree that is a yellow wreck in
August onto a clean *Celeste* stool I have been saving.

How I handle wood. I cut scions from the least symptomatic wood on a
tree I already trust for fruit. I do not take wood from a sickly
newcomer the first year it is in the yard. I watch it. If I must
propagate a symptomatic variety because it is the only stick, I put it
on a sacrificial understock at the edge, not in the middle of the
clean row.

Tools. Alcohol between trees. Clean bags. Do not store scions from a
yellow tree in the same damp paper as the clean ones. You are mostly
stopping mites and confusion, not performing surgery.

*Ficus palmata* shows up in the literature as immune, and seedlings
with palmata as a father sometimes get the same reputation. I treat
that as a research note, not a backyard cure. A palmata stool will not
scrub a virus out of the scion you just taped to it. If someone sold
you palmata as a nematode-and-virus force field, ask which clone, and
then still assume the scion is what it is.

Mites. They live in buds. A dirty bud is a dirty bud. I do not have a
home miticide program I trust enough to brag about. I do have a habit
of not fondling every fig in the neighborhood and then fondling mine.

The sanitation that actually changes outcomes in 8a is simpler than
virus theology: do not graft dry or moldy wood, do not graft a drowning
tree, do not leave fresh unions in blasting sun, and do not multiply a
plant you already regret. Mosaic is a reason to be picky about mothers.
It is not a reason to bleach the yard into sterility you will not
maintain.

If a grafted tree comes in ugly and stays ugly, replace it. There is
no spray. That is the part people skip in the forum thread. The knife
copies what the mother had; it does not scrub a virus off the blade.
