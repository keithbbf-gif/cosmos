# Chip-bud, the reliable one

If I could keep one fig method, it would be chip-bud. It works
on dormant wood and on green wood. It does not need bark slip.
It spends one bud. It forgives a diameter mismatch. Growers who
graft figs for a living of habit come back to this cut.

Two motions. On the stock: a short angled cut at the bottom of
the slot, then a slice down from above that meets it, lifting
out a chip. On the scion: the same two motions around a bud.
The fig bud sits in a thickened zone. A little rocking gets the
knife through without snapping the chip. Fit the chip into the
slot. If the slot is small, recut it longer or wider. If it is
huge, you left too much air; start a tighter slot.

Do not thumb the inner face. Slide the chip with the knife.
Cambium contact is the edges and the base. A perfect cosmetic
fit is less important than a chip that sits and a wrap that
holds.

Wrap the bud. Stretch parafilm over it. On apples you leave
the eye out. On figs the eye dries. Cover it. It will punch
through when it is ready, or you will slit the film over the
eye after you trust the knit — I usually wait until I see
green or until the next spring on a late summer bud.

I start on the stock so the chip does not wait in the wind.
Jsacadura's note from the fruit boards is the one I steal:
even failed chips have often fused; they fail later from
dry or from bad wood. So I treat dehydration as the real
opponent, not alignment mysticism.

In 8a I chip-bud from first swell into early summer, and
again in a careful August if I am banking a bud. Morning.
Shade on the west. Water the stock yesterday.

This is how you turn the bottom bud of a rare cutting into a
second plant while the top of the cutting sits in water. That
trick alone has saved more varieties in my yard than every
cleft I have ever cut.

Practice on *Brown Turkey*. It is thick-barked and common.
When your chips sit without a gap and your wrap does not
look like a mummy fight, use the good wood.

A 8a morning will give you twenty good chips. A 8a afternoon
will give you five and a headache. Stop when the latex
smears and the knife starts tearing the thickened bud zone.
That tear is how you snap the only bud on a mailed stick.
If the wood is rare, cut the stock slot first, then one
careful chip, then wrap before you admire it. Admiration
is for after the film is on.

If a chip rocks in the slot, recut the slot. Do not mash
it flat with tape and call the mash contact. Mash is
bruised cambium. Bruised cambium is a brown chip in ten
days.
