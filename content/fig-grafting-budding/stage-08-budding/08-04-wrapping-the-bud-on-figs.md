# Wrapping the bud on figs

This is the small habit that changes take rates. Cover the
bud. Stretch the film over it. Figs lose water through a
naked eye faster than people who learned on apples expect.

I use half-inch or one-inch parafilm, pulled until it
clouds and clings. Start below the chip, spiral up, two
layers over the bud, finish above. You want contact, not a
cocoon the size of a lemon. Air under loose film is a
dead space that cooks.

On green-wood chips I am religious about this. The petiole
stub goes under the film too. When the knit happens, that
petiole often yellows and comes off with the film when you
lift it — a nicer tell than guessing. Then I often
re-cover the bud lightly if the weather is still a furnace
and the bud has not moved.

On dormant chips in March I still cover. I slit or let the
bud punch when I see swell, or I wait if the bud is sitting
until next year. A late-August chip is a next-year bud
until proven otherwise. Leaving it covered through winter
is fine if the film is not girdling. Check in March.

Rubbers alone are not enough outdoors here. They hold. They
do not stop wind. Rubber plus film is what I use on T-buds
whose flaps want to open.

Do not use electrical tape over the eye and forget it. I
have girdled a flush that took, then swelled, then
strangled in a black ring I was too lazy to cut. Set a
phone reminder for three weeks and for six. Unwrap is a
date, not a vibe.

The wrap is not the union. A poor chip in a perfect wrap
is still a poor chip. A good chip in a loose wrap is a
raisin. Do the cut, then dress it like you meant the
second half of the job.

If you remember nothing else from the budding stage: on
figs the bud wears a shirt. Take the shirt off only when
the weather, and the knit, say so.

Stretch test: if the film does not go cloudy, you have
not stretched it. Unstretched parafilm is a ribbon that
leaks. I can spot my early wraps in old photos. They look
like gifts. Gifts dry out.

In a dry north wind I add a second, looser wrap as a
windbreak and take that one off first at vent time. In a
still, humid week I do not. Extra film in a swamp week
is a mold house. Match the shirt to the weather, not to
a habit you liked in March.
