# T-bud only when the bark slips

T-bud is the method people know from roses and peaches. On
figs it is optional. The bark is thick. The wood likes to
stay attached to the bud shield. You often end up cutting a
chip anyway and then also prying a T open. At that point
you did chip-bud with extra wounds.

I still use a T when the bark is slipping like wet paper and
I want to tuck a bud into a thick young stem without cutting
a deep slot. Vertical cut, cross cut, lift the flaps, slide
the bud in from the top, flap down, wrap. Some people push
from the side because fig bark is tough. Side insertion is
a fig habit, not a gift you should take to an apricot.

No slip, no T. Forcing flaps on tight bark shreds cambium
and the flaps dry open. That is a failed T that looks busy.

Wrap firmer than a chip-bud. The flaps want to spring. A
grafting rubber plus parafilm is belt and suspenders. Cover
the bud in 8a wind.

An ISHS trial T-budded a list of cultivars on *Brown Turkey*
seedlings in the last week of August and got takes, with
*Brown Turkey* on *Brown Turkey* near the top and *Golden
Celeste* nearer the bottom. Two years on, no incompatibility
showed. That is useful. It is not a command to T-bud in
August on a west wall at 4 p.m. Their nursery was not your
driveway.

I teach chip-bud first. I show T-bud so you can recognize
it and so you have it when slip is perfect and your chip
slots have been ugly that morning. I do not keep T-bud as
my pride method. Pride methods are how you ignore a better
cut.

If the T takes, you still have to cut the stock back above
the bud when you want the bud to run. Leave a stub to tie
to. Rub other sprouts. Same aftercare as a chip. The T is
only the pocket. The summer is still the summer.

I use T-bud most on a young, smooth *LSU* or *Turkey* stem
in late April when I can lift flaps with a fingernail. I
do not use it on old rough bark, on a drought week, or on
dormant mailed wood. Those three are chip-bud or wait.

If the flaps dry open by evening, the wrap was theater.
Redo it the same day if the bud still looks plump. If the
bud already dulled, that pocket is done. Move an inch over
and chip-bud. Do not stack wounds on the same line like
you are writing a novel on the bark.
