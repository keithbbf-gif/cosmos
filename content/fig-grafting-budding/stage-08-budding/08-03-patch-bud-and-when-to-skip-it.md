# Patch-bud and when to skip it

Patch-bud is a rectangle of bark-with-bud swapped for a
rectangle of bark on the stock. It is a pecan method, a
walnut method, a thick-bark method. Figs have thick bark.
In theory we should all be out there cutting postage stamps.

In practice I skip it on most 8a backyard jobs. The knife
work is four cuts that have to match. The patch dries at
the edges while you are still admiring the geometry. The
stock rectangle has to come off clean, which wants good
slip. If you have good slip, a bark graft or a T is faster.
If you do not have slip, a chip-bud does not care.

I will patch when the stock is a smooth, thick, young
trunk — think a one-inch *LSU* whip — and the only bud I
have sits on equally thick wood, and I am in the mood to
go slow. I cut the stock window first, keep the patch
moist, cut the bud patch a hair larger rather than
smaller, seat it, wrap so every edge is closed. Cover
the bud.

Failure is an edge that lifts. Once an edge lifts in our
wind, the whole stamp goes tan. There is no "mostly
seated" patch. It is seated or it is art.

People invent patch-bud because they saw a diagram. The
diagram does not mention 8a May wind or the fact that fig
latex will glue your rectangle to the knife. If you want
to learn it, learn it on a variety you do not love, in
the morning, with slip. Then go back to chip-bud for the
wood you cannot replace.

Skip it entirely if your hands shake, if the bark is
rough and old, if the day is dry and over 80°F, or if
you are converting a freeze-killed stool. Those jobs have
better chapters. Patch-bud is a specialist cut looking
for a problem. Chip-bud is already the answer to most of
the problems you actually have.

If you still want the postage stamp, cut three practice
windows on a prune pile and time yourself. If a window
takes more than a minute, the good bud will dry in your
fingers. Work in pairs: one person cuts, one person
wraps. Solo patch-bud on a hot 8a morning is how you
invent a reason to dislike a method that was already
optional.

I have one successful patch on a smooth *Purple* whip. I
also have a row of tan rectangles from the same week. The
success did not make more fruit than the chip-buds next
to it. It made a story. Stories are for the shed wall,
not for the rare stick.
