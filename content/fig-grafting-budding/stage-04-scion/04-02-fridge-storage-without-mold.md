# Fridge storage without mold

The fridge is a wet box that also dries things out. That is the whole
problem. Fig scions want to stay dormant, cool, and barely damp. They
do not want a sealed bag of condensation and they do not want a paper
towel that went crispy on day four.

I wrap wood in a barely damp paper towel — wrung out until it does not
drip — then into a bag left a little open or punched with a hole. Or
I use slightly moist sphagnum, same rule: not a swamp. Label outside
and in. Ink runs.

Temperature. The crisper is usually fine. I do not store scions next
to apples if I can help it. Ethylene is not a myth. A beer fridge at
the back of the garage is better than the kitchen fridge that gets
opened for leftovers at 10 p.m. and hits 50°F every night.

Check every two weeks. That is the habit people skip. You are looking
for white fuzz, sour smell, bark that has slipped in storage, buds
that swelled and then collapsed. Throw those sticks. Do not "try
them anyway" on a tree you like. The fridge is cheap. The understock
is not.

If the towel is dry, a sprinkle. If the bag is sweating, open it and
replace the towel. Mold in 8a refrigerators is not a moral failure.
It is July humidity living in your kitchen. You just do not get to
graft it.

How long. December wood into March is normal. December wood into May
is a longer bet. I have done it for late bark grafts. The last third
of the bag is often the weakest. Use the best sticks first.

Do not freeze them in the icebox. Do not leave them in the door.
Do not store them with cut onions and then wonder about the smell
test. If you need a smell test, you already waited too long.

When I take wood out to graft, I keep it in a shade bucket with a
damp cloth, not in my back pocket and not on the hood of the car.
A scion can die between the fridge and the cut. That death looks
like a perfect graft that never wakes up. You will blame your
technique. It was the hood of the car.

Power flickers in 8a ice weeks. If the beer fridge dies and the
garage hits 60°F for two days, the buds will swell in the bag.
Use that wood immediately as chip-buds or throw it. Do not put
it back on ice and pretend it is still January. Swollen-then-chilled
buds are mush. I have grafted that mush. It looks like wood and
behaves like cooked greens.

A paper in the bag with the variety and the cut date is worth
more than a memory of "the good purple one." Ink. Not a vibe.
