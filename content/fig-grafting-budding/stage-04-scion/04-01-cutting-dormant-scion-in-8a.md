# Cutting dormant scion in Zone 8a

The best wood in this climate is last summer's growth, pencil thick,
after the tree has actually gone to sleep. Not the giant water-sprout
as thick as your thumb. Not the stringy interior twig. A firm, well-
lighted shoot with plump buds and a pith that is white, not brown.

I cut after a real cold spell, leaves gone, usually late December
through January. If I wait until the first swell in a mild 8a February,
I am already late for storage — the buds have started to think about
work. I can still use that wood the same week. I cannot bag it for
six weeks and expect it to stay polite.

Length. Six to eight inches for whip and cleft. Two or three good
buds is enough. For chip-bud you only need one decent bud and a
little bark around it. People mail postage-stamp wood. It works if
it is alive.

Eisen wanted two-year wood. I have used two-year wood on a bark
graft when that was what the tree offered. For weekday work I want
one-year wood. It lines up. It wraps. It does not have a hollow
highway of pith the size of a drinking straw.

Avoid: wood that already tried to fruit and still holds a dried
syconium. Avoid wood from the deep shade. Avoid the last sad green
stick on a tree that mottled itself to lace in August, unless that
tree is the whole point.

Cut on a dry day if you can. Wet wood in a bag is a mushroom farm.
Label in the field, not on the kitchen counter "when I get inside."
You will not remember which bag was the good *Celeste* and which was
the neighbor's unnamed purple.

Leave the mother tree able to be a tree. Do not strip a young plant
for scions. Take a few laterals. If you only have a young plant, you
should be rooting, not opening a scion shop.

Get the wood into the fridge the same day. A stick on the dashboard
of a truck in January sun is a dried stick. I have killed good wood
on the ride home because I was pleased with myself and the bag sat
on a speaker in the window.

If a hard night is coming and you still need a tender variety, cut
*before* the night. Insurance is wood in a bag, not hope on a branch.
The branch may be tan on Thursday. The bag does not care.
