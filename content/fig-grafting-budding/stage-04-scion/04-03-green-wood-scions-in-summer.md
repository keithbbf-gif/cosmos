# Green-wood scions in summer

Dormant wood is the weekday fuel. Summer wood is what you use when the
variety showed up in June in a padded envelope, or when you decide in
the same week you taste a fruit that you want it on the other tree.

Green fig wood is a leaky pipe. It wants to wilt. It has leaves you
must deal with. It has a bud at the leaf axil that is your whole
future. I chip-bud it. I do not try a long whip with a leafy stick
and a prayer in July.

Cut in the morning. Take a current-season shoot that has started to
firm — not the floppy inch of brand-new tip, not a brown woody base
unless that is all you have. Strip the leaf blade. Leave a short
petiole if you like; on figs that petiole often yellows and pops off
when the bud takes, which is a nice tell. Some people cut the leaf
flush. Both work if the wrap is honest.

Use it the same hour. Green wood in a bag on a dashboard is cooked
protein. If I must wait, the bag goes in a cooler with a cold pack
*not touching* the wood, and I still try to be done by lunch.

Wrap the bud. This is not optional in 8a summer. Parafilm over the
bud, stretched, then shade. I have lost otherwise pretty chip-buds
because I left the bud naked "like apples" and the west wind sanded
them dead in two days.

Expect the bud to sit. A June chip-bud may not flush until next
spring. That is a take. Do not unwrap on day eight because you are
lonely. If the chip stays green and the petiole drops clean, you
are in the game. If the chip blackens, you are not.

I do not green-graft onto a drought-stressed tree. The stock should
be watered the day before, not drowning, just not crispy. A stressed
fig in August will shed your bud to save itself. I also do not
green-graft the week of a forecast 100°F unless the work lives under
cloth.

Summer wood is how you multiply a taste before you forget which tree
it was. It is not kinder than winter wood. It is just available. Treat
it like fish: fresh, cold, and cooked the same day — except you are
not cooking it. You are refusing to.

If the mail brings green wood, open it in the shade. A padded
envelope on an 8a porch for three hours is an oven. I have had
"fresh" June wood arrive already wilted. Wilted wood can still
give you a chip if the bud is firm and you wrap it in the next
twenty minutes onto a watered pot under cloth. It cannot give you
a long whip. Do not turn a wilted stick into a project. Turn it
into one honest chip and a glass of water for whatever is left.

And do not take green wood off a drought-crispy mother. She is
already spending water. Cut after a drink, in the morning, or
wait for a better week. The variety will still be there.
