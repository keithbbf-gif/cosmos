# Pocket checklist — cut day

The morning you actually cut the tree. Read this once
on the tailgate before the first heading.

**Stock.** Alive? Scratch it. Moving, not shooting,
for dormant work? Slipping, if you came to bark-graft
or T-bud? Watered yesterday, not drowning today?
West face — do you have paint and shade for a heading?

**Scion.** Still fridge-cold? Plump? No fuzz? In a
shade bucket, not on the hood? Best sticks first.

**Method.** Pencil on pencil: whip or saddle. Pencil
on inch-plus: cleft. Thick stub and slip: bark. One
bud: chip. No slip and you wanted a T: chip anyway.
You do not invent a new geometry because the stub is
awkward.

**Hands.** Gloves if latex bothers you. Glasses if
you are heading. Sharp knife. Alcohol between trees.
Do not thumb the faces.

**The cut.** Long faces. Cambium on the rim, not a
pith kiss. One edge lined up on a mismatch. Fast
enough that the wind does not tan the face.

**The dress.** Wrap like you mean it. Cover fig buds.
Seal tips and stub faces. Close the cleft chimney.
Bag if the wind is out. White bag or cloth if the
sun can see it. Stake planned, even if the stake
goes in next week.

**The plant.** Sap-drawer left on a big heading?
Suckers you can see? Union visible? Label below the
union as well as above? Date on the label?

**The weather.** Late frost in the seven-day? Heat
over 80 with a clear bag on the west? If yes, change
the plan, not the hope.

**The stop.** Dull knife, tired hands, or a tree you
are angry at — stop. Grief conversions and pride
sessions are how good stools get ugly stubs.

I have done a cut day without this card. It looks
like four unions and one missing label and a bag I
meant to put on. The card is shorter than the repair.

Count the unions before you put the knife away.
Count the labels. If the numbers differ, fix it
in the driveway, not in June. Take one photo of
the whole tree from the path so you can see bags
and drawers later when the leaves hide them.

Water can wait an hour. Shade cannot, if you just
headed a west stub at noon. Do the cloth first.
Drink water second. The plant was already thirsty
yesterday if you did the day-before drink. Today
is about not cooking the new faces.

If a neighbor stops to talk, bag the open scion
before you be polite. Conversation kills more
faces than a dull knife. The neighbor will
understand a raised hand. The scion will not.
