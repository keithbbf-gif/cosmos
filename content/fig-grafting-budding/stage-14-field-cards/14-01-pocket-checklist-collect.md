# Pocket checklist — collect

This is the card I want in the jacket when I walk out
to cut wood. It is not a poem. It is the list I skip
when I am cold and sure of myself.

**Before you leave the door.** Bags. A marker that
writes on wet plastic. A knife you already honed.
Alcohol wipe. Labels you can read next March, not a
scrap of envelope. A cooler if the car will sit.

**The tree.** Is this a mother you trust? Any mosaic
that makes you wince? Any fruit you actually liked?
If you cannot answer, do not strip it. Take a note
and come back after a season.

**The wood.** One-year, pencil, well lighted, plump
buds, pith not brown. Not the giant sucker. Not the
stringy interior. Not the stick with a dried fruit
still glued on. Cut more than you need and throw the
worst in the kitchen, not in the fridge.

**The day.** Dry if you can. After a real cold spell,
leaves gone. If the buds are already swelling, you
are cutting for this week's grafts, not for a six-week
nap in the crisper.

**The cut.** Clean. Do not peel the mother. Leave it
a plant. Get the wood off the dashboard. Shade. Same
day into barely damp paper, bag with a little air,
crisper or beer fridge. Label in and out. Variety,
date, tree.

**The fridge.** Not next to the apples if you have a
choice. Not the door. Check in two weeks. Throw fuzz.
Rewet a crispy towel. Open a sweating bag.

**The forecast.** If a tender variety is about to see
a brutal night, cut *before* the night. Insurance is
wood in a bag. Hope is not a storage method.

I have skipped this card and brought home a soup of
unlabeled sticks that all looked purple in January
light. I have also brought home perfect wood and
cooked it on the speaker of the truck. The checklist
is not for other people. It is for the version of me
that is pleased with himself.

If you only do one thing on this card, label in the
field. Everything else you can almost fake. The name
you cannot.

Last checks I skip when my hands are cold: wipe the
blade before the next tree, do not set the bag on
wet ground, do not cut from the shaded water-sprout
because it is easy to reach. Easy wood is often bad
wood. Walk around.

If you are collecting for someone else, write your
yard and the year on the bag. "Celeste" from a humid
8a stool is not the same sentence as "Celeste" from
a dry-west patio. The next person deserves the
climate note, not just the name. I want that note
when wood comes to me, so I try to send it out.
