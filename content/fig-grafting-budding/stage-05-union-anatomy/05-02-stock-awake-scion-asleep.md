# Stock awake, scion asleep

The old rule is still the right rule for dormant work: the understock
should be starting to move, the scion should still be thinking it is
January. The stock has the roots and the water. The scion has the
buds you do not want to spend before the pipes connect.

In 8a that usually means fridge wood onto a tree whose buds are
swelling in March or early April. If both are fully dormant, callus
is slow and a dry wind wins. If both are flushing, the scion tries
to be a plant on a battery and dies when the battery ends.

You can feel the stock wake up. The bark gets a little livelier. A
pruning cut wetter. Buds fat. Not leaves — leaves are late for a
classic cleft, though chip-buds still work. I would rather be a week
late with a waking stock than a week early with a frozen one.

The scion stays asleep because you stored it cold. If you left the
bag on the counter overnight and the buds pushed, you no longer have
dormant wood. You have a small plant with no roots. Use it the same
morning as a chip-bud and wrap it like summer wood, or throw it.

Bark grafts *require* an awake stock. No slip, no pocket. That is
why they sit later on the 8a calendar than whip grafts. T-buds same
story. Chip-buds do not care as much, which is why they cover the
awkward weeks.

People invert this. They graft fridge wood onto a stock that is
still winter-tight because they are excited on a Saturday. The
Saturday is not a phenological stage. Put the wood back in the
fridge. Come back when the tree says so.

Once the union takes and the scion breaks, the rule flips. Now you
want the scion awake and the competing stock buds asleep — meaning
you rub the stock buds off. The plant will feed what is growing.
If what is growing is a sucker below the tape, that is what you
built.

I write this every year on the lid of the scion box: stock moving,
scion not. It is the whole timing chapter in seven words. The rest
is weather.

A mild 8a February will tempt you. The *Celeste* swells. You have
Saturday. The scions have been in the fridge since New Year's. That
can be a legal day if the stock is truly moving and you can cover
a late frost. It is an illegal day if you are grafting because you
are bored and the stock is still tight on the north side. Walk the
whole tree. The south face lies first.

If you already cut and the stock was too asleep, you cannot unsaw
a stub. Bag extra well, keep the scions from cooking on the first
warm afternoon, and do not add four more headings "to use the wood."
The wood will keep. The stubs will not grow back for next Saturday.
