# The cambium line and the hollow pith

The only line that matters is a thin wet green just under the bark.
That is cambium. You are trying to stand that line against the same
line on the other piece. Miss it by a sixteenth and you can still
get a take if they cross somewhere. Miss it on purpose and you have
a decorated stick.

Figs make this fiddly because the pith is big. A fat cutting is a
tube with a rim of real wood. If you cut a wedge that is mostly
pith, you have a sponge. Eisen wanted one face of the wedge to show
pith and that face to look inward. The useful half of that sentence:
do not hang the scion by pith. Hang it by the rim. The outer face
of a cleft scion should show a full cambium line kissing the stock's
cambium. The inner face can be uglier.

On a chip-bud the pith question almost goes away. You take a slice
of bark-plus-wood with the bud, and a matching slot. The chip should
not be a flake of bark with no wood, and it should not be a chunk
of heartwood with a bud on top. A little wood. A lot of cambium
edge.

Alignment. People obsess over matching both sides of a whip. One
side perfect is enough if the wrap holds. On a diameter mismatch,
line up one edge and let the other hang. Do not center a thin scion
in a fat cleft so it sits in pith-to-pith nothing.

The latex will wet the cut. Wipe, work fast, do not lick. A cut that
sits in the air for five minutes in an April wind is a different cut
than the one you made. Cover it.

You cannot see cambium from across the yard. You can see it if you
look. If your eyes are older than they were, wear the cheap glasses.
I have made beautiful wedges that missed the line because I was
proud of the wedge.

Callus is not the union, but it is the first honest sign. A white
to cream lip at the edges after a couple of warm weeks means the
cells are talking. A dry brown line means they are not. Do not pick
at it to check. You will peel the conversation off.

Hollow pith is why fig grafts dry out faster than apple grafts of
the same diameter. Seal the tip of the scion. Seal the stub face.
Wrap the seam. You are closing a tube, not wrapping a twig.
