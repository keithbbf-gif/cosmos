# Diameter mismatch — what to do

Matching diameters is a luxury. The tree you want to convert is two
inches. The scion in the bag is a pencil. The cutting you rooted is
skinny. The only bud you have is on wood the size of a chopstick.
You still graft. You just pick the method that forgives the mismatch.

Pencil on pencil: whip, saddle, omega if you own the tool and both
pieces are round enough. This is the pretty union. It also is not
the usual 8a conversion job.

Pencil on a one-inch stub: cleft. One or two wedges on the edges,
cambium lined up on the outside, not floating in the pith. Two
scions on a stub is insurance. Later you keep the better one.

Pencil on a two-to-four-inch stub: bark graft when the bark slips.
You are not splitting a log to impress anyone. You are tucking
scions under bark like letters under a door.

Tiny bud on anything: chip-bud. The chip carries its own cambium
rectangle. The stock slot is cut to fit. This is how you spend a
rare bud on a thick trunk without pretending you have a matching
stick.

Scion thicker than stock: do not. Root the thick scion as a cutting
if it will, or cut a thinner lateral off it. Forcing a fat scion
onto a thin pot plant is how you snap the plant and then blame the
tape.

Omega tools lie when diameters differ. The cut is cute and the
cambiums miss. If they are not the same size, put the tool down
and cut a saddle or a whip by hand so you can choose which edge
lines up.

Wrap more, not less, on a mismatch. There is more open face. There
is more pith showing. There is more wind getting in. Seal the stub
top. Shade.

In a year the diameters will not matter. The union will bulge and
look like a knuckle and then it will look like a tree. The first
month is the only time the mismatch is allowed to kill you. Line
up one edge. Pick the method that matches the stub you have. Do
not invent a new geometry because the video used twins.

A practical 8a habit: keep a few pencil understocks in pots just
so you have twins when a matching scion arrives in the mail. The
fancy conversion on the big tree can wait a year. The matching
whip in a bucket can happen this week. People skip this because
the big tree is the one they stare at. The pot is the one that
lives.

If all you have is a fat stub and a skinny stick, bark or cleft
at the rim. Say it out loud before you cut so you do not drift
into a centered pith-to-pith "it looks even." Even is not the
goal. Contact is.
