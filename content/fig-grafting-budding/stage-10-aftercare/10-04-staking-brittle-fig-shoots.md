# Staking brittle fig shoots

A fig scion that takes will often grow like it is late
for something. Two feet of soft wood on a union that is
still a hinge. Then a storm, or a bird, or you brushing
past with a hose, and the shoot is hanging by bark.

Stake before that day. A bamboo or a leftover tomato
stake, in the ground, not jammed into the cleft. Tie the
new shoot in a figure-eight so it cannot saw against the
stake. Soft tie. Check after a week of growth. The shoot
will outgrow the first tie while you are thinking about
something else.

Cleft and bark grafts are the worst hinges. Whip unions
on matching wood are better and still not a reason to
skip a stake if the flush is long and the site is windy.
Chip-buds that explode into a single wand need a stake
as soon as they are a foot long.

Do not tie so tight you girdle. Do not use thin wire
against tender bark. I have a scar collection that is
only wire.

In 8a the spring storms are the usual thief. The summer
afternoon wind is the second. If your yard has a west
fetch, stake lower and add a second tie. If the plant is
in a pot, stake to the pot or the pot will tip and the
union will teach you about leverage.

A broken flush is not always a dead graft. If the break
is above the union and there is a bud left, it may
reshoot. If the break is through the union, you start
over on fresh wood. Clean the stub. Do not leave a
hanging strip to rot.

I stake the ones I care about and I pretend the others
are "sturdy." The sturdy ones are the ones I find on the
ground. Stake the ones you care about.

Old advice said to drive a large stake near each grafted
branch and train the new growth to it. That sentence has
survived a century because fig wood has not gotten less
brittle. Believe the century.

A 8a thunderstorm will find the unstaked wand. If you
cannot stake the day it runs, stake the day after. I
have told myself "tomorrow" and met a hinge on the
ground. The union can still be alive. The season's
shape is not.

When you tie, leave a little slack for thickening. A
tight first tie is a girdle in two weeks on a happy
LSU. Check on water days — you are already there. Two
jobs, one walk.

Remove the stake the second summer if the knuckle has
wood. Leaving a stake forever is how you grow a tree
that cannot stand in a storm without furniture.
