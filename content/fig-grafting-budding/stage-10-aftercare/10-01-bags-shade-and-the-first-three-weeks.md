# Bags, shade, and the first three weeks

The cut is a morning. The take is a month. Most fig grafts
that fail in 8a fail in the first three weeks from dryness
or from cooking, not from a rootstock curse on the forum.

A bag around the scion keeps the wind off and the humidity
up. A small zip bag, a produce bag with holes, a paper bag
over plastic on hot sites. White on the outside if the sun
can see it. Clear bag on a west stub at 2 p.m. is a death
sentence you can measure with a dashboard thermometer.

Shade cloth at 30–50 percent over a new heading is enough.
You are not building a greenhouse. You are breaking the
afternoon. Take it off when the scions are clearly running
and the stub has paint.

Water the plant, not the union. A dry fig will steal from
the scion. A flooded fig will sit in cold mud and sulk.
The day before grafting I water. The week after I check
the pot or the basin. I do not mist the tape for luck.

Do not peek every afternoon. Lifting the bag daily to
"check" is how you dry the scion and congratulate yourself
for vigilance. A glance through clear film is allowed. A
weekly lift on a cool morning is allowed. A fidget is not.

Birds and bags: a hanging bag is a toy. If you have jays,
cage the stub or use paper that does not flash. I have
lost a cleft to curiosity that was not mine.

The first three weeks you are also on sucker watch. Soft
green shoots below the union come off with a thumb. If you
wait until they harden, you are doing woodwork near a
fresh seam. Do the thumb work.

If a late frost is coming, the bag stays on and you add a
jug or a sheet for the night. If a 90°F day is coming, you
add shade or you vent. The bag is a tool, not a vow. Change
it when the weather changes.

At three weeks I want to see a scion that is still plump
and maybe a lip of callus. I do not need a parade of
leaves. Leaves can wait. Plump is the first paycheck.

Rain can sit in a bag and sour. After an 8a soaker I tip
the bag and drain it. I do not take the bag off in the
storm to "let it breathe." The storm is already breathing
on everything else. A little water in the corner is fine.
A puddle against the union is not.

If you use a paper outer bag, replace it when it turns to
felt. Felt does not shade. It holds a wet smell and then
tears onto the scion. I keep three spare paper bags in
the tote for that reason, not because I am organized,
because I have watched felt happen.
