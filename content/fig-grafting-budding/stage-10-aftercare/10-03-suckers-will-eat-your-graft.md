# Suckers will eat your graft

A fig that has been headed or wounded answers with
sprouts. They come from below the union, from the stool,
from the roots a yard away. They are the original
variety. They are vigorous. They are why a perfect April
graft is a *Chicago Hardy* bush again by July.

This is not a once-a-month job in May. This is a
walk-by-with-your-thumb job. Soft shoots rub off. Woody
shoots get a clean cut and will try again. If you cannot
walk the tree, do not build a cocktail on it.

Know the union. A painted ring or a visible knuckle. If
you cannot see the union, you will spare a sucker
because it "looks like the good one." It always looks
like the good one. It is closer to the roots. It is
hungrier.

On a freeze-killed conversion the stool may throw ten
sprouts. Keep the grafted ones plus one spare original
if you still need insurance. Remove the rest. A forest
of sprouts shades the scions and spends the roots.

Do not spray suckers with a herbicide next to a fresh
union. I have watched that idea travel. It travels to
regret. Use your hands.

A sucker you want as an inarch or as a replacement
scaffold is a sucker you label *the day you decide to
keep it*. Unlabeled keepers become the unlabeled forest
again.

In 8a the first flush of suckers is April–June. A second
flush shows up after summer water. Walk it after storms.
Walk it after you irrigate. The plant thinks it is rich.

If you travel in May, ask someone specific to rub
specific trees. "Watch the figs" is not a task. "Rub
the green shoots under the tape on the three labeled
stools" is a task.

The graft is not done when the scion leaves. The graft
is done when the scion is the strongest head on the
plant. Until then the stool is trying to fire you.

Root suckers a yard from the trunk are still the same
plant. They look like free figs. They are the original
name, and they spend the same roots your scion wanted.
I dig or cut them when I see them, unless I need one as
an inarch or a new bush. A ring of "free" *Chicago Hardy*
around a converted tree is a pretty way to starve the
conversion.

If the union is buried in mulch, rake a window once a
week in May. Out of sight is how a sucker becomes a
stem you will not have the heart to cut in July because
it already has fruit. Heart is a bad weeder. A rake is
better.
