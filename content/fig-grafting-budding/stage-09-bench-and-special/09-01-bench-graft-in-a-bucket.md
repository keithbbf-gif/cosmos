# Bench graft in a bucket

Some of the best 8a unions never see the wind in March. You
do them at a table, on potted understock or on hardwood
cuttings, and you keep the weather in a bucket.

Understock is a pot that already has roots. Whip or saddle
or chip-bud at the height you want. Wrap. Slip the top into
a tall clear bag with a couple of holes, or stand the pot
in a plastic tote with a lid cracked. Bright shade. Not a
south window that hits 100°F at noon. An east window or a
shop light in a garage that stays above 50°F is enough.

Callus likes warmth without cooking. I aim for the 60s and
low 70s. If the bag sweats into puddles, vent. If the scion
wrinkles, your humidity is low or your wood was tired.

You can bench-graft in February while the yard is still
mean. That is the point. By the time the late frost is
done, you have a plant that already decided to live. You
harden it like a tomato: more air, then a shaded porch,
then the bed.

I do not bench-graft twenty plants in a sealed tote and
leave for a week of travel. I have opened that tote. It is
a mushroom museum. Daily glance. Vent. Pull anything that
smells.

This is also where omega tools earn their keep — matching
potted cuttings, same die, same day. Outdoors they are the
same clown show as in the saddle piece; indoors they are fine.

Label the pot, not just the wrap. Wraps come off. A row of
unlabeled bench grafts is a quiz you will fail in June.

When you plant out, bury the union a little or leave it
visible? I leave it visible for the first year so I can
see suckers. I will not argue with people who bury for
winter. In 8a a visible union plus mulch has been enough
for me. A buried union on a plant I forget how to read
is how the understock takes the plant back.

The bucket is not a miracle. It is a smaller climate. Use
it when March is a liar and you still want to cut wood.

Heat is the indoor killer. A closed tote in a sunroom in
late February in 8a will still cook. I use a cheap thermometer
in the tote. If it says 85°F, the lid comes off and the
tote moves. If you do not own a thermometer, put your hand
in. If you do not want your hand there, the scion does not
want to be there either.

When you harden off, do it on the east porch, not the west
driveway. The first outdoor afternoon can undo a month of
callus. Three days of morning sun and afternoon shade, then
you can start talking like the plant lives here.
