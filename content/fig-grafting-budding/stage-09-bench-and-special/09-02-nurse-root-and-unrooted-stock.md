# Nurse roots and unrooted stock

People graft a scion onto an unrooted cutting and stick the
whole thing in mix. Sometimes both ends callus. Sometimes
the bottom roots and the top dies. Sometimes the top pushes
and the bottom never roots and you have a bouquet. I treat
this as a stunt with a purpose, not as the orchard plan.

The purpose: you have one rare stick. You chip-bud a bud
onto a cheap unrooted *Chicago Hardy* cutting, and you also
root the rest of the rare stick. Two bets. The cheap
cutting might root and take the bud. The rare top might
root on its own. If both live you have two plants. If one
lives you still have the variety.

Nurse root: a rooted helper that feeds a scion while the
scion also tries to make its own roots, or while an
approach graft takes. In figs I use it rarely. Cuttings
root so easily that a nurse is usually extra theater. I
will approach-graft a weak scion onto a neighbor stem
while it still has its own pot, then cut it free. That is
a nurse with a pot.

Unrooted stock wants extra humidity and no sun. It has no
roots to drink with. You are asking a stick to knit and
root at the same time. In 8a that means a tote, not a
driveway. If you put unrooted grafts in the open bed in
April, you are running an evaporation experiment.

I mark unrooted experiments so I do not treat them like
established pots. They do not get a week of neglect. They
do not get fertilizer. They get a glance.

When the bottom roots and the union is still a maybe, do
not pot on hard or ship the plant. Wait until the scion
is clearly pulling. A rooted understock with a dead scion
is still a useful *Chicago Hardy*. Relabel it. Do not sell
it as the scion name. That mistake walks around yards for
years.

If this chapter feels like too many moving parts, skip it.
Root the cutting. Chip-bud onto a pot that already drinks.
The stunt is for the day the mail brings four inches of
something you will not see again. Most Saturdays are not
that day.

If you do run the stunt, write both names on the pot:
understock name and scion name. When only the bottom lives,
you will be tempted to keep the scion name because you want
the story to have worked. That is how mislabeled figs walk
the earth. I have been the person who did that. I have also
been the person who ate the wrong fig for two years and
called it a strain. Ink on the pot the morning the bottom
wins.

A heat mat under an unrooted graft is optional and easy to
abuse. Warm mix, not hot mix. If the pot is hotter than a
bath you would sit in, you are cooking roots that do not
exist yet.
