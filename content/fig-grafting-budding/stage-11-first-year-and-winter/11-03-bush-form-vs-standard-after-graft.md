# Bush form versus standard after a graft

The graft does not pick the form. You do. 8a has an
opinion: bushes forgive winter better than pretty single
trunks, because a bush has extra stems and a low crown
that can hide in mulch.

A standard — one trunk, a head at belt or chest height —
is nice to mow under and nice to graft onto later. It is
also one pipe. If January kills that pipe above the
union, you are back to a stool. If the union itself is
high and dies, you may still have understock sprouts at
the base. If the union is low and the whole trunk cooks,
you have a stump.

I keep insurance varieties and experimental scions as
bushes: three to five stems, unions visible, easy to
replace a stem without losing the plant. I keep a
standard when the variety is proven here and I want
shade or a clean walk.

After a bark graft on a big stub you already have a
standard's architecture. Train two or three scions into
scaffolds and stop imagining a bush. After chip-buds on
a stool, you can let two buds become a bush and remove
the rest.

Cocktail trees want scaffolds, not a twig pile. One
variety per scaffold if you can. A bush cocktail is a
tangle you will mis-prune.

Height of the union. Low unions hide in grass and get
hit by string trimmers. High unions are easy to see and
easy to freeze. I like unions at a height my eyes meet
without a circus, painted or labeled, with mulch below
and a trimmer that is not allowed to think.

In frost pockets I bias bush. On a south wall that never
sees 18°F I will let a standard exist. The form is a
climate decision dressed up as aesthetics.

Pick the form the month the scion runs, not the month
you are tired of staking. A wand you meant to be a bush
becomes a sail. A bush you meant to be a standard
becomes a thicket. The plant will not break the tie. You
have to.

Mowing and string-trimming decide form more than taste
does. If the trimmer owns the yard, a standard with a
clear trunk lasts longer than a bush with low unions.
If you will not cage the stool, do not put the union
where nylon can find it. I have inarched more trimmer
rings than I have had to repair winter cracks.

A cocktail tree in bush form is a dare. I will not stop
you. I will tell you that you will cut the wrong stem
in year two. Scaffolds with labels are how you do not
do that. Form is a filing system as much as it is a
shape.
