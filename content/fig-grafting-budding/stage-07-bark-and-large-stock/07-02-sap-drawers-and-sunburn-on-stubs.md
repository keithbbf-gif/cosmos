# Sap drawers and sunburn on stubs

A fig you just headed is a pale animal. The canopy was its
shirt. April and May in 8a will cook the southwest face of a
newly naked trunk. The bark goes tan, then depressed, then
dead in a strip. Scions on that stub can be perfect and still
fail because the stock face under them cooked.

Paint. Diluted interior white latex on the exposed trunk and
the stub face after you seal. Old growers tied straw around
the limbs. I have used cardboard on the west side for three
weeks like a cheap awning. Anything that breaks the afternoon
hit.

A sap-drawer is a live branch you did not cut. It keeps leaves
on the tree so the roots still have a job and the sap still
has a path that is not only your four scions. It also throws
a little shade on the stub if you choose the right one. You
shorten it when the scions are running. You remove it when
you trust them. If you leave it forever, it becomes the tree
again and your scions become jewelry.

How much to leave. On a big conversion I want at least one
modest live limb, or a tuft of sprouts I will manage. On a
small pot, a sap-drawer is often just a leaf or two below
the union — and then those have to be watched so they do not
win.

Sunburn and sap-drawers are the same chapter because they are
the same mistake: treating a heading as a haircut instead of
as surgery. The plant lost a lot of skin. Give it shade and
leave it a way to drink.

I have headed a *Celeste* in one go because I was angry at
sour fruit and the weather was mild. The unions took. The
west bark still scarred. The next winter that scar was a
weak line. Two-year topwork plus paint would have been
cheaper than my mood.

If you already have a brown west face: do not pick at it.
Shade what is left. Water. See what greens from above and
below. You may be grafting onto the live side next year
and writing off a strip. Figs compartmentalize better if
you stop making it worse.

The day you head a large fig in 8a, the knife is only half
the work. The other half is a can of leftover white paint
and the branch you did not cut.

A sap-drawer you forget to shorten becomes the joke of the
tree. Set a date on the label: "shorten drawer June 1" or
whenever the scions are clearly running. I have left drawers
because the fruit on them was easy. The scions stayed nubs.
Eat the drawer fruit one last time, then cut it. The new
heads need the job.

If you have no paint, a slurry of cheap outdoor white and
water is enough. Do not use dark stain. Do not use motor
oil, which I have been told in a parking lot is "old
school." Old school sunburn is still sunburn.
