# Bark graft when the stub is thick

A three-inch fig stub does not want to be cleft. You will split it
into firewood. Bark graft is the grown-up method: head the stub,
wait until the bark slips, slide scions between bark and wood,
tie, seal.

Slip is the gate. If the bark tears, you are early or the stub is
dead. Come back after a run of mild days. In much of 8a that is
April. In a cold pocket it can be later. The tree decides.

Head clean. A saw, then a knife to tidy the rim so you are not
leaving smashed bark. Vertical slits down from the cut face, an
inch or two, just through bark. Lift the corners with a bone
folder, a butter knife, a real grafting tool if you are fancy.
Do not shred the cambium on the wood face. That face is the
floor the scion sits on.

The scion gets a long single bevel — two inches is not too long —
and a short cut on the back to help it slide. Long face against
the wood. Slide it down until it seats. Two, three, four scions
around a big stub. They lean in slightly. You will thin later.

Tie so the bark cannot spring open. Then seal every bit of open
face: the stub top, the slits, the scion tips. A big stub is a
stump. Stumps dry. White paint on the remaining trunk bark if
the heading created a sun wall.

Leave a sap-drawer if you have one. Old fig advice is still
right: do not bald a large tree in one morning if you can leave
a live branch to pull sap. You remove it after the scions are
clearly running.

In 8a I shade the stub for the first heat wave. A piece of
cloth on two stakes. The stub you just created is paler than
the canopy you removed. Sunburn on fig bark is a real wound,
and wounds on a bark-graft stub are extra doors.

This is how you convert a sour *Brown Turkey* that is otherwise
healthy. It is also how you make a cocktail on an old stool
without pretending a cleft can do a log's job. Thick wood,
slipping bark, long bevels, honest seal. That is the whole
method. The rest is waiting without poking.

If the first slit crumbs, stop the job. You are early or the
stub is a corpse. Recheck life with a fingernail lower down.
I have bark-grafted a stub that was live on one side and dead
on the other. The live side took. The dead side was a science
project. Set extra scions on the live arc, not evenly around
a clock face that includes tan wood.

And do not stand on a ladder in a 20 mph 8a spring wind to
do delicate lifting of bark. Bring the work down. Head a
lower scaffold first. Pride on a ladder is how scions end up
in the grass.
