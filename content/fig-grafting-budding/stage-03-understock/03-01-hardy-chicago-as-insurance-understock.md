# Hardy Chicago as insurance understock

*Chicago Hardy* is not a personality. It is a stool that comes back. In
Zone 8a that is the whole argument for using it under something prettier
or later or more tender than you want to bet the winter on.

The top still dies when the top is tender. I have to say that twice
because the catalog will not. A 12°F night does not ask what the roots
are. It asks what the wood above the mulch is. The insurance is this:
in April you still have a live plant, and you can put the variety back
on if you saved scions, or you can let the stool be a *Chicago Hardy*
bush for a year and eat those figs instead of a hole in the ground.

I like *Chicago Hardy* understock for three jobs. One: a variety I am
trying, *Alma* or a late LSU, that may or may not like my cold corner.
Two: a cocktail tree where I accept that January may delete a head.
Three: a young plant I will keep in a pot for two winters before it
goes in the ground. The stool can take a garage night. The scion can
be replaced.

What I do not like it for: people who want a standard tree with a
clean trunk and no suckers. *Chicago Hardy* suckers. You will cut them.
If you wanted a single stem and a clean lawn, start with a less
generous variety as the understock, or keep the union high enough that
you can see the difference, and still walk it.

Vigor. *Chicago Hardy* is not weak. It will push a scion. It will also
push its own wood if you leave a bud below the tape. Set the union
where you can see it for the first two years. Paint a ring. I have
lost unions to my own forgetfulness, not to incompatibility. *Ficus
carica* on *Ficus carica* is not the hard part.

Hardiness theater: some 8a yards never need this. A protected *Celeste*
or *Brown Turkey* sails through most winters on its own roots. Use
*Chicago Hardy* where you actually have frost pockets, young plants, or
varieties you know are on the edge. Using it everywhere is fine. It is
just extra suckering for no gain on a south wall that never sees 18°F.

Collect scions of the tender variety *before* the bad night if the
forecast is ugly. The insurance policy is only good if you still have
the wood. A hardy stool with nothing to graft is just another fig, which
is not a tragedy, but it is not the plan you told yourself in February.
