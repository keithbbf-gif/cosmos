# Converting a freeze-killed top

This is the 8a grafting job that is not a hobby. January took the canopy.
March shows you a ring of green at the base and a lot of tan wood that
sounds hollow when you tap it. You can dig the plant. Or you can use the
roots you already paid for.

Wait until you can tell live from dead. Fingernail through the bark.
Green and damp is live. Brown and dry is dead. Figs fool you in the
first warm week. A stub that looks dead on March 10 sometimes greens on
April 1. If you head into dead wood and set scions there, you grafted
onto a stick.

Cut to live wood. If that means you are at eight inches above the soil,
you are at eight inches. If live wood is only a stool, you are doing
stool work: pick two or three strong sprouts when they are still
pencil-thick and whip or chip-bud onto those, rather than pretending
you still have a trunk.

If you still have a trunk of two inches or better, and it is live, you
can bark-graft once the bark slips. That is the fast way back to a
headed tree. Leave a sap-drawer if any live side branch remains. A
fig that has just lost a canopy is not in the mood to push every
calorie into four scions on a bald stump. One small live branch keeps
the engine running. You cut it later.

Sunburn. A newly naked fig trunk in April 8a sun cooks on the southwest
face. White interior latex, diluted, on the exposed bark. Straw or
shade cloth for a month if the stub is big. Old growers bundled straw
for a reason. I have used a scrap of white row cover and a zip tie.

Do not convert to five new varieties the morning you are sad about the
old one. Convert to one variety you already eat, or to the same variety
if you have wood in the fridge. Grief makes cocktail trees. Cocktail
trees made in grief get neglected in June.

Label the live sprouts you did *not* graft. Those are the original
roots talking. If the original was good, keep one as backup. If the
original was the reason you are converting, cut those sprouts while
they are soft. They will not stay soft.

A freeze-killed top is not a sign you should quit figs in 8a. It is the
ordinary tax on tender wood. The roots are the part that understood the
assignment. Put something on them that you are willing to lose again,
or put *Chicago Hardy* / *Celeste* up there and collect fewer surprises.
