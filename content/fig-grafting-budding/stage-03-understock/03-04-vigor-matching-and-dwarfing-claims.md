# Vigor matching and dwarfing claims

Someone will tell you there is a dwarfing fig rootstock. Treat that
like a man selling a quiet rooster. Figs are not apples. We do not
have a Malling series in the shed. What we have are varieties that
grow like weeds and varieties that sulk, and unions that pass that
behavior along unevenly.

If you graft a timid scion on a rocket stool, the scion often still
behaves like itself — until the stool's suckers shade it into
oblivion. If you graft a rocket on a timid stool, the rocket usually
wins and the stool just works harder. I have not seen a reliable
home-orchard dwarf from understock alone. I have seen pots, hard
pruning, and *Petite Negra* staying small because *Petite Negra* is
small.

Vigor matching matters more on a cocktail tree than on a single
conversion. Put *LSU Purple* and *Violette de Bordeaux* on one trunk
and you are signing up to pinch the LSU. Put two LSUs together and
they will fight, but they will fight in the same language. Read the
summer growth, not the catalog's "compact."

Claims to watch:

- "*Petite Negra* roots will dwarf anything." Maybe the roots stay
  modest. The scion may still try to be a tree. You will prune.
- "Wild fig roots make a smaller tree." Sometimes they make a weaker
  tree. Weak is not dwarf. Weak is a plant that sulks in July.
- "Seedling stock is more vigorous." Sometimes. Sometimes it is
  crooked and slow. You will not know until year three.

In 8a, heat and water swing vigor harder than the name on the root.
A *Celeste* on *Celeste* in wet clay looks dwarfed. The same pair on
a raised bed looks like a different cultivar. Fix the hole before
you go shopping for a mythical dwarfing fig.

What I actually do: match jobs, not myths. Insurance stool =
*Chicago Hardy*. Conversion of a tree I like the size of = same
variety back on, or something of similar appetite. Container =
variety that is already polite, plus a pot that I will root-prune.
Cocktail = similar vigor, or a written promise to myself to prune
the bully in June.

If a seller's entire pitch is dwarfing rootstock, ask for three-year
photos of the same scion on that stock and on its own roots, in a
climate that sees 12°F. If the photos do not exist, you are buying
a story. Stories are cheap. Holes in the garden are not.

The only dwarfing I trust in this climate is a pot, a hard June
pinch, and a variety that was already polite. That trio keeps a fig
at fence height. A magic stool does not. If you need a small tree
under a window, start with *Petite Negra* or another naturally
modest name, on its own roots or on a cutting of itself, and prune
it like you meant it. Save the experimental understock for a pot
you can throw when the story fails.
