# Mosaic, nematodes, and what you imported

The knife copies the plant you had. It also copies the
problems you mailed yourself.

Fig mosaic. Yellow islands, later a rusted rim, smaller
fruit on a bad year. It rides grafts and cuttings. The
mite moves it around the yard. There is no spray that
makes a tree clean. You choose mothers. You isolate the
ugly ones. You live with some pattern on trees that
still feed you. You do not put a yellow wreck on the
cleanest stool you have unless that wreck is the only
stick of a variety you are willing to lose the stool
for.

Nematodes. Warm 8a sand and old garden soil can be a
gall factory. A declining fig with knobby roots is not
going to be saved by a new scion. The top was not the
sick part. Grafting a pretty name onto a dying root is
how you learn this twice. New soil, a box, a different
hole, maybe a clone with a reputation — and even a praised
*Ficus palmata* is not magic in nematode sand. Clone and
soil, not common name.

Soil sickness and replant issues show up in the
literature as reasons people hunt wild *Ficus*
rootstocks. That is real research. It is not a packet
you pick up at the feed store on Saturday. If your hole
kills figs, change the hole.

What you imported in the baggie: mites, mosaic, a
mislabel, sometimes scale. Quarantine new wood on a pot
at the edge for a season if you can stand the delayed
gratification. I cannot always stand it. I have also
planted a problem in the middle of the row and then
moved it with a worse word.

The graft did not create these things. The graft is a
copier. If the orchard gets dirtier after a winter of
swapping wood, the knife was the press and you were the
printer.

Be picky when you are grafting onto a tree you cannot
replace. Be looser when you are grafting onto a pot you
can throw. That is the whole biosecurity program I
actually keep.

If a new fig arrives with yellow islands in the first
heat, do not panic-multiply it onto the row. Let it
fruit. Some mosaic is cosmetic. Some is a plant that
will never pay rent. One season of watching is cheaper
than a yard of matching patterns.

Wash your hands after you fondle a mottled tree and
before you pinch a clean one. Mites are small. You are
a tall mite. I am not asking for a hazmat suit. I am
asking for the same pause you already give tomato blight.
