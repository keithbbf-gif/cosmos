# Dry scion — the usual death

When a fig graft fails, the forum will offer
incompatibility, the moon, bad karma, and a rootstock
you should have bought from a man three states away.
Look at the scion first. If it is wrinkled, tan, and
light in the hand, you grew a raisin. That is the usual
death.

How it happens. Wood that was already tired in the
fridge. A long face left in the wind while you found
the tape. A bag that cooked. A bag that was never put
on. A wrap that did not close the pith chimney. A west
wall in April. A pot that went dry. A scion that pushed
leaves on stored water and then had nothing.

How it does *not* usually happen in *Ficus carica* on
*Ficus carica*: mysterious rejection. Common figs graft
to common figs. Takes fail for water and heat and dull
knives. If you are grafting *carica* to a random
ornamental *Ficus* you bought at a box store, then yes,
you may have a compatibility problem. That is a
different mistake.

Read the corpse. Uniform dry tan: sun or wind or a
seal that never was. Wet black at the seam: rot, often
from a dead scion left in a cleft, or from a bag that
was a swamp. Green then collapsed shoot: battery death,
no union. Mold in the bag: storage or humidity without
air.

The fix is not a new brand of wax as a personality.
The fix is fresher wood, faster hands, a real wrap,
shade, and a stock that can drink. Do one graft slowly
in good weather before you do fifteen in a hurry on a
bad day.

I keep a failure note. Not a mood. A cause. "West stub,
clear bag, 88°F." "Fridge wood from December, used in
May, last stick in the bag." After a few years the
notes repeat. That repetition is the whole science of
my yard.

If you need a villain that is not you, use the wind.
The wind is real. Then still wrap better. The wind does
not mind being blamed and still killed the scion.

A useful autopsy: cut the dead scion off and look at
the faces. Dry and separate: never knit. A white lip
that then browned: knit started and then cooked or
dried. Slimy: rot after a swamp bag or a corpse left
in a cleft. Write that word on the label before you
throw the stick. Next March you will remember the word
better than the feeling.

Do not change five variables on the retry. Change the
one the corpse named. New wax plus new tool plus new
moon plus new forum advice is how you never learn
which fix worked.
