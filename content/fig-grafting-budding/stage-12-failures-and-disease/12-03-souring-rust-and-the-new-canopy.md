# Souring, rust, and the new canopy

A graft can take and still make you angry in August.
That is not a failed union. That is a variety in a
climate.

Souring. Humid 8a, an open eye, a beetle, a split after
rain. The fruit ferments on the tree and smells like a
bar. *Brown Turkey* and a lot of *Mission*-kin do this
here. Grafting them onto *Celeste* roots does not close
the eye. If you converted a tree *to* an open-eye late
fig, you may have converted it to a souring machine.
Pick earlier. Net. Or convert again to a closed-eye.

Fig rust. Orange dust on the underside of leaves in
late summer. It blows across a cocktail tree without
reading your labels. A new canopy of dense soft growth
— exactly what a happy graft makes — is a nice nursery
for rust. Air, water on the ground not the leaves at
night, and a willingness to rake. I do not promise a
spray schedule I will not keep.

Splitting. A dry spell then a heavy 8a thunderstorm.
The fruit cracks. Closed-eye types handle this better
and still crack in a cruel year. Irrigation that is
steady beats irrigation that is a guilt flood after
you forgot.

Birds. A new small canopy is easy to net. An old
*Turkey* you headed is suddenly fruiting at a height
the birds consider polite. Net the first crop on a
new head or donate it.

None of this means the graft failed. It means you now
own the variety's manners in this air. Read the fruit
for a year before you topwork the rest of the tree.
That is another reason for two-year conversions. Year
one is a tasting. Year two is a commitment.

If the new canopy is mosaic-ugly and the fruit is
small, that is a different chapter. If the new canopy
is healthy and the fruit sours, that is this one. Do
not recut a good union because August was wet. Recut
because you picked the wrong name for this yard.

A first-year canopy is often later than the same
variety on an old bush. Do not judge ripening week
from a two-foot wand and then throw the name out. Give
it a second summer on wood that had a winter. Late on
a new head and late on a mature head are different
sentences.

If rust takes the leaves in September, the union can
still be fine. Do not strip a sick canopy in a panic
and shove the plant into winter naked. Let it drop.
Rake. Decide in March with live wood, not with August
mood.
