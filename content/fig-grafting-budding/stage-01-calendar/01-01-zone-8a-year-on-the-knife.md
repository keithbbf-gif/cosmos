# Zone 8a year on the knife

I do not graft by a date I stole from a California blog. I graft by what
the trees are doing in this yard. Still, a year has a shape in 8a, and it
helps to see it once before you throw the calendar out.

**December–January.** Trees are as asleep as they get. This is scion month.
Cut wood after a stretch of real cold, when the leaves are gone and the
pith is quiet. Bag it. Fridge it. Do not graft outdoors on a 28°F morning
because a book from 1901 said winter sap is sluggish. Eisen was not wrong
about sap. He was also not standing in a wind that will desiccate a fresh
union before lunch. Collect now. Cut later.

**February.** Still collect if you forgot. Check the fridge. Throw out
anything fuzzy or bark-slipped in the bag. In the lower, warmer 8a strips
— think the mild Gulf-edge pockets — you may see bud swell on *Celeste*
before Valentine's. That is a warning, not a start gun. The stock can
move. The air can still go to 22°F.

**March.** This is the first real outdoor window for dormant grafts on
most 8a inland yards. Stock beginning to swell. Scions still in the fridge,
still dormant. Cleft, whip, saddle, side veneer. Chip-buds on potted
understock in a sheltered spot. Watch the late frost. A union that just
started to callus does not want 26°F on wet bark.

**April.** Bark starts to slip on established trees after a run of 60s.
Bark grafts and T-buds become legal. Chip-buds still work and do not need
the slip. This is also when last year's headed stubs show you who lived.
Do not panic-graft a stub that is still bleeding winter. Wait until you
can tell live from dead with a fingernail.

**May–June.** Green wood appears. You can chip-bud with current-season
wood if you wrap the bud like you mean it. T-bud if the bark still slips
cleanly. Heat arrives. Bags cook scions on the west face. Shade cloth is
not decoration.

**July–August.** Possible, not friendly. Chip-bud in the morning, heavy
wrap, shade. An ISHS trial T-budded cultivars on *Brown Turkey* seedlings
in late August and got takes. That was not my west wall at 4 p.m. If you
bud in August, you are usually banking a bud for next spring, not asking
for a flush this year.

**September–October.** I stop making new unions unless I am bench-grafting
in a controlled box. New callus heading into a first frost is a bet I
lose often enough to remember. Use the fall for labels, notes, and deciding
which boring bush gets converted in March.

**November.** Mulch the stools you care about. Especially young unions.
The wood above a six-month-old graft is not as tough as the catalog said.
First hard frost ends the year on the knife. The fridge can take a last
emergency scion cut if a storm is coming and you still need wood.

The year is a loop: collect while asleep, cut when the stock wakes, bud
when the bark or the chip allows, stop before the fall freeze trains you
the hard way. If you only remember one line, remember that one.
