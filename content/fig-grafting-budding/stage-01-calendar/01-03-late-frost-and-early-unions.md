# Late frost and early unions

Zone 8a sells itself as mild. Then April puts 28°F on a tree that believed
the sales pitch. New fig growth is tender in a way old wood is not. A
graft that has just broken bud is closer to a tomato transplant than to
the mother tree.

I used to rush. Stock swelling in early March, scions in, bags on, pride
on. A late frost took the first leaves and I called the grafts dead. Half
of them were not. The scion wood below the tip was still green. The buds
lower down pushed two weeks later. I had just wasted a week of panic and
almost cut the stubs off.

The rule I use now: frost burns leaves. It does not automatically kill a
union that has started to callus. Wait. Scratch. If the scion is brown
and dry all the way to the tape, it is dead. If it is green under the
bark, leave it alone and keep the suckers off.

Prevention is boring and it works. For potted bench grafts, the pot comes
into an unheated garage or sits under a row cover when the night is ugly.
For in-ground clefts, a paper bag or a milk jug over the scion on the
cold night is enough if you take it off in the morning. Leaving a clear
jug on through a 75°F day will cook the same scion the frost spared.

Do not graft into a known frost pocket in the first warm week. The
north-side low spot that loses tomatoes every year will lose fig scions
the same night. Put the experimental unions on the east wall, or on pots
you can move. Convert the frost-pocket tree later, or convert it to
something that can die to the ground and still fruit (*Chicago Hardy*,
*Celeste* in many yards).

A late frost after bark grafts is uglier. You have a big stub, white
paint or not, and scions tucked under bark. If the stub gets nailed and
the bark separates, the pockets fail. There is no cute jug for a
four-inch stump. That is one reason I do large topworks after the worst
frost risk, even if it means I am grafting in the first heat. Shade
cloth I can add. I cannot add a week of March.

If you already pushed early and the forecast is ugly: water the day
before so the ground holds heat, cover what you can, do not fertilize,
do not unwrap. After the night, wait three days before you decide
anything. Figs bluff. They look worse than they are, and then they look
fine while the union quietly failed. The fingernail test after a few
days is the only adult response.

Early is not a virtue. Alive in June is a virtue. In 8a I would rather
graft on April 10 and nurse heat than graft on March 5 and write an
obituary on April 8.
