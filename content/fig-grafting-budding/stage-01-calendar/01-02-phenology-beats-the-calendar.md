# Phenology beats the calendar

Zone 8a is a winter number. It is not a last-frost date. Two yards with
the same USDA tag can be three weeks apart. A coastal 8a can slip bark in
late February. A ridge 8a with cold-air drainage can still be tight in
the last week of March. If you graft because the blog said "March," you
are grafting someone else's spring.

Look at the stock. Dormant grafts want a plant that is *about* to move:
buds swelling, not shooting. Scratch a twig. Green under the bark, a little
damp, not dry brown, not pouring latex like a summer pruning wound. That
is "awake enough." The scion in your hand should still look like January —
tight buds, no leaves, no wrinkled bark.

Bark work has a different tell. Slip. You cut a short vertical and the
bark lifts from the wood in a clean sheet, like orange peel, not like
cardboard tearing. If it tears and crumbs, you are early or the stub is
dead. If it lifts and the wood underneath is slick with sap, you are on
time for a bark graft or a T-bud. Chip-bud does not need this. That is
why chip-bud is the weekday method.

Heat has a tell too. When afternoon shade temperatures sit over 80°F and
the wind is dry, a bagged scion on a west stub becomes a steam dumpling
and then a corpse. The calendar might still say "grafting season." The
west wall is saying no. Move to morning, to the east side, to a potted
understock under cloth.

Late frost has a tell you only learn once. The forecast says 29°F and
your new cleft scions have just pushed a quarter-inch of green. You cover
or you lose the push. The union may still be alive underneath. The first
leaves will not be. That is not a reason to graft two weeks earlier next
year. It is a reason to keep a milk jug and a sheet in the shed through
April.

I keep a three-line notebook, not an app. Date. What the *Celeste* buds
were doing. What I cut. In 8a that notebook is worth more than a frost-date
map, because the map is a county average and your fig is in a corner the
county did not measure.

If you need a default for a new yard: collect in the coldest month you
actually get, fridge the wood, then walk the stock every few days starting
when daffodils move. First swell, first dormant grafts. First clean slip,
first bark work. First stretch of 80s, stop bagging in full sun. That is
the whole calendar, and it travels with you if you move ten miles north.
