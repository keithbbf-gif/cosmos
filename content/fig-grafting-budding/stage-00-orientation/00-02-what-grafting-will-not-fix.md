# What grafting will not fix

People come to the knife with a grievance. The tree is too big. The fruit
sours. Last January burned the tops. A friend said *Kadota* would taste
like honey and it tasted like wet cardboard. Grafting is a tool. It is not
an apology from the climate.

It will not move you out of Zone 8a. The USDA line is a winter statistic,
not a personality. We sit at 10–15°F on the bad nights. Some 8a yards never
see 15. Some sit in a frost pocket and see 11°F twice a decade. A graft
union does not change the thermometer on the fence.

It will not give you a breba crop on wood that dies to the ground. Brebas
ride last year's growth. If your *Chicago Hardy* is a herbaceous perennial
in the cold corner, you get a main crop on new shoots and you stop
daydreaming about June fruit from old wood. Graft a breba-heavy variety
onto that stool and you still only get brebas in the years the top lives.

It will not drain a wet clay pad. Figs hate wet feet more than they hate a
brief 14°F. I have watched people bark-graft four varieties onto a tree
standing in a puddle and then blame the unions in July. The unions were
fine. The roots were drowning.

It will not make an open-eye fig behave in a wet August. *Brown Turkey*
and a lot of *Mission*-types split and sour when the humidity sits on them
and a beetle finds the eye. Putting *Celeste* on the same trunk does not
teach the *Turkey* manners. You either pick the *Turkey* earlier, net it,
or stop growing it for fresh eating in the wetter half of 8a.

It will not erase fig mosaic. UC IPM is blunt: the virus moves with grafts
and cuttings. The mite moves it too. *Ficus palmata* and some palmata
seedlings get called immune. That is interesting. It is not a disinfectant
for the scion you just taped on. If the stick mottled last August, you
copied the mottling.

It will not make a patented variety yours to sell. Home swapping of
cuttings is the fig world's gray weather. Commercial is a different
building. If you are grafting to flip trees at a market, read the tag.

It will not replace watering in a 95°F week while the bag is still on the
scion. Dehydration is the ordinary death. People want a more interesting
villain — incompatibility, "the full moon," a curse from the previous
owner. Look at the scion. If it wrinkled and browned under the parafilm,
you grew a raisin.

It will not settle a vigor fight by itself. A rocket like *LSU Purple* next
to a polite *Violette de Bordeaux* will shade the polite one out unless you
pinch. A multi-variety tree is a management job. The graft is the easy
morning.

Write down what you think the knife will fix. If the sentence is about
soil, rain, a variety's eye, or a winter that already happened, the knife
is the wrong instrument. Fix the site or change the variety. Save the
blade for a trunk that is already in the right place.
