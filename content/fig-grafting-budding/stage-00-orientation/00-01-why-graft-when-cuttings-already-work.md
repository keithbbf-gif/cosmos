# Why graft when cuttings already work

A fig cutting in a cup of water will throw roots while you are still arguing
about tape. That is the honest starting point in Zone 8a. If you have a
pencil of wood and a pot of mix, grafting is optional. Most years, optional
is the right word.

Grafting earns its keep on days the cutting cannot. The usual one: January
took the top off a tender variety and left you a stump that still wants to
live. The roots are *Chicago Hardy* or an old *Celeste* that has been
resprouting since somebody's uncle planted it. The variety you actually
wanted is gone above the snow line. A cutting of the lost top, if you even
have one, is a year behind a graft onto that established root.

Second case: you have one stick of something you will not see again. The
fig trade runs on leftover buds. People mail a six-inch scion in a baggie
and call it generous. You can root the top and chip-bud the bottom bud onto
a pot already in leaf. Two shots. That is not romance. That is arithmetic.

Third case: the tree is the wrong tree. A big *Brown Turkey* that sours
every August in humid 8a air, or a seedling that never figured out how to
be sweet. Heading it and putting two or three scions on the stubs is faster
than digging a stump the size of a washtub. You do it over two springs, not
one weekend. The tree keeps feeding itself while the new heads take.

Fourth case, the one people advertise: a fruit cocktail on one trunk.
Early *Celeste*, a midseason LSU, something late if your frost date allows
it. It works. It also means you now own a tree that wants to throw suckers
in three languages. If you will not walk it every two weeks in May, do not
build it.

What grafting does *not* do: it does not make a tropical fig winter-proof
because you stuck it on hardy roots. The top is still the top. A 12°F night
kills *Alma* wood whether the roots are *Chicago Hardy* or not. The gift of
hardy understock is the resprout and the second chance, not magic insulation
traveling up the xylem.

It also does not fix bad wood. Mosaic in the scion is mosaic in the new
tree. A dry stick from a fridge that smelled like onions is still a dry
stick. The knife is not a blessing.

In 8a I graft when I am converting, salvaging, or stretching one rare bud.
I cut and root when I want another bush of a variety I already trust. If
you cannot say which of those you are doing before you sharpen the knife,
put the knife down and take the cutting. The fig will not mind. It has been
surviving worse decisions than yours since the Bronze Age.
