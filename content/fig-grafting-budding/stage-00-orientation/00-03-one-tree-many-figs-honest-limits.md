# One tree, many figs — honest limits

The cocktail tree is the picture that sells the hobby. Four names on one
trunk, a bowl in July, another in August, a late one if October stays
kind. I have one. I also have a stepladder and a roll of tape because the
tree does not believe in fairness.

Start with vigor. *LSU Purple* and *Texas Everbearing* will outrun
*Olympian* and *Ronde de Bordeaux* if you let them. The union can be
perfect. The canopy will still lie. In year two the polite variety is a
stick in the shade of a bully. You prune the bully in June or you lose the
point of the tree. There is no set-and-forget version of this.

Second limit: winter. Zone 8a does not kill every top every year. It kills
selectively. A tender head on a hardy stool can go to zero while the hardy
head next to it only loses tips. You walk out in March to a tree that is
now 80 percent *Chicago Hardy* again. That is not failure of the union. That
is the climate editing your menu. Re-graft the hole. Or admit you wanted a
hardy orchard and stop chasing *Alma* on the north fence.

Third: disease and fruit faults do not quarantine themselves on a branch.
Fig rust blows across six inches of air. Souring beetles do not read
labels. If you graft an open-eye late fig onto a tree that already has a
closed-eye early fig, you did not build a fortress. You built a buffet
with two serving times.

Fourth: suckers. A fig stool throws shoots from below the unions like it
is getting paid. Those shoots are the original variety. They are vigorous
and they will starve a new scion that is still the size of a pencil. A
cocktail tree is a weeding job from April to July. If that sentence bores
you, plant three bushes.

Fifth: you cannot stack incompatible ripening *and* incompatible habits
and call it design. A breba-heavy type wants old wood. A main-crop type
that you keep as a bush can be cut harder. On one trunk you have to pick a
form and live with the compromise. I keep cocktail trees as open vases
with three to five scaffolds, one variety per scaffold when I can, and I
accept that brebas are a bonus, not a promise.

How many varieties. Three is a tree. Five is a project. Seven is a joke
you will tell in November when two of them are dead and one is a whip in
the interior. Leave a sap-drawer if you head a big tree. Do the work over
two springs. Label with metal or stamped tape, not a Sharpie on parafilm.
The sun eats Sharpie. You will not remember which stub was *Celeste*.

The honest pitch: one trunk, two or three varieties that ripen in
sequence, similar vigor, all willing to live through a 12°F night or all
accepted as disposable tops on a hardy stool. Walk it. Pinch it. Re-graft
the hole when January takes a head. That is a cocktail tree. The rest is
catalog copy.
