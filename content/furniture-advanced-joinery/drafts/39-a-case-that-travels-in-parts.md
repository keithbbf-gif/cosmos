The shelf came out the back talking, the same rising friction as always, and then it was in my hands and the case was two sides and a top. I had not glued the tails. I had waxed them. The customer wanted a bookcase that could leave an apartment without leaving the apartment in the stairwell. A sliding dovetail can be that joint if you design it to be done twice. Most of them are designed to be done once, which is why they seize.

<!-- PHOTO: fig-01 D:\BBF\BBF Photos — case sides and shelves apart, sliding-dovetail tails visible (filename pending shop pull) -->

The craft pack taught the glued third. This is the unglued, or lightly glued, modular cousin. The structural idea is the same. The politics are not.

## Fit for a second slide

I cut the tail a hair easier than a permanent joint. I ease the arrises. I wax. I do not taper so hard that the shelf only seats in one humidity. A one-way taper is a one-way joint. Knockdown wants a tail that can come out without taking the socket’s show corner with it.

A stopped socket still keeps the show edge clean. The stop takes a beating on every teardown. I leave a little extra meat at the stop, or I put a small hardwood shoe in the stop if the case will travel often. Soft stop, blown corner, dark hole from the sofa.

<!-- PHOTO: fig-02 D:\BBF\BBF Photos — same case assembled, show edge clean (filename pending shop pull) -->

## What keeps it from being a house of cards

Unglued sliding tails will hold a shelf against tip-out. They will not keep a tall case from racking into a parallelogram if there is no back, no top that is a real rail, no diagonal. I use a back panel that screws off, or a top that is itself a sliding dovetail plus a couple of accessible screws, or a removable back frame. Something. A knockdown case with no back is a ladder.

The climbing and bowed sliding-dovetail essays in this pack still apply if the case is canted or the shelf is a smile. For travel, I keep the geometry square unless the piece paid for the circus.

## Failures

I glued “just an inch” and then the inch was the inch that seized in June. For travel, I now glue nothing on the tails, or I glue a block that is not the tail. Be decisive.

I also blew a stop on the third teardown. Shoe. Meat. Do not treat a stopped socket like a disposable.

A case I lettered on the inside with pencil that wore off: the sides were not a pair anymore in my head. Stamp, or a chisel mark, or a story that only fits one way.

## A lettered teardown

I letter the inside of every side and the underside of every shelf. A, B, top, bottom. I do not rely on “the pretty face.” Pretty faces get finished the same. Letters live in a place a customer will not see and a future me will.

The first teardown in the shop is mandatory. If a tail seizes in the shop in June, it will seize in a stairwell in June. I wax, I try, I sneak. I do not force a seized tail toward a stopped show edge.

## The back that unscrews

A 1/4 or 1/2 back, rabbeted, screws on a regular grid. The back is the diagonal the sliding tails are not. I can pull the back in two minutes and the case becomes a set of boards. I cannot pull a nailed-and-glued back without a story. For travel, screws.

A removable back frame — a little door of a back — is nicer on a piece that will be seen from behind. Same idea. Something you can take off without a wrecking bar.

## Humidity and wax

Wax in a hot shop can get onto a finish if you are sloppy. I wax the tail cheeks and I wipe the show. I rewax after a season if the case lives with me. A customer will not rewax. So the first fit has to be the fit that will still slide in year three, which means a little easier than my pride likes.

## What I want from D:\BBF

Parts on a blanket, tails visible. The assembled show edge. A hand sliding a shelf from the back — the talking friction.

## What I will not sell as travel

A solid-oak case with glued sliding tails and a nailed back. That is a house case. I will not put it in a truck as “modular.” The word modular is for parts. Parts that do not part are a lie.

## First teardown in June

If a tail seizes in the shop in June, it seizes in a stairwell in June. I wax, I try, I sneak. I do not force a seized tail toward a stopped show edge. A blown stop is a dark hole from the sofa.

## Shop morning, modular case

I cut the sockets with the same bit I will use on the tails. I ease the tail arrises. I wax. I letter. I assemble without glue. I unscrew a back that is already a back. I take a shelf out the back and I put it back. If I am sweating the last inch, the fit is too proud for travel. I sneak now. I do not sneak in a stairwell with a customer holding a door.

<!-- PACK-W3 -->

## Stairwell June

The fit that seizes on my bench in June seizes in a stairwell in June. I sneak now. I letter. I unscrew a back. I do not glue an inch of tail “just because.” That inch is the inch that marries the case.

I will not sell a nailed-back oak case as modular. Modular is for parts. Parts that do not part are a house case. House cases are allowed. The word is not.

<!-- PACK-W4 -->

## Bit match

The bit that cut the socket cuts the tail, or the tail is a cousin and it will seize. I do not switch bits because one is already in the collet for another job. Collet convenience is a stairwell seizure.

<!-- PACK-W5 -->

I time the teardown. If it takes a tool I will not send, I change the back screws to a driver that lives in a kitchen. Knockdown that needs a shop is not knockdown. It is a house case with optimism. Optimism seizes at the stop.

<!-- PACK-W6 -->
I send a spare stop-shoe if the case will travel more than once. A blown stop is a show-edge hole. The shoe is cheaper than a plug I will always see. I letter the shoe to the socket.

<!-- PACK-W7 -->
I ease the tail tips with one pass of a plane, not with sandpaper that rounds the cheeks. Rounded cheeks are a sloppy second slide. A sloppy second slide is a shelf that tips.

I slide every shelf once in June before I like the case. June is the stairwell.

## The judgment

A case that travels in parts is a sliding dovetail you did not marry. Easy fit, wax, a stop that can take a beating, a back you can unscrew. The craft-pack tail stays in the house. This one goes down the stair. If it will not come out without a story, you built the staying kind again.
