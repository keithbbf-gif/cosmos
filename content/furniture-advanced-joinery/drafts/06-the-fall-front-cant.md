The side was a tall hopper. Not a box you set on a hearth — a case side, four feet of oak, wider at the floor than at the cornice, the old secretary posture. I had the top rail dry-fit and the tails were already arguing with the slope. A square pin board on a canted side is a wedge. The cornice would show it. The fall front would show it worse, because a fall front is a door that becomes a desk and then a door again, and it will find every twist you left in the case.

I am not writing a museum paper on antique secretaries. I am writing about the cant: two case sides that are not parallel in elevation, joined to top and bottom (or to rails) with dovetails that know about the lean. Drawers in that case get narrower as they go up, or they sit in a square interior you built behind the cant. Those are two different shops. Pick one on the drawing, not at the bench.

## Two ways to live with a cant

**True cant.** The interior is a hopper. Every drawer is a different width. Every runner is a different length. The joinery at top and bottom is compound dovetails, same family as the hopper box, only the boards are case sides and the load is a desk full of paper. This is the pretty way. It is also a month.

**Canted skin, square guts.** The outside leans. Inside, you have a square carcase or square drawer boxes, and the cant is a face: applied sides, or thick sides rabbeted so the interior stays plumb. A lot of later work does this and does not apologize. The dovetails can be square where the guts are square. The show is still a lean.

I will not moralize. I will say: if you advertise compound dovetails on a secretary, the first way is what the word means. If you are building a desk that has to ship and a budget that is not a museum’s, the second way is how you keep the fall front from becoming a research project.

## Compound tails on a case side

The hopper essay in this pack is the small version. Scale it. The side is long. The slope is shallow — secretaries often lean less than a magazine box — which makes the angle easy to miss and easy to get almost right. Almost right on a four-foot side is a gap you can see from the sofa.

Lock the bevel to the side. Carry it to the top end and the bottom end as if they were two different jobs, because the top may meet a molded cornice and the bottom may meet a plinth that is itself a small hopper. I letter everything. I do not trust memory on a piece with this many ends.

Tails on the sides, pins on the top and bottom boards, is the usual chest logic and it still works. The pins are then cut on boards that may be square in plan. Good. One less compound. The tails carry the slope.

## Runners that follow

If the interior is a true hopper, a drawer runner is not a strip you rip and repeat. Each pair of runners is a different length, and the drawer side that rides them is a different length, and the drawer front is a different width. I make a story stick for each drawer opening after the case is glued and true. Measuring the drawing is how you build a drawer that binds on one side and grins on the other.

Drawer joinery can stay half-blind and civilized. The advanced part already happened in the case. Do not also invent compound drawer fronts unless the fall front or the apron asked for a bow. One circus at a time.

If the guts are square, the runners are the usual argument — and you still have to hide the fact that the outside is leaning. The gap between the square drawer stack and the canted cheek is a shadow line or a fillet. Design it. Do not discover it at finish.

## The fall front is a truth serum

A fall front hangs on hinges at the bottom (or on a pair of pivots) and lands on stays or on lopers you pull out like a tongue. If the case is in wind, the front will not land flat. If the two sides are different slopes, the front will show a taper in the reveal. You cannot plane a reveal into fairness without making the front short.

So the case dry-fit, before any fall-front hinge is cut, has to sit flat and the two cants have to match. I strap the case, I sight the sides, I check the top opening with winding sticks. Only then do I fit the front as a door. The desk surface is a later luxury. First it has to shut.

Hinges on a canted cheek: the leaf may need a little taper in the mortise so the knuckle sits plumb to the room, not plumb to the lean. That is a small compound of its own. I mark it from the front, closed, not from a square I trusted in the abstract.

## Plinth and cornice

A plinth under a canted case is often a second hopper, smaller, or a square box that the case sits on with a shadow. If you dovetail the plinth as a square box and the case is canted, the meeting is a scribe. Scribe it. Do not fill it.

A cornice on a canted top wants moldings that return on a slope. That is millwork, and it will make you swear. Cope or miter on a compound is a different essay. Here: do not cut the case dovetails until you know how the cornice will sit. The top pin board’s thickness is part of that conversation.

## Failures

I built the square-guts version once and left the fillet until the end. The fillet became a wedge that racked the fall front. I pulled it, recut it as a consistent shadow, and I still see the nail holes I filled. Design the hide.

I also cut top tails to a remembered slope instead of the board in my hand. The cornice sat on a shoulder that was a degree off. A degree on a 14-inch top is a gap with a name. I planed the shoulder and I lost a molding reveal I liked. Measure the board.

## Pick the interior on the paper

True hopper interiors mean every drawer is a different width. Square guts mean a fillet. I pick on the drawing. I do not pick at the bench when the fall front is already hinged. The front will tell on a late decision with a tapered reveal I cannot plane into fairness.

## The judgment

A fall-front cant is a hopper that learned to be a desk. Either commit to a true sloped interior and the drawer work that follows, or build square guts and a canted skin and say so. What you cannot do is square dovetails on a leaning side and then ask a fall front to lie for you. The front will not lie. It will hang in the light and tell.
