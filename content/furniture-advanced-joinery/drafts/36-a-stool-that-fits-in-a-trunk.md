The stool was four legs, two stretchers, a seat, and a handful of pins, and it fit in a canvas bag I would not be ashamed to put in a truck. Assembled, it had to take a hip. That is the test. A knockdown stool that only holds a coat is a coat rack. I sat on it in the shop before I liked it. The first version racked. The pins were pretty brass and they bent. I replaced them with steel that looked less like jewelry and more like a chair.

<!-- PHOTO: fig-01 D:\BBF\BBF Photos — knockdown stool apart, parts labeled or nested (filename pending shop pull) -->

This is travel furniture without the chest. Same culture: nothing proud that will catch, joints you can do again, parts you can replace.

## Pins that are pins

Through-pins, tusked stretchers at this small scale, or a bolt with a captured nut. I like a through-pin with a little wedge or a cotter you can pull. Brass is fine if the section is serious. Brass that is a nail is a bent nail.

The seat can be a slab with four housings, or a piece of canvas, or leather. A slab seat wants to locate on the legs so it cannot skate. A canvas seat is a sling; the frame then is the whole chair and must not rack. Sling stools fail at the frame, not at the cloth.

## Mark the parts

I letter the joints. A knockdown you cannot reassemble without a drawing is a puzzle, not a stool. People will lose the drawing. The parts should only go together one way, or they should be identical in a way that any way is the way. I prefer lettering. I have also made four identical stretchers and been grateful.

## Failures

Pretty bent brass. Steel. Hip test.

I also made a stool whose stretchers were all different lengths by a 16th because I cut them “to the drawing” after the legs had already been shaved. Cut stretchers to the legs in front of you. Sequence.

A bag of parts with no spare pin: the pin that falls in the gravel is the pin you needed. Pack a spare. That is part of the joint.

## A hip test that is not a joke

I sit on the stool in the shop, both sit-bones, then I lean to one corner, then I twist. A chair that only holds a centered polite sit is a photo stool. The pins will tell you first. If a pin bends, the stretcher will oval. If a stretcher loosens, the seat will rack. I mark the pin that complained and I change the section, not the wood species, unless the species was the problem.

A canvas sling wants the rails to stay a rectangle. I treat those rails like a chair’s side: real tenons or real pins, not a screw in the end of a dowel. A slab seat wants housings that locate the legs so the slab cannot walk. I cut the housings after the legs are the legs I will ship, not after the drawing of the legs I hoped to have.

## Packing is a joint

The bag or the trunk is part of the design. Pins in a small cloth roll, stretchers nested against the seat, legs in a pair. If a pin can fall into a seam of the bag, it will. I sew a pocket or I tape the spare to the seat underside with a note. People lose notes. A pocket is better.

I do not pack a stool with a wet finish. Knockdown parts finish-rub each other in a bag. I let the finish go quiet, then I pad the show faces. A campaign stool with a rubbed-raw arris is a stool that traveled without a pad.

## Species and pins

Hickory pins in a softer seat housing will oval the housing. Oak pins in oak are the usual. Steel pins in a painted stool are honest and they will stain a wet rag if you leave them wet. I oil steel or I use a pin that was meant to live outside, and I still wipe it.

Brass of a serious diameter is fine on a show pin. Brass of a nail diameter is a souvenir of a hip. I have a bent one in a jar. It is not a mascot. It is a measurement I failed.

## What I want from D:\BBF

The pile, lettered, on a blanket. The assembled stool with a person actually sitting — Jack in a build photo is allowed if he sat; a posed empty stool is a catalog. The bent brass pin next to the steel that replaced it, if that frame exists. Do not invent the frame.

## South Arkansas and a canvas bag

A bag in a hot truck will cook hide if you used hide on a tenon you also pinned. I keep knockdown stool joints dry-pinned or bolted so the heat is not a glue test. If I glue a tenon on a “travel” stool, I have made a travel stool that does not travel at the joint, only at the bag. Be honest which parts open.

## Spare pin in a pocket

The pin that falls in the gravel is the pin you needed. I sew a pocket or I tape a spare under the seat. A bag seam is not a pocket. Finish is quiet before packing so parts do not rub raw in a hot truck.

<!-- PACK-W2L -->

## Shop morning, travel stool

Legs and stretchers cut to each other, not to a drawing of a hope. Steel or serious brass. Letter the joints. Hip test. Lean. Twist. Pack with a spare pin in a pocket. Finish quiet. If a pin bends, I change the section before I like the photograph.

<!-- PACK-W3 -->

## Truck heat

A canvas bag in a hot truck will cook hide on a tenon I also pinned. I keep travel stool joints dry-pinned or bolted so the heat is not a glue test. Finish is quiet before packing. Parts rub.

I will not ship a puzzle. Letters, or identical parts. A drawing in the bag will be lost. The spare pin will not be lost if it has a pocket.

<!-- PACK-W4 -->

## Hip, then bag

I sit, I lean, I twist, then I pack. A stool I pack before the hip test is a stool I will rebuild after a truck. The spare pin goes in the pocket before the bag closes. The pocket is sewn or it is not a pocket.

<!-- PACK-W5 -->

I weigh the bag. A travel stool that is a travel anvil is a design I will change — thinner slab, or canvas, or fewer pretty pins. The hip test still happens. Light is not an excuse for a bent pin. Steel or serious brass. Letters. Spare. Pocket.

<!-- PACK-W6 -->
I shake the bag before I zip it. A rattle that is not the spare pin is a pin that left its pocket. I fix the pocket. I do not trust a zip to be a pocket.

## The judgment

A stool that fits in a trunk is a chair you can do twice. Pins that do not bend, parts that know their letters, a hip test in the shop. Campaign is not a finish color. It is a pile that becomes furniture without a second shop.
