The wedge came out with two fingers and a tap. That is the compliment. A tusk tenon that needs a dead-blow and a prayer is not a knockdown joint. It is a through-tenon that forgot the point. I had the trestle in two piles on the porch: a pair of uprights, a stretcher, two tusks, and a top that would ride on cleats. A hallway will not take a six-foot assembled base. The joint exists because houses have doors.

The craft pack already argued a wedge you can see on a through-tenon you do not plan to take apart. This is the cousin you *do* take apart. The slot is longer. The wedge is a tool. The shoulder still has to shut, or the trestle walks.

## What has to be true

The stretcher’s tenon goes through the upright. The tenon has a slot. The tusk (the wedge) goes through the slot, *outside* the upright, and as you drive it, it draws the shoulder of the stretcher against the upright. Drawbore logic, removable.

If the slot is too close to the upright, the wedge jams before the shoulder shuts. If the slot is too far, the wedge bottoms and the shoulder is still a moon. I cut the slot so a partly driven wedge already kisses the upright, and a fully driven wedge shuts the shoulder without splitting the tenon. That is a fitting job. Dry-fit. Chalk. Sneak the slot, not the shoulder.

The wedge has a slope. I like a shallow slope so it holds by friction and so a bump does not launch it. A steep wedge holds like a nail and comes out like a nail — which is to say, badly. A little soap or wax on the unused faces keeps the tusk from becoming furniture.

## Wood that can be a tusk

The tusk takes abuse. I cut it from something at least as hard as the tenon, grain running the length, no runout. A pretty contrasting wedge is allowed. A pretty wedge with wild grain is a tusk that will snap the first time someone uses a hammer because they did not know about the two-finger rule. Write a note in the delivery. People will still use a hammer. Leave enough proud that a hammer has a face to hit that is not the tenon.

A second, opposite wedge — a pair — is a lock some trestles use. I have. It is more to lose. A single tusk with a small pin or a leather thong through a hole in the fat end is a tusk you will find under the sofa.

## The upright is a mortise that sees weather

The through-mortise in the upright has to be clean, walls parallel, because this joint will be assembled more than once. A ragged mortise gets worse every move. Chop it as if it were a show joint, because it is: people look at trestles. They sit on the floor at parties and they stare.

The shoulder of the stretcher should be square to the stretcher and scribed to the upright if the upright is not flat. A trestle upright often has a little life. Scribe. The knockdown does not cancel the moon.

## Top, rack, the rest of the table

A tusk stretcher keeps the two uprights from walking apart. It does not keep the top from racking by itself if the top is only sitting there. Cleats, sliding dovetails, buttons, a breadboard — the top still needs a way to be a top. I like cleats that nest over the uprights so the top cannot skate, and I like the trestle to take the rack. A dining trestle that relies on the top for all the racking stiffness is a dining trestle that loosens when someone sits on a corner.

## Failures

I cut the slot too far out. The wedge bottomed. The shoulder grinned. I plugged the slot and recut, which is a scar I can still read. Sneak the slot from a dry-fit with the shoulder already chalked.

I also used a steep decorative wedge because it photographed. It launched when a kid kicked the stretcher. Shallow slope. A thong.

A tenon that was too thin for the slot: the remaining meat split. The slot needs a tenon that is a small beam. Drawer-scale tenons do not get tusks.

## Fitting the slot from the shoulder

I dry-fit the stretcher home. I mark where the upright’s outside is. I cut the slot so a partly driven tusk already kisses that face, and a fully driven tusk shuts the shoulder without splitting the tenon. I sneak the slot, not the shoulder.

A shallow slope. A thong through the fat end. A note in the delivery that a tap is enough, because someone will still bring a hammer. Leave proud so the hammer has a face that is not the tenon.

## The top still racks

Cleats that nest, or a sliding dovetail, or buttons. A tusk stretcher keeps the uprights from walking apart. It does not make a skating top into a table. I dry-lift the trestle without the top. If it walks, the tusk is shy or the shoulders are moons.

## What I want from D:\BBF

Wedge driven, shoulder shut. Wedge in two fingers, apart. The slot and the upright’s witness mark.

## Tusk wood

I cut the tusk from something at least as hard as the tenon, grain the length, no runout. A pretty contrasting wedge is allowed. A pretty wedge with wild grain is a tusk that snaps the first time someone ignores the two-finger note.

## Shop morning, trestle

Through-mortise clean enough to assemble twice. Shoulder scribed if the upright has life. Slot fitted from a home dry-fit. Shallow tusk, thong, proud. Dry-lift without the top. Cleats for the top. Two piles for the hallway. Two fingers on the tusk. If I need a dead-blow, I recut the slot.

<!-- PACK-W3 -->

## Hallway physics

A six-foot trestle does not love a turn in a 1920s house. The tusk exists because of that turn. I fit the slot from a shoulder that already shut. I leave a thong. I write the tap note. Someone will still bring a hammer. The proud is for them.

I will not tusk a tenon that is a popsicle stick. The slot needs a small beam. Drawer-scale tenons do not get tusks.

<!-- PACK-W4 -->

## Chalk on the upright

I chalk the outside of the upright on the tenon while the shoulder is home. That chalk is the slot’s near edge. I cut from the chalk, not from a number I liked. A number I liked is how I bottomed a wedge and left a moon.

<!-- PACK-TINY -->
Two fingers is the spec. A dead-blow is a recut.

<!-- PACK-W6 -->
I wax the unused faces of the tusk so it remains a tool. A dry oak tusk in an oak slot in June can weld like a pin. Wax is not pretty. Two fingers is pretty.

## The judgment

A tusk you can pull is a through-tenon designed for a hallway. Shoulder shuts, wedge comes out with a tap, slot fitted from the life of the dry-fit. The craft-pack wedge is for joints that stay. This one is for joints that travel. If it does not travel, you cut the wrong wedge.
