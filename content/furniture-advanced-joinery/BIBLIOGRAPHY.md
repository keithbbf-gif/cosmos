# Bibliography — Advanced furniture joinery (staged)

This pack is shop-concrete case study, not a literature review. Sources below are **starting points for an editor** who wants to verify a practice, a name, or a historical form. They are not a claim that every essay was written from these pages. Uncertain shop-specific facts stay in each draft’s `verify:` list as `[VERIFY]`.

Do not invent a quotation from any of these books. If a living person is quoted later, the quote must be sourced in `verify` or cut.

## Practice (joints, chairs, knockdown)

- Krenov, James. *The Fine Art of Cabinetmaking*. Various reprints. Secret miters, sliding dovetails, the refusal to rush a shoulder.
- Hayward, Charles. *Woodwork Joints*. Evans / reprints. Hopper, bridle, scarf, rule joint — period drawings, not a house spec.
- Joyce, Ernest (rev. Alan Peters). *The Technique of Furniture Making*. Batsford. Compound work and production cousins.
- Klausz, Frank. Articles and videos on dovetailing; treat as practice notes, not as a BBF method.
- Underhill, Roy. *The Woodwright’s Shop* (and related books). Green-wood / Windsor language — caption only if the shop actually rives green. See draft 23 `verify`.
- Dunbar, Michael. *Windsor Chairmaking*. For socket-and-shrink practice, not a house claim.
- Rogowski, Gary. *The Complete Illustrated Guide to Joinery*. Taunton. Visual check against a hopper or bridle — still mark `[VERIFY]` for degrees.
- Hoadley, R. Bruce. *Understanding Wood*. Taunton. Movement, moisture, why a glued-full-length tail splits a side.

## Campaign / travel / hardware

- Hill, Nicholas / various dealers’ catalogs of campaign furniture — **rights and captions**: do not lift photographs. Use for form language (flush pulls, stacking pins, brass caps) only.
