**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not neonatology and not a diagnosis. Corrected age is something to confirm with your child’s doctor. Twins are two children. This page will not invent a delay because there are two.

CDC’s “other important things” box never forgets to ask: born prematurely? special health-care needs? That box is there because the first-year calendar can lie.

## Corrected age, in one careful paragraph

For many children born early, pediatricians use **corrected (adjusted) age** — counting development from the due date — for a stretch of the first two years, especially for motor and some developmental expectations. HealthyChildren / AAP parent pages discuss preemie milestones in that spirit. The exact cutoff (how early, until what birthday) is **not** a number this blog will freeze. Ask your clinic what they use. Write it down.

Speech-language people still want to know the **chronological** birthday for eligibility clocks (Part C ends at three, chronological) and the **corrected** story for what is fair to expect in the first seasons. Bring both dates: born, due.

Corrected age is a kindness. It is not a gag order. If you are worried, you still get to say so. Preemie follow-up programs exist because “give them extra months” is not the only sentence.

## NICU, hearing, and the long stay

JCIH treats NICU graduates as a population that often needs extra hearing attention. Ask what screens were done, whether a later audiology visit is on the calendar, and whether anyone mentioned CMV, ventilation, or meds that affect ears — only as history, not as a scare list you diagnose from.

Feeding history matters (essay 27). A mouth that learned to eat late is a history, not a speech diagnosis.

## Twins (and more)

Two children share a womb and then a language environment. Research on twins and language is a thick, sometimes gloomy literature about averages. Averages are not your Tuesday. One twin can be a chatterbox and one can be quiet. Compare each child to themselves and to the checklists, not only to each other.

Twin language — a private jargon — shows up in family stories. Mention it. It is usually not a secret code that blocks English forever. It is also not a reason to refuse a look if *neither* child is understandable to you.

Do not accept “twins talk late” as a plan. That is wait-and-see in a cute outfit (essay 39).

## What to take to visits

Discharge summary if you have it. Gestational age. Corrected age your clinic uses. Hearing results. Who the follow-up team is. Word/gesture lists **per child** if twins. Languages.

Ask whether EI already knows you. Many NICUs refer automatically. If nobody referred and you are worried, you can still call (essay 31).

## What not to do

Do not run two CDC checklists in a competition.  
Do not hide a worry because the Facebook preemie group said 18 months corrected is “still a baby.”  
Do not let anyone skip hearing because the child “startles.”  
Do not assume clinic SLPs know the NICU story unless you tell it.

Early birth is a fact that belongs on the form. It explains some calendars. It does not close the door on asking. Two children are two calendars. Ask twice if you need to.

## Who owns the follow-up

NICU developmental follow-up, the pediatrician, EI, and sometimes an audiology clinic. Write the names. Ask who is looking at communication, not only walking. Walking gets more photos. Talking gets missed in the glow of a first step.

HealthyChildren’s preemie-milestone pages are a parent door, not a substitute for your clinic’s rule. If two sources disagree on when to stop correcting age, ask your doctor to pick one and put it in the portal. You cannot run two calendars in your head forever.

For twins, request separate evaluation time if they perform for each other or go silent together. A combined hour is cheaper for the clinic and worse for the quiet one. You can ask. They can say no. Asking is still allowed.

## The third birthday still arrives on the calendar

Part C eligibility ends at three **chronological** years in the U.S. federal frame. Corrected age does not add months to that clock. If a coordinator is talking as if you have “extra time” past the third birthday because the child was early, ask them to say that again slowly. You may need the Part B conversation started earlier, not later (essay 31).

This is the kind of boring fact that saves a summer. Write the two dates on the same card: due, born, third birthday. Take the card. Do not invent a fourth date from a blog.
