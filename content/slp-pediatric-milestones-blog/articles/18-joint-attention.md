**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Not an autism screen and not a diagnosis. Sharing attention is a skill with a research name. Missing a look is a reason to talk with a clinician, not a reason to name a condition from an article.

The airplane is not the point. The check-back is.

Joint attention, in the way researchers like Tomasello and Mundy use the phrase, is the three-way knot: me, you, and the thing. A baby sees the dog. The baby looks at you to see if you saw the dog. You look at the dog and then at the baby. Something social has happened. Words like to grow in that soil.

## Where CDC hid it without the jargon

They will not print “joint attention” on the fridge sheet. They printed the behaviors:

- Looks when you call their name (9 months, social).
- Shows you an object they like (15 months).
- Points to show you something interesting (18 months, social).
- Looks at your face to see how to react in a new situation (2 years, social).

ASHA’s show/give/point cluster is the same knot. Identify the Signs’ early social items — not smiling or interacting, few gestures — overlap. The AAP’s 18- and 24-month autism-specific screens exist because clinicians wanted a validated look at social communication, not because a blog should practice on your child (Lipkin & Macias, 2020).

If you take one rule from `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md` into this essay, take this: **no article gets to finish that sentence.**

## Requesting is not the same as sharing

Arms up and point-to-ask are “I want.” Point-to-show is “I want you to see.” Both are valuable. The second one is the joint-attention poster. Families often have a lot of requesting and less showing. Mention the split. It is useful. It is still not a label.

## Reading versus being read

Sometimes adults do all the knot-tying. You follow their gaze, you name the thing, you celebrate. That is good teaching. A clinician will also want to know whether the child *initiates* the share — whether they bid for your eyes. You can notice that without making a chart on the fridge.

## Hearing and vision

A child who does not look when named may not have heard the name. A child who does not follow a point may not see the far thing. Mention ears and eyes. Boring forks are the right forks.

## What you can do that is not a protocol

Get on the floor. Put your face near the thing they already like. Name it. When they glance at you, you have a turn. CDC’s infant tips — talk about what they look at — are joint attention without the textbook.

Hand them an object and wait with an expectant face. If they show it back, you are in the game. If they do not, you still named an object and lived your Tuesday.

Do not withhold smiles until they perform a check-back. That is a grim way to spend a morning and it is not in this series.

Books are joint-attention machines. A finger, a page, a face. Essay 28.

## If you are worried

Write three stories: a time they showed you something, a time they did not look when you called, a time they used your face to check a new situation. Take the stories to the 18-month visit. Ask whether the autism-specific screen and the general screen were done, and what they were called. Ask about hearing.

You are allowed to want a specialist look. You are not required to arrive with a theory. Theories are what get families in trouble on the internet. Stories are what get families a good appointment.

## A dog, a window, a face

The dog walks by. The child’s eyes go to the dog. Then — this is the beat — to you. Then back to the dog. If you miss the middle look, you will think they only like dogs. If you catch it, you will say “you see the dog!” and you will have done the CDC 18-month “points to show you something interesting” in spirit even before the finger is perfect.

If the eyes never leave the dog and never find you, write that. If they find you only when they want a cracker, write that split (request versus share). Mundy’s papers are for clinicians. Your split is for the visit.

## Name-looking is not a trick either

Call their name from the side, once, in a quiet room, when they are not deep in a snack. If you have to escalate to a bark, mention hearing. If they look every time you are in front of them and never when you are to the side, mention vision or habit. One try. Then live. This is not a daily exam.

Books remain the gentlest machine: a page, a finger, a check-back. If books are a fight, use the airplane. The airplane does not care about your theory.
