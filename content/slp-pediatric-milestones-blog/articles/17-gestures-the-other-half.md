**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Not a gesture inventory and not a diagnosis. Hands can carry meaning before the mouth does.

A point is a sentence with the grammar left at home.

CDC’s first-year pages keep putting the body on the communication line: arms up at 9 months, wave at 12, point-to-ask at 15, show-you-something at 18 (social line), more than wave-and-point at 2 years. ASHA’s 10-to-12-month range is blunt: points, waves, shows or gives. Identify the Signs treats few gestures as a reason to talk to someone.

This is not extra. This is the other channel.

## Kinds of gesture you already know

**Request.** Arms up. Point to the unreachable cup. Lead you by the finger to the fridge. “I need.”

**Comment.** Point to the airplane for the sake of the airplane. Hold up a rock. “Look with me.” That is joint attention’s cousin (essay 18).

**Social scripts.** Wave, blown kiss, high five, nod, head shake, hush. These are the words of the culture, done by hand.

**Give and show.** Object out, eyes on your face. The give is not always generosity. Sometimes it is “you open this.” Still a message.

Iverson and Goldin-Meadow (2005) described how gesture often **paves the way** for the next spoken step — a point-plus-word before two spoken words, for example. You do not need to score their study. You need to notice whether the hands are doing tomorrow’s language today.

## What is not yet a point

A whole-hand reach toward a cookie is older and useful. A point with the index finger, and especially a look back to check that you saw, is the later object. Do not shame a reach. Do mention if the index point never arrives by the ages CDC/ASHA are talking about.

Grabbing your face to make you look can be communication. It can also be the only tool in the bag. Describe it.

## Baby sign is a cousin, not a requirement

CDC’s 9-month tips mention teaching simple signs so a baby can tell you what they want before words. Essay 38. Official signs and homemade signs both count as gestures. They do not replace the need to look at pointing and showing. A house full of signs and no point-to-comment is still a description worth giving.

## When words start, gestures should not die

At two, CDC wants *more* gestures, not fewer. Nodding yes, blowing a kiss. Families sometimes stop signing the day the first word arrives, as if the channel were a crutch. Channels can run in parallel. AAC later is the same idea with equipment (essay 41).

## Two cultures, two gesture dialects

Some families point with lips or chin. Some consider pointing rude. Tell the clinician what counting a “point” means in your house. The skill underneath is directing someone else’s attention, not a North American index finger.

## What you can do that is still human

Point at things you are talking about. CDC tells you to do this on walks in the first year. You are lending the shape.

When they reach, you can model a point on your own hand without prying their fingers into a drill. “You want the cup” plus your point is enough.

Wave when you leave. Do not sneak out every time — CDC’s 9-month goodbye tip is about trust as much as gesture.

Play peekaboo and pat-a-cake. Scripts are gesture schools.

Do not withhold the cup until a perfect point appears. That is a trick. Give the cup. Model. Live.

## The ask, without a label

If words are few *and* gestures are few, say the *and*. Identify the Signs built that *and* on purpose. If words are few and gestures are rich, say that too — it is often a more hopeful picture, and still allowed to be seen by an SLP.

Hands are not a consolation prize. They are language. Count them.

## Give, show, point — a week of notes

Monday: they slap the fridge. Tuesday: they take your hand to the fridge. Wednesday: they point at the fridge and look at your face. Those three days are a developmental movie. You do not need all three by a Friday. You need to know which frame you are in.

A give that is really “open this” is still a give. Write “gives for help.” A show that is really “hold this I don’t want” is still a transfer. Honesty helps more than generosity scoring.

## When gesture is the only fluent channel

Some children are easy to read by hand and hard to hear. That is a reason to ask, and a reason not to panic about “no language.” Language is already happening. An SLP may build speech on top of those bids, or may add pictures (essay 41). You do not have to pick the tool in the kitchen.

If gesture and speech are both thin, do not wait for a hand to save the story. Identify the Signs put few gestures on the public list for a reason. Few is a conversation. It is not a name.
