**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Not a play-based therapy protocol and not a diagnosis. Play is how children rehearse being people. You can join it without turning it into homework.

The doll gets a block for dinner. CDC put that on the 30-month cognitive line: uses things to pretend, like feeding a block to a doll as if it were food. At four, the child pretends to *be* the teacher. At two, they play next to someone more than with them. None of that is a speech-sound item. All of it is where sentences go to try on clothes.

## A cheap map of play, without a textbook

**Body play.** Peekaboo, bouncing, tickle-and-pause. The pause is the language part — you wait, they bid.

**Cause and effect.** Button, something happens. CDC’s 2-year tips like those toys because problems get solved. Words later name the button.

**Combining objects.** Block in cup at 12 months (cognitive). Dump and fill at 9 months in the tips. Combining objects and combining words like to grow in the same season. Not a law. A rhyme.

**Pretend with things.** Cup used as cup, then block used as cup, then a stick as a horse. The further from the real object, the more the child must hold a *symbol*. Words are symbols. You can see why SLPs watch this.

**Pretend with roles.** Teacher, dog, superhero (CDC, 4 years). Now language has to do the role: “You sit there.” That is why essay 33 lives next door.

You do not need to score these stages. You need to notice if play stayed only at shaking a rattle long after the calendar moved, or if pretend never shows by the ages CDC mentions. Noticing is surveillance. Scoring is someone else’s job.

## Parallel play is not a failure

At 30 months CDC says plays next to other children and sometimes with them. At two they remind you that sharing is a skill under construction. A parent who wants a 20-month-old to “play nicely with friends” is asking for a 4-year social line. The language you hear at 20 months will be more *mine* than *let’s*. That is honest, not rude.

## How talk gets into play without a script

Sit facing the toys, not the phone. Say what they are doing, in fewer words than you think you need. When they hand you the block-food, you can eat it with sound effects. You can also wait.

CDC’s older-age tips: join the pretend meal they cooked; give dress-up clothes and pots. AAP’s public “power of play” pieces (HealthyChildren) keep making the same unfashionable point: unstructured play with a person beats another worksheet.

If you narrate every second, they cannot get a word in. If you narrate nothing, the room is a silent workshop. Aim for a turn. You will miss. Tomorrow exists.

## Toys that help, toys that shout

Open-ended: blocks, cups, dolls, cars, crayons, a blanket. They accept more than one story.

Toys that play the song for you, and then play it again, do not need a speaker of English. Use them if they make your child happy. Do not expect them to teach verbs.

Screens can show pretend. They cannot easily take the block-food from a doll you are holding. Essay 29.

## When play is the worry

A child who lines things up and does not like a change has a play style. The internet will offer a label. Decline the label. Describe the play: “They like cars in a row; they get upset if I move one; they do not feed the doll.” Take the description to a person who has validated tools.

A child who wants to play but cannot get in — language too thin, speech too unclear — is a different story. The ask might start with communication, not with play therapy.

## What this series will not do

We will not give you a 12-step pretend protocol. We will not tell you to withhold toys until they talk. We will not tell you floor-time branded steps as if SLPWOW invented them.

We will tell you that the CDC sheets already treated play as data. You can treat it as Tuesday. Bring one story about play to the visit. It will explain more than a word count alone.

## A block is a verb waiting

They bang two things at 9 months (CDC cognitive). They put a block in a cup at 12. They feed a block to a doll at 30. The object stayed. The idea grew. You can sit nearby and say “you’re feeding her” once. If you say it twelve times, you are a soundtrack. Once, then wait.

If the only play is watching a show play, essay 29. If the only play is throwing, mention whether throwing is joy, frustration, or the only idea in the room. Throwing is not a diagnosis. It is a Tuesday to describe.

## Other children as props

At two, the other child is often a moving toy. At four, CDC wants them to ask to go play. In between, you will host a lot of parallel towers. You can lend “my turn” without a social-skills brand. If they never look at the other child and never look at you either, that belongs with essay 18, not with a lecture on sharing.

AAP’s play pieces keep saying adults can join without taking over. Join means you accept the doll’s block dinner. Taking over means you rewrite the menu. The language grows on the accepted dinner.
