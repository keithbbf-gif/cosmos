**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. This page is about a habit, not about your child’s future. Nobody here will tell you that waiting “caused” a disorder.

*Wait and see* is the most expensive free advice in the family.

CDC named it. Abercrombie, Wiggins, and Green (2022) listed a wait-and-see approach — keep watching a concern instead of screening, evaluating, or supporting — as a barrier to finding children who could use help. The 2022 milestone revision tried to make a missed item mean **talk now**, not **maybe next month** (Zubler et al., 2022). ASHA still worried some language items moved later and might slow referrals. Both can be true. The shared public sentence is: **if you are concerned, do not wait to get help** (ASHA milestones page; Identify the Signs).

## Waiting that is a plan versus waiting that is a shrug

**A plan:** we will do the 18-month validated screen on this date; we will check hearing; we will try these two ordinary things; we will come back in six weeks; here is the EI number if you want it sooner.

**A shrug:** boys are late; your brother was quiet; she’ll talk when she’s ready; don’t compare.

The shrug has no next date. The plan does.

You can accept a plan. You can refuse a shrug. You can build a plan the shrug-person did not offer: call Part C, find an SLP, ask for the tool by name (essays 30–32).

## Why smart people shrug

They are trying to comfort you. They remember a child who bloomed. The late-talking literature really does have a group who later look typical (essay 24). Group comfort is not individual data. The people who did *not* bloom are not at the reunion to give the toast.

They are also tired of worry. Worry is exhausting. A shrug is cheap energy. Screens and referrals cost time. Time is what they are spending without noticing.

## What you can say at the table

“I’m not asking you to panic with me. I’m asking for a date and a tool.”  
“CDC’s footer says I can call EI myself.”  
“ASHA says I don’t have to wait if I’m concerned. I am.”  
“If you’re right, the look will be reassuring. If you’re wrong, we didn’t donate a year.”

You do not have to win the argument. You have to leave with a next step.

## Lost skills are not a wait-and-see item

Every CDC page asks whether the child lost something they used to do. That question is the opposite of wait. If a word, a wave, or a look went away, call this week. This series will not name a disease. It will not soften that timing.

## Two languages, boys, twins, preemies

None of those is a shrug license (essays 23, 36). They are context. Context belongs on the form. Context is not a silencer.

## If the professional is the one shrugging

Ask what would make them refer. Ask for the screen they already owe at 9, 18, 30 months. Ask for a second opinion. You can be polite and still use the public doors.

If they offer a plan with a date, you can take it *and* call EI. Those are not mutually exclusive.

## A note on hope

Hope is allowed. Hope with a calendar is better. The 2022 lists were an attempt to give parents a calendar that does not require a fight. Use the list. Use your worry. Use the phone number.

Waiting to see can be science if someone is actually looking. Waiting to see, with nobody looking, is just waiting. CDC tried to retire that kind. You can retire it at your table even if Uncle Mike has not.

## A six-week plan you can write on a receipt

Date we will screen or return: ______  
Hearing asked: yes / not yet  
EI number called: yes / not yet  
Two ordinary things we will keep doing (talk about what they look at; read three pages): ______  
What would make us call sooner (lost skill; your gut): ______

That is a plan. “He’s a boy; they’ll talk” is not. Zubler et al. (2022) and Abercrombie et al. (2022) were trying to put something like this receipt into the culture. You can write it even if the culture at your table has not read *Pediatrics*.

ASHA’s 2022 comment on the CDC lists is your ally if someone says “the new chart means you should wait longer.” The new chart meant a missed item should be *clearer*, not quieter. If it got quieter in your house, that is a misread. Correct the misread. Keep the date.
