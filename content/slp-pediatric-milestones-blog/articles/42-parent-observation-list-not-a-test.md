**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not a screening instrument, not a standardized test, and not a diagnosis. If you check boxes here, you are making notes for a conversation. You are not scoring your child.

Print this if you want. Or steal the headings into a phone note. Leave the numbers off. Numbers turn lists into exams.

## How to use it

Write what you **saw this week**, not what you hope. Write the child’s age, whether you are thinking in corrected age (essay 36), and every language in the house. Take it to the pediatrician, EI, or SLP. Pair it with the official CDC checklist for the well-child age. That official page is still not a test. It is a better-looking note.

If a box is empty, it is empty. It is not a fail.

## What you will not do with this page

You will not add the boxes and get a quotient.  
You will not title it “delay checklist.”  
You will not post a completed one with the child’s name.  
You will not let a relative use it as a courtroom exhibit.

Identify the Signs and CDC already published public lists. This page is only a way to walk into a room with your own eyes organized. The person with the validated tool goes next (Lipkin & Macias, 2020). The person who can diagnose, if anyone, is not you and not this series.

If you get to the end and you are still worried, that sentence is the whole list. Take the worry. Leave the score.

## How this list relates to the official ones

CDC’s checklists are age-locked and 75th-percentile by design. ASHA’s are ranges. Identify the Signs is a campaign list of reasons to call. This page is a **bag**. You dump the week into the bag. You do not compute a number.

If you want one official page, take the CDC sheet for the next well-child age and this bag. If they disagree, say so at the visit (essay 01). Disagreement is information. A blended homemade cutoff is not.

Lipkin and Macias (2020) still own the screens. Nothing on this page replaces ASQ or whatever tool your clinic uses. If you catch yourself totaling yeses, stop. Put the pen down. Read the disclaimer again. Then write one story you have not written yet. The story is the unit. The box is just a reminder to look.
