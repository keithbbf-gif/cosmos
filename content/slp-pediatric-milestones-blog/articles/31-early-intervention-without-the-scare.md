**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not legal advice and not a promise of eligibility. Early intervention is a public system. Rules vary by state. This page cannot tell you whether your child will qualify.

The phrase *early intervention* sounds like someone already decided something is wrong. In U.S. law it is mostly a **phone number** and a **timeline**.

**Part C** of IDEA is the birth-to-three program. **Part B** (and Child Find) is the 3–21 school-age door, which CDC’s older checklists point to when they say call the local public elementary school. The names are ugly. The idea is not: a free look, and services if the state says the child is eligible.

## Calling is not a diagnosis

CDC’s footer tells you to call your state or territory program to find out **if** your baby can get services. The *if* is doing work. Evaluation comes after the call. Eligibility comes after evaluation. A lot of children get a look and a “not eligible, here are ideas.” A lot get services. This URL will not guess which.

You can often **refer your own child**. You do not have to wait for the pediatrician to believe you, though it is better when they are on the same team (essay 30). CDC’s Find EI page is the usual public map — `[VERIFY]` the live URL before a print edition, because CDC moves paths.

## What the first weeks often look like

A service coordinator. Paperwork. Questions about birth, hearing, languages, your concerns. Then evaluators — maybe SLP, maybe OT, maybe a teacher, maybe a team. They use tools and play and your interview. Then a meeting. Then, if eligible, a plan (IFSP) with outcomes you should recognize as *your* life, not a clinic fantasy.

You can bring your word list, your CDC page, your three stories. You can say you do not want a label; you want to know if they see what you see. You can ask how they handle two languages.

You can say no. Consent is part of the law. This is not a hostage situation.

## What it is not

It is not a judgment on your parenting.  
It is not a promise that “early therapy prevents X.” Outcomes research exists and is mixed by condition and by study. We will not sell prevention.  
It is not only for children with a named diagnosis. States have criteria that can include a percent delay on testing, a condition with a high probability of delay, or other state rules. Ask your state’s definition. Do not take a Facebook version.

## After the third birthday

Part C ends. The conversation about preschool special education (Part B) should start before the birthday if the child is in the system. If you are new at 3;1, the elementary school / Child Find door is the one on the CDC 3-year footer. Clinic SLPs still exist. You can walk more than one path. Insurance, private pay, school — messy, local, not this pack’s billing department.

## If you are afraid of “the system”

Say what you are afraid of. Records. Immigration. Being told you are a bad parent. Those fears are real in real families. You can ask the coordinator how information is shared. You can bring a trusted person. You can start with the pediatrician and ASHA ProFind if that feels safer, and still call Part C later.

Do not let fear of a folder become a year of silence if you are worried. The folder is optional after you hear the offer. The worry is already in the house.

## A calm sentence to use on the phone

“I have questions about my child’s talking and understanding. I’d like to refer them for a Part C evaluation. I am not sure they will qualify. I would like the look.”

That is enough. No diagnosis required. No scare required. A door, then a conversation, then a decision that belongs to you and the team — not to a blog.

## What “percent delay” talk means, roughly

Some states use a percent below age on a test as one eligibility path. The percent is a state rule, not a CDC milestone. A child can miss a fridge-chart item and not meet a state percent. A child can meet a percent and look “fine” to a relative. Do not square those objects. Ask your coordinator what *this* state uses. IDEA’s site is the federal frame; the phone book is local.

IFSP outcomes should sound like your house: “will ask for help at meals,” not “will improve receptive language by 2 standard deviations.” If you cannot recognize the outcome, ask them to rewrite it. You are on the team. The law put you there.

If you move states, Part C does not automatically pack the suitcase. Ask before you move. Boring, important. This blog will not do your transfer. The coordinator might.
