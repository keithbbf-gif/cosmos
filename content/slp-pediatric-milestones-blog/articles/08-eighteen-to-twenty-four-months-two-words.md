**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Not a screen and not a diagnosis. Two words together is a surveillance item. It is not a grammar exam and not a label.

“More milk.”

That is the example on the CDC 2-year language line. Not a sentence with a verb. Not a polite request. Two words that belong to each other.

The rest of the line: points to things in a book when you ask (“Where is the bear?”); points to at least two body parts; uses more gestures than waving and pointing — a kiss blown, a nod yes.

ASHA’s 19-to-24-month range says two or more words together, *more water*, *go outside*. The agreement is loud. This is the year combinations are expected for most children on these public lists.

## What counts as two words together

Two different words, produced as a unit, meaning something. *More milk. Daddy gone. My shoe. No bath.*

A memorized song chunk is not the same as a combination the child built. A single word said twice — *mama mama* — is not two words. A word plus a grunt is not two words. A word plus a point is excellent communication and still not the CDC item.

You will hear “telegraphic” in textbooks. You do not need the word. You need to notice whether the child can *pair*.

If combinations are not there and single words are pouring out, say both. If combinations are not there and single words are scarce, say both. The 24-month research literature on late language emergence often looked at small vocabularies *and* missing combinations (essay 24). You still do not get to assign that term from a blog. You get to describe what you hear.

## The book point and the body-part point

These are comprehension-plus-gesture. “Where is the bear?” is a question that needs a page, a word, and a finger. Two body parts is the same skill on a person.

If talking is hard to understand (it will be; essay 14), these items let a clinician see language without needing adult consonants. Practice them in the bath, not as a quiz with relatives watching.

## Gestures beyond wave and point

A nod, a shrug, a blown kiss, a hush finger, a “so big.” CDC wants the set to grow. Gesture does not shut off when words start. Iverson and Goldin-Meadow (2005) showed gesture often *leads* the next language step. A child who points at a shoe and says *Daddy* is already in combination territory in meaning, even if the mouth has not caught up. Mention those.

## Social line: hurt feelings and checking your face

Notices when someone is hurt or upset; looks at your face to see how to react in a new situation. Those are not speech-sound items. They are the reason we bother with speech sounds — people.

If a parent is worried about “social communication,” this is where blogs get reckless. This series will not name a condition from a missed glance. It will say: describe what you see; the 18- and 24-month autism-specific screens exist for a reason; the person who can interpret them is not a URL (essays 30, 33).

## How to talk when the words are still stubby

CDC’s 2-year tip: if they say “or nana,” you say “You want more banana.” You put the combination in their ear without making them perform it.

Play is still side-by-side more than together. Show sharing with your body. Pretend starts to flicker — dress-up shoes, a cup used as a cup. Essay 19.

Screens: still not the teacher. Under 2, AAP’s *Media and Young Minds* (2016) is about video chat as the exception and people as the curriculum.

## Intelligibility is a separate question

You may understand *more milk* and the sitter may not. At two, that is common. CDC does not put “strangers understand everything” on this page. They wait until three for “talks well enough for others to understand, most of the time” (essay 10). Do not import a 4-year sound chart into a 2-year visit (essay 13).

If *you* do not understand much of what they say, and combinations are missing, and gestures are thin — that is a fuller picture for the doctor than any one box.

## The visit

Two years is not an AAP universal autism-screen age in the same way 18 and 24 months are — 24 months *is* that age. Ask if it was done. Ask for the name of the tool. Bring your two-word examples on your phone. Bring the book-point story. You are not applying for a label. You are doing surveillance out loud.

## “Mine” is a combination in spirit

*My cup. Daddy shoe. No bath.* Possession and protest are how two-year-olds write law. If the only combinations you hear are *no + noun*, that still counts as pairing. Mention whether you also hear a doer-and-action (*doggie run* is the 30-month example; you may get a preview).

A memorized “thank you” from a parent prompt is politeness. It is not the same as *more cracker* when they are hungry. The SLP would like both facts.

## Playdates are a language sample you did not schedule

CDC’s 2-year tips: they play next to each other and do not know how to share. The language you hear will be *mine*, a yell, a point. If they never bid for the other child’s attention and never bid for yours either, write that. If they bid all day and the mouth cannot keep up, write that. Essay 33.

The 24-month autism-specific screen, if it was not done at 18 months or if someone is concerned again, is still on the AAP calendar. Ask. Then go home and do not turn the living room into a second clinic. Two words together is enough homework for a Tuesday: listen for a pair, write it down, live.
