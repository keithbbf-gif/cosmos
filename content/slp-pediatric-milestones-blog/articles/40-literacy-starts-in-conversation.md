**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not a reading curriculum and not a learning-disability diagnosis. Kindergarten has many doors. This page will not tell you a child has dyslexia because rhyme is hard.

People want to skip to letters. CDC put letters on the **cognitive** line at five: writes some letters of their name; names some letters when you point. The **language** line at five wants a two-event story, answers about a book, a longer conversation, and **rhyme**.

That split is the essay. Print is coming. Talk is already here.

## Phonological awareness is a long phrase for hearing the bits

Rhyme (*bat-cat*) is one bit. Hearing that *sun* and *sit* start alike is another. Clapping syllables is another. Teachers call the cluster phonological awareness because it predicts, in groups, how easily children later map sounds to letters. ASHA’s 4–5 public note mentions letters and writing a name. Identify the Signs mentions pretending to read and finding the front of the book.

You can grow rhyme in a song without a workbook (CDC 3-year tips: Itsy Bitsy Spider; 5-year tips: what rhymes with cat). If rhyme never shows, mention it at school or to an SLP. Mention whether speech is also hard to understand. Sounds you cannot say are harder to play with. That is a description, not a reading diagnosis.

## Book behaviors are literacy before decoding

Front of the book. Left to right in English. “Reading” the pictures (CDC 5-year tip). Answering after the story. These are **print awareness** and **narrative**. Essay 28.

A child who cannot decode *cat* in May of kindergarten and a child who cannot point to the front of a book at five are different stories. Do not mash them.

## Conversation quality, again

The turn-taking literature (essay 29) is a literacy story in disguise. Children who have been answered have more language to bring to a page. Hart and Risley’s word-count gap is a fight we will not re-litigate. Romeo’s conversational-turn work is a gentler object: back and forth.

Dinner talk about the day is CDC’s 4-year language item (*I played soccer*) growing toward the 5-year two-event story. That is composition. School will call it writing later.

## Two languages

Literacy can start in the home language. A strong story in Spanish is not a reading problem. English letter names will come with English print exposure. Do not drop the home stories to drill alphabet apps (essay 23).

## What not to buy

A 20-minute-a-day phonics promise for a three-year-old who still leaves the flap book. A diagnosis from an app that listened to them read. A claim that extra screen “reading games” replace a lap.

If a teacher says they are behind in reading, ask whether they mean language, print, sounds, or letters. Those are four referrals. Sometimes they are one child.

## Ordinary soil

Talk. Rhyme in the car. Let them tell the pictures. Put their name on a cup. Answer a why. Keep the last hour before bed quieter if you can (CDC).

If you are worried about later school, the honest early move is still the communication look — hearing, language, speech clarity — not a panic about chapter books. Literacy sits on conversation. Conversation sits on a person who waits.

You cannot fail a rhyme game. You can notice it. Noticing is this whole series.

## Name on the cup is enough print for a long time

CDC’s 5-year cognitive line wants some letters of their name, not a sentence. Magnetic letters on the fridge are optional. Seeing their name on a cubby is already print in the world. If they never notice print and never rhyme and never retell, you have a cluster for school and maybe an SLP. If they retell beautifully and ignore letters, you have a different May in kindergarten. Un-mash.

Identify the Signs’ “pretend to read” is a child who knows what a book *does*. That knowledge is older than phonics. Honor it. Do not skip it to drill *a, a, apple* at three while they are still leaving the flap.

Romeo et al. (2018) is a brain-and-turns paper, not a parenting brand. Steal one idea: the back-and-forth is worth protecting. The rest of the paper can stay in the journal.
