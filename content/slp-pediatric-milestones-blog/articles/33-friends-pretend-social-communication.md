**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not an autism screen and not a diagnosis. Social communication is a real domain. Naming a condition is a clinician’s job. This page will describe what CDC printed and how to talk about it without a label.

Talking is for someone.

CDC’s social/emotional lines are the someone: plays next to other children and sometimes with them (30 months); notices other children and joins them (3 years); asks to go play, comforts a friend, pretends to be someone else (4 years); takes turns in games (5 years). Language lines grow beside those: two back-and-forths at three, more than three at five.

Identify the Signs starts earlier: smiles and interaction in infancy. The AAP put autism-specific screening at 18 and 24 months because social communication is easy for families to worry about and hard to eyeball in a hallway (Lipkin & Macias, 2020). Use the screen. Do not use this article as one.

## Parallel play is a stage, not a snub

Two-year-olds build two towers. They steal a block. They do not host a committee. CDC’s 2-year tips say watch playdates closely and help with the words for conflicts. If you expected a best friend at 22 months, the checklist is on the child’s side.

By 30 months, “sometimes plays with them” is the phrase. *Sometimes.*

## Pretend is language in costume

A block becomes food (30 months, cognitive). A child becomes a dog (4 years, social). Roles need talk: who is the customer, who is the baby. If pretend never appears by the ages CDC mentions, describe what play *does* look like (essay 19). Description, not a forum word.

## Conversation is a social object

Two exchanges (3 years) is the smallest conversation CDC will count. More than three (5 years) is a longer rope. A child who lectures but does not answer, or who answers but never bids, is giving you a shape. Write the shape.

Unclear speech can fake a social problem: they tried, nobody got it, they stopped trying (essay 14). Thin vocabulary can fake one too. Hearing can fake one (essay 22). The SLP’s job is to un-fake. Your job is to bring the kitchen, not only the birthday party.

## “They prefer adults” and other park sentences

Some children prefer the person who understands them. That can be language. That can be temperament. That can be something a screen is built to help a clinician think about. You do not have to decide which at the sandbox.

If a parent at the park offers a diagnosis, you can say “we’re looking at it with the doctor.” Then leave.

## What you can do that is not a social-skills curriculum

Practice the small scripts CDC already likes: “Can I play with Alex?” “Can I have a turn?” You lend the words; you do not fine them for grabbing once.

Shorter playdates. One kid, not six. The same park, so the story repeats.

You can narrate another child’s feeling without a TED talk. “He’s sad. He’s hugging his person.” CDC’s 4-year tips do this.

You cannot force friendship. You can offer repetitions.

## The ask, cleanly

If social lines and language lines are both thin, say both. If social lines are thin and language is rich, say that. Ask whether the 18/24-month screens were done. Ask about hearing. Ask for an SLP or a developmental look if you are still carrying it.

Do not let a search autocomplete finish the sentence. Do not let this series finish it either. We will not.

People are the point of speech. Watching how your child tries to get into the people — that is surveillance. It is enough to start a conversation, and it is not enough to end one with a name.

## Birthday parties are a rotten lab

Noise, cake, cousins they see twice a year. A child who hides in your leg is giving you temperament and sensory load and maybe language. Write “party” on that story so nobody treats it like a Tuesday at the park with one friend.

CDC’s 4-year item — asks to go play if no children are around — is a bid. Some children bid by dragging you to the door. That counts. Some never bid and are happy in a solo world. Happiness is allowed. Your worry is also allowed. Both go to the person with a screen, not to a comment thread.

If a teacher says “social skills,” ask whether they mean talking, playing, following group directions, or something else. Those are different essays in this pack. Mash them and you will get a mush referral. Un-mash them and you will get a better hour.
