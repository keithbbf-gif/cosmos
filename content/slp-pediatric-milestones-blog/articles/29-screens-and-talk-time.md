**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Not a diagnosis. This page will not say screens cause autism, delay, or a moral failure. It will say what AAP and CDC printed about time with people.

The phone is how you keep a job and a grandmother. It is also a face that does not take a raspberry turn.

## What the 2016 AAP statement actually argued

*Media and Young Minds* (AAP Council on Communications and Media, *Pediatrics*, 2016) is the document people paraphrase until it becomes a weapon. The young-child core, in kitchen language:

- Under 18–24 months, avoid screen media **other than video chatting**.
- Toddlers who do use media later should use **high-quality** content **with an adult**, not as a babysitter.
- Preschoolers: a time cap (the statement discusses about an hour of high-quality programming) and a **family media plan**.
- No screens in bedrooms; keep them out of the hour before sleep — CDC’s 4-year tips rhyme with this.

CDC’s infant and toddler sheets repeat the video-call exception and, by 30 months and after, mention a one-hour-style cap with an adult present. ASHA’s general tips: set limits; use the time for talking, reading, and playing.

None of those sentences is “screens caused your child’s diagnosis.” Do not print that. Do not imply it. Guardrails.

## Why talk-time people keep mentioning turns

Romeo and colleagues (2018, *Psychological Science*) and Hirsh-Pasek’s group (2015) are part of a literature that cares about **conversational turns** and **quality**, not only a raw word dump. Hart and Risley’s “30-million-word gap” is a famous number that later work (Sperry, Sperry & Miller, 2019) argued with. This essay will not revive a class war about vocabulary counts. It will say: a back-and-forth with a person is the object CDC’s cooing-and-turn items were pointing at from month four.

A show can dump words. It cannot easily wait for your child’s *ba* and then say *ball*.

Video chat is the exception because a grandmother can wait.

## Background TV is a thief

A set that is always on steals looks and pauses. You do not need a study in your hand to notice that you answer the child slower when a plot is on. If you want a citation later, the background-TV literature exists; mark `[VERIFY]` before quoting a percent of fewer words. Honesty first.

## What this is not

It is not a demand that you delete the only quiet twenty minutes you get. Survival is a value. AAP is a professional society, not your night nurse.

It is not a ranking of parents who use PBS versus parents who use a phone game. “High quality” is their phrase. Co-viewing is the part you can actually do: sit, point, ask, laugh.

It is not permission to ignore a communication worry because “we don’t do screens.” Children with no screens also have late words and unclear speech. Children with screens also have rich language. Screens are a context. They are not a verdict.

## A family media plan that is not a personality

AAP’s HealthyChildren family media plan is a worksheet, not a brand of virtue. You can decide: no phones at dinner; chat with Grandma on Sundays; one show, you sit; charging station in the kitchen. You can break your own plan on a sick day. The plan exists so the default is people.

## If a teacher mentions screens

Ask what they are seeing in the room — attention, retell, conversation — not what they assume happens at home. Bring your real number if you want: “We do about X, usually with me.” Then talk about language, which is why you opened this series.

If you are worried about communication, cutting screens may free time for talk. It is not a treatment and not a cure. Do not let anyone sell you that swap as medicine.

People are the curriculum. Screens are a tool that sometimes includes a person. Keep the categories straight. That is the whole essay.

## Video chat that is actually a turn

Grandma waits. Grandma asks what the truck is. The child holds the truck to the camera. That is CDC’s show-you-something, over a wire. A looping video of Grandma is not that. A parent scrolling while a show answers itself is not that.

If video chat is the only grandparent, it is enough. AAP carved the exception because a face that answers is the object.

## The sick-day exception, without a sermon

You will use a show when the fever comes. You already know. Do not donate the next month to guilt. Return to the default when the soup is over. The family media plan is a default, not a courtroom.

Sperry, Sperry, and Miller (2019) argued that children’s verbal environments are more various than Hart and Risley’s famous gap implied. Use that as humility: you cannot count your way to virtue. You can put the phone in another room for one meal. That is a turn you can actually take.
