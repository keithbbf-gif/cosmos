# Hermann Gutzmann Sr.

The Eckstein portrait shows a Wilhelmine physician: beard, stiff collar, the look of a man who has just come from a lecture. Hermann Carl Albert Gutzmann (29 January 1865 – 4 November 1922) is the person European phoniatricians mean when they say the specialty has a father.

![Hermann Gutzmann Sr.](../assets/portraits/hermann-gutzmann.jpg)

*Hermann Gutzmann Sr. (1865–1922). Published by Adolf Eckstein. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain.*

## Bütow to the Charité

Born in Bütow, Pomerania, first of seven children, he took the Abitur at the Friedrichswerdersches Gymnasium in 1883 and studied medicine in Berlin under Ernst von Bergmann and Carl Gerhardt among others. The 1887 dissertation, *Über das Stottern*, was not a side interest. His father Albert taught deaf children and already published on speech errors. In 1890 they founded the *Medizinisch-pädagogische Monatsschrift für die gesamte Sprachheilkunde*. Medicine and pedagogy, one monthly.

1891: private outpatient clinic for the speech-impaired in Berlin. 1896: sanatorium in Zehlendorf. 1905: habilitation on respiratory disturbance in stuttering, inaugural lecture on speech disorders as a subject of clinical teaching — the year UEP still prints as phoniatrics’ academic founding. 1907: clinic into the medical polyclinic. 1909: außerordentlicher Professor. 1912: Killian brings the service into the Charité’s throat and nose clinic. A phonetic laboratory, first private, then attached.

*Des Kindes Sprache und Sprachfehler* (1894), *Physiologie der Stimme und Sprache* (1909), *Sprachheilkunde* (1912), and, the German biographers say, more than three hundred shorter works. Students from everywhere: Nadoleczny, Schilling, Stern, Seeman, Sokolowski, Panconcelli-Calzia, Wethlo, the son Hermann Jr.

## The specialty he actually founded

Not “speech-language pathology” in the ASHA sense. A medical specialty of voice and speech, later joined to pediatric audiology in German training. Teachers came through his rooms; they did not get his title. That split — physician / therapist — is the European structure Americans keep rediscovering whenever they hire an ENT and an SLP for the same voice case.

Gutzmann died in Berlin-Lichterfelde at fifty-seven, in 1922. Fröschels’s Vienna congress was two years off. The American academy was three. The son and the students kept the Charité line alive through a worse century than the father saw.

## How to read him now

Read him as a builder of rooms: journal, clinic, lecture, laboratory. Read his stuttering work as late-nineteenth-century physiology, not as a modern fluency protocol. Do not flatten him into “the German Van Riper.” Van Riper was a psychologist who stuttered and wrote for American undergraduates. Gutzmann was a physician who wanted a chair. They share a subject. They do not share a job.
