Lee Edward Travis was born in 1896 in Imperial, Nebraska, one of twelve children in a sod house, parents teaching at a local academy. All twelve went to college. He died 10 October 1987. ASHA’s founding exhibit quotes a tribute that is almost embarrassing in its size: seldom had one man carried so much of a field. For once the velvet is close to the enrollment lists.

## The three-year climb

Graceland Academy, 1915; Graceland Junior College, 1917; First World War service; then Iowa. B.A. 1922, M.A. 1923, Ph.D. in psychology 1924. Carl Seashore had built the program around him on purpose — a psychologist of speech disorders, especially stuttering. A National Research Council fellowship produced experimental papers. Associate professor, 1927; professor and director of the speech clinic, 1928. Iowa’s later history lets him lead the program through 1938.

He was a charter member of the 1925 academy. The spring dinner at his Iowa City house is the other founding scene.

## Dominance, wires, a handbook

The cerebral-dominance theory of stuttering — Orton’s shadow, Travis’s measurements — is the idea students now meet as a discarded ancestor. He was early with electrophysiology. He was wrong about the simple version of the theory. The students he trained did not stay wrong in the same way. That is a teacher’s victory.

The *Handbook of Speech Pathology* (later editions added audiology) was the big edited book: chapters on the state of each subject, Travis’s name on the spine. It is a different genre from Van Riper’s single-author textbook. Travis organized other people’s expertise. Iowa had given him the habit.

## USC, the war gap, Fuller

He left Iowa for the University of Southern California in 1938, founded and directed a speech and hearing clinic, and held psychology and speech titles into the early 1940s. The published vitae show a later return as clinical professor of psychology (1960–1964) and then a last act that startles speech pathologists: founding dean of psychology at Fuller Seminary, 1965–1975. A 1976 festschrift, *Psychologist pro-tem*, marked his eightieth year.

ASHA Honors, noted in the *Journal of Speech Disorders* in March 1948. Obituary in *ASHA*, December 1987.

## What to do with the Nebraska sod house

Use it once. It is true and it writes itself. Then get to the seminar room. Travis’s importance is not the origin story. It is the decade in which half the field’s theses ran through his shop, and the dinner table at which an academy seemed like a reasonable thing to found.

He also left Iowa in time to miss directing the 1939 orphanage study. That study is Johnson’s. The laboratory culture that made it imaginable is Travis’s too. Both names belong on the Iowa page.
