# The Wisconsin Tradition

In 1914 the University of Wisconsin hired a young Tennessee physician, just out of Cornell Medical College, to do something no American campus had posted on a door. Smiley Blanton became director of the Speech and Mental Hygiene Clinic — the first university-based speech clinic in the United States, if you take Madison’s own later accounting, and the first that later speech pathologists could point to as a parent.

Blanton was not a Ph.D. in speech. He was an M.D. who had taught speech at Cornell (1907–1911) and who read psychiatry as hard as he read phonetics. He took leave for the Phipps clinic at Johns Hopkins with Adolf Meyer, for Queen Square in London, for a diploma in psychological medicine from the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons (1923), and, in 1929–1930, for an analysis with Sigmund Freud. The Madison clinic was “speech” and “mental hygiene” in one phrase because he did not think those were two phrases.

## The first doctorates

Under Blanton the speech department grew a doctoral program. Sara Stinchfield finished in 1922. Her dissertation, *The Formulation and Standardization of a Series of Graded Speech Tests*, is the first American Ph.D. explicitly in this work. Robert West finished in 1925 — ASHA and Wisconsin both call it the second. West had wanted medicine. Blanton talked him into staying. The two students tell you what Madison was selling: tests and a clinic, not a private elocution studio.

Stinchfield went to Mount Holyoke, then to Los Angeles. West stayed, took the clinic after Blanton left in 1924, and directed it from 1928 to 1950. His textbook with Lou Kennedy and Anna Carr, *The Rehabilitation of Speech* (1937), was the book a lot of people owned before they owned Van Riper.

## A different flavor from Iowa

Iowa was psychology, electrophysiology, stuttering, Seashore’s graduate college. Wisconsin was speech as a campus department — public address, theatre, and, down the hall, a clinic with a physician’s ghost still in the furniture. West took nearly all the courses a medical student took and then wrote a public-speaking textbook (1924) anyway. The Wisconsin tradition is the one that kept a foot in the old speech department while Iowa tried to look like a laboratory.

Mildred Templin took a Wisconsin B.A. (1936) and M.S.A. in speech pathology (1937) with West as master’s advisor, then a cleft-palate interest, then a public-school job in Wauwatosa, then a Minnesota Ph.D. in child development. She is a Wisconsin export who made her name on typical children’s language, not on the clinic’s hardest cases. That, too, is a Madison habit: the field is allowed to be developmental, not only corrective.

## Blanton leaves; the clinic remains

Blanton went to the Minneapolis Child Guidance Clinic (1924–1927), to Vassar, back to Cornell psychiatry, and finally to a New York practice and, in 1951, to a religion-and-psychiatry clinic with Norman Vincent Peale. Margaret Gray Blanton, who had less formal schooling and more books than the usual adjunct, lectured at Wisconsin in 1922–23, studied phonetics with Daniel Jones in London, and remained a charter member of the 1925 academy. The Madison door outlasted them. West took it to 1950, then went to Brooklyn College, then to the College of the Pacific. He died in 1968.

The present University of Wisconsin Speech and Hearing Clinic still counts 1914 as year one. Communication Sciences and Disorders at Madison publishes short staff histories of Blanton, Stinchfield Hawk, and West as if they were departmental saints. They were not saints. They were the first people on an American campus who could say, to a parent, that the university ran a clinic for this.

## What “mental hygiene” did

The phrase is dated and a little chilling. It tied speech work to the child-guidance movement, to IQ testing, to a Progressive faith that the state could adjust a personality. It also meant that a child who stuttered might be seen with the family, not only with a tongue depressor. Later behaviorism would mock the Freudian furniture. Later family therapy would quietly take some of it back. Blanton’s clinic is the American starting point for both the overreach and the useful idea that speech trouble arrives with a household attached.
