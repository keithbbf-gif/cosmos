# Charles Van Riper

> **Portrait placeholder.** ASHA’s pioneers exhibit and Western Michigan University hold photographs. Mid-century portraits are almost certainly in copyright. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Charles Gage Van Riper was born 1 December 1905 in Champion, Michigan, on the Upper Peninsula, son of the town physician. He began to stutter at two. He died at home in Kalamazoo on 25 September 1994. (A few directories print the 26th. Memorials written by people who knew him use the 25th.) He wrote under his own name and, as Cully Gage, wrote about the U.P. The speech books are the reason he is here. The U.P. books are the reason the speech books have a voice.

## Michigan, Iowa, Kalamazoo

Northern State Normal School, then the University of Michigan: B.A. 1926, M.A. in English 1930, honors in creative writing. He taught high school English in Saline and hated the fear more than the job. In 1930 he went to Iowa to work under Travis. Ph.D. in clinical psychology, 1934. In 1936 he founded the speech clinic and the speech pathology and audiology department at what became Western Michigan University. He directed the clinic until a heart attack in 1967, became Distinguished University Professor, and retired in 1976. He did not collect campuses. He collected editions.

## The textbook

*Speech Correction: Principles and Methods* (Prentice-Hall, 1939) was not the only early text — West’s 1937 handbook exists — but it became the one undergraduates wore out. Ten editions. *The Nature of Stuttering* and *The Treatment of Stuttering* (early 1970s) were, for a while, the books people meant when they said “the literature.” *Speech Therapy: A Book of Readings* (1953) put West, Travis, Samuel Robbins, Stinchfield Hawk in one set of boards for students who would not otherwise meet them.

He published, the memorials say, on the order of two hundred pieces across eight decades. Treat the round number as a eulogy’s number. The bibliography is long enough without it.

## Modification, not magic

Between 1936 and 1958 he built what later curricula call stuttering modification: reduce the fear, change the moment of stuttering so it is less of a fight, stay in the room. He stuttered in the room. Students watched. The method is not written here as a protocol. The historical fact is that a severe stutterer became the world’s most famous teacher of stuttering and refused to sell a cure.

He is the ethical counterweight to Johnson’s 1939 study without being a saint about it. He worked on adults who could walk out. He wrote for parents (*Your Child’s Speech Problems*) in a tone that is dated and, compared with Davenport, decent.

## Kalamazoo as a capital

Iowa and Wisconsin had the doctorates. Kalamazoo had Van Riper, which for thirty years was enough to make a state-college clinic a pilgrimage. People now say “Western Michigan” and mean a research university. He started it as a clinic in 1936 because Michigan did not have one he wanted to work in.

ASHA’s pioneers exhibit is the official American life. Hugh Morris’s memorial “The Joyous Pathfinder” is the one that sounds like a friend. Read both. Then read a page of *Speech Correction* and notice that the sentences are shorter than this one. That was the gift. He could be famous and still write for a junior.
