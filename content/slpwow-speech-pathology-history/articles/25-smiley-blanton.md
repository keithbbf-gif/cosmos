# Smiley Blanton

Smiley Jordan Blanton was born 7 May 1882 in Unionville, Tennessee, to Hiram and Sallie Brunson Blanton. He died 30 October 1966 and is buried at Mount Olivet in Nashville. In between he opened the first American university speech clinic, sat with Freud, and ended in a Manhattan counseling office with Norman Vincent Peale. The last chapter surprises people who only know the Madison door.

## Cornell, then Wisconsin

B.S., Vanderbilt, 1904. Instructor in speech at Cornell, 1907–1911, while taking an M.D. at Cornell Medical College (1914). On 18 October 1910 he married Margaret Leslie Gray. In 1914 Wisconsin hired him as professor of speech and mental hygiene and director of the Speech and Mental Hygiene Clinic. He published “The University of Wisconsin Speech Clinic” in the *Journal of Educational Psychology* in 1916. That paper is a founding document: a campus admitting that speech trouble is a university responsibility.

He took leave for Phipps and Adolf Meyer (1916), for Army doctoring in the First World War (the finding aids agree he served; some later sketches blur the dates), for Queen Square (1922–23), and for a London diploma in psychological medicine (1923). In 1929–1930, and in later summers, he was in Vienna with Freud. Margaret published his analysis diary after his death (1971).

## The books with Margaret

*Speech Training for Children* (1919), *Child Guidance* (1927), *For Stutterers* (1936). Margaret’s name is on them for a reason. She lectured at Tulane, Wisconsin, Iowa, Vassar; studied phonetics with Daniel Jones; sat in John Watson’s orbit long enough to publish on newborn behavior. She was an ASHA charter member. A profile of Smiley that treats her as a helper is repeating the old program card.

## After Madison

Minneapolis Child Guidance Clinic and a Minnesota medical professorship, 1924–1927. Vassar, 1927–1931. Cornell psychiatry, 1933–1938. Then New York practice, and in 1951 the American Foundation of Religion and Psychiatry with Peale — “psychiatry performs the diagnosis and Christianity supplies the cure,” the Wisconsin finding aid quotes. Popular books: *The Art of Real Happiness* (1950), *Love or Perish* (1956), *The Healing Power of Poetry* (1960).

Speech pathology remembers the 1914–1924 clinic and the doctoral students, Stinchfield and West. The later Blanton is a different American type: the psychiatrist who writes for the general reader and shares a letterhead with a famous pastor. Both are him.

University of Tennessee holds the papers. Wisconsin holds the clinic’s institutional memory. Margaret’s diary of the Freud analysis is a primary source for the later mind, not for the 1914 timetable. When a later sketch makes Blanton a full-time speech pathologist until 1966, it is telescoping. He left the campus clinic and became a psychiatrist who still wrote about stutterers. The field kept the clinic; he kept the couch.

## What the clinic meant

It meant a physician, not an elocutionist, on a campus speech budget. It meant “mental hygiene,” a phrase with Progressive steel in it. It meant stuttering as a family event. Later Iowa would try to make stuttering a laboratory event. Both experiments are still in the furniture.
