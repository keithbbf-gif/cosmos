# H. Harlan Bloomer

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain portrait located. Try the Bentley Historical Library, University of Michigan. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Henry Harlan Bloomer took a University of Michigan Ph.D. in 1935 — *A Method of Determination of the Diaphragmatic Factor in Respiration* — and spent the rest of a long career making the midsection of the vocal tract a measurable object. A 2000 cleft-palate state-of-the-art paper dedicated itself to him as H. Harlan Bloomer, Ph.D., 1908–1999. Those are the life dates this pack uses. Do not confuse him with Harlan H. Bloomer (1943–2024), an artist born in Ann Arbor to Henry and Hope Bloomer. That obituary is a different man, probably a son. The speech pathologist is the 1935 doctorate and the 1955 ASHA presidency.

## Michigan’s ear

By 1942 he was publishing in the *Journal of Speech Disorders* on a simple method for testing the hearing of small children. In 1943, as Henry Harlan Bloomer, he described a palatograph for contour-mapping the palate in the *Journal of the American Dental Association*. In 1953, “Observations on Palatopharyngeal Movements in Speech and Deglutition” in *JSHD* — a paper dentists and surgeons still find in old bibliographies. In 1958, with Paul Gibbons, a supportive-type prosthetic speech aid in the *Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry*. In 1968, with David Prins, consonant intelligibility as a way to evaluate speech in oral-cleft patients.

This is not a stuttering life. It is a Michigan life: respiration, palate, prosthesis, the child who cannot close the velopharyngeal port, the joint clinic with dentistry and ENT. The 1935 dissertation title is the giveaway. He started with the diaphragm as a measurable factor and spent sixty years refusing to treat the vocal tract as a mystery the surgeon owned alone.

Ann Arbor’s speech clinic in those decades sat inside a university that also had a strong medical school. Bloomer used that accident. Palatography and cinefluorography were not hobbies. They were ways to put a picture in a chart that a prosthodontist and a speech person could point at together. McDonald was doing the parent-pamphlet and sensory-motor version of the same conversation at Penn State. The two men do not need to have been friends for the conversation to be real.

## The 1955 gavel and a 1956 syllabus

ASHA’s presidential list puts Harlan Bloomer in 1955. In 1956 he published “Professional Training in Speech Correction and Clinical Audiology.” He still wanted the two trained together. The job market later separated them. The paper is a snapshot of the last moment when a Michigan professor could treat the split as a curriculum problem rather than a fact.

In 1967 he reviewed Rieber and Brubaker’s international *Speech Pathology* for the *Quarterly Journal of Speech* — “A Baedeker of speech pathology.” He knew the field had other countries.

## Why he is in the user-requested roster

Because the requested names were not only the Iowa stutterers. Bloomer is the American line that runs through cleft palate, palatal lift, cinefluorography, and the idea that a speech person belongs in the surgical conference. McDonald is the Penn State cousin. Darley, who also died in 1999, is the Mayo cousin. The 2000 dedication that named Bloomer and Darley together was not a coincidence. That year the anatomic field felt the air go out of the room.

Birth and death years come from that dedication, not from a newspaper obituary in our hands. If a later editor finds a Michigan necrology with different days, print the necrology.
