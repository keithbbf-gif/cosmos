# Eugene T. McDonald

> **Portrait placeholder.** Penn State University Archives is the first ask. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Library catalogs print him as McDonald, Eugene T. (Eugene Thomas), 1916–. The dash is still open in the sources this pack could verify. He received ASHA’s Honors of the Association in 1976. He taught at Pennsylvania State University. He wrote the cleft-palate and articulation books a generation of clinicians actually used. His death date was not found in a citable necrology by 14 September 2026. If a later editor finds an obituary, add it. Do not invent one.

## Cleft palate as a joint problem

“Cleft Palate Speech: An Integration of Research and Clinical Observation,” with Herbert Koepp-Baker, *JSHD* 16 (1951), is an early postwar attempt to stop treating the palate as a surgeon’s souvenir and the speech as a teacher’s afterthought. “Speech Considerations in Cleft Palate Prosthesis,” *Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry*, same year. *Bright Promise for Your Child with Cleft Lip and Cleft Palate*, written for the National Society for Crippled Children and Adults (1959; revised edition later), is a parent book. This series will not extract its advice. It will say McDonald thought parents were part of the clinic, and that he put that thought in a cheap pamphlet as well as in a journal.

## The 1964 pair

*Articulation Testing and Treatment: A Sensory-Motor Approach* (Stanwix House, 1964) and *Cerebral Palsy* in the Prentice-Hall Foundations of Speech Pathology series (1964, with Burton Chance Jr. on related titles) are the books that made him a national name beyond cleft teams. The sensory-motor approach — sounds in shifting phonetic contexts, not only in isolation — was a mid-century alternative to older sound-by-sound drill. Later phonological theories argued with it. They still cite it.

## Boards, not just mouths

“Communication Boards for Cerebral-Palsied Children,” with Adeline R. Schultz, *JSHD* 38 (1973), sits in the ancestry of AAC. McDonald also appears in the 1975 Trace Center workshop volume on non-vocal communication techniques. A man known for palates and /s/ was already writing about boards for children who would not talk with their mouths. That is worth more than a sentimental reading. It means he followed the impairment to the tool.
