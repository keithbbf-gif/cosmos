# Robert West

> **Portrait placeholder.** ASHA Archives hold founding-era photographs. Rights not cleared for this repo. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Robert William West (1892–1968) was ASHA’s first president, the second American to take a Ph.D. in the new speech work, and the man who ran Wisconsin’s clinic from 1928 to 1950. The obituaries in *ASHA* (1968, 10: 331–332) are the place a later editor should go for the family’s dates. The public facts are already enough to fill a door.

## Reedsburg, war, Madison

Born 1892, Reedsburg, Wisconsin, into a family that took degrees seriously — a Rhodes-scholar brother, a sister with a Ph.D. in economics, a father who taught mathematics at Milton College. West’s B.A. is 1916. ASHA’s founding biography says Milton College; some Duchan pages say West Virginia College and a long spell of country-school teaching first. The 1916 bachelor’s and the later Madison degrees are not in dispute.

The war years are. ASHA says he enlisted and served two years. Duchan says hyperthyroidism kept him out of the armed forces and puts him in the medical corps at Camp Wheeler. Both cannot be simply true. This pack prints the disagreement rather than picking a cinematic version.

M.A., Wisconsin, 1920. He taught public speaking, wrote a college text (1924), wanted medicine, and stayed for a Ph.D. in speech after Blanton leaned on him. ASHA and UW–Madison: 1925. One Duchan page: 1927. Use 1925; flag 1927.

Assistant professor at Wisconsin the same year, associate 1926, professor and clinic director 1928. He had, ASHA says, already spent a stretch at Iowa as well. The Midwest was a small pond.

## The first gavel

December 1925, Hotel McAlpin: temporary president. He kept the chair through 1928. In 1943 he was president of the Speech Association of America, the old teachers’ group the academy had splintered from. ASHA Honors, 1947. He helped invent the club and then went back to the parent club with a title. That is a politician’s gift, and the new field needed one.

## The book

*The Rehabilitation of Speech*, with Lou Kennedy and Anna Carr (Harper, 1937), was the handbook a lot of people carried before Van Riper’s 1939 book became the undergraduate weather. Later editions kept the hospital noun on the spine while the field changed clothes around it. “Rehabilitation” is already a different word from “correction.” West had taken enough medical coursework to want that noun. He also wanted the clinic to produce data, not only service hours — Wisconsin’s later CSD histories still credit him with making the Madison shop a research site.

## Brooklyn, Stockton, 1968

In 1950 he left Madison for the speech and hearing clinic at Brooklyn College. In 1963 he went to the College of the Pacific. He died in 1968. Wisconsin’s later CSD department still writes him up as the first working scientist on the old speech faculty — the person who made the clinic a research site, not only a service.

He is easy to reduce to “first president.” The better reduction is: the second doctorate, the first long Madison directorship, the 1937 handbook, the man who could run a meeting. Stinchfield Hawk held the paper. West held the gavel. The association needed both hands.
