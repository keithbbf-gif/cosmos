A 70-degree shop grows scale. It does not grow winter.

We overwinter pots in the woodshop glass and we have been building more covered space because 650 plants do not fit a kindness. That kindness is good for a potted tree you do not want at 15°F. It is a mixed blessing for a cutting tray.

Warm enough, the buds move. Dim enough, the leaves stretch. Wet enough — because someone waters the citrus — the cups never lose weight. Soft enough, the scale and the mealy find a stem that never got a real dormancy.

I keep the cutting block away from the hose that fertigates. I keep it off the bench that gets Saturday traffic. I keep it cooler than is comfortable for me if I can. A cutting does not need a guest room. It needs a closet with a controlled mat.

Scale is the shop problem nobody photographs. 650 pots in glass means someone is always watering something. The hose that is right for a dry #3 is wrong for a tray of pops. I set the cutting block on a separate board with its own saucer and I write CUTTINGS on the tape so the Saturday hand does not “just give them a drink.” I do not fertigate that board. I do not share a flood tray with the citrus. If I have to root in the same room, I put the tray higher and farther from the hose bib than is convenient. Convenience is how a kind shop stays wet.

Scale on a cutting is easier to miss than scale on a 5-gallon. Look at the internodes. A cottony fluff is not a rooting success. If I see it, I deal with that plant apart from the tray. I do not spray a whole block of pops “to be safe” and change every climate at once.

What I do with scale on a cutting: isolate that cup. A cotton swab and alcohol on a visible bump if the wood is still firm and I can reach it without stripping bark. If the internodes are dotted and the leaf is sticky, that cup leaves the tray. I do not treat the whole block “because they touched.” They sat in the same weather. They did not share a bloodstream. A spray that soaks every cover and every mix is a new climate I did not ask for. Mealy gets the same exile. The tray is a nursery, not a hospital ward.

8a winter in a shop is not dormancy. Dad already said the trees usually are not fully dormant in there — cool enough they do not grow much. Cuttings read that as “maybe I should leaf.” Expect liars. Believe walls and weight.

When we move trays toward the new covered space, I watch the sun angle. New plastic is brighter than old shop dust. A tray that was perfect in the back corner will cook on week one of a new house. Harden the tray like you harden a plant.

Warm shop plus dim glass makes liar leaves. I expect them. I still believe weight and the wall. I also expect the new covered space to be brighter than the dusty corner we left. When I move a tray I do it in the morning, I give it a week of shade cloth or a step back from the south wall, and I watch the first afternoon. A cup that was perfect in the back will bleach and then collapse if I celebrate the new house by parking it in the sun. Harden the tray. The pots can take more light than the pops. Do not make them share a baptism.

Zone 7 garages are colder and cleaner for cuttings, meaner for pots. Zone 9 sheds are just outside. Our shop is a third climate. Name it. Do not call it “indoors” as if indoors were one thing.

I would rather root in a slightly too-cold corner with a thermostat than in a kind room with a hose and a window. Kindness is for the #3s in January. The stick wants less of our hospitality and more of a boring cup.

I would rather run fewer cups well than fill every kind square foot we built. Shop scale is how you start eighty sticks because the bench is empty in January and then you cannot keep the squeeze honest in March. A smaller tray I can weigh is worth more than a city of cups I water by mood. If the shop is too kind, the correction is not more gadgets. It is a cooler corner, a taped-off hose, and a tray I can lift with one hand.
