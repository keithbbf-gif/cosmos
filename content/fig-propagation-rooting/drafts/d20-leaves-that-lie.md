The leaves come out and you relax. A week later the whole thing folds like a cheap tent.

I have done the relax. It is the most expensive minute in the shop.

A fig cutting can push a leaf from stored food. That leaf is not proof of roots. It is proof the buds were alive. In 8a warmth those buds move early. The cup looks like a plant. The base may still be a dry stick or a knob or a sour mash.

What I do when the first leaf shows: take the cover off so it does not steam. Do not step up the light yet. Do not feed. Do not pot-up on the leaf alone. Look at the cup wall or wait until I see roots when I must. Weight still tells the truth. A leafy cup that went light fast is a leaf spending water the mix cannot replace.

If the leaf is pale and stretching, the corner is too dim *and* the plant is still a cutting. I would rather have a compact leaf in low light than a sail in a window. The sail cooks.

If the leaf blackens overnight, you had a cold draft or a wet cover lying on it or a mat spike. The leaf is a sensor. It is not the patient. Check the mix before you mourn the variety.

People pinch the first leaves “to force roots.” I do not. The cutting is already on a budget. I pinch a huge sail that is wicking, maybe. I do not strip a plant because a forum told me to be cruel.

8a April sun through glass will burn a tender leaf in an afternoon. Move the block back. The greenhouse is not a reward until there are roots.

I have come back from a warm February week away and found a tray that leafed while I was gone. The shop felt pleasant. The cups looked like plants. Half of them were still knobs. I did not celebrate. I took covers off the ones that were steaming, left the block in the dim corner, and I lifted cups instead of potting the pretty ones. A leaf that dropped in that week was not a death. It was a budget. The wood that stayed firm stayed in the tray.

Feed is the other lie. A pale leaf on a cutting looks hungry. It is not asking for fertigate. It is asking you not to add salts to a wound that is not drinking yet. I have watched people mix a weak fertilizer “just a sip” because the cup looked like a pot. The cup is still a climate. Food belongs after threads hit the wall and the plant is a #3, not after the first green flag.

I do not line leafy cups next to rooted cups and treat the row as one job. The rooted ones can take a brighter hour. The liars cannot. Mixing them on the same wagon for a Saturday clean-up is how the liars cook. I keep two blocks if I have to: wall-shows-white, and leaf-only. The leaf-only block stays boring on purpose.

When the leaf is real and the roots are on the wall, then you have a plant. Then light can come up. Then a shaded outdoor day. Then a pot. The order is the whole method. Reverse it and you get a pretty death.

Zone 7 growers see leaves later and trust them more, because the room was cold enough that a leaf often means roots. Zone 9 growers see leaves immediately and should trust them less. We should trust them less. Our shop is a Zone 9 story in February some weeks.

I write “leaf / no root” on a cup if I am being honest with myself. That note has saved me from potting a liar into a #3 and then wondering why it wilted in the wagon.

Believe the thread. The leaf is advertising.
