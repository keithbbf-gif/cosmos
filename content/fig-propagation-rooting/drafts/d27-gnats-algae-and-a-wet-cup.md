If the cup never gets light in the hand, you do not have a moisture method. You have a swamp, and the swamp has residents.

Fungus gnats love wet coir and a warm mat. They are not a moral failing. They are a wet-cup flag. Mosquito Bits — *Bt israelensis* — is what we have used in the water. The A/B inoculated with that and MycoStop. I am not telling you to buy a stack of biologicals. I am telling you gnats are a symptom. Dry the habit a notch. Let the top of the mix go from shiny to dull between drinks. Bottom-water a light cup. Do not keep the surface as a nursery.

How I mix the Bits: I follow the bag, I do not invent a stronger tea because I am angry at the cloud. I water that mix into the cups that are already too wet, then I stop watering from the top until the surface goes dull. I do not dunk a tray of gnats in a bathtub of leftover solution and call it a reset. That resets nothing. The larvae are in the top inch. Let that inch dry to dull. Bottom-water the light cups from a clean tray. If I see adults on the sticky card the next week, I still do not fog the shop. I look at which cups never lost weight. Those cups are the farm.

Algae is the green film on a cup that lives in light and never dries. Clear cups in a bright shop grow it. It is ugly and it is a sign. Move the block dimmer or let the surface breathe. Algae is not feeding your cutting.

Clear cups earn their keep here. I can see the green film start as a stain on the sunny side before it is a carpet. I rotate the block so the stained side faces the dimmer wall, or I sleeve the cup with a scrap of paper. I do not bleach the mix while a cutting is in it. I do not scrape algae with a stick and shove the scrape back in. If the film is thick and the wood is still firm, I can slip the root ball into a clean cup of fresh damp mix and throw the green one. If the wood is soft under that film, I throw both.

A sour smell is rot or stale water in the saucer. Empty the saucer. If the base is soft, the cutting is over. If the mix is sour and the wood is firm, change the mix and recut. Do not perfume it with more products.

8a makes all of this faster. Warm, wet, organic mix — congratulations, you invented a bottomland. DE in that test was less gnat-prone and less rot-prone when watered right. Coir was easier to keep even and easier to keep too wet. Choose your trouble.

8a April on a mat is gnat weather even if you did everything right in February. Warm nights, wet coir, a shop that never gets a freeze once the glass holds heat. I pull the mat sooner than the basement people did. Their room was 30–40% RH. Ours is not. A cup that needed the mat in January can sit on the bench in April and still root if the wood is firm. Gnats in April are often the mat plus a cover you forgot. Unplug. Vent. Then Bits if the adults are still a cloud.

I do not pour leftover fertilizer into a rooting cup because the gnats “need the soil to be healthy.” The cutting is not a lawn. No feed until it is a plant in a pot.

Yellow sticky cards will catch adults and will not fix the swamp. Fine. Use them if you want a count. Then fix the squeeze.

Zone 7 cold rooms have fewer gnats and more slow rot. Zone 9 shade-houses have gnats as a lifestyle. We get the lifestyle as soon as the mat and the coir agree.

If you see larvae and the wood is still firm, you can still win. Lighten the cup. Bits in the water. Patience. If you see larvae and the wood is already slumping, you are cataloging a death. Throw it before it shakes hands with the next cup.

The cup should get lighter. If it never does, you are not rooting. You are brewing.
