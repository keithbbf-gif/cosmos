LSU calls air-layering girdling and means a ring of bark off. That works on figs. A simpler wound — a slice, a ring that does not meet, a couple of scores — also works because figs want to root. I would rather do a clean **ring of bark** on 1-year wood than a messy chew that leaves hangers.

1-year wood roots faster than old slabs. LSU says that. I believe it in the hand. Green-brown last year’s cane, pencil to finger thick. Not a trunk unless you are doing a pot-around-the-trunk layer and you have months, not weeks.

How I ring: two shallow cuts around the cane, an inch or so apart, and peel the bark between. Cambium shows. I do not carve into the heartwood like I am mad at the tree. I wipe the knife. This is not surgery theater. It is a clean wound so the moss sits on living tissue, not on ragged bark.

I pick 1-year wood because LSU said it and because my hands agree. Green-brown last year’s cane, pencil to finger. I can girdle a thicker slab and wait longer. I do not start there on a name I want this season. Two cuts around, an inch or so apart, peel the tube of bark. If a hanger of phloem stays, I peel it. Hangers are bridges. Bridges are how the canopy keeps feeding past the wound and the moss stays a decoration. I do not oil the ring. I do not paint it. I wrap damp long-fiber sphagnum the same hour so the cambium does not dry in the 8a wind while I look for the camera.

Some people dust hormone on the ring. Optional. Same sermon as the cup. Good wood and damp moss beat a crust of gel.

Then the sleeve. Moss, plastic, tie above and below so the wad cannot slide down on a 90°F afternoon. A slipped sleeve is a dry wound with a tan line.

Ties are the other wound. I tie above and below tight enough the wad cannot slide on a 90°F afternoon, loose enough I am not strangling the bark I left. A twist tie that cuts in is a second girdle you did not mean. I check the ties after the first hot week. I shade the plastic. I do not leave a black sleeve on the west side and call it done. If I skip the ring and only score, I still wrap a fist of moss. A score can work on a fig. A clean ring works more often in my hands. I would rather peel an inch I was going to cut off in six to eight weeks than trust intact bark to invent roots because I was in a hurry.

If you skip the wound and just wrap moss on intact bark, you might still get roots on a fig. You might also get a damp decoration. I would wound. The tree can take an inch of missing bark on a cane you were willing to cut off anyway.

Do not girdle the only leader on a young pot. Do not girdle a trunk you have not decided to keep. A ring on a trunk is a commitment. A ring on a side cane is a harvest.

8a sap is moving in June. The wound will wet. That is fine. If the tree is bleeding like a hose, you picked a day after rain and fertilizer. It will still layer. Wash your hands. Do not stand there admiring it in the sun until the moss dries on the way to the branch.

Zone 7 wounds close slower. Zone 9 wounds try to callus past your moss if you wait. We get a callus that wants to be a root if the moss stays honest.

Ol’ Rusty is a grafting knife. Pruners will do the rings if they are sharp. Dull pruners crush. Crush is not a girdle. Crush is a bruise that sours.

Do not girdle in August on named wood. June start, six to eight weeks, pot in late July or early August. Spring is legal once the tree is moving — LSU’s window — and four to six weeks of warmth is their number. I still want June when I can have it. A ring on a cane in August is a bill the pot cannot pay before the dimmer. Extra wood can be a cutting. The only leader on a young pot is not a layer.

Make the wound like you mean to cut the cane off in eight weeks. Because you do.
