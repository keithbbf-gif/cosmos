You pull the stick and there is a white or cream knob at the base. Alive. No roots. The top may have leaves. Then the leaves cook the cutting because nothing is drinking.

Callus is scar tissue. It is the wound closing. It is not a root. Some varieties sit on a callus a long time. Fast and slow is real. The failure is not the knob. The failure is what you do next.

I do not panic-pot a callus. I do not drown it to “wake it up.” That is how callus-only becomes rot. I do not peel the cup open every morning to photograph the knob. Every peek is a climate change.

What I do: leave it. Check weight. If the wood is firm and the mix is damp, wait. A little more bottom heat — still on a thermostat — if the mix is cool. Loosen a pack that is a brick. Do not fertilize. Do not give it a south window because the leaves asked.

Leaves with no roots is a battery running a screen with the charger unplugged. Reduce light. The plant is spending. The cup is not earning.

A root is a white thread that keeps going. It has a tip. It is not a blob. When those threads hit the wall of a clear cup, you have a different job: pot-up, gentle, because pop roots are fragile.

8a warmth makes callus fast and also makes leaves fast. That is why our trays lie. A northern grower gets roots before a show. We get a show. Believe the wall of the cup, not the leaf.

If the callus turns brown and soft, that is rot wearing a costume. Throw it. If the callus stays cream and the wood stays firm at week five, you are in straggler country. The A/B had those. Covers went on some DE cups late. I would not invent a hormone dunk at week five as a rescue. Optional hormone belongs at stick day.

People scrape the callus off and restick. Sometimes they get roots. Sometimes they get a shorter stick and the same knob. I only recut if the knob is rotting and there is clean wood above it. I change the mix when I do.

I tell a cream knob from a thread on a clear cup without a daily autopsy. A blob sits. A root has a tip and keeps going. If I cannot see the wall, I lift the cup and I wait. I do not open a pop every morning for a photograph. That peek is how I snap the first thread I have not earned yet. I write callus-only and the date on the tape so week five does not feel like week two.

When the neighbor cup shows white and this one is still a knob, I do not slide the slow name onto a hotter corner. Extra heat without a thermostat is how you cook the one you cared about. If the mix is actually cool, the probe stays in the mix and the controller stays where I wrote it — 75–78°F, or 76–80°F if I am chasing a cool basement the way the 2023 mats did at 80°F. I do not twist the dial to “high” because I am impatient.

A video will tell you to scrape a healthy callus to restart it. I have gotten a shorter stick and the same knob. I scrape only when the knob is brown and soft and there is clean wood above it, and then the mix is new. I will put a cover on a dry DE straggler late, the way that test did, if the cup went light and the wood is still firm. I will not hormone-dunk a five-week knob as a rescue. Optional hormone belongs on stick day.

Zone 7 callus in a cold room is just slow. Zone 9 callus in a hot room is a week from leaves. We get the hot-room problem in a shop that feels pleasant to us. Pleasant to us is expensive to a leafless stick.

Name it. Callus-only is a status, not a success and not a death. Status means you wait with a dated cup. Success is a thread. Death is mush. Do not file the knob under “rooted” because you want the tray to look finished.
