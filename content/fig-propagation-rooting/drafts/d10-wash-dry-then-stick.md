I did not know how dirty a cutting was until we started scrubbing them.

The bark looks brown and honest. Then the water turns the color of tea and you see the film. That film is dust, sap, and whatever lived in the bag. It is a head start for slime once you add warmth and a damp mix.

Wash first. Soap and water is enough for clean yard wood. The Pop page also allows a dilute bleach — **10 parts water to 1 part bleach** — or straight hydrogen peroxide. Dad’s 2023 cups got a Physan 20 soak after a 24-hour hydrate. That was a basement test with mail-order wood, not a law for a stick you walked in from the yard. Do not stack every disinfectant you own on one cutting and call it science.

The order that matters: wash, rinse if you used soap or bleach, **dry the bark**, then recut, then stick. Wet bark into wet mix is how you start already dripping. The squeeze test is for the coir, not for a cutting that is still shining.

I dry them on a towel in shade. Not on a dashboard. Not in a closed plastic tub “to keep them fresh.” Fresh is dry bark and a damp mix.

Mold on the surface: wash what is firm, throw what is soft. A fuzzy node in the middle of a three-node stick may leave you two. A cane that bends like a noodle is done. Do not wash mush and put it back.

Yard wood we cut an hour ago still gets a rinse. Chainsaw dust, dog, and a tarp that held last year’s leaves are not sterile. I am not doing surgery. I am not handing the cup a gift.

8a humidity makes this less optional than a dry-western grower thinks. Our air is already a greenhouse. A dirty stick in a covered cup is a petri dish with a name on it.

I do not soak clean dormant wood for 24 hours as a lifestyle. The A/B wood had been shipped. Hydrating a wrinkled truck stick can help. Hydrating a firm yard stick until it is waterlogged is how you skip straight to rot. If I soak, I dry.

Recut after the wash. The old end has been in dirt, bleach, or a bag. A fresh cut is the wound you actually want against the mix. Score the bottom inch or two if you are following the Pop or the A/B — expose cambium, do not peel the stick like a carrot.

Zone 7 storage wood that sat two months needs the wash more than the stick you cut this morning. Zone 9 green wood needs the wash and the same-day stick. We do both in one Saturday in 8a. Two bins: dirty incoming, clean outgoing. Do not mix them because the table is small.

Yard wood gets the hose. Mail-order wood gets the shop sink. Clay grit and tarp dust stay outside. I have already scored a slick cane at the rinse and peeled it like a carrot. Wet wood hides depth. The shop towel, then the knife, then the cup. A score full of grit is a wound I just washed the bark to avoid.

I already washed and stuck wet. Bark still shining, mix already at the squeeze plus about 30% dry coir. Buried node went sour in a week on a mat that was otherwise honest — 75–78°F, probe in the mix. The mix was not the second problem. I was. Dry bark, then the fresh recut, then the cup. Physan after a 24-hour hydrate was for the 2023 shipped Figaholics wood, not for a stick I walked in from the yard.

Named cups and replaceable bed wood do not share a wash bucket. I have carried a dripping yard stick across the shop and dripped tarp tea into a 32-ounce cup of mix I had squeezed right. The cup went sour. The bed would have forgiven it. The name did not. February 8a is both jobs before lunch. Two waters. Two towels.

Dad’s wall line is still the right one: do not overwater the mix. Washing is how you keep from adding a second problem. Clean wood, damp mix, dry bark. That is the whole religion.
