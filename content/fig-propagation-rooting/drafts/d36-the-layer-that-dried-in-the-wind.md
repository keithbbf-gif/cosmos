Wind is an extra zone for layers, same as it is for winter wood.

A sleeve that was perfect on Saturday can be a rattle on Thursday if it sits on a west corner with no shade. The plastic looks fine. The moss inside is a biscuit. The wound is a scar that will not root. You open it at week six and you have a brown ring and a sermon.

I site layers on the east side when I can, or on an interior cane the hose can reach. I do not layer the romantic branch that faces the field because the photo would be good.

Ties fail in heat. Plastic slips. A slipped wad hangs like a sock and dries from both ends. I check ties after the first hot week. That is a two-minute job. Replacing a name is a year.

West wind in our lot is a daily thief. It does not look like weather. It looks like a nice June day. The sleeve rattles, the moss goes biscuit, the ring goes brown, and week six is a sermon. I site on the east side or on an interior cane. I put a white wrap or paper over the plastic so the sun is not a second wind. I leave a way to add water. After the first hot, windy stretch I lift every sleeve I started that week. Two minutes. A light wad gets a squirt. A slipped sock gets retied. I do not wait for the calendar to tell me the moss died.

Ants hollow a dry-then-wet sleeve. I have pulled moss that was a condo. If the wood is still cream, rewind. If the wood is channeled and brown, cut it off. The tree will thank you more than the ants.

8a June wind is not a hurricane. It is daily. Daily is enough. A bottle drip or a second wrap of something white over the plastic is uglier than a dead ring.

The other failure is the swamp: no wind, full sun, sealed black plastic, rain in the top tie. That sleeve sours. It smells like a vase of flowers you forgot. Same end. Different weather. Feel the wad after storms and after wind. Those are the two 8a inspections.

I do not “mist the leaves” to save a dry layer. I rewet the moss or I quit. Misting the canopy is how you pretend.

If I open a biscuit and the wood under it is still cream, I rewind the same hour with fresh wrung long-fiber sphagnum. I do not mist the leaves and walk away. If the wood is brown and dry through the ring, I cut the cane off clean below the wound and I let the tree close. I do not leave a dead ring on a name because I might “try again higher.” Higher on a tired cane is how you make two scars. Scratch above the ring before you turn that wood into cuttings. A girdled cane that baked is often tired above the ring too. Compost is allowed.

Zone 7 dry-out is a woodstove week. Zone 9 dry-out is every afternoon. We get the afternoon and the thunderstorm. Two ways to fail in seven days.

The swamp is the other week. No wind, sealed black plastic, rain in the top tie, then sun. That wad sours. It smells like a vase you forgot. Same dead ring, different weather. I feel sleeves after storms and after wind. Those are the two 8a inspections. I do not add a third inspection called breakfast. Ants will farm either failure. If I pull a condo and the wood is still cream, rewind. If the wood is channeled, cut it off. The tree would rather close a clean wound than host a farm.

If I lose a layer, I do not immediately cut the cane into cuttings unless the wood above the ring is still honest. A girdled cane that dried is often tired above the ring too. Scratch. Compost is allowed.

Start fewer sleeves. Put them on the path you already walk to the hose. June, 1-year wood, six to eight weeks, named wood does not get August. The layer that dies in the back of the lot dies of distance, not of sphagnum. I would rather three layers I can lift after a south wind than a dozen I find in October when the plastic is a rattle and the cane is a stick.
