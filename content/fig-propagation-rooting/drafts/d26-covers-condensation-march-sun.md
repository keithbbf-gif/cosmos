A cover is a tool for a dry room. Our March is not always a dry room.

The A/B used Press’n Seal on coir because the basement sat at 30–40% RH. That cover made sense. They pursed it to keep leaves from lying in condensation. They took it off when leaves began so they did not shock the plant later. DE started uncovered and got covers only when it lagged.

Copy that in a steamy 8a shop and you grow a terrarium. I cover when the mix would dry too fast — a drafty corner, a week with the heat on, DE that went light. I do not cover because the video had shiny plastic.

I keep a roll of Press’n Seal and a stack of cheap dome lids. I do not keep a religion about which one. Seal is for a single cup that is drying on the edge of the tray. A dome is for a block of cups that share a mat and a draft. A grocery bag over a pot is for a rescue that went light overnight. I write COVER and the date on the tape so I remember I put weather on that plant. A cover with no date is how January plastic is still sitting there in May. If I cannot say why the cover is on, I take it off.

If the lid is raining, vent. A few holes. A lifted corner. Off for a day. A leaf in a puddle gets spots and then a collapse you will blame on “fungus” as if fungus arrived from space. You built the weather.

March sun through glass turns a covered cup into a car interior. I have touched plastic that could raise a blister. That is not humidity. That is cook. Move the block. Unplug the mat. Take the lid off before you take a picture for a forum.

How I vent: I do not stab twenty holes like I am making a colander. Two or three on the high side, or I fold one corner back and clip it. I watch the lid for rain, not for fog. Fog on the wall can stay. Drops on a leaf get a vent the same morning. If the mix is still dark and the cup is heavy, venting is the drink. If the mix is already dull and the cup is light, the cover was doing real work and I leave it, I just give it a crack. I check this when I walk in, not at noon when the shop is already a skillet.

8a April is worse. The cover that helped in January is an injury in April. Write it on the tape: cover until leaves or until the room is already wet, whichever comes first.

I do not double-cover. A bag in a tote on a mat under a dome is three climates fighting. Pick one.

When I take a cover off, I do it in the morning, not at 2 p.m. in a hot shop. Give the leaf an hour to learn the room.

When I wean a cover I do it over two or three mornings. Day one, lid cracked. Day two, lid off until the afternoon heat, then back if the leaf flags. Day three, off. I do not rip a sweaty dome at 2 p.m. and then wonder why the leaf cooked. 8a March sun through the woodshop glass will do that in twenty minutes. Last frost here is usually early to mid-April [VERIFY] for your county. A cover that made sense in February is a trap once the glass starts throwing heat. I would rather a slightly dry cup in late March than a steamed one.

Zone 7 covers stay on longer. Zone 9 covers are often a mistake except in air-conditioning. We switch months. January cover, March maybe, May almost never.

Condensation on the *wall* of a cup can be fine. Condensation on the *leaf* is a problem. Condensation plus sun is a scald. Name which one you have before you add another gadget.

I do not cover a cup that already has algae or a sour smell. That cup needs air and a lighter hand, not a tent. I do not cover a cup sitting in a saucer of water. That is two climates stacked. I do not cover DE the same week I cover coir as a block policy. DE in that 2023 basement needed a cover when it lagged. Coir needed one taken off. Copy the plant in front of you. The plastic aisle will sell you a third climate you do not owe the stick.

The mix should hold the humidity. The cover is a backup. If the backup is doing all the work, fix the squeeze, not the plastic aisle.
