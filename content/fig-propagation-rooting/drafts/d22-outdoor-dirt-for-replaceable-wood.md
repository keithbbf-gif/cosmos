Dad will tell you a raised bed of yard dirt has rooted wood with nothing on it. That sentence is true and it is also a fence.

Outdoor dirt is for wood we can replace: prune extras, a known yard fig, a name we have three of. A mailbox name that cost sleep does not go in a bed we weed twice a summer. That name goes in a cup I can pick up.

The outdoor method is almost insulting. Stick lignified wood into dirt that drains. Shade. Water when the bed is dry, not on a guilt schedule — once or twice a week on bulk starts is the outdoor page, not a hose every afternoon. Wait. In 8a spring, the season is the mat.

Why it works here: we have a long warm shoulder. A March or April stick in a bed has weeks of soil that is not 40°F. Zone 7 outdoor beds are later and crueler. Zone 9 outdoor beds work almost too well and then August cooks the ones that never made volume.

Why it fails: standing water in clay. A full-sun bed in June on new sticks. Dogs. A label that washed off. I plant on a broad mound if the yard perches water. I do not dig a grave of potting mix in clay and call it a bed. The grave becomes a bucket.

Spacing: enough that one rot does not shake hands with the next stick. This is not a seedling flat. It is a row of bets.

I still wash the wood. I still want three nodes. I still want pencil or a bit thicker — outdoor can take the fatter cane. I do not hormone a whole bed as a ritual. Optional at most.

Shade cloth in May is cheaper than a funeral in July. Afternoon sun in South Arkansas is a different climate than morning sun. An east-side bed is a kindness.

Dogs find a bed before I do. So do kids and a hose left running. I fence what I can and I plant the row where a truck does not back up. After a two-day sit of storm water I walk the mound. If the clay perched a pond, I do not add more hose to “help.” I wait for the shoulder to drain. A stick in a puddle is the same death as a wound on the floor of a cup.

Weeding is a slow job. I hold the stick when I pull grass so I do not yank a callus I cannot see. I do not hoe a row of new cuttings like it is corn. If I want to know whether the bed is working, I lift one replaceable stick at the edge, look for threads, and put it back. I do not excavate the named end of a row I should not have planted named wood in.

A box that landed late does not get hidden in that dirt “until I have cups.” Late wood is a table job: open, sort, wash, cup or trash. The bed is not a drawer. Prune extras go in with nothing on them. Hormone stays optional and stays on stick day if I use it at all. I still want three nodes and wood I can replace.

When they move: not in August. A rooted outdoor stick can go to a #3 in fall or wait until late winter and move like a small bare-root. I like a pot before the first real freeze if the roots are still a fist. An 8a winter in a bed is fine for hardy wood that actually rooted. It is not fine for a stick that only callused and then sat. Moving a callus-only stick in the heat is how you pay twice.

Named wood and bulk wood get different manners. The bed can look messy. The cups cannot. If I mix them, I will treat the rare name like a weed and the weed like a rare name.

Use the dirt. Use it on purpose. Do not use it as a place to hide a box you opened too late.
