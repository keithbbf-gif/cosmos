Some names root in ten days. Some sit. The coir tray in 2023 had roots around day 10 on the fast ones and stragglers later. Easton Vasilika was the last one to 64/64, at five weeks. I am not making your variety that variety. I am telling you five weeks happened in a controlled basement and nobody threw the cup at day eighteen.

I keep a list of names that sat on us, not a list of names that “never root.” Those are different sentences. A name that sat five weeks in a cool basement is not a name that failed. I will not type a variety here and pin a week on it as if I ran a trial on your kitchen counter. The 2023 tray had fast ones at day 10 and a last one at five weeks. That is a range I will live with. Your quiet cup at day 18 is still inside that range if the wood is firm. I date it. I do not rename it Lazy.

The week you quit is usually week three, on a warm Saturday, when a different name already has a leaf. You dump the quiet cup. Sometimes you dump a live stick.

Firm wood, cream scratch, mix that is damp not sour, date that is not ancient — leave it. Soft wood, sour mix, wrinkled bark — do not “give it another week.” Those are different cups. The quit-week mistake is mixing them.

I will quit a cup that is mush. I will quit a cup I never dated because I cannot tell wait from neglect. I will not quit a cup because a forum poll said “should have rooted.”

Slow is not noble. Slow is a fact. You can still do small things: confirm the mat is not cold, confirm the mix is not a brick, confirm polarity. Then you stop.

What I check when a cup is slow: polarity mark still on the high end, mix still damp not a brick, mat still in the 76–80°F band if the mat is on, no sour, scratch still cream. That is a five-minute walk. If all of that is true I put the cup at the back and I do not visit it for a week. If the mat is cold I fix the thermostat. If the mix is a brick I bottom-water once and wait. If polarity is wrong I can restick the right way on a recut, once, if the wood still has a battery. I do not restick a mush.

8a gives you a gift here. A cup that roots in April still has May and most of June before the skillet. A Zone 7 cup that roots in May has a shorter outdoor runway. Use our runway. Do not throw the late ones because you want the bench for tomatoes.

If two cuttings of the same name were stuck the same day and one rooted, the other may still come. It may also be junk you missed. Scratch it. Do not assume genetics from a sample of two.

I keep slow names toward the back of the tray so I stop performing for them. Out of sight is not out of mind if the date is on a list. A list beats a daily reunion.

8a gives the late cup a runway the northern people do not get. A stick that finally shows a thread in early May still has May and June to become a pot before the skillet. I will not throw that cup on a Saturday in April because the tomatoes want the bench. I will move the tomatoes. Last frost here is usually early to mid-April [VERIFY] your county. After that date I am less scared of the shop and more scared of my own impatience. The week you quit should be a funeral for a soft stick, not a rearrangement of the furniture.

Zone 9 slow is rare because heat hurries everything, including death. Zone 7 slow is the default. We are mixed. Date it. Touch it less.

The week you quit should have a reason you can say out loud: mush, dry-through, no cambium, sour. “I got bored” is not a reason. It is how a collection stays small.
