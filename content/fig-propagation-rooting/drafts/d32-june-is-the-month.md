Count backward from the plant you want in a pot.

A fig air-layer in our weather is often **six to eight weeks** if you did the sleeve right and the branch was 1-year wood. LSU says four to six if it is warm. Warm we have. I would rather budget eight and be early than budget four and cut a bald wound.

June start, late July or early August pot. The new plant still has September and October to make a root ball that can face an 8a winter in a pot or a hole. That is the runway.

How I pick the cane in June: last year’s wood, already leafed, not a soft green whip from this spring, not a trunk I have not decided to keep. I wrap after the tree is working — leaves open, sap moving — not after one warm Saturday in April. Last frost here is usually early to mid-April [VERIFY] your county. A sleeve before that date is a dare the moss will lose on a 28°F night. June lets me skip that dare. I can still layer in late April or May if the tree is moving and frost is done. I would rather be a little late into June than early into a freeze.

August start, October maybe-roots, then November. The mother is slowing. The sleeve dries one week and sours the next. The pot you cut into is a short-season plant. Some years it lives. I would not do it to a name we have one of.

I mark the calendar the day I wrap. I do not “check in a month” without a date. Eight weeks is a date.

I write the wrap date and the earliest cut date on the tape — wrap plus six weeks, and a note that eight is allowed. LSU’s four to six weeks of warmth is real. Our 8a June is warm. I still want a mass of roots, not a scout at week four. Clear plastic lets me glance without a full unwrap. If I see a beard at five I can wait to six. If I see nothing at eight I open once. Callus and firm wood can have two more weeks if I started in June. If I started in August I do not have two more weeks that matter.

Do not layer a dormant tree. A sleeping cane will not push roots into sphagnum because you watched a winter video. Wait until the leaves are working.

Named wood does not get an August sleeve. Extra wood can get a cutting instead. I have watched people wrap in August because the tree looked lush and the internet said “while it’s growing.” Growing is not the same as having a runway. A layer cut in October is a shop plant. A layer cut in late July is a plant that can still make a pot. I want the pot. I do not want a story about how the moss looked good in September.

8a June is also the month we should be potting-up cuttings. The bench gets crowded. That is not a reason to skip the layer you planned. It is a reason not to start twelve layers on a whim in the same week you have forty cups to move.

Zone 7 June is still spring. Their August dare is worse. Zone 9 August can still be a growing month. Do not borrow Zone 9 August. Borrow their refusal to panic.

If I miss June, I take cuttings from extra wood or I wait until next year. I do not invent a “fall air-layer program.” Fall is for potting what you already started and for not pushing wood we want in March.

If June is crowded because the cutting bench is moving up, I do the layers first on the names I cannot replace, then I pot cups. Twelve whim sleeves in the same week as forty cups is how both jobs get half-walked. I would rather three sleeves I feel after every storm than a thicket of plastic I forget on the west side.

June is boring. Boring is how a layer becomes a pot instead of a story about almost.
