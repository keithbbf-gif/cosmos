I wait for a **mass** of white roots, not a scout.

A scout is a thread you can see through clear plastic at week three. Cute. If you cut then, you have a cutting with a hat. The point of a layer was a root ball. LSU says a nice mass, then a quart or a gallon of well-drained mix. I want the moss to look occupied.

I write two dates on the tape the day I wrap: today, and today plus six weeks. Eight weeks is allowed. LSU’s four to six in warmth is the floor, not a dare to cut a whisker. A scout at week three is a reason to leave the plastic alone, not a reason to get the pruners. If I want to look, I look through clear wrap. If I used white plastic I wait for the date. Opening early to “see” is how you dry the first roots and then decide the method failed.

Six to eight weeks is my 8a default on 1-year wood in June. Warm weeks count more than calendar weeks. A cool, wet June may want the eighth week. A hot June may show a beard at five. I still want a beard, not a whisker.

How I look: clear plastic, or one careful opening at the date I wrote. I do not unwrap weekly. New roots hate sun and wind. If I open, I water the moss and close it the same hour.

When I cut: below the roots, a clean pruner, in the morning. Straight to a pot that is already filled with damp mix — not a dry bucket you will “water later.” The layer has never had to drink on its own. Today it does.

Pruners wiped. I cut below the wad, I do not shave the beard to make it pretty, and I set the whole fist of moss into the hole I already made. I water until the pot is honest, then I set it in shade for a week. I do not stand it on black plastic in a 98°F afternoon to “catch up.” The layer has never had to close its own stomata against a skillet. Give it a dull week. The fruit I took off in June is not coming back. The plant can.

Leave the moss. Do not wash the beard to take a photo. Tease only if the wad will not fit the pot. The Pop warning about fragile roots applies here too.

If the date arrives and I have callus and two threads, I can wait two more weeks if June was the start. If the date arrives in October, I have a decision: pot it as a weak plant and baby it, or leave it on the mother until I am honest that I started too late. Leaving it into winter on the tree can work if the sleeve does not freeze-soak and rot. I like a pot I can move.

8a first freeze is usually early November. [VERIFY] yours. A layer cut October 20 has two weeks of growth weather and then a dimmer. It can live in the shop. It is not a yard plant yet.

If June was the start and week eight is only callus and two threads, I can wait. We still have summer. If the sleeve is shy in October I am paying for an August wrap. I can pot a weak plant and baby it in the shop, or I can leave it on the mother if the moss is still honest and I am willing to watch freeze-soak. I like a pot I can move. I do not like a sleeve I forgot on a west cane into a wet freeze. Named wood gets the June start so this decision stays easy.

Zone 7 people should cut earlier or pot into a garage. Zone 9 people can linger. We linger only if the beard is shy and the calendar still has summer.

Do not cut free on a 98°F afternoon and set the pot on black plastic. That is how a perfect beard cooks. Shade, water, leave it alone for a week.

The wait is the method. The cut is just the bill coming due.
