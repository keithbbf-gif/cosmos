A cutting is not a tomato seedling. It does not want a bright start.

The A/B ran 500–1800 lux and typically 500–600 on the mat. That is a dim room. A phone app is enough if you are curious. Your eye is enough if you know what “I can read but I cannot sunbathe” looks like.

Why low light: leaves that come early will not cook as hard. Mix will not dry from the top in a day. The stick spends less. Roots can happen in the dark. People put trays on a south sill because they want to *see* progress. Progress is not a tan.

Shop lights are fine if they are not a blast. I do not hang a 4-foot fixture six inches over pops. I do not put cups on the woodshop bench under the window that already overwinters citrus. That glass front is crowded for a reason. Cuttings can live behind it, not on it.

When roots show, I step light up. Indirect, then a brighter room, then a shaded outdoor day. The A/B said watch leaf color. Pale stretch means more light *after* there is a root. Pale stretch before roots means the stick is reaching with money it does not have — do not answer with a sunburn.

8a outdoor light in May is a weapon. Shade cloth is not decoration. A cup that lived in a shop corner will bleach on the wagon in an afternoon. Harden. One hour, then three, then a morning, then a day. Skip that and you will say the variety is tender. The variety was fine. The light was a slap.

I do not use grow-light schedules copied from cannabis forums. The cutting does not care about 18 hours. It cares that the mix is not a skillet and the leaf is not a sail in a furnace.

Zone 7 basement lights are often the only lights. Zone 9 shade-house light is already a lot. We have both rooms in one property: a dim shop and a brutal yard. Use the dim room first. Earn the yard.

A cloudy Saturday lies about the corner. The shop looks dim. Then the front clears and the same block is in a stripe of sun I did not plan. I check the glass at 2 p.m. even when the morning felt safe. I do not trust a phone lux app more than that walk. I trust the walk more than the weather app.

Saturday shop clean-up is when trays wander. Someone needs the corner. Someone sets the block under the same fixture we use for overwintered #3s. I put it back. Those pots want light. The stick does not. If we move cuttings toward new covered plastic, I watch the sun angle the first week. New film is brighter than old shop dust. A tray that was perfect behind dirty glass will cook on week one of a clean house. I do not ride cuttings on the same wagon as #3s headed to the greenhouse. That wagon is a light step I have not earned yet.

I harden on the east side of the building first, morning only, not on the wagon in May noon. One hour on that wall, then three, then a morning, then a day under shade cloth. The wagon in open gravel is a skillet with wheels. I have bleached a tray there while I answered a phone. Shade cloth is cheaper than a funeral.

I do not add night lights because I walked through with a phone and the corner looked dark. Roots do not need a shift change. If a tray gets tall and weak I still do not cut the tops to “thicken them.” I move it farther from the glass, I check that the mat is not cooking the base, and I wait for a thread on the wall. Pruning a cutting to fix light is how you spend the last of the battery.

The stick is not a seedling. Give it the light a rooting wound deserves, not the light a finished plant will want in July.
