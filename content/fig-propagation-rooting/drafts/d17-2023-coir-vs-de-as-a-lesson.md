We already ran a real comparison. I am not going to give you a new miracle mix and a fake percentage.

PapaFig wrote it up: 64 varieties from Figaholics, two cuttings each, one in coco coir, one in DE. Coir was faster. **64/64** in that basement. DE was slower and needed more watering attention. **60/64** at the end — **92%**, which is a *cup count from that test*, not “92% of customers” and not a promise about your kitchen.

Read the live post if you want the steps. I will not reprint the whole protocol as if I just discovered it. What I will steal for 8a trays is the lesson, not the trophy.

Coir held even moisture under a cover. First roots around day 10 on the fast ones. By five weeks the last straggler had rooted. DE showed nothing at day 10, lagged at three to four weeks, and needed covers added on the slow ones. DE also wanted water every few days. It is harder to wet from above and easier to dry.

Both worked when they were not drowned. That is the sentence people skip.

What I will not do: run a second “trial” in prose and invent 87%. What I will not do: tell you DE is bad because it was slower. Dad still keeps OptiSorb in the conversation. He has said if he were pressed he would mention Mycostop and OptiSorb, and also that **no specific product is necessary** — raised-bed dirt has rooted wood with nothing on it.

Use the test as a map of failure modes. If your coir is soggy, you did not copy the test. You copied the word “coir.” If your DE is a brick on top and dust at the bottom, you did not copy the dunk-and-watch. You copied the word “DE.”

8a changes the room. Their ambient RH was 30–40% in a cool basement. Our shop is not that dry. Covers that helped them will rain on you sooner. Vent earlier. Unplug the mat sooner. The percentages do not travel. The squeeze test does.

I reach for coir when I want a tray I will not babysit every other day. I reach for a bed of yard dirt when the wood is replaceable. I reach for DE when I want something that is harder to drown and I am willing to water it. Those are choices. They are not a brand war.

When I set a comparison now it is two cups of one name, same wood, same day, same mat — not a new trial I will quote. I want to see if I drowned this batch, not to mint a percentage. I photograph the pair on stick day and again when the first wall shows threads. That is a note for me. It is not a second A/B.

A buyer at the wagon does not get Dad’s cup count as a sales pitch. If someone asks which mix we like, I tell them what I reached for this Saturday and why — coir if I will not babysit, dirt if the wood is replaceable, DE if I am willing to water. I do not write 64/64 on a bag I bought this week. I write the date I stuck this wood.

Someone will still show up with a sack of DE and want the 2023 number printed on it. I send them to the live post. Basement, cool room, those covers, that RH. Our shop is wetter. Their kitchen is not in the writeup. If they want a habit they can steal, they can steal the pairing, the weigh-or-lift, and the wait past boredom. They cannot steal a scoreboard and tape it to a different climate.

Cite the post. Date it. 2023, basement, those cups, those counts. If someone quotes “100%” without the setup, they are selling. If someone quotes “92%” on a bag of mix, they are stealing Dad’s number.

A lesson you can carry: pair the cups, weigh them, write the date, wait past the week you get bored. A law you cannot carry: your kitchen is our basement.
