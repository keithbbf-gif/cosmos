A 32-ounce clear cup is not a pot. It is a weather system you can hold.

Drainage holes in the bottom. Mix that does not sit in a saucer of tea. The cutting not deeper than halfway down — the A/B was explicit: too deep and the wound rots. Most of the stick covered. A gap under the base. If the cane is too long, use a deeper container or cut a legal cutting. Do not fold a 14-inch stick into a cup and call it efficient.

Clear cups are how you see roots at the wall without a daily autopsy. They are also how 8a sun cooks a tray on a bench. Clear plus south glass is a solar oven. I keep rooting cups in low light on purpose. 500–600 lux in that basement test. You can get by with a dim shop corner. You cannot get by with a windowsill that grows tomatoes.

A pop bag is a smaller climate. Pack it. Rubber-band the top. Same gap under the base. Same rule about sun. The bag does not need a saucer. It needs a tray that does not let the bottoms sit in runoff from the neighbor.

Covers: Press’n Seal on the coir cups in 2023 to trap humidity. Off as soon as leaves show, or you shock them later. In our air I take covers off earlier than a dry-state video. If the cover is the only thing keeping the mix from drying in a day, the mix was too dry or the room is a desert. Ours is not a desert.

I do not put a cup in a closed tote “for humidity” on a mat. That is a swamp with a lid. One climate at a time.

8a March afternoons spike. A cup that was perfect at 8 a.m. can be a sauna at 2. If the block lives near the glass, I move it back. The greenhouse we wanted for winter pots is a gift and a hazard for cuttings. Winter pots want light. Cuttings want less.

Zone 7 cups live on mats longer. Zone 9 cups live under shade cloth sooner. We graduate: dim and warm, then brighter once roots show, then a shaded outdoor week before a full pot-up. Skip a step and the leaves burn or the mix dries from the top while the roots are still a thread.

The cup is also a record. Weight, date, name. When I pick it up I should know if it is light. If I cannot tell, I have too many cups and not enough hands. That is a collection problem. Thin the experiment.

I number the tray if I am running more than one mat. Left block, right block, date on the tape, probe cup marked so nobody sticks a name in the sacrificial mix. A Saturday helper will swap cups to “make it look even.” That swap is how a light cup and a swamp trade places and I lose the record. If I cannot lift every cup in the block, named wood stays and replaceable wood goes to outdoor dirt. A climate I cannot maintain is a collection problem.

Clear cups invite hands. A visitor picks one up to see the wall, tips it, and the gap under the base becomes a puddle against the wound. I keep the block where traffic does not include a tour. If a pop leaks, I do not set it in a saucer “so it can drink.” I rebag or I move that stick to a cup with holes I drilled before I mixed. Milk crates under a tray let air move. A solid tub under a tray is a pond with a lid if anything runs.

March at 2 p.m. I walk the glass. If the block has slid into the sun while we were potting #3s, I move the whole tray, not the front row. One cup at a time is how you forget the last six. I write the graduation on tape if I am being good: dim until threads, brighter in the same room, shaded outdoor day, #3 before June. I do not skip to the wagon because the cup looks like a plant.

When roots hit the wall, the climate is used up. The next climate is a #3, or at least more volume, before June if you can. A rooted cup in July in 8a is a plant in a shot glass on a skillet.

Treat the cup like weather you built. If the weather is bad, change it. Do not add a second lid and a second mat and a third spray bottle. One climate. Right.
