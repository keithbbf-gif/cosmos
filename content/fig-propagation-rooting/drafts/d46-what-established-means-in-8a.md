Established is a word people spend on a Saturday.

A fig is not established because it is in the ground. It is not established because it leafed. It is not established because it set three figs and dropped two. In 8a, I call a plant established when it has **used two summers** in that hole, the roots have left the old pot shape, and it has seen a winter without being a guest in a bucket.

The first summer it is still a pot you cannot lift. Water it like a pot. The second summer it starts to behave like a bush. The first winter I still mulch the crown and I do not trust the wood the way I trust a ten-year Celeste I am not even a fan of yet. We give long trials. We also give honest winters.

A potted fig can be “established in the pot” — roots filled, top growing, fruiting — and still be a one-night freeze from a root kill if you leave it on concrete. Do not spend the word across climates. Pot-established and ground-established are different passports.

I stop babysitting when I see new canes from below and the plant does not wilt on a normal 8a dry week the way a #3 does. I do not stop walking after rain to see if the mound became a bowl.

Jack’s first two summers line is the same fact from the other side: the hole is still a pot, only you cannot lift it. Believe that and you will water. Ignore it and you will have a stick in August and a story about nematodes, or clay, or “this variety.”

We have over thirty that lived a winter in the ground recently, and hundreds that did not have to. I like those numbers because they keep me from pretending the yard is full. The yard is a trial. The pots are the library. Established is a graduate, not a freshman with a stake.

Zone 7 established can mean “comes back from the crown.” Zone 9 established can mean “we stopped watering.” 8a established means the earth is doing the blanket work and I am not dragging a hose to that plant every rainless week in July — and I still might, because I remember the first summer.

When I use the word in a note, I want a date: in-ground spring 2025, two summers, winter 2026–27 survived. If I cannot write the dates, I cannot write established.

Summer one on the twenty-five to thirty: I water like a #3 I cannot pick up. Mix at the contact was a little perlite and castings, not a bathtub. The mound should shed a storm by morning. If it holds a puddle, I fix the grade — clay seat, not a grave. I do not fertigate it like a ten-year stool. Paint pen on a stake fades. I keep a note with a date: in-ground spring, name, common fig. I do not spend established on that note yet. Hundreds of pots stay the library. The hole is a trial.

Summer two I look for new canes from below and a plant that does not wilt on a normal dry week the way a pot does. Roots have left the old pot shape — I know because a dry week does not cook it in a day. Then I can start to say the word, after the winter in between. Two summers in the hole. A plate in year one is a bonus, not a diploma. Long trials. I have dropped names after year three when the fruit was not worth the row. I do not quote an established percent. The ones that lived are a count. The ones that did not are a count. Both stay in the notebook.

The notebook line I want is ugly and specific: name, common fig, pot-up date, in-ground month, first summer, first winter, second summer. If a cell is blank I do not write established. Pot-up before June so the cup becomes a #3 that can face the skillet. August is hold for new holes. A pot on concrete can fruit and still die in one freeze — that passport is not this one. The yard stays a trial of twenty-five to thirty. Common figs. Dates or it is not established.

The collection will keep growing. The word should not. Spend it late. The fig has time. 8a is the in-between. The plant that stays is the one you did not rush.
