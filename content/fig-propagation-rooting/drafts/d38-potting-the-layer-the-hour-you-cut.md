The cut and the pot are one job. If you cut at 8 a.m. and pot at 4 p.m., you made a cutting with extra steps and left it in the sun.

Pot ready: well-drained mix, already damp, a quart or a gallon for a sleeve, bigger if the beard is a fist. Drainage holes. Not a decorative crock. We use black nursery pots because we can write on them and move them.

I set the wad in, fill around it, water once to settle, and put the pot in shade. I do not stake unless the top is a sail. I do not strip every leaf. I will reduce a huge canopy if the beard is shy. I will not bald a plant because I am nervous.

No fertilizer the first week. The mother was the feed. A salt kick on new roots is a way to pay for impatience. Fertigate later, when it is a pot that grew.

Keep it out of 8a afternoon sun for a week or two. An east morning is enough. The wagon in July is a punishment.

If the top wilts the first afternoon, that is normal-rude, not always death. Shade and a damp (not flooded) pot. If it is still a rag on day four and the mix is sour, you had a weak beard or a swamp wad. Scratch the cane.

Label the hour you pot. The name, the date it was layered, the date it was cut. Two dates. You will want them next June when you decide if that plant can go in the ground.

8a August pots from a June layer can go to a larger pot or even a hole in fall if the roots filled. I still like a pot through the first winter for anything that is not a hardy name we have extras of. The earth is a blanket. A new layer is not a three-year stool.

Zone 7 potted layers live inside. Zone 9 can plant sooner. We can plant in September if the hole drains and we can water. We can also lie to ourselves about “established.” A month in the dirt is not established. See PapaFig on that.

I do not leave a cut layer in a bucket of water “to keep it happy” while I find a pot. That is the jar trick with higher stakes. The pot is in the shade before the pruners come out.

The pot is filled before I walk to the tree. Mix already damp — perlite and castings, not a dry peat brick I will “water later.” A quart for a sleeve beard. A #3 if the wad is a fist off a trunk. Drainage holes I can see, not a crock. Paint pen in the pocket. Shade spot picked: east bench, cloth if it is July. Then the pruners. If I have to hunt a pot after the cut, I already failed the hour. I cut below the roots, leave the moss on, set the wad in a hole I made in the mix, and fill around it with my fingers. No trowel that pries the beard apart. One watering to settle until it drips. I do not press the mix into a brick. Air is the point of the pot.

Week one is shade and the pick-up test. A quart of honest mix should go light in two or three 8a days. If it stays swamp-heavy, I drilled bad holes or I used a mix that lies. I do not fertigate a week-old layer. I do not set it on the wagon with the hundreds. Dad’s siphon is for plants that already grew. This one just learned to drink. I scratch the cane if the top stays a rag past day four. Green means wait. Brown goes on the pile. I do not invent a take rate from the ones that lived.

A June sleeve potted in August is a pot through winter — shop or huddle — not a hole. Next spring, if it is still in a quart, pot-up before June into a #3. A month in dirt in September is not established. Established is two summers in the hole after the roots leave the pot shape. Common figs. Taste first. The hour you pot is the start of that clock, not the end of the layer. August is hold for new holes and for new sleeves. The plant you cut free today can sit in shade and be boring. Boring is how a beard becomes a pot.

One hour. One pot. Then you leave it alone like a cutting you finally learned not to peek at.
