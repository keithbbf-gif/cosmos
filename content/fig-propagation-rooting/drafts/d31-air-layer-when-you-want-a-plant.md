A cutting is a bet you can afford to lose in dozens. An air-layer is a bet you make when you want the plant on the other side to already be a plant.

The mother keeps feeding it. The branch already has leaves and wood. You are asking for roots in a wet sleeve, not for a stick to invent a life from a battery. That is why a layer comes off bigger, and why it costs you a cane you will miss.

I cut sticks when I want a tray, a name multiplied, wood we can replace. I air-layer when the name is one we do not want to chip into hope, or when I want a #3-sized plant this season, or when the tree is coming out and I can save a piece that is already a crown.

We use **long-fiber sphagnum** for layers and pre-rooting. That is already how we talk about it. Not potting mix mashed in foil. Sphagnum holds air and water in a way a handful of shop mix does not, if you wring it first.

I wring the long-fiber sphagnum until it does not drip, then I wrap a fist around a clean wound on 1-year wood. LSU wants that 1-year wood in spring, and they will tell you four to six weeks of warmth is enough. I budget six to eight in 8a and I start in June when I want a plant I can pot before the dimmer. I do not start a named layer in August and hope the sleeve invents a fall. August is how you nearly kill two trees — the mother minus a cane, the piece minus a runway.

A layer is not a miracle. It can dry in a week of 8a wind. It can sit soggy and sour. It can be started in August and then the plant has no time to be a pot before the dimmer. The method is simple. The calendar is not.

LSU says spring is a good time, and you can go later if you leave four to six weeks of warmth. Fig people who do a lot of this will tell you June is the month and August is how you nearly kill two trees. I believe the August part without needing a new trial.

You will lose the fruit on that branch, or it will finish tasting like a distraction. Take the figs off. You are not running a fruiting air-layer for a plate. You are running a nursery.

A layer is one cane you are willing to lose. I pick a side cane, pencil to finger thick, last year’s wood, where I can reach a hose and see the sleeve from the path. I do not layer the only upright on a young pot. I do not layer a fruiting branch I already promised to a plate unless I am honest that the figs come off. I take the figs off the day I wrap. The branch is a nursery now. If I cannot spare the cane, I cut a stick from extra wood and I live with a tray.

Zone 7 layers need an earlier start or a pot that overwinters warm. Zone 9 can layer late and shrug. 8a has a June that works and a September that pretends to. I would rather have a potted layer in August than a new sleeve in August.

If you only have a young pot, do not air-layer it. You will take the plant you have to make the plant you want and end up with neither. Cut a stick from extra wood, or wait a year.

I would rather have three good June sleeves than a dozen “why not” wraps. Each sleeve needs a drink after wind and after a storm. Each sleeve is a cane I will miss if I rush the cut. Shop scale applies here too. A kind afternoon in June will let you wrap more than you will walk. Walk is the method. The moss is just the medium.

A tray is a chorus. A layer is a solo. Pick the song on purpose.
