The box does not get to wait until Sunday because you are tired.

A cutting in a truck is already on a clock. Heat, crush, and a wet paper towel someone thought was kindness. If you leave it on the porch and then in the mudroom, you added two climates. Open it at the table the day it lands. Sort. Photograph the bad ones before you throw them. Names and damage in the same frame.

What I look for, in order:

Crushed internodes. A flattened middle node can still leave you two ends. A flattened whole cane is firewood.

Mold. Surface fuzz on firm wood: wash. Soft, dark, sour: trash. Do not sniff and put it back.

Wrinkles. Hot truck. Soak if the cambium is still cream, dry the bark, stick now. Do not file wrinkled wood for later.

Sap all over the bag. It was moving when they packed it. Treat it as non-dormant. Root soon. The listing can say dormant anyway.

One-node chips and green whips. That is not the industry cutting. If the name is rare I may try. I will not pretend I got what I paid for.

The 2023 Figaholics wood we split for the coir/DE test was long enough to make two cuttings. That is the opposite of a chip. When someone sends you six inches of hope, you already know.

I do not argue with a seller after I have stuck the wood and waited a month. The evidence is the day-one photo. After that it is your mix.

8a March porches cook a box. 8a January porches freeze one. Neither is a rooting environment. Bring it inside, open it, then decide. Frozen wood that thaws firm can go in a cup. Frozen wood that thaws to mush cannot. Give it ten minutes on the counter before you judge.

If the box is late and the wood is awake, do not punish it with the crisper. The crisper is for quiet wood. Awake wood goes in mix or it goes in the story of how you lost a name in a vegetable drawer.

Refunds are a seller problem. Rooting is yours. I separate them so I do not “make it work” out of politeness and then blame the cup.

Zone 7 buyers wait longer for boxes and store longer. Zone 9 buyers sometimes get wood that never slept. 8a in February gets both in the same week — a northern dormant bundle and a southern milky one. Two treatments. One table. Do not put them in the same bag overnight to “keep organized.”

I left an 8a March box on the porch from noon to dark. Boards were 80°F. Inside was a wet towel someone thought was kindness and bark that had already cooked. I photographed it, asked for the refund, and threw the mush. That wood would have been a cup if I had brought the box into the shop at lunch. The porch is not a rooting room. Neither is the mudroom.

January is the other porch. Bundle came off the boards stiff. I gave it ten minutes on the shop counter, not a blast from the hose and not a seat on the heat mat. What thawed firm got washed, dried, recut, and stuck. What thawed to mush got the same photo I should have taken in March. Frozen and cooked are different deaths. Both start with a box you did not open.

I keep the day-one photo on my phone until the refund is done, not until I “see how they do.” Once I stick the wood, the evidence is mine. I have already argued from a May cup and lost because I had no picture of the crush. The shop-sink rinse and the thermostat come after the photo. A wet cutting in a pretty cup is a worse witness than a dry stick next to the torn label. If the box is a mix of 6–8 inch three-node sticks and a handful of chips, I separate them before I wash. The industry sticks get the tray. The chips get one cup if the name is rare, or they get the photo.

Open the box. The tray can wait an hour. The wood cannot wait for your weekend.
