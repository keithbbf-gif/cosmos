A heat mat plugged straight into the wall is a skillet.

The Pop page is not polite about this. Set the mat to about **75–78°F**. You have to use a thermostat or you will roast the cuttings. Probe **in the mix**, not in the air next to the tray. Air lies. The plastic cup on a cheap mat can run ten degrees hotter than the room while you are watching the leaves like they are the thermometer.

The 2023 basement mats were set **80°F** because the room was cool. The writeup says shoot **76–80°F** and adjust down if your room is not a basement. Our 8a shop in March is not a basement. A mat on “high” in a 70°F room is how you cook the base into soup. Soup plus our humidity is rot with a name tag.

I want bottom heat, not a sauna. Roots start at the wound. Leaves do not need a tropical vacation on day five. Low light, warm mix, cool-enough tops. If the tops are sweating and the mix smells sweet-sour, you built a fermenter.

No mat at all is legal. Outdoor bulk in yard dirt roots on the season. A warm shop without a mat can root lignified wood if you wait. The mat is a speed tool for winter and early spring, not a religion. If you use it, control it.

I do not stack cups two-high on a mat. The bottom row cooks. The top row is a different climate. One layer. A probe in a sacrificial cup if I have to guess.

8a April can make the mat redundant. If the shop is already 78°F in the afternoon, I unplug. A mat that runs because it is on a habit is how you lose a May tray to heat you did not need.

Setup is boring on purpose. Mat on a rigid board, not on insulation that folds and makes a hot stripe. Controller in the line. Probe in a sacrificial cup of the same mix, same volume, same hole pattern. I tape the probe so it stays in the mix, not in the air gap under a cup. Air next to the tray is a comfort reading. The wound is in the mix. I write the set point on the tape on the controller so a Saturday helper does not twist the dial toward “high.”

I do not share the cutting mat with a seed tray of tomatoes or a citrus that wants more heat. Different jobs, different plugs. A late-March cold snap will tempt me to plug back in after I unplugged for an 78°F afternoon. I check the mix at dawn, not the forecast. If the shop is 65°F at 6 a.m. and 80°F at 2, I would rather skip the mat than chase both ends.

The probe walks. I find it on the bench, in the air, taped to a cup wall. If it is not in mix, the controller is lying. I check it when I rotate. I still would rather buy a controller than a second mat. Cheap mats have hot corners; touching cups finds them faster than the box copy.

Cooked wood is not a patient. The base goes soft, the bark slips, and the mix smells like a jar you forgot. I do not “let it recover” in the same wet cup. Mush goes out. Recut only if a firm piece with nodes is left, and then new mix, and then a thermostat in the sentence. I have already lost a May tray to heat I did not need. I would rather have a slow cool cup than a fast funeral.

Zone 7 needs the mat more days of the year. Zone 9 needs it almost never except in a cold garage. We need it in January and we need to remember to turn it off. Write the unplug date on the tape next to the name.

Bottom heat is a tool. A thermostat is the handle. I will not hand you the tool without the handle.
