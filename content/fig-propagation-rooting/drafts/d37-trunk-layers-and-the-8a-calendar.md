A trunk layer is a different animal. You split a 2- to 5-gallon pot, wrap it around a stem, fill it, and you are asking for a big root system while the top stays on the mother for months. People who do a lot of this start a few weeks after last frost and leave it toward the end of the season.

In 8a that can work on an in-ground tree you are willing to cut above the pot in fall. It is how you steal a finished plant off a stool you were going to reduce anyway. It is not how you treat a #3 in a wagon.

The calendar is the whole risk. Last frost early to mid-April. [VERIFY]. Start May. Leave it through summer, water the pot when it dries, cut free before you trust a freeze on a half-new root ball — or cut free and park the pot in the shop. A trunk pot left to freeze through on the north side of the house is a 5-gallon of ice.

This is more water than a moss sleeve. It is a pot in the air. Pots in the air are cliffs. 8a July will dry it daily if the volume is small. Use a real pot, not a sandwich bag with ambition.

I would not trunk-layer a variety we have not tasted. That is a lot of wound for a maybe. Taste first, or layer a side cane, or take cuttings.

The tree below the ring will push. That is a gift if you wanted a stool. That is a thicket if you ignore it. Decide if the mother stays.

Zone 7 trunk layers are a garage story. Zone 9 trunk layers can stay outside. Our trunk layers are a shop-or-ground story in November.

If I only have energy for one kind of layer this year, I do June sleeves on 1-year wood. Trunk pots are a second-year project when the in-ground set is big enough to spare a stem. We have tens in the ground, not hundreds. Spare means spare.

How I actually hang the pot: I split a real 2- or 3-gallon nursery pot with a saw, wrap it around a stem I can spare on an in-ground common fig — finger-thick at the small end, maybe two inches if I am stealing a finished plant — and I close the seam with tape or zip ties so the mix cannot run out the slit. I fill with damp long-fiber sphagnum, or with the same open pot mix Dad uses (perlite and castings, not a peat brick). The pot has to sit on a board or hang from a strap on a stake. A 3-gallon of wet mix will peel bark if it slumps. I have watched a pot slide and leave a wet ring on the lawn and a dry wound on the tree. That layer is done. The tree is not.

I water those hanging pots when I walk the hose, which in 8a July can be daily if the plastic takes afternoon sun. I water until it drips the holes, then I stop. A trunk pot that stays swamp-heavy for three days is rotting a beard I cannot see. I do not put the fertigation siphon on a pot I filled last week. The mother is still feeding the top. Salt in a hanging bucket burns roots that have never had to drink on their own. East side when I can. West corner is a heater. After a storm I lift once. If it is a balloon, I drain it. Same puddle rule as a sleeve, only the volume is bigger and the ice in November is a 5-gallon of ice.

When I cut free I cut above the pot in the morning, same hour I pot. Paint pen on the black plastic: name, date layered, date cut. Two dates. The stump below will throw. If I wanted a stool, I leave it and I harvest suckers later like plants, not like hope. If I wanted a clean trunk, I had no business wrapping it. I would not trunk-layer a name we have not plated. Common figs only. A wound that size is a year of the tree's work for a maybe I can take as a stick instead.

I do not start a new trunk pot in August. August is hold. A May wrap with a shy beard in late August can stay on the mother through September if I can still water it. A new wrap in August is two trees in trouble: a fresh ring and a pot that will freeze before the roots fill. Next spring that potted trunk still wants a real #3, pot-up before June if it is sulking in a small pot, and a hole only if the spot drains. Established is two summers in that hole, not a fall cut and a stake.

A trunk layer is a commitment with a date. If you cannot water a hanging pot all summer, do not start one in May because a video looked like a nursery.
