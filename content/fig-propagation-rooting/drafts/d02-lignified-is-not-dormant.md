Two words get taped together on every forum and then someone puts a June stick in the fridge.

**Lignified** means the wood hardened. The bark went brown, gray, or that dirty black figs get. It is not tender green. Frost will still hurt it if the sap is moving, but the stick itself is wood, not a salad.

**Dormant** means the sap is down. You make a fresh cut and you do not get milk. The bag does not have dried sap stuck to the plastic. The tree has had enough cold to quit growing. That is a different fact.

You can have lignified wood that is still awake. We have it most 8a Januarys. The workshop is cool, the pots are not pushing, and a cut still beads. That wood roots. It does not store like a Michigan stick. Treat it as non-dormant: stick it or lose the week arguing.

Green wood is neither. Green is this year’s flush. It can root. It does not keep. I will say that until people stop filing June tips next to the apples.

How I tell in the hand:

Brown or gray bark: lignified. Green: not. Tan that still feels alive is the middle — semi-lignified. Fine for a pop if you stick it now. Not fridge wood.

Fresh cut, no milk, dry bag: dormant enough to store. Fresh cut, milky, sticky bag: root it. Do not invent a third category called “I paid for storage.”

Scratch the cambium if you are unsure. You want a moist green or cream line. Chocolate dust is firewood. Do the scratch in shade, not on a dashboard. A stick can look fine cold and show brown after it warms. Frozen wood that thaws to mush is trash. Frozen wood that thaws firm can still go in a cup. [VERIFY] with the knife, not with the invoice.

8a messes people up because our trees are half-asleep for half the winter. A Zone 7 grower gets a cleaner off-switch. A Zone 9 grower barely gets one. We get a dimmer. That is why Dad’s shop in January is full of plants that are not “fully dormant” and also not growing. Cool enough to loaf. Warm enough that a sealed wet bag still rots.

If a seller says “dormant lignified” and the bag is glazed with sap, they described the tree they wish they cut. Wash, recut, stick. Do not punish the wood for the listing.

If you cut your own tree in South Arkansas after a real cold week and the leaves are off, you are closer to the textbook. If you cut after a 70-degree spell in February, you are closer to the milky stick. Both years are legal 8a. The wood tells you which one you are in.

The shop and the yard lie in different directions the same morning. Glass front can sit 60–65°F while the in-ground stools are at 28°F. Cut a pot behind that glass and you get milk. Cut the same variety on the clay and the test cut is dry. I do not bag those two piles together. Shop wood is lignified and awake more winters than the listing language allows. It goes in mix, or it goes bad in a sealed bag while I finish watering the #3s with the hose.

I already filed the wrong pile. Lignified, pretty, still beading. I parafilmed it, double-bagged it, and put it in the crisper next to the apples because a Zone 7 thread said that is what you do with brown wood. The bag glazed. Then it soured. I washed what was firm and stuck it the same day. I lost the week I thought I was being organized. The fridge holds quiet wood. It does not manufacture dormancy.

After a 70°F February stretch I still drag a hose to the in-ground row. Those canes scratch cream and wet. That is lignified and awake. I recut, wash, and stick. I do not invent a storage week because the bark looks winter in a photo. Zone 7 gets a harder off-switch and can wait. Zone 9 barely gets bark that looks winter at all. 8a shop wood is the one that fools people who think brown equals fridge.

I store only the quiet ones, and even then I would rather see them in mix. Storage is a delay, not a method. Dormant is a condition. Lignified is a costume. Do not hang the costume in the crisper and call the tree asleep.
