Northern calendars say “after leaf drop.” That is a start, not a date on our wall.

In South Arkansas 8a, leaf drop is a negotiation. Some years a Celeste is bare in December. Some years a pot in the shop still holds a few leather leaves into January because the glass front never got the memo. I do not cut by the calendar app. I cut when the wood is quiet and I have a place to put it.

Quiet means: leaves off or mostly off, no milk on a test cut, nights that have actually been cold. One 28°F night does not make a tree dormant if the next week is 74°F and the tree believes in spring. Two or three real cold stretches will. Write the lows down. We keep a cheap thermometer for this. The zone map is a thirty-year average. Your Saturday is a night.

I like taking wood on a prune day after a cold week, when we are already thinning a bush. The cutting is a by-product of a tree we intended to shape. That wood is usually lignified, the right thickness, and we already know the name because we just read the tag.

What I will not do: strip a tree in October because a group buy opened. October wood here is often still working. You can take a semi-lignified piece and stick it as a pop. You cannot file it until March. October in 8a is not Michigan October.

Spring cuttings are legal. LSU says you can take them spring through early fall if you leave enough warm weather for roots. That sentence has a trap. “Enough warm weather” in 8a is not infinite. A cutting stuck in August has maybe eight weeks of decent root weather and then a dimmer winter. A cutting stuck in March has the whole spring. I take spring wood when I missed the winter cut or when a tree is pushing and I want wood that is awake. I do not take it as my main method for names I care about.

Last frost here is usually early to mid-April. First freeze is usually early November. [VERIFY] your own county, not mine. Those two dates are the box. Dormant wood I want in cups by late February or March so the roots see April and May, not just a heat mat in a dark corner. Green wood I want stuck the week I cut it, and I want it potted into something with volume before the first 95°F week.

Zone 7 can wait longer in the fridge because their outdoor stick date is later. Zone 9 can cut almost any month and call it “now.” 8a sits in the doorway. We get a real winter some years and a rumor of winter other years. Cut when the wood is quiet. Stick when you can give it spring. Do not copy a fridge schedule from a grower whose last frost is Memorial Day.

I do not prune after a week of rain when the yard is soup. Swollen canes look fat and ready. They are just wet. Wet bark hides freeze burn, and clay on the boots tracks into the shop and into the bags before the wash. I wait a day until I can walk the row without the ground sucking. Then I cut. The hose can wait until the sticks are on the tarp and named.

Shop pots are a different calendar than the in-ground row. Pots behind glass drop leaves later and stay milky longer. I cut the yard first. Shop wood waits on a test cut that stays dry. Mixing those two calendars on one Saturday is how a quiet bundle and a sticky bundle share a bag overnight.

I already copied a northern group-buy date in October. Semi-lignified, brown enough in the listing photo. I filed it like Zone 7 wood. The March pull was mush. October here is still working. A pop I stick the week I cut it can use the fall shoulder. A bag I forget until the mats are free in February cannot.

If the tree still has fruit mummies, pick them off before you cut. Rot on the cane is not a flavor. If the wood is freeze-burned at the tips, come back a node. Dead tips are free. Dead nodes in the middle of your “three” are not a cutting.
