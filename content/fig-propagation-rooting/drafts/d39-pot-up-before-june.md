A rooted cup in July in South Arkansas is a plant in a shot glass on a skillet.

I want volume under a fig before the first stretch of 95°F. For us that means **pot-up before June** if the roots are there. A #3 is the working size. Not a decorative pint. Not a leftover yogurt tub with a hole I burned with a screwdriver and then forgot to make two more.

Jack will tell you pop roots are fragile. Believe him. Support the wad. Do not shake the mix off to see a beard for a photograph. The photograph does not fertigate in August.

Mix for a pot is not the rooting cup. Rooting wants damp and tight contact. A pot wants air. We run a lot of perlite — I buy it wholesale from an expander — with worm castings, topsoil, leaf compost, sometimes aged chicken manure from the place. Jack has said 60/40 castings and perlite on the pots. The exact recipe moves. The rule does not: if water stands on the mix, the pot is lying.

I water the new #3 to settle, then I leave it in shade. Fertigate later. A salt kick on a week-old pot-up is how you buy wilt. Once it is growing, we fertigate in summer with a hose siphon and we put some slow-release in the pot. Winter is mostly rain barrels and very little feed. That rhythm starts after the plant is a plant.

If I miss June, I still pot-up rather than leave a rootbound cup into July. I just do it like I mean the shade: cloth, east side, no black plastic under the pot if I can help it. A late pot-up can live. It will sulk. Sulk is a month of not growing while the calendar spends heat.

Zone 7 can pot-up later because July is not our July. Zone 9 must pot-up earlier or live in a shade-house. 8a sits in the door and gets a real skillet. I do not copy a Michigan May schedule or a Tampa “anytime” schedule.

A #3 that fills can go to a 5-gallon. That is a different essay. This one is the cup-to-pot date. Miss it and you will spend August explaining why a name “hates pots.” It does not hate pots. It hates a cup in a furnace.

I mix the night before or I mix at dawn. Perlite and castings, topsoil, leaf compost, the pile we already trust. Damp, not dripping. I do not pot forty cups at noon on black plastic. I do a wagon-load in shade, support each wad the way Jack said, set it in a #3, fill, water once to settle, paint pen two sides: name and the date. The cup tag goes in the trash after I copy it. If I cannot read the cup, the #3 says unknown. Unknown is allowed. A guessed name is how a collection becomes a rumor.

We run hundreds of pots. About twenty-five to thirty live in the clay. The #3 is the working size because I can lift it, wagon it, and fertigate a block without a tractor. A pint looks busy in April. A pint in South Arkansas July is a skillet. I would rather pot fewer cups into honest #3s than keep a bench of cups “until they get bigger.” They will not get bigger in a cup after the roots hit the wall. They will cook. Common figs only. A capri or a maybe I have not tasted can wait in a labeled cup until I decide it is worth a #3 and a summer of water.

The siphon waits for new growth, not for the day I potted. A #3 that is pushing can join the summer fertigation block. A #3 that is still sulking stays on the shade rack and gets the pick-up test only. Light means drink. Heavy means wait. Overwatering a new #3 is how you buy gnats and a sour mix and then a story about figs liking wet feet. Ours do not. Clay already holds. If water stands on the surface, I did the mix wrong or the holes clogged. I do not invent a morning schedule for hundreds of pots. The ones that stay heavy skip the siphon. I do not quote a pot-up survival percent. Long trials. A name that sulks after a late June move is a calendar problem until I have two honest summers of data.

If I miss June I still pot-up, then I shade like I mean it and I treat August as hold — no hole, no 5-gallon jump, no west-wall heroics. A clay mound is not a grave I dig in a 98°F week to save a cup I should have moved in May. Established is two summers in the hole. This essay is the cup-to-#3 date that makes that hole possible later.

I would rather have fewer names in honest #3s by June than a bench of leafy liars I was proud of in April. The collection is the plants that face July, not the cups that faced a camera.
