August in 8a is not a planting month. It is a holding month.

The dirt is hot. The air is wet and then suddenly a dryer. A root ball you slide from a pot to a hole loses the only shade it had — the pot wall — and gains a clay oven. I have done it because a row “needed to look finished” before company. The company did not eat those figs. The compost pile did.

If a pot is tipping, I can slide it a few feet and right it. That is not a transplant. If a hole is waiting because I dug it in April and then life happened, I can wait until September or until late winter. The hole will still be there. I may need to re-break the glaze on the walls. I do not need to prove I am a finisher in a 98°F week.

September is a maybe. Nights ease. I can plant if I can water and if the name is one I can afford to lose. October is a better maybe. Late winter through spring is the sentence UAEX already gave you. I would rather be boring.

Cuttings and layers have their own August rules — Jack already spent them. This is the established pot, the #3 you got proud of, the plant you want in the yard. It can wait. The yard is not a deadline.

8a September still cooks a west wall. An east-wall hole in September is a kindness. A west-wall hole in August is a grudge.

I do not “water extra to make August work.” Extra water in hot clay is steam and sour. Water enough. Shade a new hole with cloth for a week if I am stubborn enough to plant in heat. Stubborn is not a method I recommend.

Zone 9 August planting happens because their fall is long. Zone 7 August planting is rare because they already missed their spring and they know it. We pretend we are Zone 9 in August and we pay like Zone 8a.

If the only day you have off is an August Saturday, use it to build the mound, stage the mix, fix the hose, and write the name on the stake. Plant in October. The fig will not thank you for your day off. It will thank you for the month you picked.

What I actually do with that Saturday: fix the hose. Clear the siphon. Write names on pots that lost a tag — paint pen, two sides. Cloth the new #3s if a cell is coming. Stage perlite and castings for an October mix. Build a mound for a late-winter plant, a seat, not a grave. I do not slide a proud #3 into hot clay because I had a day off. The dirt is hot. The pot wall was shade. A hole in August is an oven. I have composted that mistake. I do not need a new trial, and I will not invent a percent of “August plants that made it.”

August I water the block that earned fertigation, and I still pick pots up. A #3 of perlite and castings should go light. If I flood the clay under the pots, I steam the roots from below. Extra water is not a method. Hundreds of pots. Twenty-five to thirty in the ground. The in-ground first-years get a drink if that summer is still a pot I cannot lift. The ones on year two or three can wait a dry week better. I do not treat them all as guests and I do not treat them all as trees. Overwatering the hold month is how you sour a mound you already built too much like a grave.

August sun eats cheap ink. I rewrite pots that went ghost, two sides, before I forget which #3 was the spare for a hole. I write the plant date on the stake I will not use until October or late winter: name, common fig, east wall if I have one. Pot-up was supposed to happen before June. If it did not, the plant is already in a sulk. Adding a hole does not cure a sulk. Established is two summers in the hole. August does not start that clock. Long trials. Hold the pot. Then walk away from the skillet.

Hold. Holding is a skill. August is how you find out if you have it.
