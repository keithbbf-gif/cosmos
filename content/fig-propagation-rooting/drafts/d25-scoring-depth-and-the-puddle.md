Two small cuts decide a lot of rot.

**Score** the bottom inch or two so cambium shows. Do not peel a ring around the stick like you are air-layering a cup. A slit, or a couple of lines. The A/B said expose, do not remove. I have seen people skin a cutting and then wonder why it dried. You wanted a wound. You made a corpse.

The knife I use is the same one I graft with if it is sharp that morning. I set the stick on the bench, hold it like a pencil, and pull two shallow lines from an inch above the base down through the cut end. I want a pale stripe, not a trench. If I see pith I went too far. If I see only brown outer bark I did not go far enough. One side is enough on a pencil. Two sides on a fat cane. I do not chase the line around the stick until it meets itself. That is a girdle, and a girdle on a cup cutting is how you kill the battery you just planted. A score is a door. A ring is an eviction.

**Depth:** most of the cutting in the mix, one or two nodes up for the shoot, and a **1–2 inch gap** under the base in a bag. In a cup, not deeper than halfway. The wound hanging in a puddle at the bottom is the classic death. Gravity pulls the extra water there. Your “I barely water” still collects.

How I leave the gap: I fill the cup halfway, set the stick, then add mix around it until the wound sits above the floor. In a bag I do the same with a fist of mix in the bottom first so the base is not kissing plastic. I poke a hole with a finger, not a dibble that packs a well. If I can feel the stick tap the bottom when I press, I pull it back up. That tap is the puddle’s address. Clear cups make this easy. Black nursery pots hide it. I still do it by feel — lift, look at the hole, see if a wet circle is sitting under the wound. If it is, I dump and restick higher the same hour. I would rather restick on day one than nurse a sour base for three weeks.

If the stick is too long for the cup, do not coil it. Cut a legal cutting or get a taller pot. A coil makes a wet belly.

Fresh cuts on both ends when you stick. The top cut, if it is a long blank, is a wick — recut above a node. Parafilm on the top is optional and useful in a dry room. In our shop I still use it on names I care about because the mat will pull moisture out of an open wound.

Pack the mix against the bark. A air pocket next to the score is a dry cave. A mash of soup next to the score is a swamp. Pack is a hand feel. You should not be able to yank the stick out with two fingers. You should not need a hammer.

First drink is a bottom drink if the mix was already squeezed to damp. I set the cup in a tray of water until the top dulls to even, then I pull it out and let it drain until the saucer is empty. I do not stand it in a puddle overnight “to settle.” Settling is how the score sits in tea. If I top-water, I stop when I see the first drip from the holes and I empty that drip. 8a shop heat will still pull a film to the bottom by morning. That is why the gap matters more than the sermon about watering less. You can water less and still drown the last inch.

I recut the base if I scored yesterday and the stick sat on the bench overnight. A scored wound that dried is a scab. A scab does not take mix. Fresh cut, fresh score, stick the same hour. Named wood gets that courtesy. Bulk wood from a prune can wait in a damp towel, not on a dry newspaper next to the heater. If the score browns before it hits mix, I shave a new face and try again. Two tries. After that the stick is compost. I do not keep rescoring the same inch until there is no wood left.

8a clay brains want to plant deep “for stability.” That is a tree in the yard, not a cutting in a cup. Stability in a cup is mix and a hole that drains.

I do not add a rock in the bottom “for drainage.” That raises a perched water table. Holes and a mix that is not peat sludge are drainage.

Zone 7 people bury more because they are scared of drying. Zone 9 people perch high because they are scared of rot. We should be scared of the puddle. The puddle is the 8a default in a cup without holes.

If I have to choose one habit to keep from the Pop page, it is the gap under the base. The score is second. The hormone is third. The puddle is the thing I will not give a fourth chance.
