I have lost more figs to a low spot than to a headline low.

South Arkansas clay perches water. A hole you dig like a grave and fill with potting mix becomes a bucket. The potting mix drinks the yard’s water and holds it around a root ball that was already a pot-shaped stranger. Then January is ice water and July is a sour sump.

I plant on a **broad mound** if the spot perches. Same depth as the pot, maybe a hair lower if I want the crown in mulch, not a buried trunk that stays wet. I do not bury a fig like a tomato. I do not perch it like a rosemary in gravel unless drainage is the whole problem.

The native dirt gets broken, not replaced. A little of our mix to ease the transition, not a bathtub of it. The roots have to leave the party and enter the county. If you give them a party they never leave, they circle, and the first dry week they behave like a pot you cannot pick up.

UAEX has been saying the obvious for years: drain, sun, 6–8 hours, east wall if you can steal one. I plant late winter through spring when I can water for two weeks without a trip. I do not hero-plant in August and leave.

We have tens in the ground, not the whole collection. That is on purpose. A hole is a ten-year decision. A #3 is a two-year argument. I will not put a name I have not eaten into a hole I will regret. I will put a name I know, or a rootstock I might graft, or a trial I am willing to mow around.

8a winter rewards the mound. The earth is a blanket if it is earth. A bowl of ice is not a blanket. Press a heaved plant back in March and mulch. Do not assume heave is death.

Zone 7 in-ground is still the right long-term move for hardy figs if you mulch the crown. Zone 9 in-ground is default. 8a in-ground is how I get January back — if I did not plant a bucket.

I walk the hole after a rain before I commit. If there is a puddle in the morning, I pick another spot or I raise the grade. That walk is cheaper than a post-mortem in May.

How I build the mound: I mark a wide seat, not a deep grave. I break the clay glaze on the sides with the fork so the walls are not a bucket. The mound is broader than the #3 and only as tall as I need to keep the crown out of a puddle after a two-inch rain. I mix a little of our pot mix — perlite and castings — into the native dirt at the contact. I do not dump a 5-gallon of potting mix in a hole and call it amended. That bathtub is how you drown a fig in January and sour it in July. I tamp with my boot, not a dance. Air pockets under a #3 become bowls after the first storm.

I plant late winter through spring when I can water for two weeks without a trip. I do not hero-plant in August. August is hold. A hole I dug in April and abandoned can wait until September or until the next late winter. I re-break the glaze if it sealed. Paint pen on the stake and on the spare pot if the name matters. The stake fades. The pot moves. Write it twice. Twenty-five to thirty in the clay. Hundreds in pots. A hole is a ten-year decision on common figs I have eaten, or a trial I will mow around. I will not put a mail-order maybe into a mound I will regret. One in the hole, a spare in a #3 behind the shop if I care.

The first summer the hole is a pot I cannot lift. I water it like a pot. I do not fertigate it like a ten-year Celeste. I still walk after rain to see if the mound became a bowl. The second summer it starts to behave. Established is two summers in that hole, roots out of the old pot shape, a winter that was earth and not a bowl of ice. I do not spend the word on a Saturday stake. I do not quote a “took in the ground” percent. Long trials. Pot-up before June so the plant that goes in the mound is a #3 that already faced a skillet, not a cup I was proud of in April.

Dig a seat, not a grave. The clay will be there either way. You get to choose whether the fig sits on it or drowns in it.
