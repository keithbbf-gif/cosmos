I check by **weight**. I do not hold a weekly autopsy.

Every time you slide a cutting out of a pop, you break the contact you packed. Every time you peel a cover, you change the weather. Every time you pinch the base “just to see,” you can snap the first thread. Pop roots are fragile. The Pop page already said be careful at pot-up. Peeking is pot-up without the pot.

I learned this on a named stick I wanted a photo of. I slid it out on day twelve because a forum said “check for callus.” The mix fell off. The wound shone. I packed it back and told myself I had been careful. Two weeks later that cup was the sour one. I had not found a root. I had found a reason to break the contact I packed on stick day. The photo was of wet wood. I still have the photo. I do not have that plant. That is the trade.

A clear cup is the compromise. Roots at the wall are a glance. That glance can be once a week. Not once a morning. Not every time you walk to the shop for a screwdriver.

Date the cup so your brain has a job besides peeking. Day 10 is early for some names and late for none as a reason to panic. Day 21 with firm wood and right weight is wait. Day 35 with a liar leaf and a light cup is a problem you can name. Day 35 with firm wood and a callus is still wait.

I peek when the cup smells, when it is suddenly heavy, when the top wilted overnight, when the dog knocked the tray. Those are inspections. The rest is fidgeting.

Weight is a habit I can do with my eyes on the door. I pick up the same two cups every time I walk the tray — one I know is on schedule, one I am worried about. Light means drink. Heavy means wait. Same weight as yesterday means I do not invent a third action. I do not squeeze the cup to “feel the mix” until the plastic bows. That is another way to break a thread against the wall. If I need a second opinion I look at the clear wall, once. Then I put the cup back in the same hole in the tray so the light and the mat stay the same.

8a makes fidgeting worse because something is always happening — a leaf, a gnat, a 80°F afternoon. Happening is not a reason to unbury the wound.

Write a note instead of taking a photo every day. The photo habit is how covers stay off too long under a phone flash and a south window.

Zone 7 people peek because nothing moves for a month and they get lonely. Zone 9 people peek because everything moves in a week and they get greedy. We get lonely and greedy in the same tray. Put the tray where you do not trip over it.

If you must teach someone, pick one sacrificial cup. That cup is the classroom. The named wood is not.

I keep the tray where I have to walk past it and cannot sit with it. Back bench, not the table I drink coffee at. If I am teaching a kid or a neighbor, we pick one cheap cup with extra wood and that cup is allowed to be ruined. We slide it, we talk about callus, we pack it back, we write PEEKED on the tape. The named wood stays closed. A classroom that costs a collection is a bad classroom.

A calendar on the tape is the other half of not peeking. I write the stick date and the first week I am allowed to look at the wall — usually day 21, later on a slow name. Before that date the only legal reasons are smell, overnight wilt, a knocked tray, or a cup that went suddenly heavy. After that date I still do not hold an autopsy. I glance. I weigh. I walk. If I want a story I write the date and the weight in the notebook. The notebook does not snap a root.

Patience is the ingredient Dad names for fruit trees. For cuttings it is shorter and ruder: leave it alone until the cup gets light or the wall shows a thread. Your curiosity is not a nutrient.
