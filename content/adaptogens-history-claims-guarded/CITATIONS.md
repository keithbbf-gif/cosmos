# Citations and honesty rule

This series works from **named public documents** and from standard secondary
accounts of materia medica. It does not pretend to be an archival edition of
Lazarev's notebooks or a systematic review of trials.

## Honesty rule

If a date, quotation, or headcount is not in a document we can name, the
draft hedges or stays silent. "Researchers have shown" without a document
type is a claims leak. "A 1969 English-language review by Brekhman and
Dardymov" is a historical sentence.

Secondary literature disagrees in public. We keep the disagreement on the
page.

## Documents we actually leaned on

- **Brekhman II, Dardymov IV.** "New substances of plant origin which
  increase nonspecific resistance." *Annual Review of Pharmacology* 9
  (1969): 419–430. The English crossing most later writers point at.
  Some popular bibliographies list 1968 / volume 8; we treat the 1969
  volume-9 citation as the usual scholarly handle and note the drift.
- **Lazarev NV.** Contribution listed in later papers as the 7th All-Union
  Congress of Physiology, Biochemistry, and Pharmacology (Moscow, 1947).
  We did not hold the original page. We treat 1947 as a commonly *attributed*
  congress date for nonspecific-resistance talk, not as a settled first
  print of the word *adaptogen*.
- **Panossian A, Wagner H.** "Adaptogens: A Review of their History,
  Biological Activity, and Clinical Benefits." *HerbalGram* 90 (2011):
  52–63. Useful for the **dating footnote**: the authors say they did not
  find the word *adaptogen* in print before 1958, including in Lazarev's
  1947 book *Evolution of Pharmacology*. That footnote is why draft 04
  exists. We do **not** import this review's efficacy tables into our
  claims.
- **European Medicines Agency, HMPC.** "Reflection paper on the
  adaptogenic concept." EMEA/HMPC/102655/2007, adopted 8 May 2008.
  Public PDF. The conclusion that matters for our fence: the term is not
  accepted in the pharmacological and clinical terminology commonly used
  in the EU for a marketing authorisation; clinical packages then on the
  table had shortcomings; the concept could still be *considered* when
  assessing traditional herbal medicinal products.
- **Davydov M, Krikorian AD.** "Eleutherococcus senticosus as an
  adaptogen: a closer look." *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* 72 (2000):
  345–393. A long, skeptical closer look — cited as a closer look, not as
  a verdict we re-try.
- **Wagner H, Nörr H, Winterhoff H.** "Plant adaptogens." *Phytomedicine*
  1, no. 1 (1994): 63–76. Early German-language-lab English review; a
  document in the crossing, not a seal.
- **Selye H.** "A syndrome produced by diverse nocuous agents." *Nature*
  138 (1936): 32. The stress-syndrome paper later Soviet writers could
  point at. We cite the existence of the paper, not a license to treat
  "stress" as a disease we then cure.
- **Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994** (United States).
  The statute that sits under most U.S. bottle grammar. We describe the
  statute's *kind*, not every subsection.
- **Shennong Bencao Jing** (神農本草經), as a **textual tradition**.
  Compilation and dating are themselves scholarly arguments. We do not
  pretend to a first-century autograph.
- **Nanai / Goldi use of schisandra**, as reported in later reviews that
  point to Komarov (1890s) and Arsenyev (early 1900s) Far-East travel and
  botanical work. We cite that *chain*, not a field notebook we opened.

## What we did not do

- We did not re-grade Soviet or later clinical trials.
- We did not open USPTO, FDA docket rooms, or Vladivostok institute
  archives.
- We did not copy long passages from copyrighted reviews. Paraphrase and
  point.
- We did not treat Wikipedia as a source. If a fact only lived there, it
  did not enter a draft.

## How to add a source later

Name the document, the year, and the *kind* (congress abstract, reflection
paper, materia-medica recension, ethnographer's travel account, statute).
Then say what the document can bear. A travel account can bear "hunters
were described as carrying berries." It cannot bear "therefore the berries
prevent exhaustion disease."
