Most origin stories in this folder are cloudy. This one is a PDF.

On 8 May 2008 the Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC) of the European Medicines Agency adopted a "Reflection paper on the adaptogenic concept," document EMEA/HMPC/102655/2007. It is short. It is public. It was forced into existence, the paper says, while the committee was drafting a community herbal monograph on *Eleutherococcus senticosus* root. A shrub made a philosophy problem.

This draft does not treat, cure, or prevent any disease. It is not medical advice. It is a reading of a committee's public thinking.

What the paper does, in my paraphrase — go to the PDF if you need their words — is four jobs.

It **defines by quotation**. Lazarev 1947 is their attributed start (we have already put a dating quarrel on that year). Brekhman's clauses are reproduced, including the awkward pathology-gradient line. Selye is named as the sense of *stress* they mean.

It **sorts neighbors**. Tonics, in a traditional European sense, address weakness or lack of tone. Stimulants raise work capacity and then drop. Adaptogens are *reputed* to raise work capacity without that drop and to sit against the alarm-exhaustion picture. "Reputed" is their verb. I will keep it.

It **complains about the clinical pile**. Numerous studies exist, the committee knows. The packages in view had shortcomings: inclusion and exclusion, medication description, diagnosis, design, analysis. Conditions wandered. Some studies were very small. None would suffice to substantiate efficacy of eleuthero in a clearly defined clinical condition, even if the pile as a whole might justify further research.

It **refuses a class for authorisation**. "As such, the term is not accepted in pharmacological and clinical terminology that is commonly used in the EU." More clinical work in a well-defined condition would be needed for that kind of marketing authorisation. The concept may still be *considered* when assessing traditional herbal medicinal products, eleuthero root being the example on the table.

I want to sit with the refusal, because popular writing treats "not accepted" as either a scandal or a triumph.

It is not a scandal that a functional class from a Soviet review culture did not fit EU authorisation grammar. Authorisation grammar wants a defined condition. The class was built to be nonspecific. The collision is almost elegant.

It is not a triumph for a certain kind of skeptic, either. The committee did not say "the plants are empty." It said the *term* is not appropriate for a particular legal-scientific job, and that traditional-use assessment can still look at the concept. Those are different doors.

For this folder, the paper is a **fence-post we did not have to invent**. A regulator, writing in 2008, already refused to let the noun become a disease-shaped marketing class. We are not being original. We are being consistent.

There is a temptation to over-read a reflection paper. It is not a statute. It is not a monograph. It is not the last word of every European member state in every later year. It is a committee thinking in public about a word. Public thinking is a historical event. I will not make it into a global ban, and I will not make it into a secret endorsement.

Notice what the paper does with **asthenia**. It uses the traditional term, then footnotes that it does not map cleanly onto ICD-10 lines like malaise/fatigue or neurasthenia or somatoform talk. That footnote is ethnobotany in a regulatory register: *their word is not our code*. If a European committee can hold that friction, an essay can.

Notice also the **eleuthero-centrism**. The shrub is the example. Other plants are named as "may include." A reflection built around one root should not be cited as a census of the kingdom.

If you write about adaptogens and you skip this PDF in favor of a brand's white paper, you have chosen a worse primary. The PDF is free. It has a date. It has a refusal. It has a dating habit we can argue with. That is a lot to ask of six pages.

I will not tell you that the HMPC was "right about the science." I am not restaging their assessment. I will tell you they left a public record of a class that would not sit still in the grammar of authorisation. This series is written in the shadow of that record, on purpose. The shadow is a good place to do history. It is a bad place to sell a cure. We are not selling.
