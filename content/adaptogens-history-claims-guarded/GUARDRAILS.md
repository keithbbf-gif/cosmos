# Claims guardrails — adaptogens history series

These drafts are **educational ethnobotany and intellectual history**. They are
not labels, not protocols, and not a back door into disease claims.

A reviewer should be able to fail a draft by quoting a sentence. If the only
defense is "the reader will know what we meant," the sentence is wrong.

## What a draft may do

- Name plants, people, places, languages, and documents.
- Describe **traditional use** as a record of practice: who prepared what, in
  what form, under what name, in which text or oral account.
- Describe the **Soviet pharmacological wager** that coined and then exported
  the word *adaptogen*. That wager is a mid-20th-century research program, not
  a village category.
- Mark dating disputes in public. The popular "Lazarev, 1947" line and the
  "word not in print before 1958" line both circulate in secondary literature.
  A draft that picks one date and buries the other is doing marketing.
- Quote or paraphrase **regulators as regulators**. The European Medicines
  Agency herbal committee (HMPC) said in 2008 that *adaptogen* is not accepted
  terminology for an EU marketing authorisation. That is a historical fact
  about a committee, not a secret proof that the plants "don't work."
- Say that a review article exists, who wrote it, and what kind of document it
  is. Existence of a review is not a disease indication.
- Name known cautions as **historical and chemical facts** (for example,
  glycyrrhizin and mineralocorticoid-like effects have been discussed in
  ordinary pharmacology for decades) without turning the caution into a
  treatment plan or a "safe for everyone" blessing.

## What a draft may not do

- Claim or imply that a plant **diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents** a
  disease. Not in the headline. Not in a folksy aside. Not by stacking
  "researchers found" on a condition name.
- Collapse folk categories (*rasayana*, *bu qi*, tonic, *jing* nourishment,
  household tulsi) into the word *adaptogen* as if villagers had been waiting
  for Lazarev.
- Use "used as an adaptogen for thousands of years." That sentence is a
  category error with a calendar glued on.
- Invent quotations, trial counts, Olympic medals, or archive holdings.
- Give doses, stacks, "cycles," or "what I take."
- Call anything FDA-approved as an adaptogen. The U.S. Food and Drug
  Administration does not maintain an official adaptogen drug class.
- Promise "no side effects," "100% safe," "clinically proven to," "miracle,"
  or "boosts the immune system."
- Use disease names as the payoff of a historical story ("and that is why
  rhodiola treats depression").
- Speak in the wellness-bot register: *unlock*, *superfood*, *hack your
  stress*, *in today's fast-paced world*, *let's dive in*.

## Voice

Write like a person who has sat with a photocopy and a grocery label and
trusted neither on first glance. Specific objects. Uneven sentences. A little
dry humor is fine. Cheerleading is not. If a source is thin, say the trail is
thin. If two reviews disagree on a year, put both years on the table.

First person is allowed when it is a readerly "I" ("I keep a pencil in the
margin"), not a testimonial "I" ("I cured my...").

## Legal and medical posture (plain language)

This folder is not medical advice, not a dietary-supplement label, and not a
substitute for a clinician who knows the person in front of them. Dietary
supplements in the United States are regulated under a different statute than
drugs (DSHEA, 1994). Structure/function language on a bottle is a legal
artifact. It is not a finding that a plant is an adaptogen, and it is not
permission to imply disease treatment.

Traditional use is evidence that people did a thing. It is not, by itself,
evidence that the thing does what a modern disease category names.

## How a draft proves it stayed inside the fence

Every draft carries YAML frontmatter with `claims_posture:
educational-ethnobotany` and `status: draft`. Every draft ends with a
**Claims box** that a skimming reader can find without finishing the essay.
The linter in `tools/lint_claims.py` is a tripwire, not a conscience: it
catches the lazy phrases. A human still has to catch the sly ones.

## Staging

Drafts are filed under `stage-0N-*` so the argument can be rebuilt in order:

1. How to read (refusals and categories)
2. The Soviet construct
3. East Asian materia medica and trade
4. South Asian and other traditions
5. Law, aisles, and review articles
6. Reader tools and a working bibliography

"Staged" here means **ordered and still a draft**. Nothing in this folder is
cleared for a product label.
