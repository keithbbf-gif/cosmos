Form is the part of plant history everyone skips so they can get to the myth.

A decoction is a pot, time, water, a ratio someone learned, a smell in a kitchen. A powder is a mill. A wine is a solvent with a social life. A standardized extract is a factory's decision about a marker. A dropper is a theater of precision. These are not the same object with different hats. They are different technologies that later captions treat as interchangeable because the Latin name stayed still.

This draft does not treat, cure, or prevent any disease. It is not medical advice. It is a short history of **form**, which is to say a short history of what you are actually holding.

Start with water. Many of the East Asian files in this folder assume a decoction: roots simmered, a liquid drunk as a liquid, often in a formula with other items. Water is not a weak solvent in the cultural sense; it is the default. Default is a fact. A person who takes a dry capsule of one celebrity root is not "doing the decoction in a convenient form." They are doing a different act.

Then alcohol and other solvents. Tinctures, wines, a Soviet extract with a percentage, a later "dual-extracted" mushroom language. Solvents choose constituents. Choice is a technology. A wine that a Ming household might have used is not a 1:2 tincture in glycerin with a dropper that makes you feel like a chemist. Feeling like a chemist is a 21st-century emotion.

Then markers. Eleutherosides, withanolides, rosavins, ginsenosides, gypenosides — the names sit next to each other in this sentence the way they sit on labels, which is already a lie of companionship. Each marker war belongs to one plant's industrial decade. Markers can be a quality-control tool. They can also be a way to imply that the number is the tradition. The tradition was a taste, a pulse system, a pot. The number is a chromatogram.

Then the dropper. I have a petty dislike of the dropper's theater. It suggests a dose of seriousness. Seriousness is not identity, not a traditional indication, and not a reason to skip the rest of the label. A dropper of a blend is still a blend. A blend is a 21st-century summit (drafts 21, 30, 32).

Why does form matter to a claims fence?

Because **efficacy talk that ignores form is already a kind of fiction**, and we are not even doing efficacy talk. Traditional use of a decoction cannot underwrite a claim about a 50:1 extract, not in a history essay, not without a sentence that admits the jump. The jump is where disease verbs like to hide: "used for centuries" (decoction) plus a dropper photograph (factory). Century plus photograph is a costume.

Form also matters to identity. A whole root slice can be looked at. A powder is a faith. Faith is how lookalikes travel (draft 28).

Form matters to caution. Licorice as a pinch in a formula is not licorice as a solo sweet extract (draft 33). The diplomat changes when he is made to sing alone.

I will not give you conversions. Conversions are how this draft would become a cookbook. I will give you a reading habit: **name the form before you name the hope.** If you cannot name the form, you do not yet have a historical sentence.

There is a kindness in this habit for people who love their kitchen. A pot of soup with *huangqi* or *insam* is a form with a social meaning. It does not need to be upgraded to a dropper to be real. Upgrade culture is the aisle talking. The pot can refuse.

There is a kindness, too, for people who only have the dropper. You are not failing a dynasty. You are using a modern object. Call it modern. Modern objects can be discussed without a costume. They just cannot borrow a century they did not live.

I have watched a decoction reduce. The kitchen windows steam. That steam is not a mechanism. It is a reminder that plant history happened in rooms with fire. Fire is older than DSHEA. Fire is older than Lazarev. Fire is not an adaptogen. I would rather you remember the fire than remember a marker percentage. Fire does not fit on a label, which is why labels talk about droppers instead.

When you pick up the next bottle, ask: water, alcohol, powder, marker, blend? If the bottle cannot answer, the bottle is not a document yet. It is a mood. Moods are for weather. Weather is draft 35's climate, not a pot.
