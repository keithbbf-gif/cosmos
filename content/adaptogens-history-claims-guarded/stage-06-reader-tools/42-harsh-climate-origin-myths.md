"It survived the tundra, so you will" is folklore.

It is durable folklore. You will meet it on rhodiola tins, maca tubs, eleuthero sketches of taiga, cordyceps pastures, a shilajit cliff at sunset. The plot is always the same. A plant (or a fungus, or an exudate) lives where a person would be uncomfortable. Therefore the substance contains a lesson the person's body can swallow. The lesson is usually called adaptation. The noun was waiting.

This draft does not treat, cure, or prevent any disease. It is not medical advice. It is a dismantling of a plot.

Plants live where they live because of evolution, chance, pollinators, soils, and humans who moved them. A rhodiola on a ridge is good at being a rhodiola on a ridge. It is not a coach. Coaching is a human job. When we make the ridge into a coach, we are doing sympathetic magic with a topographic map.

Sympathetic magic is older than Lazarev. Eat the brave animal, become brave. It is not more scientific because we say *adaptogen* instead of *brave*. The Soviet program at least had a bench and a definition, however arguable. The harsh-climate plot does not need a bench. It needs a photograph of wind.

Notice the **selection bias**. We do not say that a weed in a parking lot will help you survive fluorescent light. We pick landscapes that already look like stories: tundra, altiplano, Himalaya, taiga. Stories have prestige. Parking lots do not. *Jiaogulan* as a weed had to be dressed as southern ginseng to get in (draft 25). Dressing is the tell.

Notice the **human erasure**. High pastures have people. Taiga has people. Andean farms have people. The plot prefers an empty sublime, because people complicate the magic with wages and overharvest and taste. Empty sublime is a colonial postcard.

Notice the **theft from Selye**. Alarm, resistance, exhaustion was a picture of an organism under load. The postcard steals "resistance" and glues it to weather. Weather is not a protocol. A person in an office is not a ridge.

I am not saying mountain plants are uninteresting. They are interesting as mountain plants: short seasons, secondary metabolites that chemists like to name, agricultural skills, trade. Interest does not require a moral. The moral ("so you will survive your inbox") is the claims leak. Inbox-survival is a structure/function cousin and a disease-prevention cousin, depending on how hard you wink.

A guarded substitute sentence: *This species has a documented range in X; people in Y used it as Z; later sellers used the range as a metaphor for human load.* The metaphor is now visible. Visible metaphors cannot finish a medical sentence without being seen.

If you write copy, try a week without weather. Sell the species, the part, the taste, the document. If you cannot, you were selling a postcard.

If you read copy, try covering the mountain with your thumb. What remains? If what remains is a Latin name and a part, you have a plant. If what remains is "ancient strength," you have a plot.

The Soviet noun is extra-attractive to this plot because *adapt-* is already in the word. The ridge *adapts*, you *adapt*, the capsule is the pun. Puns are not evidence. I like puns in jokes. I do not like them in a file that is trying not to lie.

There is a gentler version of the plot that says: people in hard places valued certain plants. That sentence can be true and still not mean the plant is a portable hardness. People in hard places valued fat, stories, kin, sleep when they could get it, and not dying of other causes. A celebrity root is easy to photograph. Kin is not.

I have been cold on a ridge and I have been tired in a shop. The ridge did not offer me a capsule. The shop did. I bought a sandwich. The sandwich has a thinner myth, which is why I trust it more as a sandwich. I do not trust it as an adaptogen. It is not one. Neither, in the postcard sense, is the ridge.

Let the plants keep their climates. Let your inbox keep its union, its sleep, its clinician if you need one. Do not make a Crassulaceae do labor politics. That is an unfair load even by Selye's picture.

## Claims box

- A folklore plot (landscape → portable hardness), not a biogeography textbook.
- This draft does not treat, cure, or prevent any disease.
- A species' range is not a metaphor you are obliged to swallow.
- Not medical advice. Not a mountain protocol.
