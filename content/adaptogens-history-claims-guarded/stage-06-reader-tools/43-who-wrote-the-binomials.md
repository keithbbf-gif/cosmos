The Latin on the jar is not a plant's secret name. It is a sentence written by someone.

*Panax ginseng* C.A. Mey. carries Carl Anton Meyer. *Eleutherococcus senticosus* (Rupr. & Maxim.) Maxim. carries Ruprecht, Maximowicz, and Maximowicz again. *Withania somnifera* (L.) Dunal carries Linnaeus and Dunal. *Lepidium meyenii* Walp. carries Walpers. *Ophiocordyceps sinensis* carries a chain that includes Berkeley and a later cleanup by Sung and colleagues. I am not reciting a liturgy. I am pointing at **authorship**.

This draft does not treat, cure, or prevent any disease. It is not medical advice. It is about credit, empire, and the little authority a binomial borrows.

Linnaean names were a good tool. They still are. They let a shop in one country point, in principle, at the same organism as a paper in another. Principle is not practice (draft 28). But the tool was also a **taking-into-account**. A plant that already had a name in Chinese, Sanskrit, Quechua, Nanai, Korean, Tibetan, Russian folk speech, was rewritten in a Latin grammar by people who were often passing through with presses, armies, missions, or a professional flora to finish.

I do not refuse the tool. I refuse the aura. The aura says: the Latin is the real name, the other names are nicknames. That aura is how *ciwujia* becomes a footnote to eleuthero, how *insam* becomes a flavor of ginseng, how *yartsa gunbu* becomes a species epithet *sinensis* that points at a nation-state the collectors were using as a map.

Author citations after the binomial are a small honesty the aisle usually drops. They look fussy. Fuss is a reminder that a European man, or a sequence of them, claimed the right to stabilize the file. Some were remarkable botanists. Remarkable botanists can still be part of an imperial infrastructure. Both sentences can sit on the page.

What does this have to do with adaptogens?

The Soviet noun is another renaming. It takes plants with local files and restabilizes them as members of a functional class. The Latin and the Soviet noun sometimes travel together as a pair of "scientific" handles, as if two European-and-Russian grammars made a plant more itself. A plant is itself in the ground. Handles are for us.

A claims-guarded habit: when you write a plant, give **one local name and one binomial**, and do not let the binomial erase the local name. When you write a class, give a date. Dates and names together make a caption. Captions prevent the Latin from sounding like nature's own voice.

There is a petty version of this draft that sneers at every dead botanist. Sneering is cheap. The useful version asks, of each handle: **who needed this name, for what circulation?** Meyer needed a genus that could include a prestige root. Maximowicz needed a Far-East flora. A 1990s exporter needed a binomial that a customs form would accept. A 2010s brand needed a Latin that looked expensive. Circulation is the history.

Women, assistants, local guides, and unnamed collectors are often not in the author citation. The citation is already a compressed credit list. Compression, as with review articles (draft 39), drops people. If you can restore a collector, restore them. If you cannot, do not pretend the Latin appeared on the plant like dew.

I will keep using binomials in this folder. I want you to see them as **documents**. Documents have authors. Authors have years. Years are not antiquity. C.A. Mey. is a nineteenth-century event. It is not a Han event. Putting C.A. Mey. on a label that says "ancient" is a time-stack, the same crime as "ancient adaptogen."

If you have a family name for a plant, that name is a document too. It does not become unscientific because it lacks a parenthetical author. It becomes unscientific when someone uses it to point at three species at once and then adds a disease. Precision is the ethics, not the Latin.

The jar will keep printing binomials. Good. Print the local name too, if you have it and you have the right to use it. And print the class-word small, or not at all. Three names is a lot for a jar. A lot is better than one name doing three jobs.

I have no disease to offer you at the end of a draft about names. I have a request: when you say *Panax*, hear Meyer in the room. When you say *adaptogen*, hear a Soviet program in the room. When you say *insam*, hear a peninsula. Rooms should not be merged without a caption.
