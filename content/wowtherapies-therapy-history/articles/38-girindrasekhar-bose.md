**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Girindrasekhar Bose — G. Bose in many citations — was born in 1887 (the Indian Psychoanalytical Society's own page has given 1886; scholarly reference usually uses 31 January 1887) and died on 3 June 1953. He took a medical degree at Calcutta in 1910, a master's in psychology in 1917, and in 1921 a D.Sc. for *The Concept of Repression*, the first doctorate of its kind at that university. He sent the thesis to Freud. Freud wrote back, on 29 May 1921, that he was glad to testify to the correctness of its principal views. The letters continued until 1937. The IPS later published them.

## 26 January 1922

The Indian Psychoanalytical Society was founded that day at Bose's house, 14 Parsi Bagan Lane, Calcutta. Fifteen original members: psychologists, philosophers, and army physicians, including British psychiatrists such as Owen Berkeley-Hill of Ranchi. Freud told Bose to write to Ernest Jones. The society became a component of the IPA. Bose remained president until his death.

This is not a story of a "remote" man receiving the light. Bose had already been teaching psychoanalytic ideas in Calcutta's psychology courses. He started, in 1933, what later writers have called the first general-hospital psychiatry unit in Asia, at R.G. Kar Medical College. The society's journal *Samiksha* began in 1947. Lumbini Park Mental Hospital was part of the same institutional weave.

## Opposite wishes

Bose's theory of repression is a theory of *opposite wishes*, not only of a single wish pushed down. He also doubted that the castration complex did the work Freud assigned it in resolving the Oedipus story. The letters are polite and stubborn. Freud is surprised, pleased, and not converted. Christiane Hartnack and Ashis Nandy have read this correspondence as a colonial and anti-colonial text as well as a technical one. Both readings belong.

The society's logo, the Ardhanarishvara, half Shiva and half Parvati, is an argument in an image: bisexuality as a mythic fact, not only a Viennese one.

## Why he is not a footnote to Freud

Because the chronology will not allow it. The thesis is 1921. The society is 1922. The European institutes were still busy expelling one another. Because a counseling history that stays on the Atlantic treats India as an export market. Because any clinician who thinks "the Oedipus complex is universal" should have to sit with a man who wrote to Freud from Calcutta and said, in effect, not like that.

Sudhir Kakar and later Indian analysts are a different generation. They are not substitutes for Bose. They are proof the argument continued.
