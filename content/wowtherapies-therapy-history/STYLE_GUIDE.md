# Style guide — WOW Therapies, therapy history series

This pack is educational history for [WOWTherapies.com](https://wowtherapies.com): psychotherapy, counseling, and clinical-psychology heritage. It is **not** the SLPWOW speech-pathology series. Do not retell Van Riper, articulation science, dysphagia, or childhood apraxia here. Those belong on the speech lane.

`voice_check: human` means the writer draft passed this file. `voice_check: edited` means a human editor read the piece aloud and tightened grammar, voice, and guardrails. Neither value is a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Who is speaking

A historically literate magazine writer who has sat in enough consulting rooms to know that a theory is not a person. Warm without being pastoral. Precise without being a textbook. The reader may be a curious client, a family member, a student, or a clinician. Do not talk down. Do not talk shop as if the room were empty of patients.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas practice brand. These essays are **heritage education**, not a menu of services and not a claim that the clinic practices every school named here.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" as a metaphor for a field
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- three-beat synonym piles ("heal, grow, and thrive")
- "the father of…" unless a contemporary actually used that phrase and you are quoting the myth, not endorsing it
- "Freud discovered the unconscious"
- "gold-standard treatment" as marketing
- invented quotes
- AI-generated faces or "period photographs" that never existed

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "empower," "in this article we will explore," "journey of healing" as filler.

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a room, a document, or a named quarrel. The first sentence should be checkable.
2. Mix sentence length. A short sentence after a long one. Do not write every paragraph as topic sentence + three examples + wrap.
3. Name the book, paper, hospital, or society. *Trauma and Recovery* (1992). The 1957 Rogers paper. Bicêtre. The Indian Psychoanalytical Society (26 January 1922).
4. Mark uncertainty. `[CITE NEEDED]` or a plain "historians disagree" beats a clean falsehood.
5. Put Freud, Jung, and the other famous names in their century. Do not offer them as current treatment manuals.
6. Give non-European and women's work full essays, not a closing paragraph titled "diversity."
7. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target 1,000–1,800 words for figures, 1,200–2,000 for era essays. Cut the recap paragraph if it only restates the headings. `check_pack.py` fails an era under 1,200 words or a figure under 1,000. Deepen with dated scenes, not with recap.

## Claims posture

See `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`. History may describe a method. History may not teach a reader to self-administer it. No session scripts, no skill worksheets, no "try this at home" protocols.

Efficacy sentences, if any, stay historical and sourced ("Linehan's 1991 trial reported…") and never become "DBT will help your borderline symptoms."

Living people get extra care: no gossip, no unsourced health claims. Linehan's 2011 public disclosure of her own history may be mentioned because she chose the *New York Times* as the venue. Do not embroider it.

## Portraits

Public domain or Creative Commons only. Document the file in `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. If there is no clean license, use type or a period object — never a generated face. See `PHOTO_NOTES.md`.

## Series voice, in one paragraph

Write as if *The New Yorker* and *Psychotherapy Networker* had to share a desk, and the desk was in a small-city clinic that still answers its own phone. The past is allowed to be strange. The present is not allowed to be sold.

## Complementary pack (do not duplicate)

| This pack | SLPWOW speech-pathology pack |
| --- | --- |
| Psychotherapy, counseling, clinical psychology | Speech-language pathology, fluency, language, swallowing |
| Freud, Rogers, Beck, Linehan, Bowlby, Fanon… | Bell, Van Riper, articulation science, school SLP history |
| Educational mental-health *heritage* | Educational communication *heritage* |

A figure who touched both (for example William James on habit and attention) may appear here only in a psychotherapy frame.

## Footer (every article)

Keep the educational note already in the draft. In WordPress it becomes a reusable block. Do not shrink it to a gray afterthought.
