# Jean Tarneaud

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Jean Tarneaud made the French language say *phoniatrie*.

Adrien Ernest Jean Tarneaud was born in Jarnac on 6 January 1888 and died in Clichy on 19 July 1972. The 1917 thesis made him a physician; the jobs made him a voice man — laryngologist at the Hôpital Bellan, physician to the Conservatoire national de musique, chef des travaux de phoniatrie at the Paris medical faculty. In 1932 he pushed the term that would name the French medical specialty. He founded the Société Française de Phoniatrie. In 1947, with Richard Luchsinger and Miroslav Seeman, he founded *Folia Phoniatrica et Logopaedica*, the IALP’s journal.

## Conservatoire and hospital

The Conservatoire appointment is not decoration. It is the French version of the singing-teacher door (essay 06): a physician with hours in a music school, a musician with a doctor who already knows what a register argument sounds like. Tarneaud’s *Traité* line and the society meetings are how Paris kept a medical voice culture that did not have to borrow all its words from Berlin.

The *Folia* founding issue’s preface carries his name with Luchsinger, Seeman, and the French speech-therapy presence of Suzanne Borel-Maisonny. That pairing — physician phoniatrics and pedagogical orthophonie — is the French fence, cousin to the German Phoniatrie/Logopädie split. An American reader who thinks “voice clinic” is an ASHA invention is missing a Paris journal.

## Residue

A word. A society. A journal that still prints the papers Hirano and everyone after him needed. The *Folia* in-memoriam note from 1972 is pious and short, as those notes are. This profile can be shorter than Gutzmann’s and still earn its keep: without Tarneaud, the French chapter of this pack is a rumor.

## Orthophonie in the same preface

Borel-Maisonny’s presence on the *Folia* founding page is the French reminder that a journal can carry two professions without merging them. Tarneaud’s Conservatoire hours are the other reminder: music school and faculty of medicine in one CV. American readers who want a single “SLP equivalent” will not find one. They will find a fence with a journal nailed to it.

Clichy in 1972 is not a glamorous death-place in the histories. It is a date. The term *phoniatrie* outlived him, which is what he wanted in 1932.
