# Phoniatrics Names the Voice

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

Hermann Gutzmann’s 1905 inaugural lecture in Berlin had a dry title and a territorial claim: speech disorders as an object of clinical teaching. European phoniatricians still treat the year as a founding date. Not because no one had helped a hoarse teacher before that afternoon, but because a medical faculty had agreed, in public, that the voice and the articulation of words belonged on the lecture list with the rest of the body.

The sibling SLPWOW pack tells Gutzmann as an ancestor of speech-language pathology. This pack needs him for a narrower reason. *Physiologie der Stimme und Sprache* (1909) is a voice book. The Berlin outpatient room of 1891, the Zehlendorf sanatorium of 1896, the move under Gustav Killian into the Charité throat-and-nose service in 1912 — those are the rooms where a *medical* voice specialty practiced before American universities hired psychologists to run speech clinics.

## A teacher’s son, a medical trade

Gutzmann (1865–1922) was the son of Albert, who taught deaf children and already published on speech errors as a school problem. The son’s 1887 dissertation was *Über das Stottern*. Father and son started, in 1890, a journal whose title is a program: *Medizinisch-pädagogische Monatsschrift für die gesamte Sprachheilkunde*. Medicine and pedagogy, one monthly. That double claim never went away. It is why a modern voice clinic still argues about who “owns” a singer — the laryngologist, the SLP, the teacher.

Students came from across Europe: Max Nadoleczny, Rudolf Schilling, Hugo Stern, Miloslav Seeman, Giulio Panconcelli-Calzia, the son Hermann Jr. The Union of European Phoniatricians still prints the elder as a father of the field and counts the books in the hundreds of shorter works.

This is not “speech-language pathology” in the ASHA sense. It is a physician’s specialty with teachers in the waiting room.

## Munich, not only Berlin

Nadoleczny (16 March 1874, Zürich – 6 November 1940, Zürich) took the Berlin habit south. After ENT training that included a stretch with Gutzmann in 1900 and Lermoyez in Paris, he settled in Munich in 1901 as a specialist in ear, nose, throat, *and* voice and speech. In 1910 he took a department for voice-and-speech patients at the university ENT clinic. He refused the Charité chair after Gutzmann’s death. Bavaria made him an associate professor instead. In 1925 he founded the German society for voice and speech medicine and chaired it into the 1930s.

Gottfried Arnold would later say he had been Nadoleczny’s student. That is how a specialty actually travels: not by a single 1905 lecture, but by a man in Munich who stayed forty years and ran free courses for children on the side.

Leopold Treitel and Albert Liebmann were already publishing German methods when Gutzmann was a student. Prague, Hamburg, and Vienna had their own densities. “Berlin” is a useful headline. It is not a monopoly.

## The fence that hardened

As the century went on, German-speaking Europe split the work. **Phoniatrics** stayed medical: larynx, resonance, neurologic voice, the prescription, the endoscope. **Logopedics** — the word Fröschels would push from Vienna in 1924 — became the therapy profession. They shared patients and fought over status. They still do, under different national laws.

Jean Tarneaud, in Paris, made the French cognate *phoniatrie* a society word in the early 1930s and, in 1947, helped found *Folia Phoniatrica* with Richard Luchsinger and Miroslav Seeman. The journal is the paper hallway between these cities.

## Why an American reader needs this page

ASHA’s origin story starts in Iowa City dining rooms and a New York hotel. That story is true and incomplete. When a modern clinician says “voice clinic” and means a joint session with an otolaryngologist, she is closer to Gutzmann’s hallway than to Van Riper’s undergraduate textbook. The names changed. The hallway is old.

The war and the 1930s would smash the German-speaking version of that hallway and ship pieces of it to New York and San Francisco. That is essay 08. Gutzmann the life is profile 23. Nadoleczny is 24.
