# Friedrich S. Brodnitz

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Friedrich Samuel Brodnitz closed his Manhattan office at ninety-two and still took the loyal ones at home. He was born in Berlin on 25 September 1899 and died at Lenox Hill Hospital on 17 October 1995, in for a skin problem, the *Journal of Voice* memorial says, and gone. The New York Times obituary (22 October 1995) called him the man who restored voices of sopranos and baritones. The *Journal* called him the dean of phoniatrics in the United States. Deans in exile get nicknames.

## Berlin, then a board exam

ENT training with Carl von Eicken at the Charité; phoniatric time with Hermann Gutzmann Jr. and Theodor Flatau, in the Union of European Phoniatricians’ telling. Judy Duchan’s page says Gutzmann Sr. died in 1922; Brodnitz’s Berlin medical year is 1924. This pack follows UEP and prints the disagreement. He left Germany. He passed the American Board of Otolaryngology in 1942. Mount Sinai’s phoniatric clinic, Beth David’s speech and voice clinic, Hunter College, a private practice that ran into 1992 — essay 08 is the hallway. This profile is the paperback.

*Keep Your Voice Healthy* first appeared in 1951 (UEP and the Folia obituary; later printings 1973, 1988). *Vocal Rehabilitation* went through mid-century editions (a fourth in 1970 is the one European CVs cite). The 1954 paper with Fröschels on chewing and nodules is an exile document. He kept sitting on the American Academy’s “actor and singer as patients” panel into old age. Berlin gave him honors late — Senate 1979, German Medical Association 1980, the Berlin ENT society 1981 — the way a country does when it wants a son back without the years.

## How to read the paperback

As Mackenzie’s hygiene genre in a mid-century American accent. Not as a protocol. The useful residue is jurisdictional: a physician who thought singers were his job, in a country that had assigned “speech” to another guild. The Gould Award and a NATS citation sat on the same shelf as the German ones. He climbed mountains until eighty-eight. The memorialists mention it because they loved him. A heritage page can mention it once and then return to the books.

Henny Brodnitz survived him; the *Journal* names a marriage of fifty-two years. That is enough family. No more.

## Ninety-two, then the house calls

The *Journal* memorial’s last image — patients coming to the apartment — is easy to sentimentalize. Keep it as a staffing fact: New York had a phoniatrician who outlived his office lease. The Gould Award and the NATS citation sit next to the late Berlin honors. A man can be decorated by the country he fled and the country that licensed him. The decorations do not settle 1933.

Read *Keep Your Voice Healthy* next to Mackenzie 1886. The genre is the heritage. The rules inside the genre are not a 2026 plan.
