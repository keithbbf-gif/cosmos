# Janina K. Casper

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Janina K. Casper died on 15 May 2008, of lung cancer. The *Journal of Voice* memorial (2008) is the warrant. It says she came to the United States from Poland in 1937 without English, and later built, in Syracuse, a career that refused to be only one organ.

## The book, and the other rooms

With Raymond H. Colton she wrote *Understanding Voice Problems: A Physiological Perspective for Diagnosis and Treatment* (first edition 1990). It is the other American shelf book next to Boone: more physiology, a Syracuse collaboration, the one supervisors hand a student who needs a mechanism. She was a founding member of the Syracuse Voice Center.

The memorialists insist on the rest. Cleft lip and palate — the first multidisciplinary clinic in Syracuse for those children and adults, in their telling. Laryngectomy — the first treatment group in that city. A year in Holland on sabbatical. The thirst for programs of research that were not only “voice” in the gala sense. Essay 15 keeps her in the knife chapter so the professional-voice meeting does not eat the cancer service.

Max Casper; two children, Michele and Jeff; four grandchildren. The *Journal* names them. That is enough family.

## Residue

A title that still shows up on syllabi. A reminder that a “voice” career, done honestly, includes the people who no longer have folds to photograph. Hunt a rights-clear portrait through SUNY Upstate / Syracuse University archives; do not scrape the memorial PDF’s snapshot.

## 1937, then Syracuse

The memorial’s immigration sentence is the opening she cannot have written for herself. A child without English who later wrote the physiology-facing American voice text is a true story and a tempting parable. Keep it one sentence. The work is the 1990 book, the Voice Center, the cleft clinic, the laryngectomy group.

Colton’s name on the title page is a collaboration, not a courtesy. Courses that say “the Casper book” and drop him are doing the ordinary injustice of memory.

Lung cancer, named in the memorial, is why a voice clinician’s death sits on a page about breath and folds without needing a parable. She was a colleague who died of a disease her students also meet. That is enough.
