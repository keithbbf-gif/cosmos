# Max Nadoleczny

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Max Nadoleczny refused Berlin and still became, for forty years, the other German address of the voice.

He was born in Zürich on 16 March 1874, son of an Austrian officer who had taken Swiss citizenship and a Zürich mother. He died in the same city on 6 November 1940. The 1940 *Practica Oto-Rhino-Laryngologica* obituary is the warrant for the days. He was sixty-six. The writer, a colleague, said he had still been full of plans.

## Mainz, then the circuit

Gymnasium in Mainz. Medicine in Munich, Kiel, Berlin; license and doctorate in Munich. ENT training 1898–1901 with Haug and Schech in Munich, Gradenigo in Turin, Gutzmann in Berlin (1900), Lermoyez at the Hôpital Saint-Antoine in Paris. In November 1901 he opened in Munich as a specialist in ear, nose, throat, voice, and speech. The list is the program. He would not be only an ear man.

In 1910 he took a department for voice-and-speech patients at the university ENT clinic. Gutzmann wanted him to habilitate; he did, in 1922, the year the teacher died. Berlin offered the Charité succession. He said no. Bavaria made him an associate professor with a teaching charge that ran across faculties — medical students, physicians, and anyone else who would sit. Judy Duchan’s page says he organized free courses for children with speech and language disabilities in Munich and left about 125 publications. The German society for voice and speech medicine, which he founded in 1925 and chaired into the 1930s, is the institutional residue.

## Why the refusal matters

A specialty that only lives in the capital dies with the capital. Nadoleczny’s no made Munich a second lamp. Gottfried Arnold later counted him as a teacher. Students from Denmark, England, Finland, Sweden, Switzerland, Japan — the 1940 obituary lists the countries the way a proud department lists them — carried the *habit*, not the door.

He left the clinic in 1939 and died the next year in Zürich. The date sits at the edge of a Germany this pack will not pretty up. A 1940 obituary written in a Swiss-adjacent journal is allowed to mourn a teacher. A 2026 heritage essay is not allowed to pretend 1933–1940 was only a scientific season.

## Residue

A society. A department that lasted a generation. A reminder that “the Berlin school” had a Bavarian annex with its own chair and its own no. Portraits may exist in Munich archives; Commons rights were not cleared at pack date.

## 125 papers and a no

Duchan’s “about 125 publications” is a working number, not a collected works. The 1940 obituary is more interested in the countries the students came from. Both counts are department pride. The historical fact underneath is the 1910 room and the 1925 society. A man who refuses Berlin and still trains Japan is the opposite of a provincial footnote.

1939–1940 remains the edge this pack will not smooth. He died in the city of his birth after leaving the Munich clinic. The sentence can stay that plain.
