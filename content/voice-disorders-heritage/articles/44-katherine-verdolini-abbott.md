Katherine Verdolini Abbott tried to make a studio sensation into a literature.

She was living at the pack date (September 2026). Published papers and academic pages only. No family novel. No home address. No generated likeness. No resonant-voice homework.

Lessac-Madsen Resonant Voice Therapy is the named program American students meet when they want something other than Boone’s menu or Stemple’s physiologic set. The name carries two studio lineages — Arthur Lessac, Mark Madsen — into a clinic dialect. The papers, many of them from her Pittsburgh and later Delaware years, argue about ease, about a barely adducted posture, about whether a sensation can be an independent variable. This profile will not reprint the independent variable as a home program. Essay 16 is the historical place of named methods.

## Lessac, Madsen, a clinic dialect

Two studio names in a therapy acronym are a border crossing (essay 06) that finally got a methods section. Whether “barely adducted” survived as a claim is a paper-by-paper question. Heritage copy names the crossing. It does not settle the EMG.

Pittsburgh and Delaware are the workplaces later CVs list. They will move. The literature stays. Workshop handouts do not belong in this repo.

Motor learning, in her generation’s sentences, was a way to talk about practice without sounding like a choir handout. Whether the sentences improved outcomes is a methods question. That they entered voice therapy’s English is a heritage fact.
