# G. Paul Moore

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

G. Paul Moore made vibration a movie.

George Paul Moore was born in Everson, West Virginia, on 2 November 1907 and died in Gainesville on 17 January 2008 (Voice Foundation notice; Northwestern’s finding aid says January 2008). He took an A.B. from West Virginia University in 1929, then an M.A. (1930) and Ph.D. (1936) in speech correction at Northwestern. The dissertation title is the career: “A Stroboscopic Study of Vocal Fold Movement.” Some later video credits print “M.D.” He was not a physician. Print the Ph.D. and the error.

## Northwestern, then Florida

He stayed on the Northwestern faculty until the last day of 1961: lecturer in otolaryngology at the medical school, director of the Voice Clinic run jointly by Speech and Medicine, director (1957–1962) of the Gould Research Laboratory at the Institute of Laryngology and Voice Disorders. With Hans von Leden he made the 1956 films — *Function of the Normal Larynx*, *Physiology of the Larynx under Daily Stress*, and the pathologic companion. Essay 09 is the seeing. This profile is the speech scientist who built the camera side.

In 1962 he became professor and chair of speech at the University of Florida (chair to 1973; Communication Sciences Laboratory director 1962–1968; Distinguished Service Professor 1977; emeritus 1980; active into the 1990s). Students organized a G. Paul Moore Symposium in 1978. The Voice Foundation named a lecture for him in 1981; he gave the first. West Virginia University gave him an honorary D.Sc. in 1974.

## Residue

A reel a student can still watch. A lecture that still opens a June meeting. A jurisdictional fact: the person who taught America to *see* the fold vibrate did not hold a medical license. Portraits sit in university archives; ask before ingest.

## A joint clinic before the Foundation

Northwestern’s Voice Clinic, Speech plus Medicine, is the hallway Gould would later nationalize as a guest list. Moore was already in it in the 1950s, without an M.D., with a camera. Florida’s later chair years made him an administrator of a department that still runs a symposium in his name. The 1981 Foundation lecture is the East Coast badge. The Gainesville symposium is the working one.

Honorary D.Sc. from West Virginia in 1974 is an earned nostalgia: the state that sent him out took him back as a scientist. Print it. Do not make it a childhood novel.
