# Care of the Professional Voice

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

In 1969 Wilbur James Gould, a New York laryngologist, founded the Voice Foundation. In June 1972 the Foundation held its first Annual Symposium: Care of the Professional Voice, in New York. Those two dates are the American festival origin of a sentence European phoniatricians had been living for decades: the singer is a patient, and the patient is a colleague.

Gould’s gift was not a new operation. It was a guest list. Physicians, scientists, speech-language pathologists, teachers, and performers in one hotel, forced to hear one another’s vocabulary. NCVS’s hall-of-fame page, written in the voice of people who knew him, says he had a talent for making himself small and other people important. That is a political skill. It is also how a specialty that had been a hallway became a meeting.

## CoMeT, the same year as a badge

On 14 August 1969, during the ninth International Congress of Otorhinolaryngology in Mexico City, Hans von Leden and ten laryngologists founded the Collegium Medicorum Theatri. CoMeT is the international, somewhat exclusive cousin: care of the artistic voice, a membership you do not get by buying a ticket. The Foundation is the big tent. Both are 1969 ideas. Both assume that “professional voice” is a category medicine had been too casual about.

Van Lawrence in Houston, consulting at Juilliard, is the hygienist of this generation — hydration, the weekday voice of a student who has to sing tomorrow. He belongs in the room even without a full profile. The room is the point.

## Philadelphia after 1989

Gould died 7 February 1994 at Lenox Hill, of a heart attack, age seventy-four. He had already handed the Foundation’s chairmanship, in 1989, to Robert Thayer Sataloff and watched the office move to Philadelphia. Sataloff’s *Professional Voice: The Science and Art of Clinical Care* is the American shelf book of the next thirty years: thick, edited, a place where a chapter on hormones can sit next to a chapter on staging. He is a physician and a musician. The double degree is not a personality detail. It is the Foundation’s thesis in one CV.

This pack does not give Sataloff a numbered profile. He was living at the staging date, still publishing, still a jurisdictional fact. Essay-length is the honest form. A statue would be premature and a press release.

## Who counts as professional

The Foundation’s own later prose is careful: teachers, clergy, attorneys, dispatchers. The early symposiums were less careful. Famous singers make better galas. (The 1973 gala became, in time, Voices of Summer.) A history that only remembers Pavarotti’s doctor is doing the gala’s work. Gould did treat famous people; NCVS names some of them. He also, if the institutional story is true, wanted the un-famous ones in the definition.

A school SLP who treats a fifth-grade teacher’s Friday voice is doing professional-voice work. The Foundation did not invent that caseload. It gave the caseload a conference badge.

## Residue

A June meeting that still happens. A journal — *Journal of Voice* — that became the American house organ of the conversation. A habit, still uneven, of putting the singing teacher’s chair in the exam room. And a temptation this series will not indulge: turning heritage copy into a list of celebrity save stories.

## A journal as a hallway

*Journal of Voice* became, after the Foundation found its feet, the American place where a singing teacher’s vowel and a surgeon’s injection could share a table of contents. That is a weaker claim than “we solved staffing.” It is a stronger claim than a gala program. Gould’s memorialists also credit seed grants that later grew NIH voice money. Eulogies inflate figures. The direction of the claim is still useful: the Foundation wanted to be a purse, not only a party.

Sataloff’s later textbook thickness is the afterlife of that purse. A thick book is a jurisdictional act. It says: this much now *counts* as voice care. Heritage copy can admire the thickness and still refuse to turn the contents into a clinic manual.
