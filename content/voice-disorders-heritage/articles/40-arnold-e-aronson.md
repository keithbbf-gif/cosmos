# Arnold E. Aronson

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Arnold E. Aronson wrote the Mayo-flavored American textbook of voice and then sat, in the same building, on the papers that remade motor speech.

*Clinical Voice Disorders* is the book. It is stronger than Boone on the neurologic and “psychogenic” chapters, colder than Moses, and — after the toxin years (essay 12) — a text later students read with a pencil in the margin. The 1969 *JSHR* papers with Frederic L. Darley and Joe R. Brown on dysarthria patterns and clusters are the sibling-pack’s Mayo story. This profile keeps them in one sentence so Aronson is not only “the psychogenic man.” He was a Mayo voice-and-motor-speech clinician who believed a differential could be taught.

## How to read the voice book

As a document of a Midwestern medical center’s confidence: you can sort a voice. As a chapter heading later dystonia work had to argue with. As a reminder that “psychogenic” in a 1970s/80s table is a historical word, not a permission to send a breaking voice away without a scope.

He is also why a heritage series cannot pretend voice and motor speech are strangers. Darley’s dedication pages and the 1975 *Motor Speech Disorders* book sit on the same shelf.

## The same building as Darley

Mayo is the address. The 1969 dysarthria papers are why motor-speech courses still say his name when they think they are not in a voice unit. *Clinical Voice Disorders* is why voice courses still say it when they think they are not in a neurology unit. The building did not care about the split.
