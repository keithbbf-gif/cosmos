# Richard Luchsinger

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Richard Luchsinger wrote the book English-speaking clinicians mean when they say there was a European textbook before Boone.

He was a Swiss otorhinolaryngologist, born 1900, died 1993. This pack does not force a day-date without a Swiss necrology in hand. He specialized in the medical study of voice — phoniatrics — and, with Gottfried E. Arnold, published *Lehrbuch der Stimm- und Sprachheilkunde* (first German edition Vienna, 1949). The second-line English edition, *Voice-Speech-Language* (Wadsworth, Belmont, 1965), is the spine American libraries still hold. He also helped found *Folia Phoniatrica* in 1947 with Jean Tarneaud and Miroslav Seeman, and he founded the Swiss society for phoniatrics, logopedics, and audiology. Secondary tallies put the shorter papers above 120, from cri du chat to laryngeal surgery.

## A textbook that is a map

*Voice-Speech-Language* is not a paperback of hygiene. It is a single-volume treatise on voice, speech, and language — physiology and pathology under one roof, the European medical claim that Gutzmann had wanted in 1905, now in a language an Iowa student could be assigned. Arnold’s American career (profile 28) is why the English edition exists. Luchsinger’s Swiss address is why the German one did.

Read the 1965 book as a *map of what counted* in mid-century European voice medicine, not as current protocol. The surgery chapters age. The ambition does not: one volume, two men, three words in the title.

## Residue

A journal. A society. A bilingual textbook line. He stayed in the conversation until 1993, which means he lived to see Hirano’s layers, Gould’s Foundation, and the toxin years. The later papers are not this pack’s job. The 1949/1965 book is.

## Swiss address, English spine

A Swiss society and a Vienna-printed German textbook and a California English edition — the geography is the career. Luchsinger did not need to emigrate to be translated. Arnold did the Americanizing. The 1965 Wadsworth book is why a student in the United States could be assigned “European phoniatrics” without German.

Day-dates remain unforced. A later Swiss necrology should be added, not guessed. The 1947 journal and the 1949/1965 books are enough to keep him on the roster.
