# Janwillem van den Berg

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Janwillem van den Berg gave American training programs a hyphen.

He started mathematics and physics in Amsterdam, hid through part of the German occupation, and finished in Groningen. On 28 August 1948 he was appointed in the physiology department. In 1953 he defended, *cum laude*, *Physica van de stemvorming, met toepassingen*. The NCVS hall-of-fame page is the English warrant for those dates; it also says Bell Labs put the monograph into English almost at once. In 1954 he published, in French, against the neurochronaxic story. On 1 September 1958 *Journal of Speech and Hearing Research* printed “Myoelastic-Aerodynamic Theory of Voice Production” (1: 227–244). The Gould Award for 1960 went to him — $350 and a plaque — for laryngeal physiology.

## What the theory was for

Against the idea that a nerve fires each cycle. For a myoelastic valve in an airstream. Muscle sets posture and stiffness; air does the honoring. Later models complicate every noun. The 1958 paper still organizes a lab. Ron Scherer, on the same NCVS page, says James Curtis at Iowa handed him the paper and sent him to wax models. That is how a Dutch dissertation becomes an American bench.

This pack does **not** force birth and death days. Several secondary pages hint at dates; no citable obituary was in hand at staging. Print the appointment, the defense, the *JSHR* year, the award. Let a later editor add a necrology.

## Residue

A theory with a hyphen. A reminder that voice science’s English canon is sometimes a translation. Hirano would give the hyphen a histology. Titze would give it a textbook. van den Berg gave it a sentence you can still say in a classroom without blushing.

## Occupation, then a bench

The NCVS page’s “forced long hiding period” in the war is the biographical fact this pack will not decorate. He studied mathematics and physics, then voice as a physical impossibility in the then-standard explanations. The 1953 title already wanted applications. The 1958 English paper is the application American programs could assign.

Iowa’s James Curtis handing Scherer the paper is a transmission story, not a gossip one. That is how a hyphen crosses the Atlantic a second time — first Bell Labs, then a seminar.
