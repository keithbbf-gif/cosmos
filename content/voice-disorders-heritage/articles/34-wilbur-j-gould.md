# Wilbur James Gould

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Wilbur James Gould died at Lenox Hill Hospital on 7 February 1994, of a heart attack. He was seventy-four. The NCVS hall-of-fame page and the *Journal of Voice* memorial (“In memory of Wilbur James Gould, M.D.”) are the warrants. This pack does not force a birth date without a citable certificate; age-at-death plus the 1994 date is enough.

## A guest list as a method

In 1969 he founded the Voice Foundation in New York. In June 1972 the first Annual Symposium: Care of the Professional Voice met. In 1973 the first gala (later Voices of Summer). He directed the Foundation about a decade, then spent the 1980s handing it, carefully, to Robert T. Sataloff. The chairmanship moved in 1989; the office moved to Philadelphia. Essay 13 is the institution. This profile is the laryngologist who thought a hotel full of mixed badges was a scientific instrument.

NCVS, in a colleague’s voice, says he treated politicians and opera stars and “ordinary folks,” and names a few of the stars. A heritage page can admit the names without becoming a save-list. He sat on the NIDCD advisory story the memorialists tell. He put money into the Denver recording-and-research center that later carried his name under Ingo Titze. He was, they say, partly responsible for dozens of voice laboratories. Those counts are eulogies. Treat them as eulogies.

## The other life the memorials insist on

Thirty-eight years with Maureen MacTernan Gould; a daughter, Tamara Gould Leuchtenburg; a Lhasa Apso named Sadie; a collection of Admiral Nelson memorabilia. The *Journal* memorial prints them because Sataloff was writing a friend. This pack reprints the family names once, from that public memorial, and stops. No more.

## Residue

A June meeting that still happens. A journal culture. A definition of “professional voice” that later Foundation prose widened past the gala. Fiberoptic looking-through-the-nose is often attached to his name in ENT surveys; log the primary paper before promoting that sentence from “often said” to “he did.”

## Denver, NIDCD, a dog in a memorial

The Gould Voice Research Center under Titze is the laboratory afterlife. The NIDCD advisory story is the federal one. Both come from friends’ prose. Treat the direction as true and the dollar figures as eulogy. Fiberoptic nasal viewing “often attached to his name” stays hedged until a primary methods paper is logged.

Sadie the Lhasa Apso is already in the *Journal* memorial. One mention here is enough to show the memorial’s tone. A second would be a magazine profile of a pet.
