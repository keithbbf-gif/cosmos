# Ingo R. Titze

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Ingo R. Titze made the physics homework required.

He was living at the pack date (September 2026). This profile uses published books and institutional pages only. No family novel. No home address. No generated likeness.

*Principles of Voice Production* (Prentice Hall, 1994; later reprints) is the book students still fear. The National Center for Voice and Speech is the American address. The Denver recording-and-research center Gould funded later carried Gould’s name under Titze’s direction — the *Journal of Voice* memorial to Gould is the warrant for that handoff. Titze’s papers on self-oscillation, phonation threshold pressure, and the cover-body as a mechanical system are how van den Berg’s hyphen and Hirano’s sandwich became problem sets.

## What a living profile is allowed to say

That the NCVS hall of fame and the ordinary academic CVs exist and should be checked before a later editor adds a prize list. That Titze’s audience is mixed on purpose: engineers, SLPs, laryngologists, singing teachers who will tolerate an equation. That the “practical applications” van den Berg wanted in a 1953 title are, in this generation, often computational.

That is enough. A heritage series that writes a living scientist as a saint is doing public relations. A series that omits him because he is alive is doing cowardice. The book and the center are the residue. The rest can wait for a necrology this pack hopes is far off.

## Threshold pressure and a required book

Phonation threshold pressure is the Titze sentence clinicians can say without a chalkboard: the voice has a price of admission in air. The 1994 book is where the sentence gets equations. NCVS is where the equations got a center. Gould’s Denver money is the patronage line already in the Gould memorial.

A later editor may add a birth year from a citable CV. This pack will not scrape one from a keynote flyer. Living scientists get books, not childhoods.

Computational larynx models, the later papers, are the afterlife of a 1953 wish for applications. A student who only meets Titze as “the hard book” has still met the right object. The center and the book are the staged facts. Interviews and keynote charm are not.
