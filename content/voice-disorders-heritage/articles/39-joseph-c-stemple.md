# Joseph C. Stemple

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.

Joseph C. Stemple tried to make voice therapy a system you could exercise as a system.

He was living at the pack date (September 2026). Published books and academic pages only. No family novel. No home address. No generated likeness.

*Clinical Voice Pathology: Theory and Management* — editions with changing colleague lists — is the textbook line. Vocal Function Exercises are the named method American students meet after Boone’s menu. The papers argue for a physiologic program: a set of tasks aimed at the larynx as a system, not a grab-bag of facilitating tricks. This profile will not print the tasks. Essay 16 is the historical place of named programs. The life, as far as a living profile may go, is a University of Kentucky (and earlier Cincinnati) clinician-scholar who wanted a paper trail.

## What a living profile is allowed to say

That the book exists and has been assigned. That VFE is a literature, not a folk recipe, and also not a settled “winner” against resonant-voice or accent-method cousins. That Stemple’s generation is the one that had to answer the charge “you only have a menu.” Whether they answered it is a methods debate. Heritage copy names the charge.

A later editor may add a prize list from a citable CV. This pack will not invent one.

## Menu versus system

Boone’s 1971 book offered techniques. Stemple’s line offered a program that claimed a physiology. Whether the claim holds is a literature, not a heritage verdict. What the heritage can say is that American training, by the 1990s, wanted something that looked more like a protocol even when it was still a craft. VFE is one answer to that want. Resonant-voice work is another. Neither should be copied from this page.

Kentucky and Cincinnati appear on ordinary academic pages as the workplaces. Those pages move. The book editions move. Cite the edition on your desk.

Assigning Stemple after Boone often gives students a spine; assigning only Stemple often gives them a brand — a curriculum choice this pack does not make. The difference is syllabus design, not a verdict from this heritage series.
