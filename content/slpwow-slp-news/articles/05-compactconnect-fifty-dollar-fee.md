# CompactConnect charges a $50 Commission fee per privilege. State fees are extra.

WASHINGTON — Feb. 4, 2026 — The first number a clinician will see on an Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact privilege is fifty dollars. It is the Commission’s administrative fee, per privilege, recorded again in the February 2026 newsletter. It is not the remote state’s fee. It is not a jurisprudence-exam voucher. It is not a promise that the portal will take the payment if the home board has not onboarded.

CompactConnect went live on 28 October 2025. ASHA’s public desk named Louisiana and West Virginia as the first issuing states that Tuesday. The November 2025 Commission newsletter called the same hour the day those two members “operationalized.” Ohio began processing on 9 February 2026. Tennessee was posted as issuing on 28 May 2026. The apply URL the Commission publishes is `https://app.compactconnect.org/Dashboard`.

## What the $50 buys, and what it does not

The February 2026 letter — Volume 2, Issue 1, dated 4 February — said the Commission had adopted a $50.00 administrative fee per Privilege to Practice. Gregg Thornton’s Rules Committee note treated that figure as part of the operational framework, alongside a home-state FBI background-check amendment. ASHA’s public news page of 30 May 2025 had already told readers the Commission would charge $50 per compact privilege and that state fees may vary.

The August 2025 newsletter described the fee rule in more mechanical language. The Commission administrative fee is paid by the licensee to the Commission for each state in which a privilege is requested. Each member state may also set a state fee under its own process. That state fee is paid to the Commission as well; the Commission remits 100 percent of the state fee to the member state. Jurisprudence requirements, the home page says, live on the Commission’s fee information — not in this article.

This desk is not inventing those state amounts. Boards change them. Some members charge jurisprudence. Some do not. A clinician pricing two remote states should budget $50 times the number of privileges, then add whatever each remote board currently posts, then ask whether a test or background-check packet is still required at home.

The $50 does not purchase a login in a non-issuing member. ASHA’s 28 October 2025 sentence still governs: practitioners cannot register with CompactConnect until the home state onboards. A privilege is equivalent to a license only in a remote member that is actually issuing.

## The portal and the five-year wait

Isabel Eliassen, writing as CompactConnect project manager in the February letter, said the data system launched that fall with Louisiana and West Virginia as its first states after more than half a decade of work. Practitioners who live in and are licensed in one of those states could buy privileges in the other. The system, she wrote, was built with regular input from board staff, Commission staff, and licensees, with a goal that it be simple and quick. “We will continue to make improvements to it over time” is a product note, not a fee waiver.

The same February letter recorded a stretch goal: all member states onboard with the ability to issue. Stretch goals do not change the cart total. They also do not change the map. As of 14 September 2026 the Compact Map still shows four issuing members and 33 enacted but not issuing.

The home page’s cost line is short: state fees and jurisprudence requirements “can be found here.” Readers should follow that Commission link rather than a third-party blog table. This pack will not reprint a fee schedule the Commission can edit tomorrow.

## How the fee showed up before launch

The $50 figure was not a surprise invented in February. ASHA published it in May 2025, when CompactConnect was still anticipated for 30 September 2025 and Arizona had just become the 36th jurisdiction. The August 2025 Commission letter said the fee rule had been approved at a special Commission meeting in 2025 and that the Rules Committee had posted FAQs on how the administrative fee was determined. The February 2026 letter then treated the adopted $50 as current Commission rule.

None of those documents publish a state-by-state price list in a form this desk will copy. Member states set state fees through their own processes. The Commission’s job, under the August 2025 description, is to collect and remit the state portion, not to flatten every board’s charge into one national number.

## What SLPs should verify

Confirm the home state is issuing before creating a CompactConnect account. Confirm each remote state is issuing before paying for that privilege. Budget the $50 Commission fee per privilege plus the remote state’s current fee and any jurisprudence the remote board still requires. Read the Commission’s fee page the week you apply. Ask the home board whether an initial FBI or state criminal-records packet is still outstanding. If the work is in schools, ask the district which educator credential it will accept — the privilege does not buy that paper.
