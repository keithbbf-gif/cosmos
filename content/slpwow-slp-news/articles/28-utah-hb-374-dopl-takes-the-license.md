# Utah’s enrolled HB 374 moves the school SLP license to DOPL on May 6, 2026

SALT LAKE CITY — May 6, 2026 — Utah’s enrolled H.B. 374, Speech, Language, and Hearing Occupations Licensing Amendments, took effect May 6, 2026. The bill’s own highlight line is the one school clinicians need: it repeals the exemption that let a person receive speech-language pathologist or audiologist licensure from the State Board of Education. The Division of Professional Licensing is now the issuing body.

That is enrolled text, not a committee talking point. The official file is the enrolled PDF on le.utah.gov. DOPL’s public notice lives on the division’s audiology page at commerce.utah.gov/dopl/audiology/.

## What the enrolled bill does to the school exemption

Section 58-41-305, as amended, still lists people who may practice without a DOPL license in narrow circumstances. One of those leftover lines is a person who holds a valid, current State Board of Education credential and performs SLP or audiology functions solely inside the school that employs them. The same section then adds the cutoff: the State Board of Education may not issue a credential to a person performing those functions on or after May 6, 2026.

A later repeal-date clause schedules subsection 58-41-305(1)(d) — the old USBE-credential exemption — for repeal on July 1, 2031. The May 6 stop on new USBE credentials and the 2031 sunset on the leftover exemption are different clocks. Do not collapse them.

Licensure requirements in the renumbered section 58-41-301 are also blunt. Beginning May 6, 2026, an individual who holds a USBE credential issued on or after July 1, 2020, and before May 6, 2026, and who performs SLP or audiology functions, shall apply to the division for licensure and renew that credential under the division’s renewal section.

USBE is out of the issuing business for those areas. Existing paper does not vanish at midnight on the effective date.

The enrolled bill also repeals the statute that created a Speech-language Pathologist and Audiologist Licensing Board and lets the division stand up temporary working groups when no board is sitting. Those are housekeeping lines. They matter if you expected a named board to take your application. DOPL is the addressee.

For people who are not on the USBE transition path, section 58-41-301 still offers the ordinary routes: evidence of a current, unrestricted ASHA Certificate of Clinical Competence, or the degree-plus-experience-plus-examination path the division specifies. The division may issue a temporary speech-language pathology license, valid for up to 12 months, to an applicant who has finished the other licensure requirements and is in a clinical-fellowship year. That temporary card is not the USBE wind-down path. Do not mix the two packets.

## How DOPL says the transition works

DOPL’s posted notice is shorter than the statute and easier to miss. Speech-language pathologists and audiologists moving from a State Board of Education credential to a DOPL license should use the standard application. They must include a copy of the USBE credential. They are not required, on that path, to submit transcripts or exam scores.

The same notice says any existing State Board of Education credential stays in effect until the expiration date printed on the document. That sentence is the division’s operational rule for the transition cohort. It is not a new five-year USBE license.

This pack is not publishing a DOPL fee. The enrolled bill points applicants to the department’s fee statute. Check the application instructions the week you file.

## What USBE told itself in June

A June 2026 Utah State Board of Education committee memo on rule R277-301 stated the legislative result in staff language: H.B. 374 transferred authority to license audiologists and speech-language pathologists from USBE to DOPL. USBE licenses with those license areas, the memo said, will expire over the next five years. Staff asked the committee to update rule text that still pointed at those areas.

A companion memo on R277-306 said preparation-program language for those license areas should come out of the educator-prep rule because the programs were no longer necessary for USBE issuance.

Five years is USBE’s description of how the remaining license areas run off. It matches the idea of printed expiration dates. It is not a promise that every USBE card lasts until 2031.

## What this is not

It is not a teaching certificate for classroom fields. Educator definitions in the enrolled bill were rewritten so that an LEA-employed SLP or audiologist is an “educator” if the person holds a DOPL credential or, during the wind-down, a USBE credential. It is not an ASLP-IC privilege. Utah enacted the compact years ago; a privilege still does not replace whatever a district’s HR office wants to see on the first day of school.

It is not automatic. The statute says the 2020–2026 USBE cohort shall apply to the division. DOPL says use the standard form and attach the old credential.

## What SLPs should verify

Read the enrolled PDF for the May 6, 2026, effective date and the “may not issue” sentence. Read DOPL’s transition notice for the no-transcript, no-exam-score path. Look at the printed expiration on your USBE document. If you were issued that credential between July 1, 2020, and May 6, 2026, the statute says to file with the division. Ask your LEA which document it will accept for the 2026–27 assignment, and do not let a compact-privilege printout stand in for the DOPL license unless the district has said so in writing.
