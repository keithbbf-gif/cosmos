# Most SLPs are still outside mandatory MIPS. They still live with two conversion factors.

ROCKVILLE — Nov. 19, 2025 — The Quality Payment Program is easy to over-read if the only number a clinic hears is “MIPS.” ASHA’s public news on the 2026 Medicare fee schedule is shorter than the rumor: most audiologists and speech-language pathologists remain exempt from reporting under the Merit-based Incentive Payment System, though reporting stays available for people who meet the eligibility tests. The same week, CMS published two conversion factors — $33.57 for qualifying Advanced Alternative Payment Model participants and $33.40 for everyone else. Exemption from MIPS is not exemption from that fork.

## Why most SLPs never trip the wire

ASHA’s public 2026 SLP fee-schedule PDF, first edition November 13, 2025, states the reason in operational language. CMS has set exclusions and low-volume thresholds. A large majority of SLPs will be excluded from mandatory MIPS participation for 2026. MIPS applies to clinicians in outpatient non-facility settings. To be *required* to participate, a clinician must meet all three of the following, as ASHA lists them: $90,000 or more in allowed charges to Medicare for professional services; 200 or more distinct Medicare beneficiaries; and 200 or more distinct procedures.

Miss one and mandatory reporting does not attach. Meet one or two, ASHA writes, and the clinician may opt in. Meet none and voluntary reporting is still open for experience. Required participants who report nothing are looking at the maximum payment reduction — ASHA cites 9 percent — applied to 2028 payments for 2026 performance. The performance threshold to avoid a penalty, ASHA says, remains 75 points.

Those thresholds are ASHA’s public restatement of CMS’s eligibility math. They are not a desk-invented safe harbor. A multi-site outpatient group that shares a TIN can look “small” to the people in the treatment rooms and still look eligible to the Quality Payment Program. The QPP participation status lookup is the check. A blog sentence is not.

## Two conversion factors, limited APM access

Beginning in 2026 the fee schedule carries two conversion factors because MACRA’s update split: 0.75 percent for qualifying Advanced APM participants, 0.25 percent for all others, plus the one-time 2.5 percent congressional increase ASHA attributes to OBBBA. ASHA says most SLPs will use the $33.40 factor.

The APM track is the other half of the Quality Payment Program. ASHA’s public PDF says only a small percentage of SLPs participate, typically those in larger systems with finance shops that can run the models. SLPs in those organizations, ASHA writes, can help the entity earn incentives by joining quality-improvement work. That is not the same as an individual SLP electing the $33.57 factor from a home office. Qualifying Advanced APM status is an entity determination. Limited access is the profession-level fact ASHA is willing to put in a public PDF.

A clinician can be MIPS-exempt and still be paid on the non-APM conversion factor. Those are different switches.

## Two SDOH measures came out of the sets

ASHA’s November public news item reports that CMS removed two Social Determinants of Health measures from quality-reporting measure sets, and that each specialty still has a “robust” quality measure set for people who do report. The public PDF names the two SDOH measures CMS removed, over ASHA’s opposition, for the 2026 performance year — the year that feeds 2028 payment adjustments: Screening for Social Drivers of Health, and Connection to Community Service Provider.

ASHA also writes that, after those deletions, SLPs who are MIPS-eligible have 10 applicable quality measures and must report a minimum of six when six apply. This desk is not reprinting that ten-measure catalog. The association’s point is flexibility: six out of ten is a selection problem, not a two-measure problem. CMS’s Quality Payment Program site is where current measure specifications live. Measure inventories move.

The PDF adds that CMS also pulled those same two SDOH measures from the APM Performance Pathway Plus set used with Accountable Care Organizations in the Shared Savings Program, and that CMS has moved to remove SDOH measures across other payment systems it maintains, including home health, inpatient rehabilitation, and skilled nursing, for 2026 services. That is program-level quality architecture. It is not an SLP treatment guideline.

## What SLPs should verify

Run the current QPP participation-status tool for the rendering NPI and TIN before building a 2026 reporting calendar — or before ignoring one. If the result is mandatory, read this year’s measure list on qpp.cms.gov, not last year’s ASHA table. If the result is exempt, still confirm which conversion factor the claims actually price under. A qualifying Advanced APM clinician and a MIPS-exempt private-practice SLP are not in the same payment lane just because neither is filling out a MIPS dashboard. Locality and the MAC still finish the claim.
