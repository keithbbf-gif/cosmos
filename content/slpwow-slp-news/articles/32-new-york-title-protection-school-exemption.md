# New York protects the SLP title. Some school settings are still exempt.

ALBANY — Sept. 14, 2026 — New York does not treat “Speech-Language Pathologist” as a courtesy title. The Office of the Professions says any use of that title — or “Audiologist” — inside the state requires licensure, except in certain exempt settings. The exempt list on the current license-requirements page is short: federal, state, or local governments; public or nonpublic elementary or secondary schools; and colleges and universities.

The same page then draws a second line that school employers already know. To provide speech services in a public school in New York, OP says, teacher certification is the appropriate credential. The Office of Teaching Initiatives, not the Speech-Language Pathology and Audiology Unit, answers that phone. The number OP publishes is 518-474-3901.

An exemption from the title lock is not a private-practice license. It is not permission to use the title on a clinic door. It is OP’s description of settings where the Education Law’s title requirement does not apply in the same way.

## The license path when you do need the title

For licensure as a speech-language pathologist, OP lists the usual gates: good moral character, age 21 or older, education, examination, and experience. The education standard is a graduate degree from a New York-registered licensure-qualifying program, an ASHA-accredited program, or the equivalent as the Department defines it, including a practicum. The examination OP names for speech-language pathology is the Praxis Series Specialty Area test, Praxis II examination 5331, with a minimum acceptable score of 162. Scores must come directly from ETS to agency code R7747. People already licensed in another U.S. jurisdiction or another country should read OP’s endorsement Path A and Path B on the same page rather than assume the 36-week clock is the only door.

The experience requirement is the one that trips people who finished the degree but not the clock. You must complete a minimum of 36 weeks of supervised experience in speech-language pathology. The experience cannot begin until after all requirements for the graduate degree are satisfied, including any examinations or a thesis. OP notes that a break — maternity leave, or a school-year summer — should be made up in the ending date.

Full-time experience is at least 36 weeks of continuous employment at no less than 35 hours a week, the familiar September-to-June school year. Part-time experience has its own floor: continuous periods of not less than six months, at least two days a week, and not less than 12 hours a week with any one employer. Part-time applicants are told to contact the State Board for the total time required under those conditions. The supervised experience must be finished within a four-year period.

At least two-thirds of the experience should be direct clinical contact with people who have communication disorders. Academic teaching, research, and administrative time that does not deal with patient management do not count.

## Form 6 and the limited license, as OP describes them

You are exempt from licensure for professional practice completed as part of that experience requirement. If the experience is beginning, or has begun, the Board office may issue what OP calls Form 6 — an acknowledgement or supervisory plan — after it has an application for licensure, the fee, school documentation that the educational program is complete, and Identification of Supervisor and Setting (Form 4A). Experience outside New York is acceptable if the supervisor holds ASHA certification.

A limited license is optional. OP created it for settings that require direct billing to Medicare. Not every setting does. A limited license authorizes an applicant to practice under a licensed, currently registered SLP in order to finish the experience requirement. OP says you may apply for it with Form 1LL after, or with, the regular licensure application. The limited license is issued for one year and may be renewed in one-year periods until the experience is done. A change of supervisor or setting requires a reissued limited license; OP says a new fee is not required for that reissue.

This desk is not inventing additional form numbers. If OP’s page lists a different packet the week you apply, use that page.

## What SLPs should verify

Read the live OP license-requirements page before you accept a New York school or clinic offer. Confirm whether the setting is one of the listed exemptions and, if it is a public school, what Teaching Initiatives will require. If you need the title, confirm that your 36 weeks have not started before the degree was fully finished. If the employer bills Medicare, ask whether they want the limited license or the Form 6 path. Fees change; OP says the fee due is the one in law when the application arrives. Do not take a dollar figure from a blog.
