# Medicare’s 2026 conversion factor is two numbers. Most SLPs use the smaller one.

BALTIMORE — Nov. 19, 2025 — The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services split the Physician Fee Schedule conversion factor for calendar year 2026. One number applies to clinicians in a qualifying Advanced Alternative Payment Model. The other applies to everyone else. ASHA’s public news desk and its public speech-language pathology fee-schedule PDF both say the same thing about the second lane: that is where most audiologists and speech-language pathologists sit.

The two published factors are $33.57 for qualifying Advanced APM participants and $33.40 for all other clinicians paid under the fee schedule. Those are CMS’s multipliers, not ASHA’s. They took effect for dates of service on and after January 1, 2026. They are also not a clinic’s deposit slip.

A conversion factor is the last number in a public formula. Each CPT code carries relative value units for work, practice expense, and malpractice. Those RVUs are adjusted by geographic practice cost indices. The locality-adjusted total is multiplied by the conversion factor. Change the factor and every code on the schedule moves, even if the code’s own RVUs sit still.

What the factor is not: a raise, a cut, or a promise that a given 92507 will pay last year’s amount plus 3 percent. ASHA’s public PDF is explicit that the two 2026 factors already include the statutory updates Congress wrote into the year — and that they do not include every reduction that then comes off the top. Locality still applies. The Medicare Administrative Contractor still adjudicates the claim. A national average in a trade PDF is a starting point, not a remittance.

## Why there are two tracks this year

Congress, not CMS, built the fork. The Medicare Access and CHIP Reauthorization Act of 2015 froze the annual fee-schedule update at zero from 2020 through 2025. Beginning in 2026, MACRA’s update splits by track: 0.75 percent for clinicians in a qualifying Advanced APM, and 0.25 percent for all others.

ASHA’s November 13, 2025, public PDF names a second, one-time piece: a 2.5 percent congressional increase for 2026 from the statute ASHA abbreviates as OBBBA — the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. ASHA’s November 13 and November 19 public notes say the published conversion factors already reflect the MACRA split plus that one-time 2.5 percent. Relative to the 2025 factor, ASHA calculates the APM track as a 3.8 percent increase and the non-APM track as a 3.3 percent increase. That is the CF column. It is not the year.

Most SLPs do not participate in a qualifying Advanced APM. ASHA says so in the public news item and again in the PDF. The $33.40 factor is the one a typical outpatient SLP should assume until the enrollment and the APM entity say otherwise. Qualifying APM status is not a membership card an individual clinician can print from a blog.

## The stack ASHA says can still approach 4 percent

On November 13 and in a November 19 update, ASHA’s public news desk wrote that SLPs “could face cuts approaching 4 percent” without more legislation, and that audiologists may see reductions of about 3 percent. The public PDF repeats the SLP figure as “potential overall cuts of approximately 4 percent without additional legislative intervention.”

That is an association estimate, not a CMS national average for every code. ASHA builds it from more than one layer. CMS’s regulatory impact analysis, as ASHA summarizes it, estimates that most individual SLPs will see about a 1 to 2 percent negative shift on top of the conversion-factor math. ASHA then adds other Medicare reductions that still need Congress if they are going to be stopped: a 2 percent sequestration cut, and geographic adjustments that vary by locality. A November 2025 continuing resolution, ASHA reported, restored the 1.0 work GPCI floor only through January 30, 2026, and eliminated the 4 percent statutory PAYGO cut through 2026. The floor is a locality story. Clinics that never lived on the 1.0 floor do not lose the same dollars as clinics that did.

ASHA also flags CMS’s new 2.5 percent “efficiency adjustment” on selected non-time-based codes and the three-year malpractice and GPCI update that lands in 2026. Those are separate policies. They do not all hit every SLP code, and they do not all hit every ZIP code. They are why a conversion factor that is up on paper can still produce a year that is down in the bank.

## Two codes ASHA used as a warning, not a table

ASHA’s public PDF narrative — not a rate table this desk will reprint — says the national payment rate for 92507, individual treatment of speech, language, voice, communication, and/or auditory processing disorder, is up about 2 percent, while 96112, developmental test administration, is down 1 percent. That is the association’s own example of why a “the fee schedule went up” sentence is the wrong sentence.

A practice whose Medicare book is mostly 92507 will not experience 2026 the same way as a practice whose book is heavy on 96112. A practice in a locality that loses the work GPCI floor will not experience 2026 the same way as a practice that never had it. ASHA says so: estimated impacts are averages based on cumulative therapy spending. Actual payment depends on locality-specific rates and the CPT codes billed.

## What the November 2025 analysis no longer settles

ASHA’s first-edition PDF and the November news item still describe a short telehealth-and-GPCI continuing-resolution window through January 30, 2026. That practitioner-authority clock was later extended by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, through December 31, 2027. The conversion factors did not move because of that extension. Payment-year math and telehealth practitioner eligibility are different instruments. Mixing them is how a clinic files the wrong year.

Congress can still write another conversion-factor patch. It can let the GPCI floor expire. It can leave sequestration in place. ASHA’s November text is advocacy as well as analysis: the association said it would keep asking for a further positive adjustment and for longer-term reform. Whether those bills pass is not a fact this desk can invent.

## What SLPs should verify

Confirm which conversion factor the claim actually prices under — qualifying Advanced APM or the $33.40 track — with the APM entity and the MAC, not with a national average pulled from a PDF. Pull the current locality file for the rendering ZIP code. Recalculate the codes the practice actually bills; do not generalize from 92507’s national plus-2 percent or 96112’s national minus-1 percent. Ask the MAC how it is applying the efficiency adjustment, MPPR, and any GPCI change after January 30, 2026. Treat ASHA’s public tables as a national teaching tool, not as the remittance.
