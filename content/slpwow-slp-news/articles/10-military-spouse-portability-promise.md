# Military-spouse portability is on the Commission’s benefits list. Four states are issuing.

WASHINGTON — Sept. 14, 2026 — The Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact Commission lists “improves portability for military spouses” among the public benefits on its home page, in the same stack as continuity of care, access, choice, telehealth facilitation, and a simpler process. The operational fact on that same page is narrower. Registration is open for licensed practitioners in Louisiana, Ohio, Tennessee, and West Virginia.

A benefit statement is not a privilege. A spouse who holds a qualifying home license in one of those four issuing states can use CompactConnect the same way any other qualifying licensee can: buy a privilege in another issuing member. A spouse whose home board is among the 33 enacted-but-not-issuing members cannot register until that board onboards. ASHA’s 28 October 2025 public note said so on launch day. The Commission has not published a spouse-only bypass.

## What the Commission has actually put in writing

The home-page benefit line is the public promise this desk can cite. It does not describe orders, installations, or a federal reimbursement. This article will not invent Department of Defense spouse-licensure statutes, assignment incentives, or compact-fee waivers that do not appear in the Commission materials we fetched.

The February 2026 newsletter recorded a more bureaucratic clue. Among the Rules Committee’s 2026 work items was a proposed policy on “military identification and submission verification/auditing process.” That is a proposed policy, listed with other unfinished 2026 business. It is not a published spouse handbook, and this desk is not guessing what documents that policy will ask for.

The August 2025 newsletter named the Department of Defense among the organizations supporting the CompactConnect *data-system* project, alongside associations and certification boards that also fund the shared platform. Project support for software is not a spouse practice act. It does not make a non-issuing member issue.

Isabel Eliassen’s February CompactConnect note said practitioners who live in and are licensed in a first-wave state could buy privileges in the other first-wave state. She did not write a separate military-spouse lane. The $50 Commission administrative fee per privilege, recorded in the same letter, is the fee the letter names. It does not, in that text, zero out for a dependent ID.

## The map is the limit of the promise

Fetched on 14 September 2026, the Compact Map still reads: 4 issuing, 33 enacted but not issuing, 4 with active legislation, 14 with none. Tennessee joined the issuing column on 28 May 2026. Ohio began processing on 9 February 2026. Louisiana and West Virginia opened on 28 October 2025.

A permanent-change-of-station that lands a family in an issuing member helps only if the clinician’s home license is also in an issuing member — or if the new state becomes the home license the ordinary way. The compact’s own location rule still applies after the household goods are unpacked: practice occurs in the state where the patient or client is located. A privilege into the new duty-station state covers practice *there* when that state is issuing. It does not cover clients who sit in a neighboring enacted-but-dark member, and it does not replace a school educator certificate if the new job is a district position.

Pending bills do not close the gap. The February letter named carryovers in Michigan and Pennsylvania and reintroductions in New Jersey and New Mexico. Those files, if they ever enact, still have to onboard. Enactment is not issuance. A spouse should not date a first day of work to a hearing notice.

## What this is not

It is not a national license for military families. It is not proof that every compact member will treat a spouse application faster than a civilian one. It is not a substitute for asking the home board what fingerprint packet it still wants. It is not Medicare enrollment, and it is not a district assignment.

Employers who recruit on installations will keep using the benefit sentence. The issuing column is the correction: four states, named, on the Commission home page.

## What SLPs should verify

Confirm the home-state license is in an issuing member before promising a start date tied to CompactConnect. Confirm the duty-station or remote-practice state is issuing, not merely enacted. Read the current Commission rules and the home board’s instructions for any identification the board actually collects; do not treat a 2026 “proposed policy” line as a finished form. Budget the $50 Commission fee per privilege plus whatever state fee the remote board posts. If the new job is in a school, ask the district which educator credential it requires after the privilege question is answered. Recheck the Compact Map the week the household moves.

This article does not offer clinical advice, billing guarantees, or diagnosis guidance.
