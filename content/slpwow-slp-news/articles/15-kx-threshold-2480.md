# Medicare’s 2026 KX threshold for PT plus SLP is $2,480

BALTIMORE — Feb. 26, 2026 — The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services published the calendar-year 2026 KX modifier threshold for outpatient therapy: $2,480 for physical therapy and speech-language pathology combined, and a separate $2,480 for occupational therapy. The targeted medical-review threshold stays $3,000.

Those figures are on the CMS Therapy Services page (updated against CR 14252) and in MM14315, the agency’s CY 2026 Physician Fee Schedule summary. They are not ASHA-only numbers.

## What the number is

The Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018 repealed the old therapy caps and kept the dollar amounts as thresholds. Above the KX line, the claim must carry the KX modifier as the supplier’s attestation that the services are medically necessary and that the record can show it. Claims over the threshold without KX are denied.

PT and SLP still share one bucket. A busy orthopedic PT in the same episode can spend most of the $2,480 before the SLP bills a single 92507. OT has its own $2,480. The targeted review number — $3,000 for PT/SLP and $3,000 for OT — is not an automatic audit of every claim. CMS has said since MACRA that review is targeted. The $3,000 line is scheduled to hold through 2028, then index to the Medicare Economic Index.

## Shared bucket, two professions

The combined PT/SLP line is the one that surprises speech-language pathologists who bill in a building that also has a busy physical-therapy caseload. CMS did not create a separate SLP threshold. The agency indexed the old combined cap. ASHA’s November 2025 public fee-schedule news repeated the same $2,480 and $3,000 figures. If the PT has already used most of the bucket, the SLP’s medically necessary visits still need the modifier once the combined incurred amount crosses the line.

Limitation-of-liability rules that first applied in 2013 still apply when a claim is denied for a missing KX, according to the Therapy Services page. CMS points readers to its August 2018 ABN FAQ for the beneficiary-notice questions. This desk is not restating those FAQs as a clinic script.

## What the number is not

It is not a clinical stop. It is not permission to keep treating without a record that would survive review. It is not a promise that a MAC will agree the minutes were reasonable. Local coverage and the plan of care still govern. It is not a school IEP minute cap, and it is not a Medicaid number.

This article does not offer clinical advice, billing guarantees, or diagnosis guidance.
