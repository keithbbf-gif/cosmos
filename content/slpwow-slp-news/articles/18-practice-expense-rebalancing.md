# CMS is raising non-facility practice expense and cutting facility PE. SLPs are not automatically ‘up.’

BALTIMORE — Jan. 1, 2026 — The 2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule reweights the practice-expense column. Non-facility PE values go up. Facility PE values go down. ASHA’s public November 2025 news item said the shift “should benefit most audiologists and SLPs, since most services are paid at non-facility rates.” The public SLP fee-schedule PDF is more specific: speech-language pathology services are, by law, paid at the non-facility rate regardless of setting, so ASHA describes the PE change as an overall positive for SLP payments under the fee schedule.

“Overall positive” is not a guarantee that a given clinic’s 2026 remittance is larger than 2025. Practice expense is one of three RVU legs. The conversion factor, the efficiency adjustment, malpractice RVUs, geographic indices, sequestration, and MPPR all still sit on the same claim.

## What CMS is changing

For years the fee schedule has carried two PE values for many codes: a non-facility amount, meant to reflect the overhead of an office that pays its own rent and staff, and a facility amount, meant to reflect a setting where the hospital or skilled nursing facility already owns the overhead. Beginning with calendar year 2026, CMS is widening that gap — increasing non-facility PE and reducing facility PE across codes paid under the fee schedule.

ASHA’s public PDF restates the agency’s rationale: the revised approach is meant to reflect higher costs in non-facility settings and the economies of scale in facilities. As part of the change, CMS is reducing the portion of facility PE RVUs allocated from work RVUs to half the amount applied to non-facility PE RVUs. Specialties that deliver most of their services in facility settings, ASHA writes, will see PE RVUs fall. Specialties that practice primarily in non-facility settings will see PE RVUs rise.

MM14315, CMS’s own CY 2026 fee-schedule summary, discusses the indirect-cost allocation methodology in the same spirit: recognize the relative resources in facility versus non-facility settings and correct distortions in how indirect PE has been allocated. The transmittal is a contractor instruction, not an SLP newsletter. It does not print a profession-level “SLPs are up 2 percent on PE” line.

## Why SLP payment uses the non-facility column

Outpatient speech-language pathology paid under Part B uses the Physician Fee Schedule. ASHA’s public PDF states that those SLP services are paid at the non-facility rate by law, regardless of setting. That statutory treatment is why ASHA can call the 2026 PE rebalancing an overall positive for SLPs even when the clinician is standing in a facility. The claim still prices off the non-facility PE column.

That is a payment-formula fact. It is not an argument that facility overhead disappeared, and it is not a statement about hospital outpatient prospective payment for services that do not ride the fee schedule. Most hospital outpatient *speech-language pathology* services are paid under the MPFS, as SLP WOW noted in its 2025 fee-schedule piece; audiology in a hospital outpatient department can be a different payment system. This story is the SLP PE column.

## What “positive” does not cancel

ASHA’s same public PDF is the document that tells members to prepare for a year that can still approach a 4 percent cut without more legislation. The conversion factor’s 2.5 percent one-time congressional bump and the small MACRA update live in one column. CMS’s regulatory impact analysis, as ASHA summarizes it, still points to a 1 to 2 percent negative shift for most individual SLPs on top of that math. Sequestration, GPCI movement after the 1.0 work floor’s January 30, 2026, CR date, the 2.5 percent efficiency adjustment on selected codes, and MPPR are other columns.

A PE increase that is real on the RVU file can be smaller than those reductions on a particular day’s claim. It can also be larger. The only honest national sentence is ASHA’s: the PE methodology change is an overall positive for SLP payments *and* actual payment still depends on locality and the codes billed.

CMS is also using some Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment System data to inform certain technical-service rates, including some remote monitoring services. That is a 2026 rate-setting method, not a second PE schedule for 92507. Do not import OPPS relatives into an SLP treatment claim unless the MAC says the code prices that way.

## What SLPs should verify

Look at the 2026 PE RVUs for the codes the practice actually bills, not at a profession-wide adjective. Confirm the claim is pricing at the non-facility PE rate ASHA describes as required by law — and ask the MAC if a line is not. Recalculate with the $33.40 or $33.57 conversion factor that applies to the clinician, the current GPCI for the locality, and any efficiency-adjustment or MPPR flag. Treat “PE rebalancing helps SLPs” as a directional statement from ASHA’s public analysis, not as a budget line.
