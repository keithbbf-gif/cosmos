# AAC access does not travel automatically when the SLP is on a screen

EASTPOINTE — Sept. 14, 2026 — A speech-language pathologist can be present on a laptop and still be absent from the parts of AAC that happen in the hallway, the cafeteria, and the moment a device is in the backpack instead of on the tray. That is not a research slogan. It is the practical gap sitting under three public records: Michigan’s published turn to virtual school SLPs, an open-access AAC research agenda on societal barriers, and Medicare’s telehealth list, which already includes the speech-generating-device evaluation and treatment codes.

## The vacancy is real. The access problem is older.

Bridge Michigan reported in February 2026 that Optimise listed 253 school SLP openings in the state. Eastpointe Community Schools was serving 149 students who needed speech services, 37 of them in preschool, with four virtual SLPs and two on-site. Superintendent Christina Gibson told the reporter, “This is not an ideal situation,” and “I think the best speech services are delivered face-to-face.” The Michigan Department of Education said it does not know how many special-education positions are filled by virtual contractors. Families, Bridge noted, are not always in a position to treat “someone on a screen plus a paraprofessional” as equivalent to an in-building clinician who can walk into a classroom.

ASHA’s 2024 Schools Survey is the national backdrop, not a Michigan census: 79 percent of school SLPs said openings outnumbered seekers. Paperwork and workload still led the challenge list. Virtual coverage is one district response to that labor math. It is not proof that AAC implementation got easier.

## What the Light papers add without becoming a how-to

Janice Light and colleagues, in *AAC* 41(3), argue that access to AAC is decided as much by policy, practice, attitude, and partner skill as by hardware. Their societal-barriers paper (DOI 10.1080/07434618.2025.2508490) is a narrative agenda, not a trial: people who need AAC are often judged “too something” to receive a system, then given few real turns. Their literacy companion (DOI 10.1080/07434618.2025.2502032) adds a second access problem: instruction that requires speech, providers who were never trained to teach print without it, and picture systems that never become text.

A remote SLP can still model, still coach a partner, still write an evaluation. The papers’ point is that the barriers that already existed in the building — low expectations, no one to program vocabulary, no literacy block that accepts a point or a scan — do not dissolve because the clinician’s face is in a window. Partner training, the societal paper notes, is a small share of the AAC research corpus. A para escorting a child to a laptop is not automatically a trained communication partner. Light and colleagues do not study Michigan. The juxtaposition is editorial, and it is labeled as such.

## Medicare’s list is a billing fact, not a school fact

ASHA’s public Medicare telehealth page, updated 3 February 2026, states that CAA 2026 extends audiologist and SLP authority to furnish listed telehealth services through 31 December 2027, and that services on the list since 2020 are permanently on the telehealth services list as of 1 January 2026. The SLP code table on that page includes 92607 and 92608 (evaluation for a speech-generating AAC device, first hour and each additional 30 minutes) and 92609 (therapeutic services for use of a speech-generating device, including programming and modification). Those codes have been on the list since 1 January 2021.

That is fee-for-service Medicare, traditional Part B. Medicare Advantage and Medicaid vary. School employment is not Medicare enrollment. A privilege under the audiology and speech-language pathology compact is not a CMS number. HIPAA enforcement, ASHA notes, resumed after the public-health emergency; consumer video apps that were tolerated then are not a compliance plan now. Listing 92607 on a telehealth page does not mean a given MAC, a given student, or a given device maker will treat a remote SGD evaluation as equivalent to a face-to-face one. It means the code is on the list for the clinicians and settings that already bill those codes.

## What this is not

It is not a recommendation of any speech-generating device, app, or virtual-staffing vendor. It is not a finding that remote AAC services are inferior or superior in a named student. It is not a billing guarantee that 92607, 92608, or 92609 will pay from a home office. It is not a claim that Michigan’s 253 openings are all AAC vacancies. Gibson’s quotes are hers, on the record in Bridge; this desk did not interview her.

## What SLPs should verify

If a district assigns AAC users to a virtual-only caseload, ask who handles device setup, classroom coaching, and literacy adaptation on site. If a medical practice bills 92607–92609 via telehealth, confirm the current ASHA/CMS list, the plan of care rules, and the MAC’s modifier and place-of-service instructions. If someone cites Light as a reason to delay AAC until an in-person SLP is hired, that is the opposite of the papers’ access argument. Read the DOI and the telehealth page. Do not take a recruiter’s “we cover AAC virtually” as a complete service model.

This article does not offer clinical advice, billing guarantees, or diagnosis guidance.
