# The compact and Medicare both care where the client is. They do not share a form.

WASHINGTON — Sept. 14, 2026 — Two public rule sets now tell speech-language pathologists to write down where the other person is sitting. They are not the same rule set. The Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact treats the encounter as practice in the state where the patient or client is located. Medicare fee-for-service telehealth treats the same geography as a billing and eligibility problem: who may furnish the service, which modifier or place-of-service code the claim carries, and whether HIPAA-grade technology is in use. Mixing the two in one note template is how people invent a policy that neither body wrote.

## Compact geography is a license fact

The Commission’s public explainer is short. A compact privilege is equivalent to a license in a remote member state that is actually issuing. Practice occurs where the client is at the encounter. A clinician in an issuing home state who sees a child sitting in another issuing state needs the privilege (and whatever jurisprudence or fee that remote board still charges). A clinician who sees a child in a member state that has enacted the compact but has not onboarded CompactConnect does not get a privilege from the map’s color. As of the Commission’s home page fetched for this pack, four states were issuing: Louisiana, West Virginia, Ohio, and Tennessee. Enactment is 37 jurisdictions. Issuing is four.

School credentials and district contracts sit on top of that. A privilege is not an educator certificate. This desk is not walking through a fictional student’s move. The operational point is documentary: the record should show the client’s location because that is the jurisdiction.

## Medicare telehealth is a different clock

ASHA’s public Medicare telehealth page, updated 3 February 2026, is the clinician-facing summary. The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, extends audiologist and SLP authority to furnish listed telehealth services through 31 December 2027. Services that have been on the list since 2020 are permanently on the telehealth services list as of 1 January 2026. The page is explicit: traditional fee-for-service only. Medicare Advantage and Medicaid make their own rules. CMS’s 26 February 2026 telehealth FAQ adds the other edge: starting 1 January 2028, unless Congress acts again, PTs, OTs, SLPs, and audiologists drop off as telehealth furnishers, and the older rural-and-facility originating-site limits return for most services.

HIPAA enforcement, ASHA notes, resumed after the federal public-health emergency. The consumer-grade workarounds of 2020 are not the current compliance story.

Place of service and modifiers are where people want a single sentence and cannot have one. CMS’s MLN901705 (December 2025) says, for institutional billing, to use modifier 95 for outpatient therapy provided via telehealth by qualified physical therapists, occupational therapists, or speech-language pathologists employed by hospitals. For professional billing it describes POS 02 (telehealth, patient not at home) and POS 10 (telehealth, patient at home) as of 1 January 2024.

ASHA’s same public page tells SLPs something more specific and easy to mash together with that MLN sentence: therapists, including SLPs, should continue to report the POS that reflects where the in-person service would have been provided, append modifier 95, and should not use POS 02 because it pays at the facility rate. ASHA’s example is an SLP who would have seen the patient in private practice (POS 11) plus modifier 95, plus GN. ASHA also notes that CMS told hospitals to use modifier 95 with a hospital POS for outpatient telehealth, aligning institutional policy.

Those instructions can both be true and still not collapse into “always bill POS 10” or “always bill POS 02.” Setting, claim form, and MAC edits differ. This article will not invent a universal POS for schools, SNFs, CORFs, and private practice. The MLN originating-site fee for Q3014 in 2026 is 80 percent of the lesser of the charge or $31.85. That is an originating-site facility fact, not an SLP home-office rate.

## What this is not

It is not a claim form. It is not permission to treat an issuing-state privilege as Medicare enrollment. It is not a finding that every telehealth visit is covered through 2027 in every plan. It does not use a made-up student name, address, or IEP. It is not a HIPAA walkthrough.

## What SLPs should verify

Write the client’s location in the record because the compact and the school or medical license may turn on it. Then open a second checklist for the payer. For Medicare FFS, read the current ASHA telehealth page and MLN901705, and ask the MAC how it wants POS and modifiers on your claim type — hospital outpatient, private practice, SNF Part B, or something else. Confirm the 31 December 2027 practitioner-eligibility date has not moved. Confirm the platform meets current HIPAA expectations. If the work is a school contract, ask the district which license and which educator credential it wants. Do not copy another setting’s POS example onto your UB-04 or 1500.
