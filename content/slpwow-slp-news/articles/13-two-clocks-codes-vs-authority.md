# Medicare telehealth in 2026 runs on two clocks: the codes and the people

BALTIMORE — Feb. 26, 2026 — Speech-language pathologists keep asking a single question — “Is Medicare telehealth covered in 2026?” — and getting a single-word answer that is wrong either way. Coverage is not one switch. It is two clocks. Clock 1 is the list of services. Clock 2 is the list of people. CMS owns the first. Congress owns the second. They do not expire on the same day, and they never have.

ASHA’s public Medicare telehealth page, updated February 3, 2026, states the split in one paragraph: all audiology and speech-language pathology services that have been covered under telehealth since 2020 are permanently covered as authorized telehealth services starting January 1, 2026, and Congress extended audiologists’ and SLPs’ authority to provide those services through December 31, 2027. CMS’s Therapy Services page cites section 6209 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, for the people-clock, including telephone assessment and management codes 98966 through 98968, through the same December 31, 2027, date. CMS’s telehealth FAQ, updated February 26, 2026, is blunter about what happens the morning after.

## Clock 1: the codes stay on the list

Medicare’s telehealth services list is a catalog of CPT and HCPCS codes the agency has decided can substitute for an in-person visit when they are furnished as telehealth. CMS, not Congress, maintains that list. In the calendar-year 2026 Physician Fee Schedule final rule, the agency also dropped the old provisional-versus-permanent distinction. New additions are permanent additions.

For speech-language pathology, the 2026 news is not that 92507 suddenly appeared. The news is that the SLP services that have sat on the list since the 2020–2021 pandemic expansions are no longer there on a temporary, year-by-year hook. ASHA’s November 2025 public news item called that an advocacy win: CMS finalized permanent inclusion of select audiology and speech-language pathology codes. ASHA’s February 2026 public page repeats it as operational fact: those services are permanently on the list as of January 1, 2026.

A code on the list is not a claim. It is permission for the *service* to be a Medicare telehealth service when a person who is allowed to furnish Medicare telehealth actually furnishes it. Leave the person off the statute and the code can sit on the list forever while the SLP still cannot bill it as telehealth.

That is not a word game. ASHA’s public page says so in the other direction: if Congress later fails to extend practitioner authority, Medicare could continue to cover the same CPT codes when a physician or a statutory “practitioner” — ASHA’s examples are a nurse practitioner or physician assistant — furnishes them. The service remains a listed telehealth service. The SLP is no longer a person Medicare will pay to deliver it that way.

## Clock 2: the people are on a statute, not a fee-schedule footnote

Section 1834(m) of the Social Security Act is the people-clock. ASHA’s public explainer is plain about the standing law: the Act defines Medicare telehealth as a service provided by a physician or practitioner. It classifies audiologists and SLPs as suppliers. Standing Medicare law does not put those suppliers on the distant-site roster. Temporary statutes have been adding them, then expiring, since the public-health emergency.

The current temporary statute is the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 — the Senate amendments to H.R. 7148, in ASHA’s February 3, 2026, write-up. ASHA says the House passed those amendments and the President signed them. The extension of Medicare telehealth authority for audiologists and SLPs runs through December 31, 2027. CMS’s Therapy Services page implements the same section 6209 date for PTs, OTs, and SLPs.

CMS’s February 26 FAQ, question 2, is the agency voice on the far side of that date: through December 31, 2027, “an extended range of practitioners may bill for Medicare telehealth services.” Starting January 1, 2028, “physical therapists, occupational therapists, speech-language pathologists, and audiologists can no longer furnish Medicare Telehealth services.”

That sentence is about people, not about 92507 falling off the list. The FAQ does not say the treatment code disappears. It says those four therapy professions drop off the furnishing roster unless the law changes again.

## The two clocks have already disagreed once this winter

ASHA’s November 2025 public fee-schedule news still described a continuing resolution that carried telehealth authority only through January 30, 2026. The first edition of ASHA’s public 2026 SLP PDF said the same. That was accurate for November. It is not the clock in force after CAA 2026. Anyone still treating the November PDF’s January 30 date as the last day of SLP telehealth authority is reading a superseded people-clock.

The codes-clock did not move in February. CMS had already finalized the permanent-list decision in the 2026 rule. Congress moved the people-clock. That is the 2026 story in one line: the list is no longer the emergency. The roster is.

## Geography is a third hand on the same watch

The FAQ’s first question is not about SLPs. It is about beneficiaries. Through December 31, 2027, Medicare beneficiaries can receive Medicare telehealth services anywhere in the United States and territories. Starting January 1, 2028, except for behavioral health services, they will generally need to be in a medical facility and in a rural area. That is the pre-pandemic geographic-and-site rule returning for most services.

For an SLP, the 2028 geography rule is almost academic if the people-clock also lapses. A rural skilled-nursing originating site does not help if the distant-site clinician is no longer a telehealth practitioner. The two expirations are written to the same morning. They are still two rules.

## What this page does not cover

This is traditional fee-for-service Medicare. ASHA’s public page says so at the top: state Medicaid programs and private insurers, including Medicare Advantage, may cover audiologist and SLP telehealth, and the policies vary. A Medicare Advantage plan that paid 92507 with modifier 95 in 2025 has not been rewritten by section 6209. The plan’s evidence of coverage has.

CMS’s FAQ also aligns hospital outpatient therapy furnished remotely to a beneficiary at home with the same December 31, 2027, window. Starting January 1, 2028, hospitals may no longer bill for those remote therapy services under that alignment. Institutional billing is not a workaround that outlives the statute. HIPAA enforcement, loosened for some platforms during the public-health emergency, has resumed; ASHA states that on the same public page.

## What SLPs should verify

Read the current CMS telehealth list for the *code*. Read CAA 2026 section 6209 — or CMS’s Therapy Services paraphrase of it — for the *person*, and do not stop at a 2025 PDF that still says January 30, 2026. Confirm the encounter is traditional fee-for-service before assuming the federal clocks apply. Ask the MAC how it wants POS, modifier 95, and the GN plan-of-care modifier on an SLP telehealth claim; ASHA’s public page and CMS’s MLN booklet do not replace a contractor instruction. If the work is Medicare Advantage or Medicaid, ask that payer. If the date of service is in 2028, do not assume either clock still favors an SLP.
