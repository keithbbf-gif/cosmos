# 92507 is exempt from Medicare’s 2026 efficiency adjustment. That is not a raise.

BALTIMORE — Nov. 19, 2025 — The 2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule includes a 2.5 percent “efficiency adjustment” on selected non-time-based diagnostic and procedural codes. ASHA’s public news item on the final rule is specific about two codes that do not take that cut: 92507 and 92557. The reason ASHA gives is not that they are speech or audiology codes. The reason is that they are on the Medicare telehealth services list.

CMS’s own summary, MM14315, says the agency finalized use of the Medicare Economic Index productivity-adjustment percentage, with a five-year look-back, producing a minus 2.5 percent efficiency adjustment for 2026. It also says CMS finalized an updated list of HCPCS codes exempt from the adjustment. The contractor list, not a headline, is the inventory of what is in and what is out.

## What ASHA says the cut is for — and what it is not

ASHA’s public 2026 SLP PDF describes the policy as a reduction to work RVUs and intra-service time for certain CPT codes. CMS’s stated theory, as ASHA reports it, is that efficiency gains over time are not fully captured in the valuation process. The 2.5 percent figure is built from productivity data for calendar years 2021 through 2025. ASHA writes that the same methodology may be reapplied every three years.

The PDF says CMS is applying the adjustment primarily to non-time-based codes that describe procedures, radiologic services, and diagnostic tests. Services in which the clinician’s time is the primary resource, and services on the approved telehealth list, are not subject to it. 92507 — individual treatment of speech, language, voice, communication, and/or auditory processing disorder — is ASHA’s explicit SLP example of an exemption. New CPT codes introduced in a given year are excluded for that calendar year.

This desk will not invent a list of other SLP codes that take the cut. CMS publishes that list. ASHA’s public PDF illustrates the arithmetic on at least one instrumental swallowing code; the dollar columns in that illustration are a table, and they stay in the PDF.

An exemption from the efficiency adjustment is not an exemption from the conversion-factor stack, MPPR, sequestration, GPCI, or the KX threshold. It is not a 2.5 percent raise. It is the absence of one 2.5 percent work-RVU reduction on a listed code.

## What SLPs should verify

Download CMS’s current efficiency-adjustment code list rather than inferring membership from 92507’s exemption. Confirm with the MAC that a billed SLP code is, or is not, on that list. Do not treat “on the telehealth list” as a shortcut without checking the current telehealth list and the exemption file. Recalculate locality rates. ASHA’s every-three-years note is a warning about 2029 methodology, not a billed 2026 modifier.
