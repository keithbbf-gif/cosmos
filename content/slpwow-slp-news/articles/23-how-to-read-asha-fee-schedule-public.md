# How to read ASHA’s public 2026 SLP fee-schedule PDF without treating it like a MAC letter

ROCKVILLE — Nov. 13, 2025 — Every January someone forwards ASHA’s Medicare fee-schedule PDF as if the attachment were a remittance. It is not. The 2026 speech-language pathology edition is a public analysis — first edition dated November 13, 2025 — of a CMS final rule. It explains policy, lists national average payment amounts, and tells readers, on page one, to contact their local Medicare Administrative Contractor for final rates and coverage guidelines. That sentence is the most important line in the file.

SLP WOW already walked through ASHA’s 2025 analysis in January 2025. This piece does not reprint that post and does not reprint ASHA’s 2026 tables. It is a user’s guide to the 2026 public packet: what the PDF can do, what the November news page adds, and where both go stale.

## What the public packet actually is

Two public ASHA products landed in mid-November 2025. The news page, updated November 19, is the short version: two conversion factors, a 2.5 percent one-time congressional increase, a warning that SLP cuts could approach 4 percent without more legislation, PE rebalancing, an efficiency adjustment with 92507 and 92557 exempt, the $2,480 KX figure, MPPR and targeted review maintained, most SLPs outside mandatory MIPS, and a telehealth-authority date that was then January 30, 2026. The PDF is the long version of the same week, with national-rate tables this desk will not paste.

Both are association analyses of CMS policy. They are not Change Request 14315. They are not the MPPR rate file updated February 24, 2026. They are not the Annual Therapy Update. They are not a MAC local coverage determination. ASHA says as much. Read them as a map of the final rule, then open the CMS page that implements the piece you care about.

The PDF’s own overview is the right altitude: modest payment updates, continued downward pressure, a one-time 2.5 percent increase that may be offset by other CMS policies and mandatory budget reductions, permanent telehealth *codes* with a then-short congressional *people* window, MPPR and targeted review still in force, most SLPs exempt from MIPS, limited Advanced APM access. If a clinic needs only that weather report, the first three pages are enough. If a clinic needs a priced claim, they are not.

## National averages are a teaching column

ASHA publishes national Part B rates so clinicians can see direction of travel — 2026 next to 2025, in the tables we are not reprinting. The PDF’s prose already contains the caution that matters more than any row: CMS impact estimates are averages based on cumulative therapy spending; actual payment depends on locality-specific rates and the CPT codes billed. ASHA’s own examples are 92507, up about 2 percent at the national rate, and 96112, down 1 percent. A caseload that is not that mix, in a locality that is not the national GPCI blend, will not cash those percentages.

The conversion factors in the PDF — $33.57 qualifying Advanced APM, $33.40 everyone else — are CMS multipliers. Most SLPs, ASHA says, use the second. Locality GPCIs still modify work, PE, and malpractice before that multiplier lands. ASHA points readers to its page on calculating locality rates. That page, the CMS Physician Fee Schedule look-up, and the MAC are the pricing path. A screenshot of Table 2 is not.

Contractor-priced codes, including some new remote therapeutic monitoring device-supply codes ASHA flags, will not have a honest national cell to screenshot in the first place.

## Three dates the November PDF cannot freeze

Telehealth practitioner authority. The PDF and the November news item describe a continuing resolution through January 30, 2026. The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, later extended audiologist and SLP authority through December 31, 2027. ASHA’s public telehealth page, updated February 3, 2026, is the association’s later sentence. CMS’s Therapy Services page cites section 6209 for the same 2027 date. Anyone still quoting the PDF’s January 30 people-clock is reading a superseded paragraph. The PDF’s statement that SLP *codes* on the list since 2020 are permanently listed as of January 1, 2026, was not superseded. Codes and people remain two clocks.

The work GPCI 1.0 floor. ASHA reported a CR restoration through January 30, 2026. Whether Congress extended that floor again is a later-statute question.

The MPPR spreadsheet. CMS updated the 2026 MPPR rate file on February 24, 2026. A November PDF cannot include a February download. Read the PDF for the structure of the year. Read CMS for the date of the file.

## How to use the sections without over-using them

Conversion factor and MACRA/OBBBA math: use ASHA to understand why there are two factors and which one most SLPs should assume. Then confirm APM status with the entity and the MAC.

Practice expense and efficiency adjustment: use ASHA to learn that SLP services are paid at the non-facility PE rate by law, that 92507 and 92557 are exempt from the 2.5 percent efficiency cut because they are on the telehealth list, and that the efficiency methodology may recur every three years. Then download CMS’s exemption list. Do not infer neighboring SLP codes.

RTM: use ASHA’s public news sentence — 2-to-15-day monitoring, 10-to-19-minute treatment management — as the policy English. Use CMS’s Therapy Services page and CR 14250 for the sometimes-therapy code numbers. Do not assign descriptors from memory.

MIPS: use ASHA for the three-threshold test and the fact that two SDOH measures came out. Then run the QPP participation-status tool.

KX and targeted review: $2,480 and $3,000 are CMS numbers. ASHA is repeating them. Confirm on the Therapy Services page.

## What SLPs should verify

Download the current public PDF, note “First Edition November 13, 2025” on the cover, and treat every telehealth-authority date inside it as a November fact. Open ASHA’s February 2026 telehealth page and CMS’s Therapy Services page before building a 2026–2027 calendar. Price claims from the locality look-up and the MAC, not from ASHA’s national table. If a row in that table would be useful internally, recalculate it; do not paste the table into a public post or a patient estimate. Keep the 2025 SLP WOW piece in the archive. It is last year’s map.
