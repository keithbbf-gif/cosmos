# Home-state FBI checks for a first compact privilege sit with the board, not the portal

WASHINGTON — Feb. 4, 2026 — CompactConnect can take a privilege application. It cannot roll a clinician’s fingerprints. The Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact Commission’s February 2026 newsletter recorded an amendment on the requirement that home states conduct FBI criminal background checks for initial applicants, with revised implementation timelines and “legal impossibility” exceptions. The work happens at the home board. The portal is downstream.

This desk is not publishing a noncompliance list. The Commission has a Compliance Committee. The February letter said that committee had not met since the prior newsletter and had not rescheduled a cancelled 17 November 2025 meeting. Absence of a published scorecard is not a finding that every member is current, and it is not a finding that any named member is behind. Readers who need a state’s status should read the current Commission rule text and ask the home licensing board.

Volume 2, Issue 1, dated 4 February 2026, put the background-check amendment in the Rules Committee’s year-in-review. The Commission, the letter said, strengthened its operational framework through several key rules, “beginning with the adoption of a $50.00 administrative fee per Privilege to Practice and an amendment on the requirement for Home States to conduct FBI criminal background checks for initial applicants.” Those updates “also included revised implementation timelines and ‘legal impossibility’ exceptions for background check compliance.”

That is Commission rulemaking, not an ASHA membership perk and not a federal employment form. “Initial applicants” is the population the letter names. It does not, in that paragraph, rewrite every renewal, every subsequent privilege, or every remote-state jurisprudence packet.

The same issue listed other 2026 rules, bylaws, and policies still on the committee’s work list — staggered officer terms, adverse-action reporting, mediation and dispute procedures, joint investigations, a military-identification verification policy, and monthly remittance of state fees. Those items were proposed work. They are not a substitute for the background-check rule the letter treated as already adopted.

## What the August 2025 letter added before launch

The 1 August 2025 newsletter described the same amendment in plainer operational English. The home state must implement or use procedures to obtain FBI and home-state criminal records for applicants in that home state who are applying for their initial — “very first time” — privilege to practice in one or more states under the compact. The Commission also amended the timeframe for members to implement the checks and the exceptions for legal impossibility.

Nahale Kalfas, the Commission’s general counsel, wrote in that August legal corner that the then-current criminal-background-check rule enabled member states to onboard without an FBI check in place, but only if they met the rest of the rule and recognized that an FBI check must be required of compact participants once the member is able to conduct it. That is counsel’s public newsletter language, not a hall pass for a clinician to skip a packet the home board still demands.

The February letter did not reprint a state-by-state exception roster. This article will not invent one. “Legal impossibility” is a Commission term of art. Whether a given board claims it is a question for that board and for the current rule text, not for a newsroom listicle.

## Why the home state is the first stop

ASHA’s 28 October 2025 public note remains the registration gate: practitioners cannot register with CompactConnect until the home state onboards. Background-check capacity is part of what boards have been working through as they onboard. The August 2025 letter said helping states through that process was the project’s biggest hurdle before launch.

A privilege is still equivalent to a license only in a remote member that is issuing. Practice still occurs in the state where the patient or client is located. Paying the $50 Commission fee does not complete a fingerprint card. A remote board that is issuing may still ask questions the home packet already answered; that is a board instruction, not a second compact.

As of the 28 May 2026 home-page update, four states are issuing: Louisiana, Ohio, Tennessee, and West Virginia. Thirty-three enacted members are not. A clinician in the 33 should not treat a neighbor’s portal screenshot as proof the home FBI process is finished.

## What SLPs should verify

Read the current Commission rule on implementation of the criminal-background-check requirement, not last year’s hearing testimony. Ask the home licensing board what it requires for a first privilege — FBI, state records, or both — and whether the board already holds a qualifying check from initial licensure. Do not assume a “legal impossibility” exception applies to you; the exception is a member-state compliance concept, not a clinician self-certification. If the home board is not yet issuing, the packet question may be premature, but it will not disappear on the morning CompactConnect opens.
