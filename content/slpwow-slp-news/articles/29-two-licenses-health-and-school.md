# A compact privilege is not a school certificate. Several states still want both.

WASHINGTON — Sept. 14, 2026 — School speech-language pathologists keep running into a paperwork problem that the interstate compact does not solve. One document is a health-profession license, or a compact privilege that a remote member state treats as the equivalent of that license. The other is whatever the education agency or the district requires before a person can be assigned to students. Those are different offices, different clocks, and, in several states this desk can document from primary pages, both still exist.

The Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact Commission describes a privilege as equivalent to a license in a remote member state that is actually issuing. Practice, the Commission says, occurs in the state where the patient or client is located at the encounter. That sentence is about practice acts. It is not an educator certificate, not an assignment letter, and not a substitute for a state department of education field code.

## New York: title protection, with school-shaped holes

New York’s Office of the Professions is explicit about the title. Any use of “Speech-Language Pathologist” in the state requires licensure, except in certain exempt settings. The list on the license-requirements page includes federal, state, or local governments; public or nonpublic elementary or secondary schools; and colleges and universities.

The same page then sends public-school speech work to a different building: teacher certification from the Office of Teaching Initiatives is the appropriate credential for providing speech services in a public school. The exemption is not a hall pass to hang out a private shingle as an unlicensed SLP. It is a recognition that some school practice sits outside the title lock — and that the education credential is the one the school side wants.

OP’s license path, for people who do need the title, still includes a minimum of 36 weeks of supervised experience after all graduate-degree requirements are satisfied. Compact privilege does not rewrite that page.

## South Carolina: educator field plus LLR

South Carolina split the problem in the other direction and then stapled the two pieces together. Effective July 1, 2020, the State Board of Education approved educator certificates in the field of speech-language pathologist. The Department of Education no longer issues the old “speech-language therapist” educator field to new applicants.

An SLP applying for the educator certificate uploads a current, valid SLP or SLP Intern license from the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation. Renewal of an educator certificate that is only in the SLP instructional-service field is the LLR license again: a copy that remains valid beyond June 30 of the year the educator certificate expires.

LLR, on its own licensure page, says no person may practice speech-language pathology without a license unless the setting is exempt under section 40-67-300. The board license is issued independently and renews on a two-year cycle that expires March 31 of the second year. Two calendars. Two agencies. One job.

## Rhode Island: even-year RIDOH card, PK–12 support certificate

Rhode Island’s practice license is a Department of Health document. R.I. Gen. Laws § 5-48.2-6 issues full audiologist and speech-language pathologist licenses for two years; they expire on the first day of July and renew biennially in even years. RIDOH’s licensing page is where fees live. This story is not printing a dollar amount.

The school side is a RIDE support-professional certificate. RIDE’s certificate-areas page describes a Speech and Language Pathologist credential valid for assignment in grades PK–12 in Rhode Island public schools. RIDE’s required-tests list codes that area as 13004. Holding the RIDOH license does not, by itself, put 13004 on an assignment.

RIDOH’s own compact note, as fetched for this pack, said Rhode Island had enacted ASLP-IC legislation and that the compact was not currently operational on that page. Enactment of a compact statute is still not a RIDE certificate.

## Utah: the school board stopped issuing the practice credential

Utah spent years letting the State Board of Education credential stand in for a practice license inside school walls. Enrolled H.B. 374, effective May 6, 2026, repealed USBE’s authority to issue those credentials and moved issuance to the Division of Professional Licensing. Existing USBE documents remain valid until the printed expiration. USBE staff told a June 2026 committee that those license areas expire over the next five years.

Utah is the conversion case. The dual-paper problem did not disappear. The practice paper changed desks. Districts will still decide what they file in the personnel folder.

## What this is not

It is not a 50-state fee chart. This pack does not invent application charges. It is not a claim that every state uses the New York exemption, the South Carolina staple, the Rhode Island even-year clock, or the Utah transfer. It is not advice to practice on a compact privilege inside a school that asked for an educator field.

## What SLPs should verify

Name the two agencies before you sign a contract: the professional board or compact commission, and the education department or district certification office. Ask which document the payroll file requires. If you hold a compact privilege, ask whether the remote state is issuing and whether the school employer will accept that privilege in place of its own certificate. In New York, read the OP exemptions and then call Teaching Initiatives. In South Carolina, keep the LLR license alive past June 30 of the educator expiry year. In Rhode Island, watch July 1 of even years and the 13004 assignment. In Utah, file the DOPL transition application if you are in the 2020–2026 USBE cohort.
