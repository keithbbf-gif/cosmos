# Congress extended SLP Medicare telehealth authority through Dec. 31, 2027

WASHINGTON — Feb. 3, 2026 — Congress put a new end date on the statute that lets speech-language pathologists and audiologists furnish Medicare telehealth. It is December 31, 2027. It is not permanent. It is not a private-plan mandate. It is not the same decision as CMS’s decision to keep SLP codes on the telehealth services list.

ASHA’s public Medicare telehealth page carries a February 3, 2026, update: the House passed, and the President signed, the Senate amendments to H.R. 7148, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026. That law, ASHA says, includes an extension of Medicare telehealth authority for audiologists and SLPs through December 31, 2027. ASHA also says it is still asking for a permanent statute and that advocates can use its Take Action Center. Advocacy is not the effective date. The effective date is the one on the enrolled act.

## What section 6209 actually names

CMS’s Therapy Services page is the agency paraphrase clinicians can cite without opening the Statutes at Large. The page says it is updating Disposition 10 to reflect section 6209 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026. That section, CMS writes, extended the ability of physical therapists, occupational therapists, and speech-language pathologists to furnish telehealth services, including the telephone assessment and management services billed with 98966, 98967, and 98968, through December 31, 2027.

The inclusion of 98966–98968 matters because those codes are easy to lose in a 92507 conversation. CMS is telling contractors that the therapy-profession extension covers that telephone assessment-and-management family for the same window. It is not a new SLP treatment code. It is a statutory list CMS is implementing on the therapy page.

The same CMS page points readers to the Annual Therapy Update for the 2026 therapy code list and dispositions. That file, not a news brief, is where a biller confirms how a given code is flagged for the year.

## What the extension replaced

ASHA’s November 2025 public news item on the 2026 fee schedule described a continuing resolution that reopened the government and carried audiologist and SLP telehealth authority only through January 30, 2026. The first edition of ASHA’s public 2026 SLP fee-schedule PDF used the same January 30 date. CAA 2026 superseded that short CR window. A clinic that froze its compliance calendar in November is a year and eleven months short.

CMS’s February 26, 2026, telehealth FAQ is the post-enactment agency voice. Through December 31, 2027, an extended range of practitioners may bill for Medicare telehealth, and beneficiaries can receive those services anywhere in the United States and territories. The FAQ’s 2028 sentences are a different story. This one stops at the extension.

## Traditional fee-for-service. Full stop.

ASHA puts the payer limit in the first screen of the public page. The Medicare telehealth policies it describes apply only to traditional fee-for-service Medicare. State Medicaid programs and private insurers, including Medicare Part C — Medicare Advantage — may cover telehealth furnished by audiologists and SLPs. Coverage, coding, and billing rules vary. ASHA’s instruction is to verify each plan. That is not hedge language. Advantage organizations write their own telehealth attachments. Medicaid agencies write theirs. Section 6209 does not rewrite either.

A hospital outpatient department, a rehab agency, and a private practice can all be looking at the same federal date and still be looking at different claims formats. CMS’s FAQ aligns remotely furnished hospital outpatient therapy to a beneficiary at home with the same December 31, 2027, window. Institutional claims still have type-of-bill and modifier rules the MAC enforces. The statute extends *who* may furnish. It does not print the UB-04.

## Permanent codes, temporary people

The extension does not decide which SLP services count as telehealth. CMS already finalized permanent placement, as of January 1, 2026, for the audiology and speech-language pathology services that have been on the list since 2020. ASHA’s February page treats that as settled. Congress’s job in CAA 2026 was the roster: keep SLPs and audiologists in the “extended range of practitioners” through the last day of 2027.

Confusing those two decisions produces two opposite errors. One is to treat a permanent code list as permanent SLP billing authority. The other is to treat a 2027 people-date as if 92507 might fall off the list next January. The list and the roster are not the same document.

ASHA’s page also notes that HIPAA enforcement, relaxed for some platforms during the public-health emergency, has resumed. The appropriations act did not revive the emergency platform waiver.

## What SLPs should verify

Confirm the date of service falls on or before December 31, 2027, and that the beneficiary is in traditional fee-for-service. Confirm the CPT code is on the current Medicare telehealth services list — permanent listing is not a license to bill a code that was never listed. Confirm with the MAC the POS, 95, and GN combination it wants on an SLP claim. If the payer is an Advantage plan or a state Medicaid agency, ignore the federal end date until that payer’s own bulletin agrees. Do not keep using ASHA’s November 2025 “through January 30, 2026” sentence as if CAA 2026 had not been signed.
