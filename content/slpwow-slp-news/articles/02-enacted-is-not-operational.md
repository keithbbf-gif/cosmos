# Enacted is not operational: 33 compact members still cannot issue privileges

WASHINGTON — Sept. 14, 2026 — Thirty-three legislatures have written the Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact into statute and still cannot sell a privilege. Four states can. The Commission’s own map keeps those facts in different colors. Recruiter slides often do not.

Enactment is how a state or territory joins the agreement. Issuance is what happens after that member onboards its licensees to CompactConnect, meets the Commission’s requirements, and opens the portal. Those are sequential acts. They are not synonyms. A governor’s signature bought a seat. It did not mint a CompactConnect account.

## Four buckets, fetched the same day

The Compact Map, retrieved on 14 September 2026, reports four counts: 33 jurisdictions with legislation enacted but not yet issuing privileges; 4 issuing; 4 with active legislation; 14 with no active legislation. The home page, last given a dated operational banner on 28 May 2026, names the issuing four — Louisiana, West Virginia, Ohio, and Tennessee — and counts 37 members in all: 36 states and one territory.

Registration, the Commission says, is open for licensed practitioners in those four states. Additional members “must onboard licensees to CompactConnect and meet the compact requirements” before they can issue. The home page tells clinicians to ask the home-state licensing board when their own state will start. It does not publish a ranked queue, and this desk will not invent one.

Onboarding is a rolling staff-and-data project. ASHA’s public news desk, writing on the first operational day, said member states complete it “in coordination with state board administrators, IT staff, CompactConnect Project Management Team, and the Compact Executive Director.” That is not a ribbon-cutting that follows the signing ceremony by a fixed number of weeks.

## What ASHA recorded on 28 October 2025

Louisiana and West Virginia went first. ASHA’s 28 October 2025 public note treated that Tuesday as CompactConnect’s launch and the first day any board issued privileges. Of the 37 members, those two had onboarded. “Practitioners cannot register with CompactConnect until their home state onboards,” ASHA wrote. Later Commission copy has not walked that sentence back.

The same ASHA piece pointed clinicians to the Compact Map for status and to the state licensing agency for a home-state timeline. It did not publish a calendar of the remaining 35. Neither did the November 2025 Commission newsletter, which dated the Louisiana and West Virginia “operationalized” hour to 28 October 2025 and treated it as the moment the compact’s mutual-recognition purpose left the statute book.

Ohio’s Speech and Hearing Professionals Board later told licensees it would process applications beginning 9 February 2026. The Commission posted Tennessee on 28 May 2026. That is the entire issuing column as of this draft.

The apply URL the Commission publishes is `https://app.compactconnect.org/Dashboard`. A clinician whose home license sits in one of the 33 will not get a working login by staring at a green marketing map.

## A privilege still needs two live boards

Under the compact, an audiologist or speech-language pathologist licensed in good standing in a member home state may obtain a compact privilege that the Commission describes as equivalent to a license in another member state. The remote state has to be issuing. A privilege into a member that has not onboarded is not a product the portal can mint.

Practice, the Commission says, occurs in the state where the patient or client is located at the encounter. Telepractice does not relocate the client. A session with a student sitting in a non-issuing member is still practice in that member. Ordinary licensure still applies there.

School employment adds gates the compact does not lift. Districts and state education agencies still assign caseloads, still write IEP service-delivery language, and still decide which educator certificate they will accept. A privilege is not an assignment letter.

## The 33 are not a forecast

The February 2026 Commission newsletter called CompactConnect the product of more than five years of work and said the stretch goal for the year was to have all member states onboard and able to issue. A stretch goal is not a ship date. The same letter recorded pending legislation in four non-members and a $50 Commission administrative fee per privilege. It did not name which of the 33 enacted members would finish CompactConnect next.

The August 2025 newsletter, written before launch, said the biggest hurdle was helping states through onboarding and asked boards that wanted to be in the initial wave to test data in a beta environment by 31 August 2025. Two boards made that first morning. Two more have made it since. The remaining enacted members are still in the “What’s next?” paragraph on the home page.

Bills and maps move. The four “active legislation” states on today’s map may not be the four named in February. The 14 with no active legislation are not pledged to stay empty. None of those buckets answers the question a working SLP actually has: is my home board onboarded, and is the remote state issuing?

## What this is not

It is not proof that a member will issue this school year. It is not a national license. It is not permission to treat a signing statement as a CompactConnect password. It is not a Medicare enrollment. It is not a source for unpublished state-by-state fee tables.

Travel companies will keep selling the finished map. The Commission’s buckets are the correction.

## What SLPs should verify

Confirm home-state onboarding on the Compact Map and with the licensing board. Confirm the remote state is issuing, not merely enacted. If the home state is still in the 33, do not build a start date around a recruiter’s “any day now.” If the work is in schools, ask the district which credential it wants. If the work is Medicare telehealth, treat the compact and CMS as separate clocks.
