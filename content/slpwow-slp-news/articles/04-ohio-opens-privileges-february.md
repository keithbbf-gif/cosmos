# Ohio began processing ASLP-IC privileges on 9 February 2026

COLUMBUS — Feb. 9, 2026 — Ohio’s Speech and Hearing Professionals Board put a Monday on the compact calendar: the board would begin processing Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact applications on 9 February 2026. As of early 2026, the board told licensees, two other states were operational and available to Ohio practitioners under a privilege — West Virginia and Louisiana.

That pairing matches the Commission’s first operational morning. ASHA’s public desk recorded Louisiana and West Virginia as the first issuing states on 28 October 2025. Ohio became the third board to process. Tennessee joined the issuing column later, on 28 May 2026, according to the Commission home page.

## What Ohio’s page said in early 2026

The board’s interstate-compact page counted 37 enacted jurisdictions as of January 2026, including one U.S. territory. It counted three states processing privilege applications as of February 2026: Ohio, West Virginia, and Louisiana. It pointed licensees to CompactConnect and to the Commission’s privilege-status updates for the current list of members actually open to Ohio licensees.

The Commission’s apply URL is `https://app.compactconnect.org/Dashboard`. ASHA’s October 2025 public note still governs the login: practitioners cannot register until the home state onboards. Ohio’s February date was that onboarding, for Ohio, becoming a processing fact rather than a hearing-room promise.

A privilege is equivalent to a license in a remote member that is issuing. It is not a license in a member that has only enacted. Practice occurs in the state where the patient or client is located at the encounter. An Ohio home license plus a West Virginia or Louisiana privilege covers practice in those remote states. It does not cover a client sitting in an enacted-but-not-issuing member.

## What to recheck after February

The early-2026 Ohio page is a dated snapshot. The Commission later posted Tennessee as issuing. Other members remain in the Compact Map’s enacted-not-issuing bucket. Boards, not this desk, publish the next processing date. School educator credentials and Medicare enrollment remain separate from the privilege.

Ohio licensees should confirm on the Compact Map — and with the Speech and Hearing Professionals Board — which remote members are open the week they apply. ASHA’s October 2025 rule still holds: no CompactConnect account until the home state onboards. Practice still occurs in the state where the client is located.
