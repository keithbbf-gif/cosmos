# A compact privilege is not an educator certificate

WASHINGTON — Sept. 14, 2026 — A compact privilege will not put a speech-language pathologist on a school’s assignment sheet. The Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact Commission describes a privilege as equivalent to a license in a remote member that is issuing. School employers still want their own paper: an educator certificate, a district contract, and IEP language that matches how the student is actually served.

The Commission’s geography sentence is the first gate. Practice occurs in the state where the patient or client is located at the encounter. A student sitting in a classroom, a living room, or a related-services office is located *there*. Telepractice does not move the child into the clinician’s kitchen. If that location is a member that has not onboarded CompactConnect, the compact does not invent a privilege. Ordinary licensure still applies. If it is one of the four issuing states — Louisiana, Ohio, Tennessee, or West Virginia, as of the 28 May 2026 home-page update — a qualifying home license plus a privilege is the compact path. It is still not a teaching certificate.

## Two professions, two stacks of paper

Licensing boards regulate the practice of speech-language pathology. State education agencies and local districts regulate who may be employed, assigned, and evaluated as a school SLP. Those stacks overlap. They are not the same form.

ASHA’s 28 October 2025 public note remains the compact login rule: practitioners cannot register with CompactConnect until the home state onboards. Thirty-three enacted members are still in that queue. A school contract that assumes “the compact covers us” in one of those 33 is assuming a portal that has not opened.

District assignment is a third document. An IEP that lists speech-language services does not hire the contractor. The local education agency still decides caseload, supervision, and whether a virtual vendor may hold the service. The compact does not rewrite those decisions.

## Utah: the education exemption came off the statute

Utah’s enrolled H.B. 374 took effect 6 May 2026. The bill repealed the exemption that had allowed an individual to receive speech-language pathologist or audiologist licensure from the State Board of Education. The Division of Professional Licensing is now the license.

DOPL’s public transition notice tells clinicians moving from a USBE credential to a DOPL license to use the standard application, attach a copy of the USBE credential, and skip transcripts and exam scores on that path. Existing USBE credentials remain valid until the expiration date printed on the document. A June 2026 USBE staff memo on educator-licensing rule amendments stated the transfer in one line: H.B. 374 moved authority to license audiologists and speech-language pathologists from USBE to DOPL, and USBE licenses with those license areas will expire over the next five years.

Utah enacted the compact years ago. Utah is not on the Commission’s issuing list as of this desk date. A new DOPL license is the practice credential the legislature just required. It is not a CompactConnect privilege, and it does not, by itself, answer a district’s remaining educator-employment questions. Those questions belong to the employer and to USBE’s current rules, not to a privilege the state is not yet issuing.

## New York: title in one office, school work in another

New York’s Office of the Professions is blunt about title. Use of “Speech-Language Pathologist” requires licensure except in listed exempt settings. Those settings include federal, state, or local governments; public or nonpublic elementary or secondary schools; and colleges and universities. The same page says that to provide speech services in a public school, “Teacher Certification” is the appropriate credential, and it sends readers to the Office of Teaching Initiatives.

The OP license still has its own experience gate: a minimum of 36 weeks of supervised experience after the graduate-degree requirements are satisfied. School employment and title use can travel on different tracks. None of that text is a compact privilege. This desk is not assigning New York a current bill status; pending files change, and the Compact Map’s “active legislation: 4” bucket should be rechecked the week it matters. The dual gate is already on the OP page whether or not the legislature ever joins.

## South Carolina: LLR license to get the educator certificate, and again to renew it

The South Carolina Department of Education’s SLP certification page, dated in the agency’s public materials as of 11 June 2026, records a State Board of Education decision effective 1 July 2020: educator certificates in the field of speech-language pathologist, not the older “speech-language therapist” field. An applicant uploads a current, valid SLP or SLP Intern license from the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation.

Renewal is the same two-key lock. An individual who holds an educator certificate only in the SLP instructional-service field may renew by submitting a current LLR SLP license that remains valid past 30 June of the educator certificate’s expiry year. Holders of additional certification fields have a separate credit requirement. Contracted SLPs are evaluated under the vendor’s protocols; district employees follow the state’s educator evaluation system. A compact privilege would not replace that LLR-plus-educator pair for someone the district is treating as a certified school SLP.

## What the compact still does not do in a school

It does not write the IEP. It does not assign the building. It does not decide whether a virtual session satisfies the district’s service-delivery model. It does not enroll the clinician in Medicare, and it does not extend a federal telehealth clock. It does not let a home-state board that has not onboarded skip CompactConnect.

Travel vendors will keep selling one credential. Utah, New York, and South Carolina — three different legal designs — all still require a second look at the education side.

## What SLPs should verify

Ask the hiring district which credential it will accept: state SLP license, educator certificate, both, or a contractor waiver. Confirm the student’s location and whether that state is issuing privileges or still only enacted. If the home state is among the 33, do not date a first day of service on CompactConnect. In Utah, read DOPL’s current transition notice and the printed expiration on any remaining USBE credential. In New York, separate OP title rules from public-school teacher certification. In South Carolina, check that the LLR license will still be valid past the educator certificate’s 30 June date.
