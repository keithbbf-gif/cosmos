# Style guide — WOW Therapies, play therapy history series

Educational history for [WOWTherapies.com](https://wowtherapies.com): how play was named as a clinical method, who paid for the room, who dissented, and what later researchers revised. It is **not** a protocol library, **not** a toy catalog, and **not** the SLPWOW speech-pathology series.

It deepens three sibling essays in `content/wowtherapies-therapy-history/` and then leaves that house:

- `anna-freud` — play as a *technique* quarrel, not the Hampstead life
- `melanie-klein` — the play technique as free association, not the whole Controversial Discussions
- `donald-winnicott` — *Playing and Reality* (1971) and the squiggle as a dated object, not the pediatric career

Do not retell Van Riper, articulation science, or dysphagia. Those belong on the speech lane. Do not retell the Freud-to-Herman calendar. Point at those slugs.

`voice_check: human` means the writer draft passed this file. `voice_check: edited` means a later editor read the piece aloud. Neither value is a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Who is speaking

A historically literate magazine writer who has sat in enough consulting rooms to know that a sand tray is not a person. Warm without being pastoral. Precise without being a textbook. The reader may be a curious parent, a family member, a student, or a clinician. Do not talk down. Do not talk shop as if the room were empty of children.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas practice brand. These essays are **heritage education**, not a menu of services and not a claim that the clinic practices child-centered, filial, Theraplay, sandplay, or any named school.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" as a metaphor for a field
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- three-beat synonym piles ("heal, grow, and thrive")
- "the father of…" / "the mother of…" unless a contemporary actually used that phrase and you are quoting the myth, not endorsing it (Schaefer's obituaries used "father of play therapy"; quote the myth, do not crown him)
- "gold-standard treatment" as marketing
- invented quotes
- AI-generated faces, AI children, or "period photographs" that never existed
- "try this at home," "set up a playroom," "here are the toys you need"

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "empower," "in this article we will explore," "journey of healing" as filler.

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a room, a document, or a named quarrel. The first sentence should be checkable.
2. Mix sentence length. A short sentence after a long one. Do not write every paragraph as topic sentence + three examples + wrap.
3. Name the book, paper, hospital, or society. *Play Therapy* (1947). Guerney 1964. APT, 1982. UNT Center for Play Therapy, 1988. *International Journal of Play Therapy*, 1992.
4. Mark uncertainty. `[CITE NEEDED]` or a plain "historians disagree" beats a clean falsehood.
5. Put Axline, Landreth, and the later names in their decade. Do not offer them as current treatment manuals.
6. Give women's work, British rooms, and non-U.S. associations full essays, not a closing paragraph titled "also."
7. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target 1,000–1,600 words for figures, 1,200–2,000 for era essays, 800–1,400 for reader-tool essays. Cut the recap paragraph if it only restates the headings. `check_pack.py` fails an era under 1,200 words, a figure under 1,000, or a reader essay under 800. Deepen with a dated object — a paper, a shop, a second book — not with recap.

## Claims posture

See `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`. History may describe a method. History may not teach a reader to self-administer it. No session scripts, no "eight principles" reprinted as homework, no toy lists as setup instructions, no filial how-tos, no Theraplay dimensions as exercises, no sand-tray scene interpretations.

Efficacy sentences, if any, stay historical and sourced ("Bratton, Ray, Rhine, and Jones, 2005, reported…") and never become "play therapy will help your child."

Living people get extra care: no gossip, no unsourced health claims. Public books, public lectures, and public interviews only. Garry Landreth died 11 June 2026; treat that as a public university record, not an occasion for eulogy-as-protocol.

## Portraits

Public domain or Creative Commons only. Document the file in `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. If there is no clean license, use type or a period object — never a generated face, and never a generated child. See `PHOTO_NOTES.md`. Almost every twentieth-century play-therapy figure in this pack is `no-portrait`. That is correct.

## Series voice, in one paragraph

Write as if *The New Yorker* and *Psychotherapy Networker* had to share a desk, and the desk was in a small-city clinic that still answers its own phone. The past is allowed to be strange. The present is not allowed to be sold. A dollhouse is a historical object. It is not this page's homework.
