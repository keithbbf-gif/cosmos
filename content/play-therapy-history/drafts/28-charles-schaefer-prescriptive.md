**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Charles E. Schaefer was born 15 November 1933, taught at Fairleigh Dickinson University in Teaneck, edited *The Therapeutic Use of Child's Play* (Jason Aronson, late 1970s), co-founded the Association for Play Therapy with Kevin O'Connor in 1982, co-edited the 1983 *Handbook of Play Therapy*, and died of a stroke on 19 September 2020 in the public Dignity Memorial notice. Obituaries and some encyclopedias called him the "father of play therapy." This series will quote the phrase as a myth. It will not crown him.

Hug-Hellmuth 1921, Klein 1932, Lowenfeld 1935, and Axline 1947 were already on the shelf. What Schaefer did, as a historical claim, is different. He organized. He edited. He treated play as a set of *powers* and techniques a clinician might choose among — a prescriptive habit rather than a single stance.

## Handbooks as institutions

A handbook is how a field tells itself it is a field. The 1983 Wiley volume, with O'Connor, gathered names who were also on the early Association board: Guerney, Jernberg, Landreth, and others. A second volume arrived in 1994. Later *Handbook of Play Therapy* editions continued the habit. This page will not table of contents them into a protocol library.

"Prescriptive play therapy," in his later vocabulary, meant matching a method to a problem. That sentence is catnip for managed care and catnip for weekend trainings. It is also a real editorial stance: the umbrella is wider than CCPT. APT's conference grid is the social form of that stance.

He founded an International Play Therapy Study Group that met in rotating countries — a workshop-economy fact, not a trial. He appeared on television. Television is a historical medium. It is not a finding.

## Fairleigh Dickinson, and a New Jersey capital

Denton had Landreth's center. Teaneck had Schaefer's university titles and a Center for Psychological Services in the FDU story. Fresno later housed APT's offices at CSPP when O'Connor moved. The field had more than one capital. Histories that only mention Texas are doing a pilgrimage, not a map.
