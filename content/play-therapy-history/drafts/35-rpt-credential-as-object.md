**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The Association for Play Therapy sells, and periodically rewrites, a credential: Registered Play Therapist, and a supervisor version, RPT-S. The manuals change. Hours change. Eligible degrees change. That changeability is the historical fact. A letterhead that says RPT in 1996 and a letterhead that says RPT in 2026 are not automatically the same object.

This essay will not publish a current hour table. Hour tables are protocols for applicants. They go stale. They belong on a4pt.org. Here the claim is smaller and easier to violate: an association credential is not a license.

## What the letters sit on top of

In U.S. states, the person who sees a child for mental-health treatment generally needs a state license or a school credential: LPC, LCSW, LMFT, psychologist, school counselor, sometimes others. APT's own public language has had to explain this, because parents and students confuse the nouns. Confusion is good for marketing and bad for law.

A person can be licensed and not RPT. A person can be RPT and still only as legal as their underlying license. A person can be neither and still play with a child in a hospital child-life role that is not psychotherapy. Three jobs. Three file cabinets.

## Why a society wanted hours

Hours make a workshop economy countable. They make a university certificate look like a path. They make a directory possible. They also make a gate. Who can afford the hours, the flights to Denton, the supervision fees? The gender-and-class essays return to that gate. Here the architectural fact is enough: a credential is a building with a toll.

International applicants meet a society that is American in address and "international" in journal title. Whether a UK BAPT route "equals" an RPT is a question employers ask and historians should refuse to flatten.

## What not to do with the letters

Do not print "licensed play therapist" as a WOW Therapies title unless a statute says those words — Arkansas does not, at last check, use them as a stand-alone license `[VERIFY on the state board page if you ever write a service line]`. Do not put RPT in schema as a medical qualification. Do not invent a clinical director who holds the letters.

Do not treat the credential as a quality guarantee. It is a membership-path guarantee: someone submitted hours the Association accepted that year.

## Why a small-city parent meets the letters

A directory search. A Psychology Today filter. A colleague's email signature. The letters travel farther than the manuals. This pack's job is to slow the letters down long enough to say what they are made of: dues, hours, a society founded in 1982, a journal founded in 1992, and a legal floor that is still the state.
