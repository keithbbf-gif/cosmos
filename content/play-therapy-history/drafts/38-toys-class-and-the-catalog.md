**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A recommended shelf is a theory that learned to shop. Mid-century playrooms already had objects. Late-century textbooks named categories and implied a completeness: family, aggressive, nurturing, expressive. Completeness has a receipt. This essay is about the receipt. It will not reprint the shelf. Reprinting would be a shopping list. A shopping list is a protocol.

## Who could buy "the" room

A university clinic in Denton could look complete because a department paid. A private office over a pharmacy in a county seat bought a tub of animals and a dollhouse from a discount store. Both have been called play therapy. The child who has never seen a two-story dollhouse, or who has only seen one on a screen, is not failing the method. The method assumed a catalog.

Aggressive-toy debates — whether to stock guns, whether to stock swords — are American culture wars in miniature. They are not timeless clinical truths. A 1991 textbook that includes a category is dating itself. A 2020s school that bans a category is dating itself. Historians can name both dates. They cannot settle the shelf for a living classroom.

## Flea markets, and the romance of the found object

Sandplay and world-technique cultures often praise the found miniature: the tarnished soldier, the broken fence. The praise is aesthetic and, sometimes, democratic. It is also a time tax. A person who can spend Saturdays hunting objects is a person with Saturdays. The catalog, ugly as it is, is faster. Speed is class.

Hospital playrooms buy washable objects because infection control is a patron. School closets buy what the PTA can stand. Each patron writes a theory on the shelf without calling it a theory.

## Children's own toys, and the theft of the familiar

Some hours use the child's toys from home. That sounds humble. It can also be a surveillance of a household's catalog. A clinician who reads a cheap plastic figure as "impoverished play" has already written a class note. This series will not teach a better note. It will say the theft is old.

Axline's 1947 room assumed materials. She did not assume a 1991 completeness. Completeness is a later professionalization. Professionalization is how a field starts to look expensive.

## What not to do with a toys essay

Do not publish an Amazon list. Do not rate brands. Do not photograph a child's toy as evidence. Do not tell a parent their house is the wrong playroom.

## Discount-store dollhouses and the completeness myth

A two-story dollhouse from a coastal catalog and a one-room plastic house from a county discount store are both houses. A method that treats only the first as real play has written a class note.
