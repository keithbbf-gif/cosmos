**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Kevin J. O'Connor is a living psychologist. In 1982 Charles Schaefer enlisted him as co-founder of the Association for Play Therapy. He built the Association's early newsletter, later served a long board stretch (APT's own emeritus page lists 1982–2003, emeritus 2004), and worked from the California School of Professional Psychology in Fresno when the Association's offices moved there. Those are membership and address facts from public society pages.

He also wrote books. *The Play Therapy Primer* (Wiley, 1991) is a public object. Later work named **ecosystemic play therapy**: the child in a system of family, school, and culture, not only in a fifty-minute bubble. This page will not teach the model. It will keep the name as a late-century attempt to stop pretending the playroom has no hallway.

## What "ecosystemic" was refusing

A playroom can become a fetish: the child, the toys, the glass, the note, as if the school bus did not exist. Ecosystemic language was a refusal of that fetish. Family therapists had already made the refusal in another vocabulary (sister pack: Satir, Minuchin). O'Connor's public books tried to make the refusal *play-shaped* for people who were going to sit APT hours anyway.

Refusal is not a protocol. It is a scope sentence: the hallway counts.
