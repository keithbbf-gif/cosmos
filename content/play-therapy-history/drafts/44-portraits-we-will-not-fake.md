**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Almost every figure in this pack is `portrait: none`. That is not a failure of research. It is the legal and ethical fact of twentieth-century photographs. Anna Freud died in 1982. Axline died in 1988. Landreth died in 2026. Living authors are living. University headshots are not free. Workshop selfies are not free. A generated "historical" face is a lie.

This essay is an instruction to the graphics agent and a warning to the import plugin that wants a featured image.

## What you may put in the frame

Type: name, dates, one epithet.

A title page if the *scan of the page* is public domain or Creative Commons and you can fill every credit bracket. Jackets are often still in copyright even when the text is old enough to discuss.

A PD toy catalog page, captioned as a catalog, not as a child's inner life.

A series template: wordmark plus essay title. Build one. Reuse it.

An empty tray the practice owns, with rights, with no scene in the sand.

## What you may not put in the frame

AI faces. AI children. "What Dibs looked like." Colorized guesses. Cropped documentary stills. Freud 1909 used as if he ran a playroom. Stock "therapist on the floor with smiling child." Patient sand worlds. Hospital beds. Doll-study stills. Atrocity.

If you cannot fill the credit block in `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`, do not upload.

## Why a child's face is a special ban

This is not only copyright. A play-therapy series that uses children's faces as decoration has already started to treat children as proof. Proof is a clinical job. Decoration is a marketing job. We will do neither with a minor's face.

*Dibs* is a book. The boy is not clip art.

## Why type is enough for a small-city site

WOW Therapies does not need a fake Vienna to look serious. It needs a sentence that can be checked. Type is checkable. A generated Hug-Hellmuth is not.

The last essay is the map: where to send a reader who came to the wrong folder.
