A sentence that begins "research shows play therapy works" is doing three jobs at once, and only one of them is history. The other two are marketing and homework. This page is a set of reading habits for the rest of the folder. It will not teach you to run a session. It will teach you to notice when a date has been asked to do a salesman's work.

## Four objects that get collapsed

**A book** is a publishing event. Axline 1947 is a Houghton Mifflin object. Landreth 1991 is an Accelerated Development object that later became a Routledge object. You can hold the edition. You can name the year. You cannot honestly say the book "is" play therapy any more than you can say *Cognitive Therapy of Depression* (1979) "is" every Tuesday in a county clinic.

**A trial or a review** is a research event. Guerney 1964 is a rationale paper in the *Journal of Consulting Psychology*. Bratton, Ray, Rhine, and Jones 2005 is a meta-analytic review in *Professional Psychology: Research and Practice*. Those papers have samples, inclusion rules, and later critics. They are not a permission slip. When this series names them, it names year, journal, and what the authors reported. It does not assign the paper as a home program.

**A society** is a membership object. The Association for Play Therapy dates itself to 1982. The British Association of Play Therapists dates itself to 1992. Dues, boards, newsletters, and a first journal issue (1992, *International Journal of Play Therapy*) are the facts. A society can issue a credential. A society cannot issue a state license. Those two words get swapped in brochure copy. They will not be swapped here.

**A room** is an architectural and economic object. A playroom in Paddington Green in 1949 is not a playroom in Denton in 1991 and not a converted closet in a public school. Square footage, who pays, who watches through glass, and whether a parent sits in the hall are historical. They are not "the" play therapy.

If a paragraph treats those four objects as one thing, the paragraph is already leaking.

## Questions that keep a claim honest

Who **paid**? A university counseling department, a hospital auxiliary, a private institute, a school district, and a parent's fee are different patrons. The 1987 UNT faculty vote to make play therapy a national focus is a budget sentence as much as a theory sentence.

Who **was in the sample**? "Children" is not a sample. Age band, referral source, and whether the comparison was wait-list, another therapy, or nothing at all change what a review can bear. The 2005 paper is useful because it made those choices visible. It is not useful as a slogan.

Who **dissented**? Klein and Anna Freud dissented in public, in London, in the 1940s. Later reviewers dissented about effect sizes, about school-based studies, about whether "play therapy" in a meta-analysis is one treatment or a family of brand names. A history that hides the dissent is a brochure.

What **was the comparison**? "Better than no treatment" is not "better than a skilled school counselor doing something else." "Better than a wait-list" is not "this is what your child needs."

What **is the verb**? *Published*, *founded*, *reported*, *credentialed*, *argued* are historical verbs. *Heals*, *cures*, *is proven to* are sales verbs. This series is allowed the first set.

## Two tempting mistakes

The first is **ancestor inflation**. Piaget's *Play, Dreams and Imitation in Childhood* (English 1951) is a developmental book. Vygotsky's lecture on play is a 1933 developmental argument. Clinicians later borrowed both. Borrowing is a historical fact. It does not make Piaget a play therapist, and it does not make a Pali text or a nursery rhyme into CCPT.

The second is **credential inflation**. An RPT is an Association for Play Therapy credential with hours and supervision rules that changed over time. An LPC, LCSW, LMFT, or psychologist license is a state object. A person can hold both. A website that treats the letters as interchangeable is not doing history. The essay on the RPT as a historical object stays with the association's own manuals.

## How this series will mark a thin trail

If I have not opened the page, I will write `[VERIFY]`. If two obituaries disagree on a year, both years go on the table. If a living person is in the sentence, the source is a book, a journal, or a public lecture — not a rumor and not a scraped headshot.

Garry Landreth died on 11 June 2026. That is a University of North Texas public notice. It is not an invitation to turn an obituary into an endorsement. Charles Schaefer died on 19 September 2020. The Dignity Memorial notice and the APT emeritus page are the public objects. The phrase "father of play therapy" appears in those rooms as marketing. This series will quote the phrase as a myth, not wear it.

## A last habit

Read the claims box at the bottom of every draft even if you skip the middle. The box is not decoration. It is the same fence in a type size a tired parent can see. If a draft ever ships without it, the draft has failed before the facts are even argued.

You do not owe this folder a child. You do not owe it a booking. You owe a history sentence the same thing you owe a newspaper: a date, a name, and a refusal to let the verb get ahead of the document.
