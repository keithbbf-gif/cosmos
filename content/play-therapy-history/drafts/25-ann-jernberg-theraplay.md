**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Ann M. Jernberg published *Theraplay: A New Treatment Using Structured Play for Problem Children and Their Families* with Jossey-Bass in 1979. The subtitle is a period object. "Problem children" is 1979 clinic English. The book named a method in which the adult is active, structured, and physically in the play — a different posture from Axline's 1947 refusal to steer.

She sat on the Association for Play Therapy's first board after 1982. That membership fact is why Theraplay is inside this series's profession story and not only a side brand. The later Theraplay Institute is a public organization with its own training economy. This page will not teach dimensions, games, or a "nurture" exercise. It will keep 1979 as a book date.

## Structured, and therefore easy to steal

A method that looks like games will be stolen by blogs. Theraplay's public face is especially stealable: grown-ups and children in motion, a vocabulary of structure and engagement that sounds like parenting advice. The historical fact is that it was a clinic method with training, not a park. This series will not help the steal.

## What Theraplay is not

It is not CCPT. It is not filial therapy, though both involve parents more visibly than a back-room child hour. It is not "just playing." It is not a license to roughhouse without a trained person and a consent process. Physical play in a clinic has a risk history this page will name without writing a safety protocol: bodies can harm, and a method that uses bodies has to answer that. The answer belongs in a training, not in a heritage essay.
