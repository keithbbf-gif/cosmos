**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1992 the Association for Play Therapy began publishing the *International Journal of Play Therapy*. Later Association lore names Kevin O'Connor's executive-director years and Cynthia K. Bromberg's guest-editor labor on the first issue; confirm those names on the masthead you can hold. The journal started semiannual and later became quarterly. APA eventually archived issues. Those are library facts.

A field that has a journal can assign a volume number in a dissertation. A field that has only books and newsletters looks, to a tenure committee, like a club. 1992 is the year the club bought a binding machine.

## What a society journal can do

It can print outcome studies, case essays, theory, and conference leftovers. It can make CCPT, filial, and sandplay look like neighbors because they share a cover. It can be indexed, later, so a 2005 meta-analysis has something to count.

It cannot make a case essay into a trial. It cannot make "international" mean the same thing as a British registration fight. Early issues were international in aspiration and American in address. That is an ordinary society-journal story. Name it.

## Semiannual, then quarterly, then a pile

Frequency is a budget. Quarterly means more pages, more reviewers, more pressure to fill. Pressure to fill is how a journal becomes both a science and a newsletter. Readers should watch the article type. A historical essay in *IJPT* is not a randomized trial in *IJPT*. Both can be useful. Only one belongs in a "research shows" sentence, and even then only with a sample.

Bratton, Ray, Rhine, and Jones (2005) published in *Professional Psychology*, not in *IJPT*. That is a placement fact. A specialty journal talks to the specialty. A general professional journal talks to psychology boards. The 2005 paper wanted the second room. The later evidence essay stays there. This essay stays with the 1992 object that made the first room bindable.

## What not to do with a volume number

Do not cite "the journal" as if it were a finding. Do not scrape PDFs you do not have rights to put on a practice blog. Do not treat an *IJPT* case as a child you may retell.

## Why a small-city clinician still meets 1992

Continuing-education flyers quote journals. Students quote journals. A parent never quotes a journal and still lives inside the prestige a journal bought. Prestige is not a protocol. It is why "play therapist" started to sound like a job a university could explain to a provost.

The next essay leaves the American binding machine for Britain, where 1992 meant a different association and, later, a different argument about who may use the name.
