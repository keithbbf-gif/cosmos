**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

American school counselors in the 1960s already wanted a method that did not look like a lecture. Articles associated with names like Alexander (1964), Muro (1968), Myrick and Holdin (1971), Nelson (1966), and Waterland (1970) — a cluster later surveys list — treated play as an educational and preventive tool inside the school. Open the paper you cite. This page will not pretend to have sat in each archive.

The patron is the district, not the parent fee, not the Association for Play Therapy (which did not yet exist). The room is often a closet. The clock is a bell. Those three facts change the method even when the textbook on the shelf is Axline.

## A different ethics, a different note

A school note can be read by a principal. An outpatient note can be read by an insurer. Both can be read by a parent, in theory. In practice the school hour is more public. Confidentiality is a lease with more keys. This series will not write a FERPA protocol. It will say that school-based play therapy inherited a building that was never designed for a one-way-glass clinic.

Later CCPT school studies in the 2000s — Dee Ray and colleagues among the public names — tried to make the closet countable. Those papers are why a 2005 meta-analysis could include school samples. They are also why a district can now ask for an effect size the way it asks for a reading score. Asking is a political event. The papers are historical objects. Cite the one you open. Do not invent a count.

## Prevention, and the child who did not ask to be a case

School play therapy often sells itself as early, short, and kind. Sometimes it is. Sometimes it is how a building medicalizes a noisy child without hiring enough adults. A history page can hold both sentences. A brochure cannot.

SLPWOW's school-SLP history is a different job in the same hallway. Do not merge them. A speech-language pathologist and a school counselor may share a child and not share a method.

## What not to do with a school essay

Do not publish a classroom guidance lesson. Do not tell a teacher to "just play." Do not photograph children in a counseling closet. Do not treat a 2000s Texas study as a 1964 Alexander paper.
