**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Garry Lee Landreth joined the North Texas education faculty in 1966, sat on the first Association for Play Therapy board after 1982, opened the Center for Play Therapy at the University of North Texas in 1988, directed it until 2003, published *Play Therapy: The Art of the Relationship* in 1991, and died on 11 June 2026. The death is a University of North Texas public notice. This page will not embroider it. It will not scrape a funeral photograph. It will stay with the public objects: a center, a book, a board.

UNT's own "Our Story" page is the institutional timeline to argue with. A first international play-therapy conference on that campus in 1973. A 1987 departmental vote, asked for by a vice president who wanted national goals, to make play therapy a focus. A 1988 center with a doctoral assistant. Those are budget sentences as much as theory sentences.

## A textbook that could be assigned

Axline 1947 is a book. Landreth 1991 is a book that behaves like a course. Later editions (2002, 2012) kept the subtitle and thickened the research chapters. Counseling programs assigned it because it is organized, filmed, and American. Child-centered play therapy (CCPT) as a *named* school in the late twentieth century runs through this object more than through 1947.

The book describes a room. This series will not republish the room. It will say that a 1991 textbook made a room teachable, and that teachability is how a method becomes a study and a credential.

He also wrote, with colleagues, about filial work and a shorter filial format that later trainings sold as a model with a session count. The count is a later object's problem. Guerney 1964 is the filial origin essay in this pack. Landreth's filial writing is a Denton descendant. Do not collapse them.

## APT, and a Texas center that became a pilgrimage

The Association's first board included him. Director Emeritus is an APT honorific on their page. The Center in Denton became, in the field's own marketing, a place people flew to in summer. Workshop economies are historical. They are not proof. They are how a university town becomes a capital.

A small-city clinician who has only the book, not the summer, is not a failed pilgrim. They are the more common case. The book traveled farther than the campus.

## Living extra care, then a death notice

Until June 2026 he was a living person in this pack's first outlines. The rule does not loosen because an obituary exists. No health speculation. No private life. Public books, the UNT page, the APT emeritus page. Monica Landreth appears in the university notice as a spouse of sixty-four years and a scholarship name. That is enough.
