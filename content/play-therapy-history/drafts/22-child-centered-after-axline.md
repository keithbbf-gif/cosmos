**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

"Child-centered play therapy" is a later name for a stance Axline wrote in 1947 without that exact brand. The initials CCPT belong to textbooks, dissertations, and grant applications. They do not belong to Houghton Mifflin. This essay is about the name becoming a school: films, manuals, outcome studies, and a habit of talking as if one hour were the whole of play therapy.

It is not a how-to. It is a sociology of a label.

## From stance to initials

Axline described a non-directive hour. Moustakas described a living relationship. Landreth, in 1991, described a course you could teach. Students needed a short name. CCPT is the short name that stuck in American counselor education.

Short names do two jobs. They let a meta-analysis put papers in a pile (Bratton, Ray, Rhine, and Jones, 2005, had to decide what counted). They also let a workshop sell a weekend as if the initials were a complete training. The pile is a research decision. The weekend is a market decision. This series will not confuse them.

Dee Ray and colleagues at UNT and elsewhere spent the 2000s publishing school-based CCPT outcome papers. Those papers are later objects. Cite the one you open. Do not invent a count. The school-based essay in this pack stays with the building. This essay stays with the label.

## Films, and the hour you can rewind

UNT and other centers made recordings. A method that can be filmed can be coded. A method that can be coded can be researched. A method that can be researched can be sold as "evidence-based" in a flyer that skips the sample. The film library is a historical actor. It is also a temptation to treat the filmed hour as the only real hour.

Private-practice hours that are not filmed still happen. They are harder to put in a journal. The evidence fights essay returns to that.

## What CCPT is not

It is not Klein. It is not sandplay. It is not Theraplay. It is not filial therapy, though filial work often trains parents in a child-centered *special hour* — a different object with a 1964 paper. It is not "play therapy," the umbrella APT uses for a conference grid.

A parent who hears "we do play therapy" and a student who hears "we do CCPT" may think they heard the same sentence. They did not.

## Why the label spread

It spread because it is teachable, because it fits a fifty-minute university clinic, and because it does not require a British Society vote. It spread because Axline's prose was already in the libraries. It spread because Landreth's book told a program how to look like a program.

It did not spread because 1947 ended the argument about interpretation. The argument moved. Some trauma trainings returned to more structured play. Some analytic institutes never left interpretation. CCPT became the American counseling default, not the human default.
