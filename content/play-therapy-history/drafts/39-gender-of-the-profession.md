**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Walk an APT conference hallway in the 1990s or 2010s and you will see a workforce that looks like American school counseling and social work: mostly women, a smaller number of men at the microphone. Then read the origin slides: Schaefer, O'Connor, Landreth, Guerney — and, if the slide is careful, Axline, Jernberg, Louise Guerney. The hallway and the slide are both true. The tension is the history.

This essay will not compliment women for sitting on the floor. Sitting on the floor is work. Work has pay. Play therapy's public story often treats the floor as vocation and the handbook as science. Vocation is a word that has been used to underpay.

## Who got the "father" sentence

Schaefer's obituaries used "father of play therapy." The phrase is a gift the field keeps giving to organizing men. Hug-Hellmuth, Anna Freud, Klein, Lowenfeld, Axline, Oaklander, Jernberg, Louise Guerney, Gil: the method papers and the trade books are not a men's club. The *society* that turned the method into a noun in 1982 had men as co-founders and women on the first board. Both facts belong on the same page.

A "mother of" sentence is not the repair. This series banned both crowns unless quoting a myth.

## Hours, supervision, and the toll

RPT hours assume a person can sit extra time after a caseload. Extra time is gendered in a profession that already leans female and already leans underpaid. University certificates assume a semester. Semesters assume childcare. None of that is a reason to abolish hours. It is a reason not to treat hours as a pure quality signal.

Filial work, which puts parents in the method, also puts a household's gendered labor on the table. The 1964 paper did not have to say "mother." American clinics often heard "mother" anyway. A history can name the hearing.

## Men in the room, and the other stereotype

A man who does play therapy meets a different suspicion in some waiting rooms and a different prestige in some university departments. Neither stereotype is a finding. Both are reasons a workforce census cannot be a compliment to one sex.

Children's hours also meet gendered toys and gendered interpretations. A truck is not a destiny. A doll is not a destiny. Clinicians who forgot that were writing the catalog, not the child. The toys essay said the receipt. This paragraph says the stereotype that came free with the receipt.

## What not to do with a gender essay

Do not write "women are naturally better with children." Do not write a recruitment ad. Do not publish a pay-gap protocol. Do not use this URL to sell a women's-leadership workshop.

## Why a small-city clinic already knows the hallway

The person who answers the phone, the person who sits on the floor, and the person whose name is on the handbook are not always the same person. WOW Therapies, as a Southeast Arkansas brand, already lives in a region that knows unpaid care. This history page will not pretend Denton invented the floor. It will pretend, if it is not careful, that the microphone invented the field. The microphone did not.

The next essay is the evidence fight: what happens when a majority-women, floor-sitting workforce is asked for a graph by a profession that still likes a white coat.
