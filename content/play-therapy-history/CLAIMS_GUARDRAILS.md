# Claims guardrails — play therapy history pack

Educational history only. These sentences must never go on wowtherapies.com, in ads, or in schema markup.

This file is a refuse list. If a draft drifts toward any line below, cut the line. Do not "soften" it into a how-to.

## Never say (treatment / DIY)

- Any instruction a reader could follow as a session: Axline's eight principles reprinted as a checklist, Landreth "be with" attitudes as a script, tracking/reflecting formulas, limit-setting sequences, "set the toys out like this."
- Toy inventories presented as a shopping list or a room-build ("you will need a dollhouse, sand, aggressive toys…"). A historian may name that a 1991 textbook *published* a room list. The historian may not republish the list as advice.
- Filial, Theraplay, CCPT, Adlerian, Gestalt, Jungian, or sandplay steps. No "try a special play time at home." No "ask yourself these ten questions after the session."
- Sand-tray scene interpretation. No "if the child buries the figure, it means…"
- "Try this at home," "use this worksheet," "self-treat," "you can play-therapy your way out of…"
- Dosing of psychiatric medication, taper advice, or "ask your doctor about [drug]" as content.
- "This method is the gold standard for [diagnosis]."
- "Studies prove play therapy works better than [school]" without a named trial, a dated review, **and** a sentence about limits.
- Crisis instructions that replace emergency services. A single line — "If you are in danger, call local emergency services" — is enough. Do not write a child-safety protocol, a mandated-reporter flowchart, or a suicide-assessment script in these essays.

## Never say (medical / legal)

- That WOW Therapies diagnoses, treats, or "specializes in" a DSM/ICD condition **in these history posts**. Service pages are a different file and a different review.
- That reading an essay is therapy, counseling, play therapy, or a substitute for a licensed clinician.
- PHI, composite child patients, or "a child once told me" stories. No invented case vignettes with identifying color. *Dibs* is a published book; treat it as a 1964 public object, not as a chart.
- Guarantees of outcome.
- Anything a reasonable reader would hear as a diagnosis of themselves or their child.
- Advice that a parent should start "home play sessions" instead of seeking care.

## Never say (history that is too neat)

- "Schaefer invented play therapy" or an unquoted "father of play therapy." The phrase appears in obituaries and marketing. Quote the myth. The 1921 Hug-Hellmuth paper, Klein 1932, Anna Freud 1927, Lowenfeld 1935, and Axline 1947 were already on the shelf.
- "Axline discovered that children heal through play." She wrote a 1947 book that applied Rogers to a playroom. Children played long before Columbus, Ohio.
- "Klein proved the infant is sadistic" or "Anna Freud proved Klein was wrong." Name the Controversial Discussions (1941–45) as a dated quarrel. Leave the reader's judgment intact.
- Refrigerator-mother, schizophrenogenic-mother, or Bettelheim's autism claims as fact. If they appear, they appear as harm.
- "Attachment style is your child's destiny" or pop-psychology play quizzes.
- Conversion-therapy "success," including any "play" used to change gender or sexuality. Name the harm. Do not teach the method.
- That a meta-analysis "ended the debate." Bratton et al. 2005 is a historical event. It did not end argument.
- That APT credentials are a license. They are association credentials. Licensure is a state object.

## Never say (brand / SEO)

- "Best play therapist in Arkansas" or any superlative about the practice.
- Disease keywords paired with a CTA ("overcome ADHD today — book now") on these URLs.
- Schema `MedicalTherapy`, `Drug`, or `treats` / `indication` on history posts. `Article` is enough.
- Fake author credentials (invented RPT-S, "our clinical director Dr. …" who is not on the license wall).
- Patient testimonials in this folder.
- Photographs of children in therapy, stock "smiling child in sand," or generated children.

## Allowed

- Dated description of what a school claimed, who paid for it, who dissented, and what later researchers revised.
- Named trials and reviews as historical events (Guerney 1964; Bratton, Ray, Rhine, and Jones 2005; later school-based CCPT studies as *named papers*).
- Controversy, including class, race, whose toys count as "the" playroom, and the gender of the workforce.
- A short "why a contemporary clinician still reads this" closing that does **not** become a protocol.
- The educational footer and claims box already in each draft.

## Living people

Eliana Gil, Sue Bratton, Dee Ray, Kevin O'Connor, Rise VanFleet, Terry Kottman, and others who were alive at last verification (2026-09-14) are not public property. No health speculation. No unsourced private life. Public books, public lectures, and public interviews only.

Garry Landreth died 11 June 2026 (University of North Texas public notice). Do not embroider the death. Do not turn an obituary into a treatment endorsement.

## If asked for both a history and a how-to

Print the history. Refuse the how-to in one sentence: this series does not publish treatment protocols.
