# Midjourney and the productization of taste

I do not have a Midjourney methods paper that I can footnote like Flamingo. That absence is the draft.

Midjourney, in public, is a **look and a place**. The place, for the years that matter here, is Discord: a chat window where you type `/imagine` and a grid of pictures comes back, and you vary, and you upscale, and other people’s prompts scroll past like a ticker of desire. The look is harder to put in a citation. Saturated, cinematic, a little holy, a little magazine. People said “it looks like Midjourney” the way they once said “it looks like Instagram.” That sentence is a historical fact even when the weights are not.

This is the productization of taste. Not “we published a UNet.” We published a *default*. Defaults are multimodal objects. They decide whether “a knight” comes out muddy-photoreal or plated-and-fogged. They decide whether faces are beautiful in a way that is one ethnicity. They decide whether the model has a god-ray habit. A paper that reports FID does not have a god-ray habit. A product does.

I will not guess their stack. People guessed anyway — diffusion, of course, in the years when everything was; later, maybe other things; always maybe. Guessing is not a source. What I can source is the **interface**: prompt, parameters as chat flags, a community feed, a subscription. What I can source is the **social role**: Midjourney was, for a lot of civilians, the first time they *made* a generated picture rather than saw one. DALL·E 2 had a waitlist and a brand. Stable Diffusion had a GPU requirement. Midjourney had a server you could join if you could stand Discord.

The feed is underestimated. Seeing other prompts is a training set for humans. It is how promptcraft became a craft instead of a private notebook. It is also how a look becomes contagious. You watch a prompt that says `cinematic lighting, 35mm, award winning` do well, and you copy the incantation. The model did not have to be open for the *culture* to be open. Midjourney opened the culture and closed the file. Stable Diffusion opened the file and let the culture fragment into a thousand LoRAs. Both are 2022. They are not the same public.

Taste as a product has a vice: it hides the joint. Users do not think about cross-attention. They think about vibes. That is allowed — photographers do not think about Bayer patterns every Tuesday — but it makes historiography harder. A Midjourney version number (v3, v4, v5…) is a public event with almost no public mechanism. I can write that v4 looked different from v3 because everyone said so and the pictures are still around. I cannot write why, not without leaving this folder’s rules.

Legal and labor fights attached to the look: style, living artists, “in the style of.” Those fights attached to Stable Diffusion too, but Midjourney’s look made the attachment emotional. A default that is *beautiful* is easier to accuse of theft than a default that is muddy. I note the attachment. I do not try the case.

A human thing. Discord as an IDE is a joke until you watch someone iterate twenty times in a thread with their friends roasting the hands. That is closer to how pictures have always been made — in rooms, with commentary — than a Colab notebook is. The research stack of 2021 lived in notebooks. The product stack of 2022 lived in channels. If you only read papers, you will miss where the verb *imagine* actually sat.

I keep Midjourney in a series about CLIP and diffusion and vision–language because it is the reception that made those words civilian. People who cannot define contrastive loss can recognize the look. That recognition is part of the public record. The next draft stays in the channels and the repos: promptcraft as folk practice, the incantations, the negative prompts, the day “trending on ArtStation” became a token.
