# SD3 and FLUX: later public lines, no origin myth

Esser, Kulal, Blattmann, Entezari, Müller, Saini,
Levi, Lorenz, Sauer, Boesel, Podell, Dockhorn,
English, Lacey, Goodwin, Marek, and Rombach's
*Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for
High-Resolution Image Synthesis* (arXiv:2403.03206)
is the 2024 paper commonly attached to Stable
Diffusion 3. Multimodal diffusion transformers
(MMDiT). Rectified flow. Multiple text encoders.
A scaling story. Weights and APIs then appeared
in a messier public sequence than the 22 August
2022 dump — including access debates that are
also part of the record. Cite the paper for the
method. Cite the specific checkpoint card for
the file you actually ran.

Black Forest Labs' FLUX.1 family (announced 2024)
is the other named open-ish line a 2025 reader
will meet in UIs. The lab's public posts and
model cards are the primary pages. Several
people named on the SDXL/LDM lineage appear in
this later company. That continuity is
journalistic and citable. It is not a secret
history, and this set will not turn it into
one.

How to read 2024 without making a new myth:

- **Do not declare a final architecture.** MMDiT
  plus flow is a live default, not the end of
  image generation.
- **Do not collapse licenses.** "Open" in 2024
  meant a spread of non-commercial weights,
  API-only tiers, and truly permissively
  licensed smaller variants. Read the card.
- **Do not pretend v1.4 is obsolete as
  history.** The 2024 lines are replies to a
  culture the 2022 file created.

I am deliberately thinner here than in the 2022
drafts. The public record is still settling.
Quality, in this folder, includes **refusing to
narrate a last six months as if it were already
a textbook chapter**. The assignment is a
history of the public multimodal stack, not a
buyer's guide.

If you need a hinge sentence: by 2024 the open
image generator was no longer only "a UNet in
CLIP's shadow." It was a family of transformer
denoisers and flow models, still text-conditioned,
still argued over in UIs, still dependent on
data that the 2022 commons made thinkable.
