The panel was flat at the ends and hungry in the middle. I had plenty of bars. I had no caul. The bars did what bars do: they bit the edges and left the field to fend for itself. The next morning I could rock a sheet of paper under the center seam. The paper was the review.

A caul is a stick you trust more than a jaw. You put it across the work, sometimes with a little crown — a smile toward the panel — so that when the clamps bite the ends of the caul, the middle comes down first. A flat caul on a slightly hollow stack presses the ends and leaves the middle a rumor. I have both mistakes in my past. I keep a pair of cauls that I have already jointed and crowned. I do not invent them during open time.

I invented one during open time. A 2x from the rack, a saw cut, a hope. The 2x had a cup of its own and a sticker stain. I closed the panel on that cup. The next morning the field had taken the 2x’s weather. I jointed the panel true and I jointed a pair of cauls that same week. They live on the wall now, waxed, labeled, too ugly to steal for a cleat. Open time is not a mill.

## Crown is a direction

The smile goes toward the work. I have flipped a caul in a hurry and put a frown on a panel. The frown makes two high spots and a valley. The valley is a starved line you will find with a scraper.

The flip was a Tuesday with hide on pecan and a clock I had already spent walking. I grabbed the caul the way it came off the wall. The smile went at the ceiling. I cranked the ends. The middle rose. I saw it as I wiped and I told myself the bars would settle it. The bars settled the ends. The scraper found the valley after the clamps came off, a dull line that would not take oil the way the rest of the top took oil. I recut that seam. I paint a crude arrow on the caul now, smile-side, so a hurry cannot invent a frown.

How much crown: enough that the middle kisses before the ends, not so much that you need a hero clamp to close the ends. I will not engrave an eighth of an inch on a wall. `[VERIFY]` against the cauls we actually jointed. The light and a straightedge are the priest.

I check the crown against a straightedge when I pull the pair down. Wood moves. A caul that was honest in January can flatten or go the other way after a wet August on the wall. I have glued a panel under a caul I had not looked at since spring. The smile had become a rumor. I joint them when they lie.

## Wax, tape, the glue that wants the caul

I wax the face or I put packing tape on it. Hide will glue a caul to a panel like a bad marriage. PVA will too if you smear. A sheet of wax paper is allowed. A sheet of newspaper is how you print the news into the finish.

I glued a caul to a walnut top with hide because I had waxed last year and trusted last year. The caul came off with a sound I still hear. It took a stripe of fiber. I scraped, I sanded, I lived with a shadow under oil. I wax the night before now, or I tape that morning. I do not trust a memory of wax.

On a veneer stack the caul *is* the press. Flat, clean, no breadcrumb. See urea. See vacuum. A veneer caul with a dent is a dent for life.

I left a dried glue crumb on a caul and I bagged a stack anyway. The crumb is still in that panel as a nipple you can find with your palm in the dark. I scrape the caul. I run a hand. A hammer scar on a caul becomes a valley in every leaf you press. I retire a scarred caul to the stove pile or I recut its face.

## Not only panels

A hopper needs sloped cauls or the bars skate downhill. A chair seat lamination wants cauls that are the shape, not a hope. A breadboard wants a caul that will not dish the end of the table. I cut the caul for the job when the job is not a rectangle. I do not borrow a panel caul and call it close.

The hopper was a bin I still use. I clamped it with bars and a flat stick. The bars walked down the slope while I reached for a pad. The corners opened. I made two sloped cauls with a stop on the downhill end so the bar has a wall. The second glue-up stayed a hopper. The first one stayed a lesson I can pick up by the rim.

I borrowed a panel caul for a breadboard once because the length was close. The caul was crowned for a field. It kissed the middle of the breadboard and left the ends of the tongue a rumor. I dished the end of a table I then had to scrape for an hour. The breadboard caul I cut later is flatter and wider and it gets its own place on the wall.

## Staging them before the bottle

The dry-fit includes the cauls. I set them, I throw the bars, I see whether the smile lands where the seam is hungry. Then I take the iron off and I open the bottle. A caul you invent after the first cheek is wet is the 2x from the rack again. I set them on the bench, waxed face up, before the brush comes out. The walk to the wall is closed time you can spend skinning a film.

## The judgment

If the bar cannot reach the middle, the caul is the clamp. Stage it in the dry-fit. Wax it. Point the smile at the work. The paper under the seam was a cheap lesson. The dining top I would have spoiled would not have been.
