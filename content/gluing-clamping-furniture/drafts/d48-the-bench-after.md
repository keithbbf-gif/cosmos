I hung the last pipe and left the yellow cap off because I was going to “use it again after lunch.” Lunch became a week. The nozzle was a yellow horn. See `d09`. The bench after is not housekeeping. It is the first move of the next rehearsal.

Cap. Date if I opened a new bottle. Hide brush washed and hanging, or standing in water I will actually change. Pot lidded, or dumped if it was the end of the hide day. Roller peeled before it becomes a sculpture. Pads scraped so they do not print yesterday’s bead onto tomorrow’s rail. Wax paper in the trash, not under a panel I will forget.

## Iron

Clamps hung where I will not hunt them. Pads back in the can. Tape on pipes checked. A parallel that is still smeared is a parallel that will print. I wipe the jaws.

## The piece

Stickers. Off steel. No tote. Tape with the pull time and the machine time still on the clamp or on the work. I have lost a tape and then guessed. Guessing is a shiny streak.

## The floor

A puddle of PVA is a skate. Hide on the floor is a brown glass. I scrape now. I have skated.

## Lunch became a week

I cut the horn and I still fought a crust in the cap for the next panel. I have opened a bottle in March and found it in August with no date and a smell I did not trust. The bottle went in the trash. The joint I did not glue with it was cheaper than the joint I would have glued with a liar.

Water I forget is a science project. A pot left open overnight is a skin I will try to “refresh” and then blame when a chair rail gels wrong. I have let a roller sit “until I clean up.” The roller became a fossil. I have printed a cured half-moon of PVA onto a cherry arris because the pad looked clean enough. It was not. Forgotten wax paper is a sticker that is not a sticker. See `d39`.

## The iron, hung like I will need it

Hunting a parallel in open time is how a shoulder skins. See `d16`. I have skipped the jaw wipe and found a grid in poplar the next week. See `d39`. Tape on pipes checked so tomorrow’s cherry does not get a bracelet. See `d15`.

I do not leave pipes on the floor as a trip and a rust farm. I do not leave a band clamp in a knot that I will spend ten minutes picking apart while hide is on a cheek. The band goes loose on its hook. The handscrews go where the handscrews live. A shop that cannot find a handscrew is a shop that will hang a diagonal bar because the bar was closer. See `d38`.

## The tape that came off on a sleeve

I have lost a tape — it came off on my sleeve — and then I guessed. Guessing is a shiny streak. See `d41`. I write the tape twice if the piece is a table I cannot replace. Once on the bar, once on the underside.

I look at the piece once more before I walk. A shoulder that opened. A pad that walked. A bead I can still lift. Then I leave it. The bench has to be able to receive the next clock.

Hide on the floor is a brown glass that will take a boot and then take a finish rag I set down without looking. I do not save the puddle for the end of the day. A cup that is still a cup belongs on a board, not near the square I will grab with a clean hand. See `d47`.

## The square under the roller

I have hunted the glue-up square and found it under a fossilized roller. See `d37`. The next case waited while I washed a thing I should have peeled at lunch. I hang the square back on the nail that is only for that square. I put the roller in the trash if it has already become art.

A date written in wet glue on a bottle will wipe off on my hand. I write the date on tape, on the bottle, after the cap is on. I have trusted a smear of yellow numerals and then could not read them in August. The horn was one liar. The smear-date was another.

## The judgment

The next glue-up starts in the mess you left. Leave a shop that can rehearse. The yellow horn and the skate are why this page is the last stage, not a postscript. Caps, then quiet. The iron hangs. The piece sits. I go eat lunch with the bottle closed.
