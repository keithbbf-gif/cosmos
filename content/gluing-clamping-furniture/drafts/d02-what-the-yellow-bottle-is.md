The yellow bottle had sat in the truck overnight. January. I squeezed and got a cough of rubbery rope that would not wet a cheek. I blamed the brand. The brand was fine. The bottle had lived a winter.

What people call “carpenter’s glue” is polyvinyl acetate with extras. The extras are why one bottle is interior-only, one will take a kitchen wipe, and one will sit in a damp porch swing a little longer before it lets go. None of them is hide. None of them is epoxy. Calling them all “wood glue” is how you glue a breadboard with the wrong clock and then act surprised when August moves the pin.

## Three jobs, one family

The interior aliphatic — the old yellow — is a furniture glue if the piece lives inside and you can stand a glue line that is not reversible. It grabs. It sands. It creeps less than the white school stuff and more than a well-run hide line. I use it on casework that will never see a mop.

The water-resistant cousin is the one shops reach for when a customer says “kitchen.” Water-resistant is not waterproof. It is a class on a label. A dining table that gets wiped is not a sink. A sink-adjacent stool is not a boat. I will use the water-resistant bottle on a table base I expect to see wet rags. I will not use it on a garden bench and then talk about craft.

The darker, longer-open cousin is the one people buy because a video said it was “stronger.” Stronger than what. It is often a little more flexible, a little more water-tolerant, and slower in the minute you need for a big glue-up. Flexibility is creep with a nicer name if the joint is a fat line under rack. I like it when I am alone with a table base and I need the clock. I do not like it as a personality transplant for every joint in the shop.

Read the sheet on the bottle you opened. Open time, clamp time, lowest application temperature — those numbers move by lot and by brand. `[VERIFY]` them that morning. Do not tattoo last year’s internet on the wall.

## What PVA will not do for you

It will not let go cleanly in twenty years. Steam and patience will wreck more wood than they save. If the piece is a chair you expect to repair, that is a hide argument, and the craft pack already made it. This pack assumes you already heard it.

PVA will not like a shop under about fifty degrees. The film will not form the way the sheet promised. I have glued in a cold lean-to because the table “had to go.” The joint looked closed. It opened on the first humid week like it had been waiting.

PVA will not fill a gap and stay a glass. It stays a plastic. A thick PVA line under a table apron is a slow hinge. The apron racks. The line shears. You blame the bottle. The bottle was asked to be a tenon.

## Cleanup is chemistry, not virtue

Wet PVA comes off with a damp rag if you are paying attention. Gel-state PVA comes off with a chisel if you wait. Smeared PVA in pores is a finish problem you cannot sand out of cherry. I have sent a smear to the finish room. The finish room sent it back with a note I did not need written down.

The glue line itself, once cured, is a different color than the wood. Under oil it can flash. Under film it can telegraph. A thin line in a well-fit edge is almost invisible. A starved, thick, or smeared line is a stripe. The stripe is not “character.”

## A shop with more than one bottle

I keep more than one PVA on the shelf and I write the open date on the shoulder with a marker. I do not run a bottle two years because it “still comes out.” If it smells sharp, if it has livered, if it has frozen, it is a donation to the trash. Glue is cheaper than a dining top.

I do not mix brands in one glue-up to “use up the old.” One clock. One film. One cleanup. Mixing a fast interior with a slow dark cousin on opposite ends of a case is how you get two corners that set while you are still spreading the third.

## The judgment I will stand on

PVA is the twentieth century’s honest furniture glue for work that does not need to come apart. It is not a moral failure. It is also not a hide pot, not an epoxy, and not a license to leave daylight in a shoulder. Pick the grade for the water and the clock. Write the date on the bottle. Recut the edge that needs a hero clamp. The yellow cap is a family. It is not a personality.
