The seam felt tacky on the third day. Not wet — skin-tack, the kind that picks up dust. August had been a sponge. I had treated the sheet’s “24 hours” like a law. The law was written in a drier county.

South Arkansas air is in the film. PVA loses water into the wood and the room. If the room is already full, the film waits. Hide dries into a glass when it can throw water. A swamp week is a longer glass. I do not invent an RH number I did not read. `[VERIFY]` if we start logging. I invent the habit: wait longer, sticker, do not load.

## Dehumidifier is a clamp

If we run one, we run it because the work is a table, not because the shop is a lab. A closed wet shop is a cure you cannot hurry with hope. A fan that only stirs hot air is comfort.

## Truck day

A green joint on a trailer is a rack and a creep. I do not ship a chair the morning I pulled clamps in a wet week. The customer can wait. The parallelogram cannot be undone at their house.

## The calendar is not a hygrometer

I have machined a tacky seam because the calendar said I could. The cutter smeared. The smear was a pale county that oil later introduced. See *Unclamp is not machine* (essay 41) and *The scar under oil* (essay 43). My finger is the meter I actually own. If the seam picks up dust, it is not a board. I do not invent an RH number I did not read.

Bradley County air in a 65-foot shop with glass on the long wall can feel like a cloth for a week. The sheet’s hours were written in a drier county. I wait longer than I want to. I sticker. I do not load.

## A fan that only stirs

I have pointed a fan at a panel and felt virtuous. The panel stayed tacky. The fan had moved the same wet air over the same wet film. I do not stack two green tops to “save space.” I have stacked. The lower top stayed a gel in the field and skinned at the edges, a doughnut of cure. See *Softwood crush, hardwood cup* (essay 39).

Winter is the other humidity story. A dry heated room can take water out of a panel too fast on one face if that face is toward a vent. I do not set a green top in front of a heater and call it a kiln. Stickers. Both sides. Shade from the glass stripe. See *Winter skin, August flash* (essay 29).

## Friday after a Thursday storm

I have promised Friday when Thursday was a storm. Friday I had a dusty skin-tack seam and a story. The story was not furniture. The road will twist a chair that is still a gel and the chair will keep a little of the twist. I do not invent the person waiting. I invent the refusal.

Hide in a swamp week will still grab a joint and still not be a glass. I stop poking. I do not scrape a hide line to “see if it’s ready” and open a shoulder I had closed. PVA will stay a plastic skin longer than my patience. Epoxy cares less about the room’s water and still cares about the room’s heat. `[VERIFY]` that day’s sheet. Urea in a bag wants to be left alone in any week. I do not open the bag to check.

## The door and the cloth air

I have closed the long-wall door against a storm and then wondered why a PVA panel still felt like skin on day three. The room was a cup. Opening the door after the rain is not toughness. It is letting the film throw water into air that can take it. A dehumidifier, if we run one, is for a table I cannot replace, not for the feeling of running a lab.

Stickers in a wet week can become moisture brands if they are not dry. I have pulled a panel off sticks and found pale ribs where the sticks sat, the field still tacky between them. Dry sticks, both faces, enough air that the seams are not a swamp with a lid.

Hide in this air will grab and still feel like leather instead of glass. I do not test it by picking at the bead. I test it by leaving it alone until a fingernail on a squeeze-bead I already planned to scrape does not drag a string. If it strings, it is not a board. I do not invent a number for that. I invent another day.

## The judgment

The sheet is a climate. Our shop is another. Believe the tack. Wait. The dusty skin-tack seam is why I stopped promising Friday when Thursday was a storm.
