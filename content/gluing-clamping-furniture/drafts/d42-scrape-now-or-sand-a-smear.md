I sent a drawer to the finish room with a ridge in the corner. The ridge cut a knuckle. The finish room sent the drawer back. I was the finish room. I sent it back to myself.

Now is the gel. Now is the inside corner. Now is the profile before hide turns to amber glass. Later is a scraper on a finished arris and a swear.

## Lift, do not smear

See `d27`. The aftermath version is: if you missed the gel, scrape. A card scraper on a flat. A chisel in a corner. Sanding a smear on oak is how you dish the rays and still leave plastic in the deep grain.

## Hide vs PVA vs epoxy

Hide: heat and a damp rag if you caught it, scrape if you did not. PVA: chisel and patience. Epoxy: chisel and the knowledge that you are carving a thermoset. Do not mix the cleanup rags. A hide rag in epoxy is a sculpture.

## Profiles and insides

A table apron with a bead: the bead is where glue hides and where a scraper will flatten a detail you spent an hour cutting. I use a stick with a matching hollow, or a rag on a dowel, or I get the gel bead out with a corner of a card before it is glass. Inside a case, a smear on a dado is a drawer that clicks. Inside a mortise leftover skin is a thickness. I look with a light. I do not assume the wipe I did at minute two finished the job.

## The sanding lie

People sand a smear because sanding feels like progress. On oak you dish the earlywood and leave plastic in the latewood. On cherry you burnish a pale patch. On maple you make a shiny county that will not take dye. A scraper is flatter and meaner and better. If I must sand, I sand a larger field so I do not leave a dish, and I still expect oil to tell on me.

## The ridge that cut a knuckle

The drawer had gone together pretty. I had wiped the faces. I had not gotten my head in the bay with a light. A ridge of PVA had set in the inside corner, hard as a piece of wire. I found it with a knuckle while I was fitting the runner. Minute two is when I am still proud of the bead. The light is when I am not.

I look in the dado. I look in the mortise leftover. A skin in a mortise is a thickness you did not cut. A smear on a dado is a drawer that clicks. I have sent a case to finish with a smear on the inside of a stile because I had looked at the show face. The show face was honest. The inside was a map. I am the person who opens that door later. I scrape the inside now.

## The quirk I flattened

I have flattened a table-apron bead with a scraper because the glue had become glass in the quirk and I treated the quirk like a flat. The bead went shy. I recut the profile on the next apron and I got the gel out with a corner of a card before it hardened. A stick with a matching hollow is slower than a card on a flat and kinder to a detail you spent an hour cutting.

A PVA rag used as a hide rag is how you paint plastic into a joint you meant to keep reversible. The pot essay already taught the damp heat. I am not writing that again. I keep the rags apart. A hide rag in epoxy is a sculpture I have made.

Hide that turns to amber glass in a moulding is a carving day. I have spent an hour picking a quirk I could have lifted in a minute when it was gel. The pot essay taught the hot rag while the film is still a film. After that, it is a chisel and a swear and a profile that comes out shy.

## Walnut still tells

On walnut you can hide more and still not hide a ridge your finger finds. I have sanded a walnut smear into a dish and called the dish figure until oil made it a county. See `d43`. If I must sand, I sand a larger field, and I still expect oil to tell on me. The rays on oak go hollow the same way. The plastic stays in the latewood like a gasket the stain will not enter.

## The judgment

Cleanup is the last clamp. If you skip it, the finish is the review. The knuckle in the drawer is why I do corners before I hang the iron.
