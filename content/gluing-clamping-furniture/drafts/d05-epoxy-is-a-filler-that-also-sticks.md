The stretcher had a cheek you could see through. Not daylight — absence. Someone had drilled out a dowel and taken the tenon with it. I mixed epoxy on a scrap of plywood I was going to throw away anyway. This was not a furniture joint. This was a missing-wood joint. Those are allowed to be different.

Epoxy is a two-part thermoset. You mix a ratio the kit actually printed. `[VERIFY]` that ratio on the bottles you opened, not the ratio you remember from a boat shop in 2011. 1:1 and 5:1 and 2:1 are not interchangeable personalities. A sloppy mix stays soft or goes off like a fever. I throw a test bead on the same scrap. If it does not kick, the joint does not get that cup.

## Gap-filling is the gift and the trap

PVA and hide want a film. Epoxy will bridge. That is why people stop paring. A 1/16 void in a new mortise is not a reason to open the epoxy. It is a reason to make a new rail or thicken a cheek with wood. I will epoxy a knot void in a slab that is already the top. I will not epoxy a dining-rail tenon I cut yesterday.

Outdoor work is the other honest house. A garden bench, a porch table, something that will see rain — hide is a joke and PVA is a short story. Epoxy and a mechanical fastener have a place. I still fit the joint. Epoxy in a well-fit outdoor cheek is stronger than epoxy in a hole you got tired of.

## Clamp pressure is gentler and still not optional

You do not starve epoxy the way you starve PVA. You also do not leave a lake that runs down a finished leg. Enough clamp to close the parts and keep them from drifting. A slip in the first hour is a joint you get to grind later. I wax the pads. Epoxy will glue a clamp pad to a rail like it meant it.

Pot life in an Arkansas afternoon is shorter than the kit’s indoor number. I mix small. I do not walk away to take a call with a cup that is already hot. When the cup gets warm, the clock in the joint is already lying.

## Cleanup is a different religion

Wet epoxy comes off with the solvent the sheet named, not with the rag you use for hide. Cured epoxy comes off with a chisel, a scraper, and language. On a show face I tape what I can tape and I wipe what I can wipe while it is still a film. I have sanded a glazed smear into walnut and then stained around a scar that would not take color. The scar is still there.

I do not mix epoxy in the hide pot. I do not store leftover mixed epoxy “for the next void.” There is no next void that wants yesterday’s cup.

## Where I will not use it

A chair I expect to steam apart. A breadboard I might have to recut. A student’s first edge-joint. Epoxy in those places is a one-way door with a shine. I also will not pour a river table and call it furniture joinery. That is a different shop. This shop builds things you can pick up by the rail.

Tinted epoxy in a crack can be honest if you admit the crack. Clear epoxy in a crack that you then stain to “disappear” is a lie the light will find. I tint toward the wood or I leave the line. I do not chase invisibility through a thermoset.

## The judgment

Epoxy is a filler that also sticks. Use it when the wood is gone, when water is the point, or when a slab already decided to be one piece. Recut when the cheek is lazy. Mix what the kit said. `[VERIFY]` the kit. Do not let gap-filling become the shop’s personality. The stretcher I saved is still a stretcher. The tenon I should have recut and epoxied instead is a story I tell so I do not do it twice.
