The accelerator hissed and the wedge was stone. Ten seconds. I had a stop block that would not creep for the rest of the afternoon. That is the correct use of cyanoacrylate in a furniture shop. The incorrect use was the time I tacked a loose chair horn “just to get it to the clamp” and then forgot the tack was the joint.

CA is thin plastic that wants close-fitting, clean, and often slightly alkaline surfaces. It grabs. It is brittle. It hates peel. A chair rail lives in peel and rack. A jig fence lives in shear you already screwed. I will CA a sacrificial fence to a plywood face and then drive screws. I will not CA a mortise.

## The horn I treated like a clamp

The horn had split along the grain at the scroll. I was alone in the 65-foot shop and the better glue was on the other bench. I told myself I would only freeze the crack so I could carry the chair without the horn flapping. Spray, hold, hiss. The crack disappeared. The chair stood. Someone needed the door. I set the chair in the corner that waits for pickup, and the afternoon became a different afternoon.

A week later the horn was a lid. The CA had glassed the show face shut and left the wood behind the crack free to move. When it let go it took a chip of finish with it. I had to cut a Dutchman I would not have needed if I had treated the tack as a tack. The clamp I meant to fetch was still on the bench. The joint I meant to make never happened. That is the whole failure: not that CA is weak, but that it looks like a repair while it is only a frozen crack.

I still use CA to hold a broken horn so I can *see* the break. I look. I decide. Then I take it apart, or I reinforce it with wood, or I cut the Dutchman on purpose. The tack is a clamp. The tack is not the repair.

## Accelerator is a one-way clock

Without accelerator, thin CA wicks and waits. With accelerator, you have a joint before you have a thought. I have glued a finger to a wedge. I have glued a pad to a caul. I have glued a stop to the wrong side of a line. The spray does not care about your intention.

The wedge jumped. My finger was the clamp. Accelerator does not ask. Debonder burns and takes skin with the glue. I keep the debonder in the same drawer as the CA because the day I store it “somewhere sensible” is the day I am standing at the sink with a maple scrap hanging off a knuckle. I have also glued a cork pad to a caul so thoroughly that I planed the pad off rather than soak the caul. That was cheaper than the finger.

Thick CA fills a little. Thick CA is still not epoxy. A void filled with CA is a void filled with glass that will crack at the first tap. I use thick CA to tack a broken horn back so I can see whether the break is worth a proper repair. Then I take it apart, or I reinforce it with wood. The tack is a clamp. The tack is not the repair.

## Where it earns the bench

Jigs. Wedges. A template you will throw away. A rubber foot that keeps walking. A split in a handle you will wrap anyway. Inlay crumbs if you already accepted the aesthetic. I keep debonder in the same drawer because I am not a hero.

The sacrificial fence is the honest job. I cut a maple strip for the router table, a thin bead along the plywood face, a wipe, accelerator from a distance so I do not fog the bit, then three screws that actually carry the load. The CA keeps the fence from creeping while I drill. If a screw spins later I still have wood and a hole, not a plastic film pretending to be a cheek.

Turners live on CA. This is not a turning shop that pretends to be a furniture shop. If we finish a knob with CA, that is a knob, not an apron.

## What it does to finish and to lungs

CA blush is a white fog. Accelerator can make it worse. On a show face you now have a plastic film that will not take stain the way wood takes stain. I tape. I use it on the inside of a jig. I do not fog a tabletop and then sand “a little.”

I fogged a walnut sample once because I wanted to see if CA would seal a hairline before oil. The blush came in like frost on the long-wall glass in January. Sanding took the frost and left a plastic shine that would not take color the way the rest of the sample took color. I threw the sample in the stove pile. I do not run that experiment on a top.

The fumes are sharp. This is not the fume chapter. I do not spray accelerator like air freshener. I do not run CA in a closed booth for fun. A window, a small bead, a lid that closes.

## Tuesday and the yellow caps

The yellow aliphatic and the CA used to live in the same coffee can because the bottles were the same height and I am a person who puts things where the hand is. I reached in a hurry and put thin CA in a mortise. The tenon went home and locked before I had a clamp in my hand. I split the rail to get it back. The mortise still has a glassy smear I scrape every time I see that rail in the scrap rack. Different shelf now. Different can. The debonder stays with the CA. The yellow caps stay with the yellow caps.

## The judgment

CA is a jig glue. It can tack, it can save a minute, it can ruin a finish, and it can make you think a chair is assembled when you have only frozen a crack. If the piece has to live in a room, use a furniture glue and a fit. If the piece is a fence, tack it and screw it. Keep the debonder. Keep the bottle away from the yellow caps so a Tuesday does not get creative.
