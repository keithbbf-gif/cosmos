I steamed a front rail and three joints let go like they had been waiting. The fourth was white fossil. PVA in a hide chair. The tenon lost more wood than the glue. That is the mix. That is the curse you leave for 2040, or for yourself in October.

<!-- PHOTO: fig-01 D:\BBF\BBF Photos — chair rail with old brown hide beside a white PVA fossil in another hole (filename pending shop pull) -->

The pot essay already made the repair argument. This is the aftermath rule: pick one religion per piece unless you have a reason you can write down. Outdoor epoxy on a hide dining chair is not a reason. A broken cheek you must fill with epoxy on an otherwise hide chair is a reason — and then you mark it, because the next steam should know.

## How I repair what I find

Hide: heat, moisture, patience, scrape to wood, same religion back. PVA: drill, pick, lose wood, maybe a new tenon. Epoxy: cut. I do not steam epoxy and call it hope.

If the chair is already mixed, I get to wood everywhere I can and I choose the new religion for the joints I opened. I do not add a third.

## Veneer blisters

Hide will iron. PVA will not kindly. Urea will not. Know what is under the skin before you heat a tabletop. A test in a back corner. A guess is a scorch.

## The tenon I threw away

I picked, I drilled, I got to something that was almost wood, and I still threw the tenon away and made a new one. I do not invent a named person in that chair. I invent the mix. I write a mark on the inside of a rail when epoxy had to stay — pencil, ugly, where a future person will look before they heat the whole animal.

I have steamed a white joint because I wanted the old chair to be one chair again. The steam took hide and left the fossil. Half a chair in my hands. Half a chair still a sculpture.

## Three religions in one post

I have opened a chair and found brown, white, and a gray epoxy in a repaired break. I cut the break. I picked the rest. I put hide back in the joints that could take hide, and I wrote epoxy on the inside of the broken post so the next steam would not be a fool. Three religions in one animal is a museum of indecision. Two is already a problem.

Yellow because the pot was cold is how the fossil gets in. The pot was not ready. The yellow bottle was. The joint was a joint. I told myself I would steam the others later and this one “didn’t matter.” It mattered when I steamed the others. See *Hide after the pot essay* (essay 3) for the bottle that is not the pot. This page is only the mix.

## Iron on the wrong blister

I have guessed what was under a skin. The scorch was a dull county in an otherwise honest walnut. I scraped. I did not get the color back. I pick a back corner now, a place a tabletop does not show. I smell. Brown hide has a smell. White fossil has a different refusal. If I still do not know, I treat it as PVA and I do not iron the field. Urea will not kindly iron either. A guess is a scorch I already made.

## When the stock is still rails

A painted kitchen cousin can be PVA from the first joint and I will not apologize. That chair is a chair that will be scraped and painted again. A hide dining animal is a different promise. I pick the religion when the stock is still rails, not when the pot is cold and the yellow is closer. See *The chair is an animal with four opinions* (essay 19).

I have opened a chair I did not glue the first time. Brown in three holes, white in one, no note. I treated it as mixed from the minute I saw the white. I did not steam the whole animal and hope. I opened what would open, I cut what would not, I chose one religion for what I put back. The person who mixed it is not a name I own. The mix is.

Getting to wood has a look. Film is a skin. Wood is pores and a little tear. I scrape until the mortise wall is not glossy. If I glue on gloss, I have glued on the last person’s religion and added mine on top. That is how a post becomes a museum.

## The judgment

One glue per animal unless the note says why. The white fossil is why I will not “just use the yellow” on a hide repair because the pot was cold.
