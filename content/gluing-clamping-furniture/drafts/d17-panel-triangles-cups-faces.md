Raking light found a ridge I had called flush with my hand. The boards were already in a triangle. The triangle was honest. The ridge was a joint I had not recut. I took the triangle apart. I jointed again. The second light was kinder.

A panel glue-up is a set of edge joints that have to become one board and then live as a table, a lid, a door. The rehearsal is not “do they touch.” The rehearsal is figure, cup, which face will take the oil, and whether any seam needs a clamp to become a seam.

## Joint that day

Edges that sat overnight pick up a little weather. I joint the edges I will glue, I mark the triangle, I glue. If I have to stop, I joint again. A jointed edge is a fresh thing.

Cups: I read the rings. Alternating heart and bark is a habit that keeps a flatsawn top from becoming a trough. It is not a law that beats a beautiful bookmatch. I will bookmatch and accept a shared cup if the top is thick enough and the finish is honest on both faces. I will not alternate rings as a superstition on a quartersawn oak that already behaves.

## Show face in the light

I decide which face is up before glue. Grain that dives, a knot I can live with, a sap streak I cannot. I mark UP. I glue with that face where I can see squeeze. A panel you flip after the film grabs is a panel you smear.

Moisture: the meter, that day. `[VERIFY]` against the stock, not against a memorized six percent. A wet board next to a dry board is a seam that will move after you leave.

## The seam that needs a hero

If I have to crank a bar to close daylight, I recut. The clamp will not stay in the house. A spring joint — a hair of hollow in the middle — is an old trick I use only when I mean it, and only a hair. A spring you can see from the door is a joint that will starve at the ends or open in the field.

## After

Sticker the panel so both faces see air. A panel left on a cold table overnight can cup to the table. The craft pack said that. It stays said. I do not stack a tool tote on a setting top.

## The triangle I redrew after coffee

I have taken a triangle apart to recut one edge and then stacked the boards back in a prettier order. The figure walked better. The triangle I redrew was honest about the new order and silent about the face I had already chosen in the light. I glued a sap streak into the middle of a field I had meant to hide. The mark is only as good as the last time you made it. I number the boards now, inside the triangle, when the stack is more than three. A three-board lid I can keep in my head. A six-board top I cannot, not after I walk the length of the 65-footer for a caul and come back sure I remember which bark edge was left.

Pencil across every seam, one stroke, on the face that will take the oil. If I have to joint again, I erase and redraw. A ghost triangle from yesterday is how I once glued a board 180 off and called the mismatch “movement.” It was not movement. It was me.

## Cups I pulled shut

A flatsawn oak board will cup toward the bark if you let it. Alternating heart and bark is how I keep a top from becoming a trough a glass will walk. I have also ignored the rings for a bookmatch I wanted — two walnut boards that opened like a moth, cups agreeing. The top was thick enough. I finished both faces. It stayed a top. The same habit on a thinner cherry lid was a lid that went dish-shaped on the stickers and then argued with its frame.

I do not flatten a cup with the bars and call the glue-up a success. I have done that. The cup came back after the film had already decided which way was down. A cupped board in a panel is a spring joint you did not mean. I joint the cup out if the thickness can spare it, or I send the board back to the pile. A hero clamp on a cup will not be in the house when August air in this county is a wet rag.

## The face I flipped

I marked UP on a cherry field and then flipped the panel because the squeeze looked prettier on the other side. The film had grabbed. The pretty side took a smear I spent a morning scraping, and the face I had chosen in the light never got the oil I had promised it. I do not flip after the bead appears. If the show face is down, I live with a mess on the down face and I protect it with wax paper, not with a mid-glue rotation.

## Meter that day, not last winter

Stock from a yard near Warren and stock that has sat in this shop all winter are not the same conversation. I have laid a wetter board next to a drier one because the figure matched. The seam closed. A month later the wetter board shrank and the seam became a valley. I meter both. `[VERIFY]` the number against the stick that day. I do not tattoo six percent on the wall and then ignore a board that still smells like the kiln.

If I have to leave the panel overnight before glue, I sticker it in the same room it will be glued in. A stack against the glass wall in January is a stack with two climates. I have oiled a top that looked flat on Friday and slightly corrugated on Monday, the glass-side board having taken a night of cold it did not share with its neighbors.

## The judgment

Triangle, light, fresh edges, no hero daylight. The ridge my hand missed is why I still walk around the bench with a raking lamp like a person looking for dust. I am looking for a lie.
