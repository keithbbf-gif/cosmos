The table had been honest in June. By September the apron had a soft parallelogram in it. Not loose pins. Not a split tenon. A glue line that had decided to be a hinge. I had used a long-open PVA in a joint that was a little fat because I was alone and I wanted the clock. The clock was kind. The year was not.

Creep is cold flow. A thermoplastic under constant load moves. PVA is a thermoplastic. Hide, once it has glassed, is closer to a brittle film. Urea and epoxy are thermosets. That is not folklore. That is why a chair rail and a table apron are hide arguments in shops that still repair their own work, and why a thick PVA line in those places is a bet.

I recut that apron in the fall. The tenon cheeks had a polished stripe where the film had moved. Shiny, like a burnished gasket. I pared to wood. I recut the mortise walls enough to take a thinner line. I used a glue that glasses, and I put a pin through because I had already learned the year. The table is still a table. The June version is the one I write from.

## Fat lines creep first

A film of PVA in a well-fit cheek can last a life. A 1/32 gasket of PVA under rack is a slow motion joint. I have seen the gasket polish itself shiny. Shiny means it moved. The fix is not a stronger bottle. The fix is a thinner line and a better cheek, or a glue that does not flow.

The fat line was my speed. Alone, a dining base, a long-open bottle, and a tenon I had not pared because the clock was already loud in my head. I buttered. I closed. The squeeze looked like plenty. Plenty in a racked apron is a hinge waiting for weather. August in a 65-foot shop with glass on the long wall will move a top. The apron takes that move as a constant lean. Plastic flows. Wood does not forgive the person who asked the plastic to be a stretcher.

The “stronger” dark PVA is often a little more flexible. Flexibility is a gift on a panel that moves across its width. Flexibility is a hinge on an apron. I pick the grade for the load, not for the adjective.

I used that dark bottle on a six-board top the same summer and the seams stayed seams. Same family. Different load. The top wants a line that can live with a little seasonal argument across the width. The apron wants a line that will not walk when someone leans to pick up a fork. I had let the adjective choose both jobs. The adjective is how I got a parallelogram.

## Load is not a drop test

People slam a joint and call it strong. Creep does not care about slams. Creep cares about the rack that lives in a table when someone leans, when a leaf is opened on one side, when a chair is tilted onto two legs for twenty years. The Forest Products people have written about glue creep as a class. I have watched it as a parallelogram. I do not invent their numbers. I invent nothing. I recut the cheek.

I have slammed that recut apron on the bench after it cured and felt proud. Pride is a drop test. The drop test is how I used to argue with myself. September is the argument that counts. I do not publish a house psi. `[VERIFY]` nothing I did not read. The shiny stripe on the old cheek is the only number I trust, and it is not a number.

Mechanical help is allowed. A pin, a drawbore, a shoulder that actually bears — wood taking the rack so the film is not the only citizen. Glue is not a clamp. Glue is also not a stretcher.

The June tenon had a shoulder that kissed in the middle and showed a hair at the ends. I closed the hair with the bar. The bar left. The hair came back as a rack. I now dry-fit until the shoulder is dark with contact, and I still pin an apron I have already failed once. The pin is not an apology to the bottle. The pin is wood doing the work I had asked a gasket to do.

## Hide is not magic, PVA is not sin

A starved hide joint creeps in the sense that it fails. A well-fit PVA case side that never sees rack will outlive the person who sneers at yellow bottles. I am not running a purity cult. I am naming the property so I stop using the long-open bottle as a personality on every rail.

I have a stack of case sides in yellow glue that have not moved. They live in a room. Nobody leans on them like a table. The film is thin. The edges were jointed that day. Those sides are why I will not stand in a door and say PVA is a moral failure. They are also why I will not put that same bottle in a chair rail and call it the same job.

Repair is the other witness. A PVA apron that crept is a misery to steam. A hide apron that loosened is a morning. That is the craft pack’s argument. This page is only the physics that makes the argument true.

I steamed the June apron anyway, because I wanted to believe I could save the tenon. I got a mess of white plastic and torn fiber. I cut it off. I made a new rail. That morning would have been a scrape and a new film if I had used a glass in June. I am not retelling the pot. I am naming why the pot’s argument shows up as a parallelogram when you ignore it.

## The judgment

If the joint will live under rack, keep the line thin and think about a glass. If the joint is a panel edge, PVA is honest. If you already glued a fat apron in a long-open bottle, do not be surprised when September draws a parallelogram. I was surprised once. I am not writing this as a rumor. I am writing it as a table I recut.
