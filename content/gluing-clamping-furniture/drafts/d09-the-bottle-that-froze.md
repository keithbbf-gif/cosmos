The cap unscrewed and took a disk of yellow leather with it. Under the disk the glue was sour and thick. I had left that bottle on the windowsill because the windowsill was handy. Sun, then night, then sun. The polymer had lived a year of weather in a week.

I write the open date on every bottle with a marker. I write it on hide jars. I write it on epoxy kits. The date is not superstition. It is how I know which bottle is the liar when two yellow caps are on the shelf and one of them smells like vinegar.

The windowsill bottle had no date. That was the other failure. I could not tell whether I had opened it in March or whether I had inherited it from a shelf I had already forgiven once. I smelled it, I poured a little on a scrap, I told myself it would “wake up” if I stirred. It did not wake up. It smeared like paste and never wetted the second cheek. I threw it away after I had already wasted a morning pretending a crust was a personality.

## Ice is not a nap

PVA that froze in a truck is a bottle I throw away. People will tell you it “comes back.” Sometimes it pours. The film it makes is not the film the sheet promised. I have used a thawed bottle on a scrap pair and watched the joint let go with a polite tap. I do not run that experiment on a customer top.

January, a bottle in a tote, a truck that sat in Warren overnight. I brought the tote in and set it by the heater like a person thawing bread. By noon it poured. I glued two oak offcuts because I wanted to believe the internet. The next day I could break the pair over my knee without a sound that meant wood. The film was a skin. I wrote “ICE” on the bottle with the same marker I use for dates and I put it in the trash so I would not have a second conversation with it.

Hide granules do not freeze the same way. The mix can. A pot left to ice is a pot I do not reheat into a religion. Liquid hide that sat through a freeze is a bottle I distrust.

I left a liquid-hide jar in the lean-to because the lean-to was “cold storage.” Cold storage is a word people use when they mean they ran out of shelf. The jar poured after it came in. The repair I used it on — a small split in a handle, nothing that would see a room — let go in a week. I do not know if the freeze did it or the age did it. I do not need to know. I distrust the jar. I write the date. I keep hide with the hide, inside.

Epoxy resins can cloud and separate. `[VERIFY]` the kit’s storage line. I do not invent a fix. If the kit looks like a science project, it is not a table.

I have stirred a cloudy resin until it looked honest and then watched the cup stay tacky on the scrap. The kit’s sheet would have told me if I had read it that morning. I read it after. The leftover went in the cup, cured as far as it would, and went to the trash. I did not “save the hardener for the next void.” There is no next void that wants a science project.

## Air is a slow thief

A cap that does not seat is a crust factory. I wipe the nozzle. I pinch the bottle to seat the cap. I store PVA out of sun. I do not store it next to the heater so it will be “ready.” Heat ages it. A warm cupboard for a week is not a process.

I used to leave the cap sitting on the bench during a long glue-up because I did not want to fight the crust mid-panel. By the last seam the nozzle had grown a scab and the next morning the leather disk was already starting. I now cap between seams. The second it costs me is cheaper than a bottle I will not trust in March.

Urea powder cakes if the lid is casual. Caked urea is a mix you will never trust. I tape the lid like I mean it.

I opened a urea tub once that had lived a humid August with a lid that only looked closed. The powder had become lumps you could pick up. I did not guess at a ratio through a cake. I bought a new tub. The bagged stuff gets a clip and a second bag. Urea is cheap next to a veneer stack you will not get back.

## How I throw glue away without drama

Into a cup, let it cure, trash. Not down a sink. Not into a pot of hide. Not “mixed into the next batch to use it up.” Using it up is how a dining rail inherits a bad afternoon.

I have mixed the last inch of a sour bottle into a fresh one because I hated waste. The fresh one inherited the smell and half the clock. I glued a case side with it. The corners that got the early spread grabbed. The last corner stayed slick. I racked the case to close it and starved the first two. I sawed the box apart. I recut. I opened a bottle that had a date and a seated cap. Waste is a gallon you bought. Waste is not a case.

I keep fewer bottles and buy smaller ones if the shop is slow. A gallon on sale is not a gift if three months of it lives open.

The sale gallon sat on the floor by the door because it would not fit on the shelf. The door is weather. I dated it the day I cracked it and I still lost the last third to a crust and a summer. I buy quarts when the bench is not a production line. The math that says the gallon is cheaper forgets the top.

## The judgment

If it froze, if it crusted, if it smells like it wants to be something else, it is not glue anymore. Write the date. Seat the cap. Keep it out of the sun and out of the truck. The top is more expensive than the bottle. I have paid that tuition.
