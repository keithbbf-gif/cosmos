I ran a thin kerf down the liar seam and the top became boards again. I jointed. I glued a film. The second top is still a top. The first top, if I had filled the hairline with epoxy and hope, would have been a gasket with a shine.

A failed glue-up is information. Starved, drowned, racked, flashed, cold. Name it. Then take it apart. Hide will help you with heat. PVA will fight you. Epoxy will make you cut. Cutting is allowed. Filling a furniture seam with a third religion is how you get a line that moves differently than the wood on both sides.

## How I split

A saw in the glue line if I can find it. A plane after. Fresh wood. No leftover gasket. If I cannot find the line, I am cutting a new edge and losing width. I design tops with a little extra for this reason when I can. I do not always have extra. Then I lose a bit of overhang and I tell the truth.

## Filler’s small honest house

A knot void in a slab you already accepted. A nail hole in paint-grade. Not a dining seam. Not a chair shoulder.

## Name the liar

I name it before I pick up a saw. Starved — no bead, or pearls and dry counties. Drowned — a river. Racked. Flashed — August skin on a cheek I closed anyway. Cold — hide rubber on oak I did not warm. See `d23`, `d29`, `d30`. If I cannot name it, I will fill it, because filler is what you reach for when you have not thought.

In the kerf I could see the film: fat at the ends, thin in the field, the banana I had cranked. See `d36`. I jointed to fresh wood. I glued a film. I do not invent that top as a sold job. I invent it as the top that did not get a gasket.

I have seen a filled seam go dull in a wet week and bright in a dry one, a little weather glass in a table. September opened it. Gaskets creep. Gaskets stain. Gaskets show under oil like a confession. See `d01`.

## The shoulder I buttered

A chair shoulder that failed is the same religion. I do not butter the daylight and clamp it “tighter this time.” I have buttered. The shoulder looked closed. A hip opened it. I opened it on purpose the second time, got to wood, recut the cheek that was gone. See `d45` if the hole is already two religions.

A narrower honest top is still a top. A full-width gasket is a story. I do not always have extra width. Then I lose a bit of overhang and I tell the truth.

## The nail hole that was allowed

I have filled once, on a paint-grade pine case, a nail hole I made myself. That fill disappeared under paint. A wormhole I have already decided is character is the same small house. A breadboard tongue I glued too far is not a void — it is a recut, or living with a split I caused. See `d21`. I have split more than once on seams I wanted to save because I was tired. Tired is how filler gets a dining seam.

## Leftover gasket is a second liar

If I split and leave a skin of old film on the new edge, the second glue-up is a sandwich of wood and yesterday. I plane until I see pores, not shine. Shine is gasket. Hide will sometimes tell you with a color. PVA is a clear liar. I hold the edge to the long glass. If it flashes like plastic, I take another shaving.

I find the glue line before I commit the kerf. Raking light. A pencil tick at both ends. A thin blade. If I wander out of the line I am making two new edges and losing more width than I budgeted. I have wandered. The top got narrower than the base. I told the truth and pulled the overhang in. A lie would have been a filler strip down the middle, a third species in a two-board argument.

Pecan in this county will telegraph a filled hairline under oil as fast as oak. I do not treat a “local” wood as a wood that hides my gasket. The weather still moves it. September still finds it.

## The judgment

Saw. Joint. Glue a film. Do not put a gasket in a table and call it a save. I have filled once. I have split more than once. The split is the save.
