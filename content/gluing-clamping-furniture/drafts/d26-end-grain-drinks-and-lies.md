The miter drank the first coat like a thirsty porch. I closed it anyway because I was in a hurry. The joint held until someone leaned a hip on the frame. Then it opened on one side like a mouth. End grain had told me the truth and I had called it “wetted.”

Long grain to long grain is a furniture joint. End grain to long grain is a dowel, a tenon cheek, a biscuit — something that still has long grain in the conversation. End grain to end grain is a miter you should reinforce, or a butt you should not trust.

## Sizing is a first coat you let disappear

If I must glue a miter, I size: a thin wash, wait until it is dull, then a real coat, then close. Hide does this kindly. PVA does it if you do not wait so long that the wash becomes a peel. I do not invent a kitchen-timer religion. I watch the sheen.

A breadboard tongue is mostly long grain if you cut it that way. The end of the table is end grain in the slot. That is why the middle glue is a short citizen and the pins do the rest. See `d21`.

## Reinforcement is not shame

A spline, a key, a biscuit across a miter, a slip feather — those are how a picture-frame table stays a table. Glue alone on a miter is a wish. I have wished. I cut keys now.

## The lie

Wet-looking end grain is often just the drink. The film never formed. A starved miter looks glued. It is stained wood. I knock a scrap pair if I am unsure. I would rather break a scrap than a frame.

## The porch that drank

The miter had gone shiny and I called it wetted. End grain will go shiny if you look at it while it is still drinking. I closed the frame because I had already staged the clamps and I did not want to wait for a dull. I keep that frame. It is not a customer piece. The hip that opened it was mine. Shiny was the lie. The first coat had gone into the tubes. There was no film, only stained wood that looked like a joint until a rack arrived.

I have trusted a butt on a small box because the box was small. The lid racked the rim and the butts opened like they had been waiting. Small is not a species of long grain.

## Watching sheen, not a kitchen timer

I have waited too long on PVA and closed a miter on a skin. The skin is not a film between two faces. It is a piece of plastic that used to be glue. The joint held until it didn’t. I have also not waited long enough and closed on a drink. Both look like I did something. Both are the porch again. `[VERIFY]` if I ever cite a sheet for the wait. In this shop I cite the dull.

Sizing the end of a top before a breadboard goes on is not romance. It is how I stop the slot from becoming a sponge I cannot get back to. See `d21`.

## Keys after the wish

A spline, a key, a biscuit across a miter, a slip feather — those are how a picture-frame table stays a table when a hip arrives. Glue alone on a miter is a wish. I have wished. I cut keys now. The key is long grain across the mouth. The mouth can still drink. The key does not care. The key is the conversation the end grain could not have.

I dry-fit the keys. A fat key in a skinny slot is a split I scheduled. A starved key is a wish with extra steps. I size the miter, I glue the key like a small tenon — cheeks, not a cup — and I do not pour the kerf. A poured kerf is a piston the width of a saw. The feather comes back at you or the corner stains.

I do not biscuit a miter as a personality. I biscuit a miter when the helper is the reinforcement I meant, and I still size. A biscuit in thirsty end grain without a wash is a biscuit in a drink. The biscuit plumps. The film still may not exist on the faces that matter.

## The scrap I would rather break

I knock a scrap pair cut from the same stick, same angle, same wash, same wait. If the scrap opens with a tap after a cure I would trust on the work, the work would have opened with a hip. I have broken the frame. The scrap is cheaper and less embarrassing, and it still goes in the stove.

I do not ask a miter to be a cheek because the piece is “only a looking-glass” or “only a small lid.” Only is how the porch gets into the house. If the piece will be leaned on, lifted, racked, set down on a floor in Bradley County that is not a reference, I reinforce. If I cannot reinforce, I change the joint. End grain will take a cup of glue and still not be a joint. I believe the drink now. I believed the shine too long.

## The judgment

Do not ask end grain to be a cheek. Size if you must. Reinforce if the piece will be leaned on. The mouth that opened on the hip is why I stopped trusting a shiny miter.
