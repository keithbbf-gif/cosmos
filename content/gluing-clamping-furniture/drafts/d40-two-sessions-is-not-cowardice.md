I wrote “ends only” on the tape. The stretchers waited on the bench like they were offended. The next day they went in with a clock that fit the sheet. The table did not get a fourth corner that was a rumor.

Hero sessions feel like craft. They feel like you are the kind of person who can keep four joints alive. August hide does not care what kind of person you are. Alone, I split. With help, I may still split if the piece is a dining base I cannot replace by Friday.

## What splits cleanly

Table ends, then stretchers. Chair front, chair back, then sides. A case’s box, then the face frame. A panel is usually one session because the edges are one clock — unless the panel is a meal of boards, in which case I glue in pairs and then join the pairs.

## What must not wait wrong

A joint that depends on another joint for square — I do not glue a side rail that needs a back I have not set. The dry-fit tells me the order. I write the order. I do not invent the order in the pot’s minute.

## The fourth corner that was a rumor

I have done the hero session. Four table corners, hide, August, alone. Three corners sat. The fourth was a shoulder I closed with a shove and a prayer because the pot was skinning and I still had a stretcher in my hand. The next morning that corner was a rumor — tight to the eye, daylight to a feeler, a little proud on the off cheek. I recut the rail. The recut took longer than the night I had saved.

I write “ends only” because I have a habit of lying to myself at the bench. The tape is uglier than the lie and harder to ignore. The stretchers were not offended. They were dry. Reaching for them is how a hero session starts — one more joint, the pot is still warm, I am still the kind of person.

## What help is allowed to buy

A second pair of hands can keep a whole chair alive if the dry-fit already wrote the order and the clamps are already on the floor. Help is not a license to invent the order while hide is on a cheek. I have had help and still split, because the animal was a dining chair and the shop was a kiln and I did not want a limp I would steam on Saturday.

Help can hold a case while I read the inside square. Help can keep a panel from banana-ing while I set the last caul. Help cannot make a six-board argument into a top. See `d36`. If the rehearsal fought me, the wet play will not become kinder because someone else is in the room.

Pairs of panel boards have a clock I can keep. A meal of six edges does not. I glue in pairs, then I join the pairs, and I treat each glued pair as a patient — not as a pile I will get to after lunch.

## The stretcher as a dry witness

I do not glue a stretcher that is the only thing keeping two ends from becoming parallelograms unless I have already decided the ends are the session and the stretcher is a dry witness. The dry-fit writes that. I stage the iron for the half I will actually glue. The other half stays dry on the bench where I can see it.

If the dry-fit already racked, I do not split my way around a bad rail. I recut. Two sessions is not a way to hide a daylight cheek overnight and hope PVA will be a jointer in the morning.

Hide in January will gel on a cold oak cheek while I am still congratulating myself for having split the play. See `d30`. Split does not mean I can wander. The half I chose still wants warm parts and a rehearsal I already timed. See `d16`. PVA will give me minutes and still not give me four perfect corners if I use the minutes to hunt a pad.

I have left a glued pair of panel boards on the saw “until the next pair.” The pair cupped. See `d39`. A session is not a pause you abandon.

## The judgment

Split the play. The offended stretchers still became a table. The hero corner I rushed in an earlier year became a recut. Two sessions is the craft. One session is the story you tell when it worked.
