I put two pipes on a hexagonal wastebasket and the box became an oval with opinions. The bars knew how to be a rectangle. The work did not. A strap around the rim, pads at the corners, and the tails sat down like they had been waiting for someone to speak their language.

Band clamps, web clamps, a truck strap in a pinch — they pull toward a center. Chairs, hoppers, coopered work, anything whose clamp line is a hoop. A bar pulls toward a jaw. If the jaw is not opposite a face, the bar invents a face.

## Pads at the corners

A strap on a sharp arris is a dent. I use scraps with a little hollow, or leather, or the corner blocks that came with the cheap web kit. I stage them dry. A strap that walks off a corner in open time is a strap that cuts.

## A strap is not a square

I still read diagonals. I still set the chair on its feet. A band can close a racked chair into a different rack. I pull even. I watch the shoulders. I do not crank until the webbing sings and then call it craft.

## Coopered and bent

A coopered door or a laminated seat wants even pressure around a curve. A vacuum bag is the other religion. A strap is the cheap cousin if the curve is gentle and the cauls are honest. I do not strap a veneer stack and hope. Veneer wants a bag or a press.

## The oval I invented with pipes

The wastebasket was a practice hex — six tails, six pins, a rim I wanted to learn before I trusted a bigger hopper. I put a pipe across two opposite faces because opposite is how I think when I am tired. Opposite on a hex is not a pair of faces. It is a pair of corners and a wish. The box went oval. Two joints opened. Two joints starved. The remaining two looked proud of themselves. I slacked the pipes before the glue died and I stood there with a hex that had learned the wrong language.

The strap went around. Pads at the six corners, staged in the dry-fit, a little hollow so the webbing would not cut. I pulled until the tails sat. I did not pull until the webbing sang. The hex came back. I keep that cheap web clamp with the pipes now, not in the junk drawer, because the junk drawer is where I left it the morning I reached for the pipes.

A hopper is the same hoop with sloped sides. Bars skate downhill. I have chased a bar down a hopper side in open time. I do not miss that chase.

## Chairs are animals with a hoop

A chair wants a clamp line that follows the seat rail around, or the crest, or the box of the seat. Pipes on a chair teach a limp. Parallels teach a limp with better jaws. See *The chair is an animal with four opinions* (essay 19) and *What each clamp lies about* (essay 32). A strap around the seat, pads at the legs, and the animal can close without being taught a rectangle.

I still set the chair on its feet. I still look at the shoulders in raking light. I still read the diagonals of the seat if the seat is a square that will take a frame. A band will happily close a racked chair into a tighter rack. I have done that. The feet rocked. The shoulders looked closed from one county and open from another. I slacked, I racked the chair true by hand, I pulled the strap even, and I walked around it twice before I trusted the quiet.

I do not crank one ratchet and leave the opposite web slack. A strap that walks up a tapered leg will cut the arris and miss the joint. I stage a stop so the web stays on the line I dry-fit.

## The cut I already made

I left a pad off one corner of a polygonal box because I was "almost done" and the strap looked centered. The webbing found the arris. The arris took a dent the width of the belt. I sanded. I still see it in raking light. Leather or a hollowed scrap is not courtesy. It is how a hoop does not become a saw.

A strap that walks off in open time is worse. The belt jumps, the corner takes a bite, and the joint you were closing opens while you put the pad back on with glue on your hands. I stage the pads with a rubber band or a bit of tape so they stay while I pull. Ugly. Faster than a jump.

## Coopered work, and what a strap is not

A coopered door with a gentle curve will take a strap over honest cauls. I have closed staves that way. A tight bend, a veneer skin, a stack that must be a skin — that is a bag or a press. See *A vacuum bag is a clamp the size of a room* (essay 35). I strapped a veneer test once and got a bubble at a county the strap never reached. The strap pulls a hoop. It does not press a field. I do not hope a hoop into a platen.

A truck strap will do in a pinch if the ratchet does not eat the work. I have used one on a big frame when the web kit was too short. I padded as if it were a meaner cousin, because it is.

## What I still check after the hoop is tight

Feet. Shoulders. Diagonals. The strap can hide a twist if you only look at the rim. A wastebasket oval is funny until it is a chair that rocks. I do not crank until the webbing sings. The song is usually a dent or a rack. See *Enough to close, not to crush* (essay 31). Then I walk. The pads are already on the bench. I do not invent one in open time.

## The judgment

If the work is a hoop, use a hoop. Pad the corners. Check the feet. The oval wastebasket is why a web clamp lives with the pipes, not in a junk drawer.
