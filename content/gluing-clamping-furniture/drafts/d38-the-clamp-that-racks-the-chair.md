I hung a parallel clamp on a side rail because the rail was the thing I wanted closed. The chair stood on three legs when I set it down. The iron had pulled a post in. The film had grabbed the limp. I steamed that one. I do not hang a parallel on a side anymore without the feet on the floor.

A chair wants even close. A strap around the seat, handscrews on the rails, a band on the back. Bars are allowed if they have a neighbor and the feet stay planted. A single diagonal bar is a racking machine.

## Watch the hooves

I glue chairs on a known flat. I look under the feet. I rock. If it rocks in the clamps, it will rock in the house. A shim under a foot during glue-up is a confession I should have recut a rail.

## Sessions

Front, back, then sides. See *The chair is an animal with four opinions* (essay 19) and *Two sessions is not cowardice* (essay 40). A whole chair in one hide sprint is how a limp gets born.

## What a rack looks like while the iron is still on

A chair that is racking in clamps does not always look dramatic. One front foot lifts a credit-card thickness. The back rail’s shoulder opens on the off side. The crest, if there is one, takes a little twist you will only see if you sight it like a winding stick. I walk around. I sit on my heels and look under. I do not trust the view from the vise end of the bench, because that view is the view of the person who already decided the clamp was in the right place.

If I have to choose between a closed shoulder and four feet on the floor, I choose the feet and I recut the shoulder tomorrow. A closed shoulder on a three-legged chair is a closed shoulder on a sculpture.

## Hide vs PVA when you already taught a limp

Hide will let you steam the limp out if you catch it before you have also pinned the joint. PVA will let you steam a lot of wood off the tenon. If I see a limp in PVA at minute twenty, I still take it apart if the film has not grabbed into a plastic I cannot open. Pride is a second chair. A pin driven into a limp is a limp you have notarized.

## The bench is a drawing

A chair on its back on the bench is a drawing. The posts are not carrying. The rack is a theory. I have dry-fit a chair on the bench and called the shoulders closed, then stood it up and watched a foot tick. The bench had been holding a twist the floor would not hold.

The floor in this shop is kinder in some counties than others. I know the county I use. I do not glue an animal on the hollow in front of the saw and then blame the tenons. Light from the long glass will flatter a crest the way it flatters a case rim. I walk the long way around. I look under each hoof. I rock with a hand on the crest, not a shove that would rack a dry chair into a lie.

I have used a shim under a foot and left it there because the clock was mean. The chair stood. The house will still find the short post. The shim is a note to recut. It is not the finished geometry of something a person sits in.

## A neighbor for every bar

I have used a pipe as a diagonal because I was short a band and August was eating the hide. The chair told me at the feet before the film finished grabbing. I took the pipe off. Strap, two handscrews, a slower close. The shoulder that had wanted the pipe still closed. It closed because the joint was a joint, not because I had a diagonal religion.

Convenience is the other racking machine. The parallel was on the bench. The side rail had daylight. I hung the bar the way I hang a bar on a table apron — one good pull, pad on the show face — and I was pleased with myself for about as long as it takes to walk around a chair. The off-side front foot was in the air. A scrap of paper slid under it. The opposite shoulder had opened a hair I had not been looking at because I had been looking at the daylight I cared about.

Two bars that oppose each other are a conversation. One diagonal bar is a speech. A handscrew on the rail is wood talking to wood. I stage the strap and the handscrews in the dry-fit so I am not inventing a clamp pattern while the pot is skinning.

## What steam cost

Steam on a hide chair is allowed. Steam on a limp you taught with a bar is a bill. The tenon came out with more wood than I wanted to lose. I pared, I recut a shoulder, I glued it again on the floor with a strap. That chair stood on four. The first version would have stood on three in a room and I would have called it “a little character.”

I do not add a pin to a three-legged chair and call the pin craft. The pin is a notary. If I already drove it, I own the limp until I cut the joint apart and start the animal over.

## The judgment

Feet down. Even iron. No heroic diagonal. The three-legged chair is why I glue animals on the floor now, like a person who has already paid.
