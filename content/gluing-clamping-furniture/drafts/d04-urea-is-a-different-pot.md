The powder looked like pale sand and smelled like a shop that had decided to be industrial for an afternoon. I mixed it in a cup I will never eat from. The veneer stack was already in cauls. Urea does not wait for you to find the clamps. Urea is the clamps’ appointment.

<!-- PHOTO: fig-01 D:\BBF\BBF Photos — plastic-resin powder, mix cup, veneer caul stack (filename pending shop pull) -->

People call it plastic resin and think it is a cousin of the yellow bottle. It is urea-formaldehyde in a can, or a close relative, and it sets into a hard, thin, heat-and-water-tougher film than PVA. Veneer shops used it because a vacuum bag or a press could stay on through a cure that does not creep the way a thick aliphatic line creeps. Bent laminations like it for the same reason. A stack of thin oak that must become a crest rail does not want a rubber hinge between the plies.

## It is not hide with a worse smell

Hide is collagen. You can reverse it. Urea is a thermoset. Heat will not kindly hand you the veneer back. That is the point and the warning. If you are skinning a panel you might want to recover, hide is still the king of the iron. If you are skinning a panel that must live as one thing through a wet kitchen and a hot car ride home, urea is in the conversation.

The mix is powder and water to a cream. `[VERIFY]` the ratio on the can you opened. Too thin and you starve the faces. Too thick and you leave ridges that telegraph through a 1/28. Pot life is a short afternoon, shorter in our heat. I mix what the stack needs, not what looks like a useful leftover. Leftover urea is a cup of future brick.

## Clamp time is not lunch

Sheets talk in hours. I talk in “do not take the bag down because you are curious.” A lamination that comes out of clamps early will spring and tell you about it for the rest of its life. Overnight is a habit I like even when the can is more optimistic. `[VERIFY]` the can.

Pressure is even more than heroic. A vacuum bag is a clamp the size of a room if the bag is honest and the platens are flat. Cauls that were good enough for PVA are not always good enough for a veneer that will show every lump. I have pressed a breadcrumb into a door. The door still remembers.

## The window stays open

Formaldehyde is not a personality. It is a fume. I run this glue with air moving and I do not make a romantic day of it. This essay is not a safety program. Read the sheet. Keep the powder off wet hands. Do not invent a respirator rule in a caption. If the shop cannot ventilate, the shop does not run urea that day. PVA will still glue a cabinet. The crest rail can wait.

I do not mix urea in the same cup as epoxy “to use the stick.” I do not store the powder next to the hide granules because someone will grab the wrong sand. I label the lid like I am labeling a poison, because for the purpose of a Tuesday afternoon it is close enough.

## Where I will not use it

A chair I expect to repair. A joint I might have to steam. A student’s first panel when hide would teach them more. Urea is a one-way door with a better water story than PVA and a worse reverse story than hide. I also will not use it as a gap filler. It is a film glue. Gaps are epoxy’s sin, not urea’s.

Plastic resin on a mortise-and-tenon dining rail is legal and usually unnecessary. The rail wants hide or a PVA you chose on purpose. Save urea for the skins and the bends. That is the shop that still smells like a mill town without pretending we are running a 1950s press line.

## The judgment

If the work is veneer or a lamination that must stay a glass, urea is a pot you mix and a bag you do not peek. If the work is a chair, walk back to hide or PVA. If the work is a gap, walk to epoxy or recut. The powder is not a personality upgrade. It is a different religion. The window stays open while you practice it.
