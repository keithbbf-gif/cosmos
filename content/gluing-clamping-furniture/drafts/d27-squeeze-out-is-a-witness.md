I wiped a wet bead with a dirty rag and painted PVA into the pores of a quarter-sawn oak lid. The lid took stain like a map of my hurry. The map is still there under a film I cannot sand off without going to the next county of rays.

Squeeze-out is information first. Even bead: good film. Pearls and dry counties: starved patches. River: drowned, and a smear coming. No bead: either a perfect starved joint or a gap. I do not celebrate no bead.

## Hide wants a hot rag

The pot essay taught the damp heat. I wipe hide while it is still a film, off the show face, out of the profile. Dried hide on a moulding is a carving day.

## PVA wants a moment

Wet-wipe PVA if you are clean and careful. Or wait until it gels and lift the bead with a chisel so you do not paint. I wait more than I used to. The gel lift is uglier in the minute and kinder in the finish room.

## Inside corners now

A drawer, a case, a place a knuckle will find. A glue ridge is a cut. I get it now. The craft pack said the finish room sent a smear back. I am the finish room some days. I send it back to myself.

## The rag that was already a bottle

That rag had last week's PVA in it, and a little stain, and whatever I had used to wipe the bench. Wet glue on a dirty rag is a second bottle. I painted the lid the way you paint a fence — long strokes, proud of the shine. The shine was glue. Quarter-sawn oak is a set of open rays. The rays drank. I thought I was cleaning. I was filling.

I have done the same thing with a paper towel that left lint in the wet film, and with a shop towel that printed its weave into a cherry arris. The dedicated rag is dedicated because I got tired of guessing what lived in the last one. It sits in a can on the glue bench. Damp for hide. Nearly dry for PVA. I do not grab the floor rag.

## The map under the film

I stained that lid because I wanted the figure. What I got was counties. Dark where the rag had missed. Pale where the pores were plastic. I sanded. I sanded again. Rays are not a thickness you can sand off and still have a lid. I went as far as I dared and then I put a film over the map and lived with it. That lid taught me more than a scrap ever did, because I had to keep it.

A wipe of mineral spirits on a raw face will preview some of this if I am paying attention. If the wipe shows a pale river, I am still in the glue chapter. See *Glue on the finish face* (essay 28) and *The scar under oil* (essay 43).

## The gel minute

PVA in this shop does not gel on a schedule I would write on a wall. August against the long glass is a short minute. January in the lean-to end is a longer one. I wait until the bead is no longer a puddle and not yet a crystal. Then a chisel, or a card scraper set to lift, not to cut wood. The ribbon comes off. The pores stay empty. That is the whole kindness.

If I wait too long, the bead is a ridge I have to scrape into the arris. If I go too soon, I am back to painting. I test a small county of the bead with a thumbnail. Soft-firm is the lift. I will not give you a kitchen-timer number. The bead is the clock.

Hide is not this wait. Hide is the hot rag while the film is still a film. I have let hide dry on a cove because I was chasing a clamp that had walked. The next day I carved the profile back.

## A ridge a knuckle will find

I have sent a drawer back from my own hand. A glue ridge in an inside corner is a cut waiting for a knuckle. After it dies it is a carving in a place you cannot get a plane. I have left a PVA string in a drawer runner because I was looking at the front. The string became a bump. The bump became a drawer that stuck in August, which I blamed on humidity until I felt the ridge with a finger.

## What I do with the ribbon

The lifted bead goes in a scrap can, not on the bench, not on a pad, not back onto a cheek I missed. I have smeared a good lift by setting the chisel down on the lid. The lid took a second map, smaller and meaner, right where a hand would rest.

I do not celebrate a dry line. I have celebrated dry lines. They were starved patches or a gap I had cranked shut. See *Starved and drowned* (essay 23). A river is a smear I have already scheduled for the finish room. I do not use last Tuesday's minute. I use this bead, this rag, this wait.

## The judgment

Read the bead. Then lift it or wipe it with a plan. Do not paint. The oak map is why I keep a dedicated rag and a dedicated wait.
