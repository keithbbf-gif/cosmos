I heard the panel creak and I took it as virtue. The next morning the top had a smile. Not cup from stickers — a clamp smile, a banana I had cranked in. The seams were starved in the field and fat at the ends. I had used the bars as a jointer and a press.

Enough pressure is the pressure that closes the daylight and stops. I watch the bead. I watch the shoulder. I stop. Another quarter-turn after hide has grabbed is a shoulder you open. Another quarter-turn on PVA is a bow. The craft pack already said the quiet is the job. This is the numberless version of the crank.

## I do not keep psi on the wall

Sheets talk pounds. Shops talk whether the seam disappeared and whether the panel stayed flat. If a reviewer wants a number, they can read a sheet and a meter we do not own yet. `[VERIFY]`. I own a straightedge and a pair of winding sticks.

## Softwood crush

Pine takes a jaw print and keeps it. Poplar too. A pad that is smaller than the jaw is a stamp. See `d15` and `d39`. Hardwood still dishes if the jaw is a corner. I pad. I stop.

## Hungry in the middle

Bars on the edges of a wide panel without cauls will close the edges and leave the field. I add cauls. I do not add anger. Anger is a banana.

## The creak was the panel telling me to quit

I remember the sound better than the morning. A green, wooden complaint from the middle of a walnut top I already knew was close. I thought: now it is really closed. I gave the last bars another quarter because the first creak had felt like a blessing. In the morning the top rocked on the bench. A smile you could see from the door. I ran a straightedge and the field was high. I ran a hand down the seams and the middle was hungry — a hairline I had pumped dry — and the ends were fat ridges I would spend a day scraping.

I sawed that top apart. I recut the edges until they closed with a tap. I glued it again and I stopped when the daylight died. No creak. The second top stayed a top. The first one had been a press I invented because I liked the sound of effort.

The banana pattern — bars all on one face, no cauls — has its own page. See `d36`. This page is the crank inside any pattern. You can alternate the iron and still ruin a panel if you treat the screw as a jointer.

## Hide grabs and then you open it

Hide will take a set in the shoulder while you are still proud of the rack. I have felt a chair rail go quiet under the handscrews and then I have given the pipe one more turn because a corner looked a hair open. The hair was the iron talking, not the joint. The extra turn opened the shoulder on the far side. I found it when I wiped. A white line where there had been contact. I slacked the pipe and the line closed again. I walked. I did not chase the last shine.

PVA is slower to grab and happier to bow. The film is still a lubricant when you are hungry. Hungry jaws slide the boards, starve the field, and leave you a panel that is thinner in the middle than you planned. I watch the bead. An even bead that stays even is enough. A bead that disappears in the field while the ends keep weeping is a panel you are pumping. I back off. I add a caul. I do not add a turn.

## What I watch instead of a meter

Daylight in the shoulder, then no daylight. A bead that appears and does not become a river. A panel that still sits on a straightedge I already trusted. Winding sticks if the work is a case or a wide top. The pad, whether it stayed over the jaw or walked into a stamp. I walk around the bench once. If I want another quarter-turn after that walk, I ask why. Usually the why is fear. Fear is not a film.

I will not publish a house psi. We do not own the meter that would make the number honest, and a number on a wall becomes a religion that ignores August and pine. `[VERIFY]` a sheet if you need to talk to a reviewer. On this bench the priest is the seam.

## Softwood remembers the extra turn

I printed a rectangle into a poplar stretcher because I padded the far jaw and forgot the near one, then I cranked as if the print would sand out. It sanded quieter. Stain found the rim of the stamp. See `d39`. Pine is the same guest book. I pad both jaws, bigger than the iron, and I stop when the joint closes. The pad is not permission to go hunting for a creak.

Hardwood is ruder in a different way. A parallel corner on a cherry arris will burnish. The shine lasts through a scrape. A hungry pipe on an oak edge will dish a place a hand will feel. I pad walnut too. Pride about "it's hard enough" is how I got a shiny bruise on a leg I had already scraped.

## Anger is a caul you do not have

A wide panel with bars only at the rims will close the rims. The field stays a rumor. I have been angry at that rumor and tightened the rims until the panel smiled. The smile felt like I had won. I had starved the middle and crowned the top. Cauls are the clamp that reaches the field. See `d14`. I stage them in the dry-fit. I do not invent them after the creak.

If a seam will not close without a hero, the hero is a recut. I have used the bars as a jointer. The joint came back in September. Glue is not a clamp. See `d01`. This page is only the hand on the screw after the joint is already a joint.

South Arkansas August will make you hurry the crank because the film is dying. Hurry is still not an extra turn. Spread less. Close sooner. Stop the same way.

## The judgment

Close. Stop. Walk. The creak was not virtue. The smile in the top was the review. I recut that top. I crank less.
