The diagonals matched. I was proud of the tape. Then I set two sticks across the case and the far stick was a little drunk. Not much. Enough that a lid would rock and a door would show a wedge. Square in plan is not flat in the world.

A case glue-up is four corners and a twist you can glue in forever. I dry-fit the box on a surface I trust, or on sticks, and I read winding before I read the bottle. If the sides were not coplanar at the bench, they will not become coplanar because PVA is optimistic.

## What winds a case

One side longer in the shoulder. A dado that bottoms at two different depths. A back that will be nailed on as a clamp — the back is a clamp, and a bad back is a propeller. I dry-fit the back. I do not invent the back after the box has set.

A floor that is not flat will wind the box while you clamp. I glue cases on the bench I have already checked, or on a known door. I have glued a case on a hollow bench and then blamed the joinery. The bench was the joinery.

## Squares inside, not only on the rim

I keep a square in the inside corner after the clamps are on. The rim can lie. The inside is where a drawer lives. A case that is pretty on the outside and pinched inside is a drawer that binds. See the drawer essay in the craft pack if you want the runner. This page is the box that runner sits in.

## The lid waits

I do not glue a lid into a case I have not seen closed. The lid is the test. If the lid waits until after the box is a box, the box has to be a box. If the lid is a panel in grooves, the grooves have to be a plane. I dry-fit the lid. I refuse the glue if the lid is the only thing holding the rim in a rectangle.

## The sticks on a Tuesday

I keep a pair of winding sticks that are just two jointed scraps, same length, a dark strip on the far edge so I can read it. I set them across the dry box, front and back, and I sight like I am looking for a deer in a bean field. The far stick should sit in the near one. When it does not, the case is a propeller, even if the tape already congratulated me.

I have trusted the tape and skipped the sticks because I was already late and the diagonals were twins. The box came out of clamps looking square from above. A lid I had already raised rocked on two corners. I shimmed that lid in the finish room like a person hiding a floor. The shims are still in a drawer. I look at them when I am tempted to call the tape enough.

The sticks go on before the bottle and again after the iron is snug, before the film has a religion. A twist you can still rack out is a twist you should rack out. A twist you find in the morning is a twist you will live with or saw apart.

## The bench that was the joinery

The hollow I blamed on the dados was a hollow in the bench. I had glued the box on a top I use for everything, and that top has a dip I ignore when I am planing a rail. The dip became the floor of the case. Four corners sat, the middle of the sides hung, and when I set the box on a door I keep for this, the sticks went drunk again. I took it apart while the PVA would still argue. I glued the next one on the door. The door is uglier than the bench. The door is flat.

I check the floor I will glue on the way I check a jointer table — straightedge, light, no romance. This 65-footer has a concrete floor that is not a reference. I have set a case on the concrete to “let gravity help” and watched one corner find a low spot the size of a coin. Gravity helped the propeller.

## The back I invented after

I have nailed a back on a box that was already a little alive, and used the nails as the last clamps. The back pulled the twist into a permanent shape. The back was plywood I cut after the glue-up, in a hurry, a little proud on one long edge. Proud on one edge is a wedge. The wedge was the clamp. The lid still rocks.

Now the back is in the dry-fit. I cut it to the opening I actually have, I set it, I see whether it wants to wind the rim. If the back is the only thing that makes the box a rectangle, the box is not a box. I recut a shoulder or I recut a dado. I do not ask a sheet of ply to be a jointer. A bad back is still a propeller. A good back is a witness I can leave in or take out before the bottle opens.

## The drawer that taught the pinch

I squared the rim with a tape and a smile. The inside corners were a hair under. A drawer I had already fit to the dry box started to bind on the way in, one runner tight, the other a rumor. The clamps had pinched the middle of the case the way bars pinch a panel if you have no caul. I had no square in the inside after the iron went on. I had walked.

If the inside is pinched I ease the bars before the film grabs. If the film has grabbed I live with a drawer I have to recut, or I swear at a PVA box and take a plane to a runner I had already finished. The runner essay in the craft pack is still the runner. This is the hole the runner has to enter.

## The judgment

Tape is not enough. Sticks, a trusted floor, a back that is part of the rehearsal. Glue a propeller and you will spend the finish room shimming a lid. I have. The sticks are faster.
