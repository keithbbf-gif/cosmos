The tenon came back at me an eighth of an inch after I thought I had seated it. I pushed. It came back. Glue was weeping from the pin hole like the joint was breathing. I had poured the mortise. The tenon was a piston.

A film joint wants two wet faces. One wet face can work if the close is immediate and the wood is kind. Two wet faces is the habit I trust. A cup of glue in a mortise is not two wet faces. It is a hydraulic cylinder.

## How I butter

Brush or finger on the tenon cheeks. A thin coat on the mortise walls if I can reach without making a lake. The floor gets what the walls leave, not a squeeze from the bottle. I have used a stick wrapped in a rag to wet walls. It looks like a folk remedy. It keeps the floor from becoming a sump.

On an edge joint I wet both edges. On a dovetail I wet the pins and I am careful in the sockets so I do not flood the show face. On a biscuit I wet the edge and I put a little in the slot. The theme is film, not pond.

## Pins and holes

A pin hole is a pressure relief if you already made a lake. It is also a stain chimney. I would rather not need the chimney. If glue weeps from a pin hole, I wipe it now. Dried hide in a pin hole is a thickness I did not plan. Dried PVA is a plug.

## The piston afternoon

I had seated the tenon with a tap I trusted. I turned to get a pad. When I looked back the shoulder had a hair of daylight and the tenon had come toward me like it had changed its mind. I pushed. It came back. Glue wept from the pin hole. I had poured the mortise because the hole looked hungry and I wanted to be sure. Sure is a cylinder. The tenon was the piston. The pin hole was the vent.

I took it apart while I still could. The floor of the mortise was a sump. The cheeks of the tenon were wet in patches and bare where the hydraulic ride had wiped them. I wiped the sump. I buttered the cheeks. I left the floor what the walls left. The second seating stayed seated. The shoulder that had been a hair stayed dark with contact, not dark with extra glue.

I have also failed to take it apart. I have assembled a piston, waited, and lived with a short shoulder because the film had grabbed while I was still arguing with the bounce. The short shoulder is a shadow you see in raking light for the life of the rail. I do not recommend the short shoulder. I recommend the two minutes of undo.

## Walls, not the well

The bottle wants to be a well-filler. The bottle is a reservoir. I do not point it into a mortise and squeeze until I feel better. Feeling better is how the piston gets born. I load the brush or the stick from a scrap, I wet the walls, I look down the hole in the light. Shiny walls, dull-enough floor. If the floor is a lake I have already poured. I wipe the lake with the stick before the tenon goes home.

One wet edge can work if the close is immediate and the wood is kind. Two wet edges is the habit I trust when August is stealing the minute. A flooded dovetail socket is a skating rink on a drawer front. I have been the finish room for that rink.

## What the pin hole told me

If I see weep, I know I poured. Glue that weeps from a pin hole will stain the show if you ignore it, and it will leave a plug if you let it cure in the hole you still need for a pin. I do not call the weep “good squeeze.” Good squeeze on a mortise-and-tenon is a thin bead at the shoulder, not a fountain from a hole I meant for wood. The fountain is a review. The review says take it apart if you still can. Dried PVA in the hole is a plug I then drill like I am repairing a mistake I could have wiped.

## The biscuit cousin

A slot is a little mortise. A lake in a slot is a little piston. I put a little in the slot and I butter the edge. I do not fill the slot like a cup and then act surprised when a biscuit hydraulics and the edge starves. See *Biscuits and dominos are alignment, not religion* (essay 22). The theme does not change because the helper is cute. Film. Not pond. Two faces. No cup.

## The judgment

Two faces. No cup. If the tenon breathes back at you, you poured. Take it apart if you still can, wipe, recut nothing, try again with a film. I have assembled a piston and then lived with a short shoulder. I do not recommend the short shoulder.
