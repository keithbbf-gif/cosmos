The rim looked like a picture. The drawer opening was a wedge. I had squared the outside with a tape and a hope. The clamps had pinched a dado. I found it when the drawer went in and came out as a complaint.

After the iron is on, I read the inside. A square in the corner. A tape on the diagonals of the opening that matters, not only the footprint. A winding look if the case is a case I will put a lid on. See *The case that winds* (essay 18). Then I wipe, I check once more, I walk. Poking a setting hide corner is how you open a shoulder that was closed.

## The back is a clamp

A nailed or screwed back can pull a box into square or into a propeller. I dry-fit the back. I do not invent it after as a rescue unless I admit the rescue. A plywood back is allowed to be a clamp. A bad back is a forever twist.

## One more quarter-turn

No. See *Enough to close, not to crush* (essay 31). If a corner is open, the iron is in the wrong place or the joint is daylight. Recut tomorrow. Do not invent a square with anger tonight.

## The footprint that lied

The tape on the outside corners had agreed with itself. I have been proud of that agreement. The sides had been pinched inward at the dado height. From the door the box looked like furniture. From the drawer it looked like a funnel.

I measure the opening that will hold a thing. Drawer bay. Door rebate. The mouth a lid has to sit in. I do not only walk the feet of the box. The feet can be honest and the mouth can still be a lie, because the clamps do not always press where the tape was.

A face frame after the box is a second chance and a second way to hide the first sin. I have hung a frame on a pinched box and called it a save. The drawer still knew. The frame made the front look square and the back of the bay stayed a wedge. The drawer went in pretty and came out with a rub you could hear. I planed the drawer to match the wedge once. That is how you teach a lie to both parts.

## How a dado pinches

The iron had been across a side that had nothing behind it but air and a thin wall. The dado was the thin wall. The jaw closed the groove the way a person closes a book they are not reading. The corner looked tight. The groove that was supposed to stay a groove became a pinch.

I dry-fit with the same clamp pattern I will use wet. If a side hollows then, it will hollow with glue in it, only faster, and I will not be allowed to unhollow it after hide has grabbed. I put a caul or a scrap in the bay so the side has a spine. I do not clamp a hollow county and hope the plywood back will iron it later.

A pipe that sits only on the front stile and the back stile will bow the field between them if the field is a wide side. That bow is a drawer that binds at the middle and rattles at the ends. I have made that drawer. I added a caul across the field the next time and the bay stayed a bay.

## Which rectangle I write down

I write the two diagonals of the opening on a scrap, not in my head. The numbers do not have to be poetry. They have to match each other closer than a drawer will forgive. I do not invent a thousandth. If they are off by a fat pencil line, I loosen, I move iron, I check the inside square again. I do not split the difference by cranking the short diagonal until the tape is happy and the side is a banana.

A winding look if there will be a lid. See *The case that winds* (essay 18). Square in plan is not flat in the world. I have glued a box that taped well and still took a propeller from a hollow bench. The inside square will not catch that. The sticks will. Both.

Afternoon through the long glass is a flatterer on a rim. Raking light on the inside corner is meaner and better. I get my head in the bay. I do not square a case from the vise end of the bench, the way I do not trust a chair from that same end. See *The clamp that racks the chair* (essay 38). That view is the view of the person who already decided the clamps were right.

## After I walk

I wipe. I check the inside once more. I look under the feet if the case will stand as a case. Then I leave it. The shop can be loud. The box cannot. A setting hide corner that gets a square shoved into it again is a shoulder you opened because you were lonely.

PVA will let you fuss longer and still punish you. A gelled film that you rack back to “true” is a film you tore. The next morning the tear is a hairline you will call figure.

If I find the pinch the next day, I do not make the drawer the apology. I recut the box, or I live with a bay I am ashamed of. I have lived with the ashamed bay. Hunting a square while hide is skinning is how the poke happens.

## The judgment

Inside, then quiet. The wedge drawer is why I keep a square that lives only for glue-ups, already on the bench, not in a drawer I would have to hunt.
