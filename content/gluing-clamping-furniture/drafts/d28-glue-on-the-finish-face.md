I oiled a cherry rail and a pale lightning bolt appeared where I had wiped a bead. The wood around it went amber. The smear stayed a plastic skin. I had sanded. I had thought I had sanded enough. Enough is deeper than the pore you filled.

Glue on a finish face is a finish problem that starts in the glue-up. Tape the show side of a dovetail. Tape the arris of a table seam if you are a sloppy roller. Dry-fit so the squeeze comes where you can lift it. Put the show face where you can see it.

## Oil is the priest that will not lie

Film finishes can bury a small smear. Oil will not. Water-based pigment will not. I test a scrap with the same glue and the same finish when I am on a wood I do not know this month. Cherry and maple are mean. Oak is a map. Walnut hides more and still does not hide a ridge.

## Tape is not shame

Blue tape on a show pin line looks like a shop that has been burned. It has. I peel the tape before the glue cures to it like a second skin. Tape left overnight is a different problem.

## The sanding that was not enough

I had taken that cherry rail to a grit I trusted. The surface felt clean. The pores did not. Cherry will take a smear down into the vessel and keep it. Sanding the face is sanding the roof of a hole that is still full. I have chased a bolt with 180 and then 220 and then a scraper, and the bolt got quieter and did not leave. Oil found it the way oil finds every plastic county.

The honest test is a wipe of mineral spirits or a drop of the oil you mean to use, on a scrap you glued the same day with the same rag habit. If the scrap shows a pale river, the rail will show a pale river. I keep a cutoff of the same board now. See `d43` when the scar is already there. This page is the minute the bead appears.

## Where the show face sits

I used to glue a table seam with the show face down on the bench because the bench was flat and I was tired of pads walking. The squeeze ran to the low side. The low side was the face someone would oil. I lifted what I could. I painted the rest. The lightning bolt was that run, later, in amber.

Show face up if I can. Show face where I can see the bead and lift it. If the work has to go show-down, I tape first, and I put wax paper between the tape and any pad that might print. I have glued a seam to a bench through a smear I did not see until I rolled the top. That smear had the texture of the bench. It was a map of the wrong county.

A dovetail pin line is a finish face that looks like joinery. Glue loves the end grain of a pin. A wipe there is a pale pin in a dark row, or a dark pin in a pale row, depending on the stain. I tape the show pins. I butter the tails where I can hide a little extra. I do not flood the pins and hope the rag is honest.

## Tape that stays too long

I left blue tape on a walnut stile overnight because I was done being careful and I wanted to go eat. In the morning the tape had a glue skin of its own. Peeling it lifted a little fiber and left a shiny county the width of the tape. Oil did not hide that either. It introduced a stripe.

I peel when the bead is lifted and the joint is closed, before the glue and the adhesive become one religion. Cheap tape can bleed adhesive into a pore. I have seen that on maple as a tacky ghost after the paper was gone. I use tape I have already trusted on a scrap of the same wood. If the scrap ghosts, the rail will ghost.

## Species that tell on you

Cherry writes in lightning. Maple writes in pale lakes. Quarter-sawn oak writes in rays, which is the lid in `d27`. Walnut will hide a thin smear from across the shop and show it at dinner, under a raking lamp, as a dull river.

I do not treat "I will film-finish this" as permission to paint. I have changed my mind in the finish room, from film to oil, more than once. The smear already voted. A sloppy roller on a table seam is how the arris gets a coat that was meant for the cheek. See `d25`. Tape is cheaper than hoping I will roll perfectly at the end of a long edge in August.

## The dry-fit that aims the squeeze

I dry-fit so I know which side the bead prefers. A sprung edge will spit glue toward the hollow. If the river wants the show face, I change the caul, or I recut. I do not plan to wipe a river off cherry and call it a system. The lightning bolt is still in that rail. I can find it with my thumb in the dark.

## The judgment

Protect the face you will oil. Lift the bead. Do not trust sanding to undo a pore full of PVA. The lightning bolt is why I tape cherry now even when I am "being careful."
