A handscrew is two wood jaws and two screws. You can set the jaws out of parallel on purpose. You can pre-set the opening in the dry-fit and then only twist a little when the glue is on. That pre-set is why they still earn a crowded bench. Open time is a clock. A clamp you have to assemble is a thief.

## Wood jaws are pads

They still dent if you crank, and they still pick up glue if you skip wax. They stain less than a pipe. They print less than a steel parallel on pine. I wax the faces when the work is a show rail. I keep a pair that are only for glue so I do not chase a pitch-covered jaw across a cherry cheek.

## Where they sit

Chair rails. Tapered work. A caul you want to hold while the bars come. A lamination edge. A small box. I do not try to span a dining top with handscrews. That is what pipes and cauls are for.

## The pipe that wanted a rectangle

The rail came off the jointer honest and tapered on purpose — thicker at the shoulder, thinner toward the tenon cheek I was not gluing yet. I reached for a pipe because pipes were what I trusted for pressure. The pipe sat on the thick end and hung in the air at the thin. I packed a scrap. The scrap walked. The rail skated. The film, which had been a film, became a smear on one face and a dry county on the other. I slacked, I wiped what I could, I reached for the handscrews I should have reached for first.

Jaws angled to the two faces. One screw to set the angle, one to close. The rail stopped skating. A bead appeared where I wanted it. I had treated those handscrews like antiques I was supposed to respect and not use. That morning they earned the hook they hang on. If a jaw is checked, I do not crank it into a show face.

## Pre-set is the whole religion

I open the handscrew on the dry-fit until the jaws kiss both faces with a light close. I leave it there, or I mark the screw with a pencil so I can find the same opening after I take it off to spread. When the glue is on I am not building a clamp. I am taking a quarter-turn I already rehearsed. Hide in August will not wait while I hunt a thread.

I have assembled a handscrew during open time — jaws loose, screws crossed, a comedy of wood — and I have watched a good film skin while I swore. That is why they hang where I can reach them, already roughly set for the work I am about to do. A clamp in a drawer is a rumor. A clamp on the hook above the glue bench is a clamp.

Near screw for the opening, far screw for the pinch, or the reverse depending on which way the taper runs. I do it in the dry-fit until my hands remember. I do not invent the memory on a chair with four rails going at once.

## The pair that is only for glue

I used a handscrew that had been holding a door overnight. The jaws had pitch and a little PVA from a different year. I put that jaw on a cherry rail. The cherry took a print of someone else's glue and a streak of pitch I found when I oiled. I keep a glue pair now. Waxed faces. No pitch. No rust on the screws if I can help it. The shop pair can hold a door. The glue pair does not leave the glue bench.

Wax is not optional on a show rail. Hide will marry a wood jaw to a cheek like a bad idea. PVA will too if you smear. I wax, or I put a scrap of wax paper on the jaw, and I still check that the paper did not become the smear. Newspaper prints. I have printed.

They still dent. A hero crank will dish pine. See `d31`. I close until the film is a film and I stop.

## Where I will not ask them to work

A dining top wants pipes and cauls. See `d36`. Handscrews on a six-board argument are a way to feel busy while the field rises. I use them to hold a caul while the pipes come. They will not replace a parallel on a box or a band on a hoop. See `d32` and `d34`. A lamination edge likes a handscrew that can follow the taper of the stack. I do not pretend a pair is a vacuum. They are a caul you can tighten.

## The hunt I do not repeat

I once spent a hide minute looking for a handscrew that was under a tarp because I had used it as a weight. The rail gelled. They hang in the open now. If the hook is empty, I am not in the glue chapter yet. I open and close them now and then so the screws do not become sculpture.

## The judgment

If the faces are not parallel, think handscrew before you invent a jig in open time. Pre-set it dry. Wax it. The tapered rail is why a pair still hangs where I can reach them without hunting.
