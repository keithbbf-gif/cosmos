The seam came out of clamps looking clean. Too clean. No bead, no witness, a matte line that looked like virtue. I knocked the boards apart the next week on a scrap pair I had glued the same way. The cheeks were dull in patches. Those patches had never been wet. I had starved a joint and called it tidy.

The other sin is the flood. Glue everywhere, a tenon that pumps back out of the mortise, a drawer interior that becomes a skating rink. Flood is not insurance. Flood is hydraulics and stain.

## What I want to see

An even film on the faces that matter. When the joint closes, a thin continuous bead. Not pearls with dry counties between them. Not a river. The bead is the witness that the film existed. I wipe it. I do not miss it.

How much: enough that I cannot see dry wood through the film, not so much that it drips off a vertical cheek before I can close. I will not invent ounces per foot. The cheek and the light are the measure.

## Starved is a clamp story too

Hungry bars pump the middle dry. A fit that was already sloppy lets the glue run into the gap and leave the high spots naked. Recut. Ease the pressure. See *Glue is not a clamp* (essay 1) and *Enough to close, not to crush* (essay 31). Starvation is often a pressure problem wearing a spread costume.

## Drowned is a mortise story too

A puddle in the floor of a mortise will push the tenon back at you. The joint looks possessed. It is just glue with nowhere to go. Butter the cheeks. Leave the floor a film. See *Both cheeks, not a puddle in the mortise* (essay 24).

## The scrap pair I keep for this

I glue a pair of scraps the way I am about to glue the work — same bottle, same room, same clock — when I am unsure whether I have a film or a mood. I keep those scraps longer than they deserve. They sit near the bottles so I remember that a matte line can be a starved line. I do not knock apart a dining seam to learn this. I knock apart a pair I can throw in the stove. The stove does not care about my virtue.

The work that taught me the other way was a drawer I flooded. Glue everywhere, a skating rink on the inside, a tenon that pumped back out of the skirt like the joint was breathing. I wiped for a night. Stain still found the smear.

## What an even bead actually looks like

I want a thin continuous witness, the width of a thread you could pick up with a nail. Pearls with dry counties between them mean I missed a stretch or the clamp pumped the middle. A river means I poured and the show face is about to become a map. No bead means a dry cheek or a gap I closed with iron. I have celebrated no bead. The scrap pair is why I stopped. The witness chapter can name the rag. This page only refuses a clean line I have to trust.

## Starved is often a bar

I have rolled a perfect film and then cranked the pipes until the middle of a pecan seam went hungry. The ends beaded like a garden hose. The middle came out matte. Hungry bars pump. A caul would have spread the kiss. A lighter turn would have left the film. I treated the crank like virtue. Virtue was a dry county in the field. I have blamed the bottle for a joint I starved with a screw.

August in this shop will also skin a thin film before you close if you spread a whole table base and then hunt a pad. The film looks wet. It is a skin. The skin is not a glue line. I spread fewer joints when the glass wall is throwing heat. I do not spread more glue to buy time. More glue is a river, not a longer minute.

## Drowned is a hole with a floor

I have stood there pushing a tenon, the joint weeping from a pin hole, sure I was in a haunted piece. I was in a cylinder. I used to pour because pouring felt like care. A drowned edge joint is a skating rink and a starved high spot in the same seam. The puddle goes somewhere. Somewhere is usually the show face and the places the clamp did not kiss. I have sanded both. I look. If I can see wood, I add a little. If I can see a lake, I scrape a little off with a stick and then close.

## The judgment

I want a witness bead I can wipe, not a clean line I have to trust. Clean after the wipe. Not clean because nothing came out. Nothing came out is a dry cheek or a gap. I have celebrated both. I do not anymore.
