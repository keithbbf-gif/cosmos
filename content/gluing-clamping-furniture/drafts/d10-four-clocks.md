I pulled clamps at the hour because the bottle said I could. I jointed the panel an hour after that because I am impatient and the thicknesser was free. The seam smeared. Not a lot. Enough that the cutter left a shiny streak and the next day the streak was a hairline. The glue had been a gel. I had treated a gel like a board.

Sheets hide four clocks in two sentences. I name them out loud so I do not mix them.

The shiny streak is still in that panel. I did not recut the whole top. I lived with a seam that looks closed in flat light and writes itself in raking light off the long glass. I tell the story when I want to wait.

## Open time

The minutes you can still assemble after the glue hits the first face. Heat shortens it. Dry wood shortens it. A big panel eats it. Hide’s open time is a sprint. PVA’s is a walk you still should not turn into lunch. Epoxy’s is a pot-life cousin. `[VERIFY]` the sheet.

If you are hunting a clamp in open time, you already lost a shoulder. That sentence lives in the craft pack. It lives here as a clock name.

I have spread four panel edges, walked to the other end of the 65-foot shop for a caul I should have staged, and come back to cheeks that had gone dull. I closed anyway. The ends grabbed. The middle peeled. That is open time spent on a walk.

## Closed time

The minutes after the faces meet and before you have to apply pressure. Some bottles care. Some shops pretend they do not. If you close a panel and then wander to find cauls, you can skin the film and starve the middle. I dry-stage the cauls. Closed time is not a coffee.

I closed a walnut pair once because I wanted the mess off my hands, then I went to find pads. When I came back the seam had a skin I could feel with a nail. I cranked anyway. The next morning I could rock paper under the field. The paper belongs to the caul essay. The minutes belong here. Closed time is the walk from “together” to “under iron.” I do not take that walk to the coffee pot.

## Clamp time

The hours the joint must stay under pressure. This is the number people read. It is not full strength. It is “you may take the iron off.” I still like overnight on structural joints. I have racked a chair at the hour to teach it a lesson. The lesson was mine.

The chair was a shop chair, not a customer chair. I pulled at the hour because I wanted the clamps for a case. I set the chair on the floor and I leaned. The rack was polite and permanent. The film had been a gel that could still move. I clamped it back, I waited the night, and I still recut one rail because I had taught the joint a parallelogram. Clamp time is permission to remove iron. It is not permission to sit, to rack, or to load a truck.

## Cure

Full strength. Sometimes a day. Sometimes more in the cold. Machine too early and you smear, you dish, you leave a seam that looks closed and behaves like a hinge. Load too early — a table on a truck, a leaf you sit on — and you creep a PVA line that would have been fine on Tuesday.

Hide reaches handling strength fast and still wants you to stop poking. Epoxy can be a day or two before I trust a slab on a base. Urea wants the bag left alone. `[VERIFY]` each religion against that day’s sheet.

The thicknesser hour is the one I fail. The machine is free. The panel is “dry to the touch.” Touch is not cure. I now write a machine time on the same tape as the pull time, and the machine time is later. If the tape says Thursday and it is Wednesday afternoon, the thicknesser can stay free. I have eaten that sentence. I still want to joint. I do not.

## One glue-up, one set of clocks

I do not mix a fast interior PVA on one end of a case and a long-open cousin on the other and then use one timer. I do not pull clamps on the “hour” bottle and machine like it was hide. I write the pull time on a scrap of tape on the clamp. The tape is uglier than a mistake.

I did the mixed-bottle case. Fast yellow on the first two corners because the bottle was in my hand. Dark, long-open cousin on the last two because I was scared of the clock. I set one timer. The first corners had grabbed while I was still spreading the last. I racked the box to close the late corners and I starved the early ones. I sawed it. I opened one bottle. I wrote one time. I do not run two religions under one piece of tape.

August and January change all four clocks. The names stay. The minutes move.

January stretches clamp and cure until a person who believes the wall clock becomes a person who machines a gel. August shrinks open and closed until a person who believes January’s memory becomes a person who skins a cheek. I read the sheet that morning. I believe the timer in the room.

I write the bottle, the pull time, and the earliest I will machine on the same scrap of tape. I stick it on the bar that will still be there when I am tempted. I have peeled that tape off because I wanted the machine. The days I leave it are the days I still have a seam instead of a shiny streak.

## The judgment

Open is assembly. Closed is the walk to the clamp. Clamp is the iron. Cure is the board. If you treat them as one hour, you will plane a gel and call it a seam. I have. The shiny streak is still the picture I use when I want to wait.
