The urea cup was already warm and the shop was already closed because the afternoon had turned. I opened the long-wall door and I did not make a story out of toughness. The smell is not hide. The smell is a reason.

This essay is not a safety program. It will not name a cartridge, a PEL, or a medical claim. The sheets on urea, PU, CA, and epoxy already talk. `[VERIFY]` those sheets. The shop rule is simpler: if the glue has a fume story, the air moves, the gloves are not optional when the sheet says skin, and leftover cups are not something I wash in the same sink I like.

## Which religions talk

Urea: formaldehyde. PU: isocyanates. CA: sharp fog, accelerator. Epoxy: amines, sensitizers. PVA and hide are kinder and still not food. I do not spread urea with a finger. See `d04`, `d06`, `d07`, `d05`.

## The lid

Powder lids tape shut. Bottles cap. Mixed cups cure in the cup and then trash, not down a drain. A hide pot has a lid so I do not cook a room for no reason.

## The afternoon that turned

It was the kind of Bradley County turn where you shut the long-wall door against rain and then forget you shut it. Hide is animal and heat. Urea is a different pot and a different reason. See `d04`. I have run urea with the door shut because I did not want the bag to cool and I did not want weather on the work. The work can take a draft. I cannot take a closed shop and a warm cup as a personality.

I mix what I will use. I do not walk away from an open urea cup to hunt a caul. I do not treat PU foam as a smell I should get used to. See `d06`. I do not spray CA accelerator in a still room for fun. See `d07`. I do not wipe epoxy with the hide rag. See `d05`.

I keep the sheets where I can find them. I do not keep a romance about “we’ve always done it this way” next to a urea bag. Always is how a closed afternoon happens twice.

## Cups that are not a sink job

I have rinsed a cup in the sink I like because I was in a hurry and the cup was “just a little.” The sink did not need a little. The cup could have sat on a board until it was a hockey puck.

I have skipped gloves on epoxy because the mix was small. Small is how a sensitization story starts. I am not writing a medical claim. I am writing that I put the gloves on now, and I do not tell myself a small cup is a different religion.

CA on a jig is a window-open job if I am using accelerator. The fog is sharp. I do it at the door, or I do not do it. A jig can wait an hour. A finger glued to a fence cannot wait an hour. I do not eat over an open pot. A lid is part of the glue-up. Close the window when the cup is trash, not while the cup is still a job.

## The long wall is the window

This shop’s window is the door in the glass wall. I prop it before I mix urea, not after the cup is already warm and I notice I cannot think. A box fan in the opening is not a personality. It is how the smell leaves instead of sitting in the rafters. I do not point the fan at a hide pot to “help it.” Hide does not want a wind that skins the brush. Urea wants the air to move past me, not at the bag.

I tape powder lids shut when I am done. I have found a urea bag the next week with a dusting on the shelf because I folded the top and called it closed. The shelf does not need that dusting. The bag goes taped. Mixed leftover stays in its cup on a board until it is a puck, then trash.

I do not wash a PU glove in the sink I like. I do not leave a CA bottle open while I hunt accelerator. The cap is the other window. Close it. The door stays open until the cup is no longer a job.

## The judgment

Air first. Sheet second. Pride never. If we cannot ventilate, we do not run that glue that day. A cabinet can wait for PVA. A crest rail can wait for a day with a door. The closed-shop urea afternoon is why I write this without romance.
