I ran a bottle down six feet of walnut and called it spread. The bead looked confident. The cheeks after a knock-apart were striped: wet, dry, wet. The bottle had drawn a road. It had not painted a county.

The bottle is a reservoir. It is allowed to start a bead. Something else has to make a film. I keep a cheap roller for panels, a brush that is only the hide brush, and I am not too proud to use a finger on a tenon when the roller is the wrong width.

## Roller

A small foam or rubber roller on a long edge is the fastest even film I know. I load it from a bead on a scrap, not from a lake in a tray I will knock over. I roll until I cannot see dry wood. I do not roll so wet that the glue drips onto the show face.

## Brush

Hide wants a brush. The pot essay already taught the sheet off the bristles. PVA will take a brush. I do not use the hide brush for PVA. The religions stay in their cups. A silicone brush is easy to wash and easy to pretend is clean when it is not. I look.

## Finger

A tenon cheek, a dovetail pin, a place a roller will not go. I wear a glove if the glue is a glue I do not want in a cut. I wash. I do not spread urea or epoxy with a bare finger and then eat an apple. See `d47`.

## Bottle only

A biscuit slot. A small repair. A place I will immediately wipe into a film with something else. A bottle-only panel is a striped panel.

## The road on six feet of walnut

The bead had looked confident. A yellow road down a long edge, the kind of line that photographs like competence. I closed it. I even had a witness in places. The knock-apart scraps I ran the same way — because I did not trust my own hurry — came apart striped: wet, dry, wet, the bottle’s path and the counties it never visited. The work would have been the same counties, hidden until oil.

The roller is a panel tool. It is the wrong width for a tenon cheek. I have rolled a tenon anyway and painted the shoulders, the end grain, and my wrist. The cheek got a film. The shoulder got a skating rink. A drip on the show is a smear I scheduled.

## The hide brush stays a hide brush

The brush that lives in the pot does not visit the yellow bottle. PVA in the hide bristles is a mixed religion in a tool. The next chair that was supposed to come apart in twenty years gets a little white fossil in the film. I have found that fossil on a brush I swore I had washed. I looked. I had not looked hard enough.

PVA will take a cheap brush. A silicone brush is easy to wash and easy to pretend is clean when it is not. I flex it. I look at the heel, where yesterday’s skin hides. A skin in the heel draws roads the way a bottle draws roads. I throw a brush that has become a sculpture. Glue is cheaper than a striped seam.

Urea has its own stick and its own cup. I do not “use the stick” across religions. The stick becomes a brick, or worse, a brick that flakes into the next film. See `d04` if the powder is the afternoon. This page is only the drawer: one tool, one cup, one wash.

## A finger is a tool I wash

The apple is not a joke. It is a thing I have done with PVA on my hand and decided not to repeat with a pot that has a sheet I actually read. See `d47`.

A gloved finger still has to make a film. I have dabbed and called it buttered. Dab is a road the width of a fingerprint. I look at the cheek in the light. If I can see dry wood I have not finished. If I can see a drip starting for the floor I have started a puddle.

## Bottle only, and when I mean it

A biscuit slot. A small repair. A crack I will immediately wipe into a film with a finger. Those are bottle jobs. A six-foot walnut edge is not a bottle job. A mortise floor is not a bottle job. I have used the bottle for both because the bottle was in my hand. The hand is not a reason.

I stage the tool in the dry-fit the way I stage the iron. If the work is a panel, the roller is on the bench before the cap comes off. If the work is hide, the brush is already in the pot. If the work is a chair rail, a glove and a rag are where my hand will be. Hunting a roller after the bead is down is how you get a road that skins in August before you have painted the county.

## The judgment

Pick the tool for the surface. Make a film, not a road. Wash the tool before it becomes a sculpture. The striped walnut is why a roller lives in the same drawer as the bottles now.
