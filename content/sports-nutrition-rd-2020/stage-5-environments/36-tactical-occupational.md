# Load carriage is not a split routine

Soldiers, firefighters, wildland crews, and some law-enforcement units live in the energy-deficit papers without buying a gym membership. Gwin’s participants did treadmill load carriage, deadlifts, and box step-ups at the end of five shorted days. That is occupational training with a tracer in the vein.

## What that literature is for

It is for the week when energy intake cannot match expenditure — field feeding, a fire, a multi-day operation — and someone still has to walk with weight. Whole-body net balance becomes the outcome that matters alongside the biopsy. EAA-enriched whey looking better than a mixed meal for whole-body NET (Gwin 2021) is a field-feeding hint, not a barracks menu I would run for a year.

The 2024 MSSE paper is the “if you can only add energy, add it as EAA, not as sugar, if MPS during underfeeding is the goal.” That is a narrow, expensive sentence. It is also the opposite of how most MREs are built.

Pasiakos’ broader military nutrition line (much of it just before and through this window) is why I stop telling a wildland firefighter that 1.2 g/kg is “fine because they aren’t bodybuilders.” Their deficit can be a physique-athlete deficit without the mirror.

## Practice I will put on a card

- When energy can be matched, use the ordinary athletic protein band and ordinary food. A firehouse that can cook is not a field problem.
- When energy cannot be matched, raise protein density and consider an EAA-intact hybrid for the post-shift feeding. Cite Gwin, do not cite a tactical influencer. The 2021 paper is five days and ten people. Treat it as a hint for the worst week, not a year-round menu.
- Strength work still pays (Tagawa 2022). A crew that only runs will not get a strength adaptation from a sachet. Load carriage is concurrent training with a pack. Protein is the constant; carbohydrate still has to match the shift.
- Sleep and the overnight gap still exist after a night tour. Snijders/Reis are allowed on a firehouse fridge.
- No medical claims about rhabdomyolysis, heat illness, or kidney stress. Occupational medicine owns those words.

I have more patience for a protein packet in a truck than I do for a 19-year-old’s “tactical” pre-workout. The first is a Gwin problem. The second is a Maughan problem. Do not mix them.
