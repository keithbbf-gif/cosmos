# Altitude steals appetite and rearranges the plate

UEFA 2021 is unusually concrete here: above about 1,500 m, appetite falls and food preference shifts toward carbohydrate at the expense of fat and protein. Body-mass hold becomes the priority on a multi-day camp. Above 3,000 m they mention iron in the context of increased red-cell production — an iron-food and screening note for the medical team, not a protein claim.

## What I will not invent

I will not invent a 2024 paper that says hypoxia raises the protein RDA by a tidy percentage. I will not write that protein “supports hematology.” Iron, energy, and a physician sit in that lane.

I will write what the football document wrote: **liquid and easy carbohydrate foods keep the day alive**; protein still has to be smuggled into those foods or it quietly disappears. A camp that only tracks body weight and hematocrit and never tracks lunch will come home lighter for the wrong reasons.

Gwin’s energy-deficit work is the rhyme if the athlete is both hypoxic and underfed — a common amateur-mountaineering pattern, less common in a well-catered endurance camp. If energy is short, amino acids get reassigned. Altitude is a great way to make energy short without meaning to.

## What the room actually looks like

I have sat in dining halls at 2,200 m where the only protein before a morning session was a triangle of processed cheese. The staff was tracking hematocrit like a religion and tracking lunch like an afterthought. Body mass drifted down 1.5 kg in a week and everyone called it “adaptation.” Sometimes it is fluid. Sometimes it is a kid who cannot face another plate of boiled pasta at 7 a.m. because the headache is already there.

UEFA’s preference-shift note matters here. If the athlete spontaneously reaches for bread and jam, that is not a character flaw. Hypoxia changes what looks edible. Your job is to put a protein food *on* the carbohydrate they will actually swallow: yogurt on the granola, eggs on the toast, milk in the cocoa, a tested isolate in the bottle they are already carrying for carbohydrate.

## A camp card

- Day one: walk the dining hall before you write the macro sheet. If the protein option is one dried-out chicken tray at 18:00, the plan is already a fiction.
- Pack a batch-tested protein and a shelf-stable dairy or soy option. Maughan 2018 still applies in a mountain hotel.
- Do not start a physique cut at 2,400 m. Gwin’s queue-reassignment papers are what a cut plus hypoxia looks like without needing a new trial.
- Keep protein in the breakfast you can control. Trommelen 2023 is permission to make dinner large if that is the only staff-plated meal; it is not permission to skip breakfast for 10 days.
- Iron screening is a medical job. I will put heme and non-heme foods on the menu. I will not write that whey “supports hematology.”

If the athlete comes home lighter, ask whether they *ate*. Altitude is a great alibi for a low-protein week. It is a terrible one.
