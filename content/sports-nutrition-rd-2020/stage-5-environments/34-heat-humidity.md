# Heat asks for water and salt. Protein still asks for a plate.

Hot-weather camps wreck appetite. That is the protein paper, even when the paper is titled something else.

## What the consensus documents actually prioritized

UEFA 2021’s stressful-environments section is fluid, cooling, carbohydrate, and the reality that players stop eating. Protein targets do not get a special heat multiplier in that document. They get a warning that the *plan* will fail because the buffet looks impossible.

There is not a 2020s tracer trial I trust that says “add 0.3 g/kg because it is 34 °C.” Sweat nitrogen losses exist as a textbook footnote. They are not why a midfielder came back from pre-season looking flat. Under-eating is.

Fuchs 2020 is a cold paper, not a heat paper, but it is the cousin: **temperature interventions change how muscle sees a feeding.** I will not invert it and claim a sauna “helps the shake.” I do not have that biopsy.

## What I have actually seen fail

A player who eats 2.1 g/kg at home will eat 1.1 g/kg in a humid pre-season if every protein food is a hot stew and the only cold options are fruit and a sports drink. The GPS looks fine on day three. The gym numbers start to look tired on day eight. Staff talks about “heat adaptation.” Sometimes that is true. Sometimes breakfast died.

UEFA did not write a heat-specific protein multiplier because they did not have one. They wrote a fluid and carbohydrate chapter and trusted you to notice that protein is riding in the same appetite. I will not invent sweat-urea math that turns a 0.1 g/kg rounding error into a new RDA. I will invent a cold-protein station.

## Practice

- Keep the daily band (1.6–2.2 g/kg unless the week is a cut). Change the *form*: cold dairy, tofu, tuna, leftover chicken, skyr, a shake that is not 80 °C.
- Put the protein in the first meal after the heat session, when they will still drink something. Waiting until a sit-down dinner at 9 p.m. is how the grams evaporate.
- Do not pile 50 g of whey onto a gut that already holds two bottles of carbohydrate drink unless you have practiced it. GI is a performance outcome.
- Cooling after *lifting* in a heat camp is still the Fuchs conversation if the camp’s job is hypertrophy. Match-turnaround cooling is a staff decision. Do not run both fights in the same hour without saying so.
- Watch energy availability. Heat plus two-a-days is how “adaptable” low energy availability becomes the other kind (Mountjoy 2023). Protein is not the diagnosis.

No heat-illness treatment claims. No “protein hydrates.” Fluid and sodium do the hydration job. Protein does the remodeling job if it gets eaten. A heat camp that only stocks carbohydrate gels has made a protein decision. They just did not write it down.
