# Hotel breakfast is a protein intervention

The paper is rarely titled “protein at the Hampton Inn.” The week is. UEFA 2021 put travel in writing because that is where the 1.6–2.2 g/kg plan goes to die: late arrival, pastry buffet, a 4 p.m. session, a restaurant that thinks “protein” means a garnish of salmon.

## What we can cite without pretending there is a travel-RCT

UEFA’s stressful-environments chapter is about fluid, carbohydrate, sleep, and the fact that food access is a performance variable. Their protein lines do not change on the road. The *application* does. Pre-sleep protein was already the gap in home data (0.1 g/kg). On the road it is worse.

Maughan 2018 (IOC supplements) is the other travel document: a tub in a suitcase is a contamination and customs problem. Food first; if you pack a powder, pack a **batch-tested** one.

Trommelen 2023 is oddly useful in a hotel. If dinner is the one real meal you can control, a large complete-protein dinner is not “wasted.” If breakfast is the only staff-controlled meal, make it a 30–40 g feeding and stop apologizing for the extra eggs.

Ferrando 2023’s EAA stand is the emergency-tool paragraph: when there is no kitchen, a complete EAA or intact-protein serving is a kinetics tool, not a lifestyle.

## A travel card that fits in a phone

- Identify the one meal you can make boring and adequate. Usually breakfast.
- Pack a tested protein if the destination is a mystery. Do not pack six untested pre-workouts.
- Evening games: a real feeding after, even if it is Greek yogurt from a French petrol station.
- Do not start a cut on the road. Gwin’s deficit papers are not a hotel protocol.

Travel is logistics. Protein R&D did not need a new molecule for it. It needed a grocery bag.

The week I remember is not a tracer week. It is a Tuesday landing, a Wednesday morning session, and a hotel that thinks protein is a single boiled egg. Staff had a beautiful 2.0 g/kg sheet. The buffet had pastry, fruit, and a sausage that was mostly filler. We bought skyr from a supermarket and put it in the team fridge. That is the intervention. Ferrando’s EAA stand is what you use when the supermarket is closed and the session is in 40 minutes. It is not the week’s identity.

Crossing time zones also steals the pre-sleep feeding because “bed” moved. I attach the protein to the *athlete’s* night, not to the local restaurant schedule. UEFA already said players under-eat that slot at home. On the road I assume they under-eat it unless I see the yogurt.
