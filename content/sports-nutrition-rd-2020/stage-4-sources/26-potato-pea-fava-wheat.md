# The new isolates have names now: potato, pea, wheat, and the blend

For a long time “plant protein” in a sports paper meant soy. The Maastricht series in 2021–2024 is the reason I now ask *which* plant.

## Potato

Pinckaers 2022 put 30 g potato-protein concentrate against 30 g milk protein after a unilateral session in 24 young men. Both raised mixed-muscle synthesis above baseline at rest; both raised it more in the worked leg; the two proteins did not differ. Potato’s leucine content in the 2021 review is the detail people skip: they cite about **8.3%**, which sits next to casein and above a lot of folklore about plants.

Potato isolate is not a baked potato. The food has a lot of starch and not a lot of protein per plate. The paper is about the concentrate.

## Wheat

Pinckaers 2021 (*British Journal of Nutrition*): wheat protein, milk protein, and a blend, young men. Wheat is the one that looks like the stereotype — lower essential profile, easier to lose to the rest of the body. The blend was the adult solution. If your vegan athlete’s “protein” is mostly bread, you do not have a philosophy. You have a leucine problem.

## Pea, fava, corn, rice

The 2021 review treats pea as leucine-respectable (they cite about 7.2%) and corn as unusually leucine-rich on paper (a striking 13.5% in their figure — that is zein-ish isolate talk, not a corn-on-the-cob snack). Rice protein’s older acute work (Joy and others, pre-window) was never the problem people thought if the dose was large. Fava is showing up in products faster than in tracer trials. I will not invent a fava MPS paper. I will treat it like pea until someone publishes the biopsy.

Pinckaers’ 2024 thesis sentence is the one I steal: when young, healthy adults eat about **30 g**, several plant proteins raise MPS in a way that is not different from milk. The qualifier is the dose. The qualifier is also “young, healthy.”

## How this changes a shopping list

I will buy a potato or pea isolate for someone who wants a non-soy, non-dairy 30 g feeding. I will not buy a wheat isolate as their only scoop. I will still prefer a mixed plate — tofu, lentils, grains, a potato food if they like it — because humans do not eat single-protein diets unless we make them.

A baked potato is still a carbohydrate with a protein rumor. The isolate paper should not be used to tell a player that fries are a leucine strategy. The 2021 review’s corn number (13.5% leucine) is the same kind of trap: that is an isolate figure, not a bag of popcorn. I say the number in a methods conversation. I do not say it at a concession stand.

Fava is the one I am most tempted to over-write because the products exist. I will not. No biopsy in this source pack. Treat as pea-class until a lab says otherwise. That is the quality rule for this whole series: if I did not read the paper, it does not get a dose.
