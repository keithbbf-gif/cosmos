# Mycoprotein is the fungal food that showed up with biopsies

Most alternative proteins arrive with a press release. Mycoprotein arrived with Exeter’s tracers.

## The 2020 acute trial

Monteyne et al. took 20 resistance-trained young men. After a unilateral session they drank either 31 g milk protein (26.2 g protein, 2.5 g leucine) or 70 g mycoprotein (31.5 g protein, 2.5 g leucine). Mixed-muscle FSR rose in both, and the *rise* was larger after mycoprotein (delta 0.040 vs 0.018 %/h). Postprandial FSR trended higher. That is *AJCN* 2020, doi:10.1093/ajcn/nqaa092.

Notice the food volume. Seventy grams of the fungal food is a serving, not a hidden scoop. The leucine match is why they used that much.

## The BCAA follow-up, same year

A lower 35 g mycoprotein serving, BCAA-enriched to match the bigger serving’s branches, still raised MPS — and still lost to 70 g intact mycoprotein. You cannot sprinkle branches on a smaller food and get the large-food effect. That paper is a quiet assassination of “just add BCAAs.”

## Three days in older adults

Monteyne 2021 (*British Journal of Nutrition*): 19 healthy older adults, 1.8 g/kg/day for three days, unilateral daily lifting. One group ate a mostly animal pattern; the other ate vegan with mycoprotein providing 57% of protein. Daily myofibrillar synthesis, rest and exercised, did not differ. Short. Not a hypertrophy trial. Still the first time a lot of us saw a fungal-forward day look like an omnivorous day on a 24-hour clock.

## How I use it

If someone already likes the food, I count it as a serious protein, not a garnish. If they do not like it, I do not moralize. GI comfort is individual. Allergen history (fungal, mold) is a clinician question, not a blog question.

I will not claim mycoprotein treats cholesterol or anything else. I will claim it has human MPS data, which is more than most “future proteins” can say.

A grocery note people skip: the 70 g in Monteyne 2020 is the *food*, delivering ~31 g protein. If a ready-meal lists 11 g protein per serving, you have not reproduced the paper. You have reproduced a side. The older-adult 1.8 g/kg day used mycoprotein for 57% of protein — that is a menu, not a garnish.

GI comfort is the real limiter I see. Some athletes love the texture. Some bounce it. I do not argue with a gut. I move them to soy, pea, potato, or dairy and keep the grams. The 2020 paper is a reason to take the food seriously when it stays down. It is not a reason to make a locker room eat a fungal mince they hate.

Dunlop 2017 (foundational dose-response) is why Exeter could even pick 70 g: they already knew the food was insulinotropic and bioavailable. The 2020 biopsy is the sports-nutrition punchline. The 2021 older-adult day is the “this is not only a student-sample trick” follow-up. We still do not have a 12-week trained-athlete hypertrophy trial with mycoprotein as the supporting protein. Say so.
