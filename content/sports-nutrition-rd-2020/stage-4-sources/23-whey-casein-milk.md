# Dairy proteins are still the calibration curve

Almost every 2020s protein paper that wants to say something new still brings a milk glass to the lab. Potato vs milk. Mealworm vs milk. Mycoprotein vs milk. 25 g vs 100 g of milk protein. Dairy is not “the best” in a moral sense. It is the ruler.

## What the ruler showed after 2020

Trommelen 2023 used intrinsically labeled milk protein at 25 and 100 g. That is a dairy paper and a dose paper. The 100 g feeding kept working for 12 hours. Milk protein is a mix — whey plus casein — which is probably why it can spike and linger.

Pinckaers 2022: 30 g potato protein vs 30 g milk protein, unilateral lift, young men. Mixed-muscle synthesis rose the same at rest and after exercise. The ruler did not beat the challenger at a matched 30 g.

Hermans 2021: 30 g lesser mealworm vs 30 g milk. Digestion, appearance, MPS at rest and after exercise — no significant differences.

Monteyne 2020 is the paper where milk *lost* the acute comparison: leucine-matched mycoprotein (70 g food / ~31 g protein) vs 31 g milk protein. The fungal food produced a larger rise in mixed-muscle FSR. Read the methods before you throw your whey out. The servings were leucine-matched, not gram-of-powder-matched in the casual sense, and mycoprotein is a whole food with a different matrix.

Oikawa 2020: whey vs collagen, older women. Whey was the muscle protein. Collagen was not. Dairy vs a glycine-rich peptide is not a close fight for myofibrillar synthesis.

## Whey vs casein in 2026 practice

I still use whey when I want a compact, leucine-dense feeding after training or when appetite is gone. I still use casein or skyr or cottage cheese when the next eight hours are sleep. The 2020s did not publish a definitive overnight whey-vs-casein deathmatch that retires that habit. They published a lot of milk-protein mixed drinks and a lot of “30 g of X equals 30 g of milk.”

Lactose, allergy, ethics, and preference are sufficient reasons to leave dairy. They are not reasons to leave *protein*. The rest of this stage is the map.

Milk as a food is still allowed to be the plan. A litre of milk is not a personality, but it is a lot of complete protein, carbohydrate, and fluid in a cheap bottle. UEFA’s leucine table is basically a dairy-and-meat shopping list with soy standing next to it. I use that table when a player asks “what does 2.5 g of leucine look like” and I do not make them buy a tub to see it.

Hydrolysates — “pre-digested” whey — show up in products as a speed claim. The 2020s did not give me a trained-athlete hypertrophy trial that says hydrolysate beats concentrate once the leucine and the grams are matched. I treat hydrolysate as a sometimes-easier gut, sometimes-worse taste, same job. If the label is charging a 40% tax for the word “hydro,” I buy concentrate and dinner.
