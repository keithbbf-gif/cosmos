# Light is a constraint. Low-protein is a second constraint.

Climbers, gymnasts, dancers, jockeys, lightweight rowers, physique athletes in a prep: the body-mass target is the sport. The mistake is treating protein as the first thing to cut because chicken has calories.

## Why this population keeps showing up in the deficit literature

Longland 2016 (foundational) and the Hector/Phillips line: you can lose fat and hold or even add lean mass in some trained people if the deficit is not stupid and protein and lifting stay in the picture. UEFA 2021 repeats that possibility and then raises protein to **2.0–2.4 g/kg** when energy is restricted.

Gwin’s 2020–2024 work is the warning label. In a 30% deficit, whole-body amino-acid demand goes up. A pretty 20 g feeding can look adequate on a poster and still leave the whole-body budget short. Physique athletes who already eat 2.5 g/kg are not the same as a climber who “doesn’t want to get bulky” and eats 0.9 g/kg of mostly toast.

Nunes 2022 and Tagawa 2020/2022 still say the lean-mass increment from extra protein is small. In a power-to-weight sport, “small” is sometimes the whole season — a kilogram of lean mass you did not give away.

## Practice without physique-industry voice

- Set protein first in the calorie budget, then fill carbohydrate to the training, then fat to the remainder. That sentence has been true since before 2020. The new papers did not retire it.
- Use isolates when food volume is the enemy. That is a legitimate use of whey or soy isolate. It is not a personality.
- Collagen is not a lean-mass protein (Oikawa 2020). If the athlete loves it for a tendon-loading routine, it sits *on top of*, not instead of, a complete protein. See the collagen draft. No healing claims.
- If the lightness is sliding into REDs territory — missed periods, recurrent bone-stress, collapsing training — protein is not the intervention. The 2023 IOC statement is. I write a referral, not a macro tweak, as the lead sentence.

I will not write “shred.” I will not write a contest-prep medical promise. I will write: **do not run a weight target and a protein deficit at the same time unless you are trying to fail both.**
