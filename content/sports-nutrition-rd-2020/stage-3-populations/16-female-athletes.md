# Female athletes are not small men — and they are not another species

The field spent decades doing protein trials in young men and then writing “athletes” in the title. The 2020s did not finish correcting that. They did give us enough female-specific data to stop two mistakes: copying a 90 kg male’s shake, and inventing a mystical female protein.

## What the reviews actually concluded

Moore, Sygo and Morton (2021, *European Journal of Sport Science*) looked at carbohydrate and protein across the menstrual cycle and compared them to male data. Their line is the one I quote: **it is premature to write sex-specific CHO or protein guidelines if energy needs are met.** Individualize for the sport, the energy availability, the contraceptive, the symptoms. Do not invent a luteal-phase powder.

Mercer et al. (2020, *Nutrients*) systematically reviewed protein requirements in pre-menopausal female athletes. Estimated needs they pulled together sit in a familiar athletic band — on the order of **1.3–1.6 g/kg** depending on endurance, resistance, or intermittent work, with some IAAO-derived figures running higher once you add a safety factor. Malowany 2019 (cited there) put a tracer-derived EAR around 1.5 g/kg in trained women and an RDI-style bump toward **1.9–2.2 g/kg** if you apply the usual variance. That is the top of the same band we already use for men who lift. It is not a different planet.

Oikawa 2020 used healthy older women, not NCAA athletes, and still matters: 30 g whey twice a day raised acute and integrated MPS; 30 g collagen did not. Sex was not the variable. **Quality** was.

Hevia-Larraín 2021 was men. I mention that on purpose. We still need the vegan-vs-omnivore training study in women. Until it exists, I will not pretend we have it.

## Practice that respects the papers and the person

- Start at **1.6–2.2 g/kg** for a female athlete who trains hard and is roughly fueled. That is Moore/Sygo/Morton’s “use the current recommendations” plus Mercer’s upper estimates.
- If she is 54 kg, 25 g is a real meal. If you copied a 40 g-or-nothing rule from a 90 kg teammate, you are the problem.
- Low energy availability changes everything. That is the REDs draft, not a “women need more protein” slogan. Protein cannot patch a 400 kcal hole.
- Do not use “estrogen is protein-sparing” as a reason to underfeed a midfielder. The 2019 IJSNEM special-populations paper discusses hormone effects on oxidation. It does not give you permission to serve a 12 g salad and call it physiology.

Representation in the literature is still bad. That is a research failure. It is not an excuse to sell a pink tub.
