# The anabolic window moved. The session did not.

I still meet athletes who will skip a good dinner to protect a 30-minute post-lift shake, and athletes who will skip the shake because someone on the internet said timing is dead. Both people are reacting to a 2013 argument with 2026 certainty.

## What did not change

Resistance exercise and protein feeding still interact. ISSN 2017 said that in sentence one of the stand, and nobody in the 2020s tracer literature took it back. Muscle stays more willing to use amino acids for hours after lifting — often talked about as a long tail, not a 30-minute hatch. Schoenfeld and Aragon’s older timing reviews already pushed the “window” out toward a matter of hours, especially if the pre-session meal was not a fiction.

UEFA 2021, writing for people who play at 8 p.m. and travel on Sundays, did not tell players to hover over a shaker in the tunnel. They told them to hit **20–25 g high-quality protein every 3–4 hours** in recovery, and to treat the night as a gap that is usually underfed.

## What 2020+ made harder to oversell

If Trommelen 2023 is right that a large feeding is still being used at 12 hours, then “immediately post” is a convenience, not a cliff. If you trained at noon and you eat a real lunch at 1:00, you are in the literature’s good-enough zone. If you trained at 6 a.m. fasted because that is when the ice time is, I would like protein (and carbohydrate, if the session earned it) at the first meal you can keep down. That is not mysticism. That is the first feeding after a stimulus.

The papers do not show that a 90-minute delay, in a person who ate breakfast, erases the week’s adaptation. They also do not show that a fasted lifter who then eats nothing until 3 p.m. is running the same program as the person who ate.

Nunes 2022 and Tagawa 2020/2022 keep dragging the conversation back to **daily intake plus the fact of lifting**. Timing studies that reshuffle the same daily protein usually find small or no chronic differences. That is why I spend my ink on the day, and only then on the clock.

## A timing card I will actually write

1. Eat a protein-containing meal in the hours around training if it does not wreck the session. Before or after both “count.”
2. If the session was whole-body or very hard, err toward the top of the per-meal range (Macnaughton’s 40 g whey finding is still the acute hint).
3. If the next meal is four hours away and the session was fasted, do not wait for a philosophical window. Eat.
4. If you already ate 40 g an hour before the session, you do not owe the shaker a second confession unless you are hungry or the day is short on protein.
5. Night sessions: see the pre-sleep draft. UEFA’s player data say that slot is where the plan usually dies.

Timing is logistics. The session is the stimulus. Protein is the material. Get those three in the same day and you have already beaten most of the marketing.
