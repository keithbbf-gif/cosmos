# Leucine is a trigger, not a religion

Somewhere around 2014, leucine left the biochemistry lecture and joined the tub. Every isolate started advertising milligrams. Every plant protein got a footnote. The useful part of that story survived the 2020s. The useful part is smaller than the marketing.

## What the position stands still say

ISSN 2017 asked for a meal that brings **700–3,000 mg of leucine** plus a full essential-amino-acid complement. UEFA 2021 put a practical flag at about **2.5 g leucine per meal** and then did the grocery math: that is roughly 25 g whey, 140 g lean beef or chicken breast, five eggs, or about 30 g soy isolate. Those are coaching numbers, not receptor Kd values.

Leucine is the amino acid that most reliably knocks on mTORC1 in human feeding studies. It is not a substitute for the other essentials. You cannot sprinkle leucine on jellybeans and call it a protein strategy. Churchward-Venne’s older work already showed that a *low* whey dose dressed with leucine can look like a larger whey dose for a few hours. That is a lab maneuver. It is not “food is optional.”

## Scores: PDCAAS vs DIAAS, and why athletes only half-care

PDCAAS capped scores at 1.0 and used fecal digestibility. DIAAS (FAO 2013) uses ileal digestibility and does not truncate, so a milk or egg protein can outrank a truncated soy. Pinckaers’ 2021 plant-protein review is the sports-nutrition translation: many plant isolates are not “incomplete” in the cartoon sense. Potato protein is leucine-rich (they cite about 8.3%). Corn protein is even richer in leucine on paper. Wheat is the one that keeps failing the eye test in isolation.

DIAAS is useful when you are comparing **isolates** or designing a vegan menu that was accidentally wheat-and-rice. It is less useful when the athlete eats mixed meals. Cheese on chili, tofu with rice, a pea-rice blend — the score of a single powder stops being the plot.

Lim et al. (2021) meta-analyzed animal vs plant protein for lean mass and strength. The animal-protein edge, when it appeared, was small and not a reason to tell a well-planned vegan they cannot train. Hevia-Larraín 2021 later showed matched 1.6 g/kg, soy-supported vegan vs whey-supported omnivore, untrained young men, 12 weeks: same leg lean mass, same fiber CSA story. Quality mattered enough to *plan*. It did not overrule dose and training.

## How I use leucine on a card

- If the meal is dairy, egg, meat, or a decent isolate, I stop counting leucine. The meal already cleared the trigger if the protein grams are real.
- If the meal is bread-heavy, I add beans, soy, potato protein, or a scoop — or I make the portion larger. Pinckaers’ line is the one I repeat: **about 30 g of several plant proteins** can move MPS like milk in young men. Wheat needs help.
- I do not sell crystalline leucine as a lifestyle. ISSN’s 2023 EAA stand is a different tool: free-form essentials are fast, and they can raise MPS at rest with small doses. That is a supplement paper, not a dinner paper.

Quality is the difference between a 25 g whey feeding that does the job and a 25 g collagen feeding that mostly does not (Oikawa 2020 — different draft). It is not a moral ranking of foods.
