# BCAAs are a 20th-century answer to a 21st-century diet

I still see tubs of flavored leucine-isoleucine-valine next to the squat rack. The athletes drinking them often already eat 180 g of protein. That is the whole review.

## The biochemical tease, and Wolfe’s 2017 cold water

Branched-chain amino acids are essential. Leucine is the celebrity. In a muscle cell dish, leucine looks like a switch. In a human who has not eaten a complete essential profile, BCAAs cannot build a complete protein. Wolfe’s 2017 *JISSN* paper — older than this series, still the cleanest short argument — said the quiet part: **BCAA formulas as sold do not reliably raise human MPS**, and they can even be the wrong tool if they compete with other essentials.

The 2020s did not rescue the tub. They walked around it.

West, Monteyne and colleagues (2020) took a *lower* dose of mycoprotein and fortified it with BCAAs to match the BCAA content of a 70 g mycoprotein serving. Blood BCAAs rose faster. MPS did **not** catch the larger intact feeding. Extra branches on a smaller protein dose were not the same as eating the protein.

ISSN’s 2023 EAA stand is, in a way, the field picking a different powder. If you are going free-form, take the **full essential set**, not three of them. The stand’s rest-day doses and the “no need for non-essentials in the sachet” line are about EAAs, not BCAAs.

## Soreness folklore

UEFA 2021 looked at the football recovery literature and shrugged: some studies report a little less soreness with protein or BCAAs after play; the effect is small; it is not why you feed players. That is the right temperature for this topic. Nobody should buy BCAAs to “feel less sore” when a 30 g milk-protein feeding and a night of sleep are sitting right there.

## When I would still allow the tub

Almost never as a *plan*. Maybe as a flavored drink an athlete refuses to drop, if the rest of the diet is already at 1.6–2.2 g/kg and we are not pretending the tub is doing hypertrophy work. Money is usually better spent on food, or on a complete EAA or whey product if food is the constraint.

If the diet is low-protein and plant-skewed, the fix is more total protein and better complementarity — Pinckaers’ 30 g story — not 5 g of cherry BCAAs at 4 p.m.

I still meet endurance athletes who drink BCAAs “so they don’t go catabolic” on a 90-minute easy run. They already ate breakfast. The run is not a famine. UEFA’s line on soreness is the one I repeat: small, inconsistent, not why you feed. If they want a flavored bottle, I would rather see carbohydrate in it if the session is long, or nothing if the session is easy. The tub is a habit with a 2010s receipt.

Free-form EAAs (Ferrando 2023) are the 2020s replacement *when* a free-form tool is actually indicated — travel, deficit, no kitchen. They are still not dinner. They are at least the full essential set instead of three amino acids performing a tribute act.
