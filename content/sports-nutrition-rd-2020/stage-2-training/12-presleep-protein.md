# The overnight gap is the one most athletes still skip

Van Loon’s group spent the 2010s proving you can digest protein in your sleep. The 2020s spent less time rediscovering that and more time asking whether it changes a training block, or just a tracer curve.

## The acute picture, still standing

Snijders, Trommelen, Kouw, Holwerda, Verdijk and van Loon’s 2019 *Frontiers in Nutrition* update is the bridge into our window: protein taken before bed is digested overnight; 40 g of casein is the dose that most consistently raises overnight MPS; pairing it with an evening lift beats protein alone. Kouw’s older-men trial (40 g vs 20 g vs 20 g plus leucine vs placebo) is the dose lesson: **40 g raised overnight myofibrillar synthesis; 20 g did not get there**, and adding 1.5 g leucine to 20 g was not a full rescue.

Reis et al. (systematic review, search to June 2020) landed in the same range: 20–40 g casein about 30 minutes before bed raised overnight whole-body synthesis in young and older men. Chronic training studies looked more helpful in young men over 10–12 weeks than in older men. They also warned that some trials did not match total daily protein, which is the whole methodological sin of this literature.

## Football noticed the hole

UEFA 2021 said the quiet part with intake data: professional players often ate about **0.1 g/kg** in the pre-sleep window. The expert group wanted closer to **0.4 g/kg** in a meal within three hours of bed, or about **0.5 g/kg** if it was a late supplement. That is a practice document citing the Maastricht line, not a new RCT. It is also why I stop calling pre-sleep protein “bro science” in a locker room. The gap is measured.

## How I use it without building a personality around cottage cheese

- If daily protein is already 2 g/kg and dinner was 50 g at 8 p.m., I do not wake someone at 10:30 for a mandatory casein. Trommelen 2023’s long clock is on dinner’s side.
- If dinner was pasta and the session ended at 9 p.m., a 30–40 g slowish feeding is the 2019–2021 habit I still like. Casein, skyr, milk, leftover chicken. Whey is not banned; the overnight clock is long, and the 2020s never proved whey “fails at night.”
- Older athletes: the acute 40 g signal is there; the chronic hypertrophy add-on is thinner. I still prefer they not go 12 hours with nothing, especially if breakfast is late.

Pre-sleep protein is not a sleep aid, and I will not write it as one. It is a feeding in a long fast. Treat it like that.
