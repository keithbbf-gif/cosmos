# Lifting is the program. Protein is the grocery list.

Every winter someone asks whether they should “eat for hypertrophy” or “eat for strength.” They mean protein. They usually need a better barbell plan and enough calories. The 2020s metas are almost rude about this.

## The size of the effect, said out loud

Nunes 2022: extra daily protein, in healthy people who were lifting, added a **small** lean-mass increment (SMD 0.22). Lower-body strength moved a bit more when intake got to ≥1.6 g/kg. Bench strength in people under 65 moved a little. This is not a transformation. This is a nudge on top of the training.

Tagawa 2022: protein plus strength training, yes. Protein instead of strength training, no. Their dose-response for strength flattened near **1.5 g/kg/day**. Morton 2018’s older regression flattened near **1.6**. Two independent groups, same neighborhood. If you are already there, the next scoop is not a new mesocycle.

Hevia-Larraín 2021 is the training-adaptation paper I keep in the vegan argument: 12 weeks, twice-weekly supervised lower-body lifting, 1.6 g/kg, soy vs whey support. Leg lean mass +1.2 kg in both groups. Fiber CSA up in both. Leg-press 1RM up in both. Untrained young men. Protein *source* did not show up as the limiter when the grams and the work were real.

## Acute MPS is not a 12-week DEXA

Trommelen 2023 will tempt every hypertrophy account. A 100 g feeding made more protein in the biopsy over 12 hours. Hypertrophy is the integral of training, energy, sleep, and many feedings. I will not take a single-session tracer and sell a “one big dinner” hypertrophy method. I will take it as permission to stop throwing away a large meal in our heads.

Macnaughton 2016 (foundational) is the acute paper I still use for whole-body days: 40 g whey beat 20 g after a lot of muscle was worked. That is a “maybe don’t be stingy after a full session” hint. It is not “40 g six times a day.”

## What I write on a strength block

- Daily protein **1.6–2.2 g/kg** if energy is at maintenance or a surplus.
- A feeding in the hours around the heavy session, 0.3–0.5 g/kg.
- Progressive overload that would work if the powder factory burned down.
- If the athlete wants to be 4 kg heavier in 16 weeks, we talk energy and volume first. Protein is how we keep the extra weight from being only pantry.

Protein does not “build muscle” in the poster sense. Training sends the signal. Amino acids are the bricks. Missing the bricks is a real problem. Buying extra bricks when the crew is already on site is a smaller one.
