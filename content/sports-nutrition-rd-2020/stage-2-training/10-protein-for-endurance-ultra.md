# Endurance protein is a repair bill, not a gel flavor

Runners still ask me if they “need protein,” as if the macronutrient were a bodybuilding add-on. They oxidize amino acids on long days. They remodel tendon and muscle. They often under-eat energy. That is a protein problem even if they never touch a barbell.

## What the consensus documents actually wrote

The 2016 ACSM/Academy/DC joint position (foundational, still on clinic walls) put endurance athletes in a similar daily band as other athletes once training volume is high: well above the 0.8 g/kg RDA, typically **1.2–2.0 g/kg** depending on the week. ISSN 2017 sits in the same neighborhood for exercising people.

Tiller’s 2019 ISSN ultra-marathon stand — edge of our window — is the document I hand to people who think 90 g of gummy bears is a nutrition plan. Ultra days wreck energy availability. Protein becomes part of the overnight repair and the next morning’s breakfast, not a mid-race trick. During the event, carbohydrate and fluid still run the race. Protein in a bottle is, at best, a GI experiment you should have tested in training.

Moore, Sygo and Morton (2021), writing about female athletes, noted that endurance protein *requirements* estimated with modern tracer methods can sit higher than the old nitrogen-balance numbers because leucine burns on the road. They still refused a special female-only gel rule if energy is met. The missing piece in most endurance camps is not a unique amino-acid formula. It is dinner.

## Football is not a marathon, and it still spends protein

UEFA 2021 is an endurance-adjacent, high-intensity intermittent document. They wanted **1.6–2.2 g/kg/day**, 0.4 g/kg at meals, and they said most Premier League recalls already clear 2–2.5 g/kg. The point for a runner is not to copy a midfielder. The point is that stop-start sports with a glycogen problem still budget protein like a remodeling cost, not like an afterthought.

## During vs after

I do not put 20 g of whey in a two-hour easy run. I do put protein at the first real meal after a long session, with carbohydrate if the next session is inside 24 hours. Co-ingestion can help glycogen when carbohydrate is not sky-high; that is an older literature (Ivy and company) and it still works as practice. The 2020s did not replace it. They just stopped pretending protein *during* the race is a performance ergogenic for most distances.

If the athlete is in a volume block and waking up sore, I look at daily protein and energy before I look at a “recovery isolate.” Nunes 2022’s function outcomes were modest. Protein will not turn a 120-mile week into a fresh set of legs. It will keep the grocery list from being the reason the week falls over.
