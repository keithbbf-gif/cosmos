# The ice bath can leave the shake on the table

Athletes love a tub at 8 °C. It feels like professionalism. Fuchs and van Loon’s group spent the 2020s asking whether that professionalism is eating the protein you just paid for.

## The 2020 study, in the numbers they published

Twelve young men lifted, then stood with one leg in **8 °C** water and the other in **30 °C** water for 20 minutes. Then they drank 20 g of intrinsically labeled milk protein and 45 g of carbohydrate. Over five hours, the cold leg incorporated fewer dietary amino acids into myofibrillar protein (0.016 vs 0.021 MPE). Acute myofibrillar synthesis rates were lower on two tracers. Over two weeks of training with deuterated water, daily myofibrillar synthesis stayed lower in the regularly cooled leg (1.48 vs 1.67 %/day). Same person, two legs, one of them chilled.

That is *The Journal of Physiology*, 2020, doi:10.1113/JP278996. It is not a Twitter summary. It is a within-subject design that is hard to shrug off.

## 2025: they went looking for the pipe

A later *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise* paper (Fuchs et al., PMID 40249909) used contrast-enhanced ultrasound. After the same 8 vs 30 °C trick, microvascular blood volume in vastus lateralis was smashed immediately after the cold dunk and was still lower at 60 and 180 minutes after an amino-acid plus carbohydrate drink. Tracer incorporation into muscle was lower in the cold leg, and the difference tracked the perfusion difference. This is a delivery story as much as a signaling story.

Roberts 2015 (foundational) had already shown regular post-lift cooling can blunt hypertrophy signaling and gains over a training block. Fuchs put amino acids in the same picture.

## What I say beside the tub

If the week is about **adaptation** — getting stronger, adding contractile protein — I do not put the legs in 8 °C between the last squat and the dinner that is supposed to feed those squats. If someone insists on cooling, I would rather they eat first, wait, and cool later, but I do not have a clean crossover that proves timing rescues MPS. The honest line is: **cooling and muscle protein feeding are in a fight.**

If the week is about **turning around in 48 hours** for a second match, and the staff’s priority is perceived recovery, that is a different conversation. I still will not pretend the tub is “enhancing the protein.” UEFA 2021 did not bless CWI as a protein strategy. They barely blessed most recovery supplements.

This is not a disease paper. It is a remodeling paper. Cold is a tool. So is the shake. They are not automatically friends.
