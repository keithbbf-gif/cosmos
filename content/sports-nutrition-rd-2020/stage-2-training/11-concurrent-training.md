# Two sessions, one amino-acid pool

The team-sport week is concurrent training whether the coach uses the word or not: lifts in the morning, pitch at night, or the other way around. The molecular interference argument (AMPK vs mTOR, the old poster) is tidier than a human who has to play on Saturday. Protein does not dissolve that argument. It does keep both sessions from sharing an empty cupboard.

## What we can say without overclaiming

Churchward-Venne’s group, just before this window, showed that after concurrent resistance-plus-endurance work, myofibrillar and mitochondrial protein synthesis in young men did not differ when the drink was carbohydrate plus whey, soy, or leucine-enriched soy. That is a 2018/2019 *Journal of Nutrition* paper, and Pinckaers still cites it in the 2021 plant-protein review. The practical read: **after a mixed session, get a complete protein in**. Do not invent a “cardio protein” and a “weights protein.”

UEFA 2021 is concurrent training in a suit. Their training-day protein line is the same remodeling budget as the match-day line: 1.6–2.2 g/kg, meals that actually contain ~0.4 g/kg, a look at the overnight gap. They do not split protein into “speed days” and “gym days.” They split *carbohydrate* by the work. That is the right instinct. Protein is the constant. Carbohydrate is the dimmer switch.

Trommelen 2023 used resistance exercise, not a 10 k. I will not pretend a 100 g feeding is a concurrent-training protocol. I will say that a night session plus a large meal is a common football life, and the long clock is a comfort.

## Interference is mostly programming and energy

If the endurance session is huge and the lift is an afterthought, protein will not save the squat. If energy availability is low, both adaptations get taxed — and that is an IOC REDs 2023 conversation, not a scoop conversation. Protein in a concurrent week is how you stop the second session from being run on yesterday’s chicken.

My card for a two-a-day:

- Protein at each meal, including the one between sessions if there is time to digest.
- Do not “save” all the protein for the lift if the pitch session is the one that actually damages muscle this week.
- If only one feeding will happen, put it after the harder neuromuscular session, and make it real (30–40 g+).
- Sleep. The pre-sleep draft is doing more work in this week than a third isolate.

A soccer midfielder on Thursday night plus Friday gym is the person this draft is for. I do not write two protein personalities. I write one daily number and I make sure the meal between the pitch and the bar is not only a gel. If the gym is the next morning, overnight protein is the concurrent tool (Snijders/Reis/UEFA). If both sessions happen in one afternoon, the feeding after the first session is also the feeding before the second. That is logistics. It is also how you stop the second session from being run on an empty pool.

Interference is real as a programming problem when endurance volume eats the lift. Protein will not un-eat it. Energy availability will not un-eat it either if the coach will not drop a session. I can only grocery. I will grocery.
