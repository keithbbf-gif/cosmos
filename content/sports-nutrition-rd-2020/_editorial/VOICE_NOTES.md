# Voice notes

Write as if you are sitting at a kitchen table with a strength coach who actually reads papers, or with an athlete who is tired of being sold a 20-gram “anabolic window.”

## Do

- Start in a real situation: a 100 g steak night, a hotel breakfast, a 6 a.m. session, a cut.
- Name the paper and what it *measured* (MPS over 12 h is not hypertrophy over 12 weeks).
- Keep the first person plural for the field (“we used to say…”) and second person for the reader.
- Prefer one clear number over three hedged ones.
- End with what you would actually put on a plate, and what you would not.

## Do not

- Open with a definition of protein.
- Use “cutting-edge,” “game-changer,” “biohack,” or “scientists have discovered.”
- Invent a composite athlete with a fake name and a movie plot.
- Flatten every paper into “more protein is better.”
- Promise a body-composition outcome from an acute tracer study.
