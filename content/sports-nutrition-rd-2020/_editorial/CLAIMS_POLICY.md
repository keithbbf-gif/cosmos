# Claims policy — this series

These files are **drafts**, not published advice and not a product label.

## Hard line

No disease claims. A draft does not say protein (or any food, isolate, or amino-acid product) treats, cures, prevents, diagnoses, or mitigates a disease. It does not imply that a high-protein pattern is a therapy.

Sports nutrition language that **is** in bounds:

- training adaptation, recovery from exercise, muscle protein synthesis
- lean-mass support in healthy, exercising people when energy and training are specified
- fueling, body-composition *goals in sport*, practical meal pattern
- energy availability as a performance and readiness problem, with referral to a clinician when REDs is in the room

Sports nutrition language that **is not** in bounds:

- “prevents sarcopenia / osteoporosis / metabolic disease”
- “boosts immunity” as infection prevention
- “heals tendons / treats injury / anti-inflammatory therapy”
- kidney, liver, or lipid *treatment* claims (lab observations in healthy volunteers can be described; they are not a medical green light)

## How we cite

Primary papers from **January 2020 onward** carry the argument. Pre-2020 work appears only when a 2020+ paper still stands on it (Moore 2009 dose-response; ISSN 2017 protein stand; Morton 2018 meta-regression; Shaw 2017 gelatin). Those older papers are labeled **foundational**.

Every draft lists sources with author, year, journal, and DOI or PMID where we have them. If a number is not in the paper, it does not appear in the draft.

## Voice

A person talking to a coach or a trained athlete. Specific. Willing to say “we do not know.” No “unlock your potential,” no “in today’s fast-paced world,” no invented locker-room stories presented as fact.
