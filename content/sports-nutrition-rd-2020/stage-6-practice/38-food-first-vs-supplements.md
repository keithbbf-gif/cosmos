# Food first is a budget, not a purity test

UEFA 2021 put “food first” in the abstract because elite football had started to look like a warehouse of sachets. ISSN 2017 said the same thing in different clothes: you *can* hit protein from food; powders are how you hit it when food is inconvenient. Both sentences can live in the same kitchen.

## What the 2020s trials actually used

Look at the methods sections. Hevia-Larraín used soy and whey *supplements* to bring habitual diets to 1.6 g/kg. Oikawa used whey and collagen packets. Gwin used formulated drinks. Trommelen used a milk-protein feeding that was a study product. The science of protein is, ironically, a science of powders. The practice of protein is still dinner.

Monteyne’s mycoprotein work is the reminder that a whole food can win an acute comparison. Pinckaers’ potato work is an isolate. You need both kinds of paper to shop.

## How I apply “food first” without becoming precious

- Build the day from foods that would still work if the powder factory closed: dairy or fortified soy, eggs, fish, legumes, tofu, leftover meat, mycoprotein if they like it.
- Count the day. If the plate already holds 1.8 g/kg, I do not add a scoop so the brand stays on camera.
- Add a **batch-tested** protein when travel, appetite, or a tight energy budget makes 40 g of food a fight. That is ISSN 2017’s practical sentence, not a surrender.
- Do not add five other tubs “because we’re already supplementing.” Maughan 2018 is the adult in the room: every extra product is a contamination and a distraction.
- Do not shame a vegan for using soy isolate. Hevia-Larraín’s vegans *needed* it to hit 1.6 g/kg in that design. Food-only vegan hypertrophy at a high dose is a different study, and we do not have the trained-woman version yet.

A collagen latte is not dinner. A 25 g whey isolate after a hotel session is not a moral failure. Food first is a budget: spend food on the boring grams, spend powder on the constraint, spend nothing on a story.

When powder is the right tool, it should be **batch-tested** for the athletes who need a clean label — see the contamination draft. That is still food-first thinking: you are buying reliability, not a second religion.
