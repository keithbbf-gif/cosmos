# What the letterheads actually agreed on

Consensus documents are not holy. They are what a room of people was willing to sign. If you only read Twitter, you would think protein science reinvented itself every Tuesday. If you read the letterheads, the through-line is almost boring.

## The stack I still put on a desk

**ISSN protein 2017** (foundational): 1.4–2.0 g/kg for most exercising people; 20–40 g or ~0.25 g/kg per feeding; leucine 700–3,000 mg; distribute every 3–4 h; food can do it; powders are practical; timing is flexible because the session’s effect lasts.

**ISSN exercise & sport nutrition review 2018** (foundational): the grocery-store version of the above, plus the rest of the supplement aisle.

**ISSN EAA 2023**: free-form essentials are fast, work at rest in small-to-moderate doses, plateau around 15–18 g at rest, and matter more when energy is short. This is the one new protein-adjacent stand inside our window.

**ACSM / Academy / DC 2016** (foundational): athletes above the RDA; periodize carbohydrate; protein in the 1.2–2.0 g/kg neighborhood depending on the sport and the week.

**UEFA 2021**: football-specific. 1.6–2.2 g/kg; ~0.4 g/kg at meals; pre-sleep is the hole; food first; 2.0–2.4 g/kg when cutting or rehabbing.

**IOC supplements 2018** (foundational): risk, not macros.

**IOC REDs 2023**: energy availability, not a protein stand. Read it so you do not try to protein-powder your way out of a clinical problem.

**Tiller ultra 2019** (edge of window): endurance-event reality; protein as repair and daily fueling, not as a race gel.

## Where they rhyme

Nobody reputable is still writing 0.8 g/kg for a training athlete. Nobody reputable is writing that timing is a 30-minute cliff. Nobody reputable is writing that a vegan who hits the grams cannot train. Nobody reputable is writing that protein is a medicine.

Morton 2018 and Tagawa 2020/2022 and Nunes 2022 are the quantitative chorus under those letterheads: extra protein helps a little, mostly with lifting, mostly if you were not already high.

## How I use a consensus

I use it as a **starting card**, then I apply the 2020–2026 papers that the next revision will have to swallow: Trommelen’s long clock, Pinckaers’ 30 g plants, Gwin’s deficit queue, Fuchs’ cold tub, Hevia-Larraín’s 12 weeks. A stand that is eight years old is not wrong. It is unfinished.
