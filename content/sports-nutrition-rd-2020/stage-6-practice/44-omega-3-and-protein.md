# Omega-3s sit next to protein. They are not a protein.

McGlory, Phillips and others spent years asking whether long-chain omega-3 fatty acids change the way muscle answers a protein feeding. That is a co-nutrition question. It is also a place this series could wander into disease claims if I am sloppy. I will not write about hearts, brains, or “inflammation” as a diagnosis. I will write about training studies and MPS.

## What I am willing to say

There is a line of work — much of it just before and into this window — where fish-oil supplementation changed anabolic signaling or the MPS response to amino acids in some groups, especially older or inactive people. There is also a line where trained young people already eating enough protein and lifting hard do not suddenly grow because they added capsules. UEFA 2021 mentioned omega-3s in the fat chapter as essential fatty acids in a normal diet, not as a hypertrophy stack. They also warned that anti-inflammatory megadoses of isolated vitamins can get in the way of adaptation. I will not invert that into “take fish oil to recover.”

If an athlete eats fatty fish a couple of times a week, I stop talking about this. If they eat no fish and no algae oil, I will mention food sources as part of a complete diet. I will not replace a missed 40 g protein feeding with 2 g of EPA/DHA and call it even.

## The pairing that actually matters

Protein plus **resistance exercise**. Protein plus **energy**. Protein plus **carbohydrate** when the next session is soon. Those pairings have 2020s metas and consensus ink. Omega-3 plus protein is a research hobby with a smaller, messier pile. Treat it as optional context, not as a third macronutrient.

No treatment claims. No “joint health” medical promises. Collagen already has its own draft and its own fence.

If a practitioner wants to run fish oil in a masters athlete who also under-eats protein, I will fix the protein first. Oikawa 2020 is sitting right there: the quality of the *protein* changed MPS in older women in six days. I do not have an equivalent six-day omega-3 paper I would put on the same card for a training athlete. Capsules are optional context. The 30 g whey feeding is the intervention.

UEFA’s fat chapter is the tone I want: essential fatty acids as part of a 20–35% energy diet, not a hypertrophy stack, and a warning about megadose isolated antioxidant vitamins getting in the way of adaptation. I will not turn that warning into a new capsule protocol. I will turn it into “eat the fish if you like it, and do not skip the chicken.”
