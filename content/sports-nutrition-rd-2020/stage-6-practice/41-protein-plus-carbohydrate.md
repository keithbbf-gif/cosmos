# Carbohydrate runs the session. Protein files the paperwork.

Chocolate milk survived because it is carbohydrate plus protein in a fridge. The 2020s did not crown a new ratio. They kept showing the two macros doing different jobs in the same hour.

## Who did what in the methods

Fuchs 2020 fed **20 g protein + 45 g carbohydrate** after lifting and cooling. The carb was there as the ordinary recovery drink; the finding was about cold, not about a 2:1 religion.

Gwin 2024 is the paper that *split* the extra energy: same EAA-whey base, extra calories as more EAA or as carbohydrate, during underfeeding. For post-exercise MPS in that deficit, the extra EAA won. That does not mean carbohydrate is useless. It means **when the question is MPS underfed, leftover calories as sugar were not the better brick.** When the question is the next session’s glycogen, the opposite instinct is still correct.

UEFA 2021 periodizes carbohydrate hard (MD-1 at 6–8 g/kg, congested fixtures even more so) and keeps protein boring and steady. That is the adult stack. Protein does not replace glycogen. Carbohydrate does not replace amino acids. Together in a recovery feeding they are how a human actually eats.

Moore, Sygo and Morton 2021 refused a special female carb-or-protein rule if energy is met. The pairing still matters as practice: the session that emptied glycogen wants starch; the session that damaged muscle wants a complete protein in the next meal. Most team-sport days are both.

## A ratio I will write, and one I will not

I will write: **a recovery meal should contain both**, unless the session was a true rest-day walk. I will not write “4:1 is the science.” The 4:1 came from older glycogen studies with low carbohydrate intakes. If the athlete already ate 2 g/kg of carbohydrate at lunch, the shake’s ratio is a rounding error.

Gwin 2021’s mixed meal vs whey vs EAA-whey in deficit: the meal had fewer EAAs per gram of protein and still did MPS work. Food pairing is allowed.

Chocolate milk survives because it is a fridge object that happens to be both macros. I will keep it on a youth-sport table (Desbrow’s food-first instinct) before I keep a 4:1 recovery powder. If the athlete is lactose-intolerant, soy milk plus a banana is the same idea. If they just lifted and they are in a hypertrophy block, I care more that the protein in that drink is complete (Oikawa’s collagen lesson) than that the ratio is pretty.

A morning pitch session plus an afternoon lift is a carbohydrate-first morning and a both-macros afternoon. UEFA already periodizes the starch. I will not periodize protein down on the pitch day. The bass line stays. The headline changes.
