# If the label needs a decoder, the protein is the last thing you know

“Amino spiking” is the cheap trick where a manufacturer puts glycine, taurine, or creatine in the tub so the crude nitrogen number looks like 25 g of whey. Nitrogen does not care whether it came from a complete protein. Your muscle protein synthesis does. Oikawa 2020 is what that looks like in a biopsy: 30 g of collagen nitrogen is not 30 g of whey.

## What I want on a panel

- A named protein: whey isolate, milk protein concentrate, soy isolate, pea isolate, mycoprotein. Not “proprietary blend.”
- A serving that states protein in grams that match a realistic scoop.
- Leucine if they are bragging about quality — and a number that is physically possible (whey ~10–12% leucine; collagen is not).
- A batch-test mark if the buyer is a tested athlete (Maughan 2018).

ISSN 2017’s quality language — EAAs, leucine, digestibility — is how you *read* a label, not how you write an ad. Pinckaers 2021 is how you stop assuming every plant isolate is a failed whey. DIAAS is a lab score. It is not printed on most tubs. Do not pretend it is.

## The 100 g dinner vs the 25 g tub

Trommelen 2023 will get abused on labels (“no upper limit”). That paper was milk protein after exercise in a tracer lab. It is not a license for a 100 g “anabolic mega scoop” of a blend you cannot identify. Food that happens to be a large steak is a meal. A mystery powder at 100 g is a GI experiment and a trust experiment.

If the math on the label only works if you believe marketing, walk away. The 2020s did not make that advice old-fashioned.

A 30-second check I actually use in a shop aisle:

- Flip to the ingredients. If the first protein is whey or soy or pea, good. If the first “protein” helpers are glycine, taurine, and creatine, you are looking at a nitrogen story.
- Divide protein grams by scoop grams. A scoop that is 40 g and claims 30 g protein is a real isolate conversation. A scoop that is 40 g and claims 30 g after you subtract a candy’s worth of flavoring plus 5 g of free amino fillers is a different conversation.
- Look for a lot number and a test mark. No mark is not automatically dirty. It is automatically *unverified*.
- If the brand is shouting Trommelen (“no ceiling”) or ISSN (“clinically proven 20 g”), ask whether the tub is the protein in those papers. Usually it is not.

Spiking is not a new 2020s crime. It is an old one that got easier to hide in a “blend.” The biopsy does not care about the marketing sentence. Oikawa already showed what the wrong protein looks like when the grams on the label are technically true.
