
## Pillar A — Craft

**Hub:** `/work/` — what this shop will and will not build, in what woods, at what scale (one-off, not production runs unless that becomes true). Process in the order a commission actually takes: talk → measure → draw → mill → join → finish → deliver.

### Craft clusters

| Working title | Intent | Notes / links |
|---|---|---|
| Commissioning a piece from a one-man shop | Commercial | Lead times, what you need to decide (size, wood, finish), what Keith decides (joinery). Link woods hub. |
| From board to finish — one table (or bench) | Build log | *One* piece, dated, with the mistakes. Hub = craft. Fashion hub only if the room is the point. |
| Joinery we actually use | Technique | Name the joints you cut. No encyclopedia of joints you have not cut this year. |
| Finishes that survive a kitchen | Technique | Oil, varnish, water-white, heat/wet. Link woods (open-pore oak vs maple). |
| Shop setup in 65 feet | Shop | Honest constraints. One optional sentence + link to FigRoots winter *only* if this piece is about the glass bay as climate, not as a cute aside. |
| Repair and refinish vs replace | Service | If you take that work. If you do not, say so on the hub and skip the cluster. |
| What we will not build | Trust | Particleboard “farmhouse” kits, mystery “live-edge epoxy rivers” if that is not the work, rush Christmas after a cutoff. Saves emails. |

## Pillar B — Woods

**Hub:** `/woods/` — a short species library **of woods this shop will sell or has on the rack**. Arkansas and nearby: white oak, red oak, walnut, cherry, pecan/hickory, maple, pine, cypress, cedar. Add exotics only when a board is actually in the room.

Each species page: where it grew (if you know), how it mills, how it moves, what it suits, a photo of *your* board not a stock texture. Link the craft hub and any piece that used it.

### Woods clusters

| Working title | Intent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| White oak vs red oak for tabletops | Decision | Open pore, tannin, outdoor vs dining. |
| Walnut that is actually local | Materials | Supply, color change in sun, sapwood. |
| Pecan and hickory — hard, cheap until they are not | Materials | Southern default; finishing chatter. |
| Softwoods we still respect (pine, cypress) | Materials | Painted vs clear; porch vs interior. |
| How we dry and what happens if you do not | Technique | Movement, checks. Link joinery. |
| Reading a board (grain, defects, what to cut around) | Education | Photograph a real rack. |
| Fig, fruitwood, and other “yard trees” | Honesty | Most fruitwood is not furniture stock. If you have not milled a slab, do not write a buying guide. A short “we do not” is enough. |

## Pillar C — Fashion (rooms and wearables)

**Fashion** here means *how the object is worn by a room* — proportion, period mix, what a 100-year house in the country can take — and, if the shop makes them, small things people wear or carry (a belt, a box, a comb). It does **not** mean inventing a fur line because the slug is `bbfur`.

**Hub:** `/fashion/` — three or four room photographs (the house you are restoring is allowed if you are willing to show it) and the rule: we do not do every style.

### Fashion clusters

| Working title | Intent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Country house, not “farmhouse content” | Style | The grandparents’ house vs catalog distressed pine. Link history (vernacular). |
| Mixing a new table with old walls | Style | One room, before/after if you have it. |
| Scale: seat height, apron, what photographs lie about | Practical | People order from phone pictures. |
| Small goods (if they exist on Etsy) | Shop | One page per *class* of SKU, not a post per listing. Link shop. If Etsy is empty, skip. |
| Seasonal cloth and wood (table linen, not a costume page) | Light | Optional Q4. Do not force a “fashion week” voice. |

Do not publish a woods page for a species you cannot photograph this week.
