## Claims guard (read before every draft)

This lane is the one that can get the household in trouble.

**Allowed**

- Historical and botanical description (what a plant was called, where it grew, how it was prepared in a cited source).
- Chemistry and lab *methods*: identity, purity, contaminants, reference standards, instrument calibration, what an assay can and cannot show.
- Structure/function wording that would survive DSHEA *if and when a product exists* — and even then, only after counsel. Until there is a product, prefer methods and history and skip structure/function entirely.
- “We ran this method on this material; here is the chromatogram description; here is the blank.”
- Clear “this is not medical advice; not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.”

**Not allowed**

- Disease treatment, prevention, or cure (“kills Lyme”, “better than metformin”, “anti-cancer”).
- Implied FDA evaluation.
- Testimonials that are medical outcomes.
- Dosing a reader for a condition.
- Fig-leaf, latex, or herb as a drug because FigRoots exists.
- Recycled Abraxas / lease / lawsuit narrative. That is Legal, not content.
- Competitor smears dressed as “R&D”.

If a sentence needs a disease name to be interesting, kill the sentence. The history hub can say “19th-c. Eclectics used X for Y *in their books*” with a citation; it cannot say *it works*.

When a product SKU is real, **stop and get a label review**. This folder does not authorize copy for a bottle.

## Pillar B — Herbal history

**Hub:** `/history/` — a geographically honest spine. Not “12 herbs that healed the ancients”.

Possible spine (pick plants you will actually read primary or good secondary sources for):

1. How we know anything (papyri, herbals, pharmacopoeias, what is lost)
2. Mediterranean and Near East (where fig history already lives — coordinate, do not duplicate Cato)
3. Chinese *bencao* as a tradition (one plant at a time)
4. South Asian *materia medica* (same)
5. European herbals → Eclectic / Thomsonian U.S. (primary texts)
6. What industrial isolation changed (alkaloids, glycosides) — this is the bridge to R&D
7. Regulation: 1906, 1938, 1994 DSHEA, in plain English — history of *law*, not a loophole guide

## Linking

- R&D hub lists method notes. A method note links the history-of-isolation or DSHEA page only when the piece is about *why the question exists*.
- History hub is sequential plus a plant index. A plant page links **one** methods page if you assayed that plant; otherwise none.
- **FigRoots:** *Ficus* history cluster ↔ FigRoots history hub, one link each, because the reader of Cato and the reader of a leaf assay are different. Grow how-to (rooting, mites) does not belong here.
- **Therapy:** no links. An SLP clinic is not an herb funnel.
- **Furniture / AI / COSMOS:** no links.
- External: PubChem, a pharmacopoeia preface, a university herbal, FDA’s DSHEA page — yes. Random blogs — no.
