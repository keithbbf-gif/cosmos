This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The object is a 1620 title page. Madrid. *Reducción de las letras y arte para enseñar a hablar a los mudos.* Juan Pablo Bonet’s name sits on a book that later AAC lectures sometimes wave like a founding charter. It is not a founding charter. It is a Spanish treatise on teaching deaf people to speak by means of a reduced alphabet and a manual method. The inheritance is narrower and more interesting than a charter: a public claim that a hand can carry letters.

Bonet was born about 1573. The *circa* is honest. He died in 1633. He served in aristocratic and courtly worlds that had a legal problem: deaf heirs and the question of whether they could be taught enough language to inherit. Education, in that setting, is not a clinic value. It is a property value. Say that before you praise the method.

## The book, not the myth

Pedro Ponce de León is the earlier Spanish name in later medals. Bonet is the one with a printed book a reader can still request. The *Reducción* describes a one-handed manual alphabet and articulation work aimed at speech. Later fingerspelling systems, including ones Deaf communities actually used, are not simple descendants. They are cousins in a large family of visible alphabets.

He wanted speech. That matters. An AAC series that recruits him as a prophet of the communication board is doing public relations. He is here because he put the visible letter into a European printed method and because every later fight about “speech first” has a relative in that aim.

## What speech-language pathology did not inherit

A credential. A test battery. A kindly modern ethics. Bonet’s century had its own cruelties, including the assumption that *mudo* named a lack rather than a language. Deaf history written by Deaf scholars is the place for that argument at full length. This profile keeps the book on the table and the annexation off it.

The sibling profession pack may mention him in a wider elocution-to-clinic story. This one is the AAC neighbor: the alphabet as a portable board. When a 2026 user spells on a device, the ghost in the machine is not Bonet’s intent. It is Bonet’s object — letters you can show.
