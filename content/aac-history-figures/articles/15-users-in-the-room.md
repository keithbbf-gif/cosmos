This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Halton Hills, 1982, in McNaughton’s memory: the planned organization should be international, multidisciplinary, and inclusive of people who used assisted communication. East Lansing kept the intention. The early photographs still look like a professional meeting with a few devices in the aisle. Intention is not occupancy.

Michael B. Williams, born 1937, has been a USSAAC president, an ISAAC board member, and the writer behind *Alternatively Speaking* pieces that clinicians quote when they want to sound humble. He describes meeting AAC people who, unlike the speech teachers of his childhood, wanted the message out. He also describes keeping every old tool: remnants of speech, paper, device. Rick Creech, speaking as the 2004 Prentke Lecturer, told ASHA he started public speaking because he was happy he could speak with a machine and wanted others to see the person, not the disability. Those two men are not “the user community.” They are two people who got on the program. The program is how a field pretends it has listened.

## What users had to teach

Rate. A device that needs forty selections for a joke is a device that kills the joke. Vocabulary. A page designed by a clinician will have *toilet* and lack the user’s actual friends. Humor. Repair. The right to be rude. The right to be boring. The right to say *no* to a research protocol. Williams’s multi-modal insistence is a technical paper disguised as a column. Creech’s early work with Baker on Minspeak is a reminder that semantic compaction had a user in the room before it had a booth.

Nothing about us without us — the disability-rights sentence — arrives in AAC late and then gets printed on banners. Research labs that include users as consultants are better than labs that use them as stimuli. They are still late.

## Who is still not in the room

Children, except as anecdotes. Adults with intellectual disability, except in Romski and Sevcik’s papers and a few user-led groups. People who do not use English. People whose devices are owned by a school. People who type slowly in public and get spoken for. People who were taught FC and then had their words taken back. People who do not want to be inspiring.

ISAAC conferences, from Boston 1984 onward, did include social space for users and families. Biennial sites moved — Cardiff, Anaheim, Stockholm, Philadelphia, Maastricht, Vancouver, Dublin, and on through Toronto 2016 and later. Travel is a filter. A filter is a politics.

User-led publications — *Alternatively Speaking*, later blogs, USSAAC’s SpeakUP — are how people who could not buy a plane ticket still entered the square. Blackstone’s *News* sometimes carried those voices into clinician mailboxes. The square is not the same as co-authorship on a methods paper. Both matter. Confusing them is how a field congratulates itself.

## Residue

A 2026 article that quotes only clinicians about AAC is doing 1978. This series therefore gives Williams and Creech full profiles, not a sidebar. It still cannot give a profile to the unnamed child on Lydell’s wooden board or to the unnamed tetraplegic man with Maling’s whistle. The unnamed are the majority. The residue is to stop speaking for them in the closing paragraph.

Meredith Allen’s later ISAAC presidency is a user in the chair, not only in the aisle. Name that as a late correction, not as proof the 1983 intention was already occupancy. Intention without occupancy is a constitution’s first draft.
