This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

East Lansing is a small city with an outsized hallway. John Eulenberg directed the Artificial Language Laboratory at Michigan State University from 1974 to 2012 in the public institutional record. He hosted the May 1983 gathering that named ISAAC. He printed *Communication Outlook*, a newsletter that let basement synthesizer people talk to each other before the journal existed. Living; public offices and publications only.

## What a laboratory of “artificial language” meant

Not science fiction. Phonemes, voice output, the ethics of a machine saying *I*. The lab sat at the seam of linguistics, engineering, and disability. Users came as collaborators more often than the 1970s photograph usually admits. *Communication Outlook* is why a lot of early device lore is recoverable at all.

Hosting a founding meeting is a specific skill. Someone has to book the room, tolerate the argument about adjectives, and not make the society a lab franchise. ISAAC’s later independence from MSU is a sign the host did the job.

*Communication Outlook* also recorded users and small manufacturers that the later refereed journal would not have space for. A field that only remembers *AAC* 1985 forward is missing the stapled years. Eulenberg’s lab name — Artificial Language — still startles people who think language must be flesh. The startle is useful. A synthetic utterance can be a person’s sentence. The lab existed to make that less mystical and more soldered.

## Residue

Every later open newsletter, listserv, and “how we built this voice” thread is *Outlook*’s grandchild. The residue is a staple, not a patent. When people say AAC began in Toronto or in Madison, add East Lansing to the triangle. A triangle is already more honest than a single star. The 1983 weekend needs a host with a printer and a tolerance for adjectives. He was that host. The lab kept printing after the weekend, which is how a founding room avoids becoming a relic.

## On the record

An artificial-language lab in a land-grant university is an American accident that became an international hotel in 1983. People flew to Michigan because someone would print what they were building. Printing is hospitality. ISAAC’s later journal is a different hospitality: slower, refereed, less basement. Both are required. Eulenberg’s decade-plus as director after the founding is the part a weekend-origin story erases.
