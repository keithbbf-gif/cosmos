This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The spine of the book is two names. For four editions it was Beukelman and Mirenda. Pat Mirenda, long at the University of British Columbia in the public record, is the reason the textbook could speak to teachers of autistic students without becoming a behavior manual or a device catalog. Living; no private biography.

Her 2003 *LSHSS* paper on functional AAC for students with autism and her later book with Teresa Iacono (*Autism Spectrum Disorders and AAC*, 2009) are how a lot of school SLPs stopped treating a picture card as either a miracle or a threat to speech. She wrote in a field that had PECS, facilitated communication, and a pile of vendor promises in the same backpack. The useful sentence in her work is the one that asks what we actually know.

## What she did to the model

The Participation Model in the textbook is a shared object. Mirenda’s classroom sense is why opportunity barriers include attitude and knowledge, not only ramps. A para who will not wait is an opportunity barrier. A principal who bans devices on the playground is an opportunity barrier. Naming those without a sermon is harder than drawing a flowchart.

When Beukelman died, the fifth edition had already moved to Beukelman and Light (2020). That is a succession, not an erasure. Mirenda’s four editions remain the ones a used-book shelf still holds.

British Columbia is not Nebraska. A Canadian scholar co-writing the American assigned book is itself a small ISAAC fact: the field’s syllabus was already international even when the publisher’s zip code was not. Her autism papers also had to live next to PECS marketing and FC videos. The useful tone in those papers is skepticism without cruelty. A history pack will not turn that tone into a method.

## Residue

Every later “autism and AAC” workshop that refuses both FC and despair is downstream of papers she made readable. The residue is a teacher who can hold a doubt and a device in the same hour.

## On the record

*Autism Spectrum Disorders and AAC* (2009), with Iacono, is a book that had to be written because school teams were drowning in methods. A handbook is not a protocol. It is a map of fights. Mirenda’s map included pictures, speech output, inclusion, and the things we do not actually know. “What do we really know?” is a better title than most workshops deserve.
