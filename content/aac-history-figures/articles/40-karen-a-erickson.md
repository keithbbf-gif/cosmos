This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A child who can request juice and cannot write a name has been given a menu, not a language. Karen A. Erickson, publicly tied to the Center for Literacy and Disability Studies at UNC Chapel Hill, spent decades saying that out loud in AAC and special-education rooms. Living; papers and centre pages only. No method list.

With David Koppenhaver and colleagues, she treated conventional literacy — alphabet, phonological work, shared reading, writing by selection — as something teams were withholding on the basis of label. Yoder’s late interviews point at the same wound. McNaughton wanted Bliss to be a bridge to print. Erickson built the research furniture that made “bridge to print” a demand rather than a hope.

## What she refused

The idea that a device vocabulary is a substitute for books. The idea that handwriting is the only writing. The idea that literacy waits for speech. Those refusals are why she is in a history pack and not only in a methods course.

The AAC-RERC and later literacy projects put her name on grants that trained people who would never read the journal. Training is a historical object. So is a centre that outlives a fashion in apps.

UNC’s Center for Literacy and Disability Studies is easy to file as “special education” and lose from an AAC timeline. Do not. Print access is a communication access. A person who can spell is harder to keep on a noun page. That sentence is Williams and Erickson in the same key, written from two sides of the table.

## Residue

A 2026 overlay that hides the keyboard behind a password called “advanced” is arguing with her. The residue is a keyguard, a teacher who waits for a letter, and a researcher who counted reading growth in students other people had already filed under *functional*. Functional, without print, is a menu. Menus are not lives.

## On the record

Shared reading with a child who selects a letter is a Thursday activity that looks small in a grant abstract. It is how literacy stops being a poster. Erickson’s centre made the Thursday activity respectable enough to write into a plan. Respectable is not the same as common. The gap is why the essay in this pack is still angry in a quiet way.
