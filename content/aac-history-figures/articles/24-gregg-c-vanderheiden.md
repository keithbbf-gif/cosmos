This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A wooden board, a boy later named Lydell in the Trace histories, and an undergraduate electrical engineer who was not supposed to be in that classroom. Gregg C. Vanderheiden’s origin story, retold in the 2023 Trace fiftieth-anniversary book, begins as a benevolent deception by a classmate. The informal group became the Trace Research and Development Center at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. In 2016 the centre’s core moved to the University of Maryland. The man is living. This profile is a public laboratory, not a biography.

## What the laboratory published

Access. Scanning. Early communication aids. Reports that other labs could copy. A 2007 memoir that places POSSUM at the front of the electric line and then walks through decades of computer access. Vanderheiden’s later work widened from AAC devices toward mainstream accessibility — the idea that the ordinary computer should already include the switch. That widening is why some AAC clinicians think he left the field. He did not leave the problem. He changed the furniture the problem sits on.

Yoder’s later interview credits Vanderheiden with inventing “the first speech-generating device.” Treat that as a mentor’s pride, not a patent office ruling. Firsts in this pack are prizes. Trace’s usable residue is a pile of designs and a habit of writing them down.

He was in the East Lansing naming conversation. McNaughton remembers him, with Yoder, helping the infant society keep both *augmentative* and *alternative*. A person can be an engineer and still care what the letterhead says.

Trace’s later accessibility work — making ordinary operating systems take a switch — is why a 2026 tablet can be an AAC surface without a custom kernel. It is also why “just use the iPad” can sound, falsely, like the engineering problem is over. The engineering problem moved. The child who still needs a reliable movement did not retire.

## Residue

Every access assessment that starts with “what movement is reliable?” is Trace as much as it is Stoke Mandeville. Every later argument that AAC should live in the operating system, not only in a dedicated box, is Trace in a 2010s coat. The iPad did not retire that argument. It made it louder.

## On the record

The 2023 Trace book is a primary memoir with co-authors who inherited the centre. Use it as a laboratory history, not as a court record of every “first.” Lydell’s wooden board is the opening object. The later object is an operating system that can take a switch without a custom box. Both objects are his hallway.
