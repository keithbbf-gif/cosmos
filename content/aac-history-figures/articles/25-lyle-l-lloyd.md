This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

West Lafayette, 12 February 2020. Lyle L. Lloyd dies at eighty-five. Donald Fuller’s USSAAC memorial is the vita this profile trusts: born in St. Louis in 1934; B.S. Eastern Illinois 1956; M.A. Illinois; Ph.D. Iowa 1965 in audiology and speech pathology with an intellectual-disability emphasis; Gallaudet 1966–69; NIH administrator 1969–77; Purdue from 1977 until retirement in 2011. The AAC chapter of that vita begins when he and Macalyne Fristoe put a stand-alone course on the books in a field still called nonspeech communication.

He was dually certified in audiology and speech-language pathology. The dual is not trivia. Early AAC sat among deafness, intellectual disability, and special education. A person who had chaired audiology at Gallaudet and run a communication-disorders desk at NICHD was unusually placed to see the seams.

## What he built that is still on shelves

A course. A doctoral pipeline — Goossens’, Page, Fuller, Zangari, Schlosser, Koul, Wendt, and others. Editorship of *Augmentative and Alternative Communication* from 1986 to 1993, including editorial awards that outlived him. The 1997 handbook with Fuller and Arvidson. A last manuscript with Fuller submitted weeks before his death. Founding labor for ISAAC (board 1983–86) and later USSAAC. Help in the long push to make AAC one of ASHA’s required knowledge areas rather than a “modality” footnote. The Prentke Distinguished Lecture, which he helped establish in 1997, is a residue with a donor’s name on it.

Kangas and Lloyd 1988 — “What are we waiting for?” — is the paper a magazine can quote without a handshake. Teams were waiting for cognitive prerequisites. He was done waiting.

He and David Beukelman ran the two American doctoral magnets of the 1980s, Purdue and Nebraska, in a rivalry Fuller describes as collegial and useful. Two magnets are better than one church.

The honorary doctorate from Pretoria in 2006 is a residue of the international pipeline: Lloyd sitting on South African dissertation committees, Alant’s CAAC sending work the other way. A Midwestern professor who takes that seriously is how ISAAC’s letterhead stops being a travel club. He also reviewed ASHA papers on FC and on the scope of practice. The guild work is not glamorous. It is how “communication modalities” became the word *AAC* in a certification handbook.

## What he was not

The lone father of the field. Fuller says some people call him that and then immediately says he was not alone. Believe the second sentence. McNaughton, Yoder, Vanderheiden, Eulenberg, Shane, and a Toronto restaurant meeting are also in the birth room.
