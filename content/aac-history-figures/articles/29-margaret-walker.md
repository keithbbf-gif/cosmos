This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A British speech therapist, a small vocabulary, and a bet that family and staff should sign the same core the learner does. Margaret Walker, with colleagues including Kathy Johnston and Tony Cornforth in the usual origin paragraph, began what became Makaton in the 1970s. Exact founding-year stamps slip between charity pages and later histories. This pack will not pick a fashionable year. It will say *1970s Britain* and mean a taught community, originally associated with people who had hearing impairment and learning disability, using signs and later graphic symbols *with* speech.

The name is a mash of names. The method is a syllabus. Unlike a device language, Makaton was meant to live on other people’s hands. That is its dignity and its risk. A taught sign vocabulary can open a classroom. It can also sit in a building that already has a Deaf sign language and pretend to be a substitute.

## What she is doing in an American magazine

SLPWOW’s readers will meet PECS and core boards first. They will meet Makaton if they read UK papers, work with immigrant families, or notice a key-word-sign poster. Walker is here so the American story does not swallow the unaided British line that also includes Paget-Gorman. ISAAC’s early presidents included Britons and other Europeans for a reason. The field was never only Purdue.

Later Makaton Charity training is a brand. This profile describes the historical bet, not a workshop syllabus and not a home program.

## Residue

A core vocabulary that staff actually use is rarer than a core vocabulary on a laminated sheet. Walker’s residue is the staff. Paget’s residue was a system. Baker’s was a sequence. The three residues do not replace each other.

Makaton’s later graphic symbols sit next to PCS and Bliss in the same British cupboard. The cupboard is the point: unaided sign plus a picture plus speech, taught as a community habit. American rooms that only fund a device can miss the habit. ISAAC’s UK members did not.

Birth and death dates are not locked in the sources this pack trusts. Do not invent them.

## On the record

Walker’s later honors, including whatever letters a charity page lists after her name, are not the story. The story is a taught core that staff could carry into a kitchen. If the staff stop signing, the system dies in a way a dedicated device does not: it dies socially. That social death is why this profile belongs next to Light as much as next to Paget.
