This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

AAC is a 1983 peace treaty between *augmentative* and *alternative*. It is a good treaty. It is also a baggy coat. Under it sit sign, gesture, objects, cards, Bliss, PCS, Minspeak, PECS, alphabet boards, partner-assisted scanning, sip-and-puff typewriters, DECtalk, eye-gaze, and a phone app downloaded on a Saturday. A student who learns the acronym in a survey course has not learned the fights.

This last era essay is not a summary for a midterm. It is a list of things the word still hides, so a reader of the profiles that follow will not mistake a vita for the field.

## Unaided / aided

Unaided means the body: sign, gesture, glance. Aided means a tool. The binary is useful in a textbook and cruel in a life. Williams’s toolbox is both. A person who loses a device on a bus still has a hand if anyone will honor it. A person whose signs are ignored in a hospital still needs the tool. Clinics that “do AAC” by handing over a tablet and never asking about the glance are doing half the 1983 word.

## Temporary / permanent

A patient after surgery who cannot speak for a week needs a channel. So does a person with ALS who will need a channel for years. The same alphabet board can serve both. The funding codes often cannot. Temporary AAC is still AAC. The word hides that when people say “AAC kids.”

## Child / adult

The public photograph of AAC is a child with a colorful grid. The founding engineering at Stoke Mandeville was adults with spinal injuries. Beukelman’s Nebraska work included degenerative disease. Aphasia after stroke — the sibling pack — sometimes meets AAC and sometimes refuses the word because aphasia already has a brand. Adults get fewer laminated hearts and worse waiting lists. Temporary intensive-care boards sit in a third photograph that neither pediatric AAC nor aphasia owns. The word still hides the week-long mouth.

## User / partner

Light 1988: interaction is not a device feature. A partner who asks twenty yes/no questions and never waits has built an opportunity barrier. The word *AAC* still lets teams write goals only on the user’s body.

## Speech first

Milan 1880 lives in IEP meetings. The fear that a board will steal speech is older than ISAAC and younger than evidence. Research on aided systems and speech development is not a cheerleading pamphlet; it is mixed, situational, and better than the fear. The word *augmentative* was supposed to calm the fear. It did not.

## Who gets believed

FC taught the field a brutal lesson about authorship. The opposite error is also live: a person with a slow device is interrupted, and the interruption is called help. Creech’s lectures keep asking the room to wait. Waiting is not in the acronym.

## Brand

SLPWOW did not invent any of this. Neither did WOW Therapies. A history series on a practice site has one job: tell the room, the date, and the fight without turning the fight into a service menu. The word *AAC* on a clinic page can mean “we have an iPad.” After these essays it should mean something older, wider, and less for sale.

A caregiver who already built a code in 1683 is also hidden under the acronym, which sounds like a 1983 invention. The invention was the letterhead. The problem is older than the letterhead. The profiles that follow are people, not progress bars.
