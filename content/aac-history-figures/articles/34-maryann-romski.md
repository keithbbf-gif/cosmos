This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Georgia State University, late 1980s. MaryAnn Romski and Rose A. Sevcik publish, in the same young *AAC* volume as Light’s interaction paper, on symbol communication among people labeled severely intellectually disabled. *Establishment of Symbolic Communication in Persons with Severe Retardation* (1988, *JSHD*) still carries the period word in the title. Keep the title. Change the hallway language. The finding was rude to the wait: people learned symbols when adults talked to the symbols and expected a reply. System for Augmenting Language (SAL) is the later name for that stubbornness.

Romski is living. Public scientific biography only. The pair is the unit of work; this pack still gives each a profile because readers search names.

## What SAL insisted on

Speech output as part of the environment, not a prize. Partners who model. A refusal to treat a low IQ score as a locked door. Kangas and Lloyd asked “what are we waiting for?” in the same years. Romski and Sevcik answered with data rather than a manifesto. Both were needed.

They did not claim that every person became a novelist. They claimed that the official story about who could have a symbol was too small. AAC’s later inclusion language owes them a citation it sometimes forgets to pay.

Georgia State’s laboratory was not a device shop. It was a language shop that happened to use speech output as part of the input. That sentence is easy to skip. Teams that hand a child a tablet and then sit silent are skipping it. SAL’s stubbornness was the adult talk around the symbol, not the symbol alone. Romski’s later writing keeps returning to that adult. Believe the return.

## Residue

A 2026 team that withholds a board until a child “shows symbolic play” is arguing with this laboratory. The residue is a device that speaks while the adult also speaks, and a researcher who counted something other than hope.

## On the record

James L. Pate appears on the 1988 *JSHD* author line. Keep collaborators visible. Georgia State’s work was never a two-person myth even when this pack files two profiles. The population the papers named — people other clinics had filed as unready — is the reason the papers still sting. A sting that lasts forty years is a finding.
