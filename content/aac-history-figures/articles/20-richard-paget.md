This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A titled Englishman with a research habit around speech and the hands: Sir Richard Arthur Surtees Paget, 1869–1955. He is in this pack because British AAC did not begin with an iPad, and because signed systems for people who were not only Deaf — later Paget-Gorman Signed Speech, developed with Pierre Gorman — sat in UK schools and clinics as an unaided channel while American rooms argued about boxes.

Paget published on the nature of speech and on gesture. The later educational system that bears his name was revised after him; “Paget invented AAC” is a slide. What he left was a bet that a systematic sign vocabulary, tied to English, could be taught. The bet has the same risks as de l’Épée’s methodical signs: it can help a classroom, and it can pretend to replace a natural sign language that already exists.

## Why a speech-language series bothers

Because Makaton, signed English, and various key-word sign programs are still in the same family photograph. Margaret Walker’s later work is a different, more taught-community project. Paget is an ancestor in the British unaided line. American SLPs who meet only PECS and core boards are missing this cousin.

Pierre Gorman, a Deaf Australian scholar, is not a footnote. A profile that prints only the baronet is doing the old caption. The system’s later life is a collaboration and then a school-service object. Dates and edition names vary; this pack will not invent a first-year stamp beyond Paget’s life dates.

Paget also published on the physics and gesture of speech — the kind of gentleman-research that looks, to a later clinician, like a hobby until a school service picks it up. The hobby-to-service path is British AAC in miniature: a titled experimenter, a Deaf collaborator, a vocabulary that can be taught, a later charity or advisory structure. American device history has a different miniature. Keep both on the map.

## Residue

Unaided AAC is easy to forget when the exhibit hall is loud. A sign system does not need a charger. It needs a community willing to learn it. Paget’s residue is that sentence, not a title.

## On the record

Paget-Gorman later lived in UK schools as a taught system with its own advisors and revisions. A 2026 American reader who has never seen it still meets its cousins: key-word sign, signed English at a special-school assembly, a poster of twenty signs next to the sink. Those cousins are not proofs that Paget “won.” They are proofs that unaided systematic vocabularies kept being reinvented whenever a device was too expensive or a community wanted hands, not batteries.
