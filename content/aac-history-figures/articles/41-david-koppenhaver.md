This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The other name on the literacy stubbornness. David Koppenhaver, publicly associated with Appalachian State and with UNC’s literacy-and-disability work, wrote with Karen Erickson until “AAC and reading” stopped being a surprise session at a conference. Living; papers only.

A history pack gives him his own file because readers search, and because pair science is how this field actually moved. Romski and Sevcik, Bondy and Frost, Beukelman and Mirenda, Erickson and Koppenhaver: the hyphen is the method.

## What he added to the refusal

Measurement. Classrooms that had been told to be satisfied with requesting could be shown print growth, or the lack of it, in students who used symbols and selection. He also wrote as a teacher-educator, which means the residue is not only a journal PDF. It is a semester of people who would otherwise have laminated nouns and called it a year.

This series will not teach a reading sequence. It will say that a sequence was published, adapted for access, and still skipped whenever a team decided literacy was a luxury.

Appalachian State and UNC are two letterheads on one stubbornness. Teacher education is the quieter one: people who will never call themselves AAC specialists still meet a student who uses a board. If those people think books are for speakers only, the journal papers do not matter. Koppenhaver’s vita sits on that quieter letterhead as much as on *AAC*.

## Residue

An alphabet on the back of a core board. A writing goal that does not require a pencil. A researcher who treated “functional” as a word that needed a definition. Koppenhaver’s residue is the definition. If the definition does not include print, it is a lunch order. Lunch orders matter. They are not the end of language. A teacher-education semester that says so in front of people who will never attend ISAAC is how a field leaves the exhibit hall for a classroom.

## On the record

Writing by selection — picking letters rather than forming them with a pencil — is the access sentence handwriting chauvinism cannot stand. Koppenhaver’s work kept putting that sentence in front of people who grade “written language” as if a fist grip were the curriculum. It is not the curriculum. The curriculum is the language. The fist is optional.
