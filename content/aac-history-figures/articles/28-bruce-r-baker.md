This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Summer 1980, in Baker’s later telling: a linguist decides that letters are the wrong miniature language for a communication computer. Icons that carry more than one meaning, sequenced, might make a generative system a person could learn. He calls the idea Minspeak. Rick Creech’s phone calls, then a visit to North Carolina, keep him in the work. In 1981 Baker and Kenneth Smith put a version on an AIM 65 with a Votrax voice. In December 1981 he signs with Barry Romich. Semantic Compaction Systems becomes the licensing home. The first commercial outing is remembered at the 1983 ASHA Convention in Cincinnati, on Express III hardware.

Vendor histories are primary sources for what the vendor believes. This pack pairs them with independent secondary accounts that repeat the Cincinnati release and the 1981 agreement. Where they decorate, we subtract.

## What the bet was

A small set of multi-meaning icons instead of a wall of single-meaning pictures. Speed for people who can learn sequences. A language-like system inside a device, not a page of nouns. The bet produced Unity and later map names, a generation of PRC users, and a teaching culture (Van Tatenhove and others) this series will not turn into a how-to.

The bet also produced a brand. Clinicians who only know one language system will assess toward it. Baker’s linguistic seriousness and the company’s sales floor are not the same object. Keep them in one vita anyway. AAC’s history is full of ideas that needed a factory.

The first icons, in his telling, included an ear for phatic talk — the “how are you” that is not information. That is a linguist’s joke sitting on a keyboard. Later Unity maps grew into teaching industries. Tracy Kovach and others appear in vendor histories as people who made the system usable for children and for users who scan. This pack will not turn those names into a second booth. It will say the idea needed teachers as much as it needed a chip.

## Residue

Core vocabulary debates, iconicity debates, and every later app that sequences symbols rather than spelling are downstream. So is Creech’s early role: a user in the design loop before the booth. That residue is more important than a trademark.
