This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A 1987 *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* review article is an unusual object in an AAC pack. Dennis H. Klatt (1938–1988), MIT, wrote the synthesis lineage that Digital Equipment Corporation shipped as DECtalk. Speech-generating devices and, later, a famous cosmologist borrowed that voice. Klatt did not attend ISAAC. He did not assess a child. He is here because AAC’s public sound, for a decade and more, was a telephone-lab instrument.

Hawking’s DECtalk voice became a public identity. Hawking is not a mascot for SLPWOW and is not a second profile. He is proof that a synthesis can become a person. Klatt died in 1988. The personed voice did not.

## What a synthesis researcher is doing in a clinical series

Votrax chips and HandiVoice already spoke. Klatt’s work made a more intelligible, more tunable English. Dedicated manufacturers needed something a stranger could understand in a clinic hallway. Intelligibility is a participation issue. Light would recognize the partner’s ear as part of the system. Klatt built the ear’s object.

He is also a reminder that AAC sits on disciplines that do not owe it allegiance: acoustics, electrical engineering, linguistics. Vanderheiden knew that. Eulenberg knew that. A survey course that only names SLPs is a guild story.

DECtalk’s later life in AAC is a borrowing with identity consequences. Some users kept a robotic voice as *me* when smoother voices became available. That choice is not nostalgia. It is authorship. Klatt did not write that ethic. He wrote a synthesizer good enough that a person could afford to have one. The ethic belongs to the users. The formants belong to MIT.

## Residue

Every later “natural-sounding” voice option, every argument about whether a child should sound like a child, every user who refuses to give up a robotic identity, sits on this laboratory. The residue is a formant, not a therapy goal.

## On the record

Klattalk, DECtalk, and the later device voices that borrowed the lineage are a citation chain, not a product review. 1938–1988 is a short life for a long sound. AAC did not commission the sound. It inherited it, the way it inherited sip-and-puff and a 1620 alphabet. Inheritance is the honest word. Invention prizes are for other magazines.
