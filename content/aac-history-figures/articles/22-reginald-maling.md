This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The whistle hung near the mouth. That is the opening object, and the company’s later “about” page is allowed to tell it as a story as long as the 1963 paper sits next to it. Reginald G. Maling, volunteering at the National Spinal Injuries Centre, Stoke Mandeville, watched men with high lesions call nurses by breath. He designed a suck-and-blow selector that could run a typewriter and a rack of environmental controls. D. C. Clarkson is the co-author on the *Paraplegia* article. The work is dated from 1960 in that article. The company that took the Latin *possum* dates itself to 1961.

This profile will not invent a birthday. Company pages and secondary memoirs do not agree on a full vita, and a magazine that prints a guessed year is doing the thing the style guide forbids. Maling is here as the public name on a switch.

## What the vita that we have actually contains

A volunteer, not a professor of AAC — the field did not exist. An exhibit in Copenhagen, 1963, that won a rehabilitation award. A JAMA note the same year translating the grid for American doctors. A company that outlived the ward culture that made it. Vanderheiden’s later genealogy of electric communication devices starts with this selector. Starting is not owning.

He did not write a symbol set. He did not assess children with intellectual disability. He solved a movement-to-choice problem for adults who had already had speech. AAC’s later children’s services stand on that problem and are not excused by it.

Clarkson’s name belongs in the caption of the 1963 paper. A profile titled Maling is a convenience, not a claim that one volunteer soldered alone. The Copenhagen exhibit, the rugby-injury demonstrator, and the JAMA note are how a British ward device became an American rumor. Rumors are how later undergraduates in Wisconsin learn they are not the first people to wire a typewriter to a breath.

## Residue

Every sip-and-puff, every single-switch scan, every “what is the reliable movement?” meeting is Maling’s residue. The Latin joke in the company name — I can — is almost too neat for this series’ taste. Keep it in the company paragraph. Keep the whistle in the lead.
