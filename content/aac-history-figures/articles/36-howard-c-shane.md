This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

1980, *Journal of Speech and Hearing Disorders*: Howard C. Shane and Anthony S. Bashir, “Election Criteria for the Adoption of an Augmentative Communication System.” The paper is a gate. Gates can protect people from unused hardware. Gates can also keep people in silence. Both uses are in the later argument. Shane, long associated with Boston Children’s Hospital in the public record, is living. McNaughton remembers him in the East Lansing naming room.

## Candidacy as a historical object

Before 1980, many rooms used informal hunches: this child is “ready,” that one is not. Shane and Bashir tried to write the hunch. Later participation language (Beukelman and Mirenda) and “what are we waiting for?” (Kangas and Lloyd) are, in part, arguments with candidacy culture. A fair history keeps the 1980 paper on the table as a first attempt to be systematic, not as a villain origin.

Boston Children’s became a place people associated with AAC assessment, including later work on visual and motor access. This series will not print an assessment recipe.

Candidacy culture and participation culture are not a morality play with one winner. A hospital that ships unused eight-thousand-dollar devices is a candidacy failure. A hospital that refuses a board to a child who cannot yet “prove” a cognitive stage is a candidacy crime. Shane’s 1980 paper is an attempt to be systematic in a decade that had hunches. Later readers may reject the criteria and still owe the attempt a citation.

## The FC years

Shane was among the clinicians who said, early and in public, that facilitated communication failed message-passing tests. That made him a target in rooms that had fallen in love with fluent sentences. It also made him useful to a field that exists to get authorship right. Essay 11 is the fight. This profile is the person who already had a 1980 paper about criteria and then watched a method skip criteria.

## Residue

Every later SGD evaluation form is a grandchild of candidacy, even when it uses participation words. The residue is to notice when the form is protecting the person and when it is protecting the budget.

## On the record

Anthony Bashir’s name stays on the 1980 paper. Boston’s later reputation for careful access work — including people who never liked FC — is a hospital culture, not a lone hero. Shane is in East Lansing’s memory and in the FC bibliography. Those two appearances are one career: criteria, then a fight about a method that refused them.
