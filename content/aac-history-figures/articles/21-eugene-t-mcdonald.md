This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The sibling profession pack already has a McDonald. This one is a different room: the cerebral-palsy ward where a card on a tray was the difference between a guessed life and a chosen one. Eugene T. McDonald (1914–1999), long associated with Pennsylvania State University and with writing that asked clinicians to understand the feelings in a room as well as the motor facts, put communication for children with CP into textbooks when many colleagues were still only stretching heels.

*Understand Those Feelings* is the title people remember when they want a human McDonald. The AAC residue is more ordinary: boards, yes/no systems, the refusal to wait for speech before offering a channel. Zangari, Lloyd, and Vicker’s 1994 historic essay sits in a generation that still had to cite a handful of mid-century clinicians as proof the card existed. He is one of the handful.

## What he did not do

He did not found ISAAC. He did not ship a synthesizer. He did not name the field. He wrote and taught in the decades when orthopedic hospitals were the laboratory and *nonspeech* was not yet a conference. Treating him as a mascot for modern SGD sales is a category error.

The profession-pack sentences about his wider career are not copied here. The man is the same. The scene is the tray.

Penn State’s later AAC reputation — including people who would work in RERC furniture long after his death — sits on a campus that already had a CP and communication habit. That is not a claim that he founded the RERC. It is a claim that American AAC has academic grandparents who wrote before the acronym, and that ignoring them makes 1983 look like Genesis. It was not Genesis. It was a letterhead.

## Residue

A laminated board in a 2026 bag is a McDonald object even when the SLP has never read him. So is the temptation to make the board only nouns. His better pages wanted the child treated as a person with feelings that required words. Feelings that require words are how a noun chart fails.

## On the record

Feelings on a ward are not a soft extra. A child who is talked about as a motor problem will get a motor program. A child who is talked with as a person who might be angry, bored, or funny will need words for those states. McDonald’s title is a method without a protocol: change what the adults think is worth saying, and the card has to grow. That is AAC work in 1958 clothing.
