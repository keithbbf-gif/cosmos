This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A course is a more stubborn object than a paper. Macalyne Fristoe, professor emerita of audiology and speech sciences at Purdue, put one of the first stand-alone AAC courses into an American university with Lyle Lloyd after they arrived in West Lafayette in 1977. Fuller’s memorial and ISAAC’s Lloyd notices repeat the claim. This pack treats “about 1977” as the honest stamp. “The first in the world” is a prize.

Her public author biography — Vanderbilt B.A., M.S., Ph.D.; faculty in psychology and audiology-speech there; later University of Alabama at Birmingham, including a language-intervention project; then Purdue as speech-clinic director and graduate-program leader — is a vita that already had language intervention in it before the acronym existed. She is also widely known as an author of the Goldman-Fristoe Test of Articulation. That test is not AAC. It is why many SLPs know her name without knowing the course. This profile will not pretend the test is the story. It will also not hide it. People are allowed to have two residues.

## Why the course matters

ISAAC could found a society in 1983. ASHA could later add a knowledge area. Neither happens in a vacuum if no one has taught a semester. Fristoe’s AAC residue is the timetable: students who had to sit still for a term and leave with a vocabulary for boards, symbols, and candidacy arguments.

Lloyd’s later fame can erase a collaborator. This pack refuses the erasure. The course has two names on the hallway memory. Use both.

Fristoe’s GFTA fame is a useful trouble. Thousands of SLPs met her as an articulation-test author and never heard that she also helped put AAC on a timetable. The split is the profession’s split: the mouth as a sound system, the person as a communicator. She lived in both rooms. A history that only files her under Goldman-Fristoe is doing the test company’s work. A history that hides the test is being cute. Print both, and keep the 1977 course as the reason she is in *this* pack.

## On the record

CEC’s communication-disorders division and ASHA fellowships sit on her public author page. Those are guild offices. The 1977 course is the AAC office. A later editor who finds a locked birth year may add it. This pack will live with “dates incomplete” rather than a guessed childhood in Nashville.
