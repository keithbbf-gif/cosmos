This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A library book, then a classroom, then a society. Shirley McNaughton, born 1931, is the public through-line from the Ontario Crippled Children’s Centre in 1971 to the first ISAAC presidency (1983–1986). Living subjects in this pack get published work and public offices, not childhood novels. The published work is enough.

She was a teacher looking for a channel for nonspeaking students with cerebral palsy. Helfman’s chapter led to Bliss. Bliss led to a team, a draftsman, a foundation (1975), and a life of arguing — productively — with an inventor, with colleagues, and with a field that still had no name. *Tales of ISAAC* is full of her firsts: the 1980 Toronto nonspeech conference, the 1982 Halton Hills planning meeting, the East Lansing naming weekend. She remembers Larry Weiss challenging Toronto to treat distributors as part of the room. She remembers Gunnar Fagerberg running uphill in the cold. Those memories are sources. They are also one person’s hallway.

## What she actually did

She did not invent Blissymbols. She did something inventors rarely do: she put the marks in front of children and then built an organization that could teach other adults to do the same without waiting for Sydney’s permission every Tuesday. Blissymbolics Communication International’s later authority to publish the marks for people with communication disabilities sits on legal paper that includes her decades.

She also did the unfashionable work of keeping Bliss visible after picture sets became easier to guess. Guessability won many rooms. Grammar lost them. Her later comments in Lloyd memorials still thank him for supporting Bliss inside AAC. That sentence is a history of a minority symbol system staying alive because a few people refused the easier sticker.

The OCCC letterhead — *crippled* — is not a gotcha. It is the institution that paid for the classroom in which the accident happened. Holland Bloorview is the later name. A teacher who stays with one symbol system for fifty years will look stubborn to people who change apps each autumn. Stubborn is how a grammar survives a fashion.

## Residue

ISAAC’s first presidency being Canadian and female is not a diversity slide. It is a fact about where the classroom energy was. A 2026 reader who thinks AAC began in an American engineering lab is missing the teacher with the library card.
