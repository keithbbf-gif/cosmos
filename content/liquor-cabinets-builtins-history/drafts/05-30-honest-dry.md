I want to praise a boring object.

A dry liquor cabinet. Doors. A landing — a top, a slide, a pull-out. An interior that fits the bottles you actually own. Maybe a light. Maybe a lock. No basin. No drain. No footrail required. No neon. It can live in a dining room, a living room, a library, a bedroom if your house is that kind of house. It can leave on our truck. It can come back if you move.

This is the dull excellent cabinet from before Prohibition, the cocktail machine of the 1930s without the legal weather, the credenza of the 1950s with a better interior. It is also what this shop already knows how to be. Casegoods. Made to order. Ferdinand. A designer in the conversation. A finish we can stand behind.

Why people skip it.

Because the word bar pulled a sink into their head. Because a photograph of a hotel pulled a rail. Because a built-in feels more "custom" than a piece, which is a confusion I have already tried to kill. Because they think dry means less hospitable. Dry means the water stays in a pitcher and a kitchen that already exists. Most houses have a kitchen. Many houses do not need a second drain ten feet from the first.

What honest looks like on a drawing.

Bottle heights measured. Glass counted. A landing that is finished like a landing. A door that does not hit a cork. A back that is finished if the piece might float. A depth that is a bottle plus a finger plus a hinge. No fake drain plug. No false book spines unless someone is paying for a joke they understand. No fridge unless we have treated the fridge as a machine with a vent.

I will put a stone top on a dry cabinet if the client wants a working surface and hates wood rings. Stone does not make it wet. A sink makes it wet. Stone on a dry piece is just a good counter that happens to sit on furniture. We still have to attach it so it can ride a truck, or we set it on site and admit a site day.

The honest dry cabinet can be tall or low. Tall is a pantry cousin — more bottles, more bend for the top shelf, a different look in a room. Low is a credenza cousin — less bend, fewer standing bottles, a top that will collect mail. Pick the body you have.

I like a dry cabinet that can take a tray. The tray is the cart's brain without the cart. You load it, you walk to the table, you walk back. The cellarette rolled. The tray is cheaper. The cabinet is the depot.

If you have a collection that is a collection, the honest cabinet is allowed to be large. Large is not a built-in. Large is a big piece. Measure the door. Measure the stair. We will split a top before we will lie about a corner.

I opened on Capitol Hill selling furniture, not plumbing. I still think most "I want a bar" sentences, after you take the photograph away, are "I want an honest dry cabinet in the room where we sit." The rest is costume.

If I am wrong, the wet episode is next door, and the built-in episode is the other side. They are more money and more calendar. They are not more virtuous.
