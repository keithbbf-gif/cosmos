Put the bottles on the bench.

I do not start with a door style. I start with the tallest bottle in the household and the fattest bottle in the household and the bottle they swear is ordinary and is not. A standard 750 milliliter wine or spirits bottle is often about eleven and a half to thirteen and a half inches tall and about three to three and a half inches through. Champagne is taller. A magnum is a different citizen. A boxed decanter is a liar. An extra-tall vodka from a duty-free is a practical joke.

If I draw a shelf from a catalog "bottle height," I will be wrong for someone. If I draw from the bottles on the bench, I will be right for this house.

Clear height is not bottle height. You need fingers. You need the shelf above to not shave the foil. You need a light that is not in the cork's way. I like a clear inch and a half above the tallest bottle as a minimum, more if the shelf is deep and the hand has to come in from the front. A tight pretty interior is how you get a chip on a neck.

Diameter is a row problem. A single row is honest. Two rows is a collection. Two rows wants depth: bottle, bottle, finger, door, hinge. People sketch two rows in a twelve-inch case and then they are angry at the door. The door was doing its job. The sketch was a wish.

Angled racks. Wine likes a rest if the cork needs to stay wet. Most spirits do not want to lie down and think about it. Do not put whiskey on a wine rack because the rack photographed well. Labels hide. Caps leak. The interior should match the liquid.

Dividers. Hepplewhite partitioned nine. Partitions stop the argument. They also steal width. A divider is a quarter inch here, a half there, times the number of bays. I have watched a "twelve bottle" drawing become nine because someone forgot the wood. Draw the wood.

Weight. A dozen full bottles is a lot of pounds on a shelf that was designed like a book shelf. Bottles are dense. I want a shelf that does not bow into a smile. A thick shelf, or a stout one, or a rail under. Glass shelves look like the contemporary glow and they flex. If we use glass, we use glass that is specified as a shelf, not a leftover door lite.

The floor of the case is a shelf that gets kicked. A removable slat floor is a gift when something leaks. A fixed velvet floor is a gift to the first red wine.

I ask the client to send a photograph of the bottles on their current shelf with a tape in the shot. Not a glamour shot. A liar shot that includes the ugly bottles. The ugly bottles are the ones that break the interior.

If they are "starting a collection," I build adjustable shelves and I do not pretend I know their future. Adjustable is not a compromise. It is humility. Fixed fitted bays are for a collection that has already confessed.

Magnums get a bay of their own or they get the top of a sideboard. They do not get jammed. A jammed magnum is how you break a neck and a friendship.

This is the episode I would play first for a younger maker. History is a pleasure. Geometry is the job. The little cellar only worked because someone measured a bottle in 1788 and left an inch and a half under the partition for melt.

Measure. Then draw. Then argue about the door.
