Draw the drain first.

I say that to designers and I say it to myself. A wet bar is a sink. The sink has a waste that has to fall, or a pump that has to be admitted. It has a supply that will drip someday. It has a vent if the plumber is living in the same century as the code. The cabinet is the jacket. If I draw a pretty door and then ask the plumber to find a path, I have designed a conflict.

Hepplewhite already wanted a plug in a dining-room drawer. The suburban rec room already committed a basin to the basement. We are not discovering water. We are refusing to let water be an afterthought.

Sequence, the only sequence I will defend.

Plumber and electrician locate the rough-in. We build the case around the real pipes, or we leave a chase that is big enough for a human hand and a wrench. The fridge, if there is one, gets its own hole and its own outlet. The sink base is a sink base: a floor that can take a leak, a back that can come off, no treasure in the bottom. Then finish. Then the first leak, because there is always a first leak, and then the conversation about whose insurance.

I am not a plumber. I will not quote your book. I will say GFCI is a conversation near basins, and the conversation belongs to a licensed person with the current page. I will say an air gap on a dishwasher is a kitchen cousin you should not invent for a bar unless you know why. I will say a refrigerator ice maker wants a saddle valve less than it wants a real supply and a shutoff you can reach without emptying the cabinet.

The wood next to a sink is kitchen wood. It can take a splash or it is the wrong wood. A sponge lives here. A lemon lives here. Alcohol lives here. That is a hostile climate for a delicate film and for MDF. I have opened sink bases that were flour.

Height of the basin. Too high and you bounce glasses. Too low and you stoop. Kitchen sink height is a starting point, not a religion, because a bar sink is often smaller and used for glasses, not roasters. Still, a toy sink in a tall counter is a punchline. Pick a sink that can take a shaker on its side.

The faucet will stain the stone or the wood behind it. That splash is a material decision. I like a backsplash that is not an afterthought tile leftover. I also like a faucet you can use with one wet hand. Handles are not jewelry.

If the wet bar is on an exterior wall in a cold place, the pipes freeze. That is not a furniture problem until the furniture is blamed. Insist on the mechanical conversation. I have seen a beautiful walnut sink base become a science fair about ice.

Dry people will ask for a wet bar because they want the word. After this episode I want them to want a drain, or to retreat to a dry cabinet with a pitcher. Both are honorable. The lie is the word without the pipe.

This shop can make the jacket. Our trucks can bring the jacket in pieces. The jacket is not the bar until the basin holds water and lets it go.

Next the honest dry cabinet — the object this bench is actually in the business of, and the one most living rooms should have asked for.
