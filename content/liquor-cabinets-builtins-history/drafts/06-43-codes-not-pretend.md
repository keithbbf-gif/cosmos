I am going to disappoint a certain kind of drawing.

This shop makes furniture. We can make the jacket for a sink and a cold machine. We can cut a hole that matches a sheet. We can leave a chase. We cannot sign your permit. We cannot be your GFCI. We cannot tell you that a basement bar in your county is legal because a basement bar in a magazine was pretty.

Near water, electricity has a conversation. The letters GFCI show up in that conversation. The distance, the exceptions, the outdoor versus indoor — those move. I will not quote a section from memory on a channel that will outlive the section. A licensed electrician with the current book is the voice. I am the voice of the door that has to miss the outlet.

Plumbing has a conversation. Traps. Vents. Air gaps. Pumps. I will not design your waste with a pretty lie. I will ask who the plumber is before I lock a sink base that cannot open.

Fire has a conversation if you hide lighting drivers and power strips in a box. I want listed parts and a way to get at them. I do not want a nest.

Accessibility has a conversation if the house needs one. Heights. Knees. Hardware that can be used with a closed fist. I will listen. I will not fake a standard I did not read for your jurisdiction.

Outdoor bars are a different climate and a different book. I mention them only to say: outdoor is not indoor with a prettier stool. Rain. Sun. Bugs. A fridge that is rated for outdoor or it is not. We are an indoor furniture shop in the public story I am allowed to tell. If we ever wrap an outdoor bar, the materials change and the trades change.

What I will pretend to be: a cabinetmaker who has measured bottles, who has seen a ring in a finish, who has watched a compressor die in a box, who has read Hepplewhite's plug and taken it seriously.

What I will not pretend to be: your general contractor, your inspector, your bartender, your lawyer, your historian of a raid that did not happen in this building.

If a project needs a GC, hire one. If it needs a furniture piece, hire us. If it needs both, hire both and let them talk before the drywall closes. The expensive fight is the fight after the wall is closed.

I opened in 1999 by calling designers because designers already knew they were not the electrician. That split of labor is not elitism. It is how a house stays standing while it gets a place to put a bottle.

Insurance is a cousin of code. If a leak from a bar we jacketed ruins a floor, the paper will ask who put the basin there. The paper should find a plumber. If the paper finds only a furniture shop, someone wrote the ticket wrong. I want the ticket to be boring and correct.

I will draw outlets on a shop drawing as circles with a note: by others, verify. That note is not cowardice. It is a fence. A younger maker who erases "by others" to look complete has just volunteered to be the electrician. Do not erase it.

If a client wants a gas tap for a showy flame, we are in a different series and a different license. I can make a shelf next to a permitted appliance. I cannot be the appliance.

The shop episodes are done. One more. How to read a room before anyone draws a bar — the three questions, the bottles on the bench, the drain or the pitcher, the truck or the wall.

I will not end with a coupon. I will end with a walk-through.
