Walk the room with a tape and the tallest bottle.

I do not start at the computer. I start at the wall you think you want, and the wall you actually stand at, and the path from there to the kitchen. If those three places are not the same place, the drawing is already in trouble.

This series gave you a map of objects. A cellarette that rolls. A cabinet that is a case. A built-in that is a wall. A wet jacket around a drain. A dry depot for a tray. A pantry that is service. A rec room that is a tavern downstairs. A glow that is a lamp. You do not need all of them. You need the one that matches the evening you already have.

Ask the three questions until they bore you.

Does it move.
Does it have water.
Does it belong to the building or to a truck.

Then ask the ones the shop added.

What is the tallest bottle.
Where does the glass live.
Where is the landing.
Where does the heat go if there is a machine.
Who wipes the rail if there is a rail.
Who signs the permit if there is a basin.

If the evening is a dining room that still wants to be a dining room, you are closer to Sargent than to a hotel. A sideboard. A dry cabinet. A cart. No stools required. The performance is the table.

If the evening is a living room that wants the bottles present and the kitchen already has a sink, you are in honest dry-cabinet country. Fitted interior. A landing. A light if you want a light. Our truck.

If the evening is a basement that wants to be a place, you are in wet built-in country. Hire the trades. Draw the drain. Let the grille be a grille.

If the evening is a photograph, wait until you have an evening. A party hat is allowed. It should not be a wall.

I opened a shop in 1999. I live above it in Ferdinand. I am the son of a carpenter. I will not invent a bar we built for a famous name. I will stand behind a case that holds what it said it would hold. That is the whole channel, in this series and in the others. The words get their jobs back. The object gets its duties. The photograph comes last.

History was not a costume trunk. The Georgian well, the Federal cooler in a painting, the lock that was inventory, the hide that was a brief, the Deco toolkit, the suburban sink — those are reasons the duties exist. You can skip the dates if you keep the duties. I would rather you keep both. Dates keep a salesperson honest.

If you make furniture, measure the bottle, then the door, then the stair. If you buy furniture, ask whether you are buying a piece or a wall. If you are a designer, write sink or no sink at the top of the page and do not let a nickname move it.

If you want a single homework assignment before you call a shop, do this. Take the bottles out. Put them on the floor where you think the piece will stand. Put two glasses. Put a lemon if you are a lemon household. Walk from there to the kitchen with a wet glass. If that walk is a speech, the room is wrong or the object is wrong. If that walk is ten seconds, you are close. Then take a picture of the bottles on the floor with a tape. Send that. Do not send a hotel.

We will not end with a coupon. We will end with the box.

The bottles can go back on the bench. The box has to be able to live here, in this room, on a Tuesday, when nobody is photographing anything.

That is a liquor cabinet. That is a bar. That is a built-in. Call it the right name, and the box can do its job.
