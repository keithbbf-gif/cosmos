A little cellar was a climate on purpose. A closed liquor cabinet is a climate by accident.

You shut the doors. You trap the last pour's smell. You trap the lemon. You trap the wet glass you meant to take to the kitchen. You trap a July. In a week the case has an opinion. In a year the opinion is a smell you cannot quite name and a white bloom in a corner.

I crack lids on wet boxes. I want a way for a dry cabinet to take a breath without looking like I forgot a door. A slightly proud reveal. A grille that is not a fridge grille, just a slot in a plinth. An interior that can be wiped to bare sense. If the interior is a maze of fixed velvet, the climate wins.

Humidity. Wood moves. Doors that were tight in January are proud in August. A bar next to a slider in a wet climate is a different bar from a bar in a dry house with winter heat. I finish interiors. I leave room in the fit. I do not promise a hairline on a pair of tall doors in a room that swings twenty grains.

Mold. Dark, still, a little sugar, a little wet. That is a pantry problem and a bar problem. The fix is wipe, dry, air, and a household that does not leave a shaker in the well. Furniture cannot parent. It can refuse a well that cannot come out.

Smell. Oak will smell like oak. A liner will smell like a liner. Cheap plywood will smell like a hardware store. If the piece lives in a small sitting room, the smell is part of the design. I like a species and a glue that I would sit with. I do not like a surprise in week three.

Heat without a fridge. Sun. A lamp. A heating vent under the case, which is a common architectural joke. Do not put a closed cabinet on a supply register. The doors will move. The bottles will bake. The register will still be there, louder, because now it blows through a plinth we cut in a panic.

Bugs. I will say it. A dark sweet cabinet can be interesting to a pantry moth and to an ant. Clean. Tight containers for the rare vermouth that should have been in the fridge. This is household, not joinery. Joinery can avoid a hollow plinth that is a highway.

The cellarette's metal liner was a climate wall. It kept water off wood. Our removable tub is that wall. If there is no tub and no sink, the climate wall is the film and the habit of leaving the door open after a wet night. I tell people to leave the door open. It is not elegant. It is cheaper than a repair.

Built-ins have the house climate behind them. A cold exterior wall will sweat a back. I want a back that can take it, or an air gap, or a conversation with the person who insulated the wall. Furniture can step forward an inch. Built-ins have to negotiate.

I live above the shop. I know what a closed room does in August. A cabinet is a smaller room. Treat it like one. Air. Wipe. Do not seal a lemon in it to be charming.

If you want a true cellar climate, that is a different machine — cooling, insulation, a door gasket. That is not a liquor cabinet. That is a wine room. Do not ask a piece of casegoods to be a cave.
