The top is not a lid. The lid we already honored on the cellarette. The top is a counter. People will set a bottle on it, and a glass, and a phone, and a lemon, and a bag, and a plant they swore they would not.

Wood tops. I like wood. I make wood. A wood top on a dry cabinet is a furniture top with a harder life. Shop film. No raw. An edge you can wipe. A thickness that does not look like a door laid down. If the top is also a leaf that folds, it is two tops. Both faces.

Wood tops on a wet bar are a kitchen argument. I have seen them work when the splash is stone or metal and the wood is away from the faucet. I have seen them fail when the faucet kisses the wood all day. The wet line is the line within a splash of the basin. That line wants a material that does not move when it drinks. Wood drinks.

Stone. Quartz, marble, soapstone, granite — I will not rank them like a catalog. Marble is a romantic and a stainer. Quartz is a workshop material that photographs as stone. Soapstone is old and soft and can take a life. Granite is a hotel lobby if you pick the loud one. Pick for the household's lemon habit, not for the showroom under lights.

Seams. A long top will have a seam or it will be a slab that needs a path into the house. Seams near a sink are a sad seam. Seams at a corner are a hip. Ease the edge. I have said it. A sharp stone edge at pour height is a clinic.

Overhangs. If people sit, the stone needs a support that is not hope. Steel. A corbel that is real. Wood that is not a 3/4 skin. If people do not sit, do not add a twelve-inch overhang because a hotel had one. Overhangs catch hips and children.

Metal. Zinc is an American counter memory. It spots. Copper is pretty and living. Stainless is a restaurant and it looks like one. A metal top on a piece of furniture can be right if the room can take a workshop note. A metal top on a quiet walnut piece is a fight unless the metal is a small inset well — the cistern lesson again. The wet part can be a part.

The wet line, drawn.

Basin. Splash. Faucet reach. The joint between top and backsplash. The joint between top and sink. Those joints fail first. I want a sink that has a rim we can understand, or an undermount the stone shop will warranty, not a mystery bowl from a photograph. I want a splash that is not a leftover tile from a bathroom.

A dry cabinet can have a stone top and still be furniture. We set it so it can ride, or we set it on site. A built-in stone top is a site day. The wood waits for the stone or the stone waits for the wood. Someone waits. Write who.

Do not cut a top for a fridge vent as an afterthought. The grille is part of the top or part of the face. If you cut a pretty slab for air, the pretty slab is now a grille with a stone halo. Design the air.

I will put a pad on a wood top before I will lecture a household about coasters. Pads are cheap. Lectures do not stick. The film still has to work when the pad is in the laundry.

The top is the part of the bar that is closest to the homework of 1933. Give it the dignity of a counter, not the dignity of a coffin lid.
