A dining table sees a meal. A bar sees a chemistry set.

Ethanol. Citric acid. Sugar. Water. Ice that becomes water. A cleaner that is too proud. A ring from a glass that was cold and then sat. If I put a jewelry finish on that climate, I will get a white mark and a phone call.

I am not going to name a product like a commercial. I will name jobs.

The landing wants a film that can take a wet glass for an hour. Conversion varnish and other catalyzed shop systems are how a lot of casegoods shops talk about that job. Stone is how a lot of kitchens talk about it. Oil that you renew with a ritual is how a romantic talks about it. Romantics with children and ice will phone.

The interior wants something that can be wiped. Open grain oak will hold a drip and darken in a map of the drip. Fill, or pick a different wood, or live with the map. Velvet interiors are for jewelry and for people who do not spill. I have seen velvet take a red wine and become a painting.

The outside can be prettier than the landing if the outside is not the landing. A door is not a top. A top is a top. I will not run the same delicate sheen over both and hope. Sheen also shows rings. A very high piano gloss on a bar top is a witness. A satin can hide a little and still wipe.

Site stain on a wet top is how you get lap marks. If we shop-finish, we shop-finish. If the piece is a built-in that must be touched on site, the touch kit is the same system, labeled, dated, not a hardware-store cousin. I have said that on the millwork side of this house. It is true on a bar.

Lead liners are not a finish. I mention them only to say: do not "protect" an interior with a historic poison. A removable tub. A modern liner. A finish that can take a leak until you notice.

Samples. Not in the shop only. In the room. Next to the stone. At six in the evening. Alcohol will not change the color in a day. Light will. The household's idea of "whiskey brown" will.

I still like pen and paper. I also like a test: a drop of lemon and a drop of whiskey on a sample, wiped at ten minutes, at an hour, at a day. If the sample fails, the piece would have failed. That is cheaper than a door.

People will ask for a raw look. Raw is a look for a photograph. Raw next to a sink is a sponge. If they want open grain and touch, we talk about a film that keeps the touch and still does the job. If they refuse a film on the landing, we go to stone, or we go to a removable glass, or we go to a conversation about pads and rituals I do not believe they will keep.

The old lacquer on a Deco cabinet chips like money when it is original. When it is yours and it chips in year two, it is a repair. I would rather a film that is slightly less glamorous and slightly more employed.

Finish is not a color name. Finish is a build: sealer, coats, cure, sheen, repair path. The name on a fan deck is the least important word on the ticket.

Next the landing itself: wood, stone, the wet line, and the edge that will meet a hip.
