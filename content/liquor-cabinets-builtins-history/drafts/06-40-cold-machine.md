A fridge is not a drawer.

It is a machine that moves heat. The heat has to go somewhere. If you hide the somewhere, the machine cooks itself and then the wood, and then the phone rings and my name is on the door.

Built-in undercounter units sold for bars and kitchens are usually front-venting. The grille at the bottom is not a decoration. Typical 24-inch units want a cutout on the order of 24 inches wide, about 34 to 34 and a quarter high, about 24 deep — and then you read the sheet, because I just lied in the useful way, which is approximately. Some makers build shallower front-vent units, around 18 inches deep, for a slimmer case. Named types, not a bid. If the sheet says front vent, I do not hide the grille behind a basket of napkins.

Freestanding units vent other directions. They need air that a boxed cupboard will not give. If you put a dorm fridge in a cupboard because the rendering looked clean, you have built a kiln. I have opened those cupboards. The plastic is tired. The wood is tired. The smell is tired.

Cutout is a hole that is true. Level. Square. The height is the height, not the height minus the pretty toe we fell in love with. The width is the width plus the sheet's clearance, not minus the filler we wanted for symmetry. Fillers go where the air is not.

Doors. A fridge door needs more than 90 degrees if the shelves come out. Against a wall, that is a 4-inch conversation some sheets will name. I name it as: do not bury the hinge in a corner and then be surprised.

Ice makers want water and a drain. Gravity drain if we can. A pump if we must, and a pump is a second machine with a second failure. Condensation is a finish problem. The ice maker is a wet line even when the rest of the bar is dry. Admit it.

Wine units and beverage units are not the same temperature job. Do not store milk and Burgundy in a story. Do not store whiskey in a wine unit unless you like whiskey cold and the unit uncrowded.

Panel-ready doors are a furniture job. A wood face on a fridge is a wood face on a machine that vibrates. The panel has to be the weight the maker allows. The handle has to be the handle the hinge can take. I will not hang a 2-inch slab of walnut because we had walnut left.

Electric. An outlet where the sheet wants it, often in the opening, often a flat plug. A cord that is not pinched by a back panel we swore was removable. I am not the electrician. I will draw the hole.

This shop's trucks do not love a fridge inside a piece. We set machines separately when we can. A cabinet that is also a shipping crate for a compressor is a crate that will get a dent.

If the client wants "no fridge, just a cabinet," I am happier than I look. Cold is a restaurant habit and a collection habit. A pitcher of ice from the kitchen is 1933 and it still works. If they want cold, we buy a machine that was designed to live in furniture, and we let it breathe like it was designed to breathe.

The grille is part of the design. Draw it. Do not hide it. The contemporary glow can survive a grille. A dead compressor cannot survive a pretty lie.
