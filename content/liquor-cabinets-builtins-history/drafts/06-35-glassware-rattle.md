The second collection is the glasses.

People remember coupes from a movie and then they buy wine glasses that are eleven inches tall and wonder why the rack bites. Stemware got taller. Rocks glasses got heavier. A tasting glass is a different citizen from a highball. If I hang a rack under a shelf, I have stolen three or four inches from the bottles below and I have invented a chime.

The rattle is information. A thin carcase sings. A door that is a skin over a hollow sings. A hanging glass that touches a neighbor sings. If you close the door and the piece rings, the piece is telling you it is a drum. I do not want a drum unless I am building a drum.

Fixes, ordinary.

Thicker sides. A mid-panel. A shelf that is a structural shelf, not a piece of glass trying to be a shelf. Felt or leather in a rack if the rack is wood. A little space. Do not pack stems like a shipping carton. Shipping cartons are for shipping.

Hanging versus standing. Hanging looks like a bar. Standing looks like a pantry. Hanging puts the rim in the dust and the foot in your hand. Standing puts the rim in your hand and the foot in the dust. I prefer standing for good glasses, with a rail so they do not walk, and hanging for the glasses you are willing to wash the rims of. That is a household choice. I will not moralize. I will dimension.

A rack that is chrome is the 1930s machine. A rack that is wood is this shop. Wood racks need a profile that holds a foot without chewing it. Rounded. Smooth. No proud screw. If I can feel a screw with a fingertip, a glass will find it.

Doors and glasses are a crash waiting for a rush. Soft-close is not a luxury on a glass cabinet. It is a cheap insurance. A catch that slams is a period-correct way to spend money on replacements.

I want a count. Not "some glasses." A number. Eight stems. Six rocks. The ugly mugs you actually use on a Tuesday. The Tuesday mugs will be the ones that live on the landing if I do not give them a home. Landings are for pouring, not for housing the overflow of a bad interior.

Rattle on a truck is a different rattle. We deliver our own work. We pad. We still do not ship a piece with hanging crystal in it if we can take the crystal out. Remove the glasses. Remove the shelves if they are glass. The client can hang the music after the piece is quiet on the floor.

If the piece is a built-in on a bouncy floor, the rattle will come from the house. A fridge compressor will add a beat. Then you have a band. Isolation pads, a stiffer carcase, fewer hanging glasses. I cannot stiffen a whole floor. I can refuse to hang twenty stems over a compressor.

The Georgian dining room kept glass in the pantry for a reason. The wet work and the crystal lived together, away from the performance. If your living-room cabinet holds twenty stems, you have moved the pantry into the performance. Fine. Wipe the rims.

A last shop sentence. If I tap a sample door and it rings like a tin, I do not use that door on a bar. I use it on a linen chest. Linen does not chime.
