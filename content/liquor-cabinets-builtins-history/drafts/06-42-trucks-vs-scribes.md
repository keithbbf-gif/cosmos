We deliver our own furniture. That sentence is on the public about page and it is a shop religion. A piece that cannot survive a ride is not finished. A piece that cannot make a stair is not designed.

I design liquor cabinets as furniture first. Finished sides. A back that can be seen if a room floats the piece. A structure that can rack a little on a lift gate and come back to square. Pads. Glass out. Doors locked or wrapped. A top that is split if the door in the house is smaller than the idea in the meeting.

A built-in is a different drawing. We take parts. We scribe. We leave the last inch to the wall. We do not pretend the last inch was made in Ferdinand to a sixteenth. Walls lie.

The failure I see is the drawing that is both. A twelve-foot run, fully finished as if it will be photographed in the round, also scribed as if it will die into a niche, also one piece as if a stair is a rumor. That drawing is three jobs. I will split it on the bench before I will split it on your landing.

Split lines can be honest. A reveal. A pair of cases with a loose top. A plinth that ties them on site. The cellarette docked under the sideboard was a split line. We are not above it.

Measure the path. Height of the chandelier. Tight turn at the landing. An elevator that is a lie in the building brochure. I have walked paths with a tape like a person who has been stuck. I will walk yours or I will ask the designer to walk it with numbers, not vibes.

Weight. Stone tops ride separately when they can. Fridges ride separately. A piece that is stone plus fridge plus a mahogany cliff is a piano. Pianos have their own men. We are furniture men. I know our backs.

A rented house gets furniture. A staged house that will be sold gets furniture unless the built-in is the reason for the sale. A house you will die in can have a wall that is a bar. That is a life decision. I will not make it for you. I will ask.

If we install a built-in, the site day includes the trades we are not. I will not "just hook up" a fridge that needs a water line. I will not "just" a faucet. Just is how a furniture shop becomes an unlicensed plumber with a nice truck.

The truck is also why I resist hotel parts that cannot be padded. A six-foot mirror. A brass rail that is a spear. We can crate. I would rather design a rail that bolts off and a mirror in panes.

I opened a storefront and lived above it. I moved a shop. I know what moving does to a piece that was built as a wall. If your life includes a move, do not glue your hospitality to the drywall.

Furniture can be architectural. Built-in can be beautiful. The truck is the test I own. If it cannot ride, it is not my ordinary object.

I will also say no to a "just this once" twelve-foot top that has to stand on edge in a box truck next to a fridge. Just this once is how a top gets a crease you will see in a raking light for the life of the piece. Split it. A split that was designed is a joint. A split that happened on I-64 is a scar.

Blankets are not a design. We use them. We still design as if a blanket can slip. Corners get blocks. Glass gets its own crate. A brass rail gets off the piece. The driver is part of the shop. I listen when a driver says a turn is a bad turn. A drawing that cannot hear a driver is a drawing that has not delivered.

If you are not in our truck's territory in spirit — if the piece will be handed to a common carrier who does not love us — the design gets more splits, not fewer. Strangers are rougher than people who have to bring the phone call home.
