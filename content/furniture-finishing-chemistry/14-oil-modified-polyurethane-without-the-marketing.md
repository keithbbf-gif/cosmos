# Oil-modified polyurethane without the marketing

Polyurethane, in the can most people buy for a table, is
usually **oil-modified PU**: an alkyd-like resin with
urethane linkages, drying oils in the mix, mineral spirits
as the carrier. It is varnish with a tougher name and,
often, a tougher film.

It is not a truck bed. It is not the moisture-cure or
two-component systems factories spray. Those exist. They
are a different liability and a different shop. This draft
is the hardware-aisle adult.

## What the urethane is buying you

Abrasion. A little more water and alcohol resistance than
a plain alkyd. A film that can take a school backpack and
a homework year. The cost is often more amber than the
ads, more plastic in the hand than oil, and a repair that
does not melt back into itself with a rag of the original
thinner.

Once it has crosslinked, a scratch is a scratch in a
skin. You sand, you blend, you live with a halo, or you
cut back a whole surface. That is the contract. Do not
sell it as "wipe on more and it disappears" unless you
are still in the oil-heavy wiping-varnish part of the
map.

## Oil-based vs waterborne PU — do not collapse the words

Waterborne polyurethane is a **polyurethane dispersion**.
Different draft. Different feel, different recoat, different
yellowing, different grain-raise fight. The word
polyurethane is a hotel that rents to both families.

If cleanup is mineral spirits, you are here. If cleanup
is water, leave this draft.

## The satin can is a geology lesson

Flatteners settle. Stir, do not shake (foam in oil PU is
a slower death than in waterborne, but you can still
brush bubbles). The last third of a satin can is a
different sheen if you have been skimming the top for a
year. Mix the can. If you cannot get a uniform slurry,
the flattener has become a rock and the can is a glossy
can now. Say so.

## Recoat is chemistry, not optimism

Oil PU has a window. Early, the new coat melts in.
Late, it wants a mechanical scuff. The can's hours are a
guess for a warm room and a thin coat. A fat coat in a
cold room is still wet when the calendar says recoat, and
you will drag wrinkles.

Cure to "school lunch" is days to weeks. I do not put a
rubber-backed mat on a new PU table in week one. The mat
prints. People call that a defect in the finish. It is a
defect in impatience.

## Brush, wipe, or the foam that everyone owns

A bristle or a good synthetic made for oil. A wipe-on
version is thinned PU; useful, slow to build, honest.
A cheap foam brush will work on a flat top and shed on a
spindle. If it sheds, you are now finishing bristles.

Spray oil PU in a house is how you decorate a garage
door with overspray. If you do not have a booth, you do
not have a spray finish. Brush is not a lesser art.

## What I will not do in this draft

I will not walk moisture-cure polyurethane, isocyanate
part-B systems, or "just add this catalyst from a
chemical supply." Those are commercial, trained, ventilated
jobs. The aisle can of oil-modified PU is already enough
film for a life of tables if you sand, wait, and do not
believe the word "permanent" on the hanger tag.
