# Blush, alcohol, and a humid Tuesday

Shellac blush is milk in the film. It looks like the
finish caught a cold. The cold is water.

Alcohol is hygroscopic. It picks up water from wet
air. As the alcohol leaves a fast film, the surface
cools (evaporation is a heat thief) and the local
air can drop below the dew point. Water lands in
the resin, or was already riding in the alcohol,
and the film dries as a fog of tiny interruptions
instead of a clear glass.

A humid Tuesday in a closed shop is a blush machine.

## What it is not

It is not wax bloom, exactly. It is not oil that
never cured. It is not stain pulling, though a
blush and a pulled dye can keep each other company
if you work wet.

If the milk is only in the last coat and a quick
alcohol wipe (a commercial retarder or a light
re-amalgamation with the same alcohol family)
clears it, you were in blush country. If the milk
is in the wood, you have a different problem.

## Prevention that is not a gadget

Work on a drier day. Work warmer than the dew
point story. Do not point a fan so hard that you
force-flash the alcohol and refrigerate the panel.
A slower cut, a slightly slower alcohol sold for
finishing if the supplier has one — not a home-
invented blend — can help.

Do not finish shellac at dusk with the windows
full of summer. The night will do the rest.

## Repair that stays inside the product

Because shellac redissolves, a light pass of
alcohol on a pad can re-melt a blushed surface
and let it dry clearer if the weather has
improved. That is a known shop move. It is also
how you make a bigger blush if the weather has
not improved.

There are commercial "blush erasers" and retarders
in the lacquer world. This series will not mix you
a solvent cocktail. If you own a product sold for
that job, use it as the label says. If you do not,
wait for a better Tuesday and re-amalgamate
gently, or sand and recarry a thin coat in better
air.

## The pad that drips

A dripping pad dumps alcohol, dumps cooling, dumps
water risk, and dumps rings. Charge the pad. Wring
it. The monastery was right about one thing: wet
is not better.

## After blush, the stack

Do not bury a blush under PU "so it doesn't show."
It will show as a ghost, and you will have locked
a defect under a film that does not melt. Clear
the blush or cut it back while you are still in
shellac country.

## A shop note on people

Blush makes amateurs feel cursed. It is weather.
The same person on a dry October day looks like a
prodigy. Take the compliment in October. Take the
day off in July if you can. The resin did not
change. The air did.
