# Maintenance years later: oil vs film

The finish you sold includes the year
you are not in the room. If that year
has no instructions, you sold a
photograph.

## Oil and hardwax

They want cleaning that is gentle, then
a refresh when the wood looks hungry —
dry, pale, no longer beading where it
used to. The refresh is the same family,
thin, wiped off. Not a new brand of
"restorer" that is silicone and perfume.

How often: a dining table might be
yearly. A chest might be never. A
window bench in sun might be twice a
year. Use is the clock, not the
calendar app.

What kills them: the wrong cleaner, a
year of standing water, a client who
adds a different oil every holiday
until the surface is a gum of
incompatible leftovers.

## Film (varnish, PU, waterborne)

They want not to be oiled. Oil on a
sound PU is a smear that collects dirt.
Clean, dry, felt pads on the vase.
When the film dulls from abrasion, you
scuff and add the same film, or you
rub out if there is thickness to give.

What kills them: a mat that traps
moisture, a cleaner that is alkali on
a film that hates alkali, UV on a
film that was interior, a chip that
lets water under and then the sheet
peels.

Recoat years later: the window is
closed. You are in bite country.
Clean, de-wax if anyone waxed, scuff
the whole field, dust like adhesion
depends on it, coat the whole field.
Spot-coating a film is how you make a
map of rectangles.

## Shellac

Damp cloth. No kitchen spray. Repair
is possible and expected. A yearly
"just in case" oil is a mistake.
Alcohol on the table is a crisis.

## Milk paint

If waxed: refresh wax, do not pile
it. If waterborne-cleared: treat as
a film. If bare chalk: accept powder
or add the clear you refused on day
one.

## The furniture polish aisle

Most of it is a short-term shine with
a long-term silicone or wax problem.
I tell clients: if I finished it, you
do not need the aisle. Soap-and-water
in a wrung cloth, or the specific
cleaner I named, or call me.

A polish that "feeds the wood" through
a polyurethane film is a bedtime
story. The wood is under a raincoat.
You are feeding the raincoat.

## Storage and moving

Felt. No moving blankets dragged
across a new film. No stacking
finished tops with newspaper that
prints in humidity. Oil that is still
curing in a sealed truck will smell
like a complaint and may print. Cure
before travel when you can. When you
cannot, separate, pad, and say the
film is still working.

## A card in the drawer

Species, date, product names, sheen,
and the cleaning sentence. Tape it
inside a rail. The next person — a
mover, a child, you in eight years —
cannot keep a chemistry you did not
write down.
