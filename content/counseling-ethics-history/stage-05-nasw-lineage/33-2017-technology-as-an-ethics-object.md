In 2017 NASW published revisions that later official summaries describe as largely focused on the ethical use of technology. The work, those summaries say, followed almost three years of deliberation after a task force that dates to about 2014 — the same year ACA gave screens a whole section.

Three crafts, one furniture problem. Email, records in the cloud, video hours, search boxes, the cheap permanence of a message. Social work's 2017 sentences are the agency craft meeting that furniture: who owns the file, who is in the frame, what a rural telehealth hour does to consent.

This folder will not teach you how to encrypt. It will not host a telehealth checklist. It will say that 2017 is the year NASW spent a revision cycle on machines.

## 2014–2017 as a shared season

ACA's Section H is 2014. NASW's technology cycle lands in 2017. APA's 2002 book had already tried to see electronic media; a full telepsychology rewrite of the ethics book did not arrive in the 2010s the way the practice did. The pandemic would later make the furniture unavoidable. 2017 is pre-pandemic particularity. It looks, from 2026, both early and incomplete. That is what dated technology sections do. They age like computers.

A 2026 ACA revision, if it prints, will almost certainly sound more "AI" than 2017 NASW. That is not a moral grade. It is a product cycle.

## The side-effect title

The same 2017 revision, in NASW's later account, changed the title of Standard 1.05 from "Cultural Competence" to "Cultural Awareness and Social Diversity." Four years later, the National Committee on Racial and Ethnic Diversity would tell the Delegate Assembly that the 2017 title had diluted a commitment, and the Assembly would put "Cultural Competence" back (Draft 34).

I am reporting a later official story about 2017, not retrying the semantic fight. The historical lesson is uglier and more useful than either title: a technology revision can move a culture sentence as a passenger, and the passenger can become the next cycle's whole weather.

Editors who want a "competence versus humility" essay should write it as intellectual history, not as a how-to. This pack will not define the terms for a caseload.

## What 2017 did not do

It did not add the 2021 self-care language. It did not rebuild the 1996 architecture. It did not make NASW an information-security vendor. It did not settle whether a Facebook search of a client is curiosity or assessment. Particular standards try to talk to that last question; this website will not apply them.

## Records, not only video

A technology revision that only talks about the hour on a laptop has missed the file. Agency social work already lived in shared electronic records before 2017. Who owns the note, who can forward it, what a portal shows a parent — those are 2017 objects even when the public summary says "technology." ACA's 2005 transfer-plan conversation is a cousin: mortality plus a file. NASW's 2017 cycle is the file plus a machine.

I will not write a records protocol. I will say that a historian who only remembers "telehealth" from 2017 has remembered the vendor's favorite noun.

The 2017 sentences also arrived before a pandemic made video ordinary and before generative models made "the assistant" an ethics noun. Dated inventories look incomplete from the next decade. That incompleteness is a feature of the genre, not a failure of the 2014 task force.

Pediatric speech telehealth, school-platform messaging, recorded swallow studies — ASHA has its own technology-ethics weather. NASW 2017 is a neighbor's dated inventory. WOW Therapies can host the neighbor's story without importing the neighbor's rules onto a speech chart.
