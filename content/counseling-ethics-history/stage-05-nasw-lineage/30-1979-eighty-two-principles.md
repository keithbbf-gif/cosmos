In 1977 the NASW Delegate Assembly created a task force to revise the code and make it more useful in practice. Charles Levy chaired it. The Assembly adopted the result in 1979. NASW's later historical notes say the 1979 revision went into effect on 1 July 1980.

Those dates are the same lesson APA teaches with June 2003: adoption is not effectiveness. A social worker's 1979 conduct is not a 1980 case.

## What 1979 built

A preamble. Six sections of brief principles. A declaration, in Reamer's later account, that the principles could ground formal ethics complaints. Later NASW teaching materials count eighty-two principles in the 1979 family as it was amended. I am taking the count from those materials, not from a hand tally. `[VERIFY]` if a live caption wants the exact 1979-as-adopted number versus the later-amended number.

The six sections, as historians list them, pointed the social worker toward self, clients, colleagues, employers and employing organizations, the profession, and society. That last pair is the tell. APA's 1953 book talked to the public as a scientist-citizen. NASW's 1979 book talked to the *agency* and to *society* as ordinary rooms of the job. Charity organization and public welfare are still in the architecture.

## Why members wanted it

The 1960 page was lovable and thin. Thin pages do not help when a colleague is impaired, when an agency asks for a file, when a fee is dirty, when a dual relationship is already in the kitchen. 1979 is social work's first attempt at a charging document. It is also still a list of principles, not yet the 1996 cathedral of standards.

Belmont is 18 April 1979. Tuskegee's public life is the 1970s. Research-ethics weather and practice-ethics weather thickened together. I will not claim Levy's committee was "answering Belmont." I will claim a reader in 1979 could not open a newspaper without meeting both weathers.

## 1990 and 1993: the 1979 house gets rooms

NASW's later notes say a Federal Trade Commission inquiry in 1990 pushed revisions on solicitation, fee setting, and payment for referrals. 1993 added principles including dual relationships and impairment. Those are not full rebuilds. They are sutures on the 1979 body — the same kind of object as APA's 2010 and 2016 sutures, quieter, about markets and the body of the worker rather than about interrogations.

A profession that needs an FTC reminder about fees is a profession that has become a market. 1990 is a useful anti-romance. Ethics codes are not only about tenderness. They are about money.

## Levy as a public name

Charles Levy's chairing is in the secondary literature. This pack will not become his biography. The usable fact is that NASW, like APA with Tolman and Hobbs, can name a person who ran a shop. Named shops are checkable. Anonymous "the profession evolved" sentences are not.

## 1979 as a charging-document bet

The 1960 page could shame. The 1979 sections could, in the association's telling, support a file. That bet is why later social workers talk about "the standards" the way psychologists talk about numbered counts. It is also why 1990's FTC suture belongs on this body: once you have a charging document, markets will test it.

I have not reconstructed a 1979 complaint. I will not invent one for color. The architecture is the evidence that NASW wanted to be able to *count*.

## What 1979 did not yet do

It did not write a mission statement of the 1996 sort. It did not list six core values as a numbered American catechism. It did not produce 155 standards. It did not say "self-care" as an integrity sentence. It did the 1970s job: categorize, enable a complaint, admit the agency.

For WOW Therapies, 1979 is why a later social worker's code sounds more organizational than a counselor's. The speech clinic's ASHA neighbor has its own organizational sentences. Different book, same discovery: the employer is an ethical object.

The next essay is the rebuild: August 1996, a mission, six values, Frederic Reamer's committee in the secondary telling, and a book students still outline from memory.
