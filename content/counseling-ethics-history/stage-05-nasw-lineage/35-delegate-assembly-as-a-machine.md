NASW's code does not change because a staff writer had a good week. It changes when the Delegate Assembly, under the association's bylaws, says it changes. 13 October 1960, 11 April 1967, 1979, August 1996, the 2020 and 2021 amendment votes: those are parliamentary dates. A historian who writes "social work evolved its ethics" without the Assembly is writing folklore.

APA has a Council of Representatives. ACA has a Governing Council. The names differ. The discovery is shared: a national code is a vote.

## Why a machine matters

Machines have calendars. You cannot sneak a standard into a living code between meetings, not if the bylaws are being followed. That slowness is a protection and a frustration. Protection: a trending workshop does not become a charging document overnight. Frustration: a harm that is already in the agencies may wait years for a sentence.

PENS was the counterexample in psychology: Board emergency adoption in days. NASW's public story about 2021 is the opposite tempo — deliberation, Assembly, a June 1 effective date. Tempos are ethics. They are also politics.

## Task forces inside the machine

Levy in the late 1970s, the 1990s revision committee, the 2014-era technology task force, NCORED's 1.05 recommendation: the Assembly does not draft from a blank page. It receives. Task forces are how a parliamentary body pretends to have read more than it can read. Hobbs would recognize the move. So would Fisher.

A task force can capture a revision. A task force can also be the only reason a revision is competent. Both captures are in the historical record of associations generally. This pack will not invent a NASW conspiracy. It will say the machine has parts, and the parts have names.

## What the machine cannot do

It cannot bind a non-member. It cannot rewrite Arkansas statute. It cannot be a licensing board. It cannot, by voting, make a laminated card into a conscience. It can make a laminated card more recent.

State chapters (NASW Texas, NASW Illinois, NASW Missouri) often become the public teachers of a revision, hosting webinars and posting FAQs. Those chapter pages are how this draft checked 2021 dates. Chapters are not the Assembly. They are the Assembly's loudspeakers.

## Chapters as loudspeakers, not as the vote

NASW Texas, Illinois, Missouri, and other chapters posted 2021 FAQs and hosted webinars. Those pages are how a general reader, including this writer, checked the 1 June 2021 date and the limited scope. Chapters can teach a suture. They cannot adopt one. A historian who cites a chapter webinar as if it were the Assembly has reversed the machine.

APA divisions and ACA regions do cousin work. Loudspeakers matter. They are still loudspeakers.

## For readers who want a villainous "association"

Associations are easy to hate. They send dues notices and they are slow. They are also the only reason a one-page 1960 oath became a book a board can cite. If you want no machine, you want 1938's tenuous unwritten code, and APA already tried that.

WOW Therapies is not a Delegate Assembly. Hosting this history is not a vote. The next stage leaves the three letterheads and puts them in the same room: confidentiality and *Tarasoff*, year-counts, competence, consent, screens, conversion-practice refusals, and who actually adjudicates.

If you remember one NASW fact from this stage, remember the spine: 1960 page, 1967 line, 1979 sections, 1996 values, 2008 names, 2017 machines, 2021 sutures, and a Delegate Assembly that had to say yes each time.

A parliamentary calendar is slower than a scandal and faster than a statute. That middle speed is why codes feel late to activists and early to boards. The feeling is not a defect in the 1960 page. It is the machine doing what machines do.
