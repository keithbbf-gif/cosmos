On 1 June 2021 a set of NASW amendments took effect. The association's own FAQ is unusually precise about scope: the changes were limited to the Purpose of the Code, the Ethical Principles (specifically the value of Integrity), and Standard 1.05. Two weathers, one effective date. Self-care entered the Purpose and Integrity language. "Cultural Competence" returned as the title of 1.05, with language NASW described as more operational, on the recommendation of the National Committee on Racial and Ethnic Diversity.

A limited amendment is a historical kindness. It stops a reader from saying "the 2021 code" as if 1996 had been replaced. The house is still 1996. The sutures are 2021.

## Self-care as an integrity sentence

American helping crafts spent the 2010s turning "self-care" from a magazine word into a workplace word. Burnout research, agency understaffing, a pandemic in the amendment's last winter — the weather is real. Putting self-care into a *code* is a different act from putting it on a tote bag. It says the worker's maintenance is not only a wellness tip. It is tied, in NASW's 2021 telling, to the profession's integrity.

This site will not teach a self-care plan. It will not tell a social worker to take a bath. It will say that a national association, in 2020–2021 Delegate Assembly votes, decided the Purpose and Integrity sentences were the right rooms for the word.

Critics can call that a neoliberal move (make the worker responsible for the agency's extraction). Supporters can call it a long-overdue admission that an impaired or exhausted worker is an ethics problem, which 1993 had already begun to name. Both readings are historical objects. This folder will not pick a tote bag.

## Cultural Competence, restored

NASW's later explanation is frank: the 2017 title "Cultural Awareness and Social Diversity" had been heard, by NCORED, as a dilution at a moment when culturally diverse publics were demanding more, not less, commitment. "Competence," in the committee's argument, could be an umbrella for awareness, humility, and sensitivity, and could be written so that demonstration — not vibe — was the point.

I am paraphrasing an official story. I am not defining competence for a caseload. I am not settling the humility-versus-competence seminar. I am dating a title change and a title restoration. Titles are documents. 2017 and 2021 are a pair.

## What the FAQ refuses

It refuses the myth that 2021 rewrote confidentiality, technology, or the six values. If your CE slide says otherwise, the slide is wrong. Limited amendments are easy to inflate because "2021 NASW Code" sounds like a new book. It is a suture year, like APA 2010 and 2016.

## Living committees, public documents

NCORED is a named NASW body. Its members are not public property. The recommendation is a public institutional act. No portraits. No gossip.

## 2020 and 2021 as two Assembly winters

NASW's public account treats the self-care language and the 1.05 restoration as 2020 and 2021 Delegate Assembly work that then shared a 1 June 2021 effective date. Two winters, one turn-on. That is a parliamentary fact, not a branding fact. A slide that says "NASW added self-care during COVID" is roughly right about weather and too neat about the vote.

The pandemic is allowed in the climate. It is not allowed to become a claim that 2021 is "the COVID code." The FAQ's limited scope refuses that inflation.

## For this site

A speech clinic in Southeast Arkansas met 2020–2021 as PPE, Zoom, and a caseload that did not shrink. NASW's 2021 words are a neighbor's official memory of the same years. WOW Therapies can host the memory without adopting the neighbor's Integrity sentence as a speech-practice slogan.
