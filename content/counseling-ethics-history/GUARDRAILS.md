# Claims guardrails — counseling ethics-code history

Educational history only. These sentences must never go on wowtherapies.com,
in ads, or in schema markup.

This file is a refuse list. If a draft drifts toward any line below, cut the
line. Do not "soften" it into a how-to.

## Never say (treatment / DIY / ethics how-to)

- Any instruction a reader could follow as a session, a charting rule, or a
  complaint: "if you hear X, call Y," "document it this way," "use this
  decision model on Tuesday," "file with the board by…"
- "Try this at home," "self-treat," "ask yourself these ten questions."
- Dosing of psychiatric medication, taper advice, or "ask your doctor about
  [drug]" as content.
- "This method is the gold standard for [diagnosis]."
- Crisis instructions that replace emergency services. A single line — "If
  you are in danger, call local emergency services" — is enough. Do not write
  a safety-planning protocol in these essays.
- A walkthrough of how to file an APA, ACA, NASW, or state-board complaint.
  You may *name* that those rooms exist. You may not staff them from a blog.

## Never say (medical / legal)

- That WOW Therapies diagnoses, treats, or "specializes in" a DSM/ICD
  condition **in these history posts**. Service pages are a different file.
- That reading an essay is therapy, counseling, legal advice, or a substitute
  for a licensed clinician or a qualified attorney.
- That this site interprets the current ACA, APA, or NASW code for a case.
- PHI, composite patients, or "a client once told me" stories. No invented
  vignettes with identifying color.
- Guarantees of outcome.
- Anything a reasonable reader would hear as a diagnosis of themselves or
  their child.
- Anything a reasonable reader would hear as advice about their own
  malpractice exposure.

## Never say (history that is too neat)

- "APA invented professional ethics in 1953." (Medicine, law, nursing, and
  social work were already writing rules. APA wrote *its* first printed
  code then.)
- "The 2010 APA amendments closed the torture question." (They changed
  Standards 1.02 and 1.03. The Hoffman report is 2015. Standard 3.04 was
  amended in 2016, effective 1 January 2017. The argument did not end.)
- "Hoffman settled every named person's reputation." The July 2015 Sidley
  Austin report is a public document. Named psychologists later disputed
  parts of it. Cite the document. Leave the reader's judgment intact.
- "ACA's 2014 code banned all dual relationships." (The 2005 and 2014 codes
  are more particular than a cartoon ban. Do not flatten them.)
- "NASW's six values are eternal." They are a 1996 committee distillation.
- "Tarasoff is the national rule." It is a California line that other
  jurisdictions rewrote, limited, or refused.
- That a code and a statute are the same object.
- Conversion-therapy "success." Name the public refusals. Do not teach the
  method.

## Never say (brand / SEO)

- "Best therapist in Arkansas" or any superlative about the practice.
- Disease keywords paired with a CTA ("overcome PTSD today — book now").
- Schema `MedicalTherapy`, `Drug`, or `treats` / `indication` on history
  posts. `Article` is enough.
- Fake author credentials.
- Patient testimonials in this folder.
- Auto-linking these posts to speech, OT, or PT service pages as if an
  ethics history were a treatment.

## Copyright (hard)

Do not paste a current or recent association code into these files. The
codes are copyrighted. Name sections. Paraphrase the public argument.
Point the reader to the association's own page for the living text.

Short historical phrases that are already in secondary literature — "clear
and imminent danger," "serious and foreseeable harm," the names of the
five APA General Principles — may appear as *labels*. They are not a
license to reprint a standard.

## Allowed

- Dated description of what a revision claimed, who voted, who dissented,
  and what later committees changed.
- Named public reports (Hoffman, July 2015; PENS, 2005) as historical
  objects.
- Controversy, including racism, sexism, eugenics, and national-security
  interrogations, when it is part of the public record.
- A short "why a contemporary clinic still reads this" closing that does
  **not** become a protocol or a complaint form.
- The educational note and Claims box already in each draft.

## If asked for both a history and a how-to

Print the history. Refuse the how-to in one sentence: this series does not
publish treatment protocols, ethics worksheets, or complaint procedures.
