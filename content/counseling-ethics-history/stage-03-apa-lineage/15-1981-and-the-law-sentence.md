The 1981 APA text, published in *American Psychologist* 36:633–638, changed the title to *Ethical Principles of Psychologists* and, in the association's own later historical PDF, became the first code to put a specific principle or standard directly on the conflict between law and ethics. Earlier printings had talked about "Moral and Legal Standards." 1981 made the collision harder to skip.

This is not yet PENS. This is not yet Hoffman. This is a late-Carter, early-Reagan document written for psychologists who already lived among subpoenas, school records, and hospital bylaws. The later "Nuremberg defense" nickname for a 2002 sentence is a nickname. The problem the nickname names is older than 2002.

## Why law showed up as a named problem

Psychologists by 1981 were licensed in more states, hired by more organizations, and pulled into more courtrooms than the 1953 writers had needed to imagine. A duty to a client and a duty to a judge do not always rhyme. A duty to an employer and a duty to a code rhyme even less.

APA's later historical memo traces the language from 1953 through the short codes and then marks 1981 as the turn toward an explicit conflict sentence. I am using that memo as a map, not reprinting its quotations at length. The map is enough: the association knew the collision was a recurring incident long before it became a national-security incident.

## 1989/1990 as a small revision inside the same family

The 1981 principles were amended 2 June 1989 and printed again in 1990 (*American Psychologist* 45:390–395). Anniversary talks sometimes swallow that amendment. It is a reminder that "the 1981 code" is already a short series. This pack will not pretend to a line-by-line 1989 diff without the pages in hand. `[CITE NEEDED]` for the specific 1989 changes. The bibliographic fact is solid: APA listed the 1990 printing as an amended 1981.

## What 1981 still was not

It was not the 1992 split between aspirational General Principles and enforceable Ethical Standards. That architecture is the next essay. 1981 still lives in a world where "principle" does more of the work.

It was not a permission slip written for interrogators. Hoffman, in 2015, looked back at the 2002 1.02 language and found that the meaningful drafting changes in that family of sentences happened before 11 September 2001 and were not, in his team's account, designed to help a government interrogation program. He also found that critics had already flagged a "Nuremberg" problem during the 2002 revision and that APA officials did not follow the flag far enough. That is a 2002 story. It needs 1981 as a grandfather, not as a villain.

## Other crafts, same collision

Counselors in schools already lived the collision: parents, principals, mandated reporting. Social workers in public agencies lived it daily. NASW's later standards about law, employers, and clients are cousin sentences. ACA's later texts about serious and foreseeable harm are cousin sentences. 1981 is psychology's public naming of a problem the other two crafts were also writing toward.

A court can still outrun all three codes. *Tarasoff* is 1976. Mandated-reporting statutes are a fifty-state pile. HIPAA's privacy rule is a 2003 compliance date. The code sentence is one instrument in a drawer. Draft 36 opens the drawer. This essay only labels the 1981 instrument.

## How to read a "follow the law" clause without a worksheet

Read it as a membership body trying not to force members into an impossible bind. Read it also as a membership body trying not to become a higher court. Both aims can be honorable. Both aims can be abused. History's job is to keep both aims on the page. A blog's job, if it is reckless, is to tell you which aim to pick on Tuesday. We will not.

For WOW Therapies, the 1981 title shift is a small object with a long shadow. *Ethical Principles* sounds softer than *Ethical Standards*. It is not always softer in practice. Names are weather. The 1992 rename — *Ethical Principles of Psychologists and Code of Conduct* — will try to have both weathers at once.

The next essay is that 1992 split: principles you cannot be expelled for, and standards you can.
