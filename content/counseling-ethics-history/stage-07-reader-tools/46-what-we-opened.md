A bibliography can be a performance. This one is a confession. `CITATIONS.md` is the working list. This essay is the voice-over: what I held, what I heard secondhand, what a later editor must reopen before anyone maps `status: draft` onto a staging site and calls it done.

## Primary objects I could name with dates

APA's own bibliographic list of prior codes, as reprinted in later editions: 1953, 1959, 1963, 1968, 1977, 1979, 1981, 1990 (1989 amendment), 1992, 2002, 2010, 2016. Hobbs 1948 as a journal article with a checkable citation. PENS as a 26 June 2005 report. Hoffman as a 2 July 2015 Sidley Austin report, revised 4 September 2015, leaked 14 July 2015. Council's 7 August 2015 vote as publicly tallied 157–1. NASW's 13 October 1960 and 11 April 1967 dates as the association tells them. 1 July 1980 and 1 June 2021 as NASW effective dates. ACA's archive list: 1961, 1974, 1981, 1988, 1995, 2005, 2014. *Counseling Today*'s May 2014 process story. *Tarasoff* 1976, *Jaffee* 1996, Belmont 18 April 1979, HIPAA Privacy Rule 14 April 2003.

Those are the spine. If a draft in this pack states one of those dates, it is stating a public object.

## Secondary objects I used as leads

Fisher's process notes via Sage chapters and *Decoding the Ethics Code*. Reamer's 2014 *Advances in Social Work* essay and his NASW Press textbook. Pope's public historical remarks on 1938 and the "tenuous" 1952 phrasing. PACA and Kaplan/Martz on 1961 length and 2014 process. Iowa Psychological Association on the 2018 ECTF charge. JISS 2019 on APA versus ACA as distinct professions. Circulating two-year / five-year comparison charts.

Secondary is not a slur. It is a status. When a draft says "later commentators say the 1961 pamphlet was five pages," that is a secondary sentence. A later editor with ACA's scan should turn it into a primary sentence or mark it failed.

## What I did not hold

The 1953 book in my hands. The 1961 pamphlet in my hands. The 1974, 1981, 1988, 1995 ACA PDFs line by line. The raw Hobbs incident cards. ECTF draft text. The withdrawn 2026 ACA draft. Arkansas board rules as currently posted. I marked those absences with `[VERIFY]` or `[CITE NEEDED]` instead of inventing particular clauses.

A clean false particularity would have been easier to read. The style guide forbade it.

## Copyright, again

I did not paste a living code. I did not paste Hoffman at length. I did not paste the Hippocratic Oath. Short historical labels — "clear and imminent danger," the five 2002 principle names, the six 1996 values — appear as labels. If a later editor finds a block quote that looks like a standard, they should cut it. The linter flags long blockquotes for that reason. It cannot catch a paraphrased theft. A human has to.

## Wikipedia

I did not use Wikipedia as a source. I may have used it, in the research pass, as a lead to a date I then checked against an association page or a bibliographic list. Leads are allowed. Citations are not.

## What a later editor should reopen first

1. APA living URL: is the 2002/2010/2017 text still the house, or did Council adopt a successor after the March 2025 comment docket?
2. ACA living URL / Nexus: did Fall 2026 print, and what are the adoption and effective dates? (September 2026 circulated in secondary blogs; Nexus said Fall.)
3. Arkansas board citations: which code, if any, is adopted by reference?
4. 1953 page count, 1961 page count, 1996 standard count, 1979 principle count — numbers I treated as circulating.
5. Year-counts (two / five) against the living APA and ACA texts.
6. ECTF comment-count particulars (2,650+ / 66 groups, 5 August 2025 note) if a caption wants the numbers.

`last_verified` on every draft is 2026-09-14. That stamp is a promise to reopen, not a halo.
