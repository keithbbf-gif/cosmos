**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A headline that says "new ethics code" is usually selling a workshop. Sometimes it is also true. The difference is four questions.

**Who voted?** Council, Delegate Assembly, Governing Council, a board on an emergency calendar. A task force does not vote a code into force. A staff blog does not. A podcast guest does not.

**When was it adopted?** 21 August 2002. 13 October 1960. End of March 2014. A missing adoption date is a missing object.

**When is it effective?** 1 June 2003. 1 July 1980. 1 June 2010. 1 January 2017. 1 June 2021. Conduct is judged, in APA's prefatory language, by the book that was on when the conduct happened. A headline that skips the lag is already a little dishonest.

**Is it a book or a suture?** NASW's 2021 FAQ said Purpose, Integrity, and 1.05 only. APA's 2016 action was Standard 3.04. People still said "the 2017 code" and "the 2021 code" as if Fisher and Reamer had been replaced. They had not.

## Previews, drafts, and the disappeared PDF

ACA's 2026 draft went down while comments were reviewed. A disappeared draft is not a secret plot. It is a process. Teaching from a disappeared draft is how a CE vendor stays ahead and how a clinician learns a sentence that may never bind.

APA's ECTF, as of 14 September 2026, had not replaced the 2002 house. Headlines about a "transformational" APA code were, at that date, charge-language, not printer-language.

## Who paid for the headline

Association press office: usually a real vote, sometimes a soft date. CE vendor: usually a preview. Newspaper: usually a scandal (Hoffman, 14 July 2015) or a culture fight (2014 values). State chapter: usually a webinar about a suture. Each payer has a tell. None of them is this website's job to become.

## What to do with the living URL

apa.org/ethics/code. counseling.org's ethics pages. socialworkers.org/About/Ethics/Code-of-Ethics. If the living URL still shows the old book, the headline is ahead of the printer. If the living URL has changed, read the prefatory dates before you change a practice — and do not change a practice from a blog.

## "Updated for AI" as a 2020s tell

Neighboring associations (CAMFT, AAMFT, and others) spent the mid-2020s writing machine-tool sentences. Headlines followed. ACA's 2026 process sat in that weather. A headline that says "ethics codes now require AI disclosure" may be describing a neighbor, a draft, or a wish. Ask which association, which vote, which effective date. If the answer is a podcast, the headline is a preview.

This pack named the weather in Drafts 28 and 40. It did not write the requirement.

## For a speech clinic's readers

ASHA has its own headline weather. This pack will not interpret it. The method transfers: vote, adoption, effective date, book versus suture. WOW Therapies can teach the method without staffing a CE desk.

If a headline makes you want a worksheet, the headline has already won. Come back to Draft 02. Dates first. Parable later, if at all.

A second tell: the headline names a symptom or a lawsuit in the same clause as the code. "New ethics code will change how you handle X" is a CE product even when X is real. History can say the book moved. It cannot say how *you* will handle Tuesday.

The next essay is the living-people rule, written so a later editor does not turn Fisher, Reamer, Francis, or Hoffman into gossip.

If you only remember one question, remember the effective date. Adoption without a turn-on is a press release. A press release is not a charging document.
