The living chairs in this pack have public jobs and private lives. The jobs may be named. The lives may not. Frederic G. Reamer wrote the books that later students use to understand the 1996 NASW architecture. Perry Francis spoke on the record about the 2014 ACA revision. David H. Hoffman signed a 2015 report. Celia Fisher's task-force role is already a Stage 3 object; here she is only an example of the rule. As of 14 September 2026, those people were living. Living people are not public property.

This pack uses their public roles, their public pages, their public books, and, in Hoffman's case, a public report. It does not use their health, their families, their hallways, or a generated face.

## Why the rule is stricter here than in a dead-figure pack

The heritage pack can write Pinel and Dix from a distance that death provides. This pack's modern chairs are still answering email. A "colorful" paragraph about a living chair is almost always gossip with footnotes. Gossip is not history. It is a different craft, and it is not ours.

The ACT pack already wrote this rule for Hayes and Kabat-Zinn. We are repeating it because ethics-code history is full of living names and because a later editor will be tempted.

## What counts as public

A journal article. A book on a publisher's page. An association press release. A *Counseling Today* interview. A report posted after a leak and then posted officially. A university directory that lists a title — used only for the title, not as a photo source.

What does not count: a conference badge photo scraped from Flickr. A Facebook about. A student's Rate-My-Professor paragraph. A "colleague told me."

## Hoffman as the hard case

The 2015 report names people and alleges conduct. Some of those people disputed the account. A history site that repeats every allegation as texture has become a second leak. This pack used the report as a document that moved a vote. It did not become a character sheet.

If you want the report, it is a public PDF. If you want a thriller, this is the wrong folder.

## Portraits

`PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` defaults to no-portrait. Type, a masthead, a title page if rights are clean. No AI faces. No "period photograph" of a 1947 committee that is actually a model.

Draft 47 will say this again for the graphics agents who do not read Stage 7.

## Dead chairs, still not a novel

Tolman died in 1959. Hobbs died in 1981. Charles Levy is a public historical name for the 1970s NASW shop; this pack did not write his private life either. Death relaxes the gossip rule a little and does not relax the invented-quote rule at all. A colorful 1947 scene I have not sourced is still a fabrication.

## For WOW Therapies

The live site should not invent a clinical director or hang a stock "ethicist" over these essays. Author field: a holding editorial user. Byline is not `voice_check: human`. That field is a QA flag.

If a living person asks to be removed from a sentence that only names a public role, the editor should look at the sentence. Public roles are how institutions are checkable. Vanity removal of a chair's name is how origin stories go anonymous and then get invented.

A public interview is still a public document. *Counseling Today*'s 2014 process piece is why Perry Francis appears here at all. If the interview had not existed, the name would not have been required. That is the test.
