**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Frank Parsons opened a Vocation Bureau in Boston in 1908 and died the same year. *Choosing a Vocation* came out in 1909 without him. The sister pack already used that fact as a founding scene for counseling-as-a-job. This essay uses it as a founding scene for counseling-as-a-craft-without-a-code.

For fifty years after Parsons, guidance workers had associations, journals, and school jobs. They did not have a five-page *Ethical Standards* with APGA's name on the cover. That gap is easy to moralize. It is more useful as a historical condition: you can have a profession's habits before you have the profession's pamphlet.

## What guidance needed a rule for

A vocational counselor in 1920 sat between a young person and an economy. The power was real: a recommendation, a test score, a whispered "not college material." The temptations were ordinary. Over-promise a placement. Share a family's business with a principal. Use a new psychological test because it looked modern. Take a fee from an employer for steering labor.

Those temptations did not wait for 1961. School systems wrote local rules. Deans wrote memos. The National Vocational Guidance Association, founded in 1913, built a culture of meetings and publications. Culture is not a code. It is the weather a code later tries to freeze.

When APGA printed *Ethical Standards* in 1961, secondary accounts say the five pages already talked about client welfare, researched interventions, and continuing education. I have not reproduced the pamphlet here. The point is the *belatedness*. The craft recognized itself on paper a half-century after the Bureau.

## Tests, the war, and the borrowed lab coat

World War I and World War II poured psychological testing into the American public's idea of "guidance." Army Alpha, later school group tests, later interest inventories: a counselor's desk became a place where a number could change a life. APA's 1953 incidents included industrial tests designed to keep workplaces segregated. Guidance offices were not innocent of that weather. A 1961 counseling code that talks about competence is, in part, talking about not waving a test you were not trained to read.

The sister pack will not be rewritten here. What this lane needs is the documentary link: counseling ethics inherited psychology's testing anxiety without inheriting psychology's research section in full. Later ACA codes would grow their own assessment language. The 1961 seed is a guidance seed.

## Four associations, then a printer

1952 is the institutional year. NVGA, the trainer group (NAGCT), the student-personnel-for-teachers group (SPATE), and the American College Personnel Association built APGA so they would have a larger voice. A larger voice wants a letterhead. A letterhead, in the Flexner climate, wants a code.

Nine years passed between the merger and the 1961 pamphlet. I do not have a satisfying archival story of those nine years in front of me. `[CITE NEEDED]` for the committee minutes. What I can say without the minutes: postwar veterans' programs, school-counselor growth, and the same professionalization wave that produced APA's 1953 book and NASW's 1960 page made a five-page guidance code look overdue rather than sudden.

## What the 1961 page was not

It was not a clinical mental-health code. The later slide toward DSM language, insurance panels, and distance counseling is 1995–2014 weather. Reading the 1961 object as if it were already about HIPAA and Instagram is a category error.

It was not a license. Virginia's 1976 statute is fifteen years downstream. Most states were later than that. A pamphlet without a board is a club document. Clubs matter. They do not revoke a state permission.

It was not APA. Commentators who treat ACA as "APA for master's clinicians" erase the guidance origin. The origin is why ACA later sounds more particular about the relationship and more comfortable with development-and-wellness sentences. Whether that is a virtue is a later argument. The genealogy is not.

## For a speech clinic that shares the word

A school-age child who comes to WOW Therapies for articulation may also have a school counselor down the hall. That counselor's professional ancestor is closer to Parsons than to Freud. The counselor's living ethics text, if the person is an ACA member or if an Arkansas board points that way, is a descendant of 1961. The SLP's ethics home is ASHA. The child does not care. The historian should.
