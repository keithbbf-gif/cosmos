**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

ACA's public archive names three codes between the 1961 pamphlet and the 1995 book: 1974, 1981, 1988. I have not produced a line-by-line commentary of those three texts for this draft. A later editor with the archive PDFs should. What this essay can honestly do is put the three years in their weather and refuse to skip them.

Skipping is the usual CE move: 1961, then jump to 2005 or 2014, where the anecdotes are better. The middle revisions are how a guidance association learned to talk like a clinical guild without yet being, everywhere, a licensed one.

## 1974: after the first duty-to-protect shock, before the first license

*Tarasoff*'s first California opinion is 1974; the better-known 1976 opinion sits two years later. Virginia's general counselor-licensure statute is 1976. APGA's 1974 code sits in that doorway. I will not claim a clause I have not held. I will claim the doorway: any American helping craft revising ethics in 1974 was revising in a country that had just been told, by a state supreme court, that a therapist's silence is not absolute.

Whether the 1974 APGA text used "clear and imminent danger" — the phrase 2005 would later retire — is a question for the PDF. `[VERIFY]`. The phrase's later fame is 2005. Do not backdate the retirement speech.

## 1981: same calendar year as APA's title shift

APA published *Ethical Principles of Psychologists* in 1981. APGA published a code in 1981. Coincidence is allowed. So is a shared weather: more licenses, more courtrooms, more third-party payers, more anxiety about the relationship. CACREP is founded in 1981. A craft that is standardizing the master's is a craft that needs a thicker house rule.

Again: I will not invent 1981 ACA sentences. I will say the year is overdetermined. Several machines turned over at once.

## 1988: late Reagan, early managed-care shadow

By 1988 the association was five years into the AACD name (Draft 24). The job was sliding toward clinical mental health in many states even when the letterhead still said "counseling and development." A 1988 code is a good place to look, when someone holds the PDF, for the first real thickening of relationship and confidentiality language that 1995 and 2005 would then rewrite.

Until that PDF work is done, the honest public sentence is bibliographic: 1988 exists. It is not 1961. It is not 2014.

## How to use an archive without pretending you have read it

Name the years. Name the weather. Mark what you have not read. Invite the next editor to read it. That is better than a fluent false particularity. This pack's style guide prefers an ugly `[VERIFY]` to a clean invention.

If I later sit with the three PDFs, this essay should grow dated paragraphs, not a new personality. The personality is already here: middle revisions are maintenance, and maintenance is how a five-page craft becomes a twenty-four-page craft.

## What the middle years were not

They were not a conversion-practice debate in the 2010s sense. They were not a social-media section. They were not HIPAA. They were the years when "counselor" in many American mouths started to mean "the person you see for distress" as much as "the person who helps you pick a shop class."

WOW Therapies' speech lane met its own middle years (ASHA revisions, school law). Different archive. Same lesson: the glamorous codes sit on either side of a lot of unglamorous reprinting.

The next essay is the name changes that sat on top of these reprints: APGA to AACD in 1983, AACD to ACA on 1 July 1992. Then 1995 can be read as a code that already knew its new name.
