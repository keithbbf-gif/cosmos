On 1 July 1992 the American Association for Counseling and Development became the American Counseling Association. The association's own history page presents the change as a way to name the common bond and the unity of purpose. Nine years earlier, in 1983, APGA had already become AACD — personnel-and-guidance giving way to counseling-and-development.

These are not cosmetic facts. A code is printed under a name. A board that adopts "the ACA Code of Ethics" is adopting a 1992-and-after letterhead. A historian who cites "the 1988 ACA code" is slightly anachronistic; 1988 is an AACD document in a strict naming. This pack will say APGA / AACD / ACA as the year requires.

## What a rename wants

1983 wanted to sound like a counseling profession, not like a personnel office. "Development" kept the guidance-and-human-growth habit so the school people would not hear a hostile takeover. 1992 dropped "development" from the national name and bet that *counseling* was now the word that gathered the rooms.

The bet mostly worked in public English. It did not end the school-versus-clinical argument. ASCA still has its own ethical standards. Counselor-education programs still argue about identity. A name is a wager. Wagers have leftovers.

## 1992, the same year as APA's split

APA's 1992 ethics book and ACA's 1992 rename share a calendar and not a committee. The coincidence is useful only as a reminder: the early 1990s were a professionalization weather for both crafts. Psychology was splitting principles from standards. Counseling was splitting a new name from an old one. Social work, four years later, would split a 1996 architecture of values and standards. Three crafts, one decade, three kinds of paper.

## How names confuse clients

A client in Southeast Arkansas may see "ACA" on a wall and hear the Affordable Care Act — the insurance marketplace that stole the acronym in ordinary American speech after 2010. A rarer collision is the American Crystallographic Association. Acronym collision is not a joke in a small waiting room. It is a literacy problem. This pack will write the full name on first use in each essay. We have not always succeeded. Editors: fix the misses.

APA's collision with the American Psychiatric Association is worse. Draft 03 already called it a student's rite. Counseling's 1992 name at least does not share three letters with a medical specialty. It shares them with a health-insurance statute. History does not have to be convenient.

## What the rename did not do

It did not create licensure. States did that, slowly, after Virginia 1976. It did not create CACREP (1981). It did not write the 1995 code by itself. It did not settle whether a school counselor and a mental-health counselor are the same profession. It put a cleaner noun on a messy coalition.

NBCC's National Certified Counselor credential (from 1982) is another noun. Clients mix certificate and license. The 1992 association name sits beside both and is identical with neither. Draft 04's three rooms still apply: association, board, court.
