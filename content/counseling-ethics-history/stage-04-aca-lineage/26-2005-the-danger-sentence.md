The American Counseling Association published a new *Code of Ethics* in 2005 and then did something associations rarely do well: it recorded a ten-part public interview series about what had changed. Part 1 is titled "The end of 'clear and imminent danger.'" The other nine parts, as the archive still lists them, walk romantic and sexual relationships, dual relationships, end-of-life care, cultural sensitivity, permission to refrain from a diagnosis, selection of interventions, transfer plans, technology, and confidentiality after death.

That list is a historical map. It is not a protocol. This essay will use the map and refuse the worksheets the map could become.

## The danger sentence

"Clear and imminent danger" had been a phrase counselors could hide in or trip over. The 2005 revisers, in the association's own telling, replaced it with language closer to "serious and foreseeable harm." I am naming the contrast as the interview series names it. I am not pasting either standard.

Why it mattered: "clear and imminent" sounded like a courtroom's emergency. A lot of real harm is serious and foreseeable without being a siren. A lot of sirens are not the counselor's to answer alone. The phrase change is an attempt to talk to mandated reporting and duty-to-protect weather without pretending every state wrote *Tarasoff* the same way.

This site will not tell you which Arkansas statute to follow. The 2005 sentence is a membership object. The statute is a state object. Draft 36 keeps them in separate rooms.

## Dual relationships, without the cartoon

Part 3's title — "Allowing dual relationships" — is a public-relations sentence. The underlying argument, as later teachers have it, was that a total ban did not fit rural life, school life, or some cultural settings, and that a more particular conversation about harm, power, and consent was more honest. The cartoon version ("2005 said dual relationships are fine") is false. The cartoon version ("only 2014 noticed small towns") is also false. 2005 is the year ACA put the nuance on the national record.

Rural Southeast Arkansas is exactly the kind of place the nuance was written toward. A speech clinic and a counseling office in the same county still share grocery stores. The 2005 conversation is a cousin of that fact. It is not a permission slip written for this website to apply.

## The other rooms, briefly

End-of-life: a 2000s bioethics weather entering a counseling code. Culture: the 1992 competencies paper arriving as code language rather than as a separate manifesto. Diagnosis: permission to refrain when a label would harm — a sentence that only makes sense after counselors became diagnosers for insurance. Transfer plans: the recognition that a counselor is mortal and mobile. Technology: email and early distance work, nine years before Section H. The dead: a confidentiality duty that outlasts the hour.

Each of those could be its own essay. This pack gives them one paragraph so 2005 is not reduced to a single phrase. Reduction is how CE slides work. We are not a CE slide.

## 2005 beside APA and NASW

APA's 2002 book was already on. NASW was still in the 1996 architecture, with a 2008 nondiscrimination update coming. ACA's 2005 revision is the middle craft's early-2000s thickening. Three calendars, one decade, three different "we needed more sentences" stories.
