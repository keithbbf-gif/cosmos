**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The American Personnel and Guidance Association printed *Ethical Standards* in 1961. Later commentators, writing after the 2014 ACA code, like to say the first document was five pages and the later one was twenty-four with a fifty-word glossary. I have not laid a ruler on a library copy of the 1961 pamphlet for this draft. `[VERIFY]` the page count against ACA's archive scan before a live caption. The usable fact is the *bet*: short, public, already a house rule.

ACA still lists 1961 as the first item in its public archive of previous editions. That listing is how a general reader can check that the pamphlet is not a workshop myth.

## What later writers say was already there

A Pennsylvania Counseling Association overview of the 2014 code, drawing on Lynn Linde and others, says the 1961 Standards already told professionals to rest practice on well-researched interventions, to hold the client's well-being as the primary concern, and to keep learning. Those three sentences — evidence, welfare, competence — are the later book's bones.

I am taking that description from secondary commentary, not from a pasted 1961 page. If a later editor holds the scan, they can tighten the verbs. Until then, the secondary claim is marked as secondary. It is still more honest than inventing a 1961 social-media section.

## What five pages cannot do

They cannot adjudicate a thick modern complaint about encryption, a Facebook "friend," or a values conflict over a referral. They cannot, by themselves, satisfy a 2010s licensing board that wants particularity. They cannot replace a statute.

They can announce a craft. That is what 1961 is for in this pack: the moment guidance-and-personnel work said, on paper, that it had standards a stranger could read. NASW had said it in first person the year before. APA had said it at book length eight years before. APGA said it in a pamphlet you could put in a school counselor's desk.

## School desks and college desks

1961's readers were more likely to sit in a school or a dean's office than in a private outpatient suite billed to an insurer. The later clinical turn would strain the pamphlet. 1974, 1981, 1988, 1995 are the strain showing up as revisions. 2005 and 2014 are the strain showing up as new rooms: diagnosis, transfer plans, technology, social media.

If you teach 1961 as already a clinical-mental-health code, you are backdating a job. The sister pack warned about that. This essay repeats the warning because ethics teachers love origin pages and origin pages attract anachronism.

## Archive as a public good

ACA's willingness to keep old editions listed — 1961, 1974, 1981, 1988, 1995, 2005 — is part of the ethics. A craft that hides its previous sentences is a craft that wants to be forgiven in the dark. A craft that lists them is a craft that can be historically read. This folder depends on that listing. It will not, however, host the PDFs.

Copyright still applies. Short historical description does not.

## Who a 1961 reader was supposed to be

A school counselor with a filing cabinet. A college personnel worker with a housing list. A vocational counselor with a test booklet. The 1961 pamphlet is written toward those desks. It is not written toward a 2014 private-practice HIPAA notice or a 2021 NASW self-care sentence. If a training program assigns 1961 as "week one, then jump to 2014," the jump erases the desks. This pack keeps the desks.

The National Defense Education Act (1958) had already begun to fund guidance as a Cold War educational project. `[CITE NEEDED]` before a live sentence that ties a particular 1961 clause to NDEA money. The climate is enough: five pages arrived in a country that had just decided school counselors were a national resource. A national resource wants a national pamphlet.

## For Southeast Arkansas

A school counselor in a Delta district in 1961 might never have seen the pamphlet. National codes travel slowly. That slowness is history. A 2014 code on a laptop travels faster and still may not be the board's text. Draft 45 will come back to what a small-city clinic actually inherits: a stack, not a tablet.

The next essays walk the middle revisions and the name changes. Keep 1961 in your pocket as the short object. Everything after is thickening.
