**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The California Supreme Court, in *Tarasoff v. Regents of the University of California* (1976), held that a therapist's duty can include protecting a foreseeable victim — a holding that sits on a 1974 opinion and then traveled, poorly and unevenly, into other states' statutes and cases. The U.S. Supreme Court, in *Jaffee v. Redmond* (1996), recognized a psychotherapist-patient privilege in federal courts. APA, ACA, and NASW, in various years, wrote confidentiality standards that are neither of those opinions.

If you mash the three objects into "therapists can't tell," you have a poster. If you mash them into "therapists must tell," you have a different poster. This essay is the un-mashing.

## Confidentiality is a professional duty

A code sentence about confidentiality is a membership (and sometimes a board) duty: do not gossip, do not treat the file as a dinner story, do not disclose without a recognized exception. The exceptions are where the history lives. 2005 ACA retired "clear and imminent danger" for "serious and foreseeable harm." NASW's 1999 suture was about disclosure language. APA's standards sit beside its law-conflict sentences.

Those are guild objects. They change when guilds vote. They do not, by themselves, tell a federal judge what is privileged.

## Privilege is a courtroom shield

Privilege is a rule of evidence: a court will not compel certain testimony. *Jaffee* is a 1996 federal-court object, written in a case about a police officer and a social worker. The opinion's existence is why a later trainee hears "privileged" as if it were a synonym for "confidential." It is not a synonym. Privilege can be narrower. Privilege can be waived. Privilege can fail when a state duty or a crime-fraud exception walks in.

This site will not map Arkansas privilege. `[VERIFY]` any live sentence that names it. Privilege is state-by-state plus federal. A blog that says "your sessions are privileged" as a comfort is already practicing a little law.

## Duty to protect is a state weather

*Tarasoff* is California. Some states wrote duty-to-warn or duty-to-protect statutes. Some limited the duty. Some refused the expansion. The sister pack already said this. This pack repeats it because ethics teachers love to teach *Tarasoff* as the national clause in every code. It is not. It is a California line that other jurisdictions rewrote.

A code that talks about serious harm is talking *toward* that weather. It is not enacting California in Arkansas.

## HIPAA is a fourth object

The Privacy Rule's common compliance date, 14 April 2003, sits nine months after APA's 2002 code turned on. HIPAA is a federal regulatory machine about covered entities and protected health information. Many counselors, psychologists, and social workers are in that machine. Some speech clinics are too. HIPAA is not an APA standard. A CE day that treats "HIPAA" and "ethics" as one PowerPoint is doing violence to both.

This folder will not teach a Privacy Rule. It will say the 2003 date belongs on any timeline that pretends "records" were only an association problem.

## 2005 ACA Part 10: the dead

ACA's 2005 interview series included confidentiality of the deceased. That is a guild object the courts and HIPAA also touch. The dead do not consent. Families want the file. Historians of ethics should notice the 2005 room without writing a script for a grief.

## For a speech clinic

An SLP's confidentiality home is ASHA plus HIPAA plus school law (FERPA) when the work is in a district. A counselor down the hall has ACA-or-board plus the same federal machines plus, maybe, a duty-to-protect statute. The shared fax is not a shared rule. WOW Therapies can say that without interpreting anyone's exception.

If you came here to learn whether to break a silence, you are in the wrong folder. If you came here to learn why the words *confidential*, *privileged*, and *duty to protect* keep being used as if they were one word, stay. They are three words. They have three addresses: 1976 California, 1996 federal, and a pile of association years.
