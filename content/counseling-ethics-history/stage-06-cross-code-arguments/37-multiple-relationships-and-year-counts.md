Public comparison charts used in counselor-education classrooms say the APA ethics text forbids a sexual relationship with a former client for at least two years after termination, and the ACA 2014 text uses five. I have not, for this draft, laid the two living PDFs side by side. `[VERIFY]` before a live caption. The circulating contrast is stable enough to treat as a teaching object, and unstable enough to forbid a tone of certainty.

Year-counts are a crude instrument. They are also one of the few places a general reader can *see* two guilds writing different numbers about the same temptation.

## Why a number at all

Because "never" and "use your judgment" both failed as public sentences. Never does not describe the life after a three-session vocational consult in 1961 the same way it describes a seven-year psychoanalysis. Judgment, without a floor, is how powerful people date former patients and then call it fate.

A floor is a membership body's attempt to be particular without being a novelist. Two years and five years are not science. They are votes. Votes can be criticized as too short, too long, too crude. The criticism is part of the history.

## Rural life, school life, the grocery store

ACA's 2005 interview series spent a part on romantic and sexual relationships and a part on "allowing dual relationships." The second part was, in the revisers' telling, an attempt to talk about small towns, schools, churches, and cultures in which a total ban on every extra role was a lie.

Southeast Arkansas is a small-town ethics problem whether a code says so or not. A clinician and a client will meet in the only pharmacy. A school counselor is already in a multiple relationship by job description: the child, the parent, the principal, the IEP team. NASW's 1993 additions, in the association's notes, included dual relationships because agency life had already arrived.

This site will not draw the line for a grocery-store wave. It will say the line has been a national argument since at least the 1990s sutures.

## What the year-count does not cover

It does not cover barter, supervision, research participants, students, supervisees, or the cousin of a current client. Those rooms have their own sentences in the three books. A slide that reduces "multiple relationships" to sex-and-years is a slide about anxiety, not about the codes.

It does not cover the fact that some harms happen inside the two or five years and some happen after. A floor is not a ceiling of risk. Draft 11's limit still holds: a number is not a conscience.

## APA particular, ACA particular, NASW structural

Commentators (Kaplan and others) have said ACA writes more particular relationship rules and APA writes more principle-driven permission to think. NASW writes the agency into the problem. Those tendencies are visible from a distance. Up close, all three books have both particularity and mush. Distance is this essay's job. Up close is a supervisor's job, not a website's.

## Students, supervisees, and the other forbidden rooms

Year-counts about former *clients* do not exhaust the relationship problem. Students, supervisees, research participants, and employees sit in other sentences in all three books. A 2014 ACA social-media section adds a wall that is not a year-count. A 1993 NASW impairment sentence adds a worker who should not be in any extra role because they should not be in the first one.

A chart that only has two numbers is a chart about one anxiety. Useful. Incomplete.
