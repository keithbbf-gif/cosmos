"Informed consent" is a traveling phrase. In the Nuremberg Code (1947) and the *Belmont Report* (1979) it is a research protection. In later APA, ACA, and NASW practice standards it is a clinical ritual: fees, recordings, risks, the right to refuse, the special problem of a court-ordered evaluation. The phrase moved house. The furniture in the new house is a form.

A form is a descendant. A descendant is allowed to be thinner than a trial. A history pack that treats a clinic's two-page intake as Nuremberg is kitsch. A history pack that pretends the form has no grandparents is amnesia.

## What practice codes were trying to catch

Money: what the hour costs, who pays, what happens when the insurer says no. Records: who may read them, when the client may read them. Fisher's 2002 process notes name a cultural shift toward patient access. Recordings: the tape, then the digital file, then the cloud. Techniques: a name (EMDR, a third-wave brand) that the client has not asked for. Distance: who is in the room when the room is a screen (ACA 2014, NASW 2017).

Those are 1980s–2010s objects. A 1961 guidance pamphlet could talk about welfare without talking about a superbills. Consent thickened when billing thickened.

## Court-ordered work as the hard case

APA's publicly discussed assessment standards include occasions when ordinary consent is strained — a court wants an evaluation; the "client" is not the person who asked to be there. ACA's texts have cousin problems in schools and in forensic-adjacent work. NASW's agency work includes involuntary hours (child welfare, hospitals) that make "consent" a bitter word.

This essay will not sort those cases. It will say the codes *know* they are bitter. A blog that writes "always get consent" as if the always were simple has not read the forensic rooms.

## What consent is not

It is not a waiver of malpractice. It is not a substitute for competence. It is not a permission to surprise a person with a method they cannot refuse without losing a child-welfare case. It is not, on this site, a downloadable template.

Students are taught to treat a signed page as a shield. History's job is to treat the signed page as a late professional invention that can be done well or as theater.

## HIPAA and the form pile

14 April 2003 added a Privacy Rule notice to many waiting rooms. Associations did not write that notice. Covered entities did, or their vendors did. The pile of paper a parent signs at a speech clinic in Southeast Arkansas is a cousin pile: ASHA, HIPAA, sometimes FERPA, sometimes a school contract. Counseling's pile has different letterheads and the same late-century faith that a signature can carry an explanation.

## Children and the second signer

A pediatric speech hour and a school-counseling hour share a problem the 1947 Nuremberg list did not write for: the person in the chair is not the person who signs. Parents, guardians, districts. Codes later grew sentences about assent, about who is the client, about school records. This pack will not sort those sentences into a form. It will say the late object called consent is even later when the signer and the speaker are not the same body.

## For this site

WOW Therapies will not publish a model counseling-consent form. It can say: the word arrived with a research passport, settled into practice codes as billing and recording and screens arrived, and remains unfinished in any room where the state is the other party.
