# Style Guide

This series talks to people who sleep in rooms, buy suites, restore testers, or specify a bed for a house that still has a chamber. The voice is magazine history: object-first, named sources, no catalog sell.

## Voice

Magazine voice, not catalog voice. Open on a bed, chest, cradle, or factory floor the reader can picture. Prefer museum accessions, pattern books, price lists, company marks, and monographs over trend lines.

Speak to the reader as a peer. No coaching, no cheerleading, no closing moral.

First person is allowed for shop or room observation (especially Arkansas / present-tense bench pieces). It is not a diary.

Cross-links to other essays in this series may use “chapter N” when they name a specific object type. Do not talk about “this chapter,” editorial briefs, or the writing project in body copy.

## Banned phrasing

Do not use: delve, landscape (metaphorical), robust, leverage, unlock, cutting-edge, game-changer, “In today’s,” “It’s important to note,” Moreover, “Whether you’re,” “In conclusion,” “At the end of the day,” “rich tapestry,” “journey,” “elevate,” “empower,” “seamless,” “holistic,” “unpack,” “nuanced” as filler.

Do not open with a dictionary definition. Do not close with takeaway bullets unless the list is a working table (dates, marks, parts).

No COSMOS. No AI. No process talk about how the draft was written.

## Facts and citations

Pin furniture history to a named source: Winterthur, the Met, MESDA, Chipstone *American Furniture*, company marks, *Time* articles, CPSC rule citations. If the fact is shop knowledge and not pinned, mark `[CITE NEEDED]`.

Do not invent voyages, prices, accession numbers, or royal commissions.

## Bradley Brand / BBF

Bradley Brand Furniture may appear in **one** Arkansas / present-tense shop slug (`36-arkansas-hardwood-bedrooms`, `43-a-shop-looking-at-a-highboy`). Elsewhere, use regional mills and forests without brand copy.

## Structure

1. Open on an object, room, or failure.
2. Name the form and woods.
3. Say how it is joined, hung, or factory-made.
4. Walk labor, class, and regulation where true.
5. End with sources and cross-links, not a sermon.

Target **1,800–2,800** words of body text unless front matter sets a different band.
