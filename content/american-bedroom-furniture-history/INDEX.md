## How to read

Chapters 1–2 stay with the colonial chamber and the bed as a textile machine. Chapters 3–7 treat the high chest and dressing table — Boston, Philadelphia, Newport — before the form leaves fashion. Chapters 8–11 are Federal, Empire, Shaker, and southern testers. Chapters 12–17 are the factory suite, Eastlake, metal beds, wardrobes, and the vanity. Chapters 18–21 are Craftsman, Greene & Greene, Mission, and the Colonial Revival highboy. Chapters 22–24 are Hollywood, waterfall, and hire-purchase. Chapters 25–30 are mid-century platforms, McCobb, blonde maple, Danish rooms, Nakashima, and the 1970s. Chapters 31–35 run the catalog suite into MALM, storage beds, the boxed mattress, and solid wood now. Chapter 36 is the Arkansas hardwood bridge. Chapters 37–45 cut across: afterlives, labor, joinery, the suite as merchandising, restoration, the present room, a shop looking at a highboy, children’s beds, the nightstand.

Bradley Brand Furniture and the Warren/Wilmar geography appear only in chapters 36, 42, 43, and 45, as a shop next to mill towns, not as a catalog.
