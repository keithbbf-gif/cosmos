## How to read

Chapters 1–12 stay with excavated rooms and the few woods that survive. Chapters 13–17 treat the medieval Christian and Islamic workshops that left pulpits, stalls, and chests. Chapters 18–25 leave the European sequence on purpose: China, Japan, Korea, South and Southeast Asia, West Africa, and the pre-colonial Americas are not “influences on the West.” They are furniture histories with their own seats, floors, and ranks.

Chapters 26–36 return to named European and American shops and pattern books. Chapters 37–40 follow industrial and post-industrial production. Chapters 41–42 cut across the series (materials; labor and sitting). Chapter 43 is the only place a reader meets American mill towns and Arkansas hardwood, and then as history, not as a catalog.
