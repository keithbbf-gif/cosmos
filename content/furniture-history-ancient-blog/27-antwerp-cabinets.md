# Antwerp Cabinets

The V&A’s Flemish cabinet on stand (W.61-1923), given by Mrs E. Hearn, is thought to be Antwerp work of the second quarter of the seventeenth century; the stand may be a nineteenth-century addition. Ebony veneer on an oak or pine carcase, painted panels (the Prodigal Son cycle, in this case), a facade of small drawers and a central door: a collector’s architecture. The 1996–97 conservation, published in the V&A *Conservation Journal* 23, had to stabilize flaking paint before the object could travel. The cabinet had become too famous to be as fragile as it was.

Antwerp in the 1600s made paintings, prints, books, harpsichords, and these cabinets. They were sold to a Europe that was inventing the collection: coins, shells, small paintings, medals. The cabinet is a *studiolo* that shuts. Reinier Baarsen and the Rijksmuseum catalogs, and Ria Fabri’s work on Antwerp cabinets, are the scholarly doors. Ebony is the skin. Ebony is also a tropical wood arriving through Portuguese and Dutch routes — a materials fact that belongs in chapter 41 as well.

## A facade of doors

The type varies: the *Kunstschrank* in Augsburg (the Pommeranian cabinet, the one now in the Getty’s cousin-literature; the Uppsala art cabinet) is more German and more encyclopedic, with hidden drawers and a program of knowledge. The Antwerp cabinet is often more pictorial: oil panels as the “precious stones” of the facade. Both are lockable theatres. A key is part of the furniture.

Veneer technology — sawing ebony thin, gluing it to a cheaper carcase, profiling moldings in the veneer rather than in the solid — is the technical sentence. The V&A conservators noted that ebony was not used in the solid for the decorative profiles. That is a shop decision about cost and about the way ebony behaves. The nineteenth-century stand on W.61-1923 is a warning: cabinets lose their legs and gain new ones in the trade.

## Spain, the *escritorio*, and the vargueno

The Spanish *vargueno* (a fall-front writing cabinet on a stand) is a cousin, with a different hardware language and a different interior of little columns. It is sometimes a colonial object as well as a peninsular one. American *varguenos* in viceregal shops mix Iberian type and local woods. Caption the wood. Do not assume Toledo.

South German and Swiss *cabinets* with architectural interiors (the “miniature palace”) feed the same hunger. The user is a person who owns small things and wants them to have a city to live in.

## Pictorial furniture

Painted cabinets put Rubens’s city on a drawer front (not always Rubens; often a specialist in small figures). The Prodigal Son on W.61-1923 is a moral cycle on a luxury object — a tension the owner may have enjoyed. Furniture here is a patron of painting. The later European split between “fine art” and “decorative art” has to work to pull the panels out of the box. Some panels were pulled; some boxes arrived in museums already looted of their pictures.
