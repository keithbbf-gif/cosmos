# Style Guide

This series is for [bradleylumbercompany.com](https://bradleylumbercompany.com): historical essays on Bradley County, Warren, Arkansas, and mill-town America. It is not a catalog. It does not sell lumber, furniture, or a tour.

`voice_check: human` means the writer draft passed this file. `voice_check: edited` means a human editor read the piece aloud and tightened grammar, voice, and non-commerce guardrails. Neither value is a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Voice

Ken Burns tone: a still photograph held long enough to hear the mill. Prefer a clock, a name, a weather, a dated newspaper over a thesis sentence. Long sentences may carry a fact. Short sentences may stop the room.

Write as a person who has stood on the square and smelled kiln-dried pine. Do not coach. Do not cheerlead. Do not close with a moral or a “key takeaway.”

First person is rare and only for a standing observation (“the depot still sits below the grade”). It is not a diary and not a brand voice.

Human voice means: a reader can hear someone talking who has read the county paper and walked the brick. No keyword stacks. No “in today’s mill towns.”

## Banned phrasing

Do not use: delve, landscape (metaphorical), robust, leverage, unlock, cutting-edge, game-changer, “In today’s,” “It’s important to note,” Moreover, “Whether you’re,” “In conclusion,” “At the end of the day,” “rich tapestry,” “journey,” “elevate,” “empower,” “seamless,” “holistic,” “unpack,” “nuanced” as filler, “hidden gem,” “best-kept secret,” “shop now,” “add to cart.”

Do not open with a dictionary definition. Do not close with a recap list. Decorative bullet stacks are out.

No COSMOS. No AI. No process talk about how the draft was written. No prices. No SKUs. No lead times.

## Facts and citations

County and town facts come first from the CALS *Encyclopedia of Arkansas* (Bradley County, Warren, Timber Industry), Rachel Silva’s 2012 downtown Warren tour script (Arkansas Historic Preservation Program), Judge David A. Bradham’s 1951 county sketch (reprinted *Bradley County Family Histories*), and named monographs (Balogh; Curry 1952; Gatewood 1976; Smith, *Sawmill*). Tornado numbers come from the 1949 *Eagle Democrat* record and later National Weather Service / Wikipedia outbreak summaries — if they disagree, say so.

If a fact is local memory and not pinned, mark `[CITE NEEDED]`. Do not invent a letter, a wage, or a conversation. If a story is too good and the paper is thin, say so.

## Commerce

`commerce: false` on every article. This lane may name Bradley Lumber Company, Southern Lumber, Arkansas Lumber, Potlatch, Robbins Flooring, and Bradley Brand Furniture as historical actors. It may not invite a purchase, a quote, or a mill tour booking.

## BBF heritage

Bradley Brand Furniture (BBF) is the long afterlife of the same hardwoods and the same county, not a sponsor credit. One or two sentences where the mill’s furniture stock, flooring plant, or later shop work is the actual subject. No product names. No “our craftsmen.” No call to action.

Preferred bridges:

- oak, gum, hickory, and pine that left Warren as flooring and furniture stock
- the 1901–1903 mill hour that later shops still date from
- a later Warren bench that still knows those species
- the Chambers years after Potlatch, named as civic history, not as a family advertisement

Saline River Workshop and Etsy are out of this series unless a sentence is literally about a living shop name in the public record. Prefer the mill and the county.

## Structure of an article

1. Open on a still: a clock, a river bend, a smokestack, a depot step, a name on a time clock.
2. Walk the dated history. Name people. Name companies. Name the year.
3. Put the mill inside the town — church, scrip, school, Jim Crow, the other Main Street — where the subject requires it.
4. Let mill-town America appear as type only after the particular has been paid.
5. Leave the reader on one remaining object or habit.
6. End with sources and sibling links, not a sermon.

One unified essay. Target 1,400–2,200 words of body text. Frontmatter and the Sources section do not count toward the band.

## Names and spelling

American spelling. Company names as they appear on letterhead of the hour: Bradley Lumber Company of Arkansas; Southern Lumber Company; Arkansas Lumber Company; Warren & Ouachita Valley; Potlatch Forests, Inc. (later Potlatch). Shortleaf pine, southern yellow pine — do not collapse them into “pine” on first mention in a woods essay.

Warren’s naming: both traditions stay in the sentence (Warren Bradley; Edward Allen Warren). The 2015 courthouse time capsule is evidence, not a gavel.

## Numbers

Board feet, employees, census counts, and death tolls need a year and a source. “About” is allowed when the paper used it. Do not round a census into a mood.
