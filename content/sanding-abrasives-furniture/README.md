# Sanding and abrasives for furniture

These are **staged drafts**. They are not published. A human still has
to mark a piece live. Read them in stage order if you want a syllabus.
Jump if you already know why the last grit failed.

This is shop writing. The mineral science is here because a grit number
on a box is not a surface. The practice is here because a mineral that
never meets raking light is a catalog.

## What this series is

Six stages of furniture-shop sanding. Not a product grid. Not a
"beginner to pro" funnel.

1. **Foundation** — what a scratch is, which scale the box used, when
   to start, how to see, and the fence this pack will not cross.
2. **Minerals** — garnet, aluminum oxide, silicon carbide, ceramic and
   zirconia, stearate, mesh, nonwoven, steel wool. What each one *does*
   to wood, not what the aisle says it is for.
3. **Machines and hands** — random-orbit, belts, wide-belts, pads,
   hook-and-loop, cork, scraper, profiles. The tool is a scratch
   pattern. You own the pattern.
4. **Surfaces** — veneer, end grain, edges, carving, plywood, old
   paint, glue, the two woods hiding in one softwood board.
5. **Into the finish** — last grit, water pop, between coats, wet
   sanding a film, rubbing out, stain as a photograph, cross-grain,
   the 400-grit blotch.
6. **Shop** — lungs, loaded paper, heat, a table schedule, the client
   who wants "buttery," and the last inspection.

## Shop-safe fence

This series stays inside **legal furniture-shop practice**.

It will talk about:

- how commercial abrasives behave on furniture wood
- grit scales, loading, pads, and the ordinary machines
- hardwood dust, shop air, and why a "quick" hand sand still wants a
  respirator
- combustible dust as a housekeeping problem, not a chemistry project
- old paint as a test-first problem
- why you stop short of sanding a mystery film into household air

It will **not** teach:

- how to manufacture, coat, or "improve" abrasive minerals
- how to defeat a dust rule, a lead rule, or a waste rule
- how to dry-sand lead or unknown old paint "carefully"
- how to improvise blasting, grinding, or any process that is not
  furniture sanding
- brand worship as a substitute for a scratch you can see

If a draft names a serious class (lead paint, blast media, floor
edgers used as furniture tools), it names the class and the reason a
furniture shop leaves it alone or hires the person with the setup.

The fence in full is `drafts/08-the-shop-safe-fence.md`. Later pieces
point back instead of preaching.

sentence could sit on the back of a big-box sleeve, it does not belong
here. A claim without a shop consequence is cut.
