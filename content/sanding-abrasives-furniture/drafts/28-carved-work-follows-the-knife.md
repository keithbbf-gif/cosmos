# Carved work follows the knife

The hollows were still fuzzy and the ridges were shiny. I had
used a pad that understood flats. In a carving, a flat pad
flats the high and leaves the low. The knife cut a landscape
of small decisions. Sanding is not a second carver unless you
mean to be one.

Your job is fuzz, tool marks you chose to take, and a tooth
the finish can live on. Not a new model of the carving.

## Tools that fit

Rolled paper on a stick. A dowel. A riffler if the mark is a
ridge. A nonwoven pad after the grit, poked into a cove,
not ground into a ridge. Detail sanders if the pad is smaller
than the feature. A ROS if you hate the carving and want it
gone — which is a valid job if you say it.

Do not put a 5-inch disc on a pierced apron and call it
blending.

## Sequence

Carvings often come off the knife cleaner than a wide-belt
top. Start finer. 150 or 180 is common. 80 is how you lose
the crisp inside corner and then spend a finish schedule
trying to make "soft antique" look like a choice.

Inside corners collect dust and leftover coarse grit. That
grit becomes the next tooth when you wipe finish through it.
Vacuum with a brush. A toothbrush is a shop tool.

## Fuzz versus cut

Some woods fuzz in hollows no matter what. Water pop (draft
34) on a carving is a commitment: everything will raise, and
you will have to get back into the hollows. I do it when the
finish is waterborne and the carving will be touched. I skip
it when the carving is oil and I am willing to live with a
little beard after the first coat, then knock it.

A scraper in a carving is a specialist move. A knife is often
better. Paper is for the surfaces the knife already finished.

## Stain

Hollows go dark. Ridges go light if you burnished them. That
map can be pretty. It can also look dirty. Even tooth, even
pressure, even last grit. Then accept that geometry still
changes color. If the client wanted a carving to stain like
a flat, the client wanted a flat.

## Shop consequence

Look at the knife work before you sand. If you cannot see
the original section after you sand, you carved. Put the
paper down.

Draft 24 was the bead. This draft is the bead's cousins in
three dimensions. Same law: keep the shape you were given.
