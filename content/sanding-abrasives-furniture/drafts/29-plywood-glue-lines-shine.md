# Plywood glue lines shine

The shelf looked like a top until 180, when the glue lines
came up like silk threads and the face started to look thin.
Plywood and MDF are wood plus glue. The paper loads. The glue
line shines. A hardwood face on plywood is still veneer (draft
25) wearing a structural costume.

Sand them like composites, because they are.

## Plywood

The face is thin. The rule is the veneer rule: start finer
than your pride, stop sooner than your anxiety. The core
will announce itself as a pale or dark crescent, or as a
void you cannot un-void.

Glue lines between plies show when you cut through a face
or when you polish the edge of a panel. Edge-sanding plywood
is how you make a striped sandwich more striped. If the edge
will show, tape, use a fine grit, and consider an edge band
as the real answer, not a finer disc.

Interior plies can be voids and patches. A ROS that dishes
will find them. Then you are filling a hole you made.

## MDF

MDF is glue and fiber. It loads paper. It burns. It makes
a dust that is not a rustic smell — fence (draft 08),
respirator, extraction. The cut feels buttery and then the
sheet is a cake.

Edges of MDF drink finish and swell if you get them wet.
Sand them fine and seal them on purpose. Do not water-pop
MDF like oak. You will grow a fuzzy muffin.

Paint-grade MDF wants a fine last grit and a real primer
schedule, not 80 to "knock the mill glaze" over the whole
face. Mill glaze on MDF is a thing; cut it with the grit
that cuts it, then get finer. A coarse pass just makes a
fuzzy face you will prime forever.

## Loading

Glue smears and resin faces want open coat or stearate and
a willingness to throw paper away. A loaded sheet on MDF
is a burnisher that then sheds a clump and gouges. Draft
07 and 42. Here the mineral is often not the problem. The
binder in the board is the problem.

## Baltic and the pretty void

A void under a thin face will telegraph as a soft spot
and then as a hole. Do not chase the soft spot with 80.
You will open it. Fill if the job is paint-grade and the
fill is allowed. On a stained show face, a void is a
conversation or a replacement panel.

I tap plywood faces. A dead knock is a warning. Sanding
is not how you find out you were right.

## Shop consequence

If the piece is plywood, ask where the face stops. If the
piece is MDF, ask whether you should be staining at all.
Stain on MDF is a different sport. Paint is the usual
honest finish.

I use plywood and MDF all week. I do not pretend they
sand like a quarter-sawn oak panel. The shine in the glue
line is the board telling you what it is. Believe it.
