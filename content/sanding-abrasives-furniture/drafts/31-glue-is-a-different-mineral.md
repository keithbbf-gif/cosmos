# Glue is a different mineral

The stain left a pale river along a joint I had "sanded
clean." The river was glue I had polished into the grain.
Paper loves to smear PVA. The smear is invisible until
color. Then it is a map of every clamp.

Glue is a different mineral. It is not wood. It does not
want the same tooth, and it will not take stain like wood.

## When to touch it

**Rubbery.** Many PVA squeezes go through a phase where a
chisel or a scraper lifts a ribbon. That is the cheap
minute. Miss it, and you have glass.

**Hard.** Chip, scrape, a sharp chisel with the grain, not
a 80-grit disc on the whole field. Local. Then return to
the named sequence so the local spot is not a valley.

**Wet.** Wiping wet glue with a damp cloth can drive it
into pores. On oak that is how you stain a ghost. A
barely-damp cloth and an immediate dry, or wait for
rubbery. I have done both wrong.

## Paper is a smearing tool

If you sand glue, you heat it and spread it. Then you
sand more because the surface feels slick, which is the
glue telling you it is still there. Then you stain.

A card scraper (draft 23) is often the first tool. A
chisel is often the first tool. Paper is the tool you
use after the glue is gone, to put the spot back into
the field's scratch.

Hide glue and other systems behave differently. Hide can
chip cleaner. Epoxy is a plastic you will not sand off a
pore with 180 and a hope. This pack will not become a
glue manual. It will say: identify the squeeze, remove
it as itself, then sand wood.

## Inspection

Raking light finds a sheen. A damp wipe (on a test area,
and not as a water-pop of the whole top) can flash a
smear. The honest inspection is often the stain on an
offcut of the same joint. If you have a complicated
base, stain a scrap joint first.

Pencil scribble can skip a slick smear. Light is better.
So is suspicion along every joint you clamped.

## Squeeze under a veneer or a laminate

Glue that squeezed under a face and dried is not a
surface smear. Sanding will thin the face before it
frees the glue. Stop. A thin knife, heat if the glue
system allows it as ordinary shop practice, or live
with a hard line. Do not ROS a bubble hoping it is
proud wood.

On a solid joint, a hairline of glue in a pore line
of oak is enough to print. The scraper gets into the
pore better than 180 does.

## Shop consequence

If the color map follows the clamps, it is glue until
proven figure. Do not "blend with a darker stain." Remove
and recut, or live with the river and say so.

I scrape now before I sand a carcass. The paper last,
the glue first. Reverse that and the mineral you are
cutting is the one you should have thrown away.
