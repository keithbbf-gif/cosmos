# Four-hundred on bare oak is a blotch machine

I do not sand to 400 on bare oak and then wonder why the
stain went blotchy and the wood looks tired. Fine grits
close the surface. Some finishes want that. Some stains
do not.

400 on bare oak is how you burnish hills, leave pores,
and then blame the tree. The pores were never going to
400. The latewood took a polish. The earlywood still
drinks. You have invented contrast and called it a
higher standard.

## What "closed" means

A very fine last grit, especially with a dull mineral or
heat, burnishes. Burnish is a shine without a film. Stain
sits on it or refuses it. Oil looks like plastic on maple
sooner. Waterborne may wet out poorly.

Oak's pores will still be holes. You cannot 400 a pore
shut with furniture paper in a polite schedule. You can
400 the fiber around it into a slick rim. Then the pore
is a dark pit in a shiny field — a look some people want
in a filled finish, and a look that surprises people who
wanted "even."

## Maple, cherry, pine

Maple blotches because the wood is already a map of
density. Extra-fine bare sanding makes the map louder
under stain. Cherry can go muddy and slick. Pine's
latewood refuses while the earlywood drinks (draft 32).

If you want a high last grit on these, you often want
it under a film that does not rely on even stain
absorption — or you want a dye plus a plan, or a washcoat,
or paint.

## When high grits belong on wood

A last 220 or 240 on maple under a clear film, if the
mineral is still cutting. A careful 320 on end grain
(draft 26) sometimes. A scraped surface you are unifying.
Not a personality called "I always go to 400."

400 belongs on films (draft 35, 37) more often than on
bare furniture wood.

## The dull-fine trap

A worn 220 is already a 400-adjacent burnisher. People
then add a real 400 and double the close. Change paper
so the last named grit is an actual cut. If you want
closed, say closed and test the can. If you want even
color, stop sooner.

## Filled finishes are a different last grit

If you are going to fill oak pores and build a film, a
higher last grit on the *fiber* can make sense so the
fill is not sitting in a 120 photograph. That is still
not 400 as a personality. It is a fill schedule. Do
the fill as the fill product says. Do not 400 the bare
board and then skip the fill and wonder why the pores
are pits.

A filled look without filler is just burnish plus hope.

## Shop consequence

If blotch appeared after a "better" sanding, ask whether
better meant finer. Finer is not a moral good. It is a
binder-and-color decision.

I have a maple sample that looks like a piebald pony
because I was showing off a 400. I keep it. It is cheaper
than a second dining top.
