# A cork block is not a personality

People talk about hand sanding like it is a moral upgrade. It
is a backup hardness decision. Cork, felt, rubber, and the bare
hand write three different flats. A block is not virtue. It is
how you keep a field from becoming a set of finger dishes.

I hand-sand because the ROS rounded an edge, or because the last
pass wants the grain, or because a profile would die under a
pad. I do not hand-sand to feel closer to the wood. I have felt
plenty close after a bad dish.

## Hardness

**A hard block** (cork is medium-hard, a felt-faced block is
kinder, a wood block is meaner) keeps the paper flat. It cuts
highs. It skips lows. That is how you flatten. It is also how
you miss a valley and polish the hills — the skip problem in
miniature.

**A soft backup** (foam, the palm) follows. It sands highs and
lows. It is how you keep a gentle curve. It is how you round an
arris you wanted, and how you dish a field you did not.

**Fingers alone** are a set of small soft pads. They write
ripples. On a carving that can be right. On a tabletop it is a
washboard waiting for stain.

Match the backup to the geometry you want to keep. Flat field:
block. Shallow scoop: foam or a shaped block. Bead: fold, or a
dowel, or a profile pad — draft 24.

## The fold

Paper folded into a sharp wad scratches with the fold, not with
the grit. Fold once, over a block, or tear a piece that sits
flat. SiC will crack on a hard fold (draft 11). AO will just
make a ridge.

A quarter-sheet on a cork block is most of hand work. The other
sizes are for corners and lies.

## Edges, again

Hand the edges. Same last grit, lighter pressure. A rolled edge
that is 80 under a 220 field will take stain like a marker. The
block can hang off an edge and round it too. Keep the block's
face on the field; use a folded piece on the arris.

## Shop consequence

Keep a cork block, a softer block, and the honesty to pick one.
If your hand-sanded top has waves in raking light, you were the
machine. The ROS at least writes a known swirl. Fingers write
your pulse.

This is not an argument against machines. It is an argument
against using the palm as a platen on a show face and calling
it craft.
