# Dust in the hooks kills the disc

The disc crawled. Then it flew. Then I found the pad looking
like a gray felt, hooks packed with dust until they were not
hooks. The disc had been sitting on a mattress of grit, spinning
a little, orbiting a little, heating a lot. Hook-and-loop dies
from dust in the hooks.

This is not a brand defect. It is the ordinary end of a pad you
never cleaned.

## What packed hooks do

The disc does not sit flat. One sector stands tall and writes
deeper. Another sector slips and burnishes. You get swirl and a
comet from the same pass. You also get a disc that will not stay
on, which people fix by pressing harder, which packs the hooks
more.

Clean the hooks. A pad-cleaning stick, a stiff brush, a dedicated
tool — not a screw-driver you use to gouge the foam. When the
hooks are shiny-bald, replace the pad. A bald pad is a PSA
without the adhesive.

## PSA

Pressure-sensitive adhesive discs sit flatter when they sit, and
they throw themselves across the shop when they do not. Heat
kills adhesive. Dust on the pad kills adhesive. A residue layer
from the last disc makes the next one a rumor.

I use PSA on a hard backup when I want a flat, honest cut and I
am willing to manage the stick. I use hook-and-loop when I am
changing grits every few minutes, which is most furniture work.
I do not mix residue. If a pad has been PSA, it is not a hook
pad anymore.

## Cheap discs, cheap hooks

A disc with lazy loop will pill and leave fuzz in the finish.
A pad with lazy hooks will never hold a mesh disc that cost
real money. Pay for the pad. The discs can be ordinary AO if
the pad is alive. The reverse is a waste of good mineral.

## Shop consequence

Once a week, or whenever a disc crawls, look at the hooks.
If you cannot see them, they are not working. This takes a
minute. It saves a recut.

Draft 20 was holes. This draft is the other face of the pad.
Together they are why a "new disc" can still sand like a
dead one. The disc was never the only tool.
