# Silicon carbide is sharper and more brittle

The black wet-dry sheet cut the varnish like it had a grudge and
then it tore. I wanted aluminum oxide toughness. I had bought a
different tooth. Silicon carbide is sharper and more brittle. It
fractures into new edges. It stays mean, and it dies in pieces
instead of politely rounding over.

That is why it belongs on films, on wet work, and on hard finishes.
It is why it is a poor hogging mineral on a dining top.

## What the fracture is for

A film is harder than wood in the way that matters to a tooth.
AO will skate and heat. SiC will bite, make a slurry if you wet
it, and keep presenting a new edge as the old one breaks. Between
coats, on a cured-enough varnish or poly, that bite takes nibs
without requiring you to lean.

On bare wood, that same bite can be too much of a knife in the
fine grits, or it can be fine if the sheet is honest and you are
not pressing. I use SiC on bare wood when that is what I have, and
I watch for a scratch that looks a little too crisp for the
number. I do not go buy SiC to flatten oak.

Wet-dry is often SiC. Wet-sanding a *film* is draft 36. Wet-sanding
*bare furniture wood* as a habit is how you raise grain, drive
grit into pores, and swell a panel you already had flat. This pack
will not make a water bath on a tabletop sound like refinement.

## Brittle means it sheds

SiC paper tears. Belts of SiC exist and they are for materials
that want a sharp tooth, not for a three-hour wide-belt shift on
hardwood if the shop has ceramic. On a hand sheet, brittle means
you fold it and it cracks on the fold. Fold once, not into a
sharp wad. Use a block when the sheet wants to split.

A torn SiC corner is a gouge waiting. Throw the scrap away. Do
not "use up" a triangle that has a hard point.

## Color

Black paper is often SiC and sometimes just black. Read the
mineral. Wet-dry packaging is a hint, not a lab result.

## Wet-dry on a dry film

You can use SiC wet-dry *dry* for nibs. It loads faster
than a stearated finish paper. That is fine for a short
pass. It is not fine for a whole tabletop dry if you
wanted the stearate's release. Pick the helper (draft
13) and the mineral separately.

A torn wet-dry corner under a block is a gouge with a
black edge. Tear a clean rectangle. Throw the triangles
away. I have kept a triangle and paid.

## Shop consequence

Keep SiC for: between-coat nibs, rubbing sequences that call for
it, wet work on a film you meant to wet, and some metals and
plastics that are not this pack. Keep AO for the wood field.
Do not sand a whole carcass with wet-dry SiC because the sleeve
said "fine finish." Fine is a number plus a mineral plus a
surface. The sleeve only had room for a number.
