# Keep the bead you were given

The apron came back from the ROS looking friendly and wrong. The
bead was a rumor. The flats were wider. The knife had cut a
shape. I had applied a brick. Millwork dies under a flat pad.

Your job on a profile is to keep the shape and take the fuzz.
Not to improve the designer. Not to "blend."

## Tools that follow

Folded paper, sized to the cove or the bead. A length of dowel
or a shaped block wrapped with paper. A profile sander if you
have one and the pads match the knife, not "close enough."
Nonwoven pads after the grit work, not instead (draft 15).

A hard flat block on a bead is how you make two new flats and
a ghost of a radius. A ROS on a molding is how you make
sausages.

## Sequence still exists

You still erase the last grit. You just do it on a curve. A
skip on a bead is a stripe that follows the room around the
table. People notice moldings because that is where hands go
and where light breaks.

Start as coarse as the machining marks, which are often finer
than a wide-belt top. A clean knife may want 150 or 180 first,
not 80. 80 on a bead is how you lose the bead and then pretend
you meant a softer style.

## End grain in the miter

Profile frames have miters. Miters have end grain. End grain
scratches deeper and drinks (draft 26). Hand those joints. Do
not spin a disc across a miter and then wonder why the corners
went dark under stain.

## Paint-grade versus show

A painted molding can take more rounding and still read as the
profile. A stained hardwood bead cannot. Distinguish the job.
If it is paint-grade, you can be a little kinder to yourself
and a little meaner to the arris. If it is show hardwood, keep
the knife's drawing.

## Machine marks versus fuzz

A dirty knife leaves ridges you can see. Those want a grit
that cuts, then the sequence. Fuzz is raised fiber. Fuzz
wants a light last grit and maybe a pop. If you treat knife
ridges as fuzz, you polish the ridges and keep the valleys.
The finish will stripe along the profile like a barber pole.

Abrasive-planed moldings from a mill can arrive already
burnished. Cut enough to take the mill shine, not enough
to redraw the section. That is a short, honest 150, then
on, not an afternoon of 80.

## Shop consequence

Before you sand a profile, look at the knife's section. After
you sand, look again, in raking light, along the length. If the
highlights have moved to new flats, you flattened a curve.

I tape the flats next to a delicate bead when I have to machine
the field. The tape is not precious. The bead is. A dead bead
is a painter's problem I created, even when there is no paint.
