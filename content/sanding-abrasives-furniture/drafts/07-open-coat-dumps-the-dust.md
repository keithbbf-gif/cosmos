# Open coat dumps the dust

The sheet went slick in a minute. Pine, 120, closed coat, because
that was what was in the drawer. It started cutting. Then it
started shining. Then a clump let go and I had a 60-grit comet
across a tabletop I had already called almost done.

Open-coat paper spaces the mineral so dust can fall out. Closed-coat
packs the mineral for a finer, more even cut — and then polishes
its own waste on pine, paint, and resinous faces. The coat is a
loading decision, not a quality grade.

## What the words mean

**Closed coat** means the backing is crowded with mineral. More
teeth per inch. A more uniform scratch when the teeth stay clean.
A faster load when the wood is soft, gummy, painted, or pine with
ideas.

**Open coat** means the maker left room. Fewer teeth. Dust has a
place to go. The cut is a little less even and a lot less likely
to die in its own waste. Softwoods, resins, old finish you have
already tested and been allowed to cut, and any wood that smells
like it will load — start open.

**Stearated** paper (draft 13) is a third move: a dry lubricant on
the mineral so waste releases. It is not the same as open coat.
You can have closed-coat stearated finish paper. You can have
open-coat belts with no stearate. Read both claims.

## Furniture consequences

A dining top in oak often forgives closed coat in the middle grits
if your extraction is real and you clean the disc. A pine apron
will not. Neither will cherry that is already heating. Neither
will a painted dresser, even after a lead test has told you the
job is a different job.

People buy closed coat because it looks "finer" on the sleeve
photo. It looks finer because there is more mineral in the
picture. That is not a finer grit. That is a denser traffic jam.

When a sheet loads, the grit number becomes a rumor. The loaded
face is finer than the number — a burnisher — until a chunk
releases and it is suddenly coarser than the number. That is the
comet. Draft 42 is the comet in full. This draft is why you chose
the sheet that made the comet cheap to avoid.

## Mesh and holes

Mesh and net discs are open coat taken seriously: the backing is
mostly hole. They still load if you sand paint or pine without
extraction. They just load later and they dump better when you
knock them. They are not magic. They are a coat decision with
better plumbing.

If your ROS has no vacuum, open coat and a knock-out habit will
save more tops than a premium mineral on a closed disc. The
mineral cannot cut if it is standing on a cake of dust.

## The drawer

I keep open coat in the coarse and middle bins for softwood and
for any stripping-adjacent work that has already cleared the
fence. I keep closed or stearated in the fine bins for hardwood
show faces and for between-coat work on a film. I do not keep
one "120" and pretend coat does not exist.

If the sheet shines before it cuts, you are already late. Change
it. The paper is cheaper than the recut. It always is.
