# Random-orbit is a swirl factory

I love the random-orbit. I also have a table in a house whose
top looks like a pond with a wind on it — little commas, the
orbit I lingered in while I looked at a phone. The machine is a
gift and a swirl factory. Both sentences are true.

The pad spins and orbits so the scratch should not repeat. "Should
not" is a design goal. A worn pad, a tipped edge, a pause, a
broken hole pattern, and you have a repeating story the finish
will tell.

## How it writes

Move. Keep it flat. Let the pad do the pressure. If you lean,
the edge of the disc becomes a finer, meaner tool and a rounding
tool. If you stop, the orbit writes a circle in one neighborhood
and that neighborhood becomes a shallow dish. You will see the
dish at dinner. You will not feel it at noon.

Speed: faster is not always finer. Faster is hotter and jumpy.
I use the speed that keeps the pad planted and the dust moving.
I do not use the speed that makes me feel like the job is
serious.

Grit changes still apply. The ROS does not erase a skip because
it is random. Random is a pattern class, not a solvent.

## Edges

The ROS loves an edge. It will round a crisp arris in the time
it takes to look at a phone. I tape, or I sand edges by hand, or
I accept a softer edge because the design wanted it. I do not
accept a softer edge because I was thinking about the radio.

If the pad hangs off the top, you are rounding and you are
loading the disc on one crescent. That crescent scratches
deeper. Then you flatten the field to match and you have a
thinner top.

## The last pass

On a show top I want a last pass with the grain after the ROS
has done the field. Hand, or a guided stroke, same last grit.
The leftover scratch then runs the way wood already runs. People
skip this because the ROS already "looks done." The four-o'clock
sun is the editor of that sentence.

Painted parts and insides can end on the machine. Distinguish
the surface that will be inspected from the surface that will
hold a drawer.

## Pads and holes

A dead pad (foam collapsed, hooks gone) writes a new machine.
Replace it. Holes that do not match the disc (draft 20) write
dust cakes and local heat. This draft assumes a living pad. Most
swirl complaints I have believed were a dead pad I did not want
to spend money on.

## Brakes and "the pad stopped"

Some machines brake the pad when you lift. Some spin down
on the wood if you lift slow. Lift like you mean it. A
spin-down comma at the end of a pass is a swirl with a
signature. I have a top with a family of those commas
along the far edge, where I always finish a stroke.

Start and stop off the work when you can. On a large top
you cannot always. Then lift clean.

## Shop consequence

Use the ROS for the field. Hand the edges. Grain-pass the show
face. Do not linger. If you need to stop, lift. If you need to
look at a phone, lift first.

The machine is not the villain. The pause is. The tip is. The
skip is. The ROS just makes those three photogenic.
