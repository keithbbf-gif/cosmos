# Ceramic and zirconia when the belt has a job

The stroke-sander belt was still cutting when a cheaper AO would
have been a shiny joke. It was also still a belt. I took a trough
out of a top once because I believed a premium mineral made the
machine polite. The mineral was doing its job. I had given it the
wrong job.

Ceramic and alumina-zirconia are removal minerals. They keep
fracturing into sharp edges under pressure and heat. They belong
on a belt that has to eat wood — or a lot of finish you have
already been allowed to cut — without glazing. They do not belong
on a last pass you are trying to make polite.

## What "keeps cutting" costs

A mineral that keeps cutting will keep cutting if you linger. A
portable belt sander with a ceramic belt is a plane with a grudge.
It will dish a field while you answer a phone. AO at least slows
down and starts to burnish, which is its own problem (draft 10)
but it gives you a dull warning. Ceramic gives you less warning.

Zirconia (alumina-zirconia) sits in the same family for shop
purposes: tough, good on belts, happy under pressure. I will not
run a dissertation on crystal phases at the bench. I will say:
if the sleeve is proud of how long the belt lasts on heavy
grinding, it is not your 220 show-face disc.

## Furniture use

**Stroke sander / wide-belt coarse and middle** when the shop has
those machines and the capture those machines assume (draft 08).
The mineral is why production can hold a grit without changing
belts every panel.

**Portable belt** on a thickness or glue problem, with a fence,
a jig, or a lot of humility. Then get off the belt and back to
a sequence a ROS and a hand can erase.

**Not** a last orbital pass on a dining top. Not a veneer. Not
an edge you still want to exist.

## Heat

These minerals are often run harder. Harder means heat. Heat
means burnish and burn. Maple will tell you first. So will a
belt that smells like a toasted finish. If you need that much
heat to keep cutting, you are removing too much in one stay, or
the belt is loaded and you are polishing waste.

## Shop consequence

Buy ceramic or zirconia when the job is removal and the tool is
a belt. Buy something kinder when the job is a photograph you
will oil. Do not let a long-life belt talk you into staying on
the machine because the belt "isn't worn yet." Worn is the wrong
metric. The metric is whether the surface still wants that tooth.

A glazed ceramic belt is still a burnisher (draft 19). Premium
mineral does not exempt you from glaze. It just takes longer to
get there, which is more time to trust it while it lies.
