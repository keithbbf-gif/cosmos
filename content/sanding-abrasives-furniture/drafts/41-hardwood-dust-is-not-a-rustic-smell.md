# Hardwood dust is not a rustic smell

I still forget the respirator on a "quick" hand sand, and
I am wrong when I forget. Oak dust is not a rustic smell.
It is a lung problem. Sanding is the quiet injury.

This draft is the shop voice of the fence (draft 08). The
fence said we will remind you. Here is the reminder without
a second sermon about things this pack will not teach.

## What I actually do

A respirator I will wear — meaning it fits, the cartridges
or filters match wood dust, and it is not in a drawer under
a pile of clamps. Extraction hooked to the ROS and the belt.
A bag I empty before it is a brick. A broom at the end, not
instead.

I do not blow the shop into a cloud with compressed air
and call it clean. That cloud is the lung's next hour and
the finish table's next week of nibs.

Hardwoods, especially as a daily habit, are the ones the
health people have been boring about for years. Softwood
dust is not a free pass. MDF dust is glue-and-fiber dust
(draft 29). Old-paint dust is a stop (draft 30).

## The quick sand

The quick sand is the one that does not feel like a job.
It is the one that writes the habit. I put the respirator
on for the quick sand or I admit I am bargaining.

A paper mask in the bottom of a drawer is not a respirator.
A respirator with a cracked seal is a necklace.

## Combustible dust, ordinary

Fine dust on motors and the water heater is a housekeeping
problem. Vacuum with a system meant for wood dust. Do not
write a chemistry project. Do not store a shop vac full of
flour-fine dust next to a spark habit. Empty it.

This is enough fire for one draft. Oil rags are a different
ordinary fire (finishing-chemistry pack). Do not mix the
two into a pile in a plastic bin.

## Finish quality is not the point, and also it is

Dust on the surface becomes a release coat and a nib farm.
Clean benches make cleaner films. That is true. It is not
why this draft exists. This draft exists because a clean
film on a sick lung is a stupid trade.

## Sanding next to the finish table

Dust that does not go in your lung still goes in the
next coat. A shop that sands and finishes in the same
hour owns nibs. Sequence the day: cut, clean, wait,
coat. I am not going to pretend I have two rooms. I
will say the broom and the hour matter.

Oak dust on a wet oil cloth is a mud that stains a
fingerprint into the top. I blow down, I tack, I wash
my hands. The fingerprint is always mine.

## Shop consequence

If the shop cannot extract, change the tool or the schedule
or the location. A dining top can be sanded on a day you
set up capture. It cannot be unsand-breathed.

I wear the respirator when I am on a machine, and I still
forget on a quick hand sand, and I am wrong when I forget.
The safety essay said it as a fence. Here: grit discipline
includes not breathing the grit.
