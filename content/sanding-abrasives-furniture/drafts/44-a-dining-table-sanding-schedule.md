# A dining-table sanding schedule

A table is where every argument in this pack gets asked
to be a clock. Here are two schedules I will actually
run, staged so you can see the grit changes and the
inspections. Not because they are the only tables,
because they are the common arguments.

## A. The oil or hardwax top (oak or walnut, solid)

**Stage 0.** Name the defect. Planed clean? Start 120.
Wide-belt 80 still visible? Start 80 or 100. Veneer?
Stop and go to a different piece; this schedule assumes
solid.

**Stage 1.** First grit, ROS on the field, holes lined
up, respirator on. Pencil. Raking light. Edges taped or
ignored by the machine.

**Stage 2.** Next grit that can erase the last. No skip
because the box is empty — buy the box. Pencil again at
the coarse-to-middle change.

**Stage 3.** 150 or 180, still erasing. Hand the edges,
same grit. Breadboards and ends as their own job, finer
if they drink.

**Stage 4.** Last grit 180 or 220 for this binder (draft
33). Hand, with the grain, show face. Garnet if I want
a dying cut. Raking light. Thumb overruled.

**Stage 5.** Vacuum. If the species wants a pop and the
finish is water-anything, pop and knock. If it is oil
and I want tooth, skip the pop on purpose.

**Stage 6.** Glue-smear hunt along every joint. Scrape
what paper would smear. Last look across, not down.

Then the can is a different pack. The sanding schedule
ends when the photograph is one you will oil.

## B. The pale waterborne top (maple or a mustard-using
family oak)

**Stage 0–3.** Same as A, but I am more afraid of
burnish and heat. Fresh AO. No lingering. Maple tells.

**Stage 4.** Last grit that still *cuts*. Not 400. Not
a dull 220. Water pop. Knock whiskers only.

**Stage 5.** Non-stearated last bare pass if the
waterborne has been fussy on a test (draft 13). Clean
as if the lubricant were a contaminant, because it
might be.

**Stage 6.** Between coats later: nibs, not flattening,
edges alive (draft 35). This schedule still owns that
future hour by not leaving dishes that I will be
tempted to "level in the film."

## Shared refusals

No belt on the show face unless I can say why. No 80
to 220. No mystery paint. No steel wool before
waterborne. No phone on the pad.

If Friday cannot hold Stage 4's inspection, Friday
cannot hold a show top. Paint the base. Tell the
truth about the top.

## The door

I write the sequence on the inside of a shop door, or
on a tape on the apron: `80–120–150–180 | ends 220 |
pop? | edges hand`. When I skip, I can see the skip
as a missing number, not as a feeling.

The board is still the critic. The schedule is how I
do not invent a new sequence every time I am hungry.
