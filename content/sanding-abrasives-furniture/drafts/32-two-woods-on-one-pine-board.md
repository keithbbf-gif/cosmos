# Two woods on one pine board

The stain made a tiger. The tiger was already there, but I
had helped. Pine earlywood is a cushion. Latewood is a rail.
Paper dishes the soft and polishes the hard. Then color
writes the difference in a louder ink.

Softwoods are two woods on one board. Cherry and maple have
moods. Pine has a built-in hardness map.

## What the paper does

A soft backup and a fine grit will sand the earlywood into
valleys and skate the latewood. A hard block and a grit
that actually cuts will still prefer the soft, but less.
A dull disc will burnish the latewood and leave the early
fuzzy.

I use open coat (draft 07) because pine loads. I use a
block on flats I want to stay flats. I do not linger with
a ROS on a pine tabletop unless I like shallow waves.

## Last grit

Pine looks polished and then drinks stain in the earlywood
anyway. A very fine last grit can make the latewood refuse
color while the earlywood still drinks — a harsher tiger.
Sometimes a slightly coarser last grit, even tooth, and a
washcoat is more honest than 320 on bare pine.

If the finish is paint, the tiger is less of a color
problem and more of a grain-telegraph problem. Fill or
accept. Do not 400 the bare pine and expect paint to
behave like enamel on metal.

## Resin

Pine gums. Heat makes it worse (draft 43). Loaded paper
makes a comet (draft 42). Work cooler, change paper, do
not "power through" a slick spot. The slick spot is resin
and it will print in the finish as a resist.

## Other softwoods

Fir, spruce, the construction cousins — same two-wood
problem, sometimes worse. Cedar is its own oil. This draft
is pine because that is the furniture softwood people
sand like oak and then blame.

## Shop consequence

If you want a quiet pine surface, you are fighting the
tree and the paper. A washcoat, a dye strategy, paint, or
a harder species may be the real grit. If you want the
tiger, do not dish it into a cartoon with a soft pad.

Inspect in raking light before color. The valleys are
already there. Stain only turns up the volume.
