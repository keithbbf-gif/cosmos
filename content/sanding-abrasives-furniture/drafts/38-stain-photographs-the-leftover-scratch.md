# Stain photographs the leftover scratch

I have wiped stain on a top I would have sworn was ready
and watched 80-grit arcs appear like someone had developed
a print. Stain is a photographer with a mean flash.
Leftover coarse scratches become ink. A skip you could not
see at noon becomes the only thing a guest can see.

Color does not hide sanding. Color reports it.

## Why

Pigment lodges in valleys. Dye wets whatever is open.
A coarse valley holds more color. A burnished hill holds
less. The pattern you left is now a contrast pattern.
Oil-based stains and water dyes are different reporters.
Both report.

If you wanted even color, you wanted even tooth, not a
finer average with leftover trenches.

## Before you stain

Raking light. Pencil. A wipe of mineral spirits or a
compatible reveal on an offcut — some shops use a wet-out
to preview. On the real top, a reveal solvent is a
decision about what you will drive into the wood. I
prefer the light and an offcut stained first.

If the offcut shows the skip, the top has the skip. Do
not hope the larger surface is more forgiving. It is
more visible.

## After you stain, if the photograph is ugly

Sometimes you wipe off and recut. Sometimes the stain
has already done its job and you are in a strip or a
live-with-it. This pack will not turn into a stripping
manual. It will say: the cheap time to find the scratch
was before the rag.

A second coat of stain will not erase a scratch
photograph. It will often make it louder.

## Gel stains and "forgiving" products

They can mute a little. They can also sit on a burnished
surface and look like paint in one place and wood in
another. Forgiving is a marketing word. Even tooth still
wins.

## Swirl versus figure

People will call a leftover ROS comma "figure" if the
species is oak. Figure follows the tree. A comma follows
the pad. In raking light, figure does not spin. A swirl
does. If you are arguing with a client about whether the
stripe is ray fleck or 80 grit, get the light low. Ray
fleck does not come in 5-inch orbits.

End grain will photograph deeper (draft 26). A breadboard
that went dark is not always a skip; it is often just
end grain being end grain. A breadboard with *arcs* in
the dark is a skip plus end grain. Distinguish before
you recut a joint you only needed to treat as a second
sequence.

## Shop consequence

If the job is stained, the last raking pass is not
optional. If the job is painted, you have more hiding
budget and still not a license to skip on a show face
that might get hit and show primer in a valley.

Draft 01 said the scratch is already the finish. This
draft is the day you cannot pretend otherwise. The
photographer does not take retakes without a recut.
