# Veneer is a skin

The crescent of pale substrate showed up first at the edge, then
in a low spot I had been "just blending." Veneer is a skin. 80
grit is a hole. Commercial faces are thinner than the furniture
myth, and a ROS that hangs off an edge is how you meet the
core.

This draft is one of the four I would keep if the set were cut.
Sand-through is not a style. It is a hole in a photograph you
cannot reprint.

## Thickness is a rumor

Old hide-glued work can be thick enough to forgive a mistake.
A lot of modern faces will not. I do not invent a millimeter
count at the bench `[VERIFY]` for a given supplier. I assume
little, I start fine enough, and I inspect the edges like they
are the whole job.

If you can see a joint in the core through a thin face, you
are already late. Stop. Do not "feather." Feathering is how
the hole grows.

## First grit

A clean, factory-sanded veneer top often wants 150 or 180
first, not 80, not 100 unless there is a real defect and a
local block. Defects on veneer are often better scraped very
lightly or left, because the alternative is a valley that
prints the core.

If the factory burnished it (draft 19), you still may need to
*cut*, but cut with a sharp 150 and a light ROS, not a belt.
A belt on veneer is a personality I do not allow in the shop.

## Edges and details

Sand-through loves edges, breadboards that are actually a
face wrapping, bookmatched seams, and any place the face
already ran out. Hand the edges. Tape if you must. Do not
let the pad hang.

Inlays and banding: the banding is often a different hardness
and a different thickness. The paper will cut the soft and
skate the hard. You will dish the soft. Then stain will make
a map.

## Repair

A sand-through that is already there is a patch, a color,
or an honest conversation. It is not more sanding. More
sanding is how you frame the hole.

If you are flattening a veneered panel that cupped, ask
whether you should be sanding at all. You may be thinning
the face on the high side and opening the low side. Moisture
and clamps are sometimes the real grit.

## Shop consequence

Start finer than your pride. Stop sooner than your anxiety.
If you see core, you are done cutting. The rest of this pack
assumes solid wood unless it says veneer. When it says veneer,
the thickness budget wins every argument including "but the
scratch is still there." A leftover 180 scratch on veneer is
better than a honest core.
