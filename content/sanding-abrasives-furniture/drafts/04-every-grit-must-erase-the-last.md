# Every grit must erase the last

Someone jumped to 180. I have been that someone. The 120 slot was
empty. The top felt smooth. A thumb is not raking light. At four
o'clock the 80-grit arcs were still there, filled with finish,
shinier than the wood around them.

Every grit must erase the previous. That is boring and it is the
whole skill.

## What a skip actually leaves

80 to 220 without the middle is how you leave 80-grit valleys with
220 polish on the hills. You have not refined the surface. You have
polished the high ground. The finish finds the valleys. People then
sand more at 220 and make shiny bowls in the field, because the
high ground was already done and the hands kept moving.

A skip can also hide in a "half step" that is not a step. 80 to
100 is a step. 80 to 150 is a hope. 120 to 180 is often honest. 120
to 220 is a skip wearing a busy afternoon.

The test is not the numbers. The test is whether the current
mineral has removed the last groove. If it has not, you are not
on the next grit. You are on a burnisher.

## Pencil, then light

Cover the surface with a light graphite scribble. Sand until the
graphite is gone. What remains is a low spot or a scratch you have
not reached. This feels childish until you see the scribble left in
a pore line or a glue joint you would have called done.

Then use raking light. A low LED. Get your eye down. Look across.
The pencil finds the low. The light finds the pattern. You want
both, because a uniform leftover scratch can erase graphite and
still be a photograph waiting for stain.

When the scribble is gone and the previous pattern is gone, change
grits. Not before. Not because the clock said the 120 pass was
"about five minutes."

## With the grain at the end

A random-orbit writes arcs. Those arcs are a pattern. On a show
top I want the last pass to be with the grain, by hand or with a
guided stroke, so the leftover scratch — there is always a leftover
scratch — runs the way the room already expects wood to run.

I do not pretend a last orbital pass at 220 is invisible. I pretend
it is invisible when I am tired. The dining room does not get tired
in the same way.

## Production pressure

A line that has to ship on Friday will skip. The return is more
expensive than the paper. If the schedule cannot hold 120, the
schedule cannot hold a show top. It can hold a painted side.
Distinguish.

If you skip, go back. If you do not have the grit, stop and buy
it. If you sand more at the fine end to "make up" for a skip, you
are carving dishes.

This draft is the one I would keep if an editor cut the set to
four pieces. The rest of the pack is how to make this rule cheaper
to obey.
