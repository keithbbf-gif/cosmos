# Aluminum oxide is the default

Most of the discs in the shop are aluminum oxide and they should
be. It lasts. It cuts hardwoods without dying in a single pass.
It is the mineral you can buy in every town that still has a
hardware aisle.

It also work-hardens. The tooth gets tougher as it works. That is
why it lasts. It is also why a dull AO disc becomes a burnisher
that still says 180 on the back. The number did not change. The
cut did.

## What "default" means

Default means: coarse and middle work on oak, maple, walnut,
ash. ROS discs. Belts when I am not hogging paint or a glue
ridge. The everyday mineral for furniture wood that is actually
wood, not a film and not a resinous pine apron.

Default does not mean: last hand pass when I want a dying cut
(garnet). Finish-sanding a cured film (silicon carbide). A belt
that has to eat a lot of material without glazing (ceramic or
zirconia). Between-coat work where I want a sharp, brittle tooth
and a stearate.

People treat AO as "sandpaper" and everything else as a specialty.
That is backwards. AO is a specialty that happens to cover most
of the day. The other minerals exist because AO is the wrong
specialist there.

## The dull-disc lie

AO will keep moving after it has stopped cutting. The surface
gets slick. Heat comes up. Maple closes. You think you are still
on 180 because the disc is still attached and the vacuum is still
singing. You are on a hard pad with memories.

When the cut slows and the heat rises, change the disc. Do not
press. Pressure is how you turn a tired AO into a burnisher
faster. Draft 43 is the heat. This draft is the mineral that
makes heat cheap if you are stubborn.

## Color and "premium"

Brown, blue, maroon — backing color is a brand costume. Premium
AO is often a better bond, a better grit sort, a better backing.
It is still AO. It will still burnish when dull. I will pay for
a disc that stays on the pad and dumps dust. I will not pay for
a story about "professional grade" that does not change the
mineral.

If a sleeve says ceramic-coated AO or some hybrid sentence, treat
it as a belt mineral until a test board says otherwise. Hybrids
are fine. Unnamed hybrids are a drawer problem.

## "Premium" AO on a cheap pad

A good AO disc on a dead hook-and-loop pad (draft 21) is
still a mattress. Buy the pad. The mineral cannot fix the
platen. I have blamed AO for swirl that was a bald pad
and a tipped edge.

AO also comes in better sorts — tighter grit, better
bond. That matters on a show face at 180. It matters
less at 80 on a repair. Spend the money where the
photograph will live.

## Shop consequence

Stock AO for the field. Inspect for the burnish. Throw dull discs
away without a eulogy. Use a different mineral when the job is
not "cut hardwood in the middle grits."

The default is a gift. It is also how a whole shop sands every
problem with one tooth and then wonders why the finish film
scratched, the pine loaded, and the last oil pass looks like 120.
