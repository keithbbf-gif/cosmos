# Last grit is a binder decision

I last-sand most furniture to 180 or 220 for oil and thin
films, and I do not chase 400 on open-pore oak unless I am
trying to burnish it into blotch. Maple can take a higher
last grit. Pine looks polished and then drinks anyway.

There is no universal last number. There is a last number
for this species and this finish.

## Oil and wiping varnish

They leave the surface you cut. A 120 last sand under oil
is a 120 photograph forever. Sometimes that photograph is
what you want — a worktop, a rustic claim, a piece that
should not look like a piano. Sometimes it is a skip you
are about to call a style.

I treat 180 as a common honest last grit for oil on oak
and walnut. 220 when the client wants quieter and the
species will take it. Garnet on that last hand pass (draft
09) if I want the tooth to die polite.

## Built films

A filled, built polyurethane can start the film earlier.
You pay in plastic. You also get a hiding budget. That
budget is not infinite. Coarse valleys under a glossy skin
are still a stripe in low sun.

I will last-sand 150 or 180 under a film that will be
built and rubbed. I will not last-sand 80 and "let the
film handle it" on a dining top. The film will handle it
by making it shinier.

## Waterborne

Waterborne wants a surface that will not raise into a
beard you cannot cut without cutting the film. Last grit
plus water pop (draft 34) is the pair. A burnished last
grit under waterborne can mean poor wet-out. Cut, don't
polish.

## Stain

Stain wants even tooth more than it wants a magic number.
A blotchy maple after 400 is draft 40. A stripy oak after
a skip is draft 38. If color is coming, the last grit is
a color decision wearing a number.

## Shop consequence

Write the finish on the door next to the grit sequence.
If the finish changes, the last number may change. People
sand every job to 220 because that is the finest sleeve
they keep. Finest is not a binder.

Do one offcut. Same last grit, same can. Look at it in
the light the room has. If you do not like the photograph,
change the grit or change the binder. Do not change both
at once and then not know which one saved you.
