# Garnet dies as it cuts

The sheet was the color of brick dust and it quit sooner than the
blue disc I had just thrown away. I used to call that cheap. Then
I looked at a last hand pass on cherry: the garnet had gone polite
in the last minutes and left a scratch oil could live with. The
aluminum oxide I compared it to was still a little too much of a
knife.

Garnet is a softer mineral. It dulls as it works. That sounds like
a defect. On a last hand pass it is why the scratch gets gentler
instead of staying sharp while you finish the field.

## What it is doing

Garnet fractures and wears. Fresh, it cuts well enough for
furniture wood. Worn, it burnishes more than it gouges. A
production belt cannot afford that. A person on a cork block, on
the last grit before oil, can.

That is the whole argument for keeping garnet in a shop that
otherwise lives on aluminum oxide. Not nostalgia. Not "traditional."
A dying cut on purpose.

It loads on pine like anything else. It is not a magic open coat.
It is not a reason to skip extraction. It is a last-pass mineral
when you want the paper to get kinder as you go, not meaner.

## Where I will not use it

Coarse work. Garnet 80 on a repair is a waste of a soft mineral
and a waste of time. A belt. A ROS that will eat a sheet before
the mineral has a chance to do its dying-cut trick. Between-coat
sanding of a hard film — silicon carbide or a finish paper belongs
there.

I also will not use a worn-out garnet as a "polish." Dead mineral
on a show top is a burnisher. Burnish under stain is blotch.
There is a window where it has softened and still cuts. After
that window, throw it away.

## Color is not a grade

Red-brown paper is often garnet and sometimes just a dyed backing.
Read the mineral line. If the sleeve will not say, treat it as
unknown and do a test board. Unknown mineral is how you mix a
dying cut with a ceramic leftover and then wonder why half the
top is a different scratch.

## Shop consequence

If your last oil pass looks too scratchy, try garnet at the same
number before you chase 320 on bare wood. If your last oil pass
looks burnished and the stain went blotchy, you stayed on the
sheet after it died.

The pack is not a garnet revival. Aluminum oxide still does most
of the work. Garnet is the mineral I keep for the last polite
argument with a show face, by hand, with the grain, when I want
the paper to get tired with me.
