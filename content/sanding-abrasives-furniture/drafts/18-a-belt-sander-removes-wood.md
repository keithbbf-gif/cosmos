# A belt sander removes wood

I have a trough in a memory of a tabletop the shape of a belt.
I believed I was flattening. I was removing. A portable belt
sander is a plane with a grudge. It does not finish furniture.
It removes wood until you take it away from the show face.

That is a legal, ordinary use. It is also how people ruin a top
in a minute and then spend a day trying to sand the valley out
with a ROS, which only widens the valley.

## What it is for

Proud glue. A local hump. A paint film you have already tested
and been allowed to cut, on a surface that can spare the
thickness. A door you are already going to repaint. A thickness
problem you have decided not to take back to the planer.

It is not for a last pass. It is not for veneer. It is not for
an edge you still want. It is not for "getting close" on a
dining top unless you have a jig, a very light belt, and a
religion about keeping the machine moving off the work as much
as on it.

## How the trough happens

You start in the middle because the middle is where you are
looking. The belt cuts most where it sits most. The ends of the
top stay high. You chase. Now you have a shallow bowl with crisp
shoulders. Finish loves the bowl. Light loves the shoulders.

Keep the machine moving. Overlap. Do not hover. Work a pattern
you could draw, then stop and use winding sticks or a straight
edge, not a thumb. If you need more than a few passes, you may
need a different tool — a plane, a thickness planer, a stroke
sander with a real platen.

## Belts

Coarse ceramic on a portable belt is a hog. Fine AO is still a
hog if you stay. Match the mineral to removal (draft 12) and
then get off the tool. A glazed belt will burnish and burn
(draft 19, 43). Change it. Do not press a glazed belt hoping
it will "polish flat."

## Dust

These machines make a lot of it. Hook up extraction. Wear the
respirator. The fence (draft 08) was not theoretical. A belt
sander in a living room is a relationship problem and a lung
problem.

## Cross-belt on a top

Running a portable belt across the grain to "flatten
faster" writes a set of trenches the ROS will spend an
hour pretending to erase. With the grain, moving, or
not at all. Cross-belt is a floor-sander habit. This
pack is furniture.

If the top is already at finished thickness, the belt
is usually the wrong first tool. A plane, a scraper, or
a local block spends less of the budget.

## Shop consequence

If the defect is thickness, ask whether a cutting tool should
have the first say. If you pick the belt, pick it as removal,
then return to a ROS and a hand sequence that can erase the
belt scratch. The belt scratch is coarse even when the number
says it is not, because the machine is heavier than the number.

I keep a belt sander. I do not let it near a finished-thickness
show top without a reason I can say out loud.
