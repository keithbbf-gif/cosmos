# A glazed wide-belt is a burnisher

The panel came off the wide-belt shining like it had already
been oiled. The ticket said 180. The belt had been 180 last
week. This week it was a burnisher wearing a number. The stain
went blotchy and the foreman blamed the wood.

A production sequence is a grit plus a brand plus a wear state.
Trust it only if the belts are not glazed and the platen is not
grooved. A small shop without a wide-belt still inherits this
problem: leftover belts on a stroke sander, a drum, a lunchbox
planer-sander, any machine that keeps running after the mineral
has become a shiny skin.

## What glaze is

Waste and heat pack the teeth. The face goes slick. It stops
cutting and starts rubbing. Rubbing is heat. Heat closes maple,
slicks cherry, and polishes a defect so it looks finished until
the can opens.

You can see glaze. It is the belt that reflects a light instead
of looking like dirt and mineral. You can feel the cut slow
down while the motor sounds the same. Change the belt. Cleaning
a glazed belt at the bench is a fence issue if someone wants
solvents and heroics. This pack's answer is: replace it.

## Platens and grooves

A grooved or worn platen prints a pattern through a perfectly
good belt. That pattern is not a grit. It is a machine scar.
If every panel has the same ghost lines, look at the machine
before you look at the mineral.

Small drums and lunchbox sanders have the same scar in
miniature. A worn drum wrap is a glaze cousin.

## Furniture shops that "don't have a wide-belt"

You still buy panels that went through one. If a purchased top
is burnished and blotchy, it may have been glazed at the mill.
Your first grit may need to *cut* that burnish, not polish it
further with 220. Start as coarse as the defect (draft 03). A
slick mill 180 can want an honest 150 or 180 that actually
cuts, then on.

Do not assume mill-sanded means "ready for finish." It means
ready for the next person to inspect.

## Shop consequence

If you run belts, schedule them by cut, not by calendar. If you
do not run belts, inspect incoming surfaces for shine that is
not finish. If stain blotches after a "fine" machine pass, ask
whether anyone cut or whether everyone polished.

Draft 08 said factories have rooms you do not have. This draft
says those rooms still ship you a surface. You own the next
scratch.
