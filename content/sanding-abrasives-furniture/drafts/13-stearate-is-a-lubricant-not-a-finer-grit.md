# Stearate is a lubricant, not a finer grit

The sheet looked frosted, like someone had spilled candle. I used
to think that meant "fine finish." It means zinc or calcium
stearate, a dry lubricant, so the waste lets go instead of caking
on the teeth. It helps 220 stay 220 a little longer on a film. It
does not make 220 into 320.

On bare wood before some finishes, that same frost can be a
contaminant. The lubricant does not vanish because you vacuumed.

## What it is for

Between-coat sanding of varnish, polyurethane, and many
waterbornes: nibs, dust, a little leveling, without loading the
paper into a gray crayon. Finish papers are often stearated on
purpose. That is the right aisle.

On resinous woods and pine, stearate can delay the comet. It is
not a substitute for open coat and extraction. It is a helper.

On a cured-enough film, I want stearate. On a film that is still
soft, I want to wait, not a slicker paper. Slick paper on a soft
film is how you ball up finish and smear it.

## Where it gets me in trouble

**Bare wood before waterborne.** Some waterbornes wet out poorly
on leftover stearate. Fisheye, crawling, a patch that looks like
the finish changed its mind. I use non-stearated paper for the
last bare-wood grits if the next can is waterborne, or I raise,
knock, and clean like I mean it. A test offcut is cheaper than a
table.

**Bare wood before stain.** Stearate can resist. Then you have a
light patch that looks like blotch and is actually wax-adjacent
chemistry you invited. If the stain card and the stearated last
pass disagree, believe the card and change paper.

**Oil.** Many oils forgive a little stearate. Some do not. I do
not treat "oil is friendly" as a rule. I treat it as a test.

## It is not a cleaner

People wipe a stearated sheet on a surface and think they are
dusting. They are spreading lubricant and broken mineral. Vacuum.
A clean tack if the finish wants one. Do not use the sanding
sheet as a cloth.

The white dust on the board after a stearated pass is not "just
wood." Treat it as something you have to remove before the can
opens.

## "No-load" as a brand sentence

No-load usually means stearate. Sometimes it means a
coat pattern. Read both. I have bought no-load paper
that was closed coat plus powder and still caked on
pine because pine does not care about the aisle name.

If the white face turns gray in a stripe, you are
loading anyway. Change it. The powder delayed the
stripe. It did not repeal pine.

## Shop consequence

Read the sleeve for "stearated" or "no-load" or a white face.
Use it on films. Be suspicious on the last bare pass before a
fussy finish. Do not buy it because the photo looked finer.

If a finish crawls after a "perfect" 220, ask what was on the
paper before you ask what was in the can. Draft 07 was coat.
This draft is the powder on the coat. They are different lies.
