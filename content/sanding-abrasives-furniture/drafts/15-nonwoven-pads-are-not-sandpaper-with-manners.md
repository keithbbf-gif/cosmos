# Nonwoven pads are not sandpaper with manners

The maroon pad took the sheen off the sealer and also took the
arrises into a soft story I had not drawn. I had treated it like
sandpaper with manners. It is a different tool. It cuts. It
conforms. It rounds. It does not have a grit number you can
trust like a sleeve.

Scotch-Brite is another brand that became a class. The class is
nonwoven nylon (or similar) with mineral in the web. Color is a
hint, not a P-grade.

## What they are good at

**Between coats**, when you want to dull a film and take nibs
without cutting a flat through an edge the way a hard sheet on a
block will. The pad follows. That is the gift and the danger.

**Profiles and carvings**, after the real grit work, to knock
fuzz without flattening a bead. After, not instead.

**Scuff-sanding a cured film** before a recoat that wants a
mechanical tooth. Even, gentle, and you still have to clean the
dust, which is now a mix of finish and colored web.

**Not** flattening a tabletop. Not erasing 80 grit. Not "the last
grit" on bare oak before stain. A pad on bare wood is a rounding
tool that leaves a scratch you cannot name.

## Color is a rumor

Maroon, gray, white, green — makers publish comparisons to grit
ranges. Those ranges move by brand and by how hard you press.
Pressing a maroon pad harder does not make it 180. It makes it
hotter and rounder.

I keep one color for between-coat scuff and I do not mix a
kitchen scouring pad into the finish drawer. Kitchen pads can
be a different mineral and a soap history you do not want on a
table.

## They shed

Colored fibers in a film are a defect you get to keep. Vacuum.
A light tack. Do not leave pad crumbs in a corner and then brush
on varnish like the corner was theoretical.

Worn pads glaze. A glazed pad is a burnisher. Throw it away. The
pad is cheaper than a cloudy table.

## Shop consequence

Use nonwoven when you want conformity and a dulling cut on a
film or a profile. Use paper when you want a named scratch on a
field. Do not let "non-scratching" on a package talk you into
skipping a grit. If it cuts, it scratches. If it does not cut,
it is a dusting cloth, and a dusty cloth is a different mistake.
