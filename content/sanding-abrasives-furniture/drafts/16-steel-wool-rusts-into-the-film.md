# Steel wool rusts into the film

I have a sample board with little orange freckles in a
waterborne topcoat. The freckles are 0000 steel wool I used
because that is what the old recipe said. The iron stayed. The
water in the finish found it. A humid week finished the joke.

Steel wool cuts. It also sheds iron. Waterborne, oxalic bleach,
and a wet climate will rust that iron in the film or in the
pores. The rust is not "character." It is a contaminant you
installed.

## Where steel wool still belongs

Oil and wax traditions that assume it, on work that will not
see a waterborne or a bleach, if you clean like you mean it and
you accept the risk. Some rubbing recipes still call for it.
Some old hands will not give it up.

I will use it on oil if I have no synthetic equivalent in the
grit I want and the piece is not about to get a waterborne
rescue. I will not use it before a waterborne on purpose. I
have the sample board.

**Do not** use steel wool on oak you plan to bleach. **Do not**
use it and then a water pop. **Do not** use it over a tannin-rich
wood and a water finish and act surprised.

## Synthetic wool

Synthetic "steel wool" (nonwoven, again) is not a personality
and it is not a betrayal of the ancestors. It is how you get a
similar scuff without iron. The cut is not identical. The rust
is absent. For furniture that will live in a bathroom-adjacent
climate or under a waterborne, that trade is easy.

If a recipe says 0000 and you switch to a gray pad, do a test.
The sheen will differ. Differ is better than rust.

## Shreds

Steel wool shreds in pores, in carvings, in the corner of a
panel. A magnet on a stick is a real tool. Vacuum is not enough
if a fiber is standing in a pore. Those fibers rust first.

Oily steel wool is a fire-adjacent wad if you mix it with
finishing-oil habits. Hang it or wet it in the same spirit as
rags (see finishing-chemistry). Do not leave a nest of oily
wool in a can of scraps.

## Shop consequence

If the next finish is water-anything, skip steel wool. If you
already used it, clean obsessively and still expect freckles.
If a client brings a piece with rust specks in an old film, that
is often this story, not a mineral in the wood.

Draft 15 was the pad. This draft is the iron. They get confused
in drawers. Separate them. Label the steel and put it higher
than the waterborne.
