# Card scraper after the paper

The tear-out was a little cave. Paper was polishing the rims and
leaving the cave. The card scraper took a shaving and the cave
became a shallow, honest spot I could then sand with the field.
A scraper cuts. It does not polish rims.

I use it after the paper has told me it is lying, and sometimes
instead of the last coarse paper on a local defect. I do not use
it as a way to skip from 80 to 220. A scraped surface still has
a tooth. That tooth still needs a named grit if stain or a thin
oil is coming.

## What it is good at

Localized tear-out. A glue smear that paper smears further
(draft 31). A mill mark that is a ridge. A show top where I
want a cut surface under oil instead of a 220 burnish — some
woods, some days, after the field is already fair.

A scraper will also tear if it is dull, if you hook it wrong, or
if you fight a knot. Dull is a burnisher. You will heat the
surface and think you are being gentle.

## After 180

On a lot of furniture I last-sand 180 or 220 and I am done with
the scraper. On a show top that will get oil, I sometimes scrape
after 180, then a light 220 with the grain to unify the tooth.
The scrape can look "too fresh" next to a burnished ROS field.
Unify, or scrape the whole field, or do not scrape.

Do not scrape through veneer. The scraper does not know about
your thickness budget. It knows about the hook you turned.

## Hands, edges, heat

A scraper gets hot. That heat is the cut working. It can also
be you pressing because the hook died. When it stops cutting,
turn a new hook. This pack will not teach you to grind a scraper
from a blank as a metallurgy project. A card sold for the work,
a file, a burnisher used as the tool it is — ordinary shop
practice. If you do not know the hook, learn it on scrap, not
on a table.

## The hook you cannot see

A scraper that "isn't cutting" is often a hook that
rolled over or never got turned. Pressing harder heats
and burnishes. Stop. Turn the hook on scrap, then come
back. This pack will not become a metallurgy class. It
will say: if you are leaning, the tool already quit.

A too-aggressive hook tears. Tear-out from a scraper is
still tear-out. Lighter hook, lighter hands, or a
different direction.

## Shop consequence

If paper is making a defect prettier and not smaller, stop
paper. Cut. Then return to the sequence so the cut does not
become its own photograph.

People treat scrapers as old-time religion and sandpaper as
modern compromise. They are both teeth. Pick the tooth that
shrinks the defect without inventing a valley. Then inspect
like you would after any grit change. The light does not care
which tool wrote the last line.
