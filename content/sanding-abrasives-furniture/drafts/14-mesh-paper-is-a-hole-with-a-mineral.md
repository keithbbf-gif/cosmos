# Mesh paper is a hole with a mineral

The first mesh disc I bought felt like I had been sold a screen
door. Then the vacuum bag got heavier and the surface stayed
visible while I cut. Net and mesh are plumbing. Dust has a path
through the tool instead of a cake on the face. They still load.
They still scratch. They still need the holes in the pad to mean
something.

Abranet is a brand people use as a class name, the way people
say Kleenex. The class is: open fabric, mineral on the threads,
extraction through the whole face.

## What changes

On a regular paper disc, dust either goes through a few holes or
it stays and becomes the next grit. On mesh, more of the face is
hole. The cut stays closer to the number longer. Inspection is
easier because you are not plowing a dust berm in front of the
pad.

That does not erase a skip. Mesh 80 to mesh 220 is still a skip.
The plumbing makes you feel like you are doing finer work because
the board looks cleaner. Clean is not refined. Look at the
scratch, not the dust.

## What does not change

Veneer still has no thickness budget. Edges still round. A dull
mesh disc still burnishes. Pine still gums, just later. If you
have no vacuum, mesh is a slightly more expensive open coat. It
will dump when you knock it. It will not invent extraction.

Interface pads still have to line up (draft 20). A thick foam
pad under mesh will soften the cut and hide a dish. Soft is not
always kind.

## Wear and the edge

Mesh frays. A frayed edge is a scratcher that is not a grit
number. Trim or throw. Do not keep a disc that has grown a
whisker.

The hooks still clog (draft 21). Mesh dumps more dust toward the
pad. If you never clean the hooks, you will invent a new problem
called "the disc won't sit."

## Grit numbers on net

Mesh makers publish P-grades. Believe them the way you
believe any sleeve: until the board disagrees. Some nets
cut a little meaner than the same number in paper because
the tooth sits on a thread and the dust is gone. A "P180
net" can leave a scratch a tired paper 180 would have
already burnished into politeness. Inspect. Do not assume
the plumbing made it finer.

When the net loads on pine, it loads in the threads. It
looks cleaner than paper and still sheds a comet. Knock
it. When the threads shine, it is a burnisher with holes.

## Shop consequence

I like mesh for ROS work on show faces when the vacuum is real.
I do not like it as a personality. Paper still belongs on a
block, on an edge, on a profile, on a lot of hand work. Mesh on
a cork block is possible and often silly.

If a catalog wants you to rebuild the whole shop around one net
system, keep the raking light. The system is a hole with a
mineral. The light is still the critic.
