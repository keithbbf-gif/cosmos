# Style guide — sanding and abrasives (furniture shop)

**Series folder:** `content/sanding-abrasives-furniture/`
**Audience:** furniture shop — scratch practice, not abrasive manufacturing.
**Fence:** shop-safe rules in `README.md` and `drafts/08-the-shop-safe-fence.md`.

## What a piece is

A staged draft is a shop essay. It opens on a surface, a light, a pad,
or a leftover scratch. It earns one judgment. It is publishable alone.

Target length is **650–1,000 words** of body copy. Shorter than a
magazine feature, longer than a grit chart. If you need a chart, make
the board. Do not pad to a word count with a third restatement.

## Openings

Start in the room.

Raking light on leftover 80-grit arcs. A disc that smells hot. Veneer
that suddenly looks like a map. Glue that the stain found first.

Do not start with a thesis about craft. Do not start with "sanding is
an essential step." Do not start with a question to "you."

The first sentence names a thing you can hold, see, or hear.

## Rhythm

Mix short and long. A three-word sentence is allowed if the next one
has to carry weight. Cut the sentence that only restates the last one.

Opinion is welcome when it is paid for by description. "A 120 finish
sand under oil is a 120 photograph forever" is a judgment. "Grit
matters" is not.

## Banned language (hard)

Do not use:

- delve, landscape (as metaphor), robust, leverage, unlock, cutting-edge, game-changer
- “In today’s…” / “In an era of…”
- “It’s important to note”
- Moreover / Furthermore stacks
- “Whether you’re a beginner or a pro…”
- “In conclusion”
- “At the end of the day”
- “Not only… but also…” as a tic
- “The key takeaway”
- “Let’s dive in”
- “This article will explore”
- “comprehensive guide”
- throat-clearing first paragraphs
- fake dualities unless the piece is actually about a real fork
- symmetrical list-filler (five tips, seven reasons)
- invented maker quotes
- invented customer quotes
- invented shop-floor dialogue attributed to named living people

If a living person is quoted, the quote must be sourced or cut.

## Claims and [VERIFY]

Uncertain numbers, brand-specific micron charts, and shop-specific
sequences that have not been confirmed on a named floor get
`[VERIFY]`. Do not invent a particle-size table. Do not invent a
wide-belt schedule. CAMI vs FEPA/P-grade is real; a specific box
in a specific year may still want a check.

Place continuity is fair: hardwood furniture, oak, maple, pine, the
ordinary American shop. Brand names appear when the object in the
hand needs a name (P-scale, CAMI, Abranet as a class of mesh). This
pack is not a catalog.
