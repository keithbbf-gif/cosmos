## Construction history (the box)

- Hayward, Charles H. “Four Centuries of Drawer Making,” in *The Woodworker: The Charles H. Hayward Years*, Vol. II (Lost Art Press reprint). Mid-17th-century side-hung grooves; early through-dovetails under walnut veneer; early-18th lapped pins; late-18th grooved bottoms with grain side-to-side; 19th-century slip mouldings. Treat as a period shop voice, not a BBF spec.
- Popular Woodworking. “Drawer Slips.” English slips; 1720s rebate-and-runner refinements; *Cabinet-makers’ London Book of Prices* (1788) “slipping drawers”; Sheraton 1803 on slips vs confined rabbets. American shops more often grooved thicker sides.
- Fine Woodworking. “A Short History of Chests of Drawers” (2005). Tills inside chests; drawers behind doors; lid retained after the case filled with drawers; mid-17th century as the moment the chest pretense is dropped.
- Greene, Jeffrey P. *American Furniture of the 18th Century*. Taunton. Drawer sides and backs commonly 3/8–1/2 in.; groove-in-side as the illustrated American method.
- Mussey, Robert D., Jr. *The Furniture Masterworks of John and Thomas Seymour*. Peabody Essex. Boston 1785–1815: bottoms in grooves; kerfed glue blocks under bottoms (a cousin of the slip, and a movement risk if the bottom is locked).
- Kugelman / Kugelman. *Connecticut Valley Furniture*. Connecticut Historical Society. Mid–late 18th-century regional practice: nailed rabbets or grooved sides; slips not the local language.

## Dating clocks (factory)

- Knapp, Charles B. U.S. Patent 63,532 (2 April 1867) — boring / joint machine that became the pin-and-scallop (“Knapp”) drawer joint. Factory line commonly dated from 1871 (Knapp Dovetailing Co., Northampton, Mass.; Beal & Hooper, East Cambridge). Joint largely out of fashion by ~1900 once machines could cut a dovetail that looked handmade. North American factory story; not a U.K. clock.
- Fine Woodworking. “The history of the cove-and-pin joint” (2018). Names, later patents (D4,302; 122,390; Clement 388,760), geography of use.
- Popular Woodworking. “Drawers Date Furniture.” Pin-and-scallop as a *terminus post quem* of the early 1870s; machine-cut dovetails as a *terminus post quem* around 1895. Hand-cut tails do **not** prove a pre-machine date.

## Trade and price books

- *The Cabinet-Makers’ London Book of Prices* (1788; later 1793, 1803). Piecework language for slipping drawers and plowing bottoms. Use for trade structure, not as a shop price list.
- Sheraton, Thomas. *The Cabinet Dictionary* (1803). Slips glued inside and “plained to receive the bottom.”
- Hayward / English trade manuals generally: “deal” = pine or fir board; “rebate” = rabbet; “lining” = secondary wood.

## Shop practice (fit, runners, glue)

- Hoadley, R. Bruce. *Understanding Wood*. Taunton. Movement: drawer-side *height* is cross-grain; bottoms that run side-to-side expand front-to-back.
- Schwarz, Christopher / Lost Art Press blog. “Drawer Runners” (Hayward excerpt, 2017). Stub-tenoned runners, kickers, slot-screwed backs so a case end can move.
- Hide-glue practice: ordinary cabinet literature (Hayward, Krenov, period glue-pot notes). Do not invent a house pot temperature. `[VERIFY]` any number printed as shop law.
- Joyce, Ernest (rev. Alan Peters). *The Technique of Furniture Making*. Batsford. Production cousins of the handmade box.

## Traditions and hardware

- Shaker: museum catalogs (Hancock, Pleasant Hill, Sabbathday Lake) for knob, flush, and half-blind habits — caption only what a photograph actually shows.
- Federal / Hepplewhite / Sheraton forms: cockbeading, thin linings, mahogany show wood. Do not invent a named commission.
- Stickley / Arts & Crafts: through tails as a show joint. Rights on catalog photographs: do not lift.
- Campaign furniture: dealer literature for flush pulls, brass corners, travel locks. Do not lift photographs.
- Tansu: Fine Woodworking “Traditional Tansu, Part 2: Drawers and Doors” (2024) and shop notes on pegged rabbets / pinned bottoms. Japanese practice is not a failed European dovetail.
- Kitchen hardware: Blum company history (roller runners mid-1960s; TANDEM late 1970s — confirm on Blum’s own timeline before printing a year as law). Accuride founded 1962 as industrial slides. Manufacturer data sheets for side thickness and width — then `[VERIFY]` what this shop stocks.
