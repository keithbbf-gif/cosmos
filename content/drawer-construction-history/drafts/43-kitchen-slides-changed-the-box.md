The slide would not go in. The box was honest poplar, square enough to live with, and a sixteenth too fat for the number on the Blum sheet. I had built it the way I build a sitting-room drawer — 3/4 sides, a little meat, a groove for a floor — and then asked an undermount rail to treat that meat as a specification. The rail declined. I stood there with a kitchen box that was a furniture box, and I could hear the difference. One of them is planed to an opening. The other is built to a catalog.

That is the new contract. A wood runner will take a shaving. A metal slide wants a width, a side thickness, a notch at the back, a hole for a hook, a locking clip at a dimension someone in Vorarlberg or Santa Fe Springs already chose. You can fight the sheet. The sheet will win in June when the drawer drops or racks or refuses to latch. I fight the sheet only when I am building the other product.

## A farrier’s company, then a rail

Julius Blum GmbH starts, on Blum’s own history page, on 1 March 1952 in Höchst. Julius Blum was a farrier and carriage smith. The first product was a horseshoe stud — a thing that kept a hoof from slipping. I like that origin more than a branding story, because it is a small steel part with a job. Furniture fittings come later. Blum’s timeline puts the first furniture hinge work in the late 1950s, a concealed hinge in 1964, and roller runners in 1966. TANDEM — a runner with a roller carriage instead of a row of separate rollers — sits in the late 1970s on that same timeline. Some Blum pages associate the launch with 1976–77. I will not flatten it to a year you can stamp on a tag. [VERIFY] against the live company page before anyone treats a digit as law.

Those years matter only as a clock. Mid-1960s roller runners are when a kitchen drawer can leave the wooden rail and still be a drawer a plant can repeat. Late-1970s TANDEM is when the hang starts to hide and the box starts to owe the sheet a thickness. I am standing on Blum’s mouth, not on a memory of a trade-show booth.

Accuride is the other common name on an American bench. Trade press and the short encyclopedia entry often say 1962, a California tool-and-die shop, slides for tape drives and copiers, then office furniture, then the rest of us. Accuride’s own anniversary story dates Fred Jordan’s A.A. Tool & Die to 1958. I will not pick a winner in a paragraph. [VERIFY] both. What I will say: the company did not begin as a kitchen brand. It began as industrial travel. The furniture box inherited a rail that already knew how to be precise.

The 3800 family is the reference a lot of shops still mean when they say “Accuride” without a number — three members, ball bearings, a side-mount that taught a generation of cabinets how wide the box had to be. Company materials put the Model 3800 in the 1980s. It is not a 1962 part. Hedge it. [VERIFY] the SKU on the bench against the sheet in the box. I will not print a load rating from memory. Those numbers move, and a remembered hundred pounds is how you hang a file drawer on a pencil-drawer rail.

## The box becomes a number

Before the slide, the drawer was fat until it fit the hole. Hayward’s world, Greene’s 3/8-to-1/2 American sides, a slip if the side was skinny — those are other Saturdays. After the slide, the *opening* is still a hole, but the box is a calculated rectangle inside it. Side-mount ball-bearing slides commonly eat about a half-inch each side. Undermounts want an inside width that is the opening minus a catalog deduction, and they want the side no thicker than the clip will hug. Blum’s current TANDEM sheets talk in millimeters and in two families of side thickness. I am not reprinting the table. The table on the wall is already out of date the day a new locking device ships. [VERIFY] the deduction for the runner you are holding.

What changed in the shop is the order of operations. I used to cut sides, cut tails, shoot the box, then think about the runner. On a kitchen I cut the opening, I read the sheet, I cut the sides to the thickness the clip will accept, I cut the length the rail wants, I notch the back, I bore for hooks and clips, and *then* I decide whether the corner is a tail, a finger, a locking rabbet, or a staple I will not sign. The joint is still a joint. It is no longer the first sentence.

A plywood floor is part of that contract more often than a solid one. The slide does not care. The slide cares that the bottom is out of its way, or that the underside is a landing for an undermount. I still run solid floors in sitting-room work. I do not owe a kitchen box a beveled oak panel because I like history.

## Wood runners are a product

I still put wooden runners in cases. Stub-tenoned at the front, slot-screwed at the back when the case end has to move, a kicker so the drawer cannot tip when it is open and full of plates. Wax. That is not nostalgia. It is a product with a different invoice. No clip. No notch. No sheet. The box can be 3/8 cedar if the job wants 3/8 cedar. The opening can be a little crooked and I can plane the drawer to it. The customer hears wood. The customer also hears a change in June if I cut the fit in January in Wilmar. I will not make this a bind sermon. Weather still belongs to wood-on-wood. A ball-bearing does not swell. The box around it still does.

People talk as if wooden runners died when Blum learned to roll. They did not die. They left the kitchen. A linen press, a mule that still wants a lid, a tool chest, a hall table with one drawer — those jobs still buy wood. A row of kitchen drawers that have to match a dishwasher door and a pair of soft-close hinges is a different sale. I will not apologize for either sale.

What I will apologize for is mixing them in one opening without saying so. A wooden runner beside a slide in the same case is a translation the next owner cannot read.

## Failures

I built a bank of kitchen boxes at 3/4 side because the poplar came in thick and I did not want to run it down. The undermount clips hugged 5/8. The 3/4 sat on the rail like a man on a fence. I ripped the sides, remade the backs, and wrote the thickness on the traveler in a box I cannot miss. The sheet is not a suggestion.

I also hung a heavy pot drawer on a light-duty side-mount because the pair was on the shelf and the heavy pair was on order. [VERIFY] the rating when you reprint this. I will say what I felt: the rail protested. The box racked. I swapped the slides before delivery. A catalog is also a load path.

A third failure was pride. I built wood-on-wood kitchen drawers for a house that already had Blum hinges and a customer who had already felt a soft-close at a showroom. The drawers were good. They were the wrong product. I replaced them. I kept one box on the wall so I remember that a correct joint can still be a wrong invoice.

## What this is not

This is not a history of every runner made in Austria or California. Grass, Hettich, Knape & Vogt, the anonymous white epoxy roller in a box-store cabinet — they all wrote numbers on boxes. Blum and Accuride are the names this bench sees. This is not a claim that Bradley Brand Furniture descends from a farrier in Höchst. It does not. This is not a load table. I will not invent what a 1966 roller would carry.

The judgment is small. If the drawer rides a sheet, build the sheet first. If the drawer rides wood, build the opening first. Do not treat the wooden runner as a museum and the slide as a virtue. They are two products. The kitchen chose one and then taught the box to obey. The sitting room can still choose the other. Write the product on the pad before you cut the side, and do not ask a 3/4 furniture habit to wear a 16-millimeter clip.
