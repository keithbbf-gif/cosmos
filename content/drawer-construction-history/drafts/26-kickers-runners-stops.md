Three pieces of maple came off the cutoff saw in a rhythm I could hear from the glass wall — runner, kicker, stop — each too short to be a board and too important to be scrap. I stacked them on the bench the way a cook stacks what the pot will need. The drawer was still a box in clamps. The case already had holes. These three sticks were going to teach the box how to live in a hole without diving, without hanging, without punching the back out of the carcase.

Side-hung drawers — a groove in the side, a runner on the case — already had their century and their essay. I will not retell the hang. This is the later, ordinary stack: the drawer sits on something, is kept from tipping by something, and is told to stop by something. Three woods. Leave one out and the other two start doing jobs they were not hired for.

## The runner is a road

A runner is a long-grain strip under the drawer side, front rail to back, housed in a solid end or joined into a web. Downward load lives here. Oak, maple, a hard scrap that will polish. End grain on a runner is a file; I have made that file and I have listened to it washboard a pine side into noise. Long grain along the travel.

Hayward wants the runner free of winding. Two runners that are the same distance apart at every station can still be twisted as a pair. The drawer then rocks. You will swear the box is out of square. The box may be fine. The roads are a propeller. I sight them with a winding stick, or I lay a drawer dummy across and feel the rock, before I wax anything. Wax on a twisted pair is perfume on a limp.

In a solid-end case the runner gets the housing and the slot-screw — the web-frame Saturday. In a case that runs the bottom drawer on the case bottom, the bottom *is* the runner. That is legal. It is also a floor that will get the first dirt and the first splash of whatever the room does. I still wax it. I still keep the drawer’s bottom from being the shoe if I can help it.

A center runner on a wide drawer is a third road. That is the rack essay. Here I will only say: if the box is wide enough to rack, the two side roads are not a complete sentence.

## The kicker is a ceiling

Pull a drawer halfway and let go of the idea that it is still a box in a hole. It is a cantilever. The front is a load. The back wants to lift. The sides want to dive. A kicker is a stick *above* the drawer side that refuses the dive. It is a runner inverted. Same species if you have it, same long grain, same respect for a solid end that must be allowed to move.

When a drawer occupies the whole top of a carcase, the underside of the top is the kicker. Hayward says so, and every tool chest that runs a tray under a lid knows it. When the top is only a pair of rails — a writing surface with an opening, a case with a gallery, a chest whose top drawer sits under a thin frame — you add a kicker. One can be enough if the rails are built out at the ends so the sides have somewhere to bear. Hayward frames the kicker between the rails when he can, or stub-tenons the front and butts the back. I have done both. Framing is cleaner. The butt-and-skew-nail version works if you are honest about the nail.

I once omitted a kicker because the top drawer was “shallow” and the customer was “careful.” The first heavy book in that drawer taught the front to nod. The nod wore a crescent on the runner noses. I added the kicker from the back, sliding it in like a confession. The books still nod a little. The wood remembers the year without a ceiling.

Kitchen boxes on metal slides do not use a wooden kicker. The slide *is* the kicker and the runner and sometimes the stop. That is a different contract, and a later essay. Wooden travel still wants the third stick.

Do not confuse a kicker with a guide. A guide keeps the drawer from wandering left and right. A kicker keeps it from rotating down. I have watched people glue a fat guide on a side and call it a kicker because it rubbed. Rubbing is not a job title.

## The stop is the last argument

Flush is a decision. Inset fronts want to land in the same plane as the rails. Overlay fronts want a reveal you can repeat. Lip fronts want the lip to kiss without slamming. None of those landings happen by hoping the drawer back will meet the case back at the right moment. The case back is often thin. It is often a panel. It is often pine. A drawer full of tools, shut with a hip, will punch a thin back into a belly. I have patched that belly. The patch is always uglier than a stop.

Stops are small. That is why they get forgotten. A block on the rail behind the front, sized so the front sits where you meant. A pair of little pads let into the runners, same job. A turn-stop or a screw-stop if you want to adjust after the finish. I like wood I can plane. I like two stops, not one, so the front cannot pivot and land drunk.

There is a second stop some people never name: the stop that keeps the drawer from coming *out*. A kicker and a well-fitted side will not always save you when a child pulls. A kick-stop, a drawer-lock, a rail that catches a horn on the side — those are cousins. For ordinary chests I cut the kicker or a hidden block so the drawer cannot pass a certain point without a lift. For file drawers I am more serious. A file drawer that leaves the case is a missile. The rack essay will say why it was already in a mood.

I set stops last, after the drawer has been shot to the opening. If you set them first, you will shoot to a rumor. The front tells you when it is flush. Then the stop copies the front.

South Arkansas will move a stop if the rail it sits on moves. A stop glued to a shrinking rail can leave the front proud in October and shy in June. I glue a stop to long-grain of a rail when I can, or I screw it so I can shim. I do not invent a gap number here. [VERIFY] any printed clearance. I sneak up.

## Species and the cheap temptation

Maple and oak take the wear. Pine stops crush. I have used pine stops because I was out of maple and the pine was already in my hand. The front slowly migrated in. The customer thought the case had “settled.” The stop had become a cushion. Hardwood for the three woods, even when the case is pine. The case can be secondary. The travel parts are primary in function if not in show.

I mill runners, kickers, and stops from the same hard offcuts so they move alike. That is a nicety. It is not a law. What is close to a law: do not mill a runner from a board that is still wet in the middle. A runner that cups is a road with a gutter. The drawer will prefer the gutter.

## Failures

I built a pretty chest and put beautiful half-blinds on the fronts and then ran the drawers on the case rails with no kickers and no stops because the openings were tight and I told myself tightness was a system. Tightness is not a kicker. The top drawers dove. The bottoms slammed the back. Two of the backs loosened on their nails. I spent a Saturday installing the three woods I had skipped to save an afternoon.

I also let a runner run long and used the extra as a stop against the case back. The back became the stop. The back lost. Length is not a stop. A stop is a stop.

A third: I put the kicker on only one side of a wide drawer to “see if one would do.” One will do on a narrow box. On a wide box the free side dove and the drawer racked as it dove. Two kickers, or one wide kicker that both sides can find, or a center kicker if the construction gives you a center. Hayward’s one-kicker note assumes the rails are built out at the ends. Mine were not. I had read the sentence and skipped the clause.

## What this is not

This is not the 17th-century hang. If the drawer rides a groove, you are in that church, and the groove is the bearing. This is not the wax essay, and it is not the taper essay. Those are how you make the three woods kinder. This is the three woods.

It is also not a claim that metal slides are a moral failure. They hired different sticks — steel ones — and they still need a stop, even if the stop is in the bearing.

The judgment is small. Under, over, and enough. The runner takes the weight. The kicker takes the nod. The stop takes the slam and the flush. Build the box as well as you like. If you skip these three, the case will finish the job with a split, a crescent, or a back that no longer sits in its rebate.
