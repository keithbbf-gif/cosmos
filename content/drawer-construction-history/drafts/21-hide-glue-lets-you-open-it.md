Steam from a wet rag and the old hide glue let go with a smell like a hide. The slip came up. The bottom stayed a board. I had not boiled anything. I had not looked at a thermometer. I had asked a joint that was made to be asked, and it answered. The film on the side was brown, brittle at the edges, still a little tacky where the steam had reached. I could have put the slip back the same afternoon. That is the whole religion.

Drawer work is repair work waiting to happen. Bottoms split. Slips wear. Blocks knock off. A tail starves. The person who opens the box in twenty years may be me, or it may be someone in a different shop who does not owe my pride anything. Hide glue is the contract that says they can get in. Ordinary cabinet literature — Hayward, Krenov, the old pot notes — treats animal glue as the furniture film. I will not invent a house pot temperature. [VERIFY] any number I am tempted to treat as law: bloom, open time, a degree on a pot, a minute on a clock. The shop fact I will stand on is the one I can smell: the joint opened.

## What reversible means on a drawer

A slip glued in hide can be lifted and a new bottom slid. A Seymour-style pad can be steamed and a locked floor can be freed. A starved pin can be warmed and the corner can be closed again with a better film. You are not dissolving the wood. You are returning the glue to a state where it will release. Water and heat do that to hide. They do not do that to a yellow bottle in any way I will call kind.

I have steamed PVA. I have also regretted it. The polymer gums. It smears. It does not let go in a sheet. You scrape a mess and you still have a mess in the grain. A drawer side with PVA in a groove is a side you may have to recut. I recut one. The owner did not need a chemistry lecture. They needed a box that ran.

Hide glue on a cold bench will skin and refuse to wet the second face. That is a shop failure I own without a thermometer. The room was cold enough that the brush dragged. I warmed the room, I warmed the work, I did not write a number on the wall. People who want a number can stand on a published pot note and [VERIFY] it against their own pot. I will not print a fiction for Wilmar.

Liquid hide in a bottle is a cousin, not a twin. It is still more openable than PVA in my experience. I will not invent a brand or a shelf life. I use what I have when the pot is a bother and the joint is small. A long slip on a sitting-room drawer still gets the pot if the pot is already the day’s tool.

## Creep is a loaded-bottom problem

PVA can creep under a constant load. That sentence is ordinary in cabinet literature. I will not invent a sag in sixteenths or a month when my shop “proved” it. [VERIFY] any measurement before it becomes a caption. What I have seen: a solid bottom, or a plywood floor, sitting in a rebate on a bead of yellow glue, shirts in the drawer for a long time, the floor slowly becoming a shallow dish. The glue did not let go. The glue moved. The runner still kissed the side. The shirts found the dish.

Hide glue is a more brittle, more glass-like film once it has set. It does not love a constantly loaded joint in every application — people use that as an argument against it in chairs — but a drawer bottom that is *supposed* to be captured by a groove, not held by a glue bead, should not be asking any film to be a joist. The creep problem is the problem of a glue asked to be structure. PVA is happy to be asked and then it slowly says yes to gravity.

I have glued a plywood bottom into a rebate with PVA “so it would never rattle.” It did not rattle. It sagged. I had made a drum skin on a flexible frame. The fix was a new bottom, a groove or a pair of slips, and glue only where a glue belongs — the front edge if I want a stop, not the long sides, not a full bed of polymer.

A loaded sock drawer is a fair test because socks are honest. They sit. They sit for seasons. A tool drawer is a ruder test; tools are points. Either way, do not ask the film under the floor to be the floor. The 18th-century bevel and groove already knew that. The bottle forgets.

## Repair is the reason I stay

I open a lot of dead people’s drawers. Not a joke — a week in a 65-foot shop that also takes repairs. Hide-glued linings come apart at the temperature of patience. I can save a side. I can save a front. I can lift a leaf if I have to, and I will not retell that telegraph here. PVA from a 1990s “tightening” is the scar I curse without raising my voice. Someone meant well. They locked a movement joint. They locked a future.

If I mix hide and PVA in one drawer — hide on the tails, PVA on the bottom because the bottle was closer — I have written two contracts in one box. The next person will open what they can and destroy what they cannot. I did that mix once. I wrote it on the inside of the back in pencil so I would be the one who was ashamed, not the next bench. I still use one glue per box unless I have a reason I can write.

Hide glue stains. It is brown. It is honest. A squeezed bead on pine is a line you can plane. A squeezed bead of PVA is a rubber you will sand into a smear. I would rather plane brown than sand rubber off a lining I wanted to stay sharp.

## Failures

I clamped a hide-glued tail through a football game and then opened the clamp to a joint that had gelled at the edge and starved in the middle. Open time is real. I will not number it. I will say: when the brush starts to drag, you have already missed. Pull the joint, clean, try again. A starved tail in hide is still a starved tail.

I also flooded a groove with hide “to seal the bottom” and created the lock Sheraton was trying to avoid with a slip. Reversible is not harmless. You can still glue a floor tight with the good glue. The good glue will then let you undo the mistake. I undid it. I should not have had to.

A student used hide on a drawer that then sat in a closed car in August. The joint crept open on its own, which is a different creep — heat and moisture you did not ask for. Hide will release in a hot wet box. That is the same virtue as the wet rag. Do not leave a newly glued drawer in a place you would not leave a dog.

## What this is not

This is not a pot manual. There will be no temperature. There will be no bloom number. There will be no minute count I cannot stand behind. [VERIFY] any figure an editor wants to add from a published note. This is not a ban on PVA. I use yellow glue on shop jigs and on plywood boxes that are a different contract. I do not use it under a loaded solid bottom I expect to service.

This is not a chemistry paper. Creep is a word the literature already spent. I am spending it on the floor of a drawer because that is where I see it.

The judgment is small. Glue the box so someone can open it. Hide glue is how this trade did that for a long time. Keep glue out of the groove that must stay a slipway. Do not ask PVA to be a joist under shirts. And do not print a number on the pot as if the number were the craft. The craft is the film you can smell, and the slip that comes up, and the bottom that stays a board.
