The bead caught my thumbnail and would not let it slide off the front. A small half-round, mitered at the corner, proud of the walnut by a thought, and the light from the long wall laid a dark thread under it. I was holding a drawer the way you hold a plate — fingers under the bottom, thumb on the face — and the edge was dressed. Not a lip that hid a gap. A bead. The old word is cockbead. The job is armor and a shadow.

Eighteenth-century case work, especially the finer English and the American Federal that learned from it, likes this thread around the drawer front. Mahogany and walnut show wood. Thin linings. A front that is a picture. The picture has edges, and edges get hit. A bead is a sacrificial wire. It takes the ding so the field stays clean. It also throws a line the room can read: here is a drawer, here is a rail, here is a little night under the round.

I am not going to give you a first year. Cockbeading shows up as an 18th-century habit, not as a Tuesday you can stamp. [VERIFY] any tighter date against the piece and a catalog you trust. I have seen later revivals that copy the thread and miss the rebate. The thread without the seat is a stick glued to a square edge. It looks busy. It does not look seated.

## Armor, then shadow

People talk about cockbeads as decoration because the shadow is pretty. The shop reason is meaner. Drawer fronts live in traffic. Knuckles. Vacuum wands. The corner of a tray. Veneered fronts — the earlier essay already told that telegraph story — have an edge that wants to lift. A bead covers the end of the leaf and gives the glue a shoulder. Solid fronts still like the bead because a ding in a half-round sands back into a half-round. A ding in a sharp arris stays a ding.

I run a bead when the front is inset and the customer is paying for a line. I do not run a bead on a kitchen slab that will wear a pull and a child’s boot. Armor has to match the war. A sitting-room drawer gets a thread. A pot drawer gets an edge I can ease with a block plane and not mourn.

The shadow is the second paycheck. A flush front in a flush opening can look like a slab with cracks. A bead stands the front off the rail by a dark hair. The case reads as a stack of framed pictures. Federal work liked that reading. So do I, when the wood is worth framing.

## How it sits: rebate, then the top

The old seating I trust is a rebate at the sides and the bottom of the front. The bead lives in that step. The miter at the lower corners has a home. The top is the argument. Early and careful work often leaves the top edge of the front as a clean arris, or treats the top bead as a separate decision, because the top of a drawer kisses a rail or a kicker and a proud round there is a wear part you will hear. Later habit — and a lot of revival work — runs the bead full width across the top as well, four miters, a picture frame.

I will not flatten that into one century. I have opened drawers where the sides and bottom were beaded in a rebate and the top was a plane shaving of show wood, no thread. I have opened drawers with a four-sided frame that was original and proud. Look at how the top bead meets the rail when the drawer is shut. If the round is crushed, someone asked a wear surface to be jewelry. [VERIFY] the sequence on the piece before you write *early* or *late* on a tag.

In this shop I rebate sides and bottom. I decide the top after I see the opening. If the top rail is close and the drawer is wide, I leave the top square and ease it. If the front sits in a generous hole and the customer wants the frame, I run the fourth bead and I cut the miters as if they will be photographed, because they will.

The bead is usually a separate stick. Sometimes it is stuck on the solid front with a scratch or a plane. Applied is easier to replace. Stuck is easier to lose when you sand. I apply when the front is veneered or when I know I will ding it in delivery. I stick when the front is a thick mahogany and I want the grain to turn the corner with the field.

## It is not a kitchen overlay lip

A kitchen overlay lip is a different contract. The front is larger than the hole. The lip covers the rail. The gap disappears behind wood. That is a door logic applied to a drawer. It is useful. It hides a sloppy opening. It is also how a lot of 20th-century American case work dresses a cheap box. Do not call that lip a cockbead because it is rounded.

I have been asked to “put the little colonial bead” on an overlay front. I can round an overlay. I cannot make it a cockbead. A cockbead rides the perimeter of an inset front and leaves the rail visible. The shadow is between bead and rail, not between lip and room. If you cover the rail, you have changed the furniture. Say overlay. Charge overlay. Leave the 18th century out of the kitchen if the kitchen is a lip.

Lipped drawers in older country work — a thumbnail lip, a small overlay on otherwise handmade boxes — are a third cousin. They date and they wear and they are not Federal jewelry. This pack will get to lips as a meeting of front and rail. This essay is the thread that does not hide the rail.

## Miters, species, glue

A cockbead miter is small enough to be a trap. The stick is often 1/8 or so proud of a tiny square. [VERIFY] any dimension I am tempted to print as house law; I cut what the front wants. Short grain at the miter wants to crumble. I cut the miters after the sticks are fitted in the rebate, and I sneak up. A shooting board for beads is a scrap with a fence. It is worth the scrap.

Species: I like the bead to match the front. A holly line is a different fashion — stringing, not a cockbead. A contrasting bead can be handsome and it will always look like a decision. If I am not ready to sign that decision, I match.

Hide glue for applied beads I may have to lift. PVA if the bead is stuck in a rebate I do not plan to open. The bead is not a structural wall. It is a hat. Hats come off in this climate if you starve the glue and then run a wet cloth along the edge every Saturday.

Bradley County summers swell a tight inset. A proud bead that was a piston fit in January will kiss the stile in July and polish itself into a flat. Leave a shadow. The bead is allowed to be seen. It is not allowed to be a wedge.

## Failures

I glued a four-sided bead on a front that already had a slight bow — not the curved-front essay, just a board that moved after I dressed it — and the top stick bridged. The miters opened in a month. I should have seated the front and then beaded, or I should have left the top square. Sequence is part of the joint.

I also rounded the edge of an overlay kitchen drawer and told myself it was “close enough to period.” It was close enough to a lie. The customer was happy. I was not, later, when I used the photograph in my head as if it were a cockbead. It was a lip with manners. I keep those photographs honest now.

A student sanded a stuck bead until it was a smear at the corners. The field of the front was still pretty. The armor was gone. We recut the front. Cheaper than pretending a smear was a shadow.

## What I refuse

I refuse a plastic “bead” moulding from a big-box aisle on a walnut front. I refuse to fill a missing bead with wood putty and paint. I refuse to call a router-roundover on a plywood slab a cockbead in a caption. The word is specific. Spend it on the thread that sits in a rebate and throws a line.

I will replace a missing bead on an old front. I will not add beads to a Shaker-habit piece that wanted a square arris and a knob. That is the later tradition essay. Habits are not interchangeable hats.

## What this is not

This is not a rule that every 18th-century drawer wears a bead. Plenty of square fronts exist, and country work often skipped the thread. This is not a dating clock as tight as a Knapp scallop. A bead can be added next year. Look at the rebate. Look at the glue. Look at whether the miters share the front’s life or sit on it like a new frame. This is not a kitchen lip.

The judgment is small. If you want the armor, cut the seat. If you want the shadow, leave the rail in the light. A round that covers the case is doing another job, and that job already has a name.
