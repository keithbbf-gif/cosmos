I heard the corner hit the rail before I saw it — a wide file drawer taking the short way out of a square hole. The pull is a single cup in the middle of that front, and the hand that used it had not been in the middle. The box went diamond. Maple side kissed oak rail. A little white wound in the finish, the kind people point at and say the wood moved. The wood had moved about an eighth of a second. The rest was geometry.

A narrow drawer is a cart with a short axle. You can pull it off-center and it still tracks. A wide drawer is a cart with a long axle and no differential. One side starts. The other lags. The front turns in plan. The rear goes the other way. That is rack. Humidity can make the fit tighter, so the rack happens earlier and leaves a brighter scar. Humidity is not the mechanism. The mechanism is a rectangle that is free to become a parallelogram because nothing in the middle told it not to.

I will not write the seasonal-clearance essay. A feeler stick will not teach a wide box manners. This is the third road, and the habit of blaming the weather for a missing one.

## Why width is a different animal

Pull lives on the front. On a narrow box you cannot get far from the center even if you try. On a blanket-width drawer, or a file, or a writing-table well that is really two drawers pretending to be one, your hand has room to be wrong. Two pulls help if you use both. People do not use both. They use the near one. The near one is an off-center load. The box rotates.

Bottom runners at the sides are two rails. Two rails will not stop rotation in plan. They will even feed it if one is slicker than the other — a little more wax on the left, a little more grit on the right, and the drawer prefers a lane. Kickers stop the dive. They do not stop the diamond. Stops stop the slam. They do not stop the diamond. You need something in the middle that the box must obey: a center runner under a groove or a pair of edges, a center guide standing on that runner, a dustboard muntin that is also a track.

Hayward draws the center runner for a pair of drawers that meet in the middle, and for any situation where there is no end to house a side runner. A hanger at the back, dovetailed down, because a center stick has nothing to sit in. A square guide glued and screwed on top of the runner. He will let you taper that guide a trifle wider at the back so the drawers have easy clearance as they go home — hospitality, not a hiding. What is important, he says, is that there is not *less* clearance at the back. Pinch at the rear is how a wide box starts its diamond.

I treat that as shop law I am willing to sign. The width at which I add the third road is not a number I will print. [VERIFY] before anyone writes 30 inches on a handout from this paragraph. What I will say: if I can pull one end and hear a corner before the drawer has traveled a hand’s length, the geometry is already unemployed.

## File drawers are the loud version

A file drawer is wide *and* deep *and* heavy. The load sits low or high depending on the folders, and it shifts when someone flips a tab. The box is a truck. Side-hung wooden travel on a file is a dare. Bottom runners without a center is a dare with a clip board. Modern file cabinets surrendered to steel slides because the slides are paired and they share the rack. That is not a moral surrender. That is an admission.

If I build a wooden-travel file — and I have, for a desk that wanted to stay a desk — I put a center track in as if I were being paid to. Groove in the bottom, runner on the case, or a pair of guides that catch a center slip. I test with a full load and a one-handed yank from the left, then the right. If it racks empty, it will rack full. If it only racks full, it still racks. The customer does not empty a file to open it.

Steel file slides have a width and a load rating. I will not reprint a catalog number here. [VERIFY] the sheet we stock. What the sheet will not tell you is that a wooden box on those slides still needs a square front and a bottom that will not become a drum. The slide takes the rack. The box still has to be a box.

## People call it humidity

<!-- PHOTO: fig-02 D:\BBF\BBF Photos — center runner and guide between a pair of drawers, or a file drawer with a center track (filename pending shop pull) -->

July in Bradley County will swell a drawer side in height and a front in width. A tighter fit racks sooner. That is true. It is also true in January if the center guide is missing and a child pulls the left cup. I have seen winter rack. The house was dry. The corner still hit. We stood there and looked at the missing muntin. The owner had been planning to run a dehumidifier. The dehumidifier would not have grown a muntin.

A swollen side can also *look* like rack because the tight corner is the one that kisses first. Diagnose before you plane. Pull from the center with two hands. If the drawer comes straight and the kiss goes away, you had geometry plus a sloppy hand. If the drawer comes straight and still binds on one side, you may have a fat side or a shy opening — plane-to-opening, not a center guide. If the drawer cannot come straight no matter where you pull, you have a diamond already built into the box: sides of different length, a back out of square, a bottom glued so it pulled the rectangle. Fix the box. Then talk about weather.

South Arkansas is a convenient villain. I live here. I use it. I will not let it steal a geometry problem.

## What I build now

Paired drawers in a wide case get a center runner as part of the web, both edges grooved if dustboards meet there, a guide on top that I wax. A single wide drawer gets the same if the opening is a blanket. A narrow stack gets two side runners and I save the hanger.

I put two pulls on a wide front when the design will stand it. Two pulls are an instruction. They are not a guarantee. I still put the third road under a two-pull blanket drawer. People are loyal to the near cup.

I do not fix rack with more taper. A taper can make the start easier and the diamond sloppier. The crutch essay already said that. I do not fix rack with more wax. Wax on two unequal roads is a faster diamond.

A shop cabinet can cheat with a hard center slip screwed to the case bottom and a groove in the drawer. It is ugly from below. It works. A sitting-room case should hide the third road in the web like it belongs there, because it does.

## Failures

I built a walnut blanket drawer with one cup pull and two oak runners and no center because the interior looked cleaner without a muntin. It racked on the second week. I crawled in from the back and hung a runner with screws in slots. It works. It looks like a repair, because it is one. The next blanket drawer got the muntin in the first glue-up. Clean is not a structure.

I also blamed July for a file that had been racking since March. I know this because the scar was already pale when I saw it in the thunderstorm season. I had written “seasonal” on the traveler like a man reaching for a familiar hat. I wrote “center guide” under it and I did the guide. The scar stayed. The sound went away.

A third: I tapered Hayward’s center guide the wrong way — narrower at the back — and created the pinch he warned against. The drawers racked as they closed. I recut the guide. Wider at the rear, or parallel. Never a funnel that tightens at the end.

## What this is not

This is not a humidity table. This is not the kicker essay, though a diving wide drawer will rack as it dives. This is not kitchen-slide history. Slides are a later contract that hid the third road inside steel. This is the wooden wide box, and the refusal to let weather take the blame for a missing track.

It is also not a demand that every pencil drawer grow a muntin. Narrow boxes forgive. Wide boxes do not.

The judgment is small. If the front is wide enough for a one-handed lie, put something in the middle that the lie has to obey. A center runner. A guide. Two pulls if you want, and still the runner. File drawers are the same sentence in a louder voice. When a corner hits, look at the parallelogram before you look at the sky.
