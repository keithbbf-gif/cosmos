## Dating and trade

- **[The Lid That Would Not Leave](drafts/01-the-lid-that-would-not-leave.md)** — `the-lid-that-would-not-leave` — 1403 words  
  A drawer starts as a till inside a chest. The lid stays on the language long after the case has filled with sliding boxes. Mid-17th-century English work is when the pretense finally drops.

- **[A Groove That Rides the Runner](drafts/02-a-groove-that-rides-the-runner.md)** — `a-groove-that-rides-the-runner` — 1353 words  
  Early drawers often hung from their sides. A groove in the side boarded a runner fixed to the carcase. It looks backwards until you open a 17th-century case and feel the hang.

- **[Through Tails Under the Veneer](drafts/03-through-tails-under-the-veneer.md)** — `through-tails-under-the-veneer` — 1361 words  
  Second-half 17th-century walnut often meant a deal front, a coarse through dovetail, and veneer asked to hide the end grain. The joint comes back as a telegraph. Hayward already drew the scar.

- **[London Priced the Slip](drafts/04-london-priced-the-slip.md)** — `london-priced-the-slip` — 1378 words  
  A slip is a billed operation before it is a romance. The 1788 London Book of Prices has a line for slipping drawers. Piecework is how a city trade remembers what the bench already knew.

- **[A Scallop You Can Date](drafts/05-a-scallop-you-can-date.md)** — `a-scallop-you-can-date` — 1699 words  
  A Knapp pin-and-cove is a North American factory clock you can feel with a thumbnail. Presence is a terminus post quem in the early 1870s. Absence proves nothing.

- **[The Machine That Wanted to Look Hand](drafts/06-the-machine-that-wanted-to-look-hand.md)** — `the-machine-that-wanted-to-look-hand` — 1636 words  
  Machine-cut dovetails around 1895 and after are a dating clock because they are even. Symmetry is a year-class, not a virtue. A hand tail can still be cut on a Tuesday.

- **[A Bead Around the Front](drafts/07-a-bead-around-the-front.md)** — `a-bead-around-the-front` — 1630 words  
  Cockbeading is 18th-century edge armor that throws a shadow. It sits in a rebate at the sides and bottom, later a full-width bead at the top. It is not a kitchen overlay lip.

- **[Country Pins and Town Pins](drafts/08-country-pins-and-town-pins.md)** — `country-pins-and-town-pins` — 1647 words  
  Pin size is time and money. Fine London pins and chunky country pins are two payrolls, not two virtues. Spacing is shop practice, not a moral.

## The box

- **[Why the Front Hides the Joint](drafts/09-why-the-front-hides-the-joint.md)** — `why-the-front-hides-the-joint` — 1576 words  
  Half-blind dovetails are the drawer-front religion: the show face stays a skin. Through tails belong at the back. The joint is allowed to be loud where no one sits.

- **[Tails Live on the Sides](drafts/10-tails-live-on-the-sides.md)** — `tails-live-on-the-sides` — 1487 words  
  Tails belong on the drawer sides and pins on the front so a pull cannot strip the face off the box. Orientation is the joint. Reverse it and the handle writes a failure.

- **[A Slip That Widens the Wear](drafts/11-a-slip-that-widens-the-wear.md)** — `a-slip-that-widens-the-wear` — 1605 words  
  An English slip is architecture: extra wood where the drawer meets the runner, a groove that does not cut the side in half. Hayward and the price-book witnesses are related, not identical. Hedge the century.

- **[A Groove in Thicker Sides](drafts/12-a-groove-in-thicker-sides.md)** — `a-groove-in-thicker-sides` — 1552 words  
  American drawers more often plough the bottom groove in a thicker side. Greene’s 3/8 to 1/2 inch is the published meat. The slip stayed English because the lining stayed thin.

- **[Bottoms Run Side to Side](drafts/13-bottoms-run-side-to-side.md)** — `bottoms-run-side-to-side` — 1651 words  
  Early drawer bottoms often ran back to front and split on the short span. Late-18th-century habit turns the grain side to side, grooved in the sides, free at the back. Hayward names the change. Hoadley explains the move.

- **[The Bevel on a Thick Bottom](drafts/14-the-bevel-on-a-thick-bottom.md)** — `the-bevel-on-a-thick-bottom` — 1548 words  
  A solid drawer bottom can be thick in the field and skinny at the rim. The bevel is a ticket into a groove, not a door panel with a different job. Eighteenth-century boxes used the trick because a thin board throughout is a floor that drums.

- **[Glue Blocks Under Boston](drafts/15-glue-blocks-under-boston.md)** — `glue-blocks-under-boston` — 1547 words  
  John and Thomas Seymour’s Boston drawers, in Mussey’s account, put bottoms in grooves and then added kerfed glue blocks underneath, planed flush as wear pads. The block is a slip’s cousin. It can also lock the floor and split it.

- **[The Back Is Allowed to Be Pine](drafts/16-the-back-is-allowed-to-be-pine.md)** — `the-back-is-allowed-to-be-pine` — 1515 words  
  Drawer backs run thinner than fronts, take through dovetails or a dado, and are often pine or poplar on purpose. The bottom passes under them or in a groove. Movement lives at the back. Humble is the job.

- **[Secondary Wood Is Not a Slight](drafts/17-secondary-wood-is-not-a-slight.md)** — `secondary-wood-is-not-a-slight` — 1524 words  
  The room sees mahogany, walnut, cherry, pecan. The linings are poplar, pine, oak, chestnut — English deal when the trade said deal. Secondary wood is a job, not a apology. The show is allowed to be a different species than the box.

- **[How Thin the Old Sides Were](drafts/18-how-thin-the-old-sides-were.md)** — `how-thin-the-old-sides-were` — 1443 words  
  Greene puts ordinary American 18th-century drawer sides at 3/8 to 1/2 inch. English sides often ran thinner because a slip carried the groove. Modern hobby boxes at 1/2 to 5/8 are frequently overbuilt. Thin is not weak if the corners are tails.

- **[A Nailed Rabbet Is a Clock](drafts/19-a-nailed-rabbet-is-a-clock.md)** — `a-nailed-rabbet-is-a-clock` — 1490 words  
  Nailed rabbets turn up in early crude boxes, later country work, and later cheap factory drawers. The joint is not automatically bad. It is a clock that never fully stopped, so you read it with other hands — nail type, species, the rest of the case.

- **[Box Joints in the Shop Drawer](drafts/20-box-joints-in-the-shop-drawer.md)** — `box-joints-in-the-shop-drawer` — 1505 words  
  Finger joints show up on shop drawers, tool chests, some campaign cousins, and the machine era that could cut them even. They are strong and they are visible. They are not period parlor jewelry, and they should not be asked to impersonate an 18th-century half-blind.

- **[Hide Glue Lets You Open It](drafts/21-hide-glue-lets-you-open-it.md)** — `hide-glue-lets-you-open-it` — 1448 words  
  Hide glue is the drawer shop’s reversible contract. Heat and moisture will open a slip, a block, a starved tail. PVA under a loaded bottom can creep. Do not turn a pot into a number. The religion is whether you can get back in.

- **[The Baseline Is the Joint](drafts/22-the-baseline-is-the-joint.md)** — `the-baseline-is-the-joint` — 1512 words  
  A marking gauge set to the thickness of the mate is the whole religion. The line you chop to is that thickness, not a number you liked on Tuesday. Pins stand proud of the line so you can plane them. Miss the line and you have missed the box.

- **[Pins First Is a Religion](drafts/23-pins-first-is-a-religion.md)** — `pins-first-is-a-religion` — 1652 words  
  Hand work usually cuts tails first and transfers. Some traditions and most jigs cut pins first. A jig is a church. Sequence is not a moral score.

- **[One-to-Six Is Not a Law](drafts/24-one-to-six-is-not-a-law.md)** — `one-to-six-is-not-a-law` — 1639 words  
  1:6 in softwood and 1:8 in hardwood is 20th-century shop-class. Eighteenth-century slopes vary, often steeper, often just visual. The angle is a choice with a failure mode.

- **[When a Dado Is Enough](drafts/32-when-a-dado-is-enough.md)** — `when-a-dado-is-enough` — 1631 words  
  A drawer back may live in a housing. The joint is allowed to be quiet. Through tails are not mandatory at the rear. The dado fails when the bottom is asked to be the only lock, or when the slot is a rumor.

- **[A Router Bit Named Drawer Lock](drafts/33-a-router-bit-named-drawer-lock.md)** — `a-router-bit-named-drawer-lock` — 1492 words  
  A drawer-lock bit cuts a locking rabbet: production joinery with a name on the shank. It is a 20th-century kitchen joint. It is strong enough for pots. It is not 1780, and staining it will not make it so.

- **[Plywood Bottoms Are a Contract](drafts/34-plywood-bottoms-are-a-contract.md)** — `plywood-bottoms-are-a-contract` — 1619 words  
  A plywood floor moves like a panel, not like a board. You may glue it. You must say so. A solid bottom is not morally better. Kitchens and sitting rooms sign different weather.

- **[Cedar in the Bottom](drafts/35-cedar-in-the-bottom.md)** — `cedar-in-the-bottom` — 1448 words  
  Aromatic red cedar smells like a moth story. It is still a board. Glue it all around and it splits. The smell fades. A lining is not a spell.

## The case and the fit

- **[Web Frames and Dust](drafts/25-web-frames-and-dust.md)** — `web-frames-and-dust` — 1653 words  
  Dustboards and web frames do two jobs: they keep the lower drawer from wearing the upper drawer’s grit, and they keep the case from becoming a racking box. English high style often used full panels. Hayward’s runners are stub-tenoned and slot-screwed so a solid end can still move.

- **[Kickers, Runners, Stops](drafts/26-kickers-runners-stops.md)** — `kickers-runners-stops` — 1659 words  
  Three woods keep a drawer honest: a runner under the side, a kicker above so the box does not dive, and stops so flush is flush and the back does not blow out. Side-hung already has its own essay — a nod, not a retelling.

- **[Plane to the Opening](drafts/27-plane-to-the-opening.md)** — `plane-to-the-opening` — 1611 words  
  Build the box fat. Shoot the sides and the top to the hole that exists. A tape measure is a rumor. Period drawers were made to openings, and each opening is its own.

- **[The Taper Is a Crutch](drafts/28-the-taper-is-a-crutch.md)** — `the-taper-is-a-crutch` — 1551 words  
  A drawer a little narrower at the back starts easier. The same taper can hide a twisted case. One of those is craft. The other is a lie the next shop will have to unwind.

- **[Wax, Not the Spray](drafts/29-wax-not-the-spray.md)** — `wax-not-the-spray` — 1525 words  
  Paraffin, beeswax, a candle heel — those are the old lubricants. Silicone spray gums. Some oils oxidize into a future bind. Graphite has a place and it is dirty. This is the lubricant essay, not the clearance essay.

- **[Wide Drawers Rack](drafts/30-wide-drawers-rack.md)** — `wide-drawers-rack` — 1569 words  
  A wide box is a parallelogram waiting for a one-handed pull. People call the corner hit humidity. It is geometry. Center guides and center runners are the old answer. File drawers make the argument loud.

- **[Quartersawn Sides Stay Calmer](drafts/31-quartersawn-sides-stay-calmer.md)** — `quartersawn-sides-stay-calmer` — 1616 words  
  A drawer side’s height is the cross-grain. Quartersawn oak or walnut moves less in that width. It is not magic. It costs more, and the ray fleck is a show when the drawer is open.

- **[Seasonal Bind Is a Drawing Error](drafts/36-seasonal-bind-is-a-drawing-error.md)** — `seasonal-bind-is-a-drawing-error` — 1590 words  
  A drawer that swells into its hole was specified that way. Clearance, grain, and a glued floor are drawing decisions. Weather is the auditor. It is not the author.

## Traditions and hardware

- **[Shaker Means the Knob and the Fit](drafts/37-shaker-means-the-knob-and-the-fit.md)** — `shaker-means-the-knob-and-the-fit` — 1490 words  
  Shaker drawers are a habit: half-blind boxes, a wooden knob, usually no cockbead, pine or cherry or maple, and a fit you can live with every day. Museum catalogs are the witness. A font is not.

- **[Federal Liked a Thin Wall](drafts/38-federal-liked-a-thin-wall.md)** — `federal-liked-a-thin-wall` — 1452 words  
  Hepplewhite and Sheraton, and the American Federal shops that read them, built drawers as trays: mahogany show, thin linings, a cockbead, inlays on a light case. Thin is the style. It is also the engineering.

- **[Arts and Crafts Shows the Tails](drafts/39-arts-and-crafts-shows-the-tails.md)** — `arts-and-crafts-shows-the-tails` — 1505 words  
  Stickley-era through tails on a drawer side are honesty theater and a real joint. Quartersawn white oak is the other picture. Do not sneer at the theater. Do not worship the pin.

- **[Campaign Drawers That Travel](drafts/40-campaign-drawers-that-travel.md)** — `campaign-drawers-that-travel` — 1550 words  
  A campaign drawer is a box that has to survive a ship: flush pulls, brass corners and straps, locks, teak or mahogany, a case that comes apart. The hardware is luggage. The lining still has to be a lining.

- **[Tansu Pegs, Not Tails](drafts/41-tansu-pegs-not-tails.md)** — `tansu-pegs-not-tails` — 1643 words  
  A tansu drawer is often a pegged rebate at the front, a pegged finger at the back, and a bottom glued and pinned to the box. Kiri linings, bamboo or wood pegs, splits in the floor. It is not a failed European dovetail.

- **[French Dovetail Is a Kitchen Word](drafts/42-french-dovetail-is-a-kitchen-word.md)** — `french-dovetail-is-a-kitchen-word` — 1523 words  
  In the American kitchen trade, a French dovetail is a sliding dovetail: the side runs into a long socket in the front. It is a machine joint with a catalog name. It is not French cabinetmaking, and it is not a secret miter.

- **[Kitchen Slides Changed the Box](drafts/43-kitchen-slides-changed-the-box.md)** — `kitchen-slides-changed-the-box` — 1474 words  
  A catalog slide writes the box: width, side thickness, notch, hook. Blum starts as horseshoe studs in 1952; roller runners and TANDEM come later on Blum’s own timeline. Accuride comes out of industrial slides. Wood runners are not obsolete. They are a different product.

- **[Undermount Hides the Runner](drafts/44-undermount-hides-the-runner.md)** — `undermount-hides-the-runner` — 1584 words  
  A concealed slide puts the steel under the box so the customer sees wood. The box still owes squareness, a spec thickness, and locking devices. Soft-close is a damper. What the runner hides is itself. What it reveals is a crooked case.

- **[Lipped, Overlay, Inset](drafts/45-lipped-overlay-inset.md)** — `lipped-overlay-inset` — 1642 words  
  A drawer front meets the rail three ways: flush in the hole, a rabbeted lip that covers the gap, or a front that sits on the frame. Period work, American country, and the 20th-century kitchen each picked a meeting. Dating follows the meeting. So does the shop.

- **[Secret Drawers Were for Paper](drafts/46-secret-drawers-were-for-paper.md)** — `secret-drawers-were-for-paper` — 1605 words  
  An 18th-century writing desk hid paper before it hid a party trick. Wells, false backs, springs, and tills are cousins: a box that does not announce itself. Do not invent a famous desk. Do not sell a gimmick as the first purpose.

- **[One Key and the Wardrobe Bolt](drafts/47-one-key-and-the-wardrobe-bolt.md)** — `one-key-and-the-wardrobe-bolt` — 1517 words  
  A half-mortise lock lives in the front the way a pin wall does: you cut a home for it. Escutcheons keep the keyhole from becoming a wound. One lock can shoot bolts into other drawers or a door. The lock is joinery’s neighbor.

- **[Pulls Are Load Paths](drafts/48-pulls-are-load-paths.md)** — `pulls-are-load-paths` — 1515 words  
  A bail, a drop, a wood knob, a cup, a flush campaign pull: each one is a handle bolted to a front that is already a pin wall. The front is an abutment. Screws have a length. Pulls fail as load paths, not as jewelry.
