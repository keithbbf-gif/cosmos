# Bibliography

Primary shop paper and the monographs the essays lean on. Popular price guides are listed under Collecting, not as 1901 evidence.

## Periodicals and catalogs

- Gustav Stickley, ed. *The Craftsman*. Eastwood / New York, October 1901–December 1916.
- Irene Sargent, essays in *The Craftsman*, especially vol. 1 (1901–02).
- United Crafts / Craftsman Workshops catalogs, 1901–12 (reprints: Turn of the Century Editions; Dover selections).
- L. & J.G. Stickley, Onondaga Shops / Handcraft catalogs.
- Stickley Brothers Company catalogs, Grand Rapids.
- Charles P. Limbert Co. catalogs, Grand Rapids and Holland, Michigan.
- *Furniture World*; *The Furniture Record* (Grand Rapids).
- *The Artsman* (Rose Valley).
- *The Upholsterer and Interior Decorator*; *House and Garden* (McHugh notices).
- Sears, Roebuck catalogs, Mission suites, c. 1906–18.
- Larkin Company premium catalogs, Buffalo.

## Stickley and Syracuse

- David Cathers, *Furniture of the American Arts and Crafts Movement* (several eds.; New York: New American Library / later Philmont).
- David Cathers, *Gustav Stickley* (London: Phaidon, 2003).
- Mary Ann Smith, *Gustav Stickley: The Craftsman* (Syracuse: Syracuse University Press, 1983).
- Kevin W. Tucker et al., *Gustav Stickley and the American Arts and Crafts Movement* (Dallas Museum of Art / Yale, 2010).
- Mark Alan Hewitt, *Gustav Stickley’s Craftsman Farms: The Quest for an Arts and Crafts Utopia* (Syracuse: Syracuse University Press, 2001).
- Winterthur Library, Joseph Downs Collection, Gustav Stickley papers (COL 60).
- Stickley Museum at Craftsman Farms, interpretive history (Morris Plains, N.J.).

## Mission as a name; McHugh; New York shops

- Anna Tobin D’Ambrosio, *The Distinction of Being Different: Joseph P. McHugh and the American Arts and Crafts Movement* (Utica: Munson-Williams-Proctor Institute, 1993).
- Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum, Joseph P. McHugh Company biography.
- Robert W. Clark, ed., *The Arts and Crafts Movement in America 1876–1916* (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1972).

## Movement surveys

- Wendy Kaplan, *The Art that is Life: The Arts and Crafts Movement in America, 1875–1920* (Boston: Museum of Fine Arts, 1987).
- Wendy Kaplan, *The Arts & Crafts Movement in Europe & America: Design for the Modern World* (Los Angeles: LACMA / Thames & Hudson, 2004).
- Leslie Greene Bowman, *American Arts and Crafts: Virtue in Design* (Los Angeles: LACMA, 1990).
- Eileen Boris, *Art and Labor: Ruskin, Morris, and the Craftsman Ideal in America* (Philadelphia: Temple University Press, 1986).
- Coy L. Ludwig, *The Arts and Crafts Movement in New York State, 1890s–1920s* (Hamilton: Gallery Association, 1983).
- Kenneth R. Trapp et al., *The Arts and Crafts Movement in California: Living the Good Life* (New York: Abbeville / Oakland Museum, 1993).
- Robert W. Winter, *The California Bungalow* (Los Angeles: Hennessey & Ingalls, 1980).

## Shops, colonies, Prairie

- Joseph Cunningham, *The Artistic Furniture of Charles Rohlfs* (New Haven: Yale University Press / American Decorative Art 1900 Foundation, 2008).
- Edward R. Bosley, *Greene & Greene* (London: Phaidon, 2000).
- Randell L. Makinson, *Greene & Greene: Furniture and Related Designs* (Salt Lake City: Peregrine Smith, 1979).
- Edward R. Bosley, *Greene and Greene: The Gamble House* (London: Phaidon / Gamble House literature).
- Nancy E. Green et al., *Byrdcliffe: An American Arts and Crafts Colony* (Ithaca: Cornell / Herbert F. Johnson Museum, 2004).
- William Lee Carter / Rose Valley literature; *The Artsman* reprints.
- David A. Hanks, *The Decorative Designs of Frank Lloyd Wright* (New York: Dutton, 1979).
- Don Marek and Grand Rapids Public Museum literature on Limbert, Stickley Brothers, Lifetime.
- Robert Koch and later Roycroft monographs; Elbert Hubbard, *The Roycroft Books* and Inn literature.
- Dard Hunter, *My Life with Paper* (New York: Knopf, 1958) and Roycroft period work.

## English sources America actually read

- John Ruskin, *The Stones of Venice* (1851–53), “The Nature of Gothic.”
- William Morris, lectures and Morris & Co. catalogs (as imported argument, not as the American shop).
- Charles L. Eastlake, *Hints on Household Taste* (London, 1868; Boston: James R. Osgood, 1872).
- C. R. Ashbee and M. H. Baillie Scott, as cited in *The Craftsman* and in Ellis-period inlay.

## Collecting and afterlife

- Tod M. Volpe and Beth Cathers, *Treasures of the American Arts and Crafts Movement, 1890–1920* (New York: Abrams, 1988).
- Bruce E. Johnson, identification and price guides (use as evidence of the 1980s market).
- Auction catalogs, 1970s–90s (Jordan-Volpe; later Christie's / Sotheby's Arts and Crafts sales).
