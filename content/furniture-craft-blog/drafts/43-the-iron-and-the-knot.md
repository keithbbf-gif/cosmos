The plane iron finds the knot and the sound changes — a tap, a shine, a little spark if you are romantic and the knot is hard enough. I stop. I look. Is this knot tight, a swirl of living wood, or is it a loose dark plug with a black ring that will fall out in a dry month and leave a hole the size of a thumb?

A tight knot can stay in a rustic top, a painted cupboard, a mill-grade stile that will be the back. A loose knot in a dining field is a hole I will be gluing a plug into, or a board I will turn into parts. I do not “stabilize” a loose knot with a river of thin CA and then sell the table as a calm object. The CA is a glass around a pebble. The pebble still wants to leave.

## Tight, loose, the ring

The black ring is often a bark inclusion or a check. I press. If it moves, it goes. If it does not move and the design can stand it, I seal it. Shellac on a pine knot is old medicine for pitch. It helps. A hot window will still test it. I do not put a pitchy pine knot over a sofa in a sunroom and then talk about character when the pitch yellows the finish.

Oak knots are usually tighter, darker, a different hardness that will telegraph under a film as a bump if you sand the field and leave the knot high. I level. I do not dish the field to make the knot flush by erasure.

## Design around, or design with

A knot at the edge of a board can disappear in a rip. A knot in the middle of a wide cathedral is the cathedral’s punctuation. I decide at the glue-up, not at the finish. If the set of boards is formal, the knot is on the underside or in the firewood. If the set is a farm table that wants to be a farm table, I keep one or two that are tight and I do not keep a crowd.

A cluster of knots is a branch collar. It can be handsome in a slab. It can be a weak place in a rail. I do not put a collar in a chair rail and then ask the rail to take a rack.

## Plugs, butterflies, the theater

A butterfly across a check is a valid mechanical and a valid look. A row of butterflies on a slab that is not checking is a costume. I put them where the wood asked, or I do not put them.

A plug in a loose-knot hole, grain aligned, same species, slightly proud then flushed: a repair. A plug in a contrasting species in a circle on a formal top: a spot. I ask before I spot.

Epoxy in a knot hole: fill, then a color if you must, then a film. It can be the right repair on a painted piece or a bar top. It is a plastic window on a clear oak dining table. I say that before I mix.

## Working past it

A plane can chip out at the trailing edge of a knot. I take a lighter cut, I scrape, I come from the other direction. A saw will dull. A drill will walk. I punch a start. I do not let a bit skate into a field.

Sanding a knot: the paper loads, then a grit comet. I clean the paper. I do not grind the knot into a crater.

## Mill grade and furniture grade

A mill will allow knots in grades that furniture sales tags pretend do not exist. Common oak is full of them. You can make good furniture from Common if you cut them out of the show and leave them in the back. That is yield, not shame. A shop that buys FAS to avoid thinking will pay the yard to think.

This county’s hardwood story includes furniture stock and also boards that were never going to be a piano. I use both. I do not put the piano knot in the piano.

## Finish

Seal, then the system. A knot will take stain darker, usually. I accept it or I washcoat. I do not try to make a knot disappear with a toner and then act surprised when a hand rubs the toner off the bump.

Oil will show every ring. Film will show the bump. Paint will show the ridge if you did not prime and sand. None of them make a loose knot tight.

## A plug in the gravy story

A loose knot in a dining field is a hole. I do not stabilize it with a glass of CA and sell calm. I plug, grain aligned, or I recut the board into parts. A tight knot at the edge can disappear in a rip. A cluster in a chair rail is a weak place. I do not ask a collar to take a rack.

Shellac on a pine knot helps pitch. A hot window will still test it. I do not put a pitchy knot over a sofa in a sunroom and then talk about character when the finish yellows.

## Butterflies that were not asked for

A row of butterflies on a slab that is not checking is a costume. I put them where the wood asked. A contrasting plug on a formal top is a spot. I ask before I spot. Common oak is full of knots; I cut them out of the show and leave them in the back. That is yield. The iron’s tap is the first diagnosis. A nick from a mineral-hard knot prints a stripe. I hone before I print.

## The iron, again

I hone after a knot that was mineral-hard. The nick will print a stripe down the rest of the board. I have printed the stripe. The stone is faster than the shame.

Then I decide, one last time, with the board on the bench in the light: is this knot a face? If I have to talk myself into it, it is a back. The face is for the room. The room did not ask for a speech about a branch. It asked for a table that will not lose a plug into a gravy boat.

I keep a board with a beautiful tight knot in the rack for a painted door or a shop stool. I do not keep it for a mystery. The mystery is how often a knot looks like destiny on the pile and like a mistake on the stand. The iron already knew. I try to listen at the first tap, not at the last coat.

A plane will chip the trailing edge of a tight knot if I am a hero. I take a lighter cut, I scrape, I come from the other side. Sandpaper loads on a knot and then throws a comet. I clean the paper. I do not grind the knot into a crater and call the crater character. Oil will show every ring. Paint will show the ridge if I did not prime. None of them make a loose knot stay.
