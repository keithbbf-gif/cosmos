The first thing is the smell. Not rotten — people who say rotten have not stood over a clean pot. It is animal and sweet and a little like a barn that someone swept. The granules have sat in cold water until they look like brown ice. The hot plate is on low. You do not boil this. Boil it and you have cooked the strength out and you will still get a glue line that looks like you tried.

I brush a thin coat on both cheeks of a tenon that already fits. The pot is right when the glue sheets off the brush and does not drip like tea. Open time is short. That is the point. You are not building a margin for a phone call. You are assembling a joint you dry-fit an hour ago.

## What hide is, without the folklore

Hide glue is collagen. You buy it in grams of bloom strength — 192, 251, the higher numbers setting harder and faster, the lower numbers a little more forgiving on veneer. [VERIFY] the number on the bag you actually opened. Liquid hide in a bottle has urea in it to keep it liquid; it is hide glue that has been talked into a longer open time and a weaker, more convenient life. I use the liquid for small repairs and for people who will not watch a pot. I use the pot when the joint matters and the shop is already warm.

Hot hide does not creep the way a thick PVA line creeps. A table apron under rack, a chair rail, a mortise that will see a season — I want a glue that stays a glass, not a rubber. PVA is strong. I am not going to pretend it is not. It is also a pain to reverse, and it can cold-creep in a joint that was a little fat. Hide’s strength in a well-fit joint is not the weak link. The wood is.

Reversibility is not a hobbyist luxury. Chairs loosen. Tabletops get a new breadboard. A restoration shop that inherits your work in 2040 will bless you or invent new curses. Heat, moisture, patience — hide lets go. The tenon survives. That is seriousness, not nostalgia. The nostalgia is the other way: imagining a glued-forever chair in a climate that does not exist.

## The pot is a tool

A cheap Crock-Pot will work if you water-bath a jar and you do not wander above the range the granules want. A real glue pot with a thermostat is better because you will forget. [VERIFY] a temperature against the supplier card; the usual shop talk is roughly 140–150°F and I will not engrave that on a wall without the card.

Fresh mix for the day. Yesterday’s pot can be reheated a time or two if it was covered and not scummed. When it smells sharp or looks stringy in the wrong way, throw it out. Glue is cheaper than a failed table.

Size the end grain if you are doing something that depends on end grain, which you should try not to. A thin wash, dry, then the real coat. On cheeks, one even coat. Starved joints come from glue that was too hot and too thin, or from a clamp that squeezed the joint dry because the fit was already sloppy.

Clamp pressure: enough to close. Hide does not want to be crushed out of a gap you should not have. If you need a clamp to pull a 1/8-inch daylight, stop and pare.

## Cleanup is part of the joint

A damp, hot rag takes hide off a show surface if you are paying attention. Dried hide on a profile is a chisel and a swear. Inside a mortise, leftover skin is a thickness you did not plan. I dry-fit, I mark, I glue, I assemble, I wipe, I check square, I walk away. The walk-away matters. Poking a setting joint is how you get a shoulder that was closed and is now a hair open.

In winter the glue gels on the brush while you are still reaching for a clamp. Warm the parts. A sun lamp, a warm room, a rub with a cloth. Cold oak will gel the film into a skin that is not a glue line. Summer in a south Arkansas shop goes the other way: open time shrinks, you move faster, you do not take the call.

## Where I will not use it

Exterior. Anything that will sit in a wet sink line. A butcher-block oil-and-water life unless the design already accepts maintenance. Hide is not waterproof. There are formulas and there are myths. I do not put hide on a garden bench and then talk about craft.

I also do not use hide to fill a bad dovetail. That is what people remember when they say the glue is weak. The glue was asked to be wood.

Epoxy has a place in a split that must become one piece and never come apart — outdoor, structural, a repair where the original wood is gone. It is a different religion. Mixing religions in one chair is how you get a joint that will not knock down and a joint that will, side by side, and a future person who does not know which is which.

## The repair bench is the argument

A side chair comes in, front rail loose, three dowels sheared, PVA in the holes like white fossil. We drill, we pick, we steam, we still lose wood. A chair from a shop that used hide and pins comes apart on purpose. You heat the joint, you tap, you scrape to wood, you see the chalk marks of the last person if they were kind. You glue it again. The customer gets a chair, not a sculpture of the problem.

Mill towns knew both glues. Hide is older in the trade than the white bottle. The bottle won the twentieth century because it was easy and because production stopped expecting the same person to repair the piece. I am not interested in reversing the century. I am interested in the pieces we still sign.

If a Wilmar job is PVA because the line is PVA, the honest move is pins and a fit that would have worked with hide. The dishonest move is a press-fit of glue and hope.

## Small practices that keep the pot honest

A brush that is only the glue brush. A lid. A note on the jar of the mix date. No sawdust in the pot — obvious, and still it happens. No “just a little water” five times until you have soup. Test on a scrap pair of cheeks if the mix is new to you that week.

For veneer, hide is still the king of the hammer and the iron. You can work a blister. You can reverse a mistake. PVA veneer work is a one-way door with a nicer smell.

I do not tell a dining-room customer a glue lecture unless they ask. They ask when they have already owned a chair that failed. Then I show them a tenon with old brown on it, clean wood under the scrape, and the same tenon going home again. That demonstration beats a paragraph about tradition. The pot is on. The brush sheets. The joint has about a minute to tell the truth.
