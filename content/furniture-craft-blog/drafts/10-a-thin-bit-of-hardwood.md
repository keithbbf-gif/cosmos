A miter is a pretty lie: two pieces of long grain meeting as if they had always been a corner, end grain tucked out of sight. Glue on a miter is mostly end grain talking to end grain. End grain drinks and does not hold. The corner opens in a dry month, a proud hairline, and then a little more, and someone says the finish failed. The finish did not fail. The joint was a handshake with no hands.

A spline is a thin bit of hardwood in two slots, long grain running across the miter so the glue has something to be glue on. You can hide it. You can show it as a stripe. I show it when the box can stand a stripe. I hide it when the room wanted a quiet corner. I do not skip it on a miter I expect to last.

## The slot is the work

A table-saw slot, a router slot, a dedicated spline jig that keeps your fingers. The slot is centered or it is biased toward the inside so a small error does not blow through the show face. On thick case stock you can run a loose tenon or a pair of splines. On a 3/4-inch box, one spline of 1/8 or 1/4 is the usual conversation. [VERIFY] the house cutter.

The spline’s grain runs *across* the corner, the same way a biscuit pretends to, except a spline can be as long as the miter and as thick as you have meat. A biscuit is short and fat and lives where the plate joiner put it. Fine for a carcase that will be screwed anyway. Not what I want on a jewelry-box corner that is the whole structure.

Feather splines in a saw kerf after the box is glued — the decorative keys you see on a cutting board — are partly show and partly a mechanical assist. They help. They are not a substitute for a spline that was in the joint when it went together, not if the box is tall and empty and will take a rack.

## Miters that I still cut

Picture frames, small lids, a plinth, a cabinet crown that is nailed and glued to a carcass that is the real structure. A miter on a piece of millwork that is going to be painted and caulked is a painter’s joint. I still like a spline if I can get one, because paint does not hold wood.

I do not miter a table apron as the primary joint and then wait. That apron wants a tenon into a leg. A mitered apron with a hidden tenon or a bridle behind it is a different, better object — the miter is the look, the tenon is the job.

Compound miters on a crown are millwork. The spline becomes a pain and the nailing becomes the practice. I will not pretend a 16-foot run of crown is furniture joinery. It is layout, cope, and paint. (The cope essay is elsewhere. The cope is the joint that looks like a miter and behaves like a shoulder.)

## Fit, glue, clamp

Miters slip. A band clamp, a dedicated miter clamp, a jig that holds the box as a box. Dry-fit until the seams shut and the box is not a rhombus. Glue in the slots and on the faces, not a hero’s amount on the end grain that will squeeze into a bead you will sand into a round corner.

If the miter opens after finish, the air got to the end grain and the wood lost moisture. A spline slows the opening; it does not repeal the handbook. Finish the inside of the box. An unfinished interior and a film exterior is a cupping recipe in miniature.

## Contrast and the temptation to be cute

A walnut spline in maple is handsome. A thick contrasting spline on a small box can look like a racing stripe. I keep the spline thin enough that it reads as a joint, not as an inlay that wanted a different project. Same-species splines disappear and do the work. I use those more than the photographs suggest.

Plywood splines are stable and I use them in painted work without shame. A plywood stripe in a clear oak corner is a look I have to be talked into.

## When a dovetail is the real answer

A box that will be picked up, dropped, asked to be a stool for a second — dovetails. A mitered box is a visual. It can be strong enough. It will never be the same kind of strong. I do not sell a mitered blanket chest as if it were a dovetailed one. The customer can have the look. They should hear the difference in one sentence, not a brochure.

Lock miters from a shaper cutter are a production answer with real glue surface. They are a kind of built-in spline. A good lock miter on a painted speaker cabinet is a good joint. A bad lock miter is a gap that follows the profile like a river.

## Boxes I have opened later

A mitered box with no spline, six years on, lid still pretty, corners open enough to take a fingernail. The owner thought the finish had “crazed.” The finish was following a joint that had never been a joint. I ran a kerf and a feather after the fact. It helped. It did not make the box a dovetailed box. I told them so. They kept the box. They did not ask me to make the next one the same way.

A lock-miter speaker cabinet, painted, still tight. That joint had glue surface. The paint was allowed. I respect that cabinet more than the pretty jewelry box with the open miters.

## Grain at the corner

A miter folds the grain around the corner if the two faces were neighbors on the board. That match is the reason people love miters. It is also why a spline of contrasting wood can interrupt a match you paid for. Same-species spline, thin, when the match is the point. Contrast spline when the box is a plane of one color and can stand a line.

End grain in a miter still drinks finish. I seal the ends before I assemble if I am staining, or I accept two darker edges. I have accepted them by accident. I do not accept them by accident anymore.

## Shop rhythm

I cut all four miters from a sled that I trust that day. I do not trust last month’s sled without a test cut. Ninety degrees on a box is four corners adding up. One 89-degree cut is a gap that migrates.

Then slots. Then splines planed to a tap. Then a dry box sitting on the bench while I look at it from the chair in the corner, because the chair in the corner is closer to how a room looks at a box. If a corner is proud, I sneak it now. Finish will not sneak it for me.

Wilmar builds cases that have to sit in air conditioning and in lake houses and in a guest room that gets closed. The spline is cheap insurance. The expensive move is a miter you admire on Friday and resend on a warranty in October. I have done the warranty. I prefer the thin bit of hardwood.
