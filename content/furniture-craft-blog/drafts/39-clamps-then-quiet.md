The dry run takes longer than the glue. I want every clamp on a pad, every caul bowed the right way, the square in the corner, the diagonal strings or the tape measure ready, the glue brush wet in a cup, the rag, the person if I need a person. Then I take it apart and I do it again with glue. If I am hunting a clamp during the open time, I have already lost a shoulder.

A glue-up is not a chance to persuade a bad tenon. It is a chance to hold a good one while the glue becomes the thin thing it should be. Hungry clamps starve a joint and bow a panel. Lazy clamps leave a daylight you will call “clamp pressure” later, when you mean regret.

## Rehearsal

I assemble the base without glue and I pick it up, if I can. I rack it. I look at the shoulders. I mark the clamps’ positions with a pencil on the bench so I am not composing under a clock. I have a tray for the pins if we are pinning. I have the mallet where my hand will be.

On a panel glue-up, I joint the edges that day, I dry-align the figure, I mark triangles across the joints, I decide which face is up. I do not glue a panel I have not seen in raking light. The light finds a ridge that the hand called flush.

## Glue as a clock

Hide is a short clock. PVA is longer and still not lunch. Epoxy is a different play, a pot life, a clamp that can be gentler because the gap-filling is the point — and the gap should still not be the point.

I butter the cheeks, not the floor of the mortise into a puddle that hydraulics the tenon out. I have hydraulicked a tenon out. It looks like the joint is possessed. It is just glue with nowhere to go.

Open time in a hot Arkansas shop is shorter. I glue fewer joints per session. A table base can be two sessions: ends, then stretchers. A hero session is how you get three good corners and one that was rushed.

## Cauls, pads, the show face

A caul that is a little crowned will put pressure in the middle of a panel. A flat caul on a slightly hollow glue-up will press the ends and leave the middle hungry. I have both problems in my past.

Pads: I do not want clamp jaws in a cherry face. I do not want a stain of cheap pipe-clamp tube on a rail — the black marks, the iron, the wood, the moisture. I keep wax paper or tape on the pipes if they are that kind of clamp. I keep wood pads.

## Square, then leave

Diagonals. A winding stick on a frame if I have a twist. A panel that is flat on sticks, not on a bench that is hollow. Then I stop touching it. Poking a setting hide joint is how you open a shoulder. I wipe the squeeze, I check once more, I walk.

The quiet is the job. People want to keep tightening. The glue is going where it is going. Another quarter-turn is a bow.

How long: the sheet. Overnight is a habit I like for structural joints even when the sheet says an hour. I have taken a chair out of clamps at the hour and racked it to teach it a lesson. The lesson was mine.

## Panels

Alternating ring direction if the boards are flatsawn. A tight joint off the jointer, not a clamp-shut gap. A gap that needs a clamp to close is a joint that will open later or starve. I recut the edge.

I do not glue a 40-inch panel out of six angry boards and then ask a breadboard to save me. The breadboard is not a priest.

Sticker the panel after, if it is not going straight to the thicknesser, so both faces see air. A panel left on a cold table overnight can cup to the table.

## Help

A second pair of hands is a tool. I ask. I do not be proud on a dining table with hide and four stretchers. Pride is a racked base.

If I am alone, I use fewer joints per go, or I use a glue with a longer clock, or I use pins that draw the joint so I am not also the clamp. Drawbore exists for this hour.

## Cleanup now, not in the finish room

A wet rag on hide, a careful chisel later on PVA that has gelled, not a smear into the pores. Inside corners of a drawer: now. A glue ridge in a drawer is a cut. On a finish face: now, because a film will not hide a glue smear; it will gloss it.

I have sent a smear to the finish room. The finish room sent it back.

## Hunting a clamp

I hunted a clamp during hide’s open time and I lost a shoulder. Now the clamps are on the bench, on pads, in the dry run, before the glue. A hero session of four stretchers in a hot shop is how I get three good corners and one that was rushed. Two sessions. Ends, then stretchers.

Hungry clamps bowed a panel and starved a joint. Another quarter-turn after the glue has started to grab is a bow. I wipe, I check diagonals, I walk. The quiet is the job.

## Pipe stain, chips, a second pair

Black marks from a pipe clamp on a wet rail: I tape the pipes or I use wood pads. A tenon hydraulicked out on a puddle in the mortise floor: I butter the cheeks, I leave the floor a film, not a lake.

I ask for a second pair of hands on a dining base. Pride is a racked frame. Alone, I use a longer clock or I drawbore so I am not also the clamp. I do not stack other work on a setting panel. I have. The dent was a pad I did not deserve.

## After the quiet

Take the clamps off and look again. A joint can creep. A panel can spring. Better now than after the finish. A little scrape, a little plane, a little honesty if a shoulder opened and I have to saw it apart and do it again. I have sawn it apart. The second glue-up was calmer because the rehearsal had finally sunk in.

The clamps hang. The piece sits. I do not stack other work on it. I have stacked other work on it. The dent was a clamp pad I did not deserve.

Clamps, then quiet. The sentence is the method. Everything else is a way of not rushing the sentence.

A panel whose edges needed a clamp to close is a panel I recut. The clamp will not stay in the house. I sticker a glued panel so both faces see air. A panel left on a cold table overnight can cup to the table. Inside a drawer, the glue ridge comes off now, not in the finish room. A ridge is a cut waiting for a knuckle. I have sent a smear downstairs. Downstairs sent it back.
