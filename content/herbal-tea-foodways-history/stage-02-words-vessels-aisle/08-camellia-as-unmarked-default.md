Every category has an unmarked term. In English hot drinks, the unmarked term is tea, and the tea that does not need an adjective is *Camellia sinensis*. Black, green, oolong — those are process words inside the default. "Herbal" is the adjective that means *not that*. The default is doing more work than it admits.

This is not a complaint about botany. The tea plant earned its libraries: Chinese and then global cultivation, the Canton and later clipper trades, British and Dutch companies, Indian and Ceylonese plantations, Russian caravan routes, Moroccan gunpowder tea, Japanese steaming, the whole violent and ordinary machinery of a stimulant staple. A foodways series about the other cups has to keep that machinery in view, because so many of the other cups exist *as answers to it*.

Substitution is the honest relationship. Fireweed leaves darkened to look like Chinese black tea in the Koporye trade are not a folk wellness. They are a cheaper leaf trying to pass. Roasted barley in a Japanese summer glass is not pretending to be Yunnan; it is a table drink that borrowed the *cha* syllable after Camellia had already taught the pause. Mint in a Maghrebi pot is not a garnish on "real tea" only — Cornwell's dissertation is careful: the herb tradition is older than the nineteenth-century green-tea boom, and the boom used the herbs. Oswego and raspberry leaf in British North America show up in the stories people tell about refusing a taxed leaf. Even "herbal tea" as a grocery phrase is a shadow of the default: you would not need the adjective if the noun had not already been taken.

The unmarked default also steals caffeine as a property of "tea." That theft makes yaupon and mate look like surprises. They are not surprises. They are holly infusions with their own social forms. They are "other" only if Camellia is allowed to own the idea of a stimulating cup. A claims-guarded series should not flip into caffeine cheerleading. It should just stop letting the default hog the definition.

There is a gender and class story in the default, and I want it without a sermon. In a lot of twentieth-century Anglophone homes, "tea" was work: the pot, the tray, the cheap leaves stretched. "Herbal" later became, in advertising, a woman's evening, a pause, a softness. That advertising is not the whole truth — men drink linden, children drink hibiscus, dockworkers drink mint — but it is a real campaign. The unmarked Camellia cup could be masculine in a British workplace and feminine in an American parlor, depending on the decade. The marked herbal cup got assigned the nightgown. Foodways notices the assignment. It does not have to obey it.

Empire sits in the default whether we name it or not. The plants in the "herbal" bin are often local; the plant in the unmarked bin was, for most of the Atlantic world, imported, taxed, and fought over. When a colony drinks a local infusion, the metropolitan writer calls it a substitute, a curiosity, or a native product. When the metropole drinks Camellia, the writer calls it tea. The asymmetry is the history. Rooibos becoming a global commodity with a European protected name is a late twist: the local plant learned to travel like the default.

I will not write a history of Camellia in this folder. Other people have done it, and this series is already too willing to wander. I will write the default as a gravitational field. Drafts that mention "real tea" in a source will keep the scare quotes. Drafts that mention a substitute will ask: substitute for whom, at what price, with what shame or pride? A Russian household drinking fireweed because Chinese leaf was dear is not the same as a twenty-first-century shop selling fireweed as a rediscovered ancestral secret. The plant can be the same. The field is not.

This draft does not treat, cure, or prevent. It is not medical advice. An essay about an unmarked noun is an essay about power in a cupboard. If you only remember one sentence, remember this: "herbal tea" is a phrase that knows it is standing next to something else. The rest of the folder is about the something else, on its own feet, without borrowing the default's medical rumors or its prestige.
