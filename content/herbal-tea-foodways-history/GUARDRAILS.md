# Claims guardrails — herbal tea as foodways history

These drafts are **educational foodways and cultural history**. They are
about cups, gardens, trade names, hospitality hours, and the grocery
category that learned to say "herbal tea." They are **not** a materia
medica, not a wellness aisle, and not a back door into disease claims.

A reviewer should be able to fail a draft by quoting a sentence. If the
only defense is "the reader will know what we meant," the sentence is
wrong.

The user brief for this folder is stricter than a generic herbal-history
pack: **claims-guarded, no disease.** Traditional sickroom use may be
named as a *register of household literature* (a receipt book had a
chapter; a dictionary still carries a sickroom sense). It may not become
the payoff of a plant.

## What a draft may do

- Name plants, people, places, languages, vessels, markets, and documents.
- Describe **how a drink is made and served**: who picks it, who pours
  it, what it is called, when it appears on a table, what it costs, what
  it substitutes for.
- Keep **Camellia sinensis** and non-Camellia infusions in separate
  bins. "Herbal tea" is an English grocery phrase. *Tisane* is a French
  word with a barley-water past. Neither word is a species.
- Treat **hospitality, season, labor, substitution, and ceremony** as
  the plot. A cup that marks Christmas, a street glass, a stillroom, a
  wartime stand-in, a council drink — those are foodways objects.
- Mark dating disputes in public. If the popular story says 1904 and the
  quieter paper says the plant was already being sold locally, put both
  on the table.
- Quote **trade associations and food-law categories** as categories
  (Tea & Herbal Infusions Europe distinguishing tea from herbal and
  fruit infusions; a protected designation of origin on a plant name).
  That is institutional history, not a health finding.
- Say that a source is thin. Oral cultures leave thinner paper. Absence
  of a colonial notebook is not proof of absence of a drink.

## What a draft may not do

- Claim or imply that a plant **diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents**
  a disease. Not in the headline. Not as a folksy aside. Not by parking
  a condition next to a cup until the eye does the rest.
- Use disease names as the destination of a historical story.
- Translate a household drink into a modern indication ("and that is why
  chamomile is for sleep disorders").
- Collapse folk food categories (*tisane*, *atay*, *cha*, *mate*,
  *karkadeh*) into a wellness class.
- Invent quotations, harvest tonnages, Olympic medals, or archive
  holdings.
- Give doses, "what I take," stacks, or kitchen-as-clinic recipes.
- Promise "no side effects," "clinically proven to," "miracle,"
  "superfood," or "boosts the immune system."
- Speak in the wellness-bot register: *unlock*, *hack your*, *in today's
  fast-paced world*, *let's dive in*.
- Recruit the East Asian, South Asian, or Mediterranean drink into a
  pharmacopeia essay. Those folders exist elsewhere in this repo. This
  one stays at the table.

## Voice

Write like a person who has stood in a kitchen and a library and trusted
neither caption on first glance. Specific objects. Uneven sentences. A
little dry humor is fine. Cheerleading is not. If a source is thin, say
the trail is thin.

First person is allowed when it is a readerly "I" ("I keep a pencil in
the margin"), not a testimonial "I" ("this cup fixed my…").

## Legal and medical posture (plain language)

This folder is not medical advice, not a dietary-supplement label, and
not a substitute for a clinician who knows the person in front of them.
A grocery tin that says "herbal tea" is a food-category artifact. It is
not a finding that the plant does a clinical job.

Traditional use, in this series, is evidence that people **prepared and
shared a drink**. It is not, by itself, evidence that the drink does
what a modern disease category names. We do not name those categories
as the point of the cup.

## How a draft proves it stayed inside the fence

Every draft carries YAML frontmatter with `claims_posture:
educational-foodways` and `status: draft`. Every draft ends with a
**Claims box** that a skimming reader can find without finishing the
essay. The linter in `tools/lint_claims.py` is a tripwire, not a
conscience: it catches the lazy phrases and a denylist of disease
payoffs. A human still has to catch the sly ones.

## Staging

Drafts are filed under `stage-0N-*` so the argument can be rebuilt in
order:

1. How to read (refusals and categories)
2. Words, vessels, and the grocery aisle
3. Africa and the Atlantic hibiscus belt
4. Europe: gardens, parlors, substitutions
5. Americas beyond the tin
6. Asia as drink, not pharmacopeia
7. Reader tools and a working bibliography

**Staged** here means **ordered and still a draft**. Nothing in this
folder is cleared for a product label or a health claim.
