# Citations and honesty rule

This series cites **named public documents** a reader can open. It does
not pretend the writer sat in every archive named in a footnote.

## Honesty rule

- A circulating date is a historical object. It is not automatically
  true.
- A travelogue is a traveler's sentence, not a village census.
- A trade association page is a category claim, not a botanist's
  monograph.
- Secondary reviews flatten. When two reviews disagree, both years stay
  visible.
- I have not held every receipt book I mention. When I am working from
  a dictionary entry, a paper abstract, or a council report, the draft
  says so.

## Documents this folder actually leans on

These are the public objects behind the recurring claims. Drafts may
add a local source; they should not invent a notebook.

| Object | Why it is here |
|---|---|
| OED / CNRTL / Anglo-Norman Dictionary on *tisane* / *ptisane* | Barley-water etymology; the later "herbal infusion" sense is younger than the barley. |
| Sidney W. Mintz, *Sweetness and Power* (1985); *Tasting Food, Tasting Freedom* (1996) | Meaning arises from use in social relationships, not from a substance "in itself." |
| Graham Cornwell, *Sweetening the Pot: A History of Tea and Sugar in Morocco, 1850–1960* (Georgetown PhD, 2018) | Mint and other herbs sat in Maghrebi pots before and beside imported green tea. |
| Tea & Herbal Infusions Europe (THIE), Hamburg; successor (2015) of ETC + EHIA | Industry distinction: *Camellia sinensis* tea vs herbal and fruit infusions. |
| Van Wyk & Gorelik, "The history and ethnobotany of Cape herbal teas," *South African Journal of Botany* (2017) | Thunberg's 1772 *Borbonia cordata* note is not a rooibos certificate. |
| Boris Gorelik / SARC ethnographic notes on rooibos | Local sale before the popular "Ginsberg, 1904" line; the 1904 date is a commercialization story, not an invention story. |
| Joubert & De Beer and related rooibos industry papers | Nortier cultivation experiments c. 1930; later export scale. Hedge tonnages. |
| EU protected designation of origin for rooibos / red bush (2021) | A name-protection fact about a plant geography. |
| Judith Carney & Richard Rosomoff, *In the Shadow of Slavery* | African botanical legacy, kitchen gardens, roselle among Atlantic plants. |
| Michael W. Twitty, *The Cooking Gene* | Hibiscus / "red drink" as African-diasporic foodways, not a clinic. |
| Crown et al., "Ritual Black Drink consumption at Cahokia," *PNAS* (2012) | *Ilex* biomarkers in beakers c. A.D. 1050–1250, far north of the holly's historic range. |
| Florida DOS / Hudson-school black-drink literature | White drink / cassina / colonial "black drink" naming; *Ilex vomitoria* epithet is a colonial scar. |
| Kalle, Sõukand et al. on *Epilobium angustifolium* / Ivan-chaj (2020) | Fermented fireweed as a fashionable recent drink in parts of Eastern Europe; the "ancient mass tradition" story is thinner than the aisle likes. |
| Russian imperial notices on Koporye / counterfeit tea (19th c., as cited in tea-trade histories) | Fireweed processed to resemble Chinese black tea; a substitution and fraud story. |
| Slow Food Ark of Taste note on Cretan *malotira* (*Sideritis syriaca*) | Landscape harvest, breakfast table, overgathering — usable without the panacea sentences. |
| Theophrastus on *sideritis* (name from iron / *sidēros*) | A naming etymology in the botanical tradition. Not a modern indication. |
| Hammett and later yaupon horticulture notes (UF/IFAS) | Southeastern holly as the region's native caffeine plant; vomiting reports are not a property of a grocery infusion. |

## What this folder does not open

- Clinical trials as proof of a foodways thesis.
- The mid-century laboratory-word pack that already lives under `content/` in this repo.
- The herbal-medicine-history pack. Different job.
- Any sentence that needs a disease name to land.
