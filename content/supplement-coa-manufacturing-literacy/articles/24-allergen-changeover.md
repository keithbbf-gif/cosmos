**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not an audit. Allergen labeling is a food-labeling duty, not a treatment claim.

A mixed plant will run whey at 6 a.m. and a "vegan" botanical blend at 10 a.m. on the same blender if you let it. Protein left in a gasket does not care about your brand story. The major food allergens are a labeling duty. They are also a changeover duty.

FALCPA and the FASTER Act (sesame) tell you what must be declared on food labels when the allergen is an ingredient. Preventive-control allergen programs live in Part 117 when that rule applies to the floor (piece 30). Part 111 still wants incoming control and a finished product that matches its label. A 3PL will refuse a lot that surprises them with milk in a vegan SKU.

## Incoming is where the surprise usually is

Soy lecithin in a flavor. Milk in a "natural" vanilla. Wheat in a botanical flow agent. Egg in a softgel story you did not read. Fish in a "marine" collagen you marketed as something else.

Collect supplier allergen statements *and* treat them like COAs: qualify, confirm when the risk is high, quarantine until the statement matches the spec (piece 20, piece 28). A statement from 2019 on a flavor house that changed its plant is a costume.

Write the allergen spec on the component: "contains / may contain / does not contain" for the list you actually manage. Then make the panel tell the same story.

## Changeover is a documented clean

Between an allergen SKU and a non-allergen SKU you need a written clean, a visual check, and, when the risk and the customer require it, a residue test. Swab or flush. Define pass.

Line clearance includes: hopper, tools, scoops, printers, carton magazines, shared scoops on a stick. Operators' gloves. The "allergen" scoop that lives in an unmarked bucket.

If the CMO says "we clean every night," ask for the last changeover record on a line that ran milk and then your vegan SKU. If that record does not exist, your vegan SKU is a hope.

Dedicated equipment is the grown-up answer for a high-volume allergen. It costs money. Cross-contact programs cost less until they cost a recall.

## The panel and the "vegan" adjective

If the product contains milk, say so in the way the labeling rule requires. If it does not contain milk but shares a line, your "may contain" / precautionary statement is a counsel-and-policy decision, not a slogan. Do not use "vegan" if you cannot defend the changeover.

Do not use allergen language as a health claim. "Dairy-free" is an ingredient fact when true. It is not "treats lactose intolerance."

## 3PL and retailer desks

Allergen mislabel is a dock-killer (piece 36). Amazon and grocery portals will ask for an allergen matrix. If your matrix says "none" and the CMO's matrix says "shared line with soy," pick one reality before you upload.

A finished-product ELISA for a specific allergen is a tool, not a personality. Use it when the changeover risk is real. Do not use it as a substitute for incoming control.

## What you ask on a walk

1. Which allergens enter this building?
2. Dedicated vs shared equipment for my SKU.
3. Last changeover record that touches my product family.
4. How labels for allergen SKUs are segregated incoming.

If the tour guide does not know which allergens enter the building, the quality unit is a lobby wall.

## Shared scoops

A stainless scoop on a string is communal property and an allergen vector. Label scoops by allergen family, or use dedicated scoops, or use single-use. A wash at the end of the day does not help the vegan blend you ran at 10 a.m. after whey at 8.

This is cheap and visible on a walk. If scoops are unlabeled, the changeover SOP is a document, not a practice.

## A flavor that was milk last quarter

The flavor house allergen statement in the file says "does not contain milk." The statement date is 14 months old. This quarter they moved a vanilla to a plant that also dries dairy flavors. They did not send a new statement. Your vegan SKU used that vanilla in three lots.

Incoming is where this dies or lives (piece 20). Put an expiry on allergen statements — 12 months, or on any plant change — and refuse issue without a current one for high-risk flavors. Then decide whether lots already shipped need a complaint-file note and a retailer notice. That decision is counsel plus quality, not a PDP rewrite alone.

Clean the line. Then print "vegan." Not the other way around.

## What this is not

An allergen program is not a medical-advice program. It is how you keep the panel honest and the gasket empty.
