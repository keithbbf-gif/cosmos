111.75(a)(2) is the sentence sales reps mean when they say "you can use our COA." You may rely on a supplier's certificate of analysis for certain component specifications *if* you qualify the supplier by establishing the reliability of that COA through confirmation of the supplier's tests or examinations. 111.95(b)(2) wants that qualification in the records.

You may not use that sentence to skip identity testing of a dietary ingredient (111.75(a)(1)). InnoMark (657518) and Western Innovations (679737) are the public cost of filing the PDF without the file.

## Qualification is confirmation, not a vibe

A questionnaire is useful. An ISO 9001 certificate is useful. A tour is useful. None of those confirm that *this* assay method on *this* extract matches what you will measure.

Confirmation looks like: you (or a lab you pay) test the same lot for the rows you intend to rely on. You compare. You do that enough times, on a written schedule, to believe the supplier's number. Then you keep doing it, less often, so the belief does not rot.

If the numbers diverge, the supplier is not qualified for that row. The drum stays in quarantine. You do not "average" your 0.9% with their 5% withanolides.

## What you can rely on, and what you cannot

**Cannot rely on the COA alone:** identity of a dietary ingredient. Run the test (piece 07).

**Can rely after qualification:** many purity, strength, composition, and contaminant rows on the *component*, if the method is appropriate and you have the confirmation file.

**Still your problem at finished product:** the bottle (piece 02). Qualifying the extract house does not finish the capsule.

## A file that looks like a file

- Approved supplier list, with the rows you rely on them for.
- Quality agreement or purchasing spec: methods, limits, change notification.
- Initial confirmation: lots, dates, your results vs theirs.
- Ongoing confirmation interval and the last result.
- Disqualification rule: one ugly miss? two? a process change they did not announce?
- The actual COAs, not a "typical" sheet.

A slide that says "approved vendors" with logos is a mood board.

## Brokers and house blends

If you buy from a broker, the COA may be the manufacturer's, the broker's retype, or a remix. Qualify the person whose number you are believing. A retyped PDF that lost the method column is a rumor (piece 15, piece 39).

A CMO "house premix" is a supplier. Qualify it. Identity each dietary ingredient that went into it, or have a written story that is not a shrug.

## Change notification

A qualified supplier that changes plant, method, or sub-supplier is a new supplier until you say otherwise. Write the duty to notify. When they do not notify and you find out from a different HPTLC pattern, you have a deviation and a purchasing problem.

## What you may say

Allowed when true: "Incoming extract lots confirmed against supplier COAs on a written schedule; identity tested every lot."

Not allowed: "Our suppliers are all GMP certified" as a substitute for the file.

Not allowed: showing a supplier's COA as if it were your finished-product release.

## A first-month program

Pick your three highest-risk ingredients (expensive botanicals, low-dose vitamins, anything with a substitution history). Confirm the last lot. Schedule the next. If the CMO will not let you pull a split, that is a quality-agreement problem (piece 29), not a personality conflict.

## How many lots before you rely

There is no magic n in Part 111. A serious program might confirm the first three lots fully, then every fourth, and always after a change. Write *a* n. Review it after a year. If the first three already disagree, you do not get to skip to "every fourth."

Do not copy a drug-industry sampling standard you do not understand just to look precise. A written, followed interval beats an unread ASTM on a shelf.

## The first confirmation that fails

You test their "5.1% withanolides, HPLC." Your lab, same lot, same claimed method family: 1.4%. Identity HPTLC matches the species. So you probably have the plant and not the marker they sold.

Do not average 5.1 and 1.4. Do not release on their number. Hold the drum. Ask for their chromatogram and standard. Run a third lab if the money is large. Disqualify that row until it matches. You may still buy from them for identity-confirmed material if you assay yourself every lot. You may not "rely on the COA" for strength after this miss.

That miss is the system working. A file with only matches is either a great supplier or a file that was never tried.

Believe a COA you have the right to believe. The right is a file. The file is confirmation. Everything else is hope with a letterhead.
