A dietary-supplement plant is usually a food plant with a blender, a filler, and a quality room. It is not a sterile drug suite. Founders tour a lobby, photograph a certificate wall, and write "pharmaceutical cleanroom" on a cart page. That adjective is a claim. The drain under the tank is the plant.

Part 111 Subpart C is physical plant. 111.15 and 111.20 are the sanitation and design neighbors. They do not require ISO 5. They do require a building that can be kept clean, that does not contaminate product, and that has water safe for its use.

## Water

Ask what water touches product, cleaning, or a tank rinse.

Municipal water is a start. It is not automatically the right water for a liquid shot or a lab. If the process claims USP Purified Water, ask for the system drawing, the last sanitization, and the last micro/chemistry results. If the process uses a hose on the floor to "rinse the blender," you have a water story whether or not anyone wrote it down.

Store hoses off the floor. Drains should not back up under a blender. A wet floor under a powder operation is a micro and a slip story.

Lab water is a third system. Dilutions made in dirty water make pretty NDs (piece 14).

## Air

Powder wants dust control so you do not cross a vitamin into a botanical SKU and so operators can breathe. Liquids want air that does not spit condensate into an open tank. Softgel and gummy rooms have their own humidity jobs.

"Positive pressure cleanroom" is a designed, measured state. A ceiling filter in a hallway is not that state. If you advertise ISO 7, you owe particle counts and a classification. If you have a packaged HVAC unit and a sticky mat, say you have a packaged HVAC unit and a sticky mat.

Pest control is air's cousin. Look at dock seals and the dry-storage corners. A bait map that ended in 2021 is a costume.

## What a walk looks like

Skip the lobby. Start at receiving (piece 20). Then dry storage. Then the water room if they have one. Then blending. Then fill. Then pack. Then the lab. Then the reserve cage (piece 43). Then the reject area (piece 23).

Look up. Look at the floor-wall junction. Look at the drain. Look at whether scoops are labeled. Look at whether the allergen line is actually separate (piece 24). Ask an operator how they clean the blender. The operator's answer is the SOP.

If they will not walk you past the glass, that is information.

## What you may say

Allowed when true: "Manufactured in a facility that follows 21 CFR 111. We can share a redacted inspection history with qualified buyers."

Not allowed: "FDA-certified cleanroom." FDA does not certify that sentence.

Not allowed: "Hospital-grade air" on a powder blend.

Not allowed: a stock photo of a sterile suite you do not lease (`PHOTO_NOTES.md`).

## Liquids and the plant you actually need

A powder-only room can be simple and still be in control. A liquid, a gummy, or an emulsion wants hygienic design: weld quality, dead legs, a CIP story or a take-apart story, and a hold-time. If your CMO is a powder plant that "also does shots," walk the tank twice.

## What this is not

A tidy plant is not an efficacy claim. It is a condition of manufacture.

## Pest maps that ended

Ask for the current pest-control map and the last service ticket. A binder from a prior tenant is a costume. Look at the dry-botanical corner and the dock. If you see evidence and they say "we don't have a problem," you have a sanitation sentence for the quality agreement: current map, tickets on request, notice of activity in rooms that hold your lots.

You do not need to become a pest expert. You need a map that is this year's.

## The hose

A powder blender is "dry cleaned" most days and wet-cleaned on Fridays. The Friday hose lives in a coil on the floor, nozzle in a puddle, because the hook broke in March. That hose is the water system. It is also the micro system (piece 10).

Fix: hook, off the floor, a flush before use, a written Friday clean that includes the hose. Cheap. The ISO-7 sentence on the website is expensive and false. Do the hook first. Then decide whether you still want the adjective.

If you walk one thing besides quarantine, walk the hose.

Do not sell the lobby. Walk the drain. Then write the sentence you can defend.
