**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not an audit. A mycotoxin result is a contaminant measurement, not a treatment claim.

Fungi grow on plants. Some of them leave aflatoxins, ochratoxin A, and a longer list of metabolites you will only run when the crop and the customer demand it. A TAMC/TYMC panel does not see those molecules. Enumeration counts cells. A mycotoxin assay looks for a toxin that may still be there after the cells are dead.

If you buy botanicals, spices, cocoa, grains, or anything that sat in a humid warehouse, the mycotoxin conversation is open.

## The usual names

**Aflatoxins B1, B2, G1, G2**, sometimes reported as "total aflatoxin," with B1 called out because many limits treat it as the driver. Corn, peanuts, some spices, and some herbs are the crops people name first. Your drum does not become exempt because it is "superfood" instead of feed.

**Ochratoxin A.** Coffee, cocoa, some cereals, some dried fruit, some herbs. A different fungus, a different row.

**Others** (DON, fumonisins, zearalenone, T-2) show up when the crop is a grain or when a customer hands you a European list. Do not run a twenty-toxin panel as theater. Do run the toxins that belong to *this* article.

Write the list on the spec. "Mycotoxins: conforms" with no names is a costume.

## Method

LC-MS/MS is the adult multi-toxin method in this trade. ELISA and other screens exist. A screen can be a first step. A release against a tight limit wants a method that can see that limit (piece 14).

Ask:

- Which toxins?
- Which method ID?
- LOQ versus your spec.
- Whether the result is B1 only or total, and whether the spec was written the same way.

Incoming is the usual place for this panel. Finished-product blends can dilute a hot drum into a number that looks fine and still be a supplier you should have rejected. Do not "blend away" an OOS incoming result without a disposition you would show an investigator (piece 22, piece 23).

## Food action levels are adjacent, not automatic

FDA publishes guidance and action levels for certain mycotoxins in certain foods. Those documents are useful context. They are not automatically your 111.70 dietary-supplement spec. A customer in the EU may hand you Commission limits that are tighter than what a US food warehouse treats as normal.

Adopt a number. Write it. If you borrow a food action level, say that you borrowed it and [VERIFY] the current FDA or EU page on the date you adopt it. This draft will not freeze a µg/kg figure that the agency can move.

## Storage is part of the test

A passing incoming aflatoxin on a lot that then sits in a leaky trailer for two weeks in July is a historical number. Mycotoxin risk is agricultural *and* warehouse. Walk the CMO's botanical cage: temperature, humidity, bags off the floor, first-expiry-first-out. That walk is not romantic. It is why two lots from the same supplier diverge.

Reserve samples will not save you if you stored them wet (piece 43).

## Spice-cabinet SKUs

Turmeric, chili, ginger, and some "greens" powders are mycotoxin regulars in food literature. If you add a spice-cabinet SKU to a vitamin line, do not reuse the vitamin incoming template. Add the toxin rows before the first PO. The first PO is how a hot drum becomes a finished lot you then have to reject (piece 23).

## A cocoa-protein example

Plant protein plus cocoa is a metals story (piece 09) and a mycotoxin story. Incoming cocoa powder: ochratoxin A reported as ND, no LOQ. You blend 8% cocoa into a 12 g scoop. A retailer in a market that uses a food ochratoxin limit asks for the number in µg/kg of the *finished* powder and of the cocoa.

You cannot answer. The incoming row was a poster. The finished lot was never tested for ochratoxin because the CMO's template is a vitamin-mineral sheet.

Write cocoa (and any other high-risk botanical or grain) onto the incoming spec with a named toxin, a method, and an LOQ. Decide whether finished-product testing is every lot, skip-lot, or incoming-only with a 111.75(d) file. Then put the decision in the lot folder, not in a Slack shrug.

## What to do with a hot incoming drum

Hold. Do not blend. Do not "we will only use 2% so it dilutes." Dilution as unofficial reprocessing is piece 23. Quality control dispositions. If you reject, document where the drum went. If you return it, the supplier's next COA on the replacement lot is a new object. Confirm it. Do not assume the replacement is clean because they were embarrassed.

## What this is not

A passing aflatoxin row is not "supports liver health" and it is not a treatment for mold exposure. Do not put organ or infection language next to this paragraph.

It is not a micro pass. You can have low TAMC and a real B1 number. You can have high TAMC and no aflatoxin. Run the test you mean.

If the plant sat in a barn, ask about the toxin. If you did not ask, do not advertise the absence.
