"Third-party tested" is a fog. Three programs have directories. If you mean one of them, say the name and put the SKU in the directory. If the SKU is not in the directory, do not put the logo on the bottle.

A COA from a 17025 lab is testing (piece 18). A mark is a program. They are friends. They are not the same person.

## USP Verified (dietary supplement program)

USP audits the facility, reviews GMP, tests products against USP standards (identity, potency, contaminants, disintegration as the program requires), and does marketplace surveillance. The mark is for **specific SKUs** that stay in the program.

Look the product up in USP's verified directory. A factory that "follows USP" is not USP Verified. A monograph and the Verification Program are related and not identical. USP \<2232\> on a spec (piece 09) is not the mark.

## NSF Certified for Sport

NSF's sport mark is about banned-substance screens (the lists NSF uses for that program), plus contents and manufacturing components. Lots are tested. The certified-product search is the source of truth.

"NSF registered" on a facility is a weaker, different sentence than "this bottle is Certified for Sport." Athletes and teams actually use the sport mark. Do not borrow it because the CMO had an NSF flag on a different program.

## Informed-Sport (LGC)

Batch testing against a banned-substance screen, plus manufacturing quality components. The Informed-Sport site lists certified products. Informed-Choice is a sibling mark with a different sampling intensity. Do not swap the names on a PDP.

## What none of these mean

- The ingredient treats a disease.
- FDA approved the SKU.
- Every bottle was tested if the program is lot-based, say "lot-tested" and keep the lot in the program.
- Last year's listing covers this year's formula revision. A label change can be a new SKU to the program.

If you drop out of the program, remove the artwork. A leftover logo is a claim.

## COA versus mark

You can have a serious COA and no mark. That is normal. You can have a mark and still owe Part 111. The mark does not eat the regulation.

If you pay for a mark, still keep the lot folder: spec, BPR, COA, reserve. Retailers who care about the mark will still ask for the folder after the first ugly press test.

## Artwork rules

These marks are trademarks. Use their artwork files and their clearance. Do not redraw a badge in Canva. Do not put three logos on a catalog white-label SKU that is in none of the directories (piece 31).

## What you may say

Allowed when true: "USP Verified. Directory ID [X]. Lot [Y] in program."

Allowed when true: "Finished lot tested for [rows] by [17025 lab]. Not USP Verified."

Not allowed: "USP grade" as a consumer fog.

Not allowed: "NSF" on a page that means the CMO's facility registration-ish certificate from 2019.

## Lot-based programs and a formula tweak

You are in Informed-Sport or NSF Certified for Sport on formula revision 3. You change the flavor system. To you it is "the same powder." To the program it may be a new SKU. Ask *before* you print the old logo on the new carton.

A directory listing that still shows the old flavor is not permission. The mark is a contract with a scope. Scope changes when the article changes. Treat a flavor swap like a spec revision (piece 19), not like a marketing refresh.

## A CMO that "has NSF" on the tour

The lobby has an NSF flag. The plant is registered in some NSF facility program. Your sport-adjacent powder is not in the Certified for Sport directory. The designer still wants the logo "because the factory has it."

Facility registration and the sport mark are different products NSF sells. Athletes search the sport directory. A facility flag does not put your lot in that search. If you need the sport sentence, enroll the SKU, pay for the lots, and wait. If you do not, say "third-party tested for [rows] by [lab]" and stop.

Borrowing the lobby flag is a trademark problem and a claims problem. It is also how you lose the first retailer who actually looks you up.

Look the SKU up. If you cannot find it, the logo is the product, and the product is a costume.
