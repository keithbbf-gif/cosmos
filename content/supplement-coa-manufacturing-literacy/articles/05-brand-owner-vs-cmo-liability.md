The contract manufacturer has a blender, a clean room that is not a clean room, and a quality unit. The brand has a Shopify theme and a name on the label. Founders talk as if the first person absorbed the Act. The Act did not get that memo.

If your name is on the bottle, you caused a labeled dietary supplement to enter commerce. Part 111 still speaks in "you." Misbranding under 21 U.S.C. § 343 still reads the panel you approved. Adulteration under § 342(g)(1) still cares how the lot was prepared, packed, or held — including the holding you chose when you picked a 3PL.

A quality agreement can allocate *work*. It does not delete *responsibility*.

## What the CMO actually is

A contract manufacturer (CMO) or contract packer (CPO) is a firm that performs manufacturing, packaging, or labeling for another person. In white-label, they may also own the formula (piece 32, piece 33). In a custom contract, you may own the formula and they execute it.

Either way they are a regulated firm. FDA inspects *their* plant. Warning letters from 2023–2025 (InnoMark 657518, Formulation Technology 672905, Western Innovations 679737, LXR Biotech 714568) name the facility that was inspected. That is the usual address on the envelope.

It is not the only address that matters. Retailers, Amazon, and state warning statutes will come to the brand. Customers will come to the brand. A plaintiff's complaint will name the name they can see. If you cannot produce the batch record, the COA, and the reserve, "my CMO has it" is a sentence you say once, then regret.

## What you cannot outsource

**The specifications.** 111.70 wants *your* product specifications, or specifications you have reviewed and that quality control has approved as yours. A supplier CoA that might or might not be the firm's spec is the Western Innovations problem.

**Identity of dietary ingredients.** 111.75(a)(1) does not say "unless you hired a CMO." Someone has to run an appropriate identity test. If the CMO runs it, you still need the record, the method, and a reason to believe the method can see the ingredient you named on the panel.

**The label.** 21 CFR 101.36 is your panel. Structure/function claims are your 101.93 problem. A CMO that "prints whatever you send" will print a disease claim if you send one. That is not a defense.

**The story you tell.** Web copy, ads, influencers. The CMO did not write your Reel. FTC will not care that the plant is in another state.

**The decision to ship.** If you never saw the finished-product results and you still uploaded the SKU, you released the lot. Sales released the lot. Quality control did not.

## What a quality agreement is for

Write, before PO1:

- Who writes and approves specifications.
- Who performs identity testing, and with what methods.
- Who performs finished-product testing, which rows, which lab, which skip-lot rule.
- How OOS is handled, and who may reprocess (111.77, 111.90).
- Who holds reserve samples, where, and whether you get a split retain (piece 43).
- How deviations and complaints flow. Hours, not "we'll loop you in."
- Who owns the formula, the batch record, and the right to take them to another plant (piece 32).
- Change control: a flavor swap is a new SKU conversation, not a courtesy email after the truck left.

Counsel drafts this. This pack does not. The empty seat stays empty. The point is that a two-page "manufacturing agreement" that only talks about price per thousand and a 12-week lead time is a purchasing document. It is not a quality system.

## Dual registration and the inspection you will not attend

The CMO's facility is registered. Your brand may also be a facility if you hold, pack, or label. A 3PL may be a facility. Part 117 may apply on the same floor (piece 30).

When FDA inspects the CMO, you may not be in the room. You will still live with the 483. Ask, in the agreement, for prompt notice of inspections, 483s, warning letters, and recalls that touch your lots. Ask for the right to review the relevant records. If the CMO refuses, that is information. It belongs in the walk-away file (piece 41).

Formulation Technology's 2024 letter is a plant letter. A brand that used that plant still had bottles in the world. The letter is public. The bottles do not get a footnote.

## Money does not move the name

Paying more per bottle does not make the CMO the brand. Paying less does not make you a "platform" instead of a manufacturer. Private-label catalogs are still labeled products. The inexpensive SKU is the one that usually has the thinnest file.

If you cannot afford finished-product testing on your nickel for the first lot, you cannot afford the SKU (piece 34, piece 40).

## A first-week practice

1. List every firm that touches the lot: ingredient suppliers, CMO, packer, lab, 3PL, you.
2. For each, write the Part 111 job they actually perform.
3. For each job, write the record you keep in *your* cabinet, not theirs.
4. If a job has no record in your cabinet, that job is a hope.

The labeled bottle has one name on it that a customer can read. That name is the one that has to answer.
