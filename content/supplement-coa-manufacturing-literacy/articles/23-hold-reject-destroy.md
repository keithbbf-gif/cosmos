A failed lot occupies space. Space is money. That is why failed lots grow legs. Part 111 wants them to grow a status instead: hold, then a quality-control disposition — reject, destroy, return, or, in a narrow set of cases, reprocess.

111.87: quality control conducts the material review and makes the disposition decision. 111.77 and 111.90 put fences around treatments, in-process adjustments, and reprocessing. 111.370 is the returned-product neighbor.

If your CMO has never destroyed anything, ask how.

## Hold is a physical word

Hold means the lot cannot be issued, shipped, or "temporarily borrowed" for a photoshoot. Same idea as incoming quarantine (piece 20), applied to finished or in-process material.

A hold that lives only in an email is not a hold. A hold that the night shipper can override because a retailer PO is due is not a hold.

Label the pallets. Lock the location. Write the reason: OOS potency, label mix, yield investigation, complaint hold, customer cancellation.

## Reject is not a vibe

Reject means this material will not become distributed product in its current form. It gets a location of its own. Mixing rejected drums with released drums is how a night shift performs unofficial reprocessing.

Document: what, how much, why, who decided, where it went. If it was destroyed, who witnessed. If it was returned to a supplier, the paperwork. If it was "donated," stop and call counsel — donation of an OOS supplement is not a cleanup method.

## Reprocess is a regulated verb

111.90: you do not reprocess a rejected dietary supplement, or treat a component to make it suitable, unless quality control reviews and approves *and* the action is permitted under 111.77.

That is the opposite of "dump it into the next blend; the assay will land." Dilution is not a default permission. Some treatments are ordinary (dry a damp powder that still meets specs after a written adjustment). Some are fan fiction (spike D3 into a failed lot until the HPLC smiles).

If you reprocess, you have a new history. The BPR must show the work. The testing starts again for the specs that the treatment could affect. You do not inherit the old COA by changing the lot number's last letter.

## Returns

Customers send bottles back. 111.370 wants a review before those bottles re-enter stock. A resealed Amazon return is not a released lot. Salvage programs that resticker returns as new are how you buy a second problem.

Reserve samples are for investigation, not for topping up a short shipment (piece 43).

## What you ask to see

1. The reject log for the last twelve months.
2. One destruction record.
3. One reprocess file, if they have one — the QC review, the MMR/BPR, the new tests.
4. Where rejected pallets sit right now.

If the reject log is empty and the plant is busy, you are looking at luck or at a culture. Write it down either way.

## Money and the founder

You will be asked to pay for a remake. That is cheaper than shipping the fail. It is not cheaper than a quality agreement that already said who pays when incoming identity fails versus when the CMO's fill weight fails (piece 29). Write that before PO1.

Do not let a remake ship under the old lot number. The old lot had a story. The new lot gets a new number and a new file.

## What this is not

Destruction is not a confession of disease. It is a disposition. Do not put "we destroy anything that isn't perfect" on a cart page as a wellness slogan. Show the SOP to a buyer who asks. Keep the adjective off the PDP.

## Destroy means destroy

A "destroyed" lot that later appears on a secondary marketplace under your brand is not destroyed. Witness, scrap ticket, or a renderer receipt. If the CMO "donates to employees," you now have an informal distribution of a rejected lot. Stop that in the agreement.

If you cannot get a receipt, assume the lot still exists until you have a reason not to.

## The unlabeled tub

A yield came up short. A tub of "extra" capsules sat on a shelf without a lot sticker because someone was going to "figure it out Monday." Monday, a helper dumped it into a released lot of the same SKU to make the count.

That dump is unofficial reprocessing, a lot-integrity failure, and a retain lie. The released lot is now a mixture. The COA speaks for a sample taken before the dump, or after, and you may not know which.

Rule: any unlabeled finished product is a hold. It gets a ticket or it gets destroyed. Helpers do not perform dispositions. If you need the count, QC opens a file, not a tub.

Hold it. Decide. Then move it to a place that matches the decision. Not to Friday's blender.
