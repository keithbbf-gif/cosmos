ISO/IEC 17025 is the standard for competence of testing and calibration laboratories. It is a real thing. It is also a logo people crop into a sales deck. The document that matters is the **scope**: which methods, which matrices, which ranges the accreditation body actually granted.

A lab accredited for coliforms in drinking water is not thereby accredited for ICP-MS lead in a botanical extract. The sticker does not know the difference. You have to.

## What 17025 is

A third-party accreditation body audited the lab's quality system and, method by method, its competence. The lab has to participate in proficiency testing for the scoped work. It has to handle uncertainty, complaints, impartiality. That is more than a nice HPLC in a back room.

It is not a product certification. It does not make your SKU USP Verified (piece 37). It does not make FDA "approve" the lot. It does not replace 111.75. It is evidence that *this lab, for these tests,* is a serious place to send a sample.

## How to read a certificate

1. Find the accreditation body and the certificate number.
2. Open the current scope PDF on the accreditor's directory, not a PNG from 2019 in the CMO's media kit.
3. Find the method that is on your COA. Find the matrix (dietary supplement, botanical, food, water, soil).
4. If the method/matrix pair is not on the scope, the 17025 claim does not cover that row. The lab might still be able to run it as an unscoped test. That is a different, weaker sentence. Ask them to say that sentence out loud.

Flexible scopes exist. They are not "we can do anything." They are a defined flexibility. Read the annex.

Expired certificates are costumes. A lab that will not send a current scope is telling you something.

## In-house CMO labs

Part 111 does not require 17025. An in-house lab can be competent. It can also be the same team that needs the batch to ship on Friday (piece 38).

If the CMO claims 17025, read the scope. Some plant labs are accredited for a short list (loss on drying, average weight) and not for the HPLC they use on your D3.

If they do not claim 17025, ask for method validation, system suitability, analyst training, and a split-sample schedule to an outside scoped lab. That schedule is cheaper than a recall and more honest than a borrowed logo.

## The reference-standard problem does not go away

LXR Biotech (714568, 23 December 2025) used a third-party lab and still failed identity, because the "standard" and the sample shared a lot number. Accreditation does not cure a stupid sample. You still have to ask what the lab compared against (piece 07).

Chain of custody matters. If the supplier shipped the sample to the lab, and also shipped the standard, you are not the customer of the truth. Pay for a split on your nickel, from a retain *you* hold, at least on a schedule.

## What to put in the quality agreement

- Named lab(s) for named rows.
- 17025 scope attached as an exhibit, updated annually.
- Permission for you to send split samples.
- A rule for when an unscoped method is acceptable (almost never for metals and identity of high-risk botanicals).

## What you may say on a site

Allowed when true: "Heavy metals by ICP-MS at [lab], ISO/IEC 17025 accredited for that method in dietary supplements. Certificate [ID]. Lot [ID]."

Not allowed: "ISO certified product." The product is not certified. The lab is accredited.

Not allowed: a 17025 logo on a page about a lot the scoped lab never saw.

## A five-minute check

1. Open the COA. Name the lab.
2. Find the directory listing.
3. Download the scope.
4. Ctrl-F the method.
5. If you cannot find it, the sticker was the product.

## Proficiency testing is the quiet page

A 17025 lab should have proficiency-test results for scoped methods. You do not need to audit PT every month. You can ask, once, whether they participate for ICP-MS metals in a food/supplement matrix. A blank stare is information. A certificate and a recent PT summary are the adult answer.

In-house labs can enroll in PT too. If they never have, the Friday conflict has less outside gravity (piece 38).

## A scope that covers water and a COA that claims botanicals

Certificate: 17025, current, real accreditor. Scope annex: drinking water metals by ICP-MS; food microbiology; pH of aqueous solutions. Your COA: lead in ashwagandha extract, "ISO 17025 accredited laboratory" in the header.

The sticker is true. The implication is not. The metals-in-botanical row is unscoped work unless the annex says so. Unscoped work can still be competent. It is a weaker sentence. Ask the lab to print *that* sentence: "This method/matrix is not on our 17025 scope." Then decide whether you still want the row, a different lab, or a validation packet.

Do not crop the gold badge onto a PDP that means the water annex.

## What to file annually

Download the current scope. Diff it against last year's. Labs add and drop methods. A dropped HPLC is how your D3 row quietly became unscoped. Put the PDF in the supplier/lab folder next to the quality agreement exhibit (piece 29).

Read the scope. Then trust the row. Not the other way around.
