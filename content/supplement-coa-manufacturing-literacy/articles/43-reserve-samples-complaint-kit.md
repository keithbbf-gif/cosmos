111.83 wants reserve samples of each lot of packaged and labeled dietary supplements that you distribute. They sit in the same kind of container-closure you ship (or one that protects the same way). They are identified with the batch, lot, or control number. They stay **1 year past the shelf-life date** if you use one, or **2 years from the date of distribution of the last batch associated with the reserve** if you do not. They are at least **twice the quantity** needed to run all the tests that decide whether the product meets specifications.

111.465 is how you hold them: against contamination and deterioration, under labeled conditions or ordinary conditions if the label is silent.

That is the whole retain. Founders skip it because it is unglamorous and it occupies a shelf. Month eleven is when the shelf earns the rent.

## Why twice the tests

You may need to test once for an investigation and still have a remain. A retain that is one bottle of a 12-test spec is a souvenir.

Calculate: what is "all tests"? Identity, assay panel, metals, micro, solvents if owed — the release set. Two times that mass or count. Then add a little because bottles break.

## Where they live

At the CMO is common. At the brand is smarter for the argument you will have with the CMO. Both is the adult answer: they keep the 111.83 retain; you keep a split for splits (piece 38) and for complaints.

A hot closet above the blender is not labeled conditions. A fridge for a room-temperature product is a different article. Write the location in the quality agreement (piece 29).

If you are the brand and you never see a retain, you will be asking a plant you may be leaving (piece 41) to overnight you a bottle they now cannot find.

## The complaint kit

When 111.553 brings you a complaint that might be a quality failure, you want:

- The retain for that lot.
- The lot folder: label, COA, BPR header, spec (piece 40).
- A written complaint record: what the customer saw, lot number from *their* bottle if they have it, photos.
- A plan: do you test the retain? Do you ask for the customer's bottle? Who decides?

Do not tell the customer the product will treat the symptom they named. Do not diagnose. If it might be a serious adverse event, the clock is counsel's and the quality unit's, not this page's.

Do not open the last retain for a social-media unboxing. It is not content.

## Pack-and-label retains

If you distribute unlabeled bulk to someone else who packs, 111.83 still has a container-closure sentence for you. If you only brand and never hold, you still want a contractual retain. The rule talks to the person who distributes packaged and labeled product. Do not assume you are outside that sentence because a 3PL applies the shipping label.

## What you ask on a walk

Show me the reserve cage. Show me *my* last lot. Show me the temperature log. Show me how you would pick a bottle at 4:55 p.m. if I called.

If they cannot find your lot in ten minutes, the retain is a policy, not a cage.

## What this is not

A retain program is not a consumer claim. Do not advertise "we keep samples of every bottle" if you keep a scoop of powder in a baggie.

## What if the customer will not send the bottle

Many will not. You still have the retain and the lot folder. Test the retain if the complaint is physical (empty capsules, off smell, foreign matter) or if you have a pattern. Do not test the retain for "it didn't work" when "work" meant a disease outcome — that is a claims conversation, not an HPLC conversation (piece 04).

Offer a replacement or a refund per your commercial policy. Open the 111.553 record either way. The record is the duty. The refund is courtesy.

## Count the shelf against the ship log

Once a quarter, list lots you shipped in the retain window. Confirm each has a retain on the shelf or a written destruction after the window closed. Missing retains are a 111.83 problem, not a housekeeping problem.

If the CMO "space-limited" and tossed month-eight bottles, you will meet that decision on a month-eleven complaint. Write a minimum shelf in the quality agreement. Pay for a small offsite if you have to. The shelf is cheaper than a guess.

It is how you investigate. Pull the quantity. Hold it the time. Use it when the email arrives. Then you are in a record, not in a guess.
