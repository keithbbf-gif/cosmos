Some plants get better after you ask adult questions. Some plants show you who they are. Walking away is a quality decision. It is also a commercial one. Write the hard stops *before* you are in love with a ship date.

## Hard stops

- Costume COA and no native file (piece 39).
- Refusal to allow a split from your retain (piece 38).
- Identity of a dietary ingredient "done" only by supplier PDF, and they will not change (piece 02, piece 07).
- No reject log, no destruction, no OOS file in a busy plant (piece 22, piece 23).
- Quarantine that does not lock (piece 20).
- They will not show a redacted BPR header for *your* lot (piece 19).
- They want you to put a disease claim on the label and "everyone does it" (piece 04).
- They will not notify you of a 483 that touches your lots (piece 05).
- Shared allergen line with no changeover record and a "vegan" panel they wrote (piece 24).
- They offer SARMs, BPC-157, or "research" peptides as dietary ingredients.

One of these can be a negotiation. Three is a pattern. Costume plus refused split is enough alone.

## Soft stops that become hard

- Every answer is a gold badge and never a SOP.
- Lead time always eats the test window (piece 34).
- Formula ownership is "we'll talk later" and you need to move in a year (piece 32).
- The only quality person you can reach is sales.

Give these a date. If the SOP has not appeared by that date, it is a hard stop.

## How to leave without shipping the last lot

1. Hold anything not yet released. Do not "take delivery and sort it out."
2. Pull or request your reserves and your files (piece 43). If they will not send electronic copies of *your* BPRs and COAs, that is why you are leaving.
3. Tell the 3PL in writing not to inbound new lots from that plant until you say so.
4. Do not announce a war on social media. You will still owe customers a clean sentence if a lot already shipped.
5. Counsel on the contract: cancellation, tooling, remaining MOQ. This pack does not write that letter.
6. If you own the formula, take the MMR pack (piece 32). If you do not, do not steal it. Buy a new recipe on purpose (piece 33).

## How not to leave

Do not ship the doubtful lot at a discount to "empty the relationship." A cheap adulterated lot still has your name on it.

Do not transfer artwork to a new plant and keep the old lot number.

Do not tell the new plant "just match what they did" without the spec file. You will buy the same hole with a new lobby.

## A sentence you can use

"We are stopping POs because we cannot verify identity and finished-product results to 21 CFR 111 on our lots. Please place remaining material on hold and send electronic copies of BPRs, COAs, and reserve locations for lots [list] within five business days."

Calm. Specific. No adjectives about their soul.

## After

Audit the catalog before you reprint (piece 42). Budget splits at the new plant from lot one. Walk the drain, not the lobby (piece 25).

## Customers during the switch

You will be late. Say "we changed manufacturers to keep lot files we can defend," not "we found a better formula that treats…" Do not invent a disease reason for a quality move. Offer refunds on the last doubtful lot if the file is empty. That costs money. It costs less than a marketplace pile-on attached to a costume COA.

## Do not ghost the file

When you leave, archive:

- Every COA and BPR header you have.
- Quality agreement and POs.
- Open deviations and complaints.
- Reserve locations and whether you retrieved splits.
- The last walk notes (drain, quarantine, lab).

A new CMO will ask what went wrong. "They were hard to work with" is not a transfer file. "Identity was supplier-PDF only; splits refused; lot 24C had no finished metals" is a transfer file.

Keep it off the public site. Keep it.

## The lot already on the water

You decide to leave on Thursday. A container left on Tuesday. You still own those bottles. Run the checklist on that lot as if you were staying (piece 40). If the file is empty, treat it as a hold at the 3PL if it has not been picked, and as a complaint-ready lot if it has.

Leaving does not retire a lot. It retires a supplier. The name on the bottle is still yours (piece 05).

Walking away is how you keep a brand. Staying for a ship date is how you keep a PDF. Choose the brand.
