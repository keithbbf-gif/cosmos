The four names on a grown-up metals panel are lead, arsenic, cadmium, and mercury. Botanicals, plant proteins, cocoa, calcium, and some Ayurvedic-adjacent herbs keep showing up in press tests. That is not a reason to write a detox claim. It is a reason to write a numeric spec and to do the serving-day math.

## The method

ICP-MS is the adult method for these four in this trade. AAS can be fine for some matrices if the lab validated it. XRF on a finished powder is a screen, not a release, unless someone has shown you why that screen is specific and sensitive enough for your spec.

Units must match. ppm, mcg/g, mcg/serving, and mcg/day are not interchangeable without arithmetic (piece 16). LOD and LOQ belong on the page (piece 14). "ND" with no LOQ is a poster.

The lab's ISO/IEC 17025 scope should include this method in this kind of matrix (piece 18). A water-micro accreditation does not cover lead in a botanical.

## USP \<2232\> is a standard, not a slogan

USP \<2232\> (Elemental Contaminants in Dietary Supplements) is how the pharmacopeia thinks about these elements in this category. It is related to, and not the same object as, USP \<232\>/\<233\> for drugs. Do not put "meets USP 232" on a gummy as a consumer claim unless you mean it and can show the work.

If you adopt \<2232\> limits, write them on the spec in the units the chapter uses, and convert to your serving. "We follow USP" with no number in the spec column is a vibe.

USP's Dietary Supplements Verification Program is a different sentence again (piece 37). A chapter and a verification mark are related. They are not identical.

## Prop 65 is not a federal GMP

OEHHA lists lead. The current safe-harbor Maximum Allowable Dose Level (MADL) for lead as a reproductive toxicant is **0.5 µg/day**. The oral No Significant Risk Level (NSRL) for cancer is **15 µg/day**. Recheck https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/chemicals/lead before you write a warning decision. This pack is a snapshot, not counsel's memo.

A lot can pass an internal or USP-style spec and still be a Prop 65 problem for California once you convert to mcg per day at your serving. A lot can carry a warning and still be a lawful supplement under federal law. Those are different machines.

Do not put "heavy-metal free" on a cacao protein. Do not put "Prop 65 safe" on a cart page unless counsel signed that adjective. The honest sentence is the number, the method, the lab, the lot, and — if you ship to CA — a warning decision that lives in a file.

## Press tests are pressure, not your release spec

Consumer Reports published a protein-powder and shake investigation on 14 October 2025 (updated 8 January 2026). Samples bought November 2024–January 2025. Metals by triple-quad ICP-MS (AOAC 2015.01). CR used its own lead "level of concern" of 0.5 mcg/day *in one serving*. About 70% of the products in that set sat above that CR benchmark. Plant-based powders ran higher on average than dairy in that set. CR named SKUs. Those numbers are **CR's benchmark, not an FDA tolerance**. FDA still has no binding lead limit for protein powder as of the date of that piece. [VERIFY] the current FDA page before you say that sentence in 2027.

Do not reprint a named competitor SKU on your site without re-opening CR's table. They update. A 2025 lot is not a 2026 lot.

Clean Label Project and similar programs are the same class: useful pressure, not your 111.70 spec.

## Do the conversion on the incoming row too

Incoming extract: 0.8 ppm lead. That is 0.8 mcg/g. Your serving uses 400 mg of extract. 0.32 mcg from that ingredient before the rest of the formula. Add the protein, the cocoa, the calcium. Then compare to *your* spec and to the Prop 65 MADL if CA is in the ship map.

Founders who paste "0.8 ppm" onto a PDP as if it were the daily exposure are doing theater. Founders who never convert at all are doing hope.

## What belongs on the finished-product COA

- Pb, As, Cd, Hg.
- Method (ICP-MS or the validated alternative).
- Spec in the same unit as the result.
- LOQ.
- Lab name and a scope you can check.
- A note, if you use it, of the serving used to compute mcg/serving.

Speciation (inorganic arsenic versus total arsenic; methylmercury versus total mercury) is a second conversation. If your spec is total, do not advertise "no inorganic arsenic" unless you measured it.

## What this is not

A passing metals row is not a treatment for anything. It is not a pregnancy claim. It is not "detox." Do not put those words near this paragraph.

It is also not a reason to skip identity or assay. A clean metals panel on the wrong plant is still the wrong plant.

Write the number. Convert the number. File the number. That is the whole metals job.
