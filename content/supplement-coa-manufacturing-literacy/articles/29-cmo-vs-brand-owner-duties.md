Piece 05 said the brand still owns the labeled bottle. This piece splits the *work*. The Act does not vanish because you split it. The quality agreement is how you avoid two people each thinking the other ran identity.

111.12 is personnel: people must be qualified for their jobs. That includes the brand person who approves labels and the CMO person who releases blends. An unqualified founder clicking "approve artwork" is a personnel problem.

## A split that usually works

**CMO typically performs**

- Physical manufacture, pack, label (if contracted).
- Incoming quarantine and component testing they agreed to run.
- MMR/BPR (you still review).
- In-process controls.
- Finished-product testing they agreed to run, in-house or by a named lab.
- Plant sanitation, water, pest.
- Their own inspectional responses.

**Brand typically performs**

- Product concept, formula ownership decision (piece 32), label content, claims, 101.93 notification if any.
- Approval of specifications (even if the CMO drafted them).
- Approval of artwork.
- Finished-product testing on *your* nickel at some interval (piece 38).
- Retailer/3PL desks, Prop 65 decisions with counsel (piece 36).
- Customer complaints intake, even if you forward to the CMO (piece 27).
- Whether to ship.

**Shared, and therefore dangerous if unspoken**

- Reserve samples (piece 43). Who holds, whether you get a split.
- OOS payment and remake (piece 22, piece 23).
- Supplier qualification of *their* vendors versus *your* nominated vendors (piece 28).
- Change control (piece 19).
- Who talks to FDA if both firms are in the room.

Write the shared list. Silence is how lots ship twice and reserves sit nowhere.

## Quality control is a function, not a vibe

111.123 wants a quality control operation. The CMO has one, or they do not. You need a person — employee or contracted — who can read a COA and say no. If the only "QC" on the brand side is the founder who also runs ads, ads will win.

That person reviews: spec, label, first-article COA, skip-lot exceptions, complaint trends. They do not have to live in the plant. They have to have the files.

## Money is a quality control

If the agreement says the CMO pays for remakes when fill weight fails, and you pay when you change the formula after PO, fewer lots will be argued by invoice. If the agreement is silent, every OOS is a relationship event.

Finished-product testing on your nickel is a line item. Budget it. A SKU that cannot afford a 17025 metals panel cannot afford to exist (piece 40).

## What to put on one page before lawyers expand it

- Spec owner and approver.
- Identity: who runs, which methods.
- Finished-product rows, lab names, skip-lot rule.
- Reserve: where, how many, your split.
- Complaint routing, hours.
- Inspection and 483 notice.
- Change control.
- Who may release to the 3PL.

Counsel expands it. This page is so you do not walk into counsel empty.

## What this is not

A split of work is not a split of the name on the bottle. Do not tell a retailer "direct all quality to the CMO" as if you left the Act.

## Artwork is a brand-side release

The CMO will print what you send. If you send a disease adjective, they may still print it. Put "brand QC signs artwork" in the SOP. The sign is a 101.93 / FTC moment (piece 04), not a design moment. A pretty proof that no quality person saw is how intended use gets created by a freelancer.

## Training is a duty on both sides

111.12 wants people qualified for their jobs. The CMO trains blender operators. You train the person who approves artwork and the person who runs this checklist (piece 40). A founder who has never read 101.36 should not be the last eyes on a panel.

Write a one-page "brand QC" SOP: who reviews COAs, who may upload a SKU, who may tell a 3PL to receive. If those three names are the same person, write a backup. Vacations are how unreviewed lots ship.

## Who calls the 3PL when the lot is ugly

The CMO finds an OOS after you already inbounded. If the agreement is silent, each side waits for the other to email the warehouse. A day of waiting is a day of possible picks.

Write: the first party who knows the OOS calls the 3PL hold *and* emails the other party, same hour. Names and after-hours numbers. Practice it once. The recall tabletop (piece 27) can use this as the first move.

Silence here is not neutrality. Silence is a pick wave.

Do the jobs you wrote down. Read the jobs they wrote down. Then ask for the record that proves both.
