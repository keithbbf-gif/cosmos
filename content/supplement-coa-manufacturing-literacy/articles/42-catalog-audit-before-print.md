**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not an audit.

Printing 40 labels on a white-label catalog is how a founder creates 40 problems on a Tuesday. Audit first. Print second. The science/QA partner seat is empty on purpose. Do not fill it with a stock photo. Do the operator work yourself or hire a real person later.

This is a desk audit. It is not a plant inspection. Do both if the dollars are real.

## For each SKU, one page

1. **Buy type.** Catalog, contract, custom (piece 33). Who owns the formula (piece 32)?
2. **Panel draft.** 101.36 names and amounts. Unit factors written (piece 16).
3. **Spec exists?** Methods, ranges, contaminant list (piece 15). If no, you do not have 111.70.
4. **Claims.** Any structure/function sentence? Substantiation file or strike the sentence (piece 04). Any disease word? Strike.
5. **Landmines.** Hemp/CBD, NMN, NAC, peptides, SARMs, "research chemicals." Check the *dated* FDA page. If the status is ugly, the SKU is not a branding exercise.
6. **Allergens.** Matrix vs plant vs panel (piece 24).
7. **COA rights.** Finished-product rows, skip-lot honesty, your split (pieces 17, 38).
8. **Marks.** Directory lookup or no logo (piece 37).
9. **Stability.** Date on the bottle has a file or the date comes off (piece 26).
10. **Kill / keep / rewrite.** One word.

If you have four SKUs, you still do ten lines four times. If you have forty, you will want a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet is not the audit. The decisions are.

## Adjectives to strip before design

FDA approved. Pharmaceutical grade. Heavy-metal free. Every bottle tested (unless the plan is every finished lot and you will say that). cGMP certified by FDA. Proprietary unique blend (on a house formula). Clinically proven (without the trial). Detox. Hormone replacement. Anti-viral. Anything in `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`.

Designers will put them back. Give design a forbidden-words list. Check the proof.

## NDI and the quiet ingredient

21 U.S.C. § 350b is not a COA row. A new dietary ingredient without a notification can be the whole SKU. If you do not know whether the ingredient was in the food supply, you do not print. Counsel. Dated FDA pages. Empty seat, not a guess.

## What you do with "kill"

Do not keep the SKU on a "phase 2" mood board while ads run on phase 1 with the same brand name and a disease hashtag. Kill means no print, no PDP, no influencer brief.

Rewrite means the panel or the claim changes, then the SKU returns to line 1.

## Empty seats

Science/QA partner: citations, hepatotoxicity, dose. Counsel: structure/function, hemp/NMN/NAC, Prop 65. Operator: this pack.

Do not invent names for those seats on the site. Do not put "our scientists" on a catalog audit you did in a kitchen.

## After the audit

Only then: first-article (piece 34), three-way match (piece 35), checklist (piece 40), dock folder (piece 36).

## Do not audit from the catalog PDF alone

The catalog lies by omission. Ask for the spec pack and a recent finished-product COA on a similar SKU before you keep a row. If they have never generated a finished-product metals row on that house blend, your SKU will not be the first miracle.

## A worked four-SKU desk

SKU A: house magnesium glycinate. Panel and MMR agree. No disease sentence. Kill adjectives: none. Keep.

SKU B: "immune support blend" with a COVID-era blog already drafted by a freelancer. The blend is a house formula you do not own. Strike the blog. Rewrite the PDP to a nutrient-gap sentence or kill the SKU. The audit does not care that the freelancer already invoiced.

SKU C: hemp gummy. FDA's 26 January 2023 CBD conclusion is still the dated page you open. Unless a later page says otherwise, this SKU is not a dietary-supplement labeling exercise. Kill for this lane. Do not "park it in phase 2" on the same brand.

SKU D: a sport powder with an NSF logo in the first design file. Directory search: not listed. Logo off. Testing sentence can stay if a 17025 row exists.

That is one hour. It saves four print POs and one marketplace removal. Do it before photography.

## Four SKUs is enough to fail

You do not need forty rows to make a mess. Four catalog SKUs with two disease adjectives, one hemp gummy, and a "USP" logo that is not in the directory will do it. Audit those four before you hire a photographer.

If a SKU cannot survive the ten lines, it does not get a mood board. It gets a kill.

A catalog is a menu. A label is a representation. Audit the menu. Then print the representation. Not the other way around.
