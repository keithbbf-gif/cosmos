The finished-product COA is a photograph of a sample that lived in a lab. The lot then gets a liner, a cotton coil or none, a desiccant or none, a carton, a pallet, a trailer, a 3PL, a porch. Subpart M is holding and distributing. 111.455 and 111.460 are the holding and distributing neighbors. 111.465 is the retain under conditions that do not ruin it.

A passing Thursday HPLC does not bless a July trailer.

## Pack is part of the article

Induction seal, liner, bottle resin, headspace, desiccant, light for oils, a carton that can take a corner drop. Stability is done in *this* pack (piece 26). If you change the bottle and keep the date, you owe a reason.

Gummies weep. Powders cake. Softgels leak. Liquids expand. Write the pack in the MMR (111.210 wants packaging described). Incoming-quarantine the components (piece 20). A pretty bottle from a broker with no spec is still incoming.

Do not ship oils in a clear jar because the brand designer liked the color of the liquid. Light is a test condition you just chose.

## Ship stress

Temperature. Time. Stack height. A pallet wrap that crushes caps. A liquid that the 3PL will not receive after May. Ask the 3PL what they refuse (piece 36). Then write the ship spec: temperature range, "do not freeze," stack limit, whether a data logger rides the first summer load.

If you sell a probiotic with a CFU-through-expiry claim, a hot week is not a marketing problem. It is an assay problem you will meet later (piece 08, piece 26).

A COA pulled at the plant after the trailer is not the same as a COA pulled before. If the lot cooked, the photograph changed. You may need a destination sample. That is rare and grown-up. It is not "every bottle tested." It is "this lane is hostile."

## The retain stays calm

The 111.83 retain sits in labeled or ordinary conditions (piece 43). It will look better than a porch bottle. That is expected. The retain tells you what the plant released. The porch bottle tells you what distribution did. You may need both to answer a complaint (piece 27).

Do not store the retain in the same hot warehouse corner you just blamed.

## 3PL is a holder

Holding is a 111 verb. A 3PL that opens cases to "kitting" is a packer. Write what they may do. A kitting operation that applies a sticker with a structure/function sentence is labeling. A kitting operation that applies a disease sentence is a worse problem (piece 04).

Pest control at the 3PL is your problem when they hold your lot. Ask. Walk once if the volume is real.

## What you may say

Allowed when true: "Lot released at the plant against spec [ID]. Distributed under [ship spec]. Retain held at [condition]."

Not allowed: "farm to door tested" when the only test was Thursday at the plant.

Not allowed: a disease claim because the seal was pretty.

## The last sentence of the pack

A COA is a signed statement about a lot at a time and a place. Distribution is another time and place. Write both. Test the first. Specify the second. Keep a retain that still looks like the first.

Then you can answer a stranger on the internet in month eleven without guessing.

## Desiccant as a silent formula change

The first-article had a desiccant. Production forgot it for 3,000 bottles. The COA was pulled from bottles that still had one, or from bulk before pack. Moisture and micro and a gummy's water activity will not care about your excuse.

Incoming-quarantine the desiccant as a component (piece 20). The BPR issue table should show it. If the issue table is empty and the artwork photo shows a canister, you have a three-way miss (piece 35) that will show up in July.

## A logger on the first summer load

Buy a cheap temperature logger. Put it in the center of the first pallet that will sit in a trailer in July. Read it at the 3PL. If the curve is ugly, you have data for a pack or carrier change, not a feeling.

You do not need a logger on every pallet forever. You need one on the hostile lane until you believe the lane. Then spot-check.

If the logger is ugly and the retain is fine, you have a distribution story. If both are ugly, you have a plant-plus-truck story. Do not guess which.

Quality is not a vibe and it is not a stock photo. It is a lot number you can defend after the truck.
