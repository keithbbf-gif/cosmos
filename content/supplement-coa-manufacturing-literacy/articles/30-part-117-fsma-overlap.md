The same building can make a protein powder that is a dietary supplement and a protein powder that is a conventional food, or can pack both. 21 CFR 111 is the supplement cGMP book. 21 CFR 117 is the FSMA preventive-controls / food-cGMP book. They overlap on sanitation, allergens, and some records. They do not replace each other.

A food-safety plan is not a finished-product COA. A COA is not a hazard analysis.

## Why both books appear

Part 117 applies broadly to facilities that manufacture, process, pack, or hold human food, with exemptions you should not guess at. Dietary supplements are food. FDA has spent years telling plants that supplement operations still have food-safety duties. A preventive-controls qualified individual (PCQI) and a written food-safety plan show up on inspections that also cite 111.70.

If your CMO says "we're 111 so 117 doesn't apply," ask them to show the exemption they mean, in writing, from counsel. This draft will not invent one.

## What overlaps

- Sanitation, pest, water, plant design.
- Allergen control (piece 24).
- Some incoming supplier programs (117's supply-chain program is a cousin of 111's supplier qualification, not a twin).
- Recall plans.
- Hold and distribution hygiene.

If those systems are real, both books get easier. If they are lobby posters, both books get cited.

## What does not overlap

**111's identity test of each dietary ingredient.** 117 will not run your HPTLC for you.

**111.70 product specifications for strength and composition of a supplement.** A HACCP-style hazard analysis may not even list "D3 assay" as a hazard. It is still a 111 specification.

**Structure/function and 101.36.** Food-safety plans do not write your panel.

**Skip-lot finished-product verification under 111.75(c).** Different sentence from a 117 verification activity.

Do not hand a buyer a food-safety plan when they asked for the lot COA. Do not hand an investigator a COA when they asked for the allergen preventive control.

## Importers

If you import components or finished lots, FSVP (foreign supplier verification) may sit on the brand or the importer of record. That is a third book. Qualify the foreign supplier on purpose. A COA that cleared a dock is not FSVP.

## What you ask

1. Do you have a 117 food-safety plan that covers my product family?
2. Who is the PCQI?
3. Where does the 111 spec system live relative to that plan?
4. Allergen and sanitation records I can see for my line.

If they have a glossy 117 binder and no 111.70 specs, you have a food plant that has not yet become a supplement plant. If they have 111 specs and a drain under the tank with no sanitation record, you have the reverse hole.

## What you may say

Allowed when true: "Facility operates under 21 CFR 111 and maintains a 21 CFR 117 preventive-controls program."

Not allowed: "FSMA certified" as a consumer seal. FSMA is a statute. It does not mail stickers.

Not allowed: implying a food-safety plan makes the product treat a foodborne illness.

## Supply-chain program vs 111 qualification

117's supply-chain program (when it applies) wants you to control hazards that a supplier must control for you. 111's supplier qualification wants reliability of COAs for component specs, and still wants identity testing of dietary ingredients.

A vendor you "approved" for 117 because they have a food-safety audit is not automatically qualified for 111.75(a)(2). Run the confirmation (piece 28). Do not staple a GFSI certificate to the identity gap.

The two programs can share a site visit. They cannot share a shrug.

## A protein powder that is both

The same powder is sold as a dietary supplement (Supplement Facts, structure/function disclaimer) and, in another carton, as a food (Nutrition Facts). Same blender. Different labels. The 111 spec system still wants identity and strength for the supplement SKU. The 117 plan still wants the allergen and sanitation story for both.

If the CMO only keeps the 117 binder because "it's all food," your supplement SKU may have no 111.70 strength spec. If they only keep 111 sheets because "it's all supplements," the food carton may be missing a preventive-control they owe.

Ask which SKUs are which, and which book is the release book for *your* carton. Then look at last lot's file and see which book actually got a signature.

Two books. One floor. Read both. Then ask for the record that belongs to the question you were asked.
