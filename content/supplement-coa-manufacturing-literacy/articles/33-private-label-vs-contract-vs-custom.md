Salespeople use "white-label," "private label," "contract manufacture," and "custom" as if they were one SKU. They are three different buys. The COA rights, the MMR rights, and the price follow the buy, not the adjective.

## Private label / catalog white-label

You pick a row from a catalog. House formula. Shared or shareable lots. Low NRE. Fast. You approve a label that must match their spec (piece 31, piece 35).

You should still receive: a spec you approve, incoming identity records, a finished-product COA story for your lot or the mapped blend lot, a reserve conversation.

You should not expect: exclusive use, a full MMR, the right to move the recipe.

## Contract manufacture of *your* formula

You bring a recipe, or you paid to own one (piece 32). They execute. Batch size, pack, and tests are in a quality agreement (piece 29). Lots should be yours.

You should receive: MMR (yours), BPR for each lot, full spec pack, the right to name the lab on splits.

You pay more. You wait longer. You can leave.

## Custom / development

They develop a formula to a brief. Ownership must be written (piece 32) or you are back in house-blend land with extra invoices. Stability may start at zero (piece 26). First-article is not optional (piece 34).

A "custom" that is a flavor change on a house blend is not custom. It is a catalog option with a longer email chain.

## A table you can take to the call

| Question | Catalog PL | Contract your formula | True custom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who owns the recipe? | They do | You do | Written assignment or fight |
| Shared tank? | Often | Should not | Should not |
| MMR to you? | Rarely | Yes | Yes, after you own it |
| Unique lot? | Maybe mapped | Yes | Yes |
| Your lab splits? | Negotiate | Write it | Write it |
| Time to first ship | Weeks | Months | Months plus |
| Cost | Lowest unit, hidden test gaps | Higher unit, clearer file | Highest NRE |

If the salesperson cannot place the quote in a column, stop the call.

## How the COA changes

Catalog: you may get a house COA. Map it to your lot (piece 31). Reject it if your panel does not match the house panel.

Contract: the COA is your spec, your product name, your lot. Four columns (piece 15).

Custom: same as contract, plus a first-article COA that is allowed to fail without a launch date attached.

## How claims change

None of the three buys change DSHEA. A custom formula does not earn a disease claim. A catalog formula does not get to say "clinically proven" because the CMO put those words on a sell sheet. Substantiation is about the labeled article, not about the purchasing pattern (piece 04).

## Pick the buy on purpose

If you are learning the operator jobs, catalog plus a serious test budget is rational. If you already know the formula you will live with for five years, pay to own it and contract it. If you are still tasting flavors, do not sign a custom NRE that assigns nothing.

## You can mix columns in one catalog

SKU 1 catalog, SKU 2 contract-owned. That is fine. Do not apply the catalog COA mapping to the owned SKU, or demand an MMR on the catalog SKU the CMO will not give. Tag each SKU with its column in your internal sheet so the wrong ask does not poison the relationship — or hide a hole.

## First-year cost besides the unit price

Catalog: tests you add on your nickel, because their included COA is often incoming-plus-fill-weight. Budget splits.

Contract: NRE for MMR and first-article, plus your QC time. Unit price higher, surprises fewer.

Custom: development fees, stability start-up, possible failed first-articles. If ownership is not assigned, you paid custom prices for catalog rights.

Write a one-year cash picture for each column before you pick on unit price alone. A cheap catalog SKU with no finished-product metals is not cheap. It is a Prop 65 and retailer surprise (piece 09, piece 36).

## A quote that sits in two columns

Unit price looks like catalog. The email says "custom." The NRE is a label plate. The quality agreement, if any, is a one-pager about lead time.

Put the quote on the table above. If ownership is theirs and the tank is shared, it is catalog no matter what the subject line says. Pay catalog money. Demand catalog-appropriate COA mapping (piece 31). Do not budget a custom stability program you will never receive.

If you want the middle column, the quote has to say you own the MMR and lots are unique. If it will not say that, it is not the middle column.

The adjective on the quote is not the buy. The ownership sentence is the buy. Get that sentence. Then ask for the COA that matches it.
