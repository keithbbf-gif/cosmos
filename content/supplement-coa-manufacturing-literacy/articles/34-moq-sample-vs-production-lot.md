The sales sample arrived in a padded envelope. It tasted fine. The COA in the email is for a lot that ended in 2024. You sign a 5,000-bottle MOQ. The production lot is a different blend, a different capsule lot, a different label revision. You will be told you are being difficult when you ask for a first-article COA on *this* run.

You are not being difficult. You are matching lots (piece 06).

## Three objects

**Sales sample.** Often from a house lot, sometimes hand-filled, sometimes a leftover. Useful for taste and size. Not a release of your production lot. Not a stability lot unless you write it so (it will not be).

**Pilot / first article.** A small run of the actual MMR, actual components, actual pack — or as close as the plant will get. This is the run you test hard: identity, assay, metals, micro, pack, label. You are allowed to fail it. That is the point.

**Production lot.** The MOQ. The one you will ship. It gets its own representative sample (111.80) and its own release (piece 22). A passing first-article does not bless a production lot that used a new extract drum.

## MOQ is a commercial number with quality consequences

Minimum order quantities exist because changeover and documentation cost money. They also tempt you to skip first-article: "we're already at 5,000, just ship."

Write the sequence in the PO:

1. First-article quantity (even 200 bottles) on the production MMR.
2. Tests you will run, including a split on your nickel (piece 38).
3. Written release of first-article before the balance is scheduled.
4. Production lot COA and your right to hold shipment.

If the CMO will not break the MOQ into that sequence, pay for a separate first-article PO. It is cheaper than 5,000 bottles you cannot defend.

## Samples that lie politely

- A sample encapsulated in a clear "pretty" capsule when production is an opaque two-piece that changes fill.
- A sample with a desiccant; production without.
- A sample of the powder in a bag; production in a gummy.
- A COA for the flavor system, attached to the sample email.
- A "pilot" run on a bench blender when production is a 400 kg ribbon.

Taste the sample. Then ignore it as a quality document.

## Batch size and the MMR

111.205 wants an MMR for each batch size. A 5 kg first-article and a 200 kg production run are not automatically the same process. Uniformity, yield, and heat differ. If you scale, say so. Add a uniformity check on the first large lot (piece 21). Do not treat the 5 kg HPLC as a 200 kg release.

## Lead time is not a test plan

Twelve weeks to ship includes, if you write it, two weeks for first-article results. If you do not write it, week twelve is a truck and a shrug.

Retailers will ask why you are late. "We waited for a valid COA" is a grown-up sentence. "The sample tasted fine" is not.

## What you may say

Do not put "lab-tested sample" on a PDP as if it were the lot on the shelf.

Do put, in the buyer file: first-article lot ID, production lot ID, both COAs, the map between them.

## Hand-fills are not first-article

A technician fills 30 capsules at a bench to "show the color." Useful for a photoshoot you should not do yet. Not an MMR run. Not a blend-uniformity object. Not a stability start.

If the PO says first-article, the run uses the production blender, the production fill, and the production pack, even if the quantity is small. Write that. Otherwise you will approve a bench object and receive a ribbon-blender object.

## Paying for 5,000 to "get the test"

Some CMOs will only run metals on a production-size lot. They want the MOQ committed first. That is a real constraint. It is not a reason to skip a written hold on the balance.

Write: the first 500 (or one case) of the production lot is first-article for quality purposes even if it is commercially the same run. You test. You release the rest. If you fail, they do not ship the rest, and the payment split is in the PO.

If they must have all 5,000 in one pack-out, you still have a hold at their warehouse until the COA and your split land. A truck that left because "it was already boxed" is a choice you wrote when you omitted the hold.

The sample is a conversation. The lot is a record. Pay the MOQ after the record exists, not after the envelope arrives.
