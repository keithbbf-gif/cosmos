**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not an audit.

Three documents describe the same object. The Supplement Facts panel (101.36). The formula in the master manufacturing record (111.210). The finished-product COA. If they disagree, you do not have a product. You have a fight.

111.410 and 111.415 are the packaging-and-labeling neighbors: you control labels, you match them to the right lot. The three-way match is how you know which label is "right."

## The panel

101.36 tells you how to declare dietary ingredients: names, amounts per serving, % Daily Value where there is one, proprietary-blend rules, the serving size.

The panel is not a mood board. If it says 125 mcg D3 as cholecalciferol, the formula must issue cholecalciferol in an amount that can meet that claim after overage, and the COA must assay D3 in a unit you can convert to 125 mcg per serving (piece 08, piece 16).

Structure/function sentences elsewhere on the pack are 101.93 plus FTC (piece 04). They are not on the Supplement Facts table, and they do not get to be true "because the COA passed."

## The formula card / MMR list

Every dietary ingredient on the panel should appear in the issue list, as the same form. Every issue-list dietary ingredient that you are claiming should appear on the panel. Excipients have their own honesty problems (allergens, piece 24) but they are not Supplement Facts rows unless you choose to talk about them.

Watch:

- Glycinate on the panel, oxide in the MMR.
- "Ashwagandha extract 10:1" on the panel, powder in the MMR.
- A house blend weight that cannot contain the sum of the amounts you listed outside the blend.
- An overage that was never written down, so the issue amount looks like a panel error.

If you do not own the formula, demand at least a panel-level match and a written statement that the MMR matches the panel you approved (piece 32).

## The COA

Identity and assay rows named in the same words. Contaminant rows that match the spec you attached to *this* panel revision. Lot and SKU match (piece 06).

A COA that assays "vitamin D" while the panel specifies D3, or assays a marker you never claimed while skipping the claim you did make, is a near-miss. Near-misses ship. Then they fail retailer audits.

## Artwork control

The approved PDF is a controlled document. Version it. Quarantine printed labels (piece 20). The BPR should show which version was issued. A Canva tweak after QC approved v3 is a new label. It needs a new match.

Foreign-language panels are additional labels, not decorations. They have to match too.

## A one-hour ritual before print

1. Freeze panel vN, MMR rev N, spec rev N.
2. Read them out loud, row by row.
3. Do the unit conversions on paper.
4. Only then release the print PO.
5. When the first-article COA arrives, repeat the match against the printed bottle, not against the PDF from memory (piece 34).

If you are about to print 20,000 cartons, this hour is cheap.

## What this is not

Agreement among three documents is not an efficacy claim. It is how you avoid misbranding a lot that was otherwise in spec.

## Serving size changes are formula changes

Marketing wants "1 capsule a day" instead of "2" because the competitor does one. The assay is per capsule. The claim per serving doubles or halves depending which way you go. Metals per day move. The COA serving footnote is now wrong.

A serving-size change is a spec revision and a three-way rematch, not a Canva edit. Freeze it like a D3 change. Then reprint. Then test against the new serving (piece 16).

## The "other ingredients" line

The panel's other-ingredients list is where flavors, colors, flow agents, and capsule shells hide. The MMR issue table is where they actually get weighed. A COA will often ignore them unless an allergen or a residual solvent lives there.

If the panel says "hypromellose capsule" and the plant ran gelatin because the vegan shell was on backorder, you have a three-way miss even if the D3 assay is perfect. Incoming labels and incoming capsules are 111.80 work (piece 20). The three-way match includes the shell.

Read other ingredients out loud. It feels petty. It is how you avoid a vegetarian claim on a gelatin lot.

Fix the document that is wrong, then reprint, then test. Then ship.
