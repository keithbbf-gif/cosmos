**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not an audit.

A quality system is not the absence of problems. It is what you write when a problem arrives. Deviations, corrective and preventive action (CAPA), product complaints (111.553), and a recall procedure are four folders that should exist before you need them.

If the CMO says "we don't have deviations, we do things right," they have deviations. They just do not write them.

## Deviation

Something in this batch did not follow the MMR, the SOP, or the spec: a missed in-process check, a wrong component issued and caught, a fill-weight excursion, a power outage mid-blend, a label that was the wrong revision for twenty minutes.

Write: what, when, how much product, immediate containment (hold), investigation, disposition (piece 22, piece 23). 111.260 wants deviations on the BPR. 111.90 is the fence if someone wants to treat or reprocess their way out.

A deviation that is only a Slack message died.

## CAPA

Corrective: fix this. Preventive: stop the class. A CAPA that says "retrained operator" with no change to the hopper design that keeps feeding the wrong scoop is a sermon.

You do not need a pharma-grade electronic system. You need a log, an owner, a due date, and a check that the change worked. If every CAPA is "retrain," the system is a classroom.

## Complaints — 111.553

You must have written procedures for product complaints. Quality control reviews them to decide whether they represent a failure to meet spec or another serious problem. You keep the record (111.570 neighbors this).

A complaint is not only "I got sick." It is "the capsules are empty," "the lot number is unreadable," "this does not match the last bottle," "I found a beetle." Instagram DMs and Amazon one-stars are intake if they contain that information. Someone has to pull them into the file.

If a complaint might be a serious adverse event, you are in a different, time-limited reporting world. Counsel and the quality unit, not a blog post, run that clock. Do not diagnose the customer in the reply. Do not tell them the product treats the symptom they named.

Brand-side: you need a mailbox that is not only the CMO's. Customers will write *you*.

## Recall

A written procedure: who decides, who calls FDA if you are in that conversation, who calls the 3PL, who pulls lot lists, who writes the customer notice. Practice it once with a tabletop. The first time you open the folder should not be the first time you read it.

Lot traceability is the physical twin. If you cannot say which lots went to which 3PL in 20 minutes, the recall procedure is fiction. The BPR issue table and the ship records are how you speak.

## What you ask a CMO

1. Deviation log, last twelve months, count only if they will not show details.
2. One CAPA that changed a physical thing, not only a training slide.
3. Complaint SOP and how *your* brand's complaints reach them.
4. Recall SOP and a lot-trace exercise on your last shipment.

If they refuse all four, you have your walk-away paragraph (piece 41).

## A recall tabletop that fits in an hour

Pick a fictional OOS: D3 at 60% of claim, 800 cases at the 3PL, 200 already picked. Walk the room through: hold email, lot list, customer sentence (no disease language), who calls FDA if you are in that conversation, who writes the web banner. Time the lot list. If it takes more than 20 minutes, the ship records are the CAPA.

Do this once before you need it. Write the holes. That is the procedure becoming real.

## Amazon is intake

A one-star says "half the capsules were empty, lot 24A." That is a complaint. It is also a fill-weight and line-clearance clue (piece 21). If the brand manager replies "sorry you didn't like it" and refunds, and nobody opens 111.553, you will meet 24A again as a pattern at lot 24K.

Rule: marketplace text that names a defect or a lot number is intake within a working day. Quality reads it. The refund can still happen. The file has to happen.

If you do not control the marketplace account, write who pulls the text, and how often.

Write the problem. Then change the thing that caused it. Then keep the paper.

## What this is not

A complaint file is not an admission that the product failed to treat a disease. It is a quality record. Do not put "no complaints means it works" on a cart page.
