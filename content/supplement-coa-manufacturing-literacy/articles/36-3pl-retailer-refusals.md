A lot can pass the CMO's spec and die at the dock. 3PLs and retailers are not FDA. They are buyers with portals, warning-statute anxiety, and a right to refuse. Your job is to send a file that answers *their* questions, not only 111.75.

## Prop 65 desks

OEHHA's lead MADL is 0.5 µg/day; the oral cancer NSRL is 15 µg/day (piece 09). A 3PL that ships to California will ask for metals in mcg/day at your labeled serving, not in ppm. Do the math (piece 16). Bring counsel into the warning decision. Do not ask the 3PL to "just put the sticker on" without a file.

Other listed chemicals (some pesticides, some solvents, some colors) show up on worse days. Do not pretend the metals row closed the statute.

## Allergen matrices

A portal row that says "none" when the plant shares a whey line is how you get a refusal or a recall (piece 24). Send a matrix that matches the plant, the incoming statements, and the panel. If those three disagree, you are not ready to inbound.

## Amazon and marketplace packets

Marketplaces ask for COAs, sometimes for each lot, sometimes for a template they file once and forget. A template from 2024 on a 2026 lot is a costume (piece 06, piece 39). When they ask for "FDA certificate," they often mean facility registration or a 111 story they cannot name. Do not upload a forged "FDA approval." Send the true document and a one-paragraph explanation.

If they ask for claims substantiation, that is piece 04, not a metals PDF.

## What a dock-ready folder looks like

For each SKU / lot:

- Label PDF (the one on the bottle).
- Finished-product COA, four columns filled (piece 15).
- Serving-day metals worksheet.
- Allergen matrix.
- Spec revision and MMR revision IDs.
- SDS if they are a warehouse that wants one.
- Contact for quality, not only sales.

Name the folder `SKU_LOT`. When the 3PL emails at 4:55 p.m., you attach the folder. You do not screenshot a Slack PDF.

## Holds that are not your OOS

A 3PL will hold for damaged cartons, for a lot number that does not match ASN, for a "best by" they think is too short, for a liquid they do not want in summer. Those are commercial. Still write them into the complaint/deviation habit so you see patterns (piece 27).

A passing COA does not un-crush a carton. Pack is part of the product (piece 44).

## What you may not do

- Upload a competitor's COA "as an example."
- Upload an incoming extract COA as the finished lot.
- Promise "Prop 65 compliant" on a listing without counsel.
- Put disease claims on a marketplace page because the plant file was clean.

## A pre-inbound call

Ask the 3PL what they rejected last quarter in supplements. Write the list. Build the folder to that list plus your 111 file. If they cannot name a list, send the dock-ready folder anyway.

## Liquids in summer

Some 3PLs will not inbound liquids or gummies after a date. Ask in March. A passing COA in July does not open a dock that closed its SOP. Plan production around the dock, not only around the lab.

## What "COA required" means in three portals

Portal A wants a PDF per inbound, any lab, and a human ticks a box. They will accept an incoming extract COA if you are sloppy. Do not be sloppy.

Portal B wants metals in mcg/day and an allergen matrix. They will refuse a ppm-only sheet. Send the worksheet (piece 16).

Portal C wants a "certificate of free sale" or an "FDA registration." Those are different documents. Registration is a facility list. It is not a COA. Send the true document plus a one-paragraph cover. Do not generate a fake "FDA certificate" to satisfy a dropdown.

Read the portal, then build the folder. Do not build one fog PDF and hope.

## The ASN that does not match the bottle

Advanced shipping notice says lot 24A, 40 cases. Bottles say 24B because pack-out used the next blend and nobody updated the ASN. The 3PL refuses or, worse, receives and slot-codes 24A.

Your COA is for 24B. Your listing still says 24A. A marketplace lot ask will pull the wrong PDF.

The dock folder includes the ASN. Someone compares lot codes on a bottle photo to the ASN before the truck leaves the plant — or at receiving, before putaway. This is boring. It is also how a good COA becomes a bad inbound.

The plant release is necessary. It is not sufficient. The dock has its own spec. Meet both.
