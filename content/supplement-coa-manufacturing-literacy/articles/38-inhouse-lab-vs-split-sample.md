Part 111 does not require ISO/IEC 17025. It requires tests that are appropriate and scientifically valid (111.75(h)). An in-house lab can meet that. It can also be the same people who promised a retailer a ship date. Friday is a conflict of interest you manage with paper and a split, not with a speech about trust.

## When in-house is fine

- Methods are written, validated for the matrix, and have system suitability.
- Analysts are trained and the training file exists (111.12).
- The lab can fail a lot. There is an OOS SOP that is used (piece 22).
- The 17025 scope, if claimed, actually covers the rows (piece 18).
- You may send splits.

Fill weight, moisture, simple HPLC on a single vitamin, a disintegration check: these live happily in-house in a serious plant.

## When Friday shows up

- The HPLC "needs another run" until it passes.
- Identity is FTIR-of-the-blend because HPTLC would take until Monday.
- Metals are "ND" with no LOQ because the ICP is at the sister plant.
- The person who signs the COA also grants overtime to ship.

LXR Biotech (714568, 23 December 2025) used a third-party lab and still failed identity because the standard was the sample. Outsourcing does not cure a bad sample. In-house does not cure a bad incentive. You need a method *and* a sample you control.

## The split

You hold a reserve or a production increment (piece 43). You send a portion to a named outside lab with a scope that covers the row. You compare to the in-house result.

Write the schedule: first three lots of a new SKU; every fifth lot; every new supplier; every OOS. Write the accept rule: if they disagree beyond a predefined delta, hold and investigate. Do not "average."

Pay for it on your nickel at least sometimes. A split the CMO designs, ships, and explains is better than nothing and worse than a split you initiated from *your* retain.

## Blind and not blind

A lab that knows the spec and the hoped-for number can still be honest. A lab that receives a coded sample from you is harder to help. Use coded splits when the row is expensive or the relationship is new.

Do not send the CMO's already-ground, already-diluted aliquot as if it were your retain. Send the article.

## What to put in the quality agreement

- In-house methods list and validation summaries available on request.
- Named outside lab for named rows.
- Your right to split, frequency, who pays.
- OOS on a split is an OOS, not a "discussion."

## What you may say

Allowed when true: "In-house HPLC for D3; split to [lab] every fifth lot. Lot 24A split results [attach]."

Not allowed: "Independent lab tested" when the only lab is the CMO's and they are not independent of the ship date.

Not allowed: ignoring an outside fail because the in-house pass was cheaper.

## How to ship a split without ruining it

Use the same pack as the retain when you can: a finished bottle, induction seal intact, from the reserve or from cases you control. Label it with lot, date pulled, and your PO to the lab. Do not send a baggie of powder scooped from a hopper the CMO selected.

Ask the lab for a chain-of-custody line and a method list that matches your spec, not their "supplement panel default." A default panel that skips your D3 and runs a caffeine you do not claim is a wasted split.

When results return, sit them next to the in-house sheet in the lot folder. Initial the comparison. That initial is the point of the spend.

## Who pays, and why that sentence matters

If the CMO always pays for the outside lab, they will choose the cheapest scoped row and the friendliest turnaround. If you always pay, you will skip splits when ads are expensive. Split the cost in the agreement: they pay in-house; you pay the outside confirmation on the schedule; they pay a repeat if in-house and outside disagree beyond the delta.

Put a yearly cap so neither side is surprised. Then run the first split on lot one, not on lot twelve when a retailer asks.

Friday will always want to ship. The split is how Thursday still has a vote. Budget the vote. Then use it.
