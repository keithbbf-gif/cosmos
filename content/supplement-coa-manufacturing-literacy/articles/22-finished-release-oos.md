Quality control releases the lot. Sales does not. The founder with a launch calendar does not. 111.87 puts material review and disposition on quality control personnel. 111.123 is the broader quality-control operations list. 111.260 wants the BPR to show that review.

If the only "release" is a Shopify upload, the lot was released by marketing.

## What a release file is

For this batch:

- MMR revision used.
- BPR complete, yields in range or explained.
- Incoming components all released (piece 20).
- In-process results in spec or dispositioned (piece 21).
- Finished-product results versus the approved spec — every row you owe this batch under the skip-lot plan (piece 17).
- Label and pack match the approved artwork (piece 35).
- Reserve pulled (piece 43).
- QC signature, date, disposition: release or reject.

The COA is one exhibit. It is not the file.

## What OOS means

Out-of-specification means a valid result sits outside the spec you wrote. It is not a mood. It is not "a little low but customers won't notice."

111.77 and 111.90 restrict what you may do with a product or component that missed a spec: you do not reprocess or treat on a hope. Quality control conducts a material review and makes a disposition. Reprocessing has to be permitted by the rule and by that review.

An OOS that is actually a lab error has a path: invalidate the result with evidence (sample mix-up, failed system suitability, calculation error), then retest under a written procedure. An OOS that is the lot has a different path: hold, investigate the process, reject or, if the rule and QC allow, reprocess.

"Retest until the number moves" without an invalidation is theater. Ask for the OOS SOP. Ask for the last OOS. If they have never had one, they are lucky or they retest quietly.

## Invalidating a result is work

You need a reason that would survive an investigator who likes details:

- The sample was the wrong lot (then you have a chain-of-custody problem).
- The standard failed.
- The instrument failed system suitability.
- The analyst used the wrong dilution and the notebook shows it.

"We don't like the number" is not on the list. "The customer is waiting" is not on the list.

If the retest is valid and still OOS, you are back in disposition. Two out-of-three passing is not a rule unless you wrote a statistically defensible one before the lot existed, and even then FDA has seen that movie.

## Who you call

The quality unit at the CMO, and *your* person who has the right to say no (piece 29). If you do not have that person, sales will say yes.

Notify: do not let a CMO silently reject and remake while you are still advertising the original ship date as if the lot existed.

## What you may not do

- Ship the OOS lot with a disclaimer on the PDP.
- Blend the fail into the next batch to "average it."
- Change the spec after the result to make a pass (unless you have a controlled spec change that applies going forward, not backward).
- Put "third-party tested" on a lot whose third-party result failed and whose in-house retest "passed."

## A release stamp is a sentence

"I have reviewed this file. The batch meets the specifications. I release lot 24A for distribution." Name, date.

## Partial shipment is still a release

The CMO wants to ship 30% of the lot that passed and hold 70% "for the extra metals row." If the 30% is released, QC released it. The file must be complete for that 30% — including the rows you owe — or you are releasing on a promise of a later page.

Do not inbound a partial that is missing a release row you already told a retailer you have. Wait for the page. Or change the spec in a controlled way *before* the lot, not during the truck.

## A 92% assay and a 90–110% spec

Result: 92% of claim. Spec: 90–110%. That is a pass. A founder who wanted 100% will ask for a retest "to be sure." A retest that lands at 88% is now an OOS on a lot you already felt good about. A retest that lands at 101% does not erase the 92% unless you invalidated 92% for a real reason.

Write the rule before the lot: how many determinations, what you do with a value inside spec that you dislike (nothing), what you do with a value outside spec (hold, investigate). Curiosity retests are how you manufacture OOS events.

If 92% worries you because stability will eat the rest, that is a spec or overage or date problem (piece 26). It is not a reason to retest a passing lot until you like it.

If you cannot say that sentence, do not upload the SKU. The calendar will recover. The warning letter will not unwrite itself.
