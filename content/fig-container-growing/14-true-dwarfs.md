# True dwarfs — Little Miss Figgy, Fignomenal, Petite Negra

Most figs are "dwarf" on the tag and eight feet in
your driveway. A true dwarf is a plant that stays
in the four-foot class without you turning into a
barber. Little Miss Figgy, Fignomenal, and Petite
Negra are the ones I will put on a balcony without
writing an apology in advance.

They still need sun. Dwarf is not shade-tolerant.
They still need a real pot. Ten to fifteen gallons
is the long-term home, not a 3-gallon ceramic you
saw next to the indoor ficus. The leaves are still
fig leaves. They still drink.

Little Miss Figgy is a sports-page plant — compact,
burgundy-green foliage, fruit that looks like a
small dark fig and tastes like a small dark fig.
Good, not a religion. It is the one you buy when
the other person in the house said no more trees.
It is also the one that looks good enough to keep
near the table.

Fignomenal is even shorter in the ads. In a pot
with decent food it is a low mound that fruits
along the new wood. People overwater it because
it looks like a houseplant. It is not a
houseplant. Give it the same drain and the same
winter as the big figs, scaled down.

Petite Negra (sometimes sold in the dwarf dark
cluster) stays small and makes a dark fruit with
real flavor if it gets heat and a full day of
sun. In Zone 7 it needs the same winter help as
any potted fig. Dwarf is not hardy. Dwarf is
short.

What dwarfs buy you is moving. A 12-gallon fabric
bag you can lift. A plant you can put on a
hand truck without recruiting a neighbor. In
Zone 7 that is not cute. That is how the plant
sees the garage every year without becoming a
project.

What they do not buy you is a heavy crop for
drying and jam. You will eat them out of hand
and maybe fill a bowl for a guest. If you want
pints, you want a bigger variety in a bigger
pot. Keep both if you have the room. Do not
expect one dwarf to be the orchard.

Prune lightly. You are not fighting a monster.
Take out the dead, the inward, the one branch
that ruins the shape. Summer pinch if it tries
to get elegant and tall. They fruit on new
growth; a light hand keeps the fruit where you
can see it.

Winter: same rules, smaller pot, faster freeze.
A small bag left out in Zone 7 is a popsicle.
Bring it in. The advantage of dwarf is that
you will actually do it.

Flavor on dwarfs varies. Some are
truly good. Some are "good for a
patio plant," which is a sentence
that means you will eat them and
not put them on a plate for
company. Fruit them a year before
you buy three more of the same
brand.

If the only place you have is a third-floor
balcony with a weight limit, this is the
chapter that matters. Buy one dwarf, one
stout pot, one saucer you empty, and do not
let anyone talk you into a Mission in a
barrel up there. The barrel will win
the argument with the floor.
