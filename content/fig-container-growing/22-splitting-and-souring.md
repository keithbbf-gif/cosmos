# Split skins and sour hollows

Two different failures get blamed on
"the rain." One is a split skin. The
other is a sour, wet hollow behind an
eye that opened. They can happen on
the same plant in the same week. They
are not the same fix.

Splitting is a water balloon. The fig
was dry, then it took a drink — your
hose or a thunderstorm — and the skin
could not keep up. In a pot you are
the weather more often than you think.
A drought-and-drown cycle in July will
split fruit even if the sky stayed
empty. Keep the mix even as the fruit
colors. You will still lose some to a
two-inch rain. You will lose fewer if
the pot was not already a crisis.

Split figs are still food if you get
there first. Eat them the same day.
They do not store. They do not jam as
cleanly. They are fine over yogurt if
you are not proud.

Souring is microbiology. Yeast and
vinegar flies enter through the
ostiole, especially on open-eye
varieties in humid air. The inside
becomes a fizz. The skin can look
almost ripe. You smell it when you
open it. There is no household spray
that makes an open-eye fig into a
closed one. Variety is the control.
Celeste, some LSU figs, a few others
run tighter eyes. Kadota and some
large purple types run looser. In
Mobile, pick the eye. In a dry Zone
9 slope, you have more freedom.

What helps anyway: pick on time. A
fig that is hanging and soft is a fig
the flies also found. Do not leave
"almost" fruit for four extra days
because you will be busy. Harvest is
part of culture.

Do not overhead-water ripening plants
every evening in a humid climate. Wet
eyes and wet leaves are a party. Water
the mix.

A fig that split and then soured is
both problems stacked. You will have
days like that after a Gulf storm.
Clean up the dropped fruit so you are
not farming flies for next week.

I do not bag every fig. People do it.
Paper bags, mesh, organza. It can
save a show plant. On a 20-gallon
workhorse in a heavy set, I net the
plant and I pick. If souring is the
story of your summer, change
varieties before you invent a
packaging career.

If you only grow open-eye figs in a
wet Zone 8 because you like the
look, keep a Celeste as the week
the tropical storm sits on you. The
open-eye fruit can still be
wonderful in a dry stretch. A
collection that can only succeed in
perfect weather is a hobby that
fails on the weekend you have
company.

The short version: even water for
splits, closed eyes for souring, and
a bowl in your hand at the right
hour for both.
