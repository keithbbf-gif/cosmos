# Pruning a pot fig as a bush

A fig in a pot is not a standard tree unless
you have a reason. A single trunk looks
formal and then you cannot get the plant
through the door. Bush form — a short stool,
three to five arms, open middle — is how you
keep fruit in reach and the pot movable.

Do the serious cutting when the plant is
dormant or just waking. In Zone 7 that is
late winter, after you know what the cold
killed, before the sap is a fountain. In
Zone 9 you have a longer window and less
excuse to wait until the plant is a tangle
in June.

Take out dead wood first. Then the branch
that rubs. Then the one that aims at the
center. Then anything you cannot defend
as fruiting wood or a future arm. What
you leave should be able to stand without
a stake in ordinary wind. If it cannot,
you have a sail on a tub.

How hard you cut depends on the crop you
want. Figs make a breba on last year's
wood and a main crop on this year's.
A hard cut — arms back to a foot or two
— throws vigorous new wood and a main
crop, and it costs you the breba. A
light cut keeps the breba and can leave
you a tall, shy plant. In a pot I usually
split the difference: keep a few arms
for structure and breba, shorten the
rest to force new fruiting wood that
stays in the net.

Summer work is pinching, not a haircut.
When a new shoot has five or six leaves
and you do not want another six feet,
pinch the tip. The plant will branch.
Those laterals carry fruit. This is how
a pot stays a pot plant instead of a
small tree in a can.

Do not seal cuts. Do not paint them.
Do not prune in late fall in Zone 7
just to look tidy for the holidays.
Tidy now is tender later. Clean tools
are enough. Figs are not oak. They
bleed milky sap and then they get on
with it.

A plant that has been in the same pot
for years will push a lot of twiggy
inside growth. Thin it. Light on the
wood ripens fruit. A dark thicket in
the middle is a rust parlor.

If you inherited a one-trunk specimen
and you like it, keep it, and accept
that you will wrap or hoist a taller
object. I would still drop a couple
of low scaffolds so a broken top is
not the end of the plant.

The test: after you prune, can you
roll the pot, net it, and pick the
far side without a ladder? If not,
you are not done. The plant will be
prettier in September if it is
shorter than your pride wants in
March.
