# Violette de Bordeaux — small pot, serious flavor

This is the fig I point at when someone says
container fruit tastes like a compromise. Violette
de Bordeaux — Negronne in some mouths — is a small
dark fig with a dense, jammy berry flavor and a
skin you do not have to negotiate with. It is not
huge. It does not need to be.

The plant is naturally more compact than a Brown
Turkey. Fifteen to twenty gallons is plenty. It
sits on a patio and looks like you meant to
design something. That vanity is useful. You will
water a plant you like looking at.

Heat helps the flavor. In Zone 9 it is at home.
In Zone 8 it is excellent in a hot spot. In Zone
7 it is worth growing if you keep the wood and
you have a wall that throws heat. Without heat
the fruit can be good and not haunting. With
heat it is why people collect figs.

It will give a breba if you save the old wood,
and a main crop on the new. The fruit is small
enough that birds treat the plant like a feeder.
Net. Or pick every afternoon in the ripe week
like a person with a hobby.

Closed-ish eye, better than the wide-open types
in humidity, not Celeste. In a wet Gulf week I
still prefer Celeste for reliability and this
one for the bowl I eat myself. That is a
reasonable two-pot yard.

Winter: not a Chicago Hardy. I would not leave
a Violette pot out for a Zone 7 single-digit
night and then write a surprised post. Garage.
In Zone 8, wrap the pot and watch the forecast
like an adult.

The plant can sulk in a pot that stays wet. It
wants the mix in the earlier draft — bark,
air, a drain. It will show yellow leaves if
you love it to death with water and nitrogen.
Keep it a little hungry after it has a frame.
Flavor figs do not need to look like a
hedge.

In a cool, cloudy Zone 7 summer it will still
grow. The fruit may be good and short of the
jam you tasted in a hot year. That is not the
plant failing. That is heat as an ingredient.
Put it on the wall that throws afternoon light
and keep the wood so next year's breba has a
chance. A few early dark figs in June are how
this variety pays the garage rent.

If you are going to keep only two varieties
and you already have a reliable brown or a
Celeste, this is the second pot. It justifies
the whole silly business of rolling plants
around. When one is truly ripe — neck soft,
dark, a little collapse — eat it in the
driveway. That is the quality-control step.
If you still think figs are just cookies
from a bag, you picked it a week early or
you bought a different plant.
