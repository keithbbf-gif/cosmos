# Salt crust and tap water

A white ring on the pot rim
is not hard-water décor. It
is what is left when the
water leaves and the
dissolved stuff stays. In a
pot you water often, that
stuff adds up. Figs show it
as burned edges, yellow new
leaves, a mix that wets
badly, and a plant that
looks hungry after you just
fed it.

Municipal water in a lot of
this band is liquid
limestone. Fine for people.
A year of it in a closed
box of bark and peat is a
slow pH walk. Zone 9
interior water can be
worse. Softened water —
the kind that traded
calcium for sodium — is
the one I will not use as
the daily drink. Sodium is
a pot killer. If the house
is on a softener, take the
fig hose from the
untreated bib if you have
one, or catch rain.

Leaching is the boring
fix. Once in a while, in
the growing season, water
until a lot leaves the
drain, wait, do it again.
You are rinsing the box.
Do this on a day you
will not then sit the
pot in the leachate.
The saucer goes empty.

A mix reset every couple
of years does more than a
product. If the crust is
thick and the plant is
chlorotic, dump and start
clean. Chelated iron can
green a plant up while
you decide. It does not
remove the salt.

Fertilizer is salt too.
The leaf-factory draft
and this one are
relatives. If you feed
hot and water little,
you built the crust
yourself. Rain is a
leach if the pot is in
it. A covered porch
never gets that charity.

Rain barrels help and
then they grow
mosquitoes and a smell
if you ignore them. I
use rain when I have
it. I do not build a
religion that fails in
a drought.

If the white crust is
only on a clay pot, the
clay is also sweating
out the water. The mix
may still be fine. Look
at the plant. Look at
the new leaves. The
rim is a clue, not a
verdict.

If you collect rain,
use it on the flavor
figs first. They show
chemistry faster in
the fruit. The
workhorse brown can
take the tap a little
longer. That is not
science so much as
how I spend a limited
barrel.

I scrape the rim when
it bothers me. That is
aesthetics. The leach
is the culture. Once a
month in a heavy
watering season is
plenty for a plant that
is otherwise happy.
More than that and you
are washing food out
as fast as you put it
in. Balance is a word I
usually hate. Here it
is just the hose,
twice, and then stop.
