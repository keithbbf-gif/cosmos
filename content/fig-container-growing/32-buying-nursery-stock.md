# Buying a fig that isn't a tourist

Spring trailers sell figs to
people who are hungry for fruit
and light on questions. You can
get a good plant that way. You
can also get a nameless brown
fig in a soggy 3-gallon that
has been sitting in the aisle
since March.

I look at the wood, not the
tag photo. A few sturdy canes
beat a spray of pale, forced
tips. Leaves should be the
right green for the week, not
yellow and not black at the
edge. Slip the pot if they
will let you. Roots should be
white-ish and present, not a
sour circle and not a hollow
mix with one string.

Name: believe it a little.
Big-box "Brown Turkey" is a
brown fig. A specialty
nursery with a reputation can
sell you a Violette that is a
Violette. A Facebook post
with a name and a zip code
three states away is a
cutting with a story. Stories
are fine if you price them
as stories.

I prefer a plant grown closer
to my climate than a plant
flown from a greenhouse that
never saw a Zone 7 April.
Not always possible. When it
is, the hardening is already
done.

Avoid the flowering / fruiting
display that has a plant in
full fruit in a tiny pot in
May. That plant spent its
savings for the display. You
can buy it if you pick the
fruit off and treat it as a
rescue. Do not buy it as a
crop.

Ask when it was potted. A
plant stuffed into a new can
last week will slump on your
deck. A plant that has rooted
its can will ship the shock
better.

Price: a cutting from a
neighbor is the best value in
this whole series if the
neighbor's plant fruits in
your county. A named plant
from a real fig person is
worth money if you want that
name. A $40 tourist fig at
the hardware store is worth
it if the plant is healthy
and you needed a plant that
day. It is not worth it if
you already have three brown
figs and you are collecting
labels.

If the mix is a waterlogged
peat brick, I do tease it
and I do move it into an
open mix the first week,
because that brick will not
dry in a kind way. That is
the exception. A healthy
rooted can waits.

Take it home, do not
repot the first afternoon
unless the pot is a swamp,
and do not put it on the
west slab at noon. The
first week is where
nursery plants die, not
the first winter. Winter
you can plan. The first
Saturday you have to
survive. Keep the receipt
photo of the tag. Names
fade. Arguments do not.
