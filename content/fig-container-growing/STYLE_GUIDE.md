# Style guide — Fig container growing (Zones 7–9)

Pot-culture notes for common fig (*Ficus carica*) in USDA Zones 7, 8, and 9.
These are **staged drafts** for a future site import, not live pages.

`voice_check: human` means the writer draft met this file. `voice_check: edited`
means an editor read the piece aloud and tightened grammar, voice, and
guardrails. Neither value is a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Who is speaking

A working gardener who has moved pots, lost wood to a bad January, and eaten
a Celeste on a humid porch. Practical, not pastoral. The reader may be on a
deck in Richmond, a slab in Houston, or a rental with a weight limit. Do not
talk down. Do not write like a catalog or a university extension bulletin.

First person is allowed for yard observation. It is not a diary.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" as a metaphor for a field (literal landscape fabric/pebbles OK)
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- three-beat synonym piles ("thrive, flourish, and succeed")
- invented USDA guarantees or "will always" crop promises

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness,"
"elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach,"
"empower," "in this article we will explore," "comprehensive guide," "let's dive."

No COSMOS. No AI. No process talk about how the draft was written.

## Zone and culture guardrails

- **culture: pot** on every essay. In-ground advice belongs in other packs.
- Name the zone honestly. Zone 8 is not "easy winter." Zone 9 is not "no winter."
- Varieties: recommend for *pots in this band*, not for every county. Tags lie.
- Do not invent a nursery SKU, a spray schedule as law, or a frost date for
  "your area" without telling the reader to use local frost dates.

## Cure theater

Avoid lines that sound like a warranty: "this always works," "guaranteed wood,"
"never fails." Prefer conditional language the draft already uses elsewhere:
"can," "in a polite winter," "when the forecast turns ugly."

## Line breaks

Calendar essays (`36`–`38`, late-series short-line pieces) may use narrow
line breaks for scanability. Prose essays should not break mid-phrase (e.g.
"even an ugly clay bed," not `even a` / `ugly` on two lines).
