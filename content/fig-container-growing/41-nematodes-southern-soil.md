# Nematodes and why pots win down South

Root-knot nematodes are why a
fig in an old Southern bed
looks like a plant that is
always a little sick. Yellow,
stunted, wilt on days it
should not, roots that are
gnarled with knots if you
ever dig. The soil is not
"tired." It is occupied.

A pot is a quarantine. Clean
mix, clean can, no garden
soil "to make it real." That
last habit is how you import
the problem you were fleeing.
If your trowel was in the
bed, wash it. This sounds
precious until you see a
three-year pot decline
because you topped it with
a scoop from the pecan
tree's dripline.

LSU figs and some old
Southern plants have a
reputation for doing
better in nematode
country. Better is not
immune. A pot still
wins because you can
start clean and stay
clean.

Do not dump old pot mix
into a bed and then take
it back. Do not set a
fabric bag on infested
soil and leave it until
roots punch through. A
board, a caddy, a slab —
something between the
bag and the ground.

Symptoms in a pot can
mimic drought and
nutrient lock. If you
have been clean and the
plant is still a knot of
stunt, slide it out. If
the roots are galls, you
have a decision. Some
people keep the plant
and live with a smaller
life. I would rather
take a cutting from the
top — nematodes are a
root problem — and
start a new pot. The
old mix goes in the
trash, not in the
tomato bed, if you
are trying to have a
tomato bed.

There is chemistry for
ground. I am not
writing a chemical
program for a
homeowner bed in a
content draft. I am
saying the pot is the
control you can
actually run without
turning the yard into
a project you will
resent.

If you take a cutting
from a stunted
in-ground plant, take
wood, not a root
division. You are
trying to leave the
knots in the ground.
Grow the cutting in
fresh mix. Do not
"help" it with a
scoop of the old hole
for microbes. Those
microbes include the
reason the hole
failed.

Zone 7 has nematodes
too, in places. The
South is just where
the story is loud.
If your in-ground fig
failed twice in the
same hole, stop
honoring the hole.
Honor a can. The hole
does not get a third
fig as a tribute.
