# Breba vs main crop — what the pot changes

A fig can feed you twice. The breba sits on last
year's wood and ripens early, sometimes in June
in this band. The main crop sits on this year's
shoots and ripens late summer into fall. A lot
of garden writing mentions this and then goes
back to fertilizer. In a pot, the distinction
is the whole winter argument.

If you lose the wood, you lose the breba. That
is why Zone 7 in-ground figs are often one-crop
plants whether the variety is "breba-heavy" or
not. The top dies. The plant resprouts. There
is no last year's wood. The pot exists to keep
that wood in a garage so June has something to
ripen.

Not every variety is worth the trouble for
breba. Some make a few early figs that taste
like they were in a hurry. Some — Desert King
is the famous San Pedro example — are grown
for the breba because the main crop wants a
wasp you do not have. Common figs in home
pots are usually main-crop plants with a
breba bonus. Know which one you bought.

The pot changes the main crop too. A plant
that did not spend April rebuilding a canopy
can set fruit on new wood sooner. In Zone 7
that can be the difference between ripe in
September and green in the first frost. In
Zone 9 it can mean a long season of both,
with a stall in the worst heat.

Pruning is how you pick a side. Hard winter
cut: main crop, compact plant, little or no
breba. Light cut: breba possible, taller
plant, more to net. I keep a couple of arms
on the flavor figs I overwinter well, and I
cut the workhorses harder so they stay on
the dolly. That is a household decision, not
a law.

A breba that sets and then drops is usually
water or a late freeze, not a moral failure.
Early fruit sits through the most bipolar
weather of the year. In Zone 8 a warm
February and a mean March will burn breba
you were already bragging about. Have a
sheet. Do not count them until they soften.

If your plant never makes a breba, you may
have a variety that does not, or you may
be pruning it off, or you may be killing
the wood every winter and calling it
normal. Look at the plant in January.
Last year's long shoots still alive? Then
you can have a breba conversation. Sticks
from the soil line? You are in main-crop
country, and the pot is not doing its
first job yet.

I eat the breba standing up. There are not
enough for a project. The main crop is the
project. Design the pot, the winter, and
the prune for that, and treat June as a
gift when it shows.
