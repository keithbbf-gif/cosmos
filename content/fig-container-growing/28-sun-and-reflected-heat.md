# Sun, glare, and afternoon burn

Figs want sun. That sentence gets
people in trouble because "sun" on
a website is not "west brick wall
plus concrete plus a black pot" in
July.

I want six or more hours of real
sky. Morning sun is the kind part.
Afternoon sun is the crop in Zone
7 and the skillet in Zone 9. A
plant that only sees sky from 7 to
10 a.m. under a porch roof will
live and will not ripen like the
catalog.

Reflected heat is a separate
ingredient. White siding, a car,
a pool, a pale paver — they bounce
light onto the lower leaves and
onto the pot wall. You get scorch
on the leaf edge and cooked roots
while the weather app says 91°F,
which was true in the shade of
the instrument.

Leaf scorch from glare looks like
the margins dried. It is not
always drought. Check the pot
weight. If the pot is heavy and
the burn is on the side facing
the wall, move the pot or put a
board between it and the bounce.
A little afternoon shade cloth
in Zone 9 is not softness. It is
how you keep photosynthesis
instead of a plant that shuts
down at 1 p.m.

In Zone 7 I err toward the
hottest honest spot I have that
I can still water. We do not have
sun to waste. A south or west
patio is a gift if the pot is
sized and the mix is right. In
Zone 8 I still want that spot
and I watch August. In Zone 9 I
want a place the plant can
retreat — not full shade, a
step back from the griddle.

Indoor sun is not a substitute
for the patio. A fig in a
window in January, if you
forced it awake, will stretch
and sulk. Let it sleep.

Young leaves bleach when you
move a garage plant into June
noon. Harden. Three days of
morning sun is cheaper than a
month of spotted leaves.

If you have one good wall, put
the flavor fig there and the
workhorse in the next-best
light. Do not line up five pots
where only the front row sees
the sky. The back row will
rust and drop fruit and you
will write a more complicated
essay than this one.

A cheap infrared thermometer
aimed at a black pot wall in
August will end an argument
faster than a forum thread.
If the wall is hot enough to
make you pull your hand back,
the roots already know.

Sun is the crop. Glare is a
tax on the crop. Learn the
difference on your own patio
at 2 p.m. in July, not from
a zone map. The map does not
own your slab.
