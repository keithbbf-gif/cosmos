# Wind, tipping, and a stake that isn't theater

A fig in a pot is a sail
on a bucket. The first
real wind after a rain
is when you learn
physics. The plant is
heavy at the top, the
pot is round, the deck
is slick.

I do not stake a bush
that is in scale with
its pot. I shorten the
bush. A six-foot whip
in a 15-gallon is not
a staking problem. It
is a pruning problem.
Cut it, and the wind
has less to hold.

When I do stake, the
stake goes in the pot,
not into the deck, and
the tie is soft and
low. A tall bamboo
lashed at the top of a
whip just moves the
break to the tie. Two
stakes and a loose
figure-eight is
enough for a first-year
column. Check the tie
monthly. Fig wood
swells. Twine becomes
a girdle while you are
proud of the height.

Tipping: a wide pot
tips less than a tall
narrow one. Fabric bags
have a low center of
gravity when they
slump, which is an
ugly virtue. Barrels
are stable until the
plant is a tree, and
then they are a tree
in a barrel. Set pots
off the wind alley
between houses. That
alley is why your
trash cans travel.
Your fig will travel
too.

After a storm I walk
the pots before I
drink the coffee.
Upright what tipped.
A plant on its side
for a day cooks
leaves on the
pavement and dries
the now-exposed
root ball. Water
after you stand it
up if the mix
poured out. Add mix
if you see roots.

On a balcony, wind
is the climate.
A rail helps if you
can secure the pot
without creating a
trip and without
violating a lease.
A dwarf is the
honest plant. A
tall fan is a
kite.

I do not use
decorative rocks
on top of the mix
to "weigh it down."
They weigh the
surface, bake it,
and do nothing
once the sail
catches. Volume
and a low prune
are the weight.

After you stand a
tipped pot up,
look at the
far side of the
trunk. The break
is often there,
hidden, a wrinkle
that becomes a
dieback in a
week. Cut to
clean wood if
it is a split.
A wrapped wound
on a fig is
usually theater.
A clean cut is
work.

If a plant tips
three times in a
summer, the site
is wrong or the
plant is too big
for the can. Move
or cut. A stake
is not a third
summer of the
same argument. The
wind does not get
bored. You do.
