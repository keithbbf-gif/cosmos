# Weight, dollies, and the deck

A 25-gallon pot full of wet mix and a
fig is not a houseplant. It is a piece
of wet furniture. People learn this in
October with their back, or they learn
it when a deck board talks back.

I buy the dolly when I buy the pot.
Planted, the thing is heavier than the
empty can suggested. Four-wheel plant
caddies with a lip are better than a
flat square that the pot slides off
when you hit the threshold. Locking
wheels matter on a slope. I have
chased a fig toward a step. Once.

Weight estimates are ugly and useful.
A 15-gallon wet can easily sit in the
80–100 pound neighborhood. A 25-gallon
is more. A half barrel is a two-person
object. If you cannot recruit a person
on frost night, do not own that pot in
Zone 7. Own two 15s.

Decks have ratings. A cluster of five
big pots along a rail is a line load
the builder did not have in mind. Spread
them. Put the heaviest over a beam if
you know where the beam is. If you do
not know, do not build a fig wall on
the cantilevered pretty part. I have
seen a rented deck with a dip that
started as "just pots."

Fabric bags on a dolly dry from the
bottom if the fabric sits in a solid
tray. Either drill the tray or use a
slatted caddy. Plastic cans on a
caddy need the drain to land over
air, not over a puddle.

Thresholds, gravel, and lawn will
defeat cheap wheels. If the garage
path is gravel, put a board down or
accept that you will carry small pots
and only roll the ones on a hard
path. Design the winter route in
June, when you are still romantic.

Elevators and balconies: check the
rules and the weight. A dwarf in 12
gallons is a balcony plant. A
Mission in a barrel is a fantasy
about a balcony.

I do not put saucers under pots on
a wood deck and forget them. Standing
water stains, and mosquitoes do not
care that you meant to empty it.
If you need a saucer for the
downstairs neighbor, empty it.

Stairs: I will carry a 7-gallon.
I will not hero a 25-gallon down
four steps on a frost night.
If the only winter space is
down stairs, the collection
lives in pots you can lift, or
you build a wrap station at
the top and stop pretending
the basement is the plan.

The dolly is not a failure of
toughness. It is how pot culture
stays pot culture. The day you
cannot move the plant, you have
an in-ground fig that happens to
be thirsty. Buy the wheels. Then
use them in June once so October
is not the first time.
