# July water without the daily panic

A potted fig in summer is a bucket of leaves attached to a limited tank.
The tank is the mix. When the tank is empty the plant does not "toughen
up." It drops fruit, then leaves, then your patience.

I water when the pot is light and the top two inches are dry, not when
the clock says Tuesday. In a 20-gallon fabric bag on a full-sun Zone 8
patio, that can be daily in July. In a 25-gallon plastic can under a
little afternoon shade, it can be every other day. Zone 9 on concrete is
sometimes twice in a day for a 15-gallon, and that is the plant telling
you the pot is too small, not that you are a bad person.

The pour itself should be dull. Water until it leaves the drain. Wait.
If nothing leaves, you either have a clogged hole or a mix so dry it is
shedding. For the second case, water in passes. A little, wait, a
little more. Or set the pot in a tray for twenty minutes and then take
the tray away. Leaving a pot in a tray all summer is how you rot the
bottom third and grow fungus gnats for company.

Morning is kinder than evening in humid places. You want the crown dry
by night. Fig rust and souring both enjoy a wet, warm dark. In a dry
Zone 9, evening water is less of a crime if that is when you are home.
The leaves can take it. The fruit and the rust cannot take a weekly
overhead shower in Charleston.

Inconsistent water is the silent crop thief. A week of perfect
attention, a weekend trip, a drowning when you get back — that cycle
splits fruit and throws the small ones. If you leave town, group the
pots in the brightest shade you have, water deep the morning you go,
and ask a human, not an app, to check the light ones. A cheap
moisture meter is a toy unless you have calibrated it against a pot
you already understand. Your hand and the weight of the pot are still
the instruments.

Rain is not a day off if the pot is under a roof edge that dumps a
gutter on it, or under a table that keeps it dry. Walk the pots after
a storm. Some will be soaked. Some will be as dry as they were at
noon. Believe the pot, not the radar.

Wilting at 4 p.m. in August, with recovery by morning, can be heat
flag, not drought. Check the weight before you flood a plant that is
already wet. A wet wilt is the ugly one — roots without air. If the
pot is heavy and the plant is flat, move it to shade, stop watering,
and pray you catch it before the smell.

I do not mist figs. I do not give them ice cubes. I do not put rocks
in the saucer "for humidity." I give them a mix that holds a day of
water and a drain that works. If that still is not enough, the pot is
too small or the site is a griddle. Change one of those before you
invent a ritual.

A note on fruiting weeks: when the figs are coloring, do not play
hero with drought to "concentrate sugars." That trick works on grapes
in a textbook and wrecks figs in a pot. Keep the tank even. The sugar
is already being made. Your job is not to startle the plant into
dropping the week you have been waiting for.
