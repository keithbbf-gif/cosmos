# Flat and column forms on a patio

Bush form is the default in
this series because it
moves. Sometimes you have a
wall and a narrow walk and
you want the fig to live
in two dimensions.

Espalier on a pot is a
real thing if you accept
that the pot must be
heavy enough not to
pitch when the wall is
windy, and that you will
tie new wood several
times a season. I use a
simple fan: a short
stool and three to five
arms on a trellis
attached to the pot, not
only to the house. If
the trellis is only on
the house, you have
married the plant to the
siding and the winter
move is a divorce.

Pick a variety that does
not want to be a
monster. Violette,
Celeste, a dwarf, an
Olympian — plants you
can keep in the fan
without weekly argument.
A Brown Turkey will do
it and will also try to
eat the trellis.

Fruit sits in the sun
on a fan. That is the
point. Rust sits less
in the middle because
there is no middle.
That is a bonus in
humid zones.

A column is a single
leader with short
laterals, like a
cordon. It fits a
balcony rail. It is
fussy. You pinch. You
do not let a shoot
become a second tree.
True dwarfs make this
less of a fight.
Fignomenal already
thinks it is a mound;
a column is a
different idea. Little
Miss Figgy can be
persuaded.

Winter: a flat plant
on a trellis is a
sail and a
storage problem.
Measure the door.
If it does not fit,
the form is wrong
for Zone 7. Zone 9
can leave the fan
on the wall with a
sheet for the rare
night.

I do not wire figs
like apples. Soft
ties, loose, checked
so they do not
girdle. Fig wood
thickens while you
are not looking.

A fan against a west
wall in Zone 9 will
cook unless you
shade the pot and
maybe the lowest
fruit. The wall that
makes espalier
pretty is the wall
that makes heat. I
would rather a fan
on the east side of
a garage than a
perfect south
display that stalls
in August.

If the form starts
to look like a
hobby that ate your
Saturdays, go back
to a bush. The crop
does not require a
shape. The patio
sometimes does. Be
honest about which
problem you are
solving. Shape is
optional. Water is
not.
