# Chicago Hardy as a movable plant

Chicago Hardy is the fig people buy when they are scared of
winter, which in Zone 7 is a reasonable fear. It is also sold
under enough names — Bensonhurst Purple in some old lists,
"hardy Chicago," whatever the big-box tag said that week —
that you should judge the plant, not the poetry.

It is a medium fig, brown-purple when ripe, with a berry
flavor that is good and not mysterious. It will not make a
flavor snob weep. It will make a crop when a fussier fig
is still deciding whether your county is real. That is the
job.

In a pot, Chicago Hardy is vigorous. Give it 20 gallons and
it will fill them. It wants to be a shrub, and it will
throw wood you can keep if you overwinter the pot. That is
the whole trick. In the ground in Zone 7 it often dies to
the crown and you get a late main crop or nothing. In a
garage it keeps last year's arms. You get a chance at
breba — not a heavy one, in my pots — and a main crop that
starts from a plant that already has a frame.

I prune it to a short vase. Three to five scaffolds, nothing
taller than I can net, nothing so thick I cannot roll the
pot through a door. It will argue for more. Let it argue.
You have the clippers.

Cold: this is the one I will leave out in a wrapped pot in
Zone 7b if I have to. I still prefer the garage for a plant
I like. In Zone 8 it is almost casual outdoors. In Zone 9
it is fine and a little wasted — you could be growing
something that needs that heat. Grow it anyway if you like
it. Hardiness is not a flavor.

Water: same as any vigorous pot fig. It wilts dramatically
and then looks at you like you did it on purpose. Do not
be trained into twice-a-day water if the pot is heavy. It
is a performer.

The fruit closes reasonably well. Not Celeste-tight. Good
enough for a humid week if the birds do not find it first.
It ripens in a decent window for Zone 7, which matters
more than a catalog's "very late" or "very early" on a
plant you have not grown.

In a 15-gallon it fruits and dries too fast. In a 25 it
becomes a shrub you respect. I have kept one in a fabric
20 on a dolly for four winters and the limiting factor
was never hardiness. It was the door width.

People treat it as flavorless because they picked it
colored-but-firm. Wait. The inside should be a dark
strawberry jam, not a pink cork. If you have only eaten
it early, you have not eaten it.

If you only keep one pot in Zone 7, this is a sane choice.
If you keep five, this is the one you put nearest the
garage door because you refuse to lose it. The fancy figs
can take the nicer spot. Chicago Hardy can take the job.
