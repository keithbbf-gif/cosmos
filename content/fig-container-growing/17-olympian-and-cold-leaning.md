# Olympian and other cold-leaning pots

Olympian is the fig I mention when someone in
Zone 7 already has a Chicago Hardy and wants
a second opinion that is not another brown
workhorse. It came out of the Northwest
conversation — cool nights, short seasons —
and it has a reputation for ripening when
the summer was not a poster. In a pot, that
reputation is the point.

The fruit is larger than Celeste, striped to
violet, with a berry flavor that can be
excellent if it gets enough heat to finish.
In a cool Zone 7 summer it still finishes
more often than the late heat-lovers. That
is the slot: not the best fig in a blazing
year, the fig that still shows up in a
mediocre one.

Florea, some of the hardy Ukrainian and
Crimean names that circulate among
cutters, and a few "very hardy" forum
favorites belong in this same drawer. I
will not recite a catalog. I will say:
for pots in Zone 7, cold-leaning means
two things, and people confuse them.
Wood that survives a freeze is one.
Fruit that ripens before October is the
other. You want both. A plant that lives
and never ripens is a large houseplant.

In Zone 9, Olympian is optional. You have
heat. Spend the pot on flavor or on an
LSU. In Zone 8 it is a nice extra if you
like the fruit. In Zone 7 it is a
strategy.

Pot size: 20 gallons. It will use it.
Overwinter like Chicago Hardy. I have no
interest in finding out the exact night
it dies in a thin can on a deck. The
garage is right there.

If you take cuttings from a neighbor,
take them from a plant that has ripened
in your county, not from a plant that
has merely survived. Survival stories
are how we collect wood that never
feeds us. Ask when they ate the first
fig last year. If the answer is "we
usually get some in a warm fall," keep
walking unless you like suspense.

I have seen Olympian sold next to plants
that need a long California fall. Read the
ripening talk, not only the hardy talk. A
"very hardy" fig that finishes in November
is a Zone 9 plant wearing a winter coat.
Ask for first ripe week, not lowest
survival temperature.

A cold-leaning pot collection in Zone 7
can be three plants: Chicago Hardy,
Olympian, Celeste. That covers live-through,
ripen-through, and humid-week. Everything
else is dessert. If dessert crowds the
garage, dessert waits a year.
