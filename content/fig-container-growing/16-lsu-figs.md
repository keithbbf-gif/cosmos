# The LSU figs in Gulf humidity

If you garden where summer is a wet blanket —
Louisiana, the upper Gulf, the Piney Woods,
parts of the Carolina coastal plain — the LSU
program is not trivia. It is the difference
between a fig that sours and a fig you eat.
These were bred for heat, humidity, and the
diseases that come with both.

LSU Purple is the one you see. Medium fruit,
red-purple, decent closed eye, flavor that is
honest and good. It loads on a pot if the pot
is 20 gallons and the sun is real. It handles
the long season of Zone 9 and the sticky
Zone 8 without asking to be a California
poster.

LSU Gold is the yellow one people walk past
and then regret. Honey flavor, beautiful when
ripe, a little more open, a little more
trouble in a rain week, still worth it if
you like a golden fig and you water evenly.

LSU Improved Celeste and Tiger sit in the
family conversation. Names and availability
wander by year and nursery. I care less
about catching every release and more about
this: if your summers rot figs, start here
instead of importing a famous California
name because a photo was glossy.

Nematodes are part of why pots and LSU
belong in the same sentence. Old Southern
soil can be a root-knot carnival. An LSU
fig is not magic. A clean pot is the
quarantine. Together they are how you grow
figs on a lot where in-ground plants go
stunted and yellow in year three.

In Zone 7, LSU Purple will grow. I would
still overwinter the pot and I would not
make it my only hardy plant. It is a
humidity specialist that also likes heat.
Give it the long season if you have it.

Pot culture details are ordinary: drain,
even water, do not nitrogen it into a
jungle. The fruit can come in a heavy
flush. Have a plan for the flush that is
not "I will think about jam later." Later
is fruit flies.

Pot size: 20 gallons for Purple if you want
a real flush. Gold can live a little smaller
and still look like a show plant when the
fruit turns. Both want the draining mix in
the soil draft. Gulf rain plus a peat
pudding is how an LSU, which should be your
easy plant, becomes a rust-and-rot project.

I do not treat LSU figs as lesser because
they are "new" or "university." I treat
them as the local tool. If you live in
their weather, they are not a novelty
slot in the collection. They are the
collection, plus one flavor fig you are
willing to fuss. A California name in a
Gulf pot is the novelty. Act like it.
