# The last week before you eat them

You did the mix, the winter, the water.
The figs colored. Then a mockingbird
taught a class on your patio and a
squirrel took the rest like a person
who does not believe in sharing.

This is not a pest in the scale sense.
This is a harvest problem. Pots make
it slightly easier because you can
move a plant under a net or closer
to the door for the ripe week. You
cannot move an in-ground fig onto
the porch. Use that.

I net. I use a soft mesh that birds
do not love, and I put it on when
the first fruit softens, not in May.
A net all summer is a vine tangle
and a rust tent. A net for ten days
is a chore I will do. Hold it off
the fruit with a couple of sticks
or a tomato cage so birds do not
peck through the holes.

Bags on individual figs work on a
dwarf with twenty fruit. They do
not work on a loaded 25-gallon
unless you enjoy thread and
swearing. I have done both. I net
the workhorses.

Squirrels laugh at some nets. They
do not laugh at a plant you rolled
to the back door and pick every
evening. They also do not laugh at
a baffled stand, but a pot on a
baffled stand in wind is a new
problem. I have used a cheap
motion sprinkler on a deck I did
not love. I have also just picked
early-ripe and finished them on
the counter for a day. A fig
picked a little shy will soften.
A fig the squirrel opened will
not.

Wasps and bees show up at ripe
fruit. They are not the enemy of
the plant. They are the reason you
look before you grab. I pick in
the morning when they are slower.

Cats are not a fig pest plan.
Dogs are not a fig pest plan.
A well-fed outdoor cat may sit
on the pot and break a branch.
Ask me how I know.

The ripe week is short. If you
are going out of town, pick
everything that has started to
soften and give it away. Do not
leave a netted buffet and hope.
Hope is how you come home to
husks.

A radio or a foil strip is a
day of comedy. The animals
learn. Net and pick are the
only methods I will still
recommend after the comedy
week. If you are opposed to
nets on aesthetic grounds,
pick smaller varieties closer
to the door and accept a tax.
Pride is also a harvest
method. It just pays the
birds.

I do not hate the birds. I just
do not grow figs for them. The
pot is a way to bring the crop
to a place I can defend for
ten days. After that the net
comes off and the plant goes
back to being furniture.
