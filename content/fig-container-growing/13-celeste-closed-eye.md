# Celeste and why a closed eye matters here

Celeste is a small fig. People who wanted a fist-sized
Mission are disappointed, and then they eat one that
ripened on the plant and become quiet. It is a sugar
fig — amber flesh, berry-honey, concentrated. The
skin is brown-violet and you can eat it without a
production. The plant is old in the South for a
reason.

The reason that matters in pots, especially from the
Piedmont to the Gulf, is the eye. The ostiole on
Celeste is tight. Rain and vinegar flies have a
harder time getting in. Open-eye figs in a humid
August will sour from the inside while the skin
still looks polite. You split one at the sink and
it smells like a bar. Celeste spoils less often.
That is not romance. That is why your grandmother
kept it.

In a pot I give Celeste 15 to 20 gallons. It is not
a giant. It can be kept as a tidy shrub. It is
hardy for a fig — better than the thin Mediterranean
types, not quite the folklore tank that Chicago
Hardy is in the coldest Zone 7. I still overwinter
the pot. I like the wood.

The crop is mainly a main crop on new wood. Breba
is not the story I tell about Celeste. What I tell
is: it ripens in time for Zone 7 if the plant had
a decent spring, and it ripens in humid Zone 8 and
9 without the souring tax. LSU's improved Celeste
types exist if you want a breeding program in the
family. Start with the real thing if you can find
a cutting from a plant that already fruits in your
county. Names wander. Flavor and a closed eye do
not wander as much.

Birds love Celeste because the fruit is small and
they can take one in a sitting. Net late. The
window from "almost" to "gone" is short.

Water: even. The fruit is thin-skinned enough to
split if you drought it and then get a two-inch
rain. You cannot stop the rain. You can stop the
drought.

The plant can look sparse compared with a turkey
type. People feed it to make it "fill in." Then
they have a leafy Celeste and a late crop. Keep
it a little tight. The fruit is the point, and
the fruit is small enough that a jungle hides it
from you and not from the birds.

If I am recommending pots for a humid Zone 8
beginner, Celeste is on the short list with an
LSU and a hardy brown. If I am recommending for
a dry Zone 9, Celeste still works, but you could
also chase larger open-eye figs that would be
foolish in Mobile. Match the eye to the air.
That is the whole Celeste lesson, and it applies
to every fig you buy after this one. A pretty
name with an open eye is a humid-climate dare.
