# Warm spell, then a freeze

The dangerous night in this band is
often not the coldest night of
January. It is the 26°F night in
late March after two weeks of 75°F
and a plant that believed you.

New leaves and swollen breba are
tender. Old dormant wood is not.
The pot makes this worse and
better. Worse: a pot in the open
heats up fast in a warm spell, so
the plant wakes. Better: you can
roll it back into the garage for
one night instead of draping a
bedsheet over a twenty-foot
shrub.

I watch the ten-day. If buds have
broken and a freeze is coming, the
plant goes back to the space it
wintered in, or against the house
under a cover that reaches the
pot. A sheet that only covers
leaves and leaves the pot open
is half a job. The pot is the
root.

Do not wrap a leafy plant in
plastic and walk away. You will
cook it on a sunny 28°F morning
or sweat it all night. Fabric,
an old quilt, a frost cloth —
something that breathes. Plastic
as an outer shell over cloth,
and off in the morning, is a
move people use. I have used
it. I do not leave it.

If the freeze wins, do not prune
the black leaves the next
afternoon. Wait. Some tips are
dead. Some will push from the
next bud. Cut when you can tell.
A plant that loses its first
leaves will often try again.
The crop will be later. In Zone
7, later is how you meet
October. That is the risk of
an early wake.

Zone 9 still gets this, just
rarer. A "once in a decade"
night in the mid-20s after a
February summer will burn
breba on pots you never
bothered to cluster. Cluster
them. One night of being
ridiculous is cheaper than a
lost June.

I keep a stack of sheets in
the same bin as the clippers.
If I have to hunt for
cover at 9 p.m., I will
talk myself out of it. The
late freeze is a
preparedness problem, not a
horticulture mystery.

Breba that froze is not a
reason to strip the plant.
The main crop is still a
sentence on new wood. Cut
the obvious dead after you
can tell, water as growth
resumes, and stop writing
the year off in March. Zone
7 people write years off
too early. That is how they
miss a September that was
still available.

If you are the person who
puts pots out on the first
70°F day because you missed
them, you are the person
this draft is for. Miss
them a little longer. The
fig does not need your
loneliness in March. It
needs its leaves after
April.
