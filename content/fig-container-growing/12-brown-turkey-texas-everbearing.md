# Brown Turkey, Texas Everbearing, and the name mess

There is no single Brown Turkey. There is a family of brown,
reliable, slightly football-shaped figs that nurseries have
been selling under that name since before anyone on the
internet was alive. Texas Everbearing is in that family.
Some plants labeled one are the other. Some are neither,
exactly. If you bought a "Brown Turkey" at a spring trailer
sale, you bought a brown fig that fruits. That is usually
enough.

What you can count on: a plant that sets a main crop without
drama, tolerates heat, and does not need a wasp. Flavor is
mild to good, honey and a little berry, lighter than
Violette de Bordeaux, less floral than a honey fig. Kids
eat them. Neighbors accept them. You will not start a
forum thread about the mouthfeel.

In pots they are workhorses. Twenty to twenty-five gallons.
Bush form. They get large if you let them. In Zone 9,
Texas Everbearing is the one people actually harvest in
quantity on a patio. It will throw fruit over a long
season when the plant is happy, which is where the
"everbearing" talk comes from. It is not a machine that
ignores stress. It is a plant that, unlike some European
figs, will keep trying after a bad week.

Zone 7: Brown Turkey types are hardy enough to live, not
always hardy enough to keep wood without help. Treat the
pot like Chicago Hardy. Garage or wrap. Do not leave a
thin-can plant out for a 5°F night because the name
sounded American.

Zone 8: this is their comfortable chair. Protect the pot
in the ugly cold snap. Otherwise they want sun and a
hose.

The name mess matters when you start collecting. If you
already have a brown fig that fruits, you do not need
three more with slightly different tags. If you are
building a small set, let this plant be the reliable
slot and spend the other pots on a closed-eye sugar fig
and a flavor fig. That is a better trio than three
shades of turkey.

Ripeness: they color before they are ready. Wait for
the neck to soften and the fruit to hang. The first
ones you pick too early will taste like a wet paper
bag and you will blame the variety. Blame your
patience.

In Zone 9 a happy Texas Everbearing will try to ripen
in waves. That is the "everbearing" you actually get:
not a supermarket conveyor, a plant that sets again
after a stall if water and heat allow. Pick the ripe
wave. Leave the green. Do not strip the tree because
you wanted a single jam day.

I like them in plastic or fabric, not in a pretty clay
pot that dries in six hours. They are production
plants. Give them a production vessel. Save the glazed
jar for the dwarf you can see from the kitchen. If the
plant outgrows the door, you prune the plant. You do
not buy a second brown fig to replace the one you
cannot move. That is how the name mess becomes a yard
mess.
