# Keep the wood through a Zone 7 winter

This is the draft the whole series leans on. Zone 7 is where pot
culture earns its keep. The ground can kill the top. The pot, if
you use it, can save it.

Wait until the plant is done. First real frost will take the
leaves. That is the cue, not a date on a blog written in
someone else's county. A fig still in full leaf in a warm
October should stay out. You want dormancy. You do not want to
carry a leafy, transpiring plant into a dark garage and wonder
why it sheds and sulks.

Once the leaves are down, clean the pot. Pick off the mat.
Check for scale on the wood — they are easier to see now.
Water so the mix is just moist. Then move.

Best home: unheated garage or closed shed that does not go
deeply below 20°F for days. A pot is more exposed than soil,
but a garage that holds 30–40°F is a spa. I have kept plants
in a corner behind the lawnmower with a cardboard screen
against the metal door. They do not need light when they are
asleep. They need to not freeze solid and not wake up.

Second best: a cold basement or crawl space that stays above
freezing and below 50°F. Watch the 50. Warm and dark is mite
weather and confused buds.

Third best: leave them out, grouped against the house, pots
wrapped, tops loosely covered after they are fully dormant.
This works in a polite Zone 7b winter and fails in the week
it hits 5°F with wind. If this is your plan, pick Chicago
Hardy and Olympian, not a thin-wood Mediterranean that
already looked homesick in October. Wrap the pot more
carefully than the branches. Dead roots kill the plant.
Dead tips are a prune.

I do not use a heated sunroom as a winter home unless I am
keeping the plant in leaf on purpose, and I am almost never
doing that. A fig that stays in leaf all winter in Zone 7
is a fig you will water, spray, and apologize to until
April.

Mice will girdle a fig in a garage if you store birdseed
nearby. I set the pots up on a pallet and I do not leave
a buffet. This sounds fussy until you lose a three-year
plant to a tidy ring of missing bark.

Check monthly. Weight. A look at the wood. No fertilizer.
No pruning show. If you must shorten something to fit
through the door, cut it clean and stop. The real prune
is late winter, just before or as the buds swell, when
you can see what the cold actually took.

Bring them out in spring the way you would a citrus you
overwintered, except the fig can take more cold once it
is still dormant. I start with a few days on the east
side of the house, back in if a hard freeze is coming,
then out for good when the forecast has lost its
meanness. A plant that leafed in the garage needs a
slower hardening or the first bright day will bleach
it.

Keep the wood and you keep the breba and a head start
on the main crop. That is the whole point of the pot
in Zone 7. If you leave the pot out "because last year
was mild," you are gardening last year.
