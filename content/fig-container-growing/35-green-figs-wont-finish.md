# Green figs that never finish

October arrives. The plant is
holding a dozen green figs the
size of walnuts. They have been
that size since Labor Day. You
want a trick. There is no good
trick. There is a diagnosis.

In Zone 7 the diagnosis is often
the calendar. The plant set late
because it rebuilt after winter,
or you fed it into a late flush,
or the variety is a long-season
type that wanted August heat you
did not have. Those figs will
not become good figs in a week
of 55°F nights. You can pick
them for a pickle project if
you like projects. You cannot
will them ripe.

In Zone 9 the diagnosis is often
the stall. Heat stopped the
ripening clock. When nights
ease, some of those figs will
move. Keep water even. Do not
strip them in a sulk in August.
Wait into September. If they
still sit there like wooden
beads, they were never going
to.

A plant in too little sun will
carry green fruit as decoration.
Move the pot next year. This
year you are collecting
experience.

A plant that is too wild with
nitrogen will do the same. The
figs exist. The plant is not
interested. Stop the food,
keep the sun, hope for a
warm fall.

People bag fruit and talk
about ethylene. A fig that
has started will respond a
little. A fig that is
botanically weeks away will
not become a Mission because
you put it in a paper bag
with a banana. I have tried
the folk version. I have
eaten the result. It was a
softer green fig.

Frost will take them. In
Zone 7 I pick anything that
has the first hint of color
before a hard night and I
let the rest go. A pot you
can roll into the garage
can buy a week if the
garage is cool-not-cold and
the fruit was close. A pot
in a warm house will drop
leaves and the fruit will
sit. I have done that too.
It is a mess.

The useful move is next
year: earlier variety,
saved wood, less August
nitrogen, more sun, a pot
that did not spend June
recovering from a wet
repot. Green figs in
October are a report card
on the season, not a
puzzle for the last week.

Next year's variety swap
is allowed. A late fig
you loved in a catalog
can become a cutting you
give to a Zone 9 friend.
Keep the pot for
something that finishes
in your actual September,
not the September in the
photo.

If you want a use anyway:
fig leaf ice cream does
not need ripe fruit, and
unripe figs have a
traditional pickle. I am
not your grandmother. I
am telling you the fruit
on the tree is not a
failure of picking
technique. It is a
failure of time. Time is
the one amendment a pot
cannot pass.
