# The first-frost night

This is a procedure, not an
essay. Zone 7 people will
use it every year. Zone 8
will use it most years.
Zone 9 will use it on the
night the neighbors text.

**Afternoon before.** Water
so the mix is moist, not
heavy. A dry pot goes
colder. A sopping pot
rots later. Pick any
fruit that has started.
Empty saucers. Clear the
path to the garage or
the wall. Inflate the
tire on the dolly you
have been ignoring.

**Dormant enough?** If
leaves are still full
and green and the frost
is light, you can cover
in place and wait for a
harder night to move.
If the night is a hard
freeze and the plant is
still in leaf, it will
drop those leaves
anyway. Move it if you
can. A leafy plant in a
dark garage for one
night is fine. A leafy
plant in a dark garage
for a month is a
different draft.

**Move.** Garage, shed,
basement. Group pots.
Do not stack foliage
into a wet pile. Do not
park a pot on a heat
vent. Do not block the
breaker box with your
hobby.

**If they stay out.**
Wall. Wrap the cans.
Cover the tops with
cloth that reaches
down. Stones or clips
so the wind does not
gift the next yard
your quilt. Off in the
morning if the sun
will cook a plastic
tent.

**Morning after.**
Uncover. Check who
tipped. Do not prune
black edges yet. A
light frost is a
leaf-drop helper. A
hard freeze on a pot
you left wet and
exposed is a root
question. Wait and
scratch wood.

**The list on the
door.** I keep a
written count. Five
pots. If I only see
four at 10 p.m. I
walk the yard. The
one behind the
tomatoes is always
the one I lose.

Practice the route in
daylight once. Wheels
catch on the same
threshold every year.
The year you learn it
at 10 p.m. with a
headlamp is the year
a pot chips a step
and you decide figs
are hard. Figs are
not hard. Stairs at
night are hard.

This night is why the
pot exists in Zone 7.
If you cannot do this
night, grow something
else, or grow Chicago
Hardy in the ground
and accept the wood
loss. Do not own a
collection you will
abandon at 8 p.m.
because the game was
on. Put the sheets in
the bin in September.
The frost does not
care what you meant
to do. It cares what
you moved.
