**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Nicholas Trigant Burrow was born 7 September 1875 and died 24 May 1950. He trained in literature, medicine (M.D., University of Virginia, 1900), and psychology (Ph.D., Johns Hopkins, 1909). He met Freud and Jung on their American visit, went to Zürich for a year of analysis with Jung, practiced in Baltimore, served as president of the American Psychoanalytic Association in 1924–25, and was later outside that association's warmth (accounts of a 1932 rupture are in the secondary literature — `[VERIFY]` the minutes before a caption says "expelled"). He founded the Lifwynn Foundation in 1926 and spent the rest of his life trying to make the group a laboratory of the species.

He is easy to steal from and hard to read. This essay will not make him readable by turning him into a proto-Yalom.

## Not a ward class

Pratt wanted compliance. Marsh wanted a revival. Burrow wanted a science of social consciousness. *The Social Basis of Consciousness* (1927) argues that neurosis is not a private possession. The "I" that psychoanalysis took as the unit was, for him, already a social symptom. A group of researchers living the inquiry could, he thought, see the symptom in the room's tensions.

Lifwynn was not Hillside Hospital. It was closer to a research commune with a theory that mainstream psychiatry found unassimilable. People later called him a founder of "group analysis" in the United States. Foulkes used a similar English phrase for a different craft. Moreno wanted credit for a different interaction. The three names on a slide are a lecture habit. The rooms were not the same.

He had been a proper analyst. The turn away from the private hour was a turn *through* the hour, not a ignorance of it. That is why the psychoanalytic guild found him irritating. Heretics who know the catechism are worse than outsiders.

## Body, instrument, overclaim

Late Burrow measured. He cared about posture, attention, the organism in a social field. Secondary writers have tried to annex him to later body therapies and even to EMDR. This pack refuses the annexation. A resemblance is not a paternity. Citation-needed genealogies are how the internet invents fathers.

What can be said without a stretch: he would not keep the group as a purely verbal object. American group therapy, in its mainstream, mostly did. The body returned through psychodrama, through encounter, through trauma fashions. Those returns do not make Burrow their supervisor.

## Why he still sits on this calendar

Because the interwar story is not only lectures to a thousand patients. Because someone already said, in 1927, that the unit of treatment might be a we that is sick. Because later systems thinkers and later large-group theorists who never read him still walk into his problem.

Because he failed, in the professional sense. Failure is historical information. A method that cannot be staffed in a CMHC is not therefore wrong; it is not therefore available. Lifwynn did not become the American group standard. AGPA's hotel did.

## What not to do with Burrow

Do not start a commune and call it phyloanalysis. Do not quote a paragraph of 1927 as if it were a consent form. Do not diagnose "the culture" with his nouns. Do not generate his face.

Do not use him to smear ordinary group psychotherapy as "not radical enough." Ordinary care has duties he did not have to meet on a Tuesday census.

A reader who wants the book should find the book. This page is a door, not a digest. The prose is thick. Thickness was his refusal of the class method's clarity.

## Baltimore, Zürich, and a presidency

A Baltimore practice after a Zürich analysis is a standard early-American psychoanalytic career. The presidency of the American Psychoanalytic Association (1924–25) is not. He was inside the guild and then not. Inside-then-not is the pattern of people who take the guild's object (the unconscious, the social) farther than the guild's furniture (the couch, the fee) will go.

Lifwynn's later publications are thick and few-readered. Few readers is not a proof of depth. It is a proof of a style that would not become a residency assignment. Yalom became the assignment. Burrow became a footnote for people who want a weirder ancestor.

Do not call him the father of group analysis in America without naming Pratt and Schilder in the same breath. Wikipedia's sentence is a slide. This pack uses LC dates and the 1927 book.

His Catholic mother and Protestant father appear in biographical sketches. This page will not psychologize the mixed household. Mixed households are common. His science is uncommon.

Phyloanalysis as a word never became a residency rotation. Words that do not rotate become footnotes. Footnotes are not failures if the question remains: is the unit of trouble a person or a we. Later family therapists asked it in another dialect. Later large-group people asked it in a hall. He asked it in a foundation with a name that still looks like a typo. Lifwynn is not a brand this site will sell. It is an address in a 1926 sentence.

## How to hold a difficult book

*The Social Basis of Consciousness* will not repay a reader who wants Yalom's sentences. It will repay a reader who wants to see 1927 already refusing the private hour as the only unit. Refusal is not a method you can staff on a Tuesday. It is a question you can keep on a calendar so later systems thinkers do not think they were first.

A later editor who finds the APA minutes around 1932 should paste a line into this essay and remove the `[VERIFY]`. Minutes beat a secondary "expelled." Until then the presidency years and the 1926 foundation are the granite. Granite is enough for a heritage page that refuses fathers.

Do not annex him to EMDR, to trauma fashions, or to a commune you are planning. He is dead. The book is in libraries. The we that is sick remains a problem that ordinary group psychotherapy can name without becoming Lifwynn.
