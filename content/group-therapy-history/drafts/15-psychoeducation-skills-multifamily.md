**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Not every circle is a process group. By the 1980s and 1990s a lot of the circles that American clinics could still fund were classrooms: medication education, relapse prevention, "illness management," family nights with a slide projector, and then, with more theory, skills-training groups and multifamily psychoeducation. The people who loved Yalom's here-and-now sometimes called these rooms "not really group." The people who ran them sometimes called process groups a luxury. Both insults are historical data.

This essay will not teach a skill. It will not excerpt a handout. Manuals are copyrighted and are not a website's to assign.

## Teaching as treatment

Pratt's class was already teaching. Marsh's revival was already teaching. What changed late in the century was the *manual*: session-by-session pages, fidelity checklists, a grant that wanted to know whether the leader skipped module four. Cognitive-behavioral groups for depression and anxiety, often in the British IAPT later wave as well as in U.S. clinics, treated the group as a way to deliver a protocol to several people at once. Delivery is an industrial word. It is also how a public system tries to be fair.

Psychoeducation for families of people with schizophrenia grew from a refusal of the old mother-blaming stories (the sister pack's systems essay has that wound). If the family is not the cause, the family might still need information, sleep, and a room that is not a trial. William McFarlane's multifamily work, gathered in a 2002 Guilford book and in 1990s trial papers, is one American line: several families in one room, a curriculum, a long enough time to be more than a lecture. Numbers in those papers belong to the papers. This site will not turn them into a promise.

## Skills groups and a public life

Marsha Linehan's 1991 trial and 1993 books made a skills-training group a named part of a treatment, next to individual sessions, not a cheap substitute. The sister pack's Linehan essay is the life and the 2011 *New York Times* disclosure. This essay is the *room*: a whiteboard, a handout, a homework review, a different contract from process. People still call it "group therapy" on a schedule. The contract is closer to a class. Linehan's tradition knew that. Internet copies of the handouts often do not.

The skills room traveled into places that never ran the rest of the treatment. A stand-alone "DBT group" in a CMHC could be a lifeline or a brand. Fidelity is a historical fight: who owns a method when the county wants the acronym. This pack will not police acronyms. It will say the fight exists.

Other third-wave groups — mindfulness-based rooms, ACT groups — belong also to the ACT/mindfulness sister pack. Pointer, not a reprint.

## Multifamily and the two-way street

A room of several families can reduce shame. It can also expose a family to other families' fear. McFarlane's model tried to structure the exposure. Earlier multiple-family experiments (Laqueur and others, mid-century) had already put more than one household in a circle. The late-century difference was the psychoeducational frame: information as respect, not as a double bind.

Consent in a multifamily room is a thicket: who speaks for a silent member, what is said about a hospitalization, what a child hears. History can name the thicket. History cannot write the form.

## What not to do with a manual

Do not download a skills PDF and run a Tuesday. Do not tell a family that psychoeducation "prevents schizophrenia." Do not tell a process-group loyalist that teaching is beneath them. Do not tell a skills-group loyalist that conversation is self-indulgence. Those are sectarian manners.

Do not imply WOW Therapies offers DBT or multifamily groups. It is an SLP practice. If a later service page ever names a real group, that page needs a license and an insurance review — not a history CTA.

The classroom circle is easy to mock if you have never needed a fact. It is easy to worship if you have never needed a conversation. Late-century clinics needed both and were funded for one. That imbalance is the era.

## Relapse prevention and the last page

Relapse-prevention groups in addictions and in mood clinics borrowed a last page: a curriculum that ends. Ending is what process groups resist and what insurers buy. Marlatt's later mindfulness-based relapse work sits in the ACT/mindfulness pack. Here the object is the last page itself — a historical preference for a room that can be closed in a chart.

Illness Management and Recovery and other SAMHSA-circulated packages of the 2000s are public-domain-ish curricula with their own citations. `[CITE NEEDED]` before a caption treats any one package as the type. They are descendants of the medication group and of Pratt's class. Descendants can be respectful. They can also be a DVD and a tired facilitator.

Families who already knew the diagnosis from years of nights did not always need a slide. They needed sleep and a ride. A psychoeducation room that cannot say that is a lecture with snacks. McFarlane's longer multifamily form tried to be more than snacks. Trying is in the book. The book is not a county's staffing.

## Homework as a late-century object

Homework in a skills group is Pratt's diary without tuberculosis. The rhyme is structural. The ethics differ: a 1993 manual assumes literacy, time, and a home that will not punish the worksheet. Homes that punish worksheets are why some people drop. Dropout is in the trial papers. This site will not assign a worksheet to fix it.

Family nights with a slide projector — 1980s CMHC — are the ordinary ancestor of McFarlane's longer form. Ordinary ancestors get no Guilford book. They get a coffee urn and a social worker who also has the crisis beeper.

A slide projector in a 1984 family night is an ancestor with no ISBN. The social worker who also held the beeper is the uncited author. Guilford books arrive later and get the citation. This pack names the beeper so the ISBN does not steal the whole room.

SAMHSA packages of the 2000s traveled as PDFs. PDFs are how a class method leaves a building. Leaving a building is Pratt's afterlife without Pratt's visitor. This pack names the missing visitor every time a curriculum arrives without a home visit.

A later editor who wants a number from a multifamily trial should open the PDF in the same sitting as this paragraph. Sitting is the method. Memory is how percentages become slogans.

A last page is a historical preference. Process groups resist it. Insurers buy it. This era sat between those wants.
