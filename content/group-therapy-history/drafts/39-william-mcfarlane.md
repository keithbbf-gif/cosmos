## 1995 New York hospitals, then Portland

The numbered object this pack will name is McFarlane and colleagues, "Multiple-Family Groups and Psychoeducation in the Treatment of Schizophrenia," *Archives of General Psychiatry* 52 (1995). The paper describes 172 people, aged 18–45, at six public hospitals in New York, randomly assigned to single-family or multiple-family psychoeducation and followed for two years. The authors report lower two-year relapse in the multiple-family arm and say both arms beat an expected individual-treatment rate they cite from earlier literature. Those sentences are the paper's claims. They are not a 2026 promise that a group "prevents" a diagnosis. Open the PDF before any caption uses a percentage. A *Family Process* paper the same year follows four-year relapse. Same rule: the PDF, not memory.

Guilford in 2002 gathered that trial literature into *Multifamily Groups in the Treatment of Severe Psychiatric Disorders*. The book's historical claim, for this series, is length: not a single lecture night, not a weekend. Public systems wanted a grant that could look like more than a night. Grants end. Families remain.
