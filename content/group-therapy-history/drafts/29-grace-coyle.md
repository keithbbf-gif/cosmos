**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Grace Longwell Coyle was born in North Adams, Massachusetts, in 1892 and died in 1962. She was a social worker and teacher, not a psychiatrist. That is why she is on this calendar. A history of "group therapy" that never leaves the hospital will miss the settlement house, the YWCA, the youth club, and the professionalization of *group work* as a social-work method.

*Social Process in Organized Groups* (1930) and *Group Work with American Youth* (1948) are the public books. Western Reserve (later Case Western) was the long teaching address (1934–1962). She presided over the National Conference of Social Work (1940) and the American Association of Social Workers (1942), and later the Council on Social Work Education (1958–1960). She sat on the wartime War Relocation Authority — a fact that belongs in any honest life, because "group" and "government" were not innocent partners in 1942.

## A science of the club

Coyle wanted group work to be thinkable, teachable, and more than a personality. Process, purpose, the relation of the small group to the community: those are her objects. A settlement club is not a therapy group. It has a civic goal. Collapsing the two is how psychiatry annexed social work's rooms after the war. This pack refuses the annexation.

The 1934 Fletcher Farm institute for YWCA and settlement workers is a dated scene: two weeks, forty people, a farm school in Vermont. Training as a retreat is older than Bethel. Bethel got the myth. Coyle got the profession's textbooks.

Her brother David Cushman Coyle wrote New Deal-era economics. The family sat near public policy. She advocated expanded government services. A later reader who wants a purely clinical ancestor will be disappointed. She was a civic professional.

## Gender of the room

Social group work was a women's profession in a way AGPA's early psychiatric presidencies were not. That is labor history. The circle in a YWCA is a different sovereignty than the circle at Hillside. Girls and women organized, were organized, and were told the organization was for their "development." Development is a soft word with a hard budget.

Black settlement houses and Ys ran group work that Coyle's national roles touched unevenly. A 1948 book titled *American Youth* should be read with a question about which America. The question is not a takedown. It is how you read a national textbook.

## What not to do with Coyle

Do not call club work therapy. Do not call therapy club work. Do not use her WRA year as a smear or as a cleansing. It is a wartime appointment that later historians of incarceration must include. Do not scrape Case Western portraits.

A small-city after-school program is closer to Coyle than to Yalom. Closer means: purpose, community, a worker who is not a doctor. WOW Therapies' educational nights, if they ever exist, would be cousins of her civic room — still not therapy, still needing facts. This page will not invent those nights.

## YWCA laboratory and a war appointment

Head of the YWCA national laboratory division (1930–34) is a research job inside a women's organization. Research inside a Y is not a university's research. It is closer to Pratt's visitor: knowledge in the service of a program. She wanted the knowledge to be scientific anyway. The 1930 book is that want.

The War Relocation Authority appointment in 1942 places her near the incarceration of Japanese Americans. A heritage page that skips it to keep a founder clean is doing the thing this pack refuses. A heritage page that uses it only as a smear is doing another refused thing. It is a fact for the next historian to sit with the WRA papers and her letters.

Wellesley and the New York School of Social Work are the training addresses in the biographical record. North Adams is the birth. Western Reserve is the long labor. Four addresses, one profession that psychiatry's group calendar keeps forgetting.

A two-week institute at Fletcher Farm in 1934 is Bethel's quieter older cousin: time away, a mixed occupational room, a faith that process can be taught. She taught it as social work, not as therapy. The not is the whole reason she sits on this calendar. Psychiatry's group story keeps eating its neighbors. This pack will not plate her as an appetizer to Slavson.

## How to hold a civic professional

A woman who ran national social-work bodies and also wrote about small groups is easy to split into "leader" and "theorist." She did not split. Process in organized groups was, for her, how a democracy might work at the scale of a club. Clubs can be kind. Clubs can exclude. She wrote in a country that excluded. The 1948 title *American Youth* should be read with that country in mind.

WRA 1942 remains in the file. Sit with it. Do not use it as a cancellation or a cleansing. Use it as a date.

A 1930 Richard R. Smith binding is not a psychiatric journal. That is the point of her chair. Social process in organized groups is a civic science. Civic science is what psychiatry's group calendar keeps eating. This pack will not plate her.

Western Reserve from 1934 to 1962 is a twenty-eight-year teaching labor. Twenty-eight years is longer than most institutes last. Labor like that does not photograph. This pack put her on the calendar so a photograph was never required. Type and 1930 will do.

This file is not a psychiatry footnote. 1930, 1934 Fletcher Farm, 1942 WRA, 1948, Western Reserve 1934–62. Civic science. Twenty-eight years of teaching. Type. Do not plate her next to Slavson as an appetizer.
