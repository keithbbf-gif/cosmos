**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Irvin David Yalom was born in 1931 in Washington, D.C., and is living as of last verification (2026-09-14). The sister pack's figure essay holds the existential novels and the public storyteller. This essay uses only the group objects: the 1970 textbook, the 1973 encounter study with Lieberman and Miles, the 1983 inpatient book, and the later editions that added Molyn Leszcz's name to the textbook.

No health speculation. No lecture-photo scrape. No listicle from *The Gift of Therapy*.

## A professor who wrote sentences residencies could assign

Stanford psychiatry in his decades let a man run groups, write, and teach. The 1970 book made the outpatient process group a craft with named factors. The era essay holds the list and the checklist problem. The man is the writer who had sat in groups and had read, and who made the sitting teachable without making it a revival.

He wrote about inpatient groups when the census no longer allowed a year. The 1983 book is a dated attempt to take the snapshot hour seriously. Administrators who wanted the prestige of his name and the throughput of a three-day stay were not reading both books at once.

The 1973 study put his name on a harm literature. That is to his credit. Famous clinicians who only write successes are a different historical type.

## Living, therefore limited

He is not public property. This series points to the books. It does not reprint cases. His prefaces wrestle with disguise. A local clinician who writes "in the style of Yalom" without consent is not honoring him.

Later editions of the textbook are dated objects. Cite the edition you mean. Leszcz's co-authorship is succession, not a disappearance.

Washington grocery, Russian-Jewish household, a boy reading in a back room — those memoir facts belong to the books he chose to publish. They are not a brand story for a Southeast Arkansas SLP practice.
