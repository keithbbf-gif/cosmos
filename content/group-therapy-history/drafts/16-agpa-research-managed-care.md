**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In February 1943, at the American Orthopsychiatric Association meeting in New York, S. R. Slavson chalked a luncheon invitation on a blackboard. About fifty people came. A constitution followed. The organization was first the American Group Therapy Association; in 1952 it became the American Group Psychotherapy Association. The logo still likes 1942. House histories admit the fuss. This essay is not a membership brochure. It is a history of how a practice got a hotel, a journal, a certification culture, and then a century that did not promise it a living.

## An umbrella after a fight

Early AGPA, in later tellings, was psychoanalytically strict and psychiatrically proud. The 1960s and 1970s brought encounter, TA, Gestalt, psychodrama, and people who did not want a Freudian gate. Scheidlinger's 1991 essay on "conceptual pluralism" is a dated peace treaty: the association as an umbrella. Umbrellas leak. They also keep a field from splitting into unreadable tribes — or they hide the split in a plenary.

The *International Journal of Group Psychotherapy* became a place where American group work talked to itself. Alonso, Ormont, Rutan, Stone, Lieberman, Burlingame: names that reappear in the figure essays. A journal is not a proof of quality. It is a proof of enough people to peer-review.

Certification (CGP and later marks) is a labor-market object. It tells insurers and clinics that someone sat through a curriculum. It does not tell a patient whether Tuesday will be kind.

## Measurement

Gary Burlingame and colleagues spent decades doing what encounter leaders said could not be done: aggregating trials, asking whether small-group treatments move symptoms, asking under what conditions. Meta-analyses are historical objects with inclusion rules. They are not a slogan. This pack will not say "research proves group works." It will say: a research program existed, it found effects in many protocols and settings, and it kept stumbling on the same limits — dropout, leader fidelity, what was measured, who was excluded.

Process-group loyalists sometimes ignored the numbers. Manualized-group loyalists sometimes ignored the process. AGPA tried to hold both in the same ballroom. The ballroom is a compromise you can walk through.

## Managed care and the eight-session circle

The 1990s taught American clinicians a new arithmetic. Insurers bought "group" as a cheaper hour and then asked why it lasted a year. Eight sessions of interpersonal learning is a different object from eighty. Some clinics invented "focus" as a virtue. Some dumped process and kept psychoeducation because psychoeducation has a last page. Patients felt the dump as abandonment. Abandonment is a clinical word and a billing word.

Rutan and Stone's *Psychodynamic Group Psychotherapy* (1984) is a book from just before that vise tightened; later editions (with Shay) write inside the vise. The book is a dated attempt to keep a long group thinkable. Thinkable is not the same as payable.

## Zoom as a dated room

In 2020 groups moved onto screens because buildings closed. The move was not a philosophy. It was a public-health order plus a software industry. People discovered they could attend from a parked car. People discovered they could not smell the room. People dropped. People stayed who had never been able to get childcare. Platforms recorded or did not, leaked or did not, sat inside HIPAA conversations that bored everyone and still mattered.

This series will not settle whether online groups are "as good." It will say they are a 2020– object with ancestors in telephone groups and in rural outreach. A small-city practice that never runs psychotherapy groups still learned, that year, that a circle can be a grid of faces. Speech-language telepractice is a neighboring, licensed story. Do not merge it here for SEO.

## What not to do with an association

Do not treat AGPA membership as a WOW credential. Do not scrape conference selfies. Do not turn a meta-analysis into a booking line. Do not tell a reader to "find a CGP" as if this page were a directory. Directories exist elsewhere and go stale.

Do notice the long arc: class, ward, war, stage, T-group, encounter, textbook, manual, screen. The arc is not progress. It is a set of rooms. A later editor who wants a wave-six closer can send a reader back to essay 01. The chairs were already there. The bill was not.

## Certification, journals, and the empty chair on Zoom

The *International Journal of Group Psychotherapy* is an American voice with an international name. European *Group Analysis* is another voice. They cite each other unevenly. Uneven citation is how a field stays provincial while saying "international."

CGP hours, videotape review, and institute diplomas are labor. Labor can be a guild wall. Guild walls protect patients from untrained weekends. Guild walls also keep out the social-group-work cousin and the rural worker who cannot fly to a hotel. Scheidlinger's umbrella did not abolish the wall. It painted it.

Zoom's empty square — camera off, a name in a box — is a 2020– object that earlier telephone groups only sketched. A conductor who cannot see hands cannot do the work some theories require. Other theories never required hands. The screen sorts the theories without announcing the sort.

## Hotels, vendors, and the exhibit hall

An AGPA meeting is a hotel: plenaries, institutes, an exhibit hall of books and liability insurance. Exhibit halls are how a profession admits it is also a market. This pack is not anti-market. It is anti-pretending the hotel is only a seminar.

When these essays are imported to staging WP, do not auto-link "AGPA" to a membership sales page. A history post is not a recruiter.

An eight-session authorization is a literary form. It has a beginning the clinic chooses, a middle the patient barely meets, and an end the insurer writes. Process-group theory was not written in that form. Psychoeducation can be stuffed into it. The stuffing is the 1990s. Zoom then stuffed the stuffing into a grid.

A noindex flag on a staging import is a 2026 manners this folder already wrote into WP_IMPORT. Manners are not optional for Zoom paragraphs. Platforms change names. Sentences that name a platform will stale. Stale is why a later editor must read wave six again.

A later editor who imports wave six should keep the hotel and the screen in separate sentences. Hotels have plenaries. Screens have squares. The century ended by trying to make them one room. They are not one room.
