**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Kurt Lewin was born in 1890 and died in February 1947. He was a German-Jewish psychologist who left a country that had no more room for him, taught in America, founded the Research Center for Group Dynamics at MIT, and believed that a group could be studied without being reduced to a pile of individuals. The T-group era essay holds New Britain and Bethel. This essay holds the man who did not live to see Maine.

## Field, climate, democracy

The 1930s experiments with Ronald Lippitt and Ralph White on autocratic, democratic, and laissez-faire leadership climates are still in every textbook. Textbooks flatten them into a civics lesson. The papers were about children and clubs and a refugee's interest in how authority feels in a room. Feeling in a room is not a soft topic after 1933.

"There is nothing so practical as a good theory" is the sentence everyone quotes. Quoting it is not the same as having a theory. Lewin had one: life space, vectors, the idea that behavior is a function of person and environment. Later OD consultants used the sentence as a business card. He is not responsible for every card.

## 1946, then absence

The Connecticut interracial workshop asked for change agents, not patients. Participants walked into the staff's night meeting and disagreed. He welcomed the disagreement as data. That welcome is the T-group's conception — if one must have a conception. Conceptions are myths. The dated event is enough.

He died before the 1947 Bethel summer. Bradford, Lippitt, and Benne carried the work. A history that puts him in a Bethel group photo is a false history. This pack will not.

Race was in the 1946 brief. The afterlife whitened. He cannot be used as a halo for a corporate offsite that never mentions fair employment.

## What not to do with Lewin

Do not run a "Lewinian" lab on staff. Do not put him on a diversity poster without the 1946 commission's name. Do not confirm a Commons portrait without a file-page read.

Type plus "New Britain, 1946" as text is safer.

A small-city manager who talks about "unfreezing" is using a later popularization of his change talk. Popularizations lose the refugee. The refugee is the point of this figure essay. Group dynamics, as he practiced it, was not a toy. It was a science built by a man who had seen groups used to destroy.

## MIT, children, and the unfinished summer

The Research Center for Group Dynamics was a wartime-and-after institution: science in the service of a democracy that had just fought. That service can be noble. It can be a grant. He wrote as if the two could be one. Later DOD and corporate funders kept the grant and thinned the noble.

The climate studies with children are easy to teach as a cartoon of three leadership styles. Cartoons lose the refugee's question: how does a room become cruel without anyone announcing cruelty. That question is why he is on a therapy-history calendar at all. He did not run therapy groups as his job. He ran the science that T-groups and then encounter raided.

February 1947 is a hard stop. Do not put him in a Bethel circle. Do not use a Commons face until the file page is read. Type plus 1946 New Britain is the honest graphic.

A good theory, in his sentence, was practical. Practical later meant a vendor deck. The deck kept the sentence and lost the 1946 commission. This figure essay exists to put the commission back: interracial, interreligious, fair employment, Connecticut. If a graphic is needed, set that list in type. Not a face. Not a Bethel postcard.

## How to hold an absence

The most important fact in this figure essay is a missing summer. He is not in Bethel. Graphics that put him there are lying. Type is safer than a Commons face with a thin file page.

The 1946 commission's brief — fair employment, interracial and interreligious tension, Connecticut — is the graphic this pack prefers. List the brief. Do not list "authenticity." Authenticity is the afterlife that whitened the brief.

A February 1947 death is a stop sign. Graphics that run the sign are lying. New Britain 1946 is the workshop. Bethel 1947 is other people's summer. Write it on the wall if you need a wall.

MIT's Research Center for Group Dynamics is a wartime-and-after letterhead. Letterheads move — later toward Michigan, in the institutional story. He did not move with the later letterhead. He died. The sentence "Lewin founded NTL at Bethel in 1946" fails two facts at once. This file exists to fail that sentence in public.

This file is not the Bethel essay. Bethel is 1947 and other people's summer. This file is 1890–1947, New Britain 1946, a missing Maine, a commission on fair employment. The missing summer is the figure.

A later editor should open a 1946 Connecticut workshop account and a 1947 Bethel program and lay them on one table. Two summers. One dead man between them. The table will make a false caption impossible. If a Commons portrait is used, open the file page the same morning. Thin authorship stays `confirm`. Type plus the commission's brief is still the better graphic.

New Britain is in Connecticut. Bethel is in Maine. February sits between them. Do not move the man after February.

Connecticut 1946 is a workshop with a justice brief. Maine 1947 is a laboratory summer he did not attend. Justice briefs and laboratory summers are not synonyms. This file exists to stop the synonym.
