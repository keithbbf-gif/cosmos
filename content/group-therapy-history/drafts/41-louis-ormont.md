**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Louis R. Ormont was born in 1918 and died in 2008. *The Group Therapy Experience* (1992) is the public book: a late-century New York modern-analytic account of what a conductor does with feeling in a room. Students still name him when they talk about "using" the group. Using is a verb that needs an ethics. He wrote as if the verb could be taught.

This essay will not teach the verb. It will place a 1992 book next to Rutan and Stone 1984 and Yalom's editions: three American ways to keep a long group thinkable while managed care shortened the hour.

## Modern analytic, not a 1938 Wolf copy

He is in a lineage that includes Wolf's analysis-in-groups and Spotnitz's modern psychoanalysis, with a group-specific craft. Lineage fights are institute fights. This page will not pick a winner. It will say: New York had rooms where the conductor's induced feeling was the data. That is a different center than Yalom's interpersonal learning and a different center than a skills board.

Charisma again. People described him as a presence. Presence is not a method you can ship to a county clinic. The book is what shipped. Books flatten presence. Flattening is how a teaching life becomes a shelf.

## 1992 as a date

By 1992 encounter was a memory, CMHCs were tired, and a hardcover about long analytic groups was almost a resistance literature. Resistance literatures can be precious. They can also keep a craft alive until a census changes its mind. He wrote for people who still had a weekly room. Many readers no longer did.
