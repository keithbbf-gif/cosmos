**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Carl Ransom Rogers was born in 1902 and died in 1987. The sister pack's figure essay is the person-centered life: the 1957 paper, the conditions, the counseling-center years. This essay is only the loud decade when he sat in rooms that were not a counseling hour and wrote *Carl Rogers on Encounter Groups* (1970).

If you want Rogers the clinician, go there. If you want encounter the fashion, see the era essay. Here is the hinge: a famous listener on the road.

## A modest book in a loud year

He describes groups that mill, resist, and sometimes loosen. He does not write like a prophet of intensity. He writes like a man who has seen people drop their public face and is not sure the drop is always a good. That uncertainty is why this pack can use him. Catalogs that used his name as a blessing were not always reading the uncertainty.

Large-group demonstrations — hundreds of people, a famous man in a chair — are a different object from an eight-person outpatient group. He knew. The public wanted the famous man. The method wanted a listener. Those wants collide. Collision is the 1970 tour.

He had already been suspicious of the expert. Encounter culture took that suspicion and sometimes made it a hatred of any craft. Craft-hatred injures. He is not the author of every weekend that used his adjective.
