**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Siegmund Heinrich Fuchs was born in 1898 in what was then Germany and died in 1976 as S. H. Foulkes, a London name for a refugee who had already changed countries once. He trained in medicine and psychoanalysis, sat at Northfield from 1943, published in *The Lancet* in 1946, issued *Introduction to Group-Analytic Psychotherapy* in 1948, and helped make the Group-Analytic Society (1952) and later the Institute of Group Analysis into peacetime addresses.

The Northfield era essay holds the hospital. This essay holds the conductor who trusted conversation more than Bion's dark poetry — and who still wrote as a psychoanalyst.

## A refugee's English

The name change is not a trivia item. British medicine in the 1930s–40s received Central European Jews and asked them to be useful. Fuchs/Foulkes was useful in a military hospital that needed groups more than couches. English became the language of a method that still tastes, in translation, of German thought and of Freud. Students who meet only the later IGA house style can forget the refugee. Forgetting the refugee is how a method becomes native and then conservative.

He had run groups before Northfield, in Exeter, in the telling of later historians. `[VERIFY]` the Exeter years against a biographical monograph before a caption. The war made the work visible.

## Of, by, including

His prepositions are the famous inheritance: therapy of the group, by the group, including the conductor. The sentence is a theory of location. The illness is not only inside one speaker. The cure is not only inside one expert. The conductor is not outside. American process-group teachers said similar things in a different accent. Foulkes's accent is "matrix" — a web of communication that is the group. Matrix is a word later writers either loved or found foggy. Fog is not always a defect. Sometimes it is a refusal to turn a circle into a list of factors.

Eve Lewis co-authored with him in 1945. Elizabeth Foulkes later kept an archive and a memory. Women in the early papers are easy to index away. Do not.

He was not Bion. The two are paired on slides because they shared a building and a decade. They did not share a temperament. Pairing them as "the Northfield pair" is the error the era essay already refuses.

## An institute's father

Peacetime required a curriculum, a journal (*Group Analysis*), a way to qualify. Foulkes became a founding object. Founding objects get protected. Protection can freeze a 1948 book into a church. Later group analysts argued with him, imported other schools, and still said "matrix" on automatic. Automatic language is the fate of success.

American AGPA did not become IGA-west. Visitors crossed. Translations lagged. A U.S. resident who read Yalom 1970 and never read Foulkes 1948 still lives in a country that imported the *idea* of a conductor more than the IGA diploma.

## What not to do with Foulkes

Do not run a "group-analytic" hour because you like the prepositions. Training is a years-long object in that tradition. Do not interpret your family dinner as a matrix. Do not scrape IGA portraits.

A small-city clinician who will never see London can still use one Foulkesian caution: you are in the group you conduct. The caution is not a technique. It is a placement. Placement is the beginning of ethics.

## Exeter, London, and the matrix as a habit

If Exeter groups predate Northfield, they matter because they show the method was not only khaki. `[VERIFY]` the years. London peacetime then made a curriculum that could forget khaki. Forgetting khaki is how a war method becomes a profession. Remembering khaki is how a profession stays honest about manpower.

The *Group Analysis* journal taught a house style: conductor, matrix, resonance, location. House styles help beginners and freeze intermediates. He cannot be blamed for the freeze. He wrote the 1948 book in a country rebuilding a health service. The NHS is a political object in the neighboring CBT pack's IAPT essay. Here it is the reason a conductor could be a public employee.

German-Jewish refugee, English name, psychoanalytic loyalty: the three facts should stay in the same paragraph so a student cannot pick one.

A conductor who trusts conversation can still drown a beginner in "matrix." He cannot be asked to police every later use. He can be read in 1946 and 1948 as a refugee doctor in a military hospital and then in a rebuilding city. Those dated reads are better than a house-style poster. This pack prefers the *Lancet* to the poster.

## How to hold a conductor

A conductor who includes himself in the treatment is making an ethics claim: you do not get to stand outside and interpret as if you were weather. Weather is Bion's tone. Inclusion is Foulkes's. Both sat in Birmingham. Both wrote after. Pick with your eyes open.

The IGA later became a world with qualifying hours. Hours are a guild. Guilds protect and exclude. He needed a peacetime guild because a war hospital is not a profession. Professions outlive wars. They also forget them. Read 1946 when the house style gets too calm.

Fuchs is the name to keep in parentheses so an English house style cannot complete the assimilation. Assimilation is a professional success and a historical loss. This file holds both.

This file is not the Northfield essay. Northfield holds Hollymoor, two movements, khaki. This file holds Fuchs-to-Foulkes, 1948, a peacetime institute, the prepositions. Keep khaki in the other file. Keep the name-change here.
