**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

J. Scott Rutan is living as of last verification (2026-09-14). He founded the Center for Group Psychotherapy at Massachusetts General Hospital / Harvard Medical School (1985–1996 in later NSGP telling), co-founded the Boston Institute for Psychotherapy, served as president of the Northeastern Society for Group Psychotherapy (1978–1980) and later of AGPA, and wrote, with Walter N. Stone, *Psychodynamic Group Psychotherapy* (Collamore Press, Lexington, Massachusetts, 1984; 222 pages). Later Guilford editions added Joseph J. Shay; the fifth Guilford edition is 2014. This essay uses those public objects. No health speculation. No conference-headshot scrape.

## A textbook against disappearance

The 1984 book is 222 pages of an attempt to keep a psychodynamic group thinkable just as the American hour began to shrink. Later editions write inside managed care and then inside virtual groups — the fifth edition's opening astonishment that personal groups might be replaced by screens. Dating the editions is the honest way to read them. A 1984 sentence and a 2014 sentence are not the same object.

Alonso is in the citations and in the Boston school. The 1979 paper on intimacy and individuation is a joint object. This pack splits the lives so neither is only a co-author.

He taught a lot of people who still run Tuesday groups. Teaching lives do not always write famous 1970 books. They write the book a supervisor actually assigns. Assignment is power. The Guilford edition is power.

## Stone, Shay, and a center's afterlife

Walter N. Stone is the 1984 co-author, a Cincinnati-and-elsewhere teacher whose name this pack will not drop. Joseph J. Shay joins later editions. Three names on a Guilford spine are a Boston-and-friends school. Schools are not cults if they date their editions and admit the census.

The MGH Center for Group Psychotherapy is an institutional object: a place that trained people who then left for cities without centers. Leaving is how a method travels. Travel thins the method. Thinning is not always a fall. Sometimes it is a Tuesday group in a town that never heard of Collamore Press.

## 1988 address, observation group, two spines

His AGPA presidential address, delivered 11 February 1988 in New York and printed in *IJGP* in 1989 as "Making Society's Groups More Therapeutic," is a public object. This page will not turn it into civic advice. It will date a Boston teacher speaking from the association's lectern just as managed care was about to shrink the hour his 1984 book had assumed.

NSGP later recorded that residents at the MGH center were asked to sit for a year in an observation group he ran with Anne Alonso and to lead a supervised group for two years. That training density is a local accident of a hospital that still believed groups belonged in a psychiatry residency. Most American cities did not copy it. Interns who left Boston carried a rumor of seriousness. Rumors thin. Thinning is how a method arrives in a town that never heard of Collamore Press.
