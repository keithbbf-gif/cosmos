**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1921 in Vienna, J. L. Moreno — born 1889 in Bucharest, a man who liked a myth as much as a method — ran something he later called the Theatre of Spontaneity. Audiences and players traded the script for the moment. He brought the taste to New York, worked with children and with prisoners, published *Who Shall Survive?* in 1934, and built a stage at Beacon. He claimed, in a 1932 Philadelphia setting and in later retellings, the phrases "group therapy" and "group psychotherapy." Other people were already gathering patients. The fight over the phrase is the fight over whether a circle is a lecture, a conversation, or a play.

This essay will not teach a warm-up. Psychodrama has trainers, institutes, and a literature of harm when untrained people copy what they saw at a conference. History can describe a stage. It cannot lend you one.

## Sociometry before the spotlight

*Who Shall Survive?* is a book about maps of choice: who picks whom, in a school, in a cottage, in a reformatory. Moreno and collaborators (Helen Hall Jennings among the important names) made diagrams that look, to later eyes, like network science in ink. The Hudson School for Girls work is often dated to the early 1930s; `[VERIFY]` the April-day legend some textbooks repeat before treating it as a founding sunrise. The maps were used to rearrange living groups. Rearrangement is power. A diagram of "stars" and "isolates" can help a cottage breathe. It can also mark a child.

Sociometry traveled into American social psychology and then into corporate "team" tools that never say Beacon. The travel is a success and a flattening. Moreno wanted spontaneity and a cosmic language of encounter. HR wanted a seating chart.

## The stage at Beacon

Psychodrama asked a person to step onto a space, take auxiliaries, reverse roles, and try a scene that life had frozen. Zerka Toeman Moreno, who became his partner and then the method's great teacher after his death in 1974, insisted that the work had craft and ethics, not only charisma. Charisma was plentiful. Visitors described a show. Shows attract imitators.

Group psychotherapy of the talking kind often kept the body in a chair. Psychodrama stood the body up. That is why analytic groups were suspicious and why encounter culture later stole the standing-up without the craft. Fritz Perls's Gestalt demonstrations, another stage, are a neighboring object (and a different estate). Do not mash Perls and Moreno into "experiential." They had different metaphysics and different debts.

Prisons and hospitals used versions of the method because a stage can hold a lot of people and because administrators like visible activity. Visible is not the same as consented. A protagonist in front of a ward has a different privacy problem than a protagonist in a closed training group. Moreno liked publicity. Publicity is in the archive. So is the need, later, for consent forms that his early decades did not write in modern ink.

## The phrase war

If Moreno coined "group psychotherapy" at an APA meeting in 1932, he did not coin the practice. Pratt, Lazell, Marsh, Burrow, and unnamed social workers were already in rooms. What he wanted was credit for an *interactional* group, not a class. The want is understandable. The lawsuits-in-prose that followed are tiring and human. AGPA's New York world (Slavson) and Beacon's world did not merge into one happy American school. They competed for trainees and for the word.

Europe had its own psychodrama institutes. Latin America took the method seriously. A history that stays in Beacon is a New York-state history. This pack's figure essays on Jacob and Zerka split the life from the stage; this era essay keeps the stage as an object that outlived them.

## What not to do with a stage

Do not try a role reversal at a staff retreat because you read a paragraph. Do not put a child on a stage for adults' learning. Do not use prison psychodrama stills. Do not call a family argument at Thanksgiving "psychodrama" as a joke; the joke hides a method that can open something a joke cannot close.

Do not generate an AI Moreno. Type is enough.

A contemporary drama therapist, a Playback Theatre company, and a psychodrama diplomate are different credentials. This series will not sort your state's licenses. It will say: the stage entered the clinic as a Romanian-American immigrant's ambition and a woman's long teaching life, and the clinic never fully digested it. Undigested ancestors keep returning. That is not a reason to swallow them whole on a Wednesday night.

## Journals, Hudson, and the missing program

Moreno founded journals that still sit on specialty shelves. A journal is how a method becomes citable. Citation is how a stage enters a medical library that would not buy a ticket. *Who Shall Survive?* already wanted to be science: maps, numbers of choices, a claim that rearrangement could be shown. Later network scientists who never say Beacon are a success he would have sued for in prose.

The Hudson School work — cottages, girls, diagrams — is the scene textbooks love. Love it carefully. A reformatory is not a lab with consent forms. Rearranging living groups on the basis of a star-and-isolate map is an intervention in a child's daily life. This pack will not reprint a map.

April legends about an "official start" need a program in hand. Until the program is in hand, treat 1932 as Moreno's claim and 1934 as a book's year. Claims and books are different objects.

Drama therapy, Playback, and sociodrama (the group as protagonist) are later neighboring crafts with their own associations. Do not merge them for a tag cloud.

## Auxiliaries and the unpaid other

An auxiliary ego plays a mother, a boss, a dead friend. The playing is labor. In training it is a skill. In a ward it can be a patient assigned to another patient's scene. Assignment is power. Zerka's later teaching tried to make the labor chosen and debriefed. Early publicity photos rarely show the debrief. This pack will not show the scene.

Sociodrama — the group, not the person, as protagonist — is Moreno's other noun. It traveled into education and into organizational work. It is not a therapy how-to on this page. It is a reminder that he thought in more than one scale.

International institutes after 1974 did not wait for Beacon's permission. That independence is a success and a trademark fight. Fights over names are how methods stay property. Property is not the same as history.

A ticketed Vienna evening and a Beacon weekend are not the same economy. One sold a night. The other sold a method. The method outlived the night because Zerka and a set of institutes kept teaching when the ticket office closed. Teaching is slower than publicity. Slower is why this era essay had to be longer than a coinage fight.
