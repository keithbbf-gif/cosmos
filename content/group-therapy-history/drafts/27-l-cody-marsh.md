**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Lindus Cody Marsh died in Tucson on 4 November 1949, aged sixty-six (JAMA). That puts his birth about 1883. He had been a minister before he was a physician (Albany Medical College, 1929 — a late medical degree). He worked at Kings Park State Hospital and Worcester State Hospital, directed a Boston Re-educational Institute, and later ran La Casa del Encanto in Tucson. The 1935 *JNMD* paper lists Tucson. The life is a map of American asylums plus a Southwest last act.

The interwar era essay holds the 1931 lecture list. This essay holds the pastor who thought a ward should be a school.

## Revival as a hospital technology

"By the crowd they have been broken, by the crowd they shall be healed." The line is hymn and program. He meant lectures, testimony, singing, a final exam, recovering patients as teachers. He said the class was an addition, not a substitute for individual treatment. He said many patients left happier. Happiness in a 1931 state hospital is a serious claim and not a trial.

"Better hospital citizens" is the phrase that should keep a later reader awake. A class that improves morale can also improve compliance with an institution that did not deserve compliance. He wrote as a believer in reeducation. Believers in reeducation have a long American history, some of it generous, some of it a project of making people easier to house.

The 1936 relatives' group paper is an ancestor of later family psychoeducation. He got there as a pastor who thought the household needed a lecture too.

## Tucson

A 1934 *NEJM* piece on the psychiatric hazards of medical practice shows him addressing physicians as a man who had left the big Eastern hospitals. Arizona is not a footnote. It is where a revivalist-hospital man went when the East was done with him, or he with it. `[VERIFY]` the Casa's character before a caption — sanitarium, school, both.

## What not to do with Marsh

Do not revive the revival. Do not use the motto on a clinic wall. Do not treat "hour of happiness" as outcome data. Do not invent his theology.

A small-city hospital chaplain who sits in a patient education hour is closer to Marsh than a process-group conductor is. Closer is not a blessing. It is a lineage of rooms that teach. Teaching can be respect. Teaching can be a sermon the patient did not ask for. He knew it was a sermon. He thought sermons saved. History's job is to keep the thought visible, not to preach.

## Minister, late M.D., Tucson sun

A 1929 medical degree after a ministry is a second career in the same vocation: speaking to a room about how to live. The 1931 paper is a minister's hospital. The 1934 *NEJM* piece on physicians' hazards is a minister's warning to doctors. The Tucson last act is a minister's west.

La Casa del Encanto — the name is Spanish for a house of charm or enchantment — wants a local historian before a caption. Enchantment is a dangerous word next to psychosis. He may have meant hope. Hope and enchantment are not synonyms in a desert sanitarium.

Aged sixty-six in November 1949: a life that did not see AGPA's hotel decades. He is an interwar object. Later textbooks that quote only the motto are quoting a hymn and skipping the exam at the end of the lecture series. The exam is the tell. He really did think it was a school.

A final examination at the end of a psychosis lecture series is a school so literal it embarrasses later group therapists. Embarrassment is useful. It shows how far the 1970 textbook traveled from a 1931 hymn. Both objects gathered people. Only one pretended the teacher was not a teacher. He never pretended. Give him that.

## How to hold a motto

The motto will be the only sentence later slides keep. Keep the exam, the hymn, the "better hospital citizens," the 1936 relatives' group, the 1949 Tucson death, the late M.D. after a ministry. Those objects make the motto a program instead of a proverb.

La Casa del Encanto still wants a local historian. Enchantment next to psychosis is a word to handle with tongs. He may have meant a house of hope. Hope is not enchantment. This pass will not caption the Casa until a Tucson file is open.

A JAMA obituary is a better life date than a textbook memory. 4 November 1949, Tucson, aged 66. Albany 1929. Kings Park. Worcester. The motto is the least of those facts. Keep the exam.

*Mental Hygiene* in 1931 is a journal with a movement's name. Movements publish hope. He published a lecture list and an exam. The exam makes the hope a school. Schools grade. Grading a psychosis class is a 1931 fact this pack will not revive and will not hide.

This file is not the interwar essay. That essay holds four men. This file holds a minister's late M.D., a 1931 exam, a 1949 Tucson death, a motto that slides keep. Keep the exam. Drop the slide if you must drop something.

A later editor should open *Mental Hygiene* 1931 at the lecture list, not at the motto. The list is the school. The motto is the hymn. Then open JAMA December 1949 for the death line. Tucson, sixty-six, Albany 1929. If La Casa del Encanto has a local file, read it before any caption uses enchantment next to a diagnosis. Tongs.

Aged sixty-six is not a textbook guess. It is JAMA's line. Keep JAMA's line.
