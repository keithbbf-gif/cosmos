**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Saul Scheidlinger was born in 1918 and died in 2012. He is the figure who would not let AGPA forget children after Slavson's woodshop became a hotel of adult therapists. He also wrote the 1991 *IJGP* essay that named the association's move from a strict Freudian gate to an "umbrella." Umbrella is a peace word. Peace words hide remaining fights. He knew; the essay is a history, not a hymn.

## Children after the founding

The children's-activity era essay and the Slavson figure hold the 1930s–40s origin. Scheidlinger's career is the middle and late century: adolescent groups, the claim that a child in a circle is not a little adult, the institutional work of keeping a section and a literature alive. Without that work, this pack's child essay would be a prologue without a sequel.

He wrote in a century that invented new school diagnoses and new special-education laws. Retrofitting those laws onto his earlier papers is a student error. Read the year on the paper.

## Pluralism as an organizational fact

1991 is a dated peace: encounter had happened, TA had happened, research had happened, and AGPA could either split or hold. He described the hold. Holding is not the same as agreeing. An umbrella that cannot say no is a puddle. An umbrella that only says no is a sect. His essay is useful because it treats the association as a historical object, not as a family.

European and Latin American group cultures are not AGPA chapters. He wrote as an American journal's man. This pack's Foulkes and Moreno essays are the reminder that the umbrella was never the world.
