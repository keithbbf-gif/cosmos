**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Samuel Richard Slavson was born in 1890 and died in 1981. He is the reason this American pack cannot start every story in Birmingham or Beacon. He ran activity groups for children at the Jewish Board of Guardians, wrote *An Introduction to Group Therapy* in 1943, and chalked a luncheon note on an AOA blackboard that same winter. The association that followed made him its first president and then spent decades being more psychiatric than his woodshop.

The children's-group era essay holds the room. This essay holds the organizer.

## An engineer of a profession

He was not only a clinician. He was a man who wanted standards, a journal, a gate. The early constitution's supervised-hours rules and the invitation to APA members (so the house histories say) show a wish to sit next to psychiatry's prestige. Wish is not a sin. It is how a children's-agency method becomes a hotel plenary.

Nathan Ackerman sat on the first steering committee. Family therapy's later American address thus shares a 1943 table with group therapy's. The table is a fact. It does not make the two fields one.

Slavson's later *Analytic Group Psychotherapy* (1950) moved toward talk and toward a Freudian seriousness that the 1943 book already implied. The move tracked the market and the association. Children remained in his writing; they did not remain the association's center.

## Jewish Board, immigrant city

JBG was a New York Jewish social-service world. Slavson's groups were a minority institution's tool in a country that still had quotas and gentlemen's agreements. A history that treats AGPA as a generic "American" birth without that address is bleaching the file.

He wrote in a diagnostic language that later school systems would not use. Do not retrofit ADHD onto 1943. Do not reprint his cases. Children in print remain children.

## What not to do with Slavson

Do not put tools in a room and claim his name. Do not photograph children. Do not treat first-president status as a reason to ignore later pluralism (Scheidlinger's 1991 peace treaty). Do not scrape AGPA portraits.

Type: "S. R. Slavson, 1890–1981."

A small-city clinician who never joins AGPA still lives in a country whose group profession began, in one of its tellings, with a blackboard and a children's agency. The other tellings (Pratt, Moreno, Northfield) remain true. He is the American *organizational* ancestor. Organizations decide who may speak at the podium. That is a group fact he would have recognized.

## First president, later shadow

Twelve psychiatric presidents after him, in the house telling, is a fact about prestige. It is also a fact about who got the podium while he became the founder one cites and does not assign. Founders who are not assigned become statues. Statues do not select children for an activity group.

He lived until 1981. He saw encounter, TA, and Yalom's textbook. The seeing is not recorded here as a late interview unless a later editor finds one. Assume a long life that outlasted the woodshop's centrality.

Jewish Board archives, if a historian is granted them, are the place for the children's names this pack will not print. Archives have rules. Blogs do not get the names.

His 1890 birth is a nineteenth-century American immigrant childhood in the biographical sketches. `[CITE NEEDED]` for the exact origin city before a caption. The JBG address is enough for this pass.

A blackboard at a hotel registration desk is a fragile founding. It depends on a conference already happening (AOA) and a man willing to chalk. Fragile foundings get logos with earlier years. 1942 on a logo and 1943 in the minutes is the AGPA fuss. He is inside the fuss. The woodshop is older than the fuss. Keep the woodshop in the first paragraph when someone only wants the presidency.

## How to hold a first president

A first president who began in a children's agency will be remembered as an association. Remember the clay. Clay is why this pack exists as a circle lane and not only as a hotel lane. Hotels forget clay. INDEX wave 3 puts him with Coyle and Konopka on purpose: professions of sitting that are not all psychiatry.

1981 is a long watch. He saw the field become something the 1943 book did not describe. We do not have his late interview on this pass. Do not invent one. The books and the blackboard are enough.

A 1943 Commonwealth Fund book is a wartime American object: paper, an agency, children who were not soldiers. Northfield's khaki gets the myth. His clay gets the association. This pack put clay on the calendar so khaki cannot keep the whole origin.

Commonwealth Fund as a publisher in 1943 is a philanthropic American object, not a trade press. Philanthropy decided which children's methods got paper in a war year. Paper is a politics. This pack names the press so "Introduction to Group Therapy" does not look like it fell from the sky.

This file is not the children's-activity essay. That essay holds clay and the after-school clock. This file holds a first president, a 1943 press, a 1943 blackboard, a 1981 death. Clay first. Presidency second. That order is the point.

A later editor should open the 1943 Commonwealth Fund book to the table of contents before opening AGPA's history page. Contents first, blackboard second. The contents are children. The blackboard is a profession. This pack's wave 3 exists because those two objects are not the same and because hotels forget the first. If the origin city of his childhood is found, paste it. Until then JBG is the address that matters.
