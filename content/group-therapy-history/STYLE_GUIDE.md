# Style guide — WOW Therapies, group-therapy history series

This pack is educational history for [WOWTherapies.com](https://wowtherapies.com): group psychotherapy, group analysis, T-groups, mutual-aid fellowships, therapeutic communities, and social group work. It is **not** the individual-hour psychotherapy series and **not** the SLPWOW speech-pathology series. Do not retell a full Freud, Rogers, or Beck biography here. Those lives belong on the consulting-room pack. A figure who touched both (Yalom, Rogers in encounter, Balint) appears here only in a *group* frame.

`voice_check: human` means the writer draft passed this file. `voice_check: edited` means a human editor read the piece aloud and tightened grammar, voice, and guardrails. Neither value is a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Who is speaking

A historically literate magazine writer who has sat in enough circles to know that a group is not a cheaper hour. Warm without being pastoral. Precise without being a textbook. The reader may be a curious client, a family member, a student, or a clinician. Do not talk down. Do not talk shop as if the room were empty of patients.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas practice brand. These essays are **heritage education**, not a menu of services and not a claim that the clinic runs every kind of group named here.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" as a metaphor for a field
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- three-beat synonym piles ("heal, grow, and thrive")
- "the father of group therapy" unless you are quoting a myth, not endorsing it
- "gold-standard treatment" as marketing
- invented quotes
- AI-generated faces or "period photographs" that never existed

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "empower," "in this article we will explore," "journey of healing" as filler, "safe space" as a slogan.

## Group-specific bans

- Icebreakers, warm-ups, "try this circle," empty-chair scripts, doubling / role-reversal how-tos, sociometry as a parlor game
- AA / Twelve Step instructions, slogans as homework, or "find a meeting tonight" as treatment advice
- "How to start a therapy group," fee schedules, screening checklists written as a protocol
- Encounter-group exercises, marathon-group recipes, or "let it all out" as counsel
- Any line a reader could follow as a session

History may describe that Moreno used role reversal. History may not teach the reader to do it.

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a room, a document, or a named quarrel. The first sentence should be checkable.
2. Mix sentence length. A short sentence after a long one. Do not write every paragraph as topic sentence + three examples + wrap.
3. Name the book, paper, hospital, or society. Pratt's 1907 *JAMA* paper. Hollymoor / Northfield. Bethel, 1947. *Encounter Groups: First Facts* (1973). *The Theory and Practice of Group Psychotherapy* (1970).
4. Mark uncertainty. `[CITE NEEDED]` or a plain "historians disagree" beats a clean falsehood.
5. Put Pratt, Moreno, Foulkes, and Yalom in their century. Do not offer them as current treatment manuals.
6. Give social work, mutual aid, Latin American operative groups, and women's work full essays, not a closing paragraph titled "diversity."
7. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target 1,000–1,800 words for figures, 1,200–2,000 for era essays. Cut the recap paragraph if it only restates the headings. `check_pack.py` fails an era under 1,200 words or a figure under 1,000. Deepen with dated scenes, not with recap.

## Claims posture

See `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`. History may describe a method. History may not teach a reader to self-administer it. No session scripts, no skill worksheets, no "try this at home" protocols.

Efficacy sentences, if any, stay historical and sourced ("Lieberman, Yalom, and Miles reported…") and never become "group will help your anxiety."

Living people get extra care: no gossip, no unsourced health claims. Public books, public lectures, and public interviews only.

## Portraits

Public domain or Creative Commons only. Document the file in `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. If there is no clean license, use type or a period object — never a generated face. See `PHOTO_NOTES.md`.

## Series voice, in one paragraph

Write as if *The New Yorker* and *Group Analysis* had to share a desk, and the desk was in a small-city clinic that still answers its own phone. Circles are allowed to be strange. The present is not allowed to be sold.
