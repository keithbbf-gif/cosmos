# Claims guardrails — group therapy history pack

Educational history only. These sentences must never go on wowtherapies.com, in ads, or in schema markup.

This file is a refuse list. If a draft drifts toward any line below, cut the line. Do not "soften" it into a how-to.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas **speech-language pathology** practice. These essays are heritage education about group psychotherapy and its cousins. They are not a menu of groups the clinic offers, and they are not permission to invent a group program on the service pages.

## Never say (treatment / DIY)

- Any instruction a reader could follow as a session: go-rounds to run, "empty chair" scripts, psychodrama warm-ups, T-group feedback formulas, Yalom factor checklists to apply to oneself, DBT skill steps, exposure hierarchies, "ask the group these ten questions."
- "Try this at home," "start your own process group," "you can encounter your way out of…," "self-treat in a circle of friends."
- Dosing of psychiatric medication, taper advice, or "ask your doctor about [drug]" as content.
- "Group is the gold standard for [diagnosis]."
- "Studies prove group works better than individual" without a named trial or review, a date, and a sentence about limits (selection, dropout, setting, what was measured).
- Crisis instructions that replace emergency services. A single line — "If you are in danger, call local emergency services" — is enough. Do not write a safety-planning protocol, a suicide-group script, or a "how to hold someone in group" checklist.
- How to screen members, set fees, obtain malpractice coverage, or "launch a group in your practice."

## Never say (medical / legal)

- That WOW Therapies diagnoses, treats, or "specializes in" a DSM/ICD condition **in these history posts**. Service pages are a different file and a different review.
- That reading an essay is therapy, counseling, group, or a substitute for a licensed clinician.
- That a church basement, an AA meeting, a Facebook group, or a corporate retreat is "the same as" psychotherapy. Name cousins. Do not collapse them.
- PHI, composite patients, or "a client once told me" stories. No invented case vignettes with identifying color. No reconstructed Northfield, Synanon, or encounter-group scenes presented as eyewitness.
- Guarantees of outcome. "The group will hold you" is a slogan, not a claim this site may make.
- Anything a reasonable reader would hear as a diagnosis of themselves, their child, or their marriage.

## Never say (history that is too neat)

- "Pratt invented group therapy in 1905." (He ran a tuberculosis **class**. Historians argue whether the class is psychotherapy. Moreno later claimed the phrase "group psychotherapy." Pratt himself kept calling it a class. Date the argument; do not award a patent.)
- "Pinel struck off the chains" recycled as a group-therapy origin myth. (See the sister pack and Weiner 1979, 1994.)
- "Bion and Foulkes ran one Northfield experiment together." (There were at least two phases, different staff, different relations to the army. Bion and Rickman were first and brief. Foulkes, Bridger, Main and others were the longer second movement.)
- "Lewin founded NTL at Bethel in 1946." (The 1946 workshop was New Britain, Connecticut. Lewin died February 1947. Bethel's first National Training Laboratory summer was 1947, without him.)
- "AA is group therapy." (AA is a mutual-help fellowship with its own literature and traditions. Clinicians have learned from it and have also harmed people by medicalizing it. Keep the distinction.)
- "Encounter groups were harmless growth" **or** "encounter groups were a cult." Name the 1973 Lieberman–Yalom–Miles study, name Synanon, name Rogers's caution, and leave the reader's judgment intact.
- Refrigerator-mother, schizophrenogenic-mother, or "the group caused the psychosis" as fact.
- Conversion-therapy "success" in any group format. Name the harm. Do not teach the method.
- That a skills-training group is "not really therapy" **or** that a process group is "the only real group." They are different historical objects.
- That Zoom groups "don't count." They are a dated object (2020–) with their own limits. History can describe them. History cannot settle whether they are "as good."

## Never say (brand / SEO)

- "Best therapist in Arkansas," "best group in the Delta," or any superlative about the practice.
- Disease keywords paired with a CTA ("overcome PTSD in our group — book now") on these URLs.
- Schema `MedicalTherapy`, `Drug`, or `treats` / `indication` on history posts. `Article` is enough.
- Fake author credentials (invented PhD, "our group therapist Dr. …" who is not on the license wall).
- Patient testimonials in this folder.
- Implied service: "WOW Therapies now offers process groups / DBT skills / psychodrama." If the live site later adds a real group, that is a service-page change after license and insurance review — not a history-post CTA.

## Allowed

- Dated description of what a school claimed, who paid for it, who dissented, and what later researchers revised.
- Named trials and studies as historical events (Linehan 1991; Lieberman, Yalom, and Miles 1973; Burlingame meta-analyses as dated papers).
- Controversy, including racism, sexism, colonial psychiatry, coercive encounter, and the abuse of "community," when it is part of the record.
- A short "why a contemporary clinician still reads this" closing that does **not** become a protocol or a WOW booking line.
- The educational footer already in each draft.
- Honest cousins: camp meeting, Quaker meeting, TB class, AA, consciousness-raising, multifamily psychoeducation. Cousin ≠ identity.

## Living people

Yalom, Linehan, Rutan, Leszcz, Burlingame, McFarlane, and others who were alive at last verification (2026-09-14) are not public property. No health speculation. No unsourced private life. Public books, public lectures, and public interviews only.

## If asked for both a history and a how-to

Print the history. Refuse the how-to in one sentence: this series does not publish treatment protocols or group-launch kits.
