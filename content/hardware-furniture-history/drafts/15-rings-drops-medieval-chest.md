A medieval chest is luggage that learned to stay home.

The hardware is not a decoration on a sculpture. It is how you lift the thing, lock the thing, keep the lid from walking off. Iron rings. Hasps. Straps that are hinges and armor at once. Corner bindings. A lock plate that is also a face. The ring is a pull and a handle for two men. If you have ever tried to move a boarded chest that has lost its rings, you know why they were not optional.

I am not going to invent a parish and a year and a smith named Thomas. The type is enough: oak or pine, nailed or pegged, iron that is black and a little furred, a lid that is a seat when it is shut. The ring sits on a plate or through a staple. Your hand goes through it the way your hand goes through a door ring. This is architectural ironmongery that sat down on a box.

When brass drops show up in the late seventeenth century — William and Mary, the pear, the hollow back, the thin chased plate, the cotter pin clenched inside the drawer — they are remembering this without saying so. A drop is a ring that got pretty and learned to hang. You still put a finger through. You still pull. The chest has become a chest of drawers. The motion is the same. The metal got yellow.

Dealers love a "coffor" story. Some of those stories are true. Many chests were painted, many were too useful to survive as museum objects, many were cut down. The rings get stolen because they are portable. Empty staples on a boarded chest are a kind of honesty. Something was here. It was heavy enough to take.

The six-board chest is the vernacular cousin: a box from six boards, nailed or pegged, a lid, maybe a till inside the top. Hardware on those is iron or it is a later apology. I have opened tills that still had a pinch of dust that was once a letter. I have also opened tills that had a mouse nest and a bottle cap. The ring on the front does not care. It was for a hand and a rope.

If you put a brass bail on a boarded six-board chest, you have written 1750 on 1650, or 1980 on both. The handshake should match the luggage. A ring is not rustic chic. It is how you carry a household. When the household stopped moving, the ring stayed, and that is how luggage became furniture without changing its mind.
