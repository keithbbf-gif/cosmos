A handmade screw is a small autobiography.

The slot is off-center. The head is not round. The threads were filed or forced through a plate and they wander. The shank does not taper to a gimlet point; it ends blunt. No two are twins. Before roughly the 1810s, in the Western furniture I am talking about, that is the family.

Then machines start cutting threads and the body gets even while the slot is still a hacksaw. Then, in the mid-1840s — sources say 1846, or 1848, they do not sit still — a pointed gimlet screw, tapered, the modern idea. Then, about a decade on, a machine-cut slot that finally lands in the middle. Then a long plateau of slotted screws that look alike. Then Phillips, 1930s, a cross for power drivers and assembly lines. Robertson, square, 1908, a Canadian sanity that the United States mostly ignored. Pozidriv, later, a plus sign that is not Phillips and will strip if you pretend it is.

Treat these as bands, not birthdays. The articles disagree by a few years and they should. Factories do not change on a holiday. Old screws stay in barrels. New screws go into old holes the first time a hinge loosens.

One Phillips screw dates a repair. Five Phillips screws date a campaign. A single handmade screw in a plate of machine screws is a souvenir or a lie. Look at more than one. Look at the hinges *and* the lock *and* the pulls. Look at the dovetails. Hand-cut tails that wander, machine tails that do not. Circular-sawn boards are a late story for thick furniture stock. If the screws say 1820 and the saw says 1900, believe the saw, then ask who restocked the hardware drawer.

The trap is certainty. Dealers love a screw lecture because it sounds like science. It is closer to weather. Useful. Not a verdict. I have been wrong in both directions: called a piece late because of one bright screw, called a piece early because someone had a bag of reproduction handmade nails. The wood, the oxidation in a hole, the paint skins, the lock mortise — those have to agree.

If you replace screws, match the drive and the metal and the length. Do not put a 2-inch drywall screw through a drawer front because it bit. It will come out the other face and greet the shirts. I have greeted those shirts. They were not improved.
