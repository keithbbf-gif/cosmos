A till lock is a small tyranny.

It lives on a drawer — often the top drawer of a desk or a chest — and it does more than lock that drawer. On a lot of English and American casework the till drawer locks a rail or a pin that keeps the drawers below from opening. One key, a whole stack of no. That is a household idea. The person with the key is the person who decides whether the house is open.

A box lock or chest lock is a different tyranny. Hasp or hook, a lid, a bolt that comes up or across. Wardrobe locks throw a longer bolt, sometimes two. Rolltop and tambour locks have to catch a slatted river of wood. Desk galleries have tiny locks for tiny drawers that held stamps and the kind of secrets that are just ink.

Secret compartments are usually not magic. They are a lock you did not see, or a spring, or a well under a drawer that only opens when the drawer is out. Dealers love to discover them. Owners love to believe in them. I have found more rat nests than secrets. The few secrets I have found were boring: a deed, a key, a photograph. Boring is the correct temperature for a secret that survived.

When a till lock seizes, people force the top drawer and break the rail. Then the lower drawers are free and the desk is injured. Oil, patience, the right bit key, a locksmith who still understands furniture and not just cars. I am not always that patient. I have been the person with a screwdriver and a bad idea. The desk remembers.

Wooton desks and other patent offices of the late nineteenth century turn this into a building: a dozen little locks, or one lock that stands in for a dozen doors. I have only opened reproductions and photographs. The idea is the same tyranny at a louder volume. A clerk's life in pigeonholes.

A desk that keeps secrets is not romantic if you think about who the secrets were kept from. A clerk. A wife. A son. Locks are politics at the scale of a pigeonhole. I will not write a novel on a till. I will say: if the top drawer is the only one with a lock, look for the rail. If the rail is sawn through, someone already wrote the ending.
