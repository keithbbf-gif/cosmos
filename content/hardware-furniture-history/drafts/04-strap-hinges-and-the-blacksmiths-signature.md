A strap hinge is a sentence written across a chest.

One leaf is long. That is the point. It reaches onto the lid or the door so the leverage of opening does not tear out a short pair of butts that were never invited. The other side of the knuckle is a shorter staple, a pintle driven into the stile, a matching strap on the carcase. The long arm is what you see in the photograph if the photographer has any sense. Most don't. They crop to the panel.

Decorative ends get all the love in the books: bean, spear, fleur-de-lis, rattail curling off like a fern. I like them. I also do not trust them. A decorative end is a Saturday. The strap itself is Thursday — the day the lid had to work. If the fleur-de-lis is crisp and the strap is dead even and the screws are black-oxide Phillips from a blister pack, you are looking at a kitchen that wanted a story. The story is fine. Call it that.

On an older chest the strap often shares the job with a hasp. Lid, hinge, lock, one family of iron. The hasp is a strap that learned to fold and receive a padlock or a pin. You can feel the relatedness in the file marks. Same shop, same week, sometimes the same bar. When the hinges are wrought and the lock plate is stamped brass from a later century, the chest has been through a divorce. It happens. People wanted the lid to lock in a year that did not love iron.

Nail pattern first. Always. Early straps are clenched or clenched-and-clinched, the points turned over inside so a lid cannot shrug them out. Later, screws. Later still, screws that do not match the iron — bright steel in a black strap, or brass screws a smith would not have reached for. I have seen a beautiful rattail hung with drywall screws. I did not take a picture. Some things you just close the lid on.

The signature is not a name. Village smiths did not usually stamp a brand on a chest hinge. The signature is the walk of the hammer, the way the barrel is a little fatter on one side, the way the decorative split was drifted instead of sawn. Two chests from the same fire will rhyme. They will not photocopy.

If you are putting a strap on a new chest because you like the look, good. Make it long enough to do the job. A six-inch decoration on a lid that wants ten inches of iron is jewelry. The old ones were not jewelry first. They were the only way to keep a heavy lid from teaching the hinges about regret.
