Then the pull wants to be a small sculpture.

Tapered brass. A walnut plug in a brass cup. A ceramic olive. A slim bar that floats on two posts. Teak and a leather loop. The handshake becomes a design object. Magazines photograph it. The drawer is an excuse.

Some of these are wonderful in the hand. A good tapered pull has a balance; your fingers find a waist. A bad one is sharp at the lip and leaves a crease in your palm. Sculpture that forgot about skin. I have installed both. The wonderful ones make you open a drawer you do not need. That is a stupid test and I use it anyway.

Mid-century casework often wants a clean front. The pull is the only permitted event. So it had better be worth it. Or — and this is the next turn — it had better vanish. Routed undercuts. Hidden finger slots. Later, tip-on, push-to-open, the Blum religion of no hardware on the face. The sculptural pull is a brief window when the face is clean *and* the hand is honored. Before it, plates. After it, nothing.

Danish, Italian, American suburban, the cheap copies from a warehouse in New Jersey: the shape travels faster than the joinery. You will find a beautiful bar on a particleboard credenza that is already failing at the cam locks. The sculpture is not the furniture. The sculpture is the part they spent the dime on.

Eames and Knoll pieces often want almost no pull at all — a tray, a recess, a leather loop that is closer to a luggage tag than to a bail. The expensive nothing. Suburban copies put a brass boomerang on a credenza and hoped. Some of those boomerangs are charming. Some are a weapon at hip height.

If you replace mid-century pulls with cup pulls because a kitchen blog said so, you have written a different decade on the slab. Sometimes that is a kitchen and the slab needed help. Sometimes it is a credenza and you have insulted it. I cannot legislate your kitchen. I can say the sculptural pull was a specific kindness: one event, well made, no plate, no eagle, no vine. A waist for the fingers. Then the century decided even that was too much talk.
