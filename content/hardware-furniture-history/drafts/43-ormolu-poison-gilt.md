Some furniture hardware is sculpture that learned to sit on a corner.

Ormolu — gilt bronze, the French mounts on commodes and clocks and the kind of table that has never been near a kitchen — is not a cup pull. It is a goat, a mask, a frieze, a capital. It is attached with pins and screws and will. Mercury gilding (fire gilding) put gold on bronze by mixing it with mercury and driving the mercury off with heat. The gold stayed. The mercury went into the air and into the gilder. This is not a metaphor. This is a workshop body count that the pretty rooms do not mention.

I am not a specialist in French mounts. I have lifted a few and been surprised by the weight. Bronze, not pot metal. Chasing that is actually chasing. A back that is not a stamped sheet. When it is real, it is a different trade than Birmingham's bail. When it is not real, it is spelter and paint and a prayer.

The line between hardware and mount is a snob line and also a useful one. A hinge must work. A mount must convince. Sometimes a mount hides a screw. Sometimes a mount is the screw's excuse. On a commode the mounts are the face and the lock is almost shy. On an English chest the lock is the face and the mounts are absent. Two ideas of what metal is for.

Do not polish ormolu with the pink paste you use on a kitchen tap. You will take the gilt and leave the bronze looking like a wound. Museums have literature. Read some of it before you love a goat to death. If the gilt is already gone, you are looking at bronze, and bronze can be honest. Do not have it re-gilt in a hurry by someone who thinks gold paint is fire.

I put this draft in a hardware series because I want the poison in the record. Beauty has a shop. The shop had a fume. The drawer pulls in the rest of this series are mostly innocent of mercury. This one is not. If we are going to talk about metal and furniture, we can talk about the metal that killed the men who made it shine. The room is still shining. That is the uncomfortable part.
