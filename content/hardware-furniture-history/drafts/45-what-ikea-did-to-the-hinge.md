IKEA is a convenient villain. The villain is bigger than a store.

Particleboard, a cam lock, a dowel, a 35 mm cup, a door that is a slab of foil, a drawer that is a box of foil, optional legs in a bag. Hardware becomes a system for assembling a volume, not a face for a household. The cam lock is a lock that does not lock against a person. It locks a panel to a panel. Yale would not recognize the job. The hinge does not show. The pull may not exist. You push. Something in the cup or the runner sighs and the slab comes toward you.

This is a loss if you wanted a handshake. It is a loss if you wanted a key that meant *no*. It is a loss if you wanted a smith's drunk line. I have written forty-four drafts in that direction. I meant them.

It is not only a loss. A kitchen that a renter can take apart is a kitchen that does not belong to a landlord's taste forever. A hinge you can click off with a thumb (CLIP, 1985, and its cousins) is a labor kindness. Soft-close is a kindness to a small apartment and a sleeping shift worker. A cam lock is ugly and it is also a way to stay out of glue. Glue is a marriage. A cam is a visa.

Decoration left the hardware and went into the photograph of the room. The room is white. The hardware is inside. I cook in that room. I also keep a chest with a till lock in the other room, because I like a word that means *no*, and because a wooden knob is still the best handshake I know.

The series ends here because this is where most people's hands actually live. Not in 1762. Not in a Bramah window. In a cupboard that was in a flat-pack last spring. If you can see the cup, the cam, the absence of a pull, you can see the rest of the history as a set of refusals that led here. We refused the visible knuckle. We refused the eagle. We refused the key. We did not refuse motion. Motion is still the job. The metal still admits the joint will move.

Take a photograph of the carving if you want. Then open the door and look at the part that lets you. That part has a century. It has a town. It has a patent, or a page, or a heat. It has been looking at you the whole time. This was only a way to look back.
