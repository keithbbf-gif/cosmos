Do not call it oriental brass.

A tansu wears iron the way a face wears an expression. Lock plates as big as a hand. Corner mounts. Drawer pulls that are not Chippendale bails playing dress-up. The word for a pull is *hikite* — a hand-pull — and the metal is *hikite kanagu*. *Warabi-te* is the bracken-sprout shape, a classic. *Hiru-te* is the leech, if you want the glossary's zoology. Recessed cups have their own names. I am not going to pretend I grew up with these in my mouth. I learned them because calling every Asian chest "brass hardware" is how you miss the object.

Sendai tansu, late Edo into Meiji: zelkova, chestnut, cedar, a red-brown lacquer that shows the grain, iron fittings hammered and fixed with lacquer baking. Lock plates with clouds, dragons, peonies, lions. Three trades on one chest — joiner, lacquerer, metalworker. The metal is not an afterthought ordered from Birmingham. It is a third maker.

The iron can be the whole show. A merchant's chest with a safe compartment and a sword space is a piece of furniture that expects to be read as security and display at once. The plate is a lock and a crest. You can feel the hammer. You should. If the plates are thin stamped steel painted black from a reproduction house, the chest may still be old and the face may not be.

Chinese hardwood cabinets are a different grammar — round lock plates, hasp and pin, sometimes baitong — and I am giving them their own draft so I do not mash two countries into one aisle. Korean bandaji another. The insult is the mash.

Kaidan tansu — stair chests — add a different hardware problem: treads that are also lids, a run of boxes that have to take a foot and a hand. The fittings have to survive both. I have not built one. I have climbed one that was doing the job in a shop that should not have let me. The iron at the corners was the only reason I trusted the stair.

If you put English willow plates on a tansu because the iron felt "too much," you have performed a small colonialism with a screwdriver. The iron was the point. The wood is the body. The iron is the look that meets you. Handshake, but also a warning: this opens for a key, and the keyhole is in a cloud.
