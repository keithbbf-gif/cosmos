If you only remember 1964, you miss the labor.

The concealed cup got the door off the face of the hinge. The rest of the Blum timeline is about how many minutes a cabinetmaker spends marrying that door to a box, and how the door behaves when a kid slams it. MODUL, 1977, is a hinge system, not a poem — a way to standardize the boring and the plates. CLIP, 1985, is the one I will defend in a bar: the door clicks onto the plate without a screwdriver dance. You hang a run of doors. You take one off to set a shelf. You put it back with your thumb. That is not a gadget. That is hours, times a thousand kitchens, times forty years. Blum's own anniversary copy says the CLIP idea has been used billions of times. I cannot count billions. I can count the times I have been grateful I did not have to find the right screw in a dark carcase.

INSERTA later (they put 1993 on the timeline) expands the tool-free religion into the cup itself: expand into the 35 mm hole, no screws in the door if you do not want them. CLIP top, 1999, is adjustment without tears — or with fewer tears. BLUMOTION, 2001, is the soft close as a family of behaviors. CLIP top BLUMOTION, 2009, puts the damper in the hinge so the kitchen stops slamming even if the drawer already learned manners.

I am reciting a company timeline. I know how that sounds. I am doing it because the alternative is a fog called "European hinges evolved." They did not evolve. They were tooled, dated, sold at interzum, copied, and then they became the air. Häfele will sell you six brands. Grass will swear theirs is sweeter. Salice will be in the shop that does not want to pay Blum's premium. The air is still the 35 mm cup and a plate and a door that thinks it is a slab.

The horseshoe stud matters here, not as cute origin, but as a reminder that this is metal-forming. A farrier knows a nail that fails. A hinge company that starts in nails and studs knows volume. Volume is what remade the cabinet. Not a designer in Milan. A plant in Höchst and then North Carolina (they start assembling in the U.S. in 1984) and then everywhere a kitchen is a rectangle.

If you restore a 1920s pantry and put CLIP top plates on it, you have not committed a crime. You have changed the year the doors live in. The old butts can go in a tin and wait. I have a tin like that. It is more honest than throwing them out. The cabinet was remade. The old knuckles remember the first job.
