Not every piece of hardware is a handle.

A caster is how a heavy thing agrees to move without becoming luggage. Brass wheels, leather wheels, porcelain wheels, later rubber that marks a floor worse than the brass ever did. They wreck hardwood. They save backs. They snap off and then the chest sits on a wound. Period casters are a dating tell and a floor-ethic problem. I have put felt pads over good brass cups because I like the floor more than I like the look. That is a household decision. The caster is still there, unemployed.

A stay is how a lid or a fall agrees not to go too far. Quadrant stays on a secretaire. Chains on a cheap box. A hinged arm on a bar. Without a stay, a fall-front desk becomes a catapult and then a repair. I have seen the repair. It is always uglier than the stay would have been.

A catch is how a door agrees to stay shut without a lock. Bullet catches, roller catches, magnetic later, the elbow catch inside a pair of doors, the turn button on a cupboard that never earned a lock. A good catch is a small click. A bad catch is a door that opens in the night and teaches you about gravity.

Table forks, leaf slides, table clips — the drop-leaf world again. Hardware that only exists because rooms are small. Card-table catches. Piano lid props. The little iron that keeps a tall clock's hood from walking. Once you start seeing this family you cannot stop. The furniture is full of minor officials. They do not get photographed. They do the government.

Quadrant stays on a secretaire are pretty when they work and a gouge when they don't. The quarter-circle brass rides in a slot; dirt and a bent arm will plow the interior. I have planed out a gouge and still seen it in a raking light. Install the stay before you finish the interior if you are building new. If you are old, clean the track and stop forcing the fall past the stay's opinion.

I group them here because a series on hinges, pulls, and locks can pretend those three are the whole state. They are the ministers. The casters and stays and catches are the clerks. When a piece "doesn't work," it is often a clerk. The hinge is fine. The pull is fine. The lock throws. The quadrant is bent. Fix the clerk. The minister will look brilliant again.
