Read the 1861 patent. Linus Yale Jr., Philadelphia. "Locks for Drawers, Closets, Cupboards, &c." Not a bank vault. Not, first, your front door. A revolving tumbler that is also the bolt. A case whose face is the escutcheon. Screws and a spur into the lock rail of a drawer.

The cylinder lock that emptied bit-keys from the world's doors is a furniture idea that got promoted.

1865 is the one the museums put on a card: patent 48,475, the flat serrated key, the plug in a cylinder, pins to a shear line. That is the key in your pocket if you still have a house key that is not a chip. Between 1861 and 1865 there is a war and a refinement. Collectors talk about scarce 1861 examples, a demonstration lock, a missing link from Yale Sr.'s sliding ideas to the rotating plug. I have not held one. I have read the 1861 text. The text is cupboards.

This matters to me because furniture historians sometimes treat Yale as a door company that later made cam locks. The cam lock in a cheap cabinet is a grandchild, yes. The first patented sentence is the drawer. The front door stole a furniture problem and never gave it back. Every apartment cylinder is a till lock that grew up.

Yale Jr. had been a portrait painter. The obituaries like that. I like that the painter's lock is small. A painted likeness is a small precision. A pin that has to meet a shear line is a small precision. I will not force the metaphor past that.

On a cabinet today the descendant is often ugly: a cheap cylinder, a cam, a stamped escutcheon that wiggles. The idea is not ugly. The idea is that a small flat key can be unique enough, and replaceable enough, and cut by a machine. Bit keys were poetry. Flat keys are print. Furniture needed print. There were too many drawers.

Cam locks in office furniture are the unlovely heirs: a cylinder, a flat cam, a quarter turn, a cheap stamped rose. They lock a locker and a cocktail cabinet with the same shrug. I do not ask them to be Bramah. I ask them to remember they started as a drawer sentence.

If you find a nineteenth-century desk with a later Yale-style cylinder in a mortise that wanted a bit-key lock, you are looking at a promotion in reverse: the door lock coming home to the drawer that invented its problem. The wood will have extra holes. The extra holes are the family tree.
