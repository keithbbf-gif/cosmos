Charles Eastlake wrote a book about household taste. Factories wrote a style and put his name on the hardware.

The pulls I mean are stamped brass, geometric, incised lines, sometimes a square rosette, sometimes a linear plate that looks like it was drawn with a ruler that had opinions. After the Revival years of knobs and heavy wood, stamped brass comes back and it wants to look honest. Honesty, in a factory, is a texture. A line rolled into thin metal. A black fill in the groove. A catalog page that says Eastlake whether or not the man would have let that pull in his house.

I have a soft spot for them. They are not trying to be rococo. They are not trying to be an eagle. They are trying to be modern in a year that still had gaslight. The incising photographs well. It also fills with wax and dirt and then looks like it has been everywhere, which it has.

Colonial Revival will try to put willow plates back on these pieces. Don't, unless the holes say so. An Eastlake commode with Chippendale brasses is a costume party. The incised plate belongs to the incised wood — the shallow carving, the ebonized grooves, the machine that made "handwork" at a speed a chisel cannot.

Catalogs make this a national style. Grand Rapids, the mail-order houses, the ability to order a bedroom set and the knobs that already match it. The village smith is not invited. The smith's grandson may be running the press. That is the joke and the wound.

The wood and the metal like to rhyme. Shallow grooves on a walnut drawer front, ebonized, then a plate with the same geometry in brass. When they rhyme, the factory was paying attention. When a floral porcelain knob sits on that front, someone shopped later with a different room in mind. The rhyme is the date if the screws agree.

Honesty is a word I do not give to a stamp without a fight. The fight here is: the stamp is honest about being a stamp. It does not pretend to be chased by a man in a lane. It pretends to be taste. Taste is a catalog. Eastlake the writer might have winced. Eastlake the aisle did not wince. The aisle won.
