A key is the lock's public half.

The bit key: a bow you can feel in the dark, a stem, a bit that is a little skyline. The pipe key: Bramah's small rude tube, slots in the end, nothing like a storybook key. The barrel key. The flat key: Yale's print edition, serrated, light, easy to copy, easy to lose, easy to put on a ring with twelve others until your hip hurts.

Bows are class marks. A simple loop. A kidney. A crest. A stamped number. I have emptied an estate drawer of keys that no longer had doors. That is a kind of death certificate. The bows were still handsome. The bits were still specific. Nothing in the house would take them. We keep them anyway. Of course we do.

Key blanks change with power. Hand-forged, then drop-hammered, then cast once malleable iron will take it (the conservation literature puts that around 1816 for some trades), then milled by the million. The bow's shape follows the process. A hand-forged bow is a little drunk. A stamped bow is a logo.

I will not tell you how to impression a key. I will tell you that a furniture key is often smaller than people expect, and that the "skeleton key" in a junk bowl is usually a bland ward key that opens three dressing tables and none of the ones you care about. Skeleton means the bit is reduced to pass many wards. It is a compromise, not a master of the universe.

Weight is information. A Bramah pipe is a precise little object. A till key can be surprisingly slight. A wardrobe key has a longer reach. If someone cut a modern blank to fit an old lock, the bow will look wrong even when the bit works. The pocket knows. The old bows polish in the pocket. The new ones stay painted.

Bows polish in a pocket along the high spots. A key that has lived will have a bright equator and a darker valley. A cut-last-week blank is even. Even is not always new — a drawer key that lived on a hook can stay even — but even plus a sharp milling burr is new.

When a piece is sold "key included," believe the key only after it throws the bolt. I have bought the romance. The romance was a key for a different desk. It looked right in the photograph. The photograph does not turn.
