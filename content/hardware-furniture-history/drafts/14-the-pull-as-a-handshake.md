You do not meet a chest with your eyes first. You think you do. The photograph trained you. In a room you meet it with your hand.

The pull is a designed first contact. Someone decided where your fingers would go and what they would feel — a bail that swings, a knob that is a mushroom, a cup you hook, a leather tab, a routed undercut, nothing at all and a push to click. A good pull tells your hand the weight before the drawer moves. A bad pull is a bruise waiting for a hip. A missing pull is a speech. It says: someone yanked this off, or someone is waiting for the right one, or someone believes a slab should not be touched.

I care about this more than is reasonable. I have opened drawers by the underside of the front because the knob was gone and the wood was already dented from other people doing the same. That dent is a record of a failed handshake. I have also opened drawers with a Chippendale willow plate that was put on last year, bright as a coin, screws too long, punching through the inside face so you feel them when you drop a shirt in. That is a handshake with a pin in it.

Style books treat pulls as period markers. They are, when they are original. William and Mary drops, Queen Anne bails, Federal ovals, Empire knobs, Eastlake stamps, Mission hammers, Deco steps. Fine. Learn the shapes. Then look at the wood around them. Extra holes. A shadow. A filled mortise for a post. The handshake changed and the palm print stayed.

A pull also sets a class. Brass that was chased and gilt is not the same sentence as a wooden knob, and neither is the same as a pot-metal Deco handle that crumbled in a moving van. You can spend more on the handles than on the carcase. People have. You can also refuse the whole game, as the Shakers mostly did, and still have a drawer that opens like a kindness.

I do not want a buying guide. I want you to notice that furniture is a social object and the pull is the social part. Hinges you can ignore until they sag. Locks you can ignore until you cannot. The pull is every time. It is the only hardware that has to be polite.
