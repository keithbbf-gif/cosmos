I am staying careful here. Dealer blogs are loud. Classical hardwood scholarship is deeper than this draft. What I will not do is invent a dynasty for a lock I met in a shop.

The cabinets I mean — hardwood, often later Qing or later still, sometimes older bones with newer metal — often lock with a round plate, a hasp, a pin. Not a mortise till. A face lock. The plate is the event. Brass, or baitong (paktong), that white copper-nickel look that Westerners keep calling silver. Cloud-shaped mounts at corners and hinges. A different theory of closed: the doors meet, the hasp drops, the pin or padlock takes the hasp. You can see the security. You are supposed to.

This is closer to a chest hasp than to a Bramah. It is not primitive. It is public. A Western desk hides the bolt in the wood. A lot of Chinese cabinets wear the bolt on the chest like a clasp on a coat. The lock is part of the composition. Take it off to "modernize" and the doors look like they lost a nose.

Reproductions are a forest. New plates on old wood, old plates on new wood, whole cabinets made last year in a finish that photographs as 1800. I do not have X-ray eyes. I have screw types, tool marks, the back of a plate, the way a hinge seat sits in a rail. I also have humility. If a specialist in huanghuali wants to correct this draft, good. Stage 4 is for that.

Do not swap these plates for cup pulls. I have seen it. A kitchen-minded owner, a pair of doors, "we'll just put nice hardware on." The nice hardware was from an aisle that thinks all cabinets are kitchens. The round plate was the hardware. It was already nice. It was the face.

Baitong (paktong) is a copper-nickel alloy with a quiet white face. Western catalogs call it silver and then are surprised when it does not tarnish like silver. It should not. If a plate is bright as chrome and thin as foil, it is probably not the alloy I mean.

Two countries, two grammars. Tansu iron is a third. If your language is only "Asian antique brass," you are shopping, not looking. Look at the hasp. Look at whether the pin is a pin. Look at the cloud. The cloud is not a Chippendale piercing. It is a different sky.
