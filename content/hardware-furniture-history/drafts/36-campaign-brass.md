Campaign furniture is a packing problem with mahogany on it.

The British (and the trades that sold to them) made chests that could go on a ship, stack, crate, sit in heat that ruined hide glue, and still be a chest of drawers when the tent was up. The hardware is flush. Pulls let into the face so nothing catches. Corner guards. L-brackets that are a second joinery. Straps. A lot of brass. Christopher Schwarz and the people who have crawled inside these chests keep saying the same things: earlier pulls are skeletonized, later ones go rectangular; the brass is not only fashion; if the glue lets go in the tropics the screws in the guards still hold a corner.

I believe the packing story. I also believe the fashion story. Flush brass looks like money. It looks like military competence. A civilian sideboard can wear the look and never see a gangway. After a while the look is the product. The Boer War is a rough end of the high-style window; then armies make their own ugly traveling boxes and the gentlemen's makers fade. The flush pull survives in yacht furniture and in expensive reproductions and in every catalog that wants to say *campaign* without saying *empire*.

Indian-made pieces exist in the same weather. Thin brass, nailed not screwed, not always let in flush. Schwarz, via Sean Clarke and others, notes the difference. I have not done a catalog census. I have seen thin plates that were trying. Trying is not the same as a London flush job, and a London flush job is not innocence. The furniture travels because someone else is being governed.

As a maker, the flush pull is extra labor. You chop a pocket. You file a plate. You sink screws that must not proud. Twenty pockets on a chest. The chest gets heavier in hours, not in wood. That labor is why the style feels rich even when the mahogany is honest.

If you put proud cup pulls on a campaign chest because you hate how flush pulls pinch, I understand the hand. I do not understand the eye. The flush is the sentence. The sentence is: I will not catch on the crate. Change it and the chest stays home, which may be fine. Call it home. Do not call it campaign.
