The hammer mark is a moral.

Arts and Crafts, Mission, the Stickley weather — iron and copper that want you to see the work. A bail on a square plate. A rivet that is a rivet. A surface that is all little moons from the peen. The sentence is: a person stood here. Sometimes a person did. Sometimes a trip-hammer stood here and the person watched. Factory sincerity is a real product. It sold then. It sells now.

I have held copper pulls where the hammering is a language and copper pulls where the hammering is a font. The language wanders. The moons overlap, some deep, some barely there, the plate slightly dished. The font is even, marching, a texture map. Both can be beautiful. Only one is a day's work.

Oak likes this hardware. Dark oak, quartersawn flake, a copper that has gone brown-black. Brass would be too yellow, too Georgian, too much like the century they were walking away from. Iron is sterner. Copper is the one that still feels like a palm.

Reproductions are endless. The tell is often the back and the screws, again, always the screws. Also the weight. Old copper is sometimes thicker than the stamped sheet that learned the look. Sometimes it is not; I will not make a religion of thickness. I will make a religion of looking at more than one part.

If you polish hammered copper to bright penny, you have missed the point so hard I do not know how to help you. The dark is the years. The years are the moral they paid for. A bright penny is a different movement. Call it that and I will calm down.

Roycroft, Limbert, the unnamed shop that sold "Mission" to a furniture store in Ohio: the hardware dialect spreads faster than the joinery. A true Stickley piece can have quiet iron. A catalog Mission piece can have louder copper. Loud is not authentic. Quiet is not authentic. The screws and the oak and the finish have to vote.

I am not a Stickley purist. I have put simple steel bars on oak and been happy. The hammered story is just the loudest version of a wish that never goes away: let the joint show, let the tool show, let the metal not pretend it was born pretty. The wish is older than the movement. The movement gave it a catalog.
