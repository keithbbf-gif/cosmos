Patina is a record. Polish is an edit.

I have done the edit. A rag, a pink paste, a Federal oval that went from brown to coin in four minutes. The room clapped. The plate lost the years that told you it had been in a hand. I do not do that anymore unless the owner has been told, in words, that they are deleting evidence, and they still want the coin. Their house. Their deletion. I will not pretend it is care.

Brass that has gone almost black in the chasing grooves is doing a job. The grooves were cut to hold shadow. You take the shadow out and the rococo goes flat. Copper that has gone brown is the Arts and Crafts they paid for. Iron that has a stable oxide is not a crisis. Iron that is actively flaking into a chest lid is a crisis. Learn the difference before you meet a wheel.

Match the screw, not the Pinterest plate. A correct dull steel screw in an old hinge is better than a bright brass screw that looks "nice." Length matters. Diameter matters. Drive matters. If you strip a head, stop. A ruined slot is a new date.

Reversible work. Wax fills you can get out. Screws you can unscrew. A plate you did not epoxy. I have seen epoxy used as a personality. It is not. It is a decision that the next person cannot undo without a fight.

Love that looks like theft: dipping hardware in a tank, glass-beading a plate, sanding a hammered surface so it photographs "clean," replacing all the pulls because two were missing. Missing two is a chance to make two. Replacing twelve is a chance to lie. If you cannot make two, leave ten old and two honest new. The drawer will survive the mismatch. It may not survive the lie.

I am not a museum. I live with furniture. I oil a lock. I wax a dent. I do not strip a chest to the nude because a video said wood should be thirsty. Thirsty is a sales word. Records are quieter. Leave the quiet on the metal unless the metal is failing. Failing is rust that eats, zinc that sugars, a hinge pin that has walked. Those you fix. Shine is not a fix.
