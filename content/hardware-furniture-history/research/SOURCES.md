# Sources

Stage 1. Read, not waved at. Drafts cite these by short name.

## Hinges and fittings

- Blum company history (Höchst / Höchst timeline): founding 1 March 1952 (horseshoe stud); ANUBA hinge 1958; first concealed hinge into production 1964; TANDEM + MODUL 1977; CLIP tool-free hinge 1985; CLIP top 1999; BLUMOTION 2001; CLIP top BLUMOTION 2009. https://www.blum.com/al/en/company/company-history/
- Blum CLIP hinge 40-year note (launched 1985). Blum Group, 2025.
- European cup-hinge practice: 35 mm cup, typical ~13 mm depth, overlay tables. Trade PDFs (WW Hardware; CabinetParts Blum brochure).
- SOSS Door Hardware, company history: Joseph (Joe) Soss, idea after a trip home from the Philippines; hinge company later Warren, Mich., then Pioneer, Ohio (Universal Industrial Products). https://www.soss.com/history/
- Soss U.S. patents for invisible/concealed hinges, e.g. US 1,682,860 (filed 1925, issued 1928); later 1,687,271 (1928), 1,797,802 (1931), 2,021,702 (1935). Inventor name on the patents: Joseph Soss — not Ralph.
- Fine Woodworking / Popular Woodworking on the rule joint: specialty drop-leaf hinge, unequal leaves, knuckle mortised under; named for the folding carpenter's rule.

## Pulls and brass

- Pegs and 'Tails, "Furniture Brasses" (2012): Chippendale *Director* 1762 plate notes on brass from wooden moulds; Harewood invoice 26 Aug 1774 for carving patterns, casting, chasing; Chippendale likely bought much work from cabinet founders (e.g. William Bent, St. Martin's Lane).
- University of Houston, *The Engines of Our Ingenuity* ep. 2824, "A Brass Catalogue": anonymous c.1780 Birmingham brass catalogues; 33 brass founders listed in Birmingham in 1770; bail handles and backplates as the stars.
- Jessica Frankis, PhD thesis, University of Birmingham, 2021, *The Eighteenth-Century Birmingham Brass Trade*.
- Joan Parcher, AIC Wooden Artifacts Group, 2011: study collection ~10,000 pieces; Federal plates vs reproductions (rolling wrinkles; thick square iron nuts on earlier work); marked Birmingham makers Thomas Hands and William Jenkins. Builds on Don Fennimore.
- Antique Trader, "Brass Class": William and Mary cast drops on thin chased plates; Queen Anne / Chippendale post-and-bail; Federal stamped Hepplewhite plates (often still Birmingham), eagles; later Empire/Rococo Revival shift toward knobs; Eastlake/Aesthetic stamped brass returns.

## Locks

- J. A. Berry, "English Furniture Locks," Regional Furniture Society: pre-1778 furniture locks largely warded (rim, flush, or mortise); Bramah 1784 furniture lock with round protruding brass face and small pipe key; sliders in a barrel; key cut first, lock fitted to key (reverse of Barron practice).
- Robert Barron, 1778 English patent: double-acting tumbler / "father of the English lever system" (Building Conservation, "The Ancient Art of the Locksmith").
- Joseph Bramah, 1784 lock patent; challenge lock in the Piccadilly shop from 1790; A. C. Hobbs at the Great Exhibition 1851, ~51 hours over 16 days. Challenge lock now Science Museum, London; rebuilt since (sources note slide/spring counts changed).
- Jeremiah Chubb, detector lock 1818, after 1817 Portsmouth Dockyard burglary / government prize. Six-lever later; Hobbs also picks Chubb at 1851 exhibition.
- Linus Yale Jr., U.S. 31,278 (29 Jan 1861): improvement in locks for drawers, closets, cupboards, etc.; revolving tumbler-as-bolt. U.S. 48,475 (27 June 1865): familiar flat serrated key and cylinder pin-tumbler.

## Dating, screws, joinery

- *Journal of Antiques*, "System for Dating Country and Primitive Furniture, Part One": handmade vs machine screws; first machine-made screw dated there to 1846; machine-cut slot 1856; circular saw / planer caveats.
- Georgetowner "Antiques Addict": partially machine-made screws c.1812–mid-century; gimlet pointed screw often given as 1848; slots still hand-cut for a stretch after machine bodies.
- Fine Art Restoration, "Historic handles": replacements common; early posts and nuts vs modern hex; cotter-pin mounts on early drops.
- Appraisily / trade consensus: one Phillips screw dates a *repair*, not a piece. Hand-cut vs machine dovetails as corroboration.

## Regional and special forms

- JAANUS, *hikite*: metal door/drawer pulls; also recessed sliding-door pulls.
- The Carpentry Way (Nov 2012): *hikite kanagu* glossary — *warabi-te*, *hiru-te*, *ume-komi-shiki* / *hako-hikite*.
- Sendai Tansu Cooperative, history: late Edo origin; hammered iron fittings, lacquer-baked; lock plates with clouds, dragons, peonies; *warabite* handles.
- Christopher Schwarz / Lost Art Press, *Campaign Furniture*: flush brass, corner guards, skeletonized early pulls, later rectangular plates; packing and tropical glue failure as reasons for the brass; roughly 1790s–Boer War as the high-style campaign window.
- Popular Woodworking, campaign chests: flush hardware so cases stack and crate; lots of L-brackets and corner guards.
