A couch is a wooden argument wrapped in someone else’s crop. In 1904 Ballman founded or co-founded Fort Smith Couch and Bedding Company. The Historical Society puts it two years after folding beds and two years before Border Queen cabinets. The sequence is a town learning that “furniture” is not one trade.

Higgins told the *Times Record* that furniture pulled spinoffs: upholsterers, cloth, mirrors, steel fittings. He was describing the industrial city. The 1904 letterhead is the spinoff with a name. A couch house has to buy textiles, springs, tacks, and labor that is not a lathe crew. Bedding adds ticking and the kind of cleanliness argument that a dresser never had to make.

## Frames first

North Arkansas Wood Products, decades later and a different county, would saw frames for a “Perfect Chair Kit” and sell them to a marketing company. That is the frame business in the open. Fort Smith Couch and Bedding, if it was a full manufacturer, had to be frame *and* cover, or it was a cover house buying frames. I do not have the 1904 bill of materials. I will not decide which. I will say that every upholstered object hides a hardwood decision: gum or oak, dowels or screws, a spring bar that will rack if the corner is cheap.

The 1912 bulletin’s furniture table is mostly case-goods thinking — gum and oak by the million feet. Upholstery eats those feet as hidden rails. It also eats wire and cloth that never appear in a Forest Service wood table. A town that adds a couch company is adding imports. Cotton and wool do not grow on Belle Point in factory quantity. The railroad that shipped dressers out now shipped batting in.

## Women, and the finish-room cousin

Upholstery rooms in American factories were one of the places women were allowed on the floor earlier than in some millwright crews. I do not have a 1904 Fort Smith payroll that proves that here. Turney Wood Products in Harrison would note, in 1966, four women in the finish department as news. Do not back-date Harrison onto 1904 Fort Smith. Do say that a bedding house changes who walks in the door. That change is a later essay’s job to document, not this one’s job to invent.

## Fire, moth, and the other inventories

A wood shop burns. A bedding shop burns faster and smells like a different disaster. Insurance, sprinklers, the way you store butts of cotton — these are factory facts that case-goods men can ignore until they add a couch line. I have no 1904 fire story for this company. I will not write a fire for color. I will write that the product class forces a different kind of caution, and that a historian who finds a fire should put it in `verify` and then in the body.

Moths are a quieter inventory. Bedding that sits in a warehouse through an Arkansas summer is a biology problem. Case goods are a moisture problem. The 1904 company had to staff both.

## Riverside is not this plant

Riverside Furniture, after 1946, became the mid-century name people attach to Fort Smith upholstery. In 1996 it lost an 80,000-square-foot upholstery plant and fifty jobs to the tornado. That plant is not Fort Smith Couch and Bedding. If you collapse them you get a century-long sofa that never existed. The 1904 house is an early admission. Riverside is a later scale. The craft — frame, spring, cover — is the rhyme.

FFO Home making mattresses in a piece of the old Whirlpool building is a still later rhyme. Fort Smith keeps returning to sleep as a manufactured object. The buildings change. The need does not.

## Why the date matters for the kitchen

Border Queen in 1906 is a hard kitchen object: wood, tin maybe, a Hoosier-style logic. Couch and Bedding in 1904 is a soft parlor object. Ballman was selling the whole house, not a style. A dealer who could get a cabinet and a couch from the same town was a dealer who could stop in Fort Smith and be done. The 1921 cooperative only makes sense if 1904 and 1906 already exist.

A Warren shop that builds islands and cutting boards is on the 1906 side of that split. Soft goods are not its trade. The useful inheritance is the refusal to pretend one object is the town. Fort Smith’s furniture century was a catalog. This pack’s job is to keep the catalog from collapsing into “oak dressers.”

The photograph I want is a bare frame, not a staged living room. If the library has webbing on a bench, even from a present-tense repair, pull it and say what year it is. A 1904 couch in a stock photo is someone else’s parlor.
