A folding bed is a room admitting it is also a hallway. In 1902 Edward Ballman founded or co-founded Fort Smith Folding Bed and Table Company. The Historical Society lists it first among the specialized houses he started after Ballman-Cummings. I take the date and the product class as earned. I do not take a surviving catalog I have not held.

Folding furniture is easy to joke about. The jokes miss the factory reason. A fold is how you ship. A bed that stands in the day is a bed that can live in a boarding house, a small parlor, a western house that is still adding rooms. A table that collapses is a table that can ride in a crate without paying for air. Fort Smith in 1902 was a shipping town with a western customer. The product is the geography.

## Not the later folding town

Hold the cousins apart or the SEO will smear them. Fort Smith Plywood Company filed a trademark in 1966 for “FORT SMITH FOLDING TABLES CHAIRS.” That mark later associated with Samsonite Furniture in Tennessee. McCourt Manufacturing, on North 3rd, is a 1983 folding-chair house that still exists in public listings. Neither is Ballman’s 1902 company. The city kept making objects that fold because the freight argument never died. The letterheads changed.

If you write “Fort Smith has always made folding furniture,” you are not wrong as a habit and you are wrong as a corporation. This essay is the 1902 habit at its first named company.

## What a folding-bed factory has to know

Hardware. The 1917 Metal Products company is the later confession that wood is not the whole object. In 1902 the fittings were already the failure point: hinges that sag, catches that do not catch, a spring that eats a child’s finger. I have no 1902 hardware patent with Ballman’s name on it in front of me. I will not invent one. I will say that a folding-bed house that cannot control iron will become a lawsuit house.

Wood movement is meaner on a fold. A panel that swells will not go back into a cabinet. A table leaf that cups will not sit flat after the trip. Gum, which the 1912 table says Arkansas furniture lived on, moves. White oak moves less in some cuts and still moves. A factory that ships folds into Texas summer is a factory that has to finish and fit for a climate, not for a showroom morning.

Labor is assembly more than carving. Ornate wartime furniture was one answer to a dead wagon market. Folding is another: less ornament, more mechanism, more units. The “king” story likes turned posts. The 1902 company likes a crew that can hang a hundred hinges without teaching each hinge a new song.

## Why Ballman split the line

Ballman-Cummings could have added folding beds as a SKU. He started a company instead. Specialization is how you hire a manager who only worries about one failure. It is how you tell a dealer this town makes a *kind*. It is how you keep a permanent river plant from depending on one fashion in sideboards.

The 1921 Manufacturers Company would later try to sell the kinds together. That only works if the kinds first exist as separate, competent houses. 1902 is the first split in the Society’s list. Couch and bedding in 1904, kitchen cabinets in 1906 — a four-year burst of product architecture. Read it as industrial, not as restlessness.

## Rooms that were still being invented

American houses in 1902 were not the ranch houses of the 1950s. They were still hall rooms, rented rooms, parlor bedrooms, the Murphy-bed decades beginning to teach cities that a wall could be a bed. I will not claim Fort Smith invented the Murphy. I will claim a western factory town had customers whose rooms were small and whose freight bills were large. Folding is the intersection.

A Ken Burns still, if you want one: a bed half-folded, the mattress stripe, a window with river light, no one in the frame. The object should look slightly ashamed, because folding furniture always does. Shame is not the factory’s problem. Cubic feet are.

## What a later shop can take

A kitchen island does not fold. A cutting board stack does. Freight is still the quiet partner in a Warren shop that ships LTL or parcel. Ballman’s 1902 lesson is not “make a folding island.” It is: name the constraint (room, car, climate) and make a company that answers only that constraint.

Do not put 1902 folding beds in a heritage page as if Bradley Brand built them. Put them in a history page as a Fort Smith answer to a small room and a long rail. The hinge in the photograph, if we have one, should be a hinge we own or a hinge we have rights to. A stock Murphy-bed photo is a lie in this pack.
