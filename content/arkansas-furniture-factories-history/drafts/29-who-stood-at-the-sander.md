Edward Ballman has a school. Herman Udouj has an obituary that can fill a column. The sander has a belt. I do not have the sander’s name. That is not a poetic emptiness I am proud of. It is a failure of the sources this pack used, and it should sit in the middle of the pack like a missing tooth.

A furniture factory is a set of stations. Rough mill, glue, machine room, sanding, stain, rub, trim, pack. Founders pass through on tours. The stations keep the same shoulders. If Fort Smith was a hub for eighty years, that is eighty years of shoulders. Some of them are in city directories under “laborer” and “finisher.” Most of them are not in the *Journal*.

## What I will not invent

I will not give you a composite worker named Earl. I will not give you dialect. I will not give you a Black sanding crew or a white sanding crew as a fact, even though Fort Smith was a Jim Crow city with Howard Elementary and Lincoln High and a 1912 lynching the Encyclopedia does not skip. Race structured the jobs. I do not have the furniture plants’ job books. Assigning a race to a station without a book is a different kind of lie than the booster lie.

I will not give you a wage in 1910 dollars I pulled from a national average and dressed as local. I will not give you a finger lost in a jointer. Those things happened in this industry. They happened here if they happened here. A later oral-history project can name them. This essay can only refuse the composite.

## What the public record still gives

Turney Wood Products in Harrison, 1966: four women in the finish department, noted in a company newsletter as news. Carpenters Local 2746 on the contract. A 1968 bankruptcy that tried to shed the contract. That is a documented floor, in another Arkansas furniture town, and it belongs in the next two essays. It is also proof that floors *can* be documented when someone keeps a newsletter.

Riverside, 1996: fifty people in an upholstery plant that a tornado took. The Fed named the count, not the fifty. Still a count.

Pratt: no workers. Capital arguing with capital.

Higgins: little shops and garages, a train of smaller industries. Labor as a city, not a hero.

Census: Fort Smith’s population curve. A city that triples in the 1880s is a city that imported labor. Some of it came from Indiana with Ott. Some of it came from the county. Some of it came from the Territory. The sander is in that import whether we know his name or not.

## Dust as a working condition

Sanding is the furniture job that does not photograph as skill and is skill. Grit, pressure, knowing when the stain will blotch because you burned the gum. Dust that is gum dust and oak dust and whatever the finish room vents did not catch. Mid-century plants got better at dust. Nineteenth-century plants got men who coughed. I will not write a medical novel. I will write that a hub’s air was a work product.

A present-tense shop with a collector is living after that sentence. It should not congratulate itself in this paragraph. It should keep the collector on.

## Why founders eat the page

Founders leave paper that archives want: charters, lawsuits, obituaries, school names. Workers leave time books that companies throw away and interviews that no one funds. A Ken Burns film, if it is lazy, will use a still of a lathe and a voice-over about industry. A less lazy one will find a paycheck. This pack, at writer pass, has not found the paycheck. The honest move is to say so and to point at the archives that might still have it: Fort Smith Museum of History, UAFS special collections, union remnants, church directories, the collector in a family attic who has a Riverside badge.

## A shop in another county

A 65-foot shop in Warren with a handful of people is not a factory floor and should not steal this essay’s moral as “we are the workers now.” It is a different scale with names that can still be said in a room. Say them on a heritage page if they want to be said. Do not paste them onto 1910 Fort Smith.

Still frame: a belt, dust, no face. If a later editor gets a rights-cleared photograph of a named Fort Smith finisher, replace the hole. Until then, the hole is the most accurate picture this pack can print. Ballman can keep the school. The belt kept the town fed. I would like to know who held it. I do not. That sentence is the essay.
