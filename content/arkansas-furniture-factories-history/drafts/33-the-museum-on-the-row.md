Chuck Girard, speaking as executive director of the Fort Smith Museum of History, gave the *Times Record* the riverfront list this pack has been living on. About two dozen manufacturers. Ayers, Garrison, DeSoto, Ward, Covey, Fort Smith Chair. A tornado that hit furniture row. A door that cheaper Chinese furniture then walked through. That is a museum voice in a newspaper. It is also, for several essays here, the whole map.

<!-- PHOTO: fig-01 D:\BBF\BBF Photos — museum case or a labeled object, only if owner-held (filename pending shop pull) -->

The *Encyclopedia of Arkansas* says the Fort Smith Historical Society formed in 1977 and still operates out of the Museum of History. Volume 38 of the Society’s *Journal* is the Ballman door. Benjamin Boulden writes the city’s Encyclopedia entry and is credited to both Society and Museum. This is not a coincidence. In a city whose national story is a fort and a judge, the factory story survives because a small institution decided it should.

## What a museum can hold

A stencil. A photograph of Garrison after the fire, if they have rights to show it. A suite that a family donated. A oral-history tape if someone funded the tape. A school group that hears “we used to make furniture” on a Saturday.

What it cannot hold: the 1912 table, unless someone prints it. The Pratt opinions, unless someone curates them. The fifty names from the upholstery plant. The garages Higgins mentioned. A museum is a building with a budget. This pack should be grateful and should not pretend the building is the industry.

The *Journal* is the cheaper, thicker holding. Myers on Ballman. Other issues I have not read and should not fake. An editor who sits down with the run will find more names than Girard said in one interview. That sitting-down is the next honest step after this writer pass. Staged writing means we used what was in reach and marked what was not.

## 1996 as the museum’s weather

The Democrat-Gazette’s 30-year piece credits Museum photographs of the Eads fire and the ruined downtown. Disaster is how industrial buildings re-enter the civic camera. People who would not visit a factory will look at a factory that has been opened by wind. That is human and a little ugly. Use the photographs only with rights. Use the fact of the photographs anyway: the Museum became a furniture-row archive in part because the row was wrecked on film.

Girard’s import sentence sits in that weather. A director looking at empty lots will reach for a national cause. He may be right. He is still a witness, not a trade statistician. This pack has tried to keep him as a witness.

## The fort will always win the parking lot

Belle Point, the National Historic Site, the marshals museum, Miss Laura’s — those are wayfinding. The furniture museum, if it exists, is a room inside a local museum. Do not resent the parking lot. Resent a visitor’s guide that cannot spare a sentence for the row. A heritage page in another county should link the row without pretending to be the Museum.

Bradley Brand Furniture is not a satellite campus of the Fort Smith Museum of History. If a later exhibit wants a present-tense South Arkansas shop as “what remains,” that is a curator’s decision and a rights decision. It is not this writer’s to offer as a loan.

## How to use the institution without emptying it

Cite the *Journal*. Cite Girard as Girard. Do not scrape the archive PDF for a full reprint of Myers. Do not download the Eads fire photo into `D:\BBF` and call it ours. Ask. Pay if they ask. Caption with their credit. The Society has been doing the work since 1977. A cloud-agent pack in 2026 is a late, loud cousin. Behave like a cousin, not like a looter.

Still frame: a case with a labeled drawer-back, or a bound *Journal* on a table. If we cannot photograph inside the Museum, photograph a bulletin we have rights to — Harris and Maxwell is public domain — and admit the Museum is the other door. Two doors. One town. The row, or what is left of the idea of the row, lives between them.

I want a later editor to walk in and come back with three names Girard did not say. That would be a successful use of this essay. A successful use is not a virtual tour. It is a visit.
