Chuck Girard, speaking as executive director of the Fort Smith Museum of History, gave the *Times Record* the riverfront list this factory history has been living on. About two dozen manufacturers. Ayers, Garrison, DeSoto, Ward, Covey, Fort Smith Chair. A tornado that hit furniture row. A door that cheaper Chinese furniture then walked through. That is a museum voice in a newspaper. It is also, for several essays, the whole map.

The *Encyclopedia of Arkansas* says the Fort Smith Historical Society formed in 1977 and still operates out of the Museum of History. Volume 38 of the Society’s *Journal* is the Ballman door. Benjamin Boulden writes the city’s Encyclopedia entry and is credited to both Society and Museum. This is not a coincidence. In a city whose national story is a fort and a judge, the factory story survives because a small institution decided it should.

## What a museum can hold

A stencil. A photograph of Garrison after the fire, if they have rights to show it. A suite that a family donated. An oral-history tape if someone funded the tape. A school group that hears “we used to make furniture” on a Saturday.

What it cannot hold: the 1912 table, unless someone prints it. The Pratt opinions, unless someone curates them. The fifty names from the upholstery plant. The garages Higgins mentioned. A museum is a building with a budget. Be grateful. Do not pretend the building is the industry.

Girard’s two dozen is a spoken count, not a directory page. Higgins told the same paper the town was once considered one of three furniture hubs in the United States, and that furniture peaked in the late 1950s and early 1960s. The *Journal*, volume 38, called furniture the city’s largest industry and job provider in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Those three sentences — spoken list, hub boast, journal ranking — are the civic file. They agree that the industry was large. They do not replace a plant-by-plant time book.

The *Journal* is the cheaper, thicker holding. Myers on Ballman. Other issues I have not read and should not fake. An editor who sits down with the run will find more names than Girard said in one interview. That sitting-down is the next honest step. Use what is in reach. Mark what is not.

The Society’s birth year matters. 1977 is after the peak window Higgins named, after Norge and Whirlpool had already taught the town a second object, after Pratt had already written the partnership fight into the *Arkansas Reports*. The Society was founded to keep a city’s memory while the city’s factories were already changing objects. Furniture, in that timing, is a thing you archive because you can see it thinning.

The Encyclopedia’s attractions list puts the Regional Art Museum in the Vaughn-Schaap House inside Belle Grove, the nature center on old Chaffee land, the national cemetery as the oldest of the state’s three. Those are other rooms. The furniture story, if it has a room, shares a building with the Society. Sharing a building is how a factory town keeps a story that does not have a federal site.

## 1996 as the museum’s weather

The *Arkansas Democrat-Gazette*’s thirty-year piece credits Museum photographs of the Eads fire and the ruined downtown. Disaster is how industrial buildings re-enter the civic camera. People who would not visit a factory will look at a factory that has been opened by wind. That is human and a little ugly. Use the photographs only with rights. Use the fact of the photographs anyway: the Museum became a furniture-row archive in part because the row was wrecked on film.

The National Weather Service’s 1996 assessment puts the touchdown at 11:12 p.m. on the west side of downtown, historic buildings first, then an industrial strip, then the north-side houses, then the river. Garrison is in that path. So is the kind of brick a museum already knew how to label. Girard’s import sentence sits in that weather. A director looking at empty lots will reach for a national cause. He may be right. He is still a witness, not a trade statistician. Keep him as a witness.

## The fort will always win the parking lot

Belle Point, the National Historic Site, the marshals museum that opened in 2023, Miss Laura’s — those are wayfinding. Miss Laura’s is the remaining building from the regulated district the Encyclopedia calls “the Row,” a different row, 1907 to 1924, with federal paint now because it is the visitor bureau. The furniture museum, if it exists, is a room inside a local museum. Do not resent the parking lot. Resent a visitor’s guide that cannot spare a sentence for the riverfront manufacturers.

A heritage page in another county should link the row without pretending to be the Museum. Bradley Brand Furniture is not a satellite campus of the Fort Smith Museum of History. If a later exhibit wants a present-tense South Arkansas shop as “what remains,” that is a curator’s decision and a rights decision. It is not this writer’s to offer as a loan.

Boulden’s additional-information list is the adult stack: Bearss and Gibson on the fort, Faulk and Jones as an illustrated city history, Higgins as the later teaching book, Carson on Sunbelt culture, Goodspeed as the old county reprint, the *Journal* run since 1977. A visitor who only sees the gallows has not read that stack. A writer who only cites Girard’s interview has not either. The Museum is the door. The stack is the room.

The Society’s *Journal* is a serial, not a pamphlet. Volume 38, April 2014, is the Ballman issue this history used. Other issues exist. I have not read them and will not invent their tables of contents. An editor who sits with the UAFS PDF run — the April 2014 complete issue is the public door — will find more furniture names than one interview can carry. That is the next job: walk in, come back with three names Girard did not say.

1898 is the Museum’s older weather. A tornado that killed more than fifty people already tore the city once. 1996 is the storm that hit a furniture memory still standing as brick and then, four days later, as fire. Two storms. One institution holding the later photographs. Do not merge the years. Do use the fact that disaster is how the civic camera returns to industrial brick.

## How to use the institution without emptying it

Cite the *Journal*. Cite Girard as Girard. Do not scrape the archive PDF for a full reprint of Myers. Do not download the Eads fire photo into `D:\BBF` and call it ours. Ask. Pay if they ask. Caption with their credit. The Society has been doing the work since 1977. A later essay series is a late, loud cousin. Behave like a cousin, not like a looter.

UAFS can hold a class. The chamber can hold Manufacturing Week. Those are civic stills next to the Museum’s stills. They are not sanders. Do not confuse a week of posters with a labeled drawer-back.

Still frame: a case with a labeled drawer-back, or a bound *Journal* on a table. If we cannot photograph inside the Museum, photograph a bulletin we have rights to — Harris and Maxwell is public domain — and admit the Museum is the other door. Two doors. One town. The row, or what is left of the idea of the row, lives between them.

I want a later editor to walk in and come back with three names Girard did not say. That would be a successful use of this essay. A successful use is not a virtual tour. It is a visit.
