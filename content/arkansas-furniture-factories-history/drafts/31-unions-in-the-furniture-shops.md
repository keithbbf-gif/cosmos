On June 28, 1968, the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Arkansas told Carpenters Local 2746 that Turney Wood Products, bankrupt, did not have to honor the labor agreement it had signed before the filing. The shop in Harrison had been a union shop in its own newsletter’s telling — a three-year contract covering wages, hours, and conditions. The receiver had reopened the plant, cut pay, laid people off. The union sued. The contract lost.

That is the cleanest furniture-union story this pack has, and it is not a Fort Smith story. I am putting it first anyway, because factory labor history that only follows the riverfront will pretend Arkansas furniture had no unions. It had at least this one, and it had a court date.

## What a carpenters’ local is doing in a furniture plant

The Brotherhood of Carpenters and Joiners did not only hang doors. Industrial locals in wood products are an old American pattern: the union follows the material. A laminated-pew factory in Harrison is wood products with a church customer. Local 2746’s paper, if we had the whole of it, would read like a mill contract more than a residential-carpenter contract. Wages, hours, conditions — the Encyclopedia’s trio — are the trio every such paper carries.

The 1967 sale of controlling interest to Tulsa businessmen, the March 1968 shutdown, the March 22 filing, the May reopen, the June decision, the June auction: that is a six-month lesson in how fast a contract becomes a souvenir. Claude Turney’s civic prizes the year before do not appear in the opinion. They do not have to. The court was not asked about prizes.

## Fort Smith’s quieter file

I have not found, in the sources this writer pass used, a published bargaining history for Ballman-Cummings, Fort Smith Chair, or Riverside. That is not the same as “open shop forever.” It is a research gap. Adam Carson’s work on Fort Smith’s conservative economic culture is the place an editor should start before writing a thesis about why the gap exists. A booster city can have unions and still talk as if it does not. A booster city can also crush them early and leave no pamphlet.

The 1917 general strike is the loud Fort Smith labor event in the Encyclopedia: telephone operators, then the town. Furniture is not named in the summary I have. Do not hang a dresser on that strike. Do notice that the city knew how to stop. A furniture pack that never mentions 1917 is a pack that thinks industry is only objects.

## Pratt without a bargaining table

The Pratt opinions are capital. No local files an amicus about sanders. When Ballman-Cummings stops manufacturing, the people who stop clocking in are not the plaintiffs. Union or not, they are the silent half of a liquidation. A labor essay has to say that even when the union paper is missing.

Riverside’s 1,300 in the 2001 Fed story, if it holds, is a large enough payroll to have had some form of representation or a deliberate policy against it. I will not guess which. I will say that a thousand-person furniture campus in the 1990s sat in a national industry that was already losing shops to right-to-work states and then to water. Arkansas was not a bystander in that map.

## After the contract dies

Turney’s buildings: Plant A still standing, other plants gone, Encyclopedia 2026 update. The local’s paper is a citation. The pews are in churches that may not know the court date. That is ordinary. It is also why a factory pack should keep the citation: otherwise the pews become “handmade Arkansas heritage” with the fight sanded off.

A present-tense small shop is almost never a bargaining unit. Do not romanticize that as freedom and do not scold it as failure. It is a scale. This essay is about the scale that had a local number.

Still frame: a stapled contract, or a parking lot. No raised fist clip art. If an editor finds a Fort Smith furniture local — number, years, a strike vote — this essay should be rewritten around that number. Until then, 2746 and 1968 are the load-bearing marks. They are enough to stop the sentence “Arkansas furniture was just families.” Families sign contracts too. Sometimes they sign them on the other side.
