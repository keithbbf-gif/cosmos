## Fort Smith — primary and near-primary

- Fort Smith Historical Society. *The Journal*. Vol. 38, no. 1 (April 2014): Matthew Myers, “Edward Ballman: King of Fort Smith Furniture Manufacturing,” 28–29. PDF via UAFS library: `https://library.uafs.edu/sites/librarydev.uafs.edu/files/Departments/fshsj/38-01_complete_issue.pdf`.
- Fort Smith Historical Society archive biography: “Edward Ballman,” March 2007. `http://www.fortsmithhistory.org/archive/March2007/BallmanBio.html`. Ott, Meier & Co. (1878); Reynolds sale c. 1880; Huff / Meier / Ballman; 1885 river move; Huff drowning; Meier retirement c. 1891; Ballman-Cummings 1894; specialized companies 1902–1921.
- Boulden, Benjamin. “Fort Smith (Sebastian County).” *Encyclopedia of Arkansas*. `https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/fort-smith-988/`. Population table; Gold Rush outfitting; railroads in the 1870s; natural gas 1887 and glass; furniture as a metropolitan industry; Norge 1962 / Whirlpool 1968; 1898 tornado.

## Court and corporate

- *Pratt v. Ballman-Cummings Furniture Co.*, 254 Ark. 570 (1973). `https://law.justia.com/cases/arkansas/supreme-court/1973/73-5-1.html`. Ayers Furniture Industries partnership, November 1967; ICC trailer-loading explanation; John Ayers as chief officer of both houses.
- *Pratt v. Ballman-Cummings Furniture Co.*, 261 Ark. 25 (1977). `https://law.justia.com/cases/arkansas/supreme-court/1977/76-269-0.html`. Appraisal fight; Ballman-Cummings without manufacturing operations; Fort Smith Chair still marketing as Ayers and profitable 1971–73.
- *Carpenters Local Union No. 2746 v. Turney Wood Products, Inc.*, 289 F. Supp. 143 (W.D. Ark. 1968). `https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/289/143/1419172/`.

## 1996 tornado

- National Weather Service. *Ft. Smith and Van Buren, Arkansas, Tornado of April 21, 1996*. Assessment PDF: `https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/ftsmith.pdf`. Touchdown 11:12 p.m. CDT west side of downtown; industrial and warehouse damage; ~10-mile path.
- Arkansas Democrat-Gazette. “‘Tremendous roar’: 30 years later…” April 18, 2026. Eads Brothers building fire April 24, 1996 after utilities restored; two children killed north side; ~1,800 homes substantially damaged.
- Fort Smith Museum of History photographs of Garrison Avenue and the Eads fire — **rights required**. Do not lift.

## Wood supply (statewide)

- Harris, J. T., and Hu Maxwell. “Uses and supply of wood in Arkansas.” In *Wood-using industries and national forests of Arkansas*. U.S. Department of Agriculture, Forest Service Bulletin 106, 1912. Biodiversity Heritage Library: `https://doi.org/10.5962/bhl.title.66270`. Table 8: red gum 9,061,000 bd ft (58.94%); white oak 5,586,000 (36.34%); together ~95%; 15,373,000 bd ft total furniture wood; 85.44% grown in Arkansas. White oak “generally an outside wood”; red gum inside and outside; shortleaf pine almost absent from furniture despite dominating other industries. Chairs reported separately (Table 10).
- Encyclopedia of Arkansas. “Bradley County.” `https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bradley-county-750/`. Southern, Bradley, and Arkansas Lumber companies at Warren; 100,000–150,000 bd ft/day by 1907.
- ARGenWeb. “Bradley Lumber Company of Arkansas.” `http://www.argenweb.net/bradley/places/lumberco.html`. Postcard text: furniture stock, hardwood flooring, pine millwork — **stock, not a finished-furniture house**.
- ARGenWeb. Bradley County history notes, 1951 compilation. Fullerton / Weyerhaeuser / Rittenhouse; Potlatch purchases 1956–58.

## Statewide factories

- Welky, Ali. “Turney Wood Products, Inc.” *Encyclopedia of Arkansas*. `https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/turney-wood-products-inc-9133/`. - Akin Furniture, Monticello: company materials claim 1985 hospitality / senior-living furniture. `[VERIFY]` before a payroll sentence.
