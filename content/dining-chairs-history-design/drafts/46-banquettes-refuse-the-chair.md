A banquette is a bench that signed a lease with the wall. Upholstered, often buttoned, often too deep, a breakfast nook or a restaurant booth or an open-plan kitchen’s way of seating three where two chairs would stand. It refuses the individual dining chair: no pull-out, no stack, no splat to photograph as a set. It brings back the medieval problem in foam: the middle person wants out.

This series ends here because the type is the limit of the brief. If the seat does not come to the table, the table must come to the seat, and the ergonomics change.

## What you gain

Capacity along a wall. Storage under the sit if someone built a lid (crumbs will find the lid). A look that photographs as “cozy.” A place a child can kneel. A wheelchair can sometimes take the open side more easily if the chairs there leave — the access essay’s gift.

## What you lose

The scoot. The ability to change a chair height with a pad without changing a built-in. The ability to hang or stack. A back that is often a wall: rake is the carpenter’s, not the chairmaker’s. A seat that is often too deep, so you slouch or you perch. A foam that is a lounge at a dining height, so the plate becomes a reach.

Servers in restaurants learn the booth. Households learn it the first time someone in the corner needs the toilet during a long dinner.

## Pairing

The table at a banquette is often a pedestal or a trestle that can be kicked or slid. Apron clearance is still a thigh. Depth of the banquette plus the table’s overhang is a new subtraction. People build banquettes from a Pinterest depth and then live in a C-shaped spine.

Height: the same camp as a chair if you want the same table. Many banquettes sit lower and softer. Then the table is wrong or the sit is a shrug.

The free side still wants chairs. Those chairs must match the banquette’s finished sit, not a catalog “dining” number. Mixed heights at a booth are a photograph I have sat in and hated.

## Older refusals

Hall benches, refectory benches, Shaker long seats, Roman couches for a different posture — the individual side chair was never inevitable. The banquette is the twentieth-century American kitchen’s version, then the 2010s renovation’s version. The farmhouse-industrial sibling essay owns some of that room. This hour owns the refused chair.

## Shop practice

If we build a banquette, we treat it as a bench with a numbered sit, a numbered depth, a foot that can find a rail, and an open end. We do not build a trap with a foam that dies. We put real chairs on the free side, taped to the same height.

If the client wants only a banquette and a pedestal, we talk about getting out, and about the person who will hate the deep sit. Then we can still build it. Honesty is not a refusal to build.

The dining chair remains the subject of this pack. The banquette is the reminder that dinner will sit on whatever the floor plan allows. Measure that. Do not let a cushion erase the tape.

When you want the individual machine back — a pair you can move, recane, hang, tilt-test, and bury when the meal is over — you want a chair. That is where the series started: the front rail and the thigh, the gap, the lean. The rest was history teaching the same contact.

## The middle person wants out

A hall never solved this. Neither did the breakfast nook. A bench along a wall seats three where two chairs would stand, and the person in the middle will need the toilet during a long dinner. Servers in restaurants learn the booth. Households learn it the first time someone in the corner asks out and everyone on the open side has to stand, or the table has to slide, or a child crawls under.

A banquette with an open end can be a gift. The wheelchair essay already named that gift: the chairs on the free side leave, the wheelchair takes the open side, the wall owns the other. A U-shaped booth is a restaurant habit that renovated kitchens copied. The U is a trap with a foam that dies. I will build an open end. I will talk about the U before I build it.

Storage under the sit if someone built a lid — crumbs will find the lid. A hinge that dumps a seat full of people when you lift it is a hinge I will not specify. A lid that is a show surface and a crumb trap is still a crumb trap. I have opened those lids. The diary lives in the box as well as on the cloth.

## Depth, rake, and the C-shaped spine

A banquette seat is often too deep. Chair depth is back contact to front edge for a dining femur; banquette depth is often a lounge number copied from a photograph. People slouch into a C or they perch on the front edge and lose the back. Either way the plate becomes a reach if the table was placed for the slouch. I have sat in a 2010s kitchen nook whose banquette was a sofa depth at a dining height. The talk was fine. The sit was a lounge that could not lounge because the table was there.

Rake on a banquette is the carpenter’s, not the chairmaker’s. The back is often a wall. A wall does not have a splat you can move. A wall has a board or an upholstered slab at an angle someone chose from a section drawing. If that angle is a lounge, the salt is a reach. If that angle is a church pew, the long dinner is a tax. Specify the rake as a number on the ticket, the way you would specify a chair. Do not let the wall invent it.

Height: the same camp as a chair if you want the same table. Many banquettes sit lower and softer. Then the table is wrong or the sit is a shrug. Foam that is a lounge at a dining height is the long-dinner myth in a built-in. A firm sit that can take a thin pad is an adult banquette. A heavenly ten minutes that bottoms at forty is a sofa that wandered into breakfast.

## Pairing the free side

The free side still wants chairs. Those chairs must match the banquette’s finished sit, not a catalog dining number. Mixed heights at a booth are a photograph I have sat in and hated: the banquette people in a shrug, the chair people in a hole, or the reverse. Tape both. Compress the banquette foam with a fist. Write the remainder to the underside. The measuring essay’s five numbers still apply. The banquette is just a bench that cannot leave.

A child can kneel on a banquette. That is a gain and a crumb path. A child in the corner of a U is a trapped child. The children essay already refused to make a side chair do a strap job. This hour refuses to make a built-in do a high-chair job. A booster on a banquette steals the same gap a booster on a chair steals. Try it before a holiday.
