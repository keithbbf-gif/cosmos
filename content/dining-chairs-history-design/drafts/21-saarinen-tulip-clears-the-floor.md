Eero Saarinen wanted the slum of legs gone. The Pedestal group — table and chairs — is one argument in two objects. The dining-room pack already told the table half. This hour is the side chair: a molded shell on a single stem, a swivel in many versions, a sit that turns toward the person on your right without walking four feet.

The floor comes back. You can see who dropped a napkin. You can sweep. You also see every scuff on the stem. A tulip chair in a house with gravelly shoes is a maintenance personality.

## Shell on a stem

The shell is a single surface again, like Eames, but the support is a column. Swivel, when it exists, is the compass seat’s heir: you turn to talk. That is a dinner kindness I will praise without a slogan. Non-swivel versions exist; know which you have before you promise the turn.

The stem needs a wide foot. People trip the foot. Children ride the swivel. The bearing is a part. It will announce itself one Thanksgiving.

Upholstered shells are the common dining version. The foam is the sit. It compresses. A 1950s cushion and a 2020s replacement are not the same height. Pair to the table after the recover, not before. A “tulip” with a fat modern foam can sit you too high for a period pedestal table and too low for a later thick top.

## When the table is not a tulip

A Pedestal chair at a rectangular table with a deep apron is the old collision in a new dress. The stem may clear the apron better than four cabrioles, or the shell’s front edge may not. Mock it. The catalog pair is chair-and-table from the same religion. Mixed marriages are allowed. They are not automatic.

A Pedestal chair at a farm trestle can look like a truce or a fight. I have seen both. The sit does not care about the fight. The sit cares about the shell’s lip and your thigh.

## The sit

You are in a bowl. Some people feel held. Some feel stuck. Getting out of a low, soft tulip after wine is a different rise than out of a Stickley. Host theater is weak; these chairs are a set of equals. That is a social fact as much as a design fact.

Arms on the armchair version wrap. They can make the bowl safer for a long sit and harder to slide into at a crowded table. Side chairs are easier in a row.

Vinyl and fabric each have a dinner life. Vinyl wipes and sweats. Fabric stains and breathes. Leather is the expensive wipe. Choose the stain you can live with.

## Copies and gardens

Unlicensed “tulip chairs” are a plastic industry. Some are outdoor cousins. Some are brittle. A dining room that buys eight brittle shells will buy them twice. Knoll (and authorized production) is one conversation. The silhouette is another. Pay accordingly.

Outdoor powder-coat stems at an indoor oak table are a look some houses want. The sit can be fine. The foot will still scuff the floor.

I like these chairs when the whole pair is the religion and the recover is numbered. I like them less as a single accent chair at a claw-and-ball table, which is a magazine joke that sitters then have to eat in.

A shop in oak cannot turn a tulip without becoming a different shop. What it can learn is the cleared floor and the swivel as a dining kindness. A wooden chair that turns easily and does not occupy with eight posts is a problem worth solving without fiberglass.

## The bowl, the rise, and the bearing

You are in a shell that holds like a shallow dish. Some people feel kept. Some feel stuck. Getting out of a low, soft tulip after wine is a longer rise than out of a Stickley or a 14. I watch older sitters at a holiday table: the ones in tulips use the table edge as a crutch. That is a dining fact, not a joke about age. Gravelly shoes scuff the stem and then the scuff is the whole view, because there are no four legs to hide it.
