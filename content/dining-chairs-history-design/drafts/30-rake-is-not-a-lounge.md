Rake is the back’s tilt off the vertical, and sometimes the seat’s tilt off the level. A little of both can keep you in the chair without a death-grip in the thighs. A lot of both is a lounge. Dinner is a forward activity: knife, glass, a face across the table. If the chair wins, the plate loses.

I do not start with a sacred 95 degrees. I start with a person trying to salt the potatoes. If they have to crunch forward out of a deep recline, the rake is a costume.

## Seat rake

A seat that is dead level can feel like you are sliding out, especially in slick plastic or polished leather. A slight back-down tilt — a few degrees — is a habit on a lot of wooden dining chairs if you look at the rail. Too much tilt and the front rail rises into the thigh (the next-but-one essay) and the gap dies.

Upholstery can add rake the frame does not have. A foam wedge sold as “posture” is often a lounge wedge. I am suspicious of wedges at dinner.

Windsor saddles dish instead of tilting the whole plane. The dish is a local rake. It can be kinder than a flat plank. A router dent called a saddle is not a dish.

## Back rake

Federal square-backs are often quite upright. Balloon-backs and some Grecian tablets give you more. Mission slats can be nearly vertical and then feel stern. Shells (Eames, Jacobsen, tulip) bake a curve that is a rake by another name.

Office chairs add a recline mechanism because the workday includes a phone stare at the ceiling. Dinner includes a toast and a lean in. A mechanism is usually the wrong tool. A fixed rake that allows a rest between courses is enough.

If you pad a vertical slat-back, you have invented rake and stolen depth. Again: one variable.

## Historical accidents

Chairs recut at the back legs change rake. Tipped feet change rake. A chair that sat well in 1810 can lounge or perch after a bad tip. Look at the rear stile’s angle to the floor, not the style name.

Period rooms sometimes recut for later floors and later sitters. Museums are not innocent. Measure the chair in front of you.

## Shop practice

We set rake on the rear post and we sit in a mock before we commit six. A customer who wants “a little lounge” at dinner will get a little, then we put a plate in front of them. If they reach, we take it back.

## Failure is a reach, or a jam

Too much back rake: the salt is a reach, the glass is a reach, the face across the table is a conversation with the ceiling. People crunch forward. The crunch is the review. They will call the chair comfortable in the store, where there is no plate, and mean at dinner.

Too little rake, with a slick seat: you hold yourself in with your thighs. That grip is fatigue. A little seat tilt or a dish would have done the holding. A sharp front rail will punish the grip.

Too much seat tilt: the front rail rises, the thigh meets wood, the gap dies. People add a pad to “soften” and then they are higher and still jammed. Fix the tilt. Do not stack sins.

Mixing chairs at one table with different rakes is how one guest looks slouched and one looks punished. Heights can match and the photograph still lie. I sit the mix with a plate. If one person is in a lounge and one is in a pew, the set is not a set. It is a storage problem wearing matching stain.

## The plate test in the shop

The mock can be crude: two sticks at an angle, a plywood seat, a sawhorse table. The body is not crude. If the shoulders climb, height is the other essay. If the thighs jam, the gap is the other essay. If they crunch to the salt, rake is this one.

I will not print a degree as house law. The method is the plate test. Arkansas oak chairs get the rake that survived the plate, not the rake that survived a catalog rendering. A rendering has no salt.

## Historical accidents you can still sit

I have tipped a chair on the shop floor and watched the back become a lounge without anyone touching the splat. Two saw cuts at the rear feet will do that. So will a later caster that adds height at the front and not the back. So will a rug that is thick under the front legs and flat under the rear because the chair never sits quite square. Rake is a geometry with the floor. Change the floor and you have changed the chair.

A saber-leg Empire side that was recut to stand on a later parquet can come to dinner as a pew. A balloon-back whose rear legs were pieced after rot can come to dinner as a club chair. The style name will not warn you. The rear stile’s angle to the floor will.

Thonet’s hoop has a lean that is baked into the steam. You cannot recut it without recutting the idea. A Cesca’s tube rake is a bend; if the bend is tired, the sit is tired. A Series 7’s waist is a flex that behaves like rake for the first ten minutes and then is just a curve. None of these is a degree you set with a sliding bevel on a post. All of them still have to pass the salt.

When we mix a ladderback (upright) with a shell (curve) at one cloth, I sit both with a plate before I call it a set. Matching stain is not matching posture. One guest will look slouched. One will look punished. The photograph will lie and the dinner will not.

If a client brings six chairs with six rakes and asks for a table, I am not building a table that forgives all six. I am asking which chairs stay. The plate test is cheaper than a second set.
