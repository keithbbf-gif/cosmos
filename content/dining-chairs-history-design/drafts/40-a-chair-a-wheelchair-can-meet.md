A wheelchair at dinner is not a special event in a lot of houses. It is a seat. The dining chairs have to get out of the way, and the table has to offer a cave: underside height, width, depth, nothing in the knee path that a pedestal or a drawer invented for looks.

I am not writing a full access manual. I am writing the dining-chair half: lightness, arms that leave, ends that are not thrones, a floor the wheels can use, and a refusal to treat the matched set as more important than the person.

## The chair’s job is to leave

A Mission post that takes two people to move is a barrier. A stacker you can move with one hand is a tool. A Windsor, a 14, a Series 7, an INGOLF — light chairs are access furniture even when they were not sold as such.

Host armchairs that cannot leave because they complete a photograph are a problem. Side chairs at the end, or an empty end, are often the honest place: more room, a view of the table, a path.

Banquettes that trap a middle sitter also trap a transfer. A banquette with an open end can be a gift. The last essay takes the type.

## The table’s cave

Aprons, trestle beams, pedestal spreads, cranks, hidden leaves — all of these occupy the cave. The gap essay was about a sitting thigh. This hour is about footrests, knees, and the length of a wheelchair. Get under the table with the actual chair, not a guess.

A round pedestal can be better or worse depending on the spread of the foot. A four-leg table with a deep drawer apron can be worse than it looks from above.

Floor: a thick rug that chairs dent is a rug wheels hate. Thresholds to the dining room are part of the furniture whether the shop cut them or not.

## Transfer and dignity

I will not narrate a transfer I have not been asked to help with. I will say: a chair that scoots, a table that does not require a shrug to reach a plate, and a setting that is not a children’s booster unless that is what was asked. Height of the table still wants the same pairing honesty as every other sit.

Office wheelchair guidance (Allsteel’s universal-design pages, and others) is a cousin. Dining is a social table, not a workstation, but the cave is the same geometry.

## Shop practice

When we know a wheelchair is part of the household, we design the table first and the chairs as objects that leave. We do not sell eight arms. We radius corners that shins and rims will find. We do not put a stretcher in the one bay that needs to be a cave.

A southern oak shop can do this without a special catalog name. It is the underside and the weight of the side chair.

If we do not know, we still avoid a table that cannot lose a chair. Someone will visit.

## The cave, object by object

Aprons occupy the cave. A deep drawer apron on a four-leg table can be worse than it looks from above. Trestle beams occupy the cave if they run where a knee or a footrest needs to go. Pedestal spreads occupy the cave in a circle; a round pedestal can be better or worse depending on the foot. A Saarinen tulip clears the floor in a way the sibling mid-century essay already praised for a different reason. Here the praise is simpler: there is a cave if the spread of the foot does not fill it.

Cranks, hidden leaves, and slides occupy the cave on tables that open. I have put a tape on an underside that looked open from a chair and was a machine from a wheelchair. The gap essay was about a sitting thigh. This hour is about footrests, knees, and the length of a wheelchair. The pair is still a pair. Height of the table still wants the same pairing honesty as every other sit. A shrug to reach a plate is a shrug whether the seat has wheels or legs.

We do not put a stretcher in the one bay that needs to be a cave. A stretchered oak side is a good dining chair and a bad object if it is parked in the bay a wheelchair will use. The stretcher can live on the other chairs. The bay that is a cave stays a cave.

## Floor, rug, threshold

A thick rug that chairs dent is a rug wheels hate. A pad under a rug that makes a lip is a lip. Thresholds to the dining room are part of the furniture whether the shop cut them or not. A beautiful oak floor with a half-inch rise at the doorway is a barrier I did not bill and still have to name.

I radius corners that shins and rims will find. A sharp table corner at transfer height is a different injury from a sharp front rail. Both are bars. The rail essay owns the thigh. This hour owns the rim and the shin.

## Failure cases

Eight matching armchairs around a pedestal whose foot fills the cave. A Mission set that cannot be moved without a second person. A banquette U with no open end. A thick piled rug and a proud threshold. A trestle beam in the only bay that could have been a cave. A host throne that will not leave the end because the photograph would look incomplete. A table with a drawer apron that photographs as “storage” and sits as a wall.

Treating this hour as a charity paragraph at the end of a style series. It is a dining-chair requirement. The matched set is a fashion. The meal is a person at a plate. Lightness, arms that leave, ends that are not thrones, a floor the wheels can use, and a refusal to treat six identical posts as more important than the person who came to eat — those are the chair half. The table half is the cave. Measure both. Get under the table with the actual chair.
