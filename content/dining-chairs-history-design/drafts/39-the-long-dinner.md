A weeknight plate is a short sit. A holiday is a long one. The same chair can be a pleasure at forty minutes and a tax at three hours. Catalogs skip this because they cannot SKU a duration. Office literature talks about shifts and microbreaks. Dinner talks about courses, standing to serve, a child who leaves, wine, a story that will not end.

I ask how long before I ask which splat.

## What changes with time

Feet go tingling if the height is high. Thighs print a rail if the edge is sharp. A back you never needed for soup becomes a bar or a mercy. Foam bottoms. Cane hammocks a little. People slouch into a C. People perch forward to stay in the talk. The chair that only works in one posted pose fails.

A chair that lets you change — turn on a compass or a swivel, shift a thigh in a decent gap, find a stretcher with a foot — lasts longer than a chair that locks you in a pretty outline.

Standing is part of a long dinner in houses that still serve from the table. A host chair you cannot get out of is a trap. Arms that clear matter more at hour two, when you rise more.

## Historical durations

Balloon-backs assume a program. Federal shields assume a more upright meal. Cafés assume a stay that is not a family’s. Banquets assume a bench or a rank. I do not invent a Victorian hour count. I say the furniture was willing to stay.

People who “cannot sit in old chairs” often cannot sit still for the length those rooms expected, or they are in a recut chair at a new table. Both can be true.

## Office duration is the wrong twin

A workday is longer and lonelier. A meal is social and interrupted. Transferring a mesh chair’s virtue to dinner is how you get wheels and arms that do not clear. Transferring dinner’s virtue to a workday is how you get a backache and a Wishbone on a Zoom call.

If the dining table is the desk, buy a desk chair for the day and a dining chair for the meal, or accept a compromise and do not call it ergonomic. The lumbar essay already refused the knob.

## Shop practice

We sit in the prototype through a long talk, not a long speech about craft. If I want to stand after fifty minutes, the rake or the rail is wrong for a holiday client. If I forget the chair at fifty minutes, we are closer.

A thin pad for a long dinner is sometimes the adult edit. A lounge cushion is not. Specify the hour with the pad.

I like a long dinner in a chair that is a little too hard at minute ten and still honest at minute one-twenty. I distrust a chair that is heavenly at minute ten and gone at minute forty. Foam is often that second chair.

Ask the client about Thanksgiving, not about their favorite Instagram chair. The answer is a duration.

## What the sit does after the plate is empty

Forty minutes forgives a stern splat. You can perch, cut, pass a dish, and leave. Three hours will not forgive the same splat. Feet go tingling if the height is high. Thighs print a rail if the edge is sharp. A back you never needed for soup becomes a bar or a mercy. Foam bottoms. Cane hammocks a little. People slouch into a C. Stand to fetch a bottle and sit again without a fight with the arms. Those are duration tools. A rake that turns dinner into a lounge makes the salt a reach at minute twenty and a grievance at minute ninety. The rake essay already refused the lounge. This hour only needs the clock.

## Historical durations without a sermon

John F. Kasson’s *Rudeness and Civility* is a manners book, not a furniture book, but it is useful for the clock. Nineteenth-century American dining could be a performance with courses. The balloon-back and the sprung seat are furniture that believed that performance. A café 14 believed a different clock: coffee, a newspaper, a conversation that could end when the table was needed. A refectory bench believed a different clock again: a hall, a rank, a meal that was not a family’s Sunday.

I will not paste a course count onto a Hepplewhite shield and call it data. I will say that a hard upright chair at a short meal is a pleasure, and that the same chair at a wedding supper is a tax unless the sitter can change position. Mission slats that hit you at the shoulder blades are honest for an hour and a lecture at three. A Wishbone’s Y is a contact you can live with if the seat is right; it is a bar if you slouch and stay slouched. A Series 7 nest is a short-meal chair that a lot of houses use for long meals because the stack was the point. The sit will tell you at minute fifty.

People who cannot sit in old chairs are sometimes in a correct old chair at a new thick-top table. The gap essay owns that pair. This hour owns the clock that makes the pair worse. A jammed thigh at minute ten is a complaint. A jammed thigh at minute one-twenty is a departure.

## Office duration is the wrong twin, named again

A workday is longer and lonelier. A meal is social and interrupted. Office literature talks about shifts and microbreaks and a lumbar knob. Dinner talks about courses, standing to serve, a child who leaves, wine, a story that will not end. Transferring a mesh chair’s virtue to dinner is how you get wheels and arms that do not clear. Transferring dinner’s virtue to a workday is how you get a backache and a Wishbone on a video call.

If the dining table is the desk, buy a desk chair for the day and a dining chair for the meal, or accept a compromise and do not call it ergonomic. BIFMA X5.1 and X10.1 and ISO 9241-5 are workstation papers. They may be named as cousins. They are not a holiday. The lumbar essay already refused the knob. I will not bring it back as a duration accessory.

A thin pad for a long dinner is sometimes the adult edit. A lounge cushion is not. Specify the hour with the pad. Foam is often that second chair. Down is that chair with a better accent. A firm felt or a haircloth or a well-tied spring can be the first chair if the height and the rail are right.

## The shop long-sit, and what I change

We sit in the prototype through a long talk, not a long speech about craft. If I want to stand after fifty minutes, the rake or the rail is wrong for a holiday client. If I forget the chair at fifty minutes, we are closer. I do not need a laboratory. I need a plate, a conversation, and a refusal to declare the chair finished after a ten-minute perch.

What I change after a long sit is rarely the splat. I radius a front rail. I take a little out of a rake. I lower a finished sit by a felt, or I raise it by refusing a dying foam. I add a stretcher a foot can find. I refuse a host arm that trapped me at minute sixty when I wanted to stand. Those are duration edits. A new piercing in a splat is not.

Ask the client about Thanksgiving, not about a photograph of a chair. Ask how many hours, how many rises, whether anyone serves from the table, whether children leave and come back, whether the same chairs will be used for a weeknight plate. The weeknight can forgive a stern splat. The holiday will not. The answer is a duration. Catalogs skip this because they cannot SKU it.

## Failure cases

A memory-foam dining pad that feels kind on Tuesday and bottoms on a holiday. A rake copied from a lounge and sold as dining. A host armchair you cannot leave when the roast needs the other room. A balloon-back recovered in a plush that stole an inch and then compressed another, so the sitter meets the apron at hour two. A café chair asked to do a wedding. A Mission slat asked to do a three-hour talk without a place to put a foot.

Buying six chairs after sitting in one for four minutes in a store. The store sit is a perch. The holiday is a clock. I have sent people home to sit in their existing chairs through a real meal and then tell me what hurt. The answer is usually a rail, a height, a gap, or a foam. It is rarely a splat. Fashion is above the rail. Duration lives below it and in the back you did not need until the talk outlasted the plate.
