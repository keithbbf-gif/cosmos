# Style Guide

This series is a staged magazine history of **healthcare and nursing-home furniture** — beds, casegoods, seating, wet-room chairs, nurse-station work, materials, and the channel that actually put those objects on a Michigan wing. The channel era in view is the **Medline book plus a Medilodge-class operator**: a national distributor’s punchout and house-brand SKUs meeting a multi-building skilled-nursing buyer who needed a room package, a replacement chair, and a warranty that would answer the phone.

It is written for a reader who has stood in a resident room, a purchasing office, or a loading dock — or who wants to understand those rooms without being sold a suite. It is **not** a clinic brochure, a survey consultant’s product, or a living manufacturer’s catalog.

Sibling furniture packs (`content/arts-crafts-mission-furniture-deep/`, living-room and bedroom writers) own domestic and Arts-and-Crafts oak. This pack owns the institutional object: the electric hi-lo, the overbed table, the vinyl dining chair, the wardrobe rod a surveyor can reach.

## Voice

Write as a person who has read the spec sheet, the survey appendix, and the packing list — and who remembers that those three documents rarely describe the same chair. Prefer the particular: a 2006 FDA zone drawing, a CMS F-tag number, a Batesville wood-and-metal bed, a Medline MDS prefix, a Medilodge-era room package, a CAL 117 hangtag. Do not open with cosmic time. Do not close with a sermon.

The human voice here is BBF furniture-lane magazine, not LinkedIn, not SEO, not a DON in-service script. Channel knowledge is allowed. Invented memoir is not. Do not write “I sold this building in 2009.” Do write “On a Medilodge-era wing the book and the floor disagreed.”

Banned phrasing and habits:

- delve, landscape (as metaphor), robust, leverage, unlock, cutting-edge, game-changer
- tapestry, underscore, seamless, ever-evolving, elevate your space
- “In today’s rapidly…”
- “It’s important to note”
- stacked *Moreover* / *Furthermore*
- “Whether you’re a buyer or a caregiver…”
- “In conclusion”
- throat-clearing first paragraphs
- symmetrical three-item filler used as a substitute for an argument
- fake omniscience: “scholars agree,” “everyone knows,” “the industry was forever changed”
- COSMOS, AI, or internal process talk
- buy-buttons, “shop the look,” affiliate asides, current catalog copy, “add to cart”
- clinical how-to: rail-use protocols, fall-program recipes, thickeners, transfer counts

Allowed: doubt, catalog claim versus floor wear, a missing capacity tag, a later mattress that opened a gap the 2006 drawing did not imagine. A claim without a source is marked `[CITE NEEDED]` or `[VERIFY]`. Invented purchase orders, invented resident names, and invented survey citations are not permitted.

## Educational line

Every essay carries this line, italic, immediately after the H1:

*Educational history of healthcare and nursing-home furniture. Not a purchasing specification for a named building, not a survey opinion, and not clinical advice about beds, rails, or seating for a particular resident.*

Do not expand it into a legal essay.

## Openings

Start on an artifact, a dated document, a room, a catalog page, or a loading-dock object. Right:

- “The 2006 FDA guidance draws seven openings in a hospital bed system and then writes dimensional limits for only four of them.”
- “William A. Hillenbrand founded Hill-Rom in Batesville, Indiana, in 1929.”
- “The overbed table is the piece the aide uses twenty times a shift and the one purchasing replaces from the book.”

Wrong: “Since the dawn of care, humans have needed a place to sit.”

## Length and shape

Target **1,800–2,600 words** of body text (YAML and the `## Notes` / `## Figure plan` blocks do not count). A piece should have:

1. A concrete opening (object, room, statute, catalog, or test)
2. A middle that moves through making, specifying, buying, and wearing — not a Wikipedia walk-through
3. A close that leaves a residual fact or tension, not a recap of the H2s

One essay, one problem. Adjacent chapters may overlap; they should not repeat the same paragraph in different clothes.

## Channel rule (Medline / Medilodge)

**Medline** is a distributor-manufacturer founded in 1966 by Jim and Jon Mills (family line from A. L. Mills’s 1910 Chicago garment shop). The 1979 glossy catalog is a documented object. House-brand prefixes such as MDS appear on later punchout furniture. Medline is a *channel*, not the inventor of the hospital bed.

**Medilodge** (also styled MediLodge) is a Michigan skilled-nursing brand. Around 2011 the Washington Township–based MediLodge Group ran about fifteen SNFs and one assisted living community under CEO Gail Clarkson (~2,500 beds). In 2013 Crain’s Detroit Business reported a sale of fifteen homes to Prestige Healthcare of Louisville. Do not collapse Medilodge into Ciena Healthcare (a separate Southfield operator founded by Mohammad Qazi in 1998). Do not write an ownership exposé. The brand name is here because it is the kind of multi-building Michigan buyer the Medline-era book was written for.

Use “Medline/Medilodge-era” for the roughly 1990s–2010s channel habit: punchout catalog, house brand versus name brand, corporate spec versus building inventory, capital versus supply, freight claims through the distributor. Do not invent a PO number, a wing color, or a DON’s name.

## Scholarship and specs

Cite in running prose and in a short notes block: statute section, F-tag, FDA guidance date, IEC number, manufacturer history with a named source, or `[CITE NEEDED]`. Prefer:

- FDA, *Hospital Bed System Dimensional and Assessment Guidance to Reduce Entrapment* (10 March 2006)
- IEC 60601-2-52 (medical beds); older IEC 60601-2-38 for powered hospital beds
- CMS State Operations Manual Appendix PP: F584 / §483.10(i) homelike; F558 accommodation; F700 / §483.25(n) bed rails; F917 / §483.90(e) bed, furniture, closet (older F461 language)
- OBRA 1987 (Nursing Home Reform Act)
- CAL TB 117-2013; historical CAL TB 133; NFPA 101; BIFMA seating standards
- Hillenbrand / Hill-Rom company histories (1929; electronic bed commonly dated 1950)
- Medline company histories (1966; 1979 catalog; 1984 Mundelein move)
- Eden Alternative (Bill Thomas, 1991); Green House Project (early 2000s, Tupelo)
- Nightingale, *Notes on Nursing* (1859)

When a date, “first,” or capacity number is model-specific or contested, say so. Typical ranges may be stated as ranges. Do not invent a SKU’s exact travel or a Wyzenbeek count you did not read.

## Figures

Every essay carries a captioned figure plan of **four to seven** images, each beginning with `**Fig. N.**`. Prefer public-domain Nightingale plates, USPTO / FDA drawings, historic trade-catalog pages, CMS/FDA figures that are U.S. government works, and museum objects with named licenses. Each figure needs: suggested filename, object title, source, date if known, license note, alt text, and a one- or two-sentence magazine caption.

Full series documentation lives in `PHOTO_CAPTIONS.md`. Do not upload a living manufacturer’s current catalog as if it were free.

## Names and spelling

- **skilled nursing facility (SNF)**; nursing home in historical sentences
- **hi-lo**, **low-low**, **deck height** (sleeping surface of the frame, not top of mattress unless said)
- **overbed table**, not “TV tray”
- **geri chair** is hallway speech for a medical recliner / lounge chair on casters; say so
- **casegoods**: bedside cabinet, wardrobe, dresser
- **HPL** high-pressure laminate; **TFL** thermally fused laminate
- **CAL TB 117-2013** (smolder); **TB 133** (historical open-flame public occupancy)
- **F584, F700, F917** as current SOM tags; note older numbers when citing pre-renumbering letters
- Hill-Rom (hyphen); later Hillrom / Baxter (2021 acquisition) only as afterlife, not as a shop link
- Invacare, Carroll (the Elyria / Carroll bed line common on SNF floors), Joerns, Drive, GF Health
- Nemschoff, Kwalu, Wieland as *classes* of healthcare seating/casegoods, not as current SKUs
- Medilodge / MediLodge: use **Medilodge** in running copy after first mention of the styled name
- Batesville, Indiana; Mundelein, Illinois; Washington Township, Michigan

## Brand and SEO (BBF lane)

This is **Website-GC staged copy** for the BBF furniture lane. SEO lives in the title, `meta_description`, `tags`, and in natural use of words a reader types: hospital bed, nursing home furniture, overbed table, geri chair, healthcare vinyl, bed rail entrapment.

SEO does **not** live in a first paragraph that names every keyword. Do not write “Looking for durable nursing home chairs?” Do not append shop links. Living manufacturers appear as history and as channel nouns. They do not get a buy-button, a “shop our line,” or a current price.
