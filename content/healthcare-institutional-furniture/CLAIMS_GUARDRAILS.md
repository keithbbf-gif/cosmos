# Claims guardrails

This pack is educational furniture history and spec literacy. It is not a buying guide for a named building and not a care plan.

## Never say on a draft

- A named living resident, family, or aide (composite hallway speech is allowed: “the aide,” “purchasing,” “the DON”)
- Invented PO numbers, invoice totals, or Medilodge wing colors presented as fact
- “This SKU prevents falls” / “this chair is survey-proof”
- Step-by-step rail installation, fall-mat recipes, transfer counts, or restraint-reduction protocols as instructions
- Current list prices, “call for quote,” affiliate links
- Collapsing Medilodge into Ciena, or Medline into Hill-Rom
- “FDA approved” for a furniture line (beds are devices; most casegoods are not)
- A Wyzenbeek, bleach ratio, or travel-inch number not tagged to a source or `[VERIFY]`

## Always say when relevant

- Educational italic under the H1
- F-tag and CFR numbers when the survey is the subject
- That the 2006 FDA guidance is guidance, not a statute
- That IEC 60601-2-52 is a manufacturer/test-lab standard
- That typical capacity and height ranges vary by model year
- `[CITE NEEDED]` or `[VERIFY]` on contested “firsts” and model-specific inches

## Channel nouns that are allowed

Medline (distributor-manufacturer, 1966), Medilodge / MediLodge (Michigan SNF brand; 2013 Prestige sale of a block of homes), Hill-Rom / Hillenbrand, Invacare / Carroll, Joerns, Drive, Stryker (as hospital-device history, not an SNF furniture line), Nemschoff, Kwalu — as history and as classes, not as a storefront.
