# Outlines — one problem per essay

Facts below are the spine. Writers expand them into 1,800–2,600 words. Do not invent PO numbers, resident names, or “firsts” without a source or `[VERIFY]`.

## 01 — The Ward Bed and the Private Room

**Problem.** Institutional furniture begins as a ward problem (air, beds in rows) and becomes a private-room package the SNF still buys.

**Must use.** Florence Nightingale, *Notes on Nursing* (1859): beds, ventilation, not crowding. Nightingale wards as architecture. Progressive-era and postwar U.S. hospital private/semi-private shift. Nursing home as a different building type (converted house, then purpose-built double room). The SNF room inherits the hospital bed and the motel dresser.

**Open on.** A Nightingale sentence about beds, or a double room with a cubicle curtain.

## 02 — Batesville Makes a Hospital Bed

**Problem.** The American hospital bed company is a furniture shop that walked into hospitals.

**Must use.** John Hillenbrand / Batesville timber and casket line. William A. Hillenbrand founds Hill-Rom, 1929, Depression. Year of hospital visits. Wood-and-metal bed against white tubular steel. Sister firm Batesville Casket. Electronic bed commonly dated 1950 in company histories `[VERIFY the “first” claim]`. Later Hillrom / Baxter 2021 as afterlife only.

**Open on.** Batesville, 1929, a wood-and-metal deck.

## 03 — Crank, Electric, Hi-Lo

**Problem.** The deck learned to move: manual crank, then motors, then height as a transfer and fall variable.

**Must use.** Gatch / Fowler positions as medical language that became furniture geometry. Manual cranks at the foot. Electric actuators. Hi-lo as SNF language. Deck height versus top-of-mattress. Typical modern SNF travel often cited in the 7–30 inch deck neighborhood — treat as a range, `[VERIFY]` any exact travel. IEC 60601-2-38 then 60601-2-52 as the electrical/medical-bed standards path.

**Open on.** A hand crank under a painted footboard.

## 04 — OBRA Did Not Specify the Chair

**Problem.** The 1987 Nursing Home Reform Act changed rights and survey; it did not publish a furniture catalog.

**Must use.** OBRA 1987, signed by Reagan, effective 1990 implementation wave. Quality of life, dignity, restraints. Furniture enters later through SOM Appendix PP and F-tags, not as a bill of materials. The chair still had to be bought.

**Open on.** The statute’s silence.

## 05 — Homelike as a Survey Word

**Problem.** “Homelike” is a CMS word that can ding a mauve vinyl stacker and still accept an oak-laminate box.

**Must use.** §483.10(i) / F584: safe, clean, comfortable, homelike. Surveyor examples: furniture that does not reflect home, missing drapes, missing personal items. F558 accommodation: seat height, depth, arms that help a resident stand. F917 / §483.90(e) (older F461): bed of proper size and height, mattress, functional furniture, individual closet. Homelike is not a finish color.

**Open on.** A surveyor looking at a chair.

## 06 — Eden, Green House, and the Room

**Problem.** Culture change asked the room to stop looking like a hospital. The purchase order often stayed a hospital PO.

**Must use.** Bill Thomas, Eden Alternative, 1991 (New York nursing home). Plants, animals, children as the myth; furniture as the unpaid bill. Green House Project, early 2000s, first houses associated with Tupelo, Mississippi. Pioneer Network. Household model versus double-loaded corridor. What actually changed: sometimes the chair, sometimes only the language.

**Open on.** A living-room grouping at the end of a corridor that is still a corridor.

## 07 — The Medline Book Is a Channel

**Problem.** Medline is how a chair got a SKU, not how a bed was invented.

**Must use.** A. L. Mills, 1910 Northwestern Garment / meat-packing aprons; 1912 Mills Hospital Supply. Irving Mills. 1966 Jim and Jon Mills found Medline (after Cenco sale of the earlier firm). 1972 public / 1977 private again. 1979 $300,000 glossy catalog. 1984 move toward Mundelein. Punchout, GPO, house-brand MDS-class prefixes on later furniture. Distributor drop-ship versus warehouse. Do not claim Medline designed the first hospital bed.

**Open on.** The 1979 catalog or a later punchout furniture page.

## 08 — Medilodge-Era Purchasing

**Problem.** A multi-building Michigan operator bought rooms the way a chain buys rooms: corporate spec, building exceptions, the book in the middle.

**Must use.** MediLodge Group, Washington Township, Michigan. ~2011: Gail Clarkson CEO; about 15 SNFs + 1 AL; ~2,500 beds; 3,000 employees (*Provider*, May 2011). 2013: Crain’s Detroit Business, sale of 15 homes to Prestige Healthcare (Louisville). Do not merge with Ciena (Qazi, 1998, Southfield). Buyer seats: administrator, DON, maintenance, corporate purchasing, therapy. Corporate color board versus what the wing already had.

**Open on.** A corporate spec sheet and a mismatched replacement chair.

## 09 — Mauve Vinyl and Oak Laminate

**Problem.** Finish eras are as dated as wallpaper: mauve/teal healthcare vinyl; honey oak TFL; then “residential” woodgrain that still wipes.

**Must use.** 1980s–90s healthcare palette. Post-OBRA “homelike” as a laminate story. Thermofoil edges that peel. What a 2005 Medilodge-era room actually looked like: oak box, teal seat, cream wall. Later gray woodgrain.

**Open on.** A color chip in a binder.

## 10 — The Bleach Number After 2020

**Problem.** COVID did not invent bleach-cleanable vinyl. It made the card in the file a nightly argument.

**Must use.** EPA List N as a disinfectant class (not a furniture standard). Healthcare vinyl cards that already claimed 1:10 bleach. What EVS actually poured versus the hangtag. Isolation rooms, outdoor visits, plastic on the lounge. The chair that survived 2020 and the chair that did not.

**Open on.** A vinyl card with a bleach ratio.

## 11 — What the SNF Bed Spec Hides

**Problem.** Travel, capacity, and “assist rail compatible” are not the same as the bed that arrived.

**Must use.** Typical SNF electric bed as a class (Carroll / Invacare-class, Joerns-class, Medline house-brand class) — classes, not current SKUs. Capacity often 350–450 lb on standard frames `[VERIFY model]`. Width 35–36 inch deck common. Pendant versus siderail controls. Battery backup. Floor lock versus casters. What the sheet omits: mattress height, wall clearance, the outlet behind the nightstand.

**Open on.** A one-page spec that looks complete.

## 12 — Low Beds and the Fall Program

**Problem.** Low-low decks were bought as fall furniture. They created new transfers and new gaps.

**Must use.** Deck heights advertised in the 7–10 inch neighborhood `[VERIFY]`. Mats, alarms, and the bed as a program. Staff knee and back. The resident who cannot get up from a 7-inch deck. Not a how-to.

**Open on.** A bed at its lowest click.

## 13 — Bariatric Frames and the Door

**Problem.** Capacity is a number. The doorway, the elevator, and the toilet are the building.

**Must use.** 500 / 600 / 750 / 1,000 lb classes as catalog language. Expanded width. Reinforced casters. The 36-inch door that will not take a 42-inch frame. Shared rooms. Dignity as a furniture problem.

**Open on.** A frame that will not turn the corner.

## 14 — Seven Zones, Four Limits

**Problem.** The 10 March 2006 FDA guidance maps seven entrapment zones and writes dimensional criteria for zones 1–4 (about 80% of reports). Zones 5–7 wait.

**Must use.** 390 reports Jan 1985–March 2000. Hospital Bed Safety Workgroup. Zone 1 within the rail; 2 under the rail between supports; 3 between rail and mattress; 4 under the rail at the ends; 5 between split rails; 6 rail end to head/footboard; 7 head/footboard to mattress end. Guidance ≠ law. IEC 60601-2-52 later. HBSW cone/tool methods for 1–4.

**Open on.** The zone drawing.

## 15 — Assist Rails and the Word Restraint

**Problem.** A half-rail can be a transfer handle or a restraint depending on the care plan — furniture cannot settle the word.

**Must use.** OBRA restraint reduction. CMS F700 / §483.25(n): alternatives first; entrapment assessment; informed consent; correct size/weight; manufacturer instructions. Assist / half / three-quarter language on spec sheets. Not a protocol.

**Open on.** A half-rail in the raised click.

## 16 — Mattresses That Open a Gap

**Problem.** The mattress is in the 2006 system. Foam replacement, air overlay, and low-air-loss change Zone 3.

**Must use.** FDA text: mattress as contributor or mitigator. Compression, width, depth. Specialty surfaces versus the original deck. Channel habit: mattress from one book, bed from another.

**Open on.** A gap you can put a fist in.

## 17 — Headboard as Furniture and Device

**Problem.** Hillenbrand’s wood-and-metal idea never died: the bed is still trying to look like furniture.

**Must use.** Wood-look panels, laminate inserts, residential headboards on medical frames. IEC bed as a device. The panel that is also a crash point. Medilodge-era “homelike” headboards that still have a pendant hook.

**Open on.** A cherry-look panel on a steel frame.

## 18 — Trapeze, Helper Bar, Not a Chair

**Problem.** Transfer hardware is sold next to furniture and is not furniture.

**Must use.** Base-mounted trapeze, helper handles, floor-to-ceiling poles. Weight ratings. Clearance. The chair that was supposed to do the same job. Not a transfer lesson.

**Open on.** A trapeze chain over an empty deck.

## 19 — The Overbed Table

**Problem.** The most used piece on the floor is a castered tray with a gas spring or crank that fails first.

**Must use.** Typical tops ~15×30 or 18×32 inches `[VERIFY]`. Height travel often advertised near 28–45 inches `[VERIFY]`. H-base versus U-base for wheelchair. Pneumatic vs crank. Cup-holder vanity. Weight on the top (meal, chart, elbows). Channel: cheap house-brand tables as consumables.

**Open on.** A table that will not stay up.

## 20 — Bedside Cabinets and One Drawer

**Problem.** The cabinet is specified as storage and used as a bumper, a pantry, and a chart shelf.

**Open on.** An open drawer with a hairbrush and a form.

## 21 — Wardrobes and the Reachable Rod

**Must use.** §483.90(e) individual closet, racks and shelves accessible. Wheelchair reach. Out-of-season storage allowed elsewhere. Shared rooms and the stolen hanger. Built-in versus freestanding.

**Open on.** A rod at 66 inches over a wheelchair.

## 29 — Lobby Vinyl versus Nemschoff-Class
