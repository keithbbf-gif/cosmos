A director of nursing is not a designer. She is the person who will live with the drawer at 2 a.m.

I would rather have twenty minutes with her than a month of finish boards. The Medline-era jobs that failed were the ones where purchasing froze a page and nursing met it on install week.

I am not teaching you to ambush a DON. I am not inventing a conversation. I am listing the questions I wanted on the table, and why each one earns its minutes. If she does not have time, you are not ready to buy a floor. A floor bought without nursing is a photograph that will be corrected by hips.

## The room

I want the census of room types in her mouth, not in a brochure. How many doubles, how many privates, how many designated bariatric, how many memory. A 121-bed house on a public list is a number. The mix is a furniture program. I will not invent Montrose’s mix. I will not let a JPEG of a single specify a floor of doubles.

Where does the lift stand in a double. That is the question that kills more pretty packages than any finish fight. If she cannot answer it in words, we walk. We do not walk it with only the administrator. Administrators see census. DONs see hips.

Which side is the working side, and who decided. If the answer is “the rendering,” the rendering is wrong. If the answer is “whichever bed is by the window,” I want that written, because families will fight it and staff will still need the aisle.

Where does a daughter sit. F917 names a visitor. The leftover often does not. If the daughter sits on the bed, the bed is doing two jobs and one of them is a fall.

Where does the overbed park. If the answer is “in the aisle,” the aisle is a crash. If the answer is “we put it in the toilet,” the toilet is a crash of a different kind.

What is the closet now — a door, a wardrobe, a rumor. “Use existing” is not an answer. Existing in a 1968 plant is often a 12-inch coat catch in the dark. I want to hear whether everyday clothes live in the room and whether the roommate can reach them.

## The bed

What fleet is on the floor, and what is coming. A mixed fleet is a Zone 3 factory if mattresses get punched as one SKU. I will not ask her for a catalog number I can print. I will ask for maker families, deck widths, and whether anyone still has the match list.

Low-bed program or not. If yes, the nightstand height and the chair seat become a different conversation, and the mat — if they still believe in mats — becomes a furniture object whether purchasing filed it under falls.

Rails: full, half, none, mixed. Who assesses zones, and when did they last. Which mattress, which overlay. Who owns the gap if we change a headboard.

If the answer to that last one is “the mill,” I leave the headboard off. A mill can own a stain. A mill cannot own a 120-millimetre opening it did not design. The 2006 file is 413 deaths. I will not add a decorative panel to that file because a suite wanted to match.

## The body

Who cannot stand from the current chairs. That sentence is worth more than a vinyl binder. If she names a wing, I want therapy in the next ten minutes, not a promise to “look at seat height later.”

What seat heights has therapy asked for. What is the real weight census, not the brochure. A plus sign on a chair is not a 500-pound program. Door width is part of the program. The leftover is part of the program.

Who eats in the room, and why. The last question is a dining spec. The overbed count at noon is the number. If half the floor eats in bed because the dining room apron hits a footplate, I am not going to solve that with a prettier nightstand. I will still specify an overbed that locks and a chair that can come to a table if the table ever gets honest.

## The wipe

What do you spray on a Tuesday. What did you spray last outbreak. Will you test a scrap, including an edge. Who owns condemned vinyl.

If infection control is a different person, I want them in the same twenty minutes. A DON who says “we use bleach” and an EVS supervisor who says “we use the quat” is a finish failure waiting to happen. I will not name an approved wipe. I will name the method: actual product, actual dilution, actual dwell, a scrap of the actual finish, including an edge, looked at in a week. A verbal chemistry is how maple goes white on a door edge while the sample board still photographs.

Who owns condemned vinyl is a money question wearing an infection question. If no one owns it, the split chair stays in the day room until a family photographs it.

## The lock

Who steals, in the building’s real story — roommate, visitor, staff, phantom. Who should open the drawer. Memory-care hardware: care plan or catalog.

I will not suggest a lock she does not want. I will not hide one she does. A lock only staff can open is not “security” in the F917 sense. Protecting clothing from casual access is in the guidance. Punishing a resident for having a wallet is not. A memory unit that copies the standard lock because the page matched is how you buy a pretty hazard. The memory-care draft will sit with that. Here I only need her to say whether the hardware is a care-plan object or a catalog object.

## The channel

Is Medline a contract, a habit, or a person you trust. Those are three different routes. A contract has a tier and a punchout. A habit has a Tuesday. A person has a trunk and a memory of which chair arm ripped in the last building. I want to know which one I am marrying.

Who is the rep, and will they walk the leftover with us. If the rep will not walk the leftover, the rep is a punchout. I can live with a punchout for twenty nightstands after a flood. I cannot live with a punchout as the designer of a new plant.

Who installs, and do they work around lunch. A crew that needs the room empty for three days is a kitchen crew. This is not a kitchen.

Where is the attic stock from the last buy. If the answer is “there isn’t any,” I write extra pulls on this buy before anyone value-engineers the crown.

## Fire, sound, and the cousin tags

I would ask what edition of NFPA 101 the last survey used, and whether the house is sprinklered as a fact or as a hope. I am not her fire marshal. I am the person who will otherwise ship a wingchair into a heat-release conversation because someone said homelike. The 117 / 101 draft is the long version. The short version is: name the adopted edition before you name the floral.

I would ask where the television lives and whether anyone has measured the room as an acoustic package. F584 includes sound. A hard case-good package on a hard floor with a television is a sound package whether we named it or not.

I would ask whether anyone still thinks a wooden plank over a rail is a kindness. If yes, we stop and open the zone draft. Kindness that joins the bed system is not a mill courtesy.

## The punch list

Who signs. How fast can we recut a rod. What did the last vendor get wrong.

The last question is the specification. People will tell you if you let them complain. I have learned more from a five-minute complaint about a drawer that loosened in a week than from a twenty-page finish board. The drawer is a staff object. Staff will say so if purchasing is not in the room performing.

If she says the last vendor “was fine,” I ask what they cut on Thursday. Fine houses still have a two-inch scar. That scar is the document.

## What I would not ask

I would not ask her favorite color first. I would not ask her to pick a cathedral arch. I would not ask her to bless a JPEG on a phone in a hallway while a call light is going.

I would not ask her to violate a GPO if that is her job to keep. I would ask her to write the inches the GPO page left out.

I would not ask her to tell me a resident’s name. This series has no faces. A room type and a body type are enough.

I would not ask her to pick a clock style. I would ask her where a clock can live that is not the overbed.

## After she answers

I write the answers on the drawing. If purchasing wants a different nightstand, the drawing still has the lift. The lift is the DON’s sentence. It stays.

If twenty minutes produced no lift answer, no wipe name, and no working side, I do not freeze a page. I come back. Motion without those three sentences is how a mill ships a pretty failure.

This is educational, not a script for a sales call. If you are a family, you can ask a shorter set: can she stand from the chair, can she reach the rod, is there a clock, is the curtain a wall, is there daylight between the mattress and the rail. You do not need a mill to ask those. You need the nerve to ask them before the bedspread conversation starts.
