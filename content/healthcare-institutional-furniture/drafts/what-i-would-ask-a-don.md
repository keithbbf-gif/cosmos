# What I would ask a DON

A director of nursing is not a designer. She is the person who will live with the drawer at 2 a.m.

I would rather have twenty minutes with her than a month of finish boards. The Medline-era jobs that failed were the ones where purchasing froze a page and nursing met it on install week.

I am not teaching you to ambush a DON. I am listing the questions I wanted on the table. If she does not have time, you are not ready to buy a floor.

## The room

- How many doubles, how many privates, how many designated bariatric, how many memory?
- Where does the lift stand in a double?
- Which side is the working side, and who decided?
- Where does a daughter sit?
- Where does the overbed park?
- What is the closet now — door, wardrobe, rumor?

If she cannot answer the lift question, walk the floor together. Do not walk it with only the administrator. Administrators see census. DONs see hips.

## The bed

- What fleet is on the floor, and what is coming?
- Low-bed program or not?
- Rails: full, half, none, mixed?
- Who assesses zones, and when did they last?
- Which mattress, which overlay?
- Who owns the gap if we change a headboard?

If the answer to the last is “the mill,” I leave the headboard off.

## The body

- Who cannot stand from the current chairs?
- What seat heights has therapy asked for?
- What is the real weight census, not the brochure?
- Who eats in the room, and why?

The last question is a dining spec. The overbed count at noon is the number.

## The wipe

- What do you spray on a Tuesday?
- What did you spray last outbreak?
- Will you test a scrap, including an edge?
- Who owns condemned vinyl?

If infection control is a different person, I want them in the same twenty minutes. A DON who says “we use bleach” and an EVS supervisor who says “we use the quat” is a finish failure waiting to happen.

## The lock

- Who steals, in the building’s real story — roommate, visitor, staff, phantom?
- Who should open the drawer?
- Memory-care hardware: care plan or catalog?

I will not suggest a lock she does not want. I will not hide one she does.

## The channel

- Is Medline a contract, a habit, or a person you trust?
- Who is the rep, and will they walk the leftover with us?
- Who installs, and do they work around lunch?
- Where is the attic stock from the last buy?

If the rep will not walk the leftover, the rep is a punchout.

## The punch list

- Who signs?
- How fast can we recut a rod?
- What did the last vendor get wrong?

The last question is the specification. People will tell you if you let them complain.

## What I would not ask

I would not ask her favorite color first. I would not ask her to pick a cathedral arch. I would not ask her to bless a JPEG on a phone in a hallway while a call light is going.

I would not ask her to violate a GPO if that is her job to keep. I would ask her to write the inches the GPO page left out.

## After she answers

I write the answers on the drawing. If purchasing wants a different nightstand, the drawing still has the lift. The lift is the DON’s sentence. It stays.

This is educational, not a script for a sales call. If you are a family, you can ask a shorter set: can she stand from the chair, can she reach the rod, is there a clock, is the curtain a wall. You do not need a mill to ask those.
