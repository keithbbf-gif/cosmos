# A walk through a real SNF room

This is a composite. It is not Medilodge of Montrose’s room 12, and it is not Chenal Heights, and it is not a resident. It is the room I kept walking in the era: a double, a corridor, a package that almost fit.

I will not decorate it.

## The door

Thirty-two or thirty-six. The wardrobe in the carton wants thirty-six and a straight shot. The plant gives you thirty-two and a turn. Already the crown is a rumor.

The bathroom is shared or it is not. The toilet is a later essay. The grab bars are not ours. The nightstand will try to live in the swing. I move it on the drawing before I move it on the floor.

## The leftover

Two beds on old centers. A track in the ceiling. Eighty square feet each if the plan is kind. The leftover for wood is a strip.

I put the working side on the aisle and the bedside on the wall. Families will hate the photograph. The lift will not.

The overbed has no parking place in the JPEG. I steal a sliver at the foot and admit the sliver is also a trip.

## The bed

A device from a different page. Low or not. A rail up or a rail gone. A mattress that may not be the mate.

I look for daylight at the rail and at the headboard. I do not perform a test. I write “assess” on the punch list if the daylight is a canyon. I keep our headboard off the system unless the bed-maker wants it.

The plug is behind where a wardrobe wants to sit. I move the wardrobe or I move the bed. I do not pinch the cord.

## The curtain

I pull it through the bend. It sticks. The panel is short. The roommate’s face is available at the toilet. The crown we shipped will catch the mesh if I leave the cathedral on. I cut the cathedral. That is Thursday.

## The wardrobe

A rod at a residential height. A seated person cannot hang a shirt. I write a lower rod. I write a divider because this is a double and the clothes were becoming communal. I write an anchor because the box is tall and the floor is slick.

The interior is finished because I have smelled the other kind.

## The chair

Seventeen inches of fashion or twenty inches of function. I sit. I stand. If I need a lunge, the chair is a restraint. I look at the arm cap. I look at the cleanout. I look at the vinyl map.

The visitor sits on the bed or on a folding chair in the closet. F917 imagined better. The leftover did not.

## The bedside

One drawer, a sealed box, a top with a lip. A lock the DON has not decided. I leave the lock off the default and on the options. The top is not glossy enough to be a glare under the only lamp.

A cup ring from the last package is still in the laminate of the condemned pile in the courtyard. That pile is the spec.

## The clock

There is a television. There is not a clock. I put a surface where a clock can live that is not the overbed. It is a small sentence in a tag and a large sentence in a night.

## The sequence out

Back to the corridor. The station is a bunker with a corbel. I can or cannot see a face from a wheelchair. The day room has a sofa I will not sit in twice. The dining room’s apron will hit a footplate at noon. The overbeds will be busy.

This is one room. The floor is a hundred of them and a staff ratio. Furniture cannot staff the floor. Furniture can stop stealing the leftover.

## Why a composite

Because a named room would be a person. Because a named invoice would be a boast. Because the era was a route, not a showroom.

If you walk a house on the public project list, or a house like them, and you can still see these fights, the series has done its job. If you only see cherry, start again at the door.
