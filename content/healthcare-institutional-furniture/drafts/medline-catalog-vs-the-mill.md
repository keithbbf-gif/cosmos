A catalog is a face. A mill is a smell.

I say that without romance. The punchout smells like a warehouse and a PDF. The mill smells like stain and a spray booth. The object on the floor is a marriage, and the logo on the drawer is the face the DON already had a number for.

This draft is the last channel hour. The first one explained the route. This one explains the **two documents** that never quite match.

## What the page can carry

A title. A JPEG. Three finishes. A vinyl name that will change. A width. A price I will not invent. A lead time written by a hopeful person. A logo stack.

It cannot carry a leftover. It cannot carry a lift. It cannot carry Thursday’s two inches. It cannot carry the wipe you actually use. It cannot carry who holds the key. It cannot carry the working side. It cannot carry a sprinkler clearance. It cannot carry whether the headboard joins the bed.

If you treat the page as a shop drawing, you will build the JPEG. The JPEG is lit from the aisle. The resident lives at the pillow. Those are different rooms.

A punchout screenshot forwarded as a spec is how the worst drawings I ever saw got born. A screenshot has no rod height. It has a color word that will not exist in two years. It has a smile in the marketing copy that I am not allowed to write here because I banned it.

## What the mill drawing can carry

Rod height. Interior finish. Edge radius. A note that the headboard does not join the bed. A lock option. A crown that can be cut. A finish system, not a color word. A scrap to test. A sealed-box note. A pull that does not catch a cuff. An anchor for a tall box on a slick floor. A divider in a double so clothes are not communal.

It cannot carry the GPO memory. It cannot carry the truck. It cannot carry the account. It cannot carry the Tuesday visit that makes a DON trust a face. It cannot invoice a chair on the same statement as a mattress cover.

If you treat the mill drawing as a punchout, you will lose the bid and keep the inches. Invisibility inside a page is how a lot of honest wood reached buildings that would never have found Warren, Arkansas on a map. Losing the inches to win the bid is how the same wood becomes a complaint.

## The honest marriage

The grown-up Medline-era job used the page as a **door** and the drawing as a **contract**. The rep opened the door. The mill put inches on paper. Purchasing paid the page. Nursing signed the drawing’s punch list.

The dishonest job used the page as the contract. The mill found out the rod was wrong when the daughter hung a dress on a chair.

Bradley Brand could live in the honest marriage. We could not live in a JPEG. A lot of wood still got into buildings because someone, once, asked for the drawing.

The marriage has a sequence. Door first is fine. Contract before freeze is not optional. A page that freezes at midnight because the capital meeting was yesterday is a flood procedure wearing a new-plant job. I will do a flood. I will not pretend a flood is a design.

## House brand ethics

If the drawer says Medline and the box says Warren, both sentences can be true. The face is the warranty phone. The mill is the repair phone, if the face calls.

I will not pretend that arrangement is a fraud. It is how a small shop reaches a 121-bed house in Genesee County. Medline’s public story — 1910 A.L. Mills, 1966 Jim and Jon Mills in Evanston, later Northfield — is the story of a face that got very good at being in the building. A mill rides that face or it does not ride at all.

I will pretend it is a fraud if the face promises a wipe the mill never saw, or a capacity the mill will not sign, or a “healthcare grade” that names no test. Adjectives are the page’s vice. Inches are the drawing’s job.

Educational duty: **read the label as a route.** Call the face for a part. Call the mill if you can find it and the face will not. Do not call a dead 800 number on a 2009 sticker and decide the object is trash until you have smelled the box. A sealed maple box with a missing pull is a pull. A swollen particleboard core with a peeled edge is oatmeal. The logo will not tell you which.

Three ways the work showed up, said generally, not as a confession of a particular label: a named mill on a special buy; a house brand with the distributor’s name and the mill’s joinery; a private-label run for a chain that froze a drawing. Medilodge-as-operator and the Arkansas and Texas names on our public list are the kind of customers who could freeze a drawing. The page might never have been public. The inches still had to be true.

## When to skip the page

New plant. Memory care. Bariatric room type. A leftover that is a sliver. A bed fleet change. A wipe you cannot name. A headwall the furnishings page never saw. A double whose closet is a rumor.

Those jobs need a drawing first. The page can still order the chairs if the inches are already written.

When to use the page: twenty nightstands after a pipe burst, same leftover, same rod, attic stock exists. Four chairs after a wound-care program changed the foam. A pull from the hide you bought like a grown-up. That is what a replenishment channel is for. Gloves taught the house that route. Furniture may borrow it for replacement. Furniture may not borrow it for a building.

## What a JPEG hides on purpose

Grain direction that will not exist in the lot you receive. A chair that has no one standing up from it. A double photographed as a single. A working side with a plant instead of a lift. A curtain that is open so you cannot see that the track dies short of the toilet. A clock the stylist brought and took home. A family recliner that will win the leftover after install week.

I do not hate photographers. I hate using their work as a dimension. If the only document in the job folder is a JPEG, the job folder is not a job folder. It is a mood.

Shop knowledge: I have been asked to “match the picture.” I can match a finish system within a lot. I cannot match a lifestyle. The lifestyle is a daughter, a lift, and a bleach bottle. None of those fit in the rectangle.

## Finish system versus color word

The page says cherry. The mill has to say which film, which sheen, which edge, which interior, which test scrap. Cherry as a color word will change when the laminate lot changes. A finish system that will still exist is how a floor stays kin. Close in a double is a roommate story.

Vinyl is worse. Programs change suppliers. The chair dies when the program changes and the hide is gone. Write the grade and buy attic stock, or accept a floor that becomes a sample board.

I will not name a wipe as approved. I will keep saying: test an edge. The bleach draft is the long version. The page will not do that test for you.

## The Warren sentence

This is the one brand sentence I will allow.

Bradley Brand is a hardwood shop in Warren, Arkansas, with a public story that includes healthcare FF&E and a project list that includes Medilodge of Montrose and the Arkansas and Texas houses named on that record. We were a mill in a channel. We were not the channel. Quality Healthcare Furniture was a face we used when the customer was the facility and not the kitchen. The catalog was often someone else’s face.

If that sentence sounds like a brochure, strike it and keep the inches. The inches are the work.

## What I want a later editor to keep

Do not turn this draft into a vendor comparison. Do not add SKUs. Do not add a CTA. Do not add a photograph of a resident. Keep the two documents on the table. Keep the marriage honest.

The page sells sameness. The mill sells a rod. A building needs both. A series that only loves the mill is a craft blog. A series that only loves the page is an ad. This folder is supposed to be neither.

If you are holding a piece from the era and you can still see both documents in it — a face on the sticker, a rod height that was written by a human — the marriage held. If you can only see a logo, start over at the leftover. The leftover was never on the page.
