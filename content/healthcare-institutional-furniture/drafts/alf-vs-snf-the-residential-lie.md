# ALF vs SNF, the residential lie

Assisted living sold a hotel. Then the guests got old in it.

A skilled-nursing house is a CMS machine with tags and a bed that is a device. An assisted-living house is a state-licensed residential story with a dining room that photographs like a brunch. The furniture catalogs followed the story. The hips did not.

I have specified both. The lie is not that ALF wants to look like a house. The lie is that **a house chair will remain a house chair** when the building’s census quietly becomes a nursing census.

## Two licenses, one body

A person does not change species when they change licenses. Sit-to-stand, wipe, flame, and a rod they can reach do not care whether the surveyor is CMS or a state ALF team.

What changes is the **excuse**. A SNF has F917. An ALF has a brochure. The brochure will let you buy a 17-inch skirted sofa longer. The hips will not.

The modern assisted-living industry is a late-twentieth-century product. The 1990s and 2000s built a lot of it: apartment-like units, a lobby that wanted to be a hotel, a dining room that wanted to be a restaurant, a memory wing that wanted a softer name than locked. States licensed it. CMS did not adopt it as a certified SNF just because the residents were old. That split is the whole furniture problem. One body. Two books.

42 CFR Part 483 applies to certified SNFs and NFs. It does not automatically apply to every ALF. Do not drag F917 onto a license it does not own and then congratulate yourself for being thorough. Do not ignore sit-to-stand because F917 is not in the state book. The body is still the body.

ADA scoping differs by facility type. A long-term-care sleeping room in the 2010 Standards is not the same sentence as a public dining room in a residential building. I will not do your scoping. I will say that a chair a person cannot leave is a failure in both licenses.

When Bradley Brand’s public list says SNF/ALF, it is naming a furniture job that already knew this. A mill that only heard “residential collection” heard the brochure. A hardwood box from Warren can look like a bedroom; it cannot keep a hotel sofa honest after the hips arrive.

## The hotel package

ALF common spaces want a lobby chair that looks like money. Deep seat. Low. A fabric you cannot wipe. A coffee table. A lamp with a shade that is a fire and a glare.

That package is how you sell a daughter on move-in day. Ninety days later the resident is eating in a chair she cannot leave, and the dining room has quietly added a wheelchair table the brochure did not show.

If you are going to buy the hotel package, buy a **second, honest package** for the year the census changes. Attic stock the honest chair. The hotel chair can live in the marketing photos. I am not telling you to lie to the daughter. I am telling you the photograph and the fifth year are different specifications.

Lobby sofas with skirts are lint traps and urine traps wearing a piped edge. The chair draft already refused skirts. I will refuse them in a lobby that old people actually sit in. A lobby that is only a marketing room can have whatever the photographer wants. Admit which lobby you are buying.

Coffee tables at shin height are fall lines. In a SNF I would not specify one in a day room without a reason. In an ALF the reason is the rendering. A pedestal that a walker can straddle is kinder than a four-leg ranch table that catches a cane. I will not decorate a coffee-table program. I will ask who is going to stand up from the sofa after they have used it.

Dining is where the lie shows first. ALF dining wants banquettes, windsor chairs, a wood top with a live edge story. Banquettes trap hips. Windsor stretchers catch feet. A live edge is a wipe fiction. The SNF dining draft already asked for a table you can leave and an apron that does not hit a knee. Those sentences do not expire because the license is friendlier.

## Apartments vs. rooms

ALF apartments can have real closets. That is a gift. Use it. Do not add a wardrobe that becomes a tip. Do add a chair a person can stand from, a bed that may become a device without warning, and a table that is not a coffee table at shin height.

A real closet still wants a rod a seated person can use. The closet rule is CMS language, and the body does not care. A walk-in that only a standing adult can use is a hotel closet. Hotel closets are how you get a chair full of clothes.

Kitchenettes are a wipe and a burn. Residential appliance packages are how you buy a fire. I am not your kitchen designer. I am telling you the nightstand logic applies to the cabinet under the microwave: sealed boxes, radiused corners, a pull you can use. An open particleboard edge under a sink is soup. A tiny round knob on a dish drawer is a residential insult we already named in the ADA draft.

Beds in ALF apartments start as consumer objects and become devices. A residential queen with a decorative rail is a Zone conversation waiting for a hospital mattress. The 2006 FDA guidance includes private homes in the settings. If you are a family taking a bed home, you are in that sentence. If you are a facility putting a hospital bed into an apartment because the person declined, you are in a system conversation. Do not mix a used rail, a used deck, and a foam from a different page. The used-furniture draft is the long version. The lie is thinking the apartment will never need the system.

Bathrooms in ALF units are where the hotel dies. Grab bars that were specified as “decorative towel bars” are not grab bars. Thresholds that were specified as hotel elegance are falls. A mill that sells a pretty vanity into a bathroom that cannot take a wheelchair has sold a costume. Measure the door before you fall in love with the quartz.

## When ALF becomes SNF without moving

Memory care inside an ALF is often a SNF job with a softer name. Bariatric census is a SNF job. A rehab-heavy ALF is a SNF job. If the building has a lift and a med cart and a shower chair, stop buying hotel sofas.

The Medline channel was weaker in some ALFs because the buyer was a designer, not a DON. Designers are not the enemy. Designers who will not sit in the chair and stand up are the enemy. A designer who will sit, stand, wipe the arm, and ask who holds the key is a colleague. I have worked with both. I will not invent a conversation. I will say the floor tells you which one signed.

Skilled beds added to an ALF campus — a “health center,” a “care suite,” a licensed SNF wing off the courtyard — are how a hotel chair inherits a tag it was never built for. I have seen the condemned pile. It is always the sofa. The sofa looked like money in year one and like a reservoir in year six. The wood chairs that could take a new cover survived. The foam sculptures did not.

Personal belongings are furniture too, in both licenses. A resident’s own recliner from a daughter’s garage is not a specification failure. It is a rights object until it becomes a fire, a tip, or a blocked egress. The OBRA draft already named that fight for SNFs. ALF houses have the same fight with less federal language and more brochure language. The recliner is still heavy.

## Fire and the sprinkler story

ALF occupancies and SNF occupancies can sit in different 101 chapters. Health-care occupancy and residential board-and-care occupancy are not the same sentence. Sprinklers, furnishings, and the E1537 heat-release sentence may differ. Do not copy a SNF chair spec into an ALF without asking the AHJ, and do not copy an ALF hotel sofa into a SNF because the owner likes the look.

The 117 federal floor still exists for upholstered furniture in the consumer sense — TB 117-2013 as 16 CFR part 1640 since June 25, 2021. Occupancy extras still exist. The fire draft is the long version. A sofa that met a smolder floor has met a smolder floor. It has not thereby met the Life Safety Code your fire marshal is holding.

Cubicle curtains show up the day the ALF double or the “companion suite” appears. Companion suites are doubles with better marketing. The curtain draft still applies. A hotel drape on a cubicle track is a flame story someone hoped would pass because the print was damask.

Mattresses have their own book. Do not put a chair’s 117 label on a mattress. Do not put a hotel pillow-top into a hospital-bed deck and call it homelike. Homelike is a direction. It is not a foam.

## Channel notes

A punchout “senior living” page that mixes ALF and SNF without saying which license is a costume. Ask which survey book the piece has to survive.

The Medline-era furnishings channel was built for a DON who already bought gloves. ALF purchasing often ran through a designer, an owner’s spouse, or a hospitality vendor who had never heard of a cleanout. That is not a moral ranking. It is why the same mill could ship an honest maple box into a SNF and a skirted lounge into an ALF in the same month and feel like two different companies.

Special-buys for an ALF chain that later added skilled beds inherited a hotel chair. I have seen the condemned pile. It is always the sofa.

Named geography on the public project list — Tuscany Village, Pearland; Chenal Heights; Baywind Village, League City — sits in the same senior-living world as the SNF jobs. I will not invent which of those buildings were ALF on the day we were there. I will say the furniture job does not care about the brochure the day the lift arrives.

## The honest sentence

Write: “This building will get older than the rendering.” Then specify sit-to-stand, wipe, and rod height as if the license might change. If the owner refuses, you have a record of who chose the brochure.

The residential lie is not that people want a house. It is that a house can be bought as a fabric. A house is a height, a reach, and a way to leave a chair.

## Walkthrough

I sit in the lobby chair and stand without a lunge I will not have at 86. I look at the dining chair the same way. I open the apartment closet from a chair. I look at the bed and ask whether it is still a consumer object. I look at the memory wing and ask whether the hardware is a care plan or a finish. I look for the second, honest chair. If there is no second chair, the fifth year will invent one in a panic, and panic furniture is how you buy the wrong height twice.

If the only honest chair is in the marketing closet, the building is a photograph.
