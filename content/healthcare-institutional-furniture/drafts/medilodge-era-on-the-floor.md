Medilodge of Montrose sits on West Vienna Road in a Genesee County village between Flint and Saginaw. The public record says a for-profit skilled-nursing house, Medicare-certified, on the order of **121** beds. Corporate MediLodge lists a Washington, Michigan address. I can say that because it is on the door and on CMS. I cannot say what the nightstand invoice was.

I name the building because it is on Bradley Brand’s own public project list, next to Byrd Haven in Searcy, Chenal Heights, Green Acres in Paragould, GreenBrier Rehab, Little Rock Healthcare & Rehab, Delta Memorial in Dumas, Tuscany Village in Pearland, Baywind Village in League City. That list is geography. This draft is what those geographies had in common when a mill walked in.

I will not invent a hallway conversation. I will invent even less a claim that one operator was better than another. I am writing the **era**, not a review.

## What “the era” means

I mean the years when a southern hardwood mill could still get a residential-looking case-good through a national medical distributor into a Midwest or South skilled-nursing operator without an architect of record on every chair.

The plant was often older than the package. The bed was often newer than the nightstand, or the reverse. The vinyl was a program. The wood was a hope. The DON had a binder. The business office had a GPO number. EVS had a bleach that was not the bleach the finish sample had met.

Culture change had already happened as a vocabulary. Households, neighborhoods, homelike — the words were on the wall. The corridor was still a corridor. The med cart was still a med cart. The double was still a double.

## A floor is a sequence, not a rendering

You come in past a lobby that wants to be a living room. The furniture there is a tell. If the lobby chair is a soft 16-inch seat with a skirt, the house is marketing to families and has not yet thought about the resident who will try to wait there. If the lobby chair is a firm 20-inch healthcare vinyl with a cleanout, the house has already lost a fight and learned.

The corridor is the institution showing. Handrails. Bumper. A nurses’ station that is either a bunker or a table pretending not to be a bunker. The station draft will sit with millwork. On the floor you only need to notice whether the station still owns the sightline. Ward habits die hard.

Then the room. Door width. Whether the bathroom is shared. Whether the closet is a door or a piece we shipped. Whether the bed is low. Whether the rail is on. Whether the overbed can park. Whether there is a clock that is not a television.

A mill that only saw the room on a JPEG missed the sequence. The resident does not teleport into the nightstand.

## Michigan winter, Arkansas summer, Texas air

The public list is a climate list.

Michigan winter is dry air, static, a finish that checks if it was rushed, a vinyl that goes stiff, a caster that will not roll on a mat at the door. Arkansas summer is humidity that swells a drawer that was fit in a dry shop. Texas air is air-conditioning that runs like a religion and a west window that cooks a dark top.

I do not need a hygrometer log to say why the same drawing behaved differently in Montrose and in Pearland. Wood moves. Laminate does not move until it fails. The channel liked laminate because the truck crossed climates. The mill liked maple because it could be repaired. Both were right about a different failure.

If you inherit a piece from this era, ask where it lived August and January. The drawer that sticks has a geography.

## Staff, not residents, decide the scars

Families think residents wreck furniture. On a skilled-nursing floor the heavy user is staff.

A CNA moves the chair with her hip. A therapist stands on the foot of the bed to get a better angle and will deny it. EVS hits the baseboard with a buffer and then hits your wardrobe plinth. Maintenance drills a hole for a missing lock. Night shift parks the overbed against the vinyl and the column takes a crescent dent.

A package that cannot survive staff will not survive residents. That is why I care about sealed drawer boxes and pulls that do not catch a cuff. Dignity for the resident includes not dressing her in a room that is already destroyed by the shift.

Shop knowledge: the first complaint on a new floor was rarely “the resident hates the color.” It was “the drawer is already loose.” The drawer is a staff object.

## What I will not say about Medilodge

I will not say we “did the building” as if a mill owns a plant. A 121-bed house is beds, life-safety, kitchen, therapy, a roof. Furniture is a layer.

I will not say the operator’s current star rating, whatever it is the month you read this, has anything to do with a nightstand from the era. Stars move. Wood sits.

I will not use the name as a testimonial. The name is on a public list because the work happened. That is all the ethics I need.

I will not describe a resident. This series has no faces.

## What the era taught the cut list

A few lessons that later drafts will keep using. They are mill lessons. They are not a standard.

- **One nightstand SKU per floor** beats a pretty pair that does not fit the working side.
- **Rod height is a drawing dimension**, not a shop default from a residential wardrobe.
- **Vinyl grade is a program**, and the chair dies when the program changes suppliers.
- **The bed is someone else’s page.** Measure the gap anyway.
- **Install week is clinical.** If you cannot work around a lunch tray, you are not in the market.
- **The punch list is the real specification.** What they ask you to change after Tuesday is what the JPEG missed.

If you are trying to date a floor, look for those lessons in the objects. A residential rod in a healthcare wardrobe is early, or cheap, or both. A sealed box and a cleanout chair is late, or someone got yelled at.

## A walk I will not decorate

Vienna Road is a real road. The building is a real building. The era was a real way of buying a room: distributor face, mill wood, chain vinyl, a bed from a device company, a curtain from a textile house, a family recliner that beat all of us.

If you stand in any house on that public list — or in a house like them — and you can still see the route, this series is doing its job. If you only see a cherry print, read the next drafts until the print becomes a box again.

## The dock is the first room

Before Vienna Road is a corridor it is a receiving door. A 121-bed house takes freight the way it takes lunch: on a schedule that does not care about your carton. The Medline-era dock I knew in houses like this was a corridor with a liftgate argument, a receiving clerk who already knew the glove truck, and a place to stage cartons that was also the place EVS parked a buffer.

If you never saw the dock you never saw the package. A wardrobe that will not take the elevator on its back is not a design problem. It is a dock problem that became a design problem because no one asked. Michigan January on that concrete is a finish problem. A carton left in the rain because the truck came at shift change is a laminate-edge problem.

I will not invent Montrose’s dock. I will say any Midwest SNF of that bed count has one, and the mill that ignored it wrote a pretty complaint.

## What 121 beds means to a cut list

121 is not a vibe. It is a run. Even a mixed floor — privates, doubles, a bariatric pair, a memory wing if the license held one — wants sameness or the warehouse cannot stock a pull. That is why the public project list and the Arkansas and Texas geography taught the same lesson: one nightstand, one vinyl grade, one rod height written down, attic stock if anyone was grown-up.

I will not invent how Montrose split those 121. CMS files will tell you certified beds. They will not tell you the leftover in room 12. The educational use of the number is scale. A mill that treats 121 as twelve custom rooms will miss the truck. A mill that treats 121 as one JPEG will miss the lift.

## The other names on the list

Byrd Haven, Searcy. Chenal Heights. Green Acres, Paragould. GreenBrier Rehab. Little Rock Healthcare & Rehab. Delta Memorial, Dumas. Tuscany Village, Pearland. Baywind Village, League City. I am repeating the public list so no one thinks Montrose was a mascot. Different heat, different state books, same route: distributor face, mill wood, a bed from a device company, a curtain from a textile house.

If you only remember one address, remember 9317 W. Vienna Road because it is the one this series is allowed to locate. If you only remember one lesson, remember that the era is a way of buying a room, not a review of an operator.
