# Headwalls and overbed light

A headwall is not furniture. It is a **vertical dock** for oxygen, vacuum, power, nurse call, and a light that will glare on a glossy top.

A mill that ignores it will put a wardrobe on the only outlet and a headboard over the only gas outlet. I have moved both. I have also been asked to build a “wood headwall” that was a panel with holes. Holes in a gas wall are not a finish.

## The hospital leftover

Headwalls grew up in acute care. Nursing homes inherited a cheaper cousin: a few outlets, a call, a light, sometimes gases, sometimes a strip that is trying to look like a hotel sconce.

F584 wants adequate and comfortable lighting, resident control where feasible, less glare. A hard overbed fluorescent with no dim and a high-gloss nightstand is a glare kit. A lamp on the bedside is a belonging and a tip and a bulb EVS will not dust.

Write the light as a program: ambient, task, night. Furniture either helps or fights. A tall wardrobe that shadows the only reading light is a fight.

## Power is a leftover

Beds want a plug. Air surfaces want a plug. Chargers want a plug. A lift may want a plug. The room has two.

If the wardrobe eats one and the bed eats the other, the family lamp is a power strip and a fire story. Specify outlet locations on the furniture plan. I have cut a notch I hated because the alternative was a cord under a door.

Oxygen tubing is a trip. Furniture feet should not be a snag garden. Casters should not roll over a hose as a habit.

## The decorative headboard again

A wood panel that covers the headwall is how you buy a service call and a Zone 6. If the headwall has to be reached, the panel has to come off without a millwright. If the panel is furniture, keep it off the gases.

Matching the nightstand is not a reason to cover a valve.

## Cubicle track, sprinklers, TVs

The ceiling is a political convention. Track, sprinkler, TV bracket, curtain mesh, a smoke detector that was already there. Furniture that wants height — a tall wardrobe, a tall headboard — has to sit in that convention.

I measure the ceiling politics before I freeze a crown. Thursday’s two inches usually live here.

## Channel notes

Medline-era furnishings pages did not include the headwall. Facilities pages sometimes did. The split is the interface problem again.

A special-buy wood package that never saw the electrical drawing was a pretty fight. Get the electrical drawing.

## Walkthrough

I look at the head of the bed before I look at the wood. How many plugs. Where the tube goes. Whether the light is a slap. Whether a panel is covering a service. Whether the wardrobe is a shadow.

If the room is dark at the pillow and bright at the aisle, the lighting program is for staff. F584 imagined the resident too.
