# Common myths

A myth in this trade is usually a compression that made a punchout easier.

I will not close with a recap list. I will treat each myth as a small room. The long versions live in the other drafts. If you only copy this page you will still have more than an adjective, which is the point.

## Homelike is a finish

No. Homelike is a CMS direction in 42 CFR 483.10(i) and Appendix PP, F584: an environment as close to a private home as possible, personal belongings to the extent possible, furniture that is not the institutional leftover, a chair, a clock, a closet you can reach. A cherry print can be part of that. It cannot be the whole of that.

OBRA 1987 made the word federal. The industry heard a stain. I have seen bid language that said “homelike package” and then specified a 17-inch lounge chair and a rod no seated woman can use. The word on the page was decoration. See the OBRA and F584 drafts.

## Healthcare grade means it passed a test

No. It means a catalog needed an adjective. Ask which test, which edition, which report. If the answer is a smile and a JPEG, you have a costume. If the answer is a named method and a date, you have a start.

Healthcare grade is cousin to “commercial grade” and “contract grade.” Those words have sold a lot of vinyl. They have not sat in the chair at 2 a.m.

## CAL 117 means the chair is legal in a SNF

No. California Technical Bulletin 117-2013 is a smolder floor. The federal government later adopted that floor in 16 CFR part 1640. NFPA 101 as adopted on the last survey is a different house, with 260 / 261 / ASTM E1537 sentences and a sprinkler tradeoff that is not a furniture slogan. TB 133 is a ghost that still talks in old binders.

A 117 label on a SNF lounge chair is not a permission slip. See the fire draft. Do not update a flame number from memory.

## BIFMA means healthcare

No. BIFMA X5.1, X5.4, X5.5 are mostly office and contract durability. A pass is a pass. It is not a sit-to-stand. It is not a bleach wipe. It is not F917. It is not a SNF occupancy stamp.

I will not insult a BIFMA report. I will not let it stand in for a DON’s sentence about who cannot leave the chair.

## A plus sign means bariatric

No. A plus sign means a thicker stretcher or a wish. Capacity is a number and a test and a door width and a leftover. A bariatric bed is wider. The working side wants more, not less. The toilet plan is a different object. Dropping a standard package into that room because the area table still says 80 or 100 meets the floor and fails the body.

Do not invent a capacity I have not seen on a sheet. Write the number you will sign.

## Rails equal safety

No. Rails are geometry and, depending on use, a restraint. They are also openings. From January 1, 1985, to January 1, 2006, FDA’s files held about 691 entrapment reports: 413 deaths, 120 injuries, 158 near-misses. Zones 1–3 carry a recommended limit smaller than 120 mm. Zone 4 is tighter: smaller than 60 mm. Calling a rail an assist bar does not change an opening.

See the zone draft and the side-rail draft. I will not teach you how to get around a restraint definition.

## Low bed equals no fall

No. Low bed equals a shorter fall and a relocated climb. Mats and nightstands become the program. A cabinet drawn for a mid-height deck looks drunk when the deck is a fall program. A chair that was fine at a mid bed is a different reach when the resident is closer to the floor.

The low-bed draft will sit with the culture. Here the myth is enough: height is a clinical number, not a slogan.

## The mattress width on the box is the gap

No. The edge is the gap. A “36-inch” mattress on a deck that wanted a mate is how Zone 3 is manufactured after the fact. Replacement foam from a different page, a cover that ripped, a body that compressed the edge for a week — those are the 2006 guidance’s warning about replacement components.

Measure the canyon as inhabited, or get the person whose job is measurement. A box label is a shipping number.

## Matching grain is a package

No. Matching heights and a named interface with the bed is a package. Grain is a photograph. Vinyl programs change. Laminate lots change. A replacement bedside two years later will be “close.” Close in a double is a roommate story.

The bed’s plastic end caps will never be your maple. Stop asking them to be.

## Medline made this nightstand

Maybe the face did. Maybe a mill did. Medline’s public story is 1910 A.L. Mills and 1966 Jim and Jon Mills in Evanston, a manufacturer-distributor at a scale that makes a hardwood shop look like a workbench. Furniture rode that face. Read the label as a route. See the catalog-vs-mill draft.

Do not call the piece “a Medline” the way people call a tissue a Kleenex unless you mean the route. The wood may be Warren, or Indiana, or a polymer shop, or a later copy.

## A sofa makes a day room homelike

A sofa makes a sinkhole unless it is firm, high, and wipeable. Two chairs and a table will let more people leave. F584’s uncomfortable-furniture sentence does not expire because the fabric is floral.

I have sat in day-room sofas I will not sit in twice. That is a specification. Homelike that traps a hip is a warehouse with a skirt.

## ADA is a chair with a tall seat

A tall seat that cannot enter the room is a hallway object. Accessibility is a type: door, toilet, leftover, rod, chair, bed, dining surface, operable part. The 2010 Standards are a legal document with scoping I will not fake. A “ADA chair” sticker is not scoping.

If the wardrobe rod is at a residential height and the only accessible object is a tall seat in the corridor, you have specified a hallway.

## Used is thrift

Used wood with a good sealed box can be thrift. A refinished dresser can be the clock surface if it does the job and does not create a new hazard. A used bed with a camping mattress is a system you invented. FDA already knew these devices move from hospital to nursing home to private home. The quilt does not declassify them.

The used-and-refurbished draft will sit with the Facebook listing. Do not buy a gap because it was cheap.

## The punchout price is the price

I will not invent a dollar. I will say the punchout number is wood-plus-memory-minus-Thursday. Add freight, install, a liftgate argument, after-hours, attic stock, haul-away, and the two-inch cut when the sprinkler was a rumor.

If you compare a mill-direct quote to a punchout price, you are comparing different animals. One is wood and finish. The other is wood, finish, memory, truck, and someone to call when the drawer sticks.

## The resident wrecks furniture

Staff are the heavy users. A CNA moves the chair with her hip. EVS hits the plinth with a buffer. Night shift parks the overbed against the vinyl. Maintenance drills a hole for a missing lock. Specify for a hip check and a buffer, not for a quiet sleeper.

Dignity for the resident includes not dressing her in a room that is already destroyed by the shift. The first complaint on a new floor was rarely the color. It was the drawer that was already loose. The drawer is a staff object.

## I can hide a rail with a wooden plank

Then you have joined the bed system. Do not, unless the bed-maker and the assessment say so. A plank is Zone 6 or 7 with good intentions. The 2006 file does not care about your intentions. Decorative is a gap with stain on it.

If a DON asks for a kindness, the kindness may be a low bed, a mat program, a chair she can leave, a clock she can see. The kindness is not a mill panel screwed onto a device.

## 80 square feet is a design

No. 80 per resident in a multiple and 100 in a single are floors in 42 CFR 483.90(e)(1)(ii). They are the smallest rectangle the federal book will still call a bedroom. Furniture lives in the leftover after two bed envelopes, a curtain track, and a lift. A brochure that says “spacious” at 100 is the federal minimum wearing an adjective.

## The old F-tags are gone, so the old problems are gone

No. 2017 renumbered a pile. F252 and F461 became other numbers. F584 and the furniture/closet language that teaching files often map to F917 still talk about chairs, clocks, and reachable hanging space. The building did not become a different building. The binder got new tabs.

Those are enough myths to start a fight in a break room. If a vendor adds a new adjective, treat it as a myth until it has an edition. If the adjective is “solutions,” I already banned the word. Ask for the inch.
