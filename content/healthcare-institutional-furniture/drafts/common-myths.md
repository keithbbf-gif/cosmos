# Common myths

A myth in this trade is usually a compression that made a punchout easier.

I will keep these short. The long versions are the other drafts. If you only copy this page, you will copy a list, and I told you not to close with a list. So I will treat each myth as a small room, not as a bullet.

## Homelike is a finish

No. Homelike is a CMS direction: as close to a private home as possible, personal belongings, a chair, a clock, a closet you can reach. A cherry print can be part of that. It cannot be the whole of that. See the OBRA and F584 drafts.

## Healthcare grade means it passed a test

No. It means a catalog needed an adjective. Ask which test, which edition, which report.

## CAL 117 means the chair is legal in a SNF

No. 117-2013 is a smolder floor. NFPA 101 as adopted is a different house. 133 is a ghost that still talks. See the fire draft.

## BIFMA means healthcare

No. BIFMA is mostly office durability. A pass is a pass. It is not a sit-to-stand, a wipe, or a tag.

## A plus sign means bariatric

No. A plus sign means a thicker stretcher or a wish. Capacity is a number and a test and a door width.

## Rails equal safety

No. Rails are geometry and, depending on use, a restraint. They are also openings. The 2006 file is hundreds of deaths. See the zone draft.

## Low bed equals no fall

No. Low bed equals a shorter fall and a relocated climb. Mats and nightstands become the program.

## The mattress width on the box is the gap

No. The edge is the gap. Replacement foam is how Zone 3 is built on Tuesday.

## Matching grain is a package

No. Matching heights and a named interface with the bed is a package. Grain is a photograph.

## Medline made this nightstand

Maybe the face did. Maybe a mill did. Read the label as a route. See the catalog-vs-mill draft.

## A sofa makes a day room homelike

A sofa makes a sinkhole unless it is firm, high, and wipeable. Two chairs and a table will let more people leave.

## ADA is a chair with a tall seat

A tall seat that cannot enter the room is a hallway object. Accessibility is a type: door, toilet, leftover, rod, chair, bed.

## Used is thrift

Used wood with a good box can be thrift. A used bed with a camping mattress is a system you invented.

## The punchout price is the price

The punchout price is wood-plus-memory-minus-Thursday. Add freight, install, attic stock, and the two-inch cut.

## The resident wrecks furniture

Staff are the heavy users. Specify for a hip check and a buffer, not for a quiet sleeper.

## I can hide a rail with a wooden plank

Then you have joined the bed system. Do not, unless the bed-maker and the assessment say so.

Those are enough myths to start a fight in a break room. If a vendor adds a new adjective, treat it as a myth until it has an edition.
