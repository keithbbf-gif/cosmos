# Mattress fit is a gap

The cheapest way to build an entrapment zone is to buy a mattress on Tuesday that does not know the deck from Monday.

FDA’s 2006 guidance is blunt about components. A bed system is frame, rails, mattress. Replacement parts change openings. Compressible foam changes openings. Articulation changes openings. The Medline-era punchout was equally blunt in the other direction: mattresses are a consumable. Consumables get the glove treatment — price, dock date, a width that is a nickname.

I do not make mattresses. I have to live next to them. A nightstand specified to a 36-inch deck looks drunk next to a 32-inch foam that has slumped. A rail that was honest on the original pillow-top is a canyon on a thin replacement.

## Width is a name, not a measurement

“36-inch mattress” is a trade name. The foam, the cover, the zipper, the way it is stuffed, and the way it is centered on the deck decide the **edge**. Zone 3 lives at the edge.

A cover that is slick will let a person slide toward the rail. A cover that grabs will let a person dent the edge and open a gap anyway. A waffle or air surface that is proud of the deck will change the rail’s lower relationship. A foam that is tired in the middle will crown the edge and then collapse it.

I will not give you a brand ranking. I will give you a procedure: **measure the gap with the mattress that will be on the bed at 2 a.m.**, not the mattress in the cut sheet.

## Length and Zone 7

A short mattress on a long deck is a Zone 7 kit. Pillows get stuffed in the vacancy. Feet find the vacancy. A decorative footboard that was “close enough” becomes a trap.

A long mattress that bunches at the foot is a different Zone 7. Articulation will bunch it more. If the house uses an overlay, you have a third thickness no one measured.

Shop knowledge from rooms I walked: the overlay is the forgotten component. Pressure programs love overlays. Gap programs hate surprises. Write the overlay on the same line as the mattress or admit you are guessing.

## Height and the rail’s lower edge

Zone 2 and Zone 3 care about how high the sleeping surface is relative to the rail’s lower edge. A thicker mattress can close a space or create a new one. A thinner mattress can open a bay that the original set closed.

The 2006 dimensional recommendations assume you know which surface you are measuring from. A tired foam is a different surface than the sample.

Low-air-loss and similar powered surfaces are devices on devices. They have their own hoses, their own power, their own way of failing flat at night. I am not going to specify one. I am going to say that a furniture height chosen for a six-inch foam will be wrong for a powered surface that sits higher and sounds like a fridge.

## The punchout crime

A distributor can sell you a bed from vendor A, a rail kit from a later year, and a mattress from vendor C, all on one statement, all “healthcare.” That statement is not a system.

The honest channel move is a **matched set** from the bed-maker, or a written assessment after a mix. The dishonest move is a JPEG of a quilted top and a width nickname.

If you are a mill being asked to “match the bed,” ask which bed and which mattress. If they cannot name both, you are matching a ghost.

## Covers, wipes, and the edge that dies

Infection control will wipe the cover. Some covers survive. Some coat themselves in a film that changes friction. Some crack at the zipper and become a moisture story and a gap story at once.

A cracked cover is how foam soaks and slumps. A slumped edge is Zone 3. EVS did not mean to design a zone. The spec that said “wipeable” without a replacement interval did.

Write a replacement interval if you are writing a real program. I will not invent the month. The house’s wound-care and infection-control people know when their surfaces die. Ask them, not the punchout.

## What I write next to a bedside

On a teaching drawing I write: “Bedside height and setback assume mattress X on deck Y. If mattress changes, revisit Zone 3 and the seated reach.”

That sentence has saved me from a pretty nightstand that became a cliff next to a thin foam. It will not save you if no one owns the mattress.

The mattress is not furniture. It is the reason furniture looks wrong, and the reason a rail becomes a death. Treat it as a dimension, not as bedding.
