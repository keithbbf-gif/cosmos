# Infection control surfaces

Infection control does not buy furniture. Infection control **forbids** furniture, and then lives with what purchasing bought.

A surface is not “clean” because a catalog says healthcare. A surface is a **chemistry, a seam, a texture, and a person with thirty seconds and a wipe**. If those four do not agree, you have a pretty biofilm.

I am not an epidemiologist. I am a mill who has had a finish fail a wipe it was never shown.

## The wipe is the spec

Ask EVS what they spray. Then ask what they spray when they are busy. Then ask what they sprayed during the last outbreak. Those are three chemistries.

Quats, bleach, hydrogen peroxide, alcohol — they do not treat a conversion varnish, a vinyl, and a raw edge the same. “Cleans easily” is not a test. A manufacturer’s chemical-resistance chart is a start. A chart that does not name **your** product is a costume.

The bleach-and-hardwood draft is the wood half. This draft is the general rule: **the wipe is part of the bill of materials.**

A thirty-second wipe is not a laboratory contact time. Contact time is a label on a bottle. Furniture people who confuse those two sentences will specify a surface that “takes bleach” and then watch EVS flash a rag across it and go. I want a surface that can survive the flash and the bath, because both will happen. The bath is the outbreak. The flash is Tuesday.

I will not name a brand of wipe as approved. I will name the method: the actual product, the actual dilution, the actual dwell, a scrap of the actual finish including an edge and a seam. Leave it. Look at it in a week. That is a test. A verbal “we use bleach” is not a test.

## Seams are the reservoir

Bacteria are not impressed by a JPEG. They like a staple line, a welt, a zipper, a laminate joint, a screw hole, a cup holder that does not drain, a cleanout that is not actually open.

A chair with a thousand welts is a residential button-tuft in a costume. A vinyl without a welt museum, and with a cleanout, is an institutional chair. A wood top with an open pore and a failed edge is a sponge.

I have argued for fewer seams the way I argue for rod height: it is not taste. It is a place a wipe cannot go.

Hardware is a seam family. A cup pull with a crescent that will not drain is a reservoir. A knob with a ring of raw wood behind it is a reservoir. A caster horn that collects hair is a reservoir. Specify a pull you can wipe in one pass, or specify that EVS will not, and then do not act surprised.

The underside is a seam family too. Chairs get tipped. Tables get flipped. A finished face and a raw bottom is a residential habit. Spills go down. A wipe that never sees the underside is a hope.

## Vinyl, PU, woven “healthcare,” and the wet look

Healthcare vinyl is a family, not a saint. Some take bleach. Some crack. Some get sticky and then dirty forever. Polyurethanes can be kinder to skin and meaner to some chemistries. Woven Crypton-class and its cousins can look like a house and still wipe if the program is real — and they can be a lie if someone bought the name and not the finish system.

The old wet-look vinyl was honest about being a slick. Slick is a slide. Matte is a house. Matte shows every wipe streak. There is no free choice.

Foam without a barrier is a reservoir. A barrier that is a garbage bag is a noise and a heat. Specify the barrier as a material, not as a hope.

Temperature belongs here. Facilities first certified after October 1, 1990, live in **71–81 °F**. Vinyl at the top of that band, on a west wall, in a chair a person sits in for two hours, is a different chemistry than a swatch in an air-conditioned showroom. Plasticizer walk, tack, and a cover that never looks clean again are climate stories wearing a SKU name I will not invent.

A moisture barrier that rustles is how a house learns the resident is sitting in a bag. A barrier that is missing is how foam becomes a history. I want the barrier named on the same line as the vinyl. If the upholsterer changed suppliers between the mockup and the floor, the report on the mockup is a cousin, not a twin.

## Laminate, solid surface, phenolic, “wood”

Horizontal surfaces in wet rooms want a chemistry that is not a furniture romance. Solid surface and phenolic have their place on overbeds and work tops. Laminate has its place if the edge is real. Wood has its place if the finish is a system and the house will not bathe it.

Copper and copper-alloy stories (CuVerro and cousins) had a moment. I will not recite an EPA public-health claim I have not opened this week. `[CITE NEEDED: the specific EPA registration language if an editor wants the copper paragraph to carry a number.]` I will say: a kill claim on a dry alloy does not replace a wipe, and an alloy top on a particleboard box is still a box.

Laminate fails at the edge. Always the edge. A field-applied iron-on band that lifts is oatmeal in a month. A pod-applied band with a real adhesive and a sealed joint is a different object. Purchasing that buys “laminate” without buying the edge is buying a date with the core.

Solid surface can take a burnish. It can also look like a laboratory in a room that F584 wants to be homelike. I will not pick your culture. I will pick a seam that can be joined and a sink cutout that does not leave a raw lip. Phenolic is honest and hard and loud. Hard plus loud is F584’s sound paragraph again.

Wood is the bleach draft. The short version: a film that is intact at the edge is a wipe. A film that is a showroom silk is a stain.

## Texture is a fight with homelike

F584 wants texture. Infection control wants a wipe. A deeply embossed “oak” vinyl is a dirt map. A flat vinyl is a hospital. A house that wants both will have to pick which rooms get which, or pick a texture shallow enough that a wipe is not a fiction.

Bedspreads and curtains are textiles with their own flame and wipe stories. The curtain draft will sit with cubicle track. Do not pretend a quilt is a wipeable surface. It is a belonging.

Button-tuft, nailhead, a welt museum — those are residential signals that became “homelike” in a binder. They are also a thousand places a rag cannot go. I have refused them on a SNF chair. I will refuse them again. Texture that is a grain tick is one fight. Texture that is a hole is another.

Soft goods will win some rooms because belonging wins some rooms. Plan a launder or a replace, not a wipe that is a fiction. A bedspread that cannot go to the laundry is a bedspread that will be a survey photograph.

## What the catalog means by “antimicrobial”

Catalogs love a word that sounds like a program. “Antimicrobial,” “inhibits,” a silver story, a copper story — I will not recite a claim I have not opened. `[CITE NEEDED: the specific EPA or manufacturer claim language if an editor wants a named product treated as more than a swatch.]`

What I will say as a mill: a finish additive is not EVS. A treated vinyl that still has a welt museum is still a welt museum. A treated laminate with a peeled edge is still oatmeal. Do not let a word on a page replace a wipe, a seam, and a contact time.

GREENGUARD and its cousins are indoor-air stamps. Useful. Not a kill claim. Not a sit-to-stand. The BIFMA draft will sit with stamps that are not the job. Here: if the only healthcare sentence on the sheet is an air logo and the word antimicrobial, you have a costume.

## Channel notes

Medline is, among other things, an infection-prevention company. That is why their furniture channel inherited a wipe culture. It is also why a punchout page could say “healthcare vinyl” and mean a SKU that existed that quarter.

The good rep brought the chemical chart. The bad rep brought a swatch. Ask for the chart.

A special-buy that froze a vinyl for a chain froze a chemistry. When the vinyl mill changed the formulation, the chairs died in a year. Attic stock is not sentimental. It is infection control.

I will not invent a Medline vinyl name or a Medilodge wipe protocol. I will say the channel taught me that furniture and infection prevention shared a catalog and not always a sentence. A grown-up buy put the wipe on the same page as the finish. A late buy put a healthcare adjective on a residential welt.

Bradley Brand’s hardwood argument only holds if the film can take the wipe the house actually uses. If it cannot, laminate and polymer win, and a mill that loves maple has to shut up. I can shut up.

## What I do not claim

I do not claim a furniture finish sterilizes a room. I do not claim copper replaces a hand. I do not claim a cleanout makes a chair safe for a wound. I claim this: **a surface that cannot take the wipe you actually use is not a healthcare surface**, no matter how many times the catalog says the word.

I do not claim CMS infection-control tags — F880 and cousins in the current SOM — are a furniture code. They are a program. Furniture is a component. Open the SOM you are using. Do not tattoo a teaching-file number onto a 2567 from memory.

## Walkthrough

I ask EVS what is in the bottle today. I wipe an edge, not a face. I look at welts, pulls, cup holders, and the underside. I look for the chair in the courtyard that lost the argument.

If the rag catches, the surface is a reservoir. If the finish blushes, the finish was a sample.
