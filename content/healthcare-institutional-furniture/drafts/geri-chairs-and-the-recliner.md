# Geri chairs and the recliner

“Geri chair” is a nickname that does too much work.

Sometimes it means a high-back recliner on casters with a tray. Sometimes it means a tilt-in-space seating system. Sometimes it means the vinyl thing in the hallway that has become a person’s address. Sometimes it means a family La-Z-Boy that staff hate and the resident loves.

I will not police the nickname. I will police the **job**. If the object is a place a person lives for hours, it is a clinical seating program wearing furniture language. If it is a place a person sits after dinner, it is a chair. If it is a place a person is parked because the building is short a staff member, it is a restraint conversation that this series will not launder.

## The hallway address

The scar I will not decorate: a person in a recliner in a corridor, tray up, television down the hall, because the room is a double and the roommate has visitors.

F584 wants a layout that maximizes independence and does not pose a safety risk. A corridor is an egress. A recliner in an egress is a furniture object doing architecture’s job badly. Dignity tags have opinions about people who live in hallways. I am not the surveyor. I am the mill who was asked to make the hallway “look nicer” around the recliner. I cannot.

If your package includes recliners, write **where they park**. If the answer is the corridor, you do not have a package. You have a staffing plan.

## Tilt, tray, and the slide

A recliner that opens is a different machine than a chair that sits.

Tilt-in-space keeps the hip angle and changes the gravity. Recline opens the hip and invites a slide. A tray can be a table, a restraint, a crumb trap, and a place to clip a mitt. Casters can make the object a transport, which is how you buy a tip and a pinched foot.

I am not going to specify a tilt angle. Therapists and seating clinics exist. What I will say as a furniture person: **a slick vinyl plus a recline plus a tired foam is a slide to the floor**. The catch, the belt, the tray are how houses try to fix a cover and an angle they already bought.

If you are on the punchout, “geri recliner” is not a spec. Write capacity, tilt vs. recline, tray yes/no, caster lock, cleanout, moisture barrier, and who owns the seating assessment.

## The family recliner

Personal belongings are in F584 on purpose. The floral recliner from the living room is a belonging. It is also a weight, a fabric that will not take bleach, a motor cord, a fire story, and a tip story.

I have seen houses write a policy that looks like a ban and then keep the chair because the daughter is louder than the policy. I have seen houses take the chair because a fire marshal was louder than the daughter. Both are the homelike tag doing its fight.

If the chair stays, someone has to own flame, egress, and whether the motor cord is a trip. If the chair goes, someone has to own the grief. Furniture people who pretend this is a swatch comparison are lying.

## Transport vs. lounge

Wheels make a recliner a wheelchair’s cousin. Wheelchairs have a seating culture, a cushion culture, a footrest culture. Geri chairs on casters often have none of that and still get pushed to the dining room.

If you are going to transport people in a recliner, you are in a device and a safety conversation I will not fake. If you are not, take the casters off or lock them so the object stops being a hallway vehicle.

The dining draft will sit with whether a recliner can pull to a table. Usually it cannot. Usually the person eats at the tray. The tray is then the dining program. Be honest.

## Channel notes

Medline-era catalogs mixed “medical recliners,” “clinical recliners,” “geri chairs,” and “lift chairs” in a way that trained buyers to think they were one object. A lift chair is a motor that stands a person. A clinical recliner is often an infusion cousin. A geri chair is often a long-term sit. They share vinyl and a back. They do not share a job.

A mill like ours was rarely in the mechanism. We were in the day-room chair that was supposed to keep people out of the geri chair. When the day-room chair failed sit-to-stand, the geri chair census went up. That is a package problem, not a vinyl problem.

## What I will not call kindness

A tray in the up position all afternoon is not a table setting. A recliner tilted so far that the person cannot see a face is not rest. A hallway of matching recliners is not a living room.

Kindness would be a chair a person can leave, a room they can be in, a table they can get to, and a staff ratio that is not a furniture problem. I can build the chair. I cannot staff the floor.
