# Activity and day room

The day room is where a building pretends it has a living room.

It has a television that is too high, a sofa that is too soft, a piano no one tunes, and a table that is bingo on Tuesday and church on Sunday. If you specify it as a residential great room, EVS will hate you and the residents will slide out of the sofa.

I have built day-room tables and I have argued against the sofa. The sofa is the scar.

## The sofa problem

A sofa is a social object and a transfer disaster. Two people sink toward the middle. The person on the end cannot load an arm that is a pillow. The moisture barrier, if any, is a rumor. The skirt is a lint trap. The cushions walk.

If a house wants a sofa because families photograph it, write a **firm, high, cleanout, healthcare-cover sofa with real arms**, or write two chairs and a table. Two chairs and a table will do more sit-to-stand than a $4,000 sinkhole.

I am not anti-soft. I am anti-hole. A person who cannot stand from the day-room sofa will live in the geri chair. That is a package.

## Mix the seats

A good day room is a **height mix**. Some 19. Some 20. Some 21. A rocker only if it cannot walk and cannot pinch. A recliner only if it has a parking place that is not the egress. A wheelchair parking place that is a place, not a leftover in front of the TV.

One SKU is a warehouse. The channel loves one SKU. The bodies do not.

Weight ratings belong on every object, not only the “bariatric chair” in the corner. The corner chair becomes a stigma. Mix capacities without making a throne that announces a diagnosis.

## Tables that work

Activity tables take craft glue, communion cups, and a 200-pound lean. They want the same radiused corners and honest bases as dining, plus a top that can take a cut from a scissors program.

Height again: wheelchair and chair. Flip-top if the room is also something else. Storage for the bingo blower that is not a trip.

A coffee table in front of a sofa is a shin and a walker fight. If you need a surface, make it high enough to push on or light enough to move. The residential coffee table is a trap.

## Television geometry

The TV is the fireplace. If it is high, necks suffer. If it is the only light, glare on a glossy top becomes F584. If the only chairs that can see it are the sinkhole sofa, you have specified a vision program with furniture.

Write a seating arc, not a sofa and a hope. Leave a wheelchair slot in the arc. Do not put a table in the only path to a chair.

Sound: a hard day room plus a television plus hearing aids is how a building fails comfortable sound while passing a finish inspection.

## Pianos, birds, and the homelike pile

Homelike wants texture and personal objects. Activity wants a birdcage and a pile of dolls. Infection control wants neither.

I will not pick the bird. I will say that **furniture has to survive the program you actually run**. If the program is glue and dirt, specify tops accordingly. If the program is a piano, specify a bench a person can stand from, or do not specify a bench.

Dolls and stuffed animals will live on the chairs. That is a belonging. It is also a cover that will not wipe. Plan a wipe that is not a war.

## Channel notes

Medline-era day rooms were often a dump of leftover resident-room chairs plus a sofa from an office catalog. The leftover chairs were usually the better objects.

A special-buy “residential living room group” was how you got the sinkhole. If you inherit one, the first repair is not a new cover. The first repair is a firm chair someone can leave.

Bradley Brand’s wood tables belong here when the edge is sealed and the base is honest. They do not belong here as a coffee table in front of a walker path.

## Walkthrough

I sit in every type. I stand. I roll a chair to the TV. I listen. I look for the condemned sofa in the courtyard. I look for the recliner in the egress. I look for the coffee table’s bruises.

If I cannot stand, the day room is a waiting room for the hallway.
