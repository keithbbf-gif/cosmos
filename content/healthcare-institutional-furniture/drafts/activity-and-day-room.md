# Activity and day room

The day room is where a building pretends it has a living room.

It has a television that is too high, a sofa that is too soft, a piano no one tunes, and a table that is bingo on Tuesday and church on Sunday. If you specify it as a residential great room, EVS will hate you and the residents will slide out of the sofa.

I have built day-room tables and I have argued against the sofa. The sofa is the scar.

## The sofa problem

A sofa is a social object and a transfer disaster. Two people sink toward the middle. The person on the end cannot load an arm that is a pillow. The moisture barrier, if any, is a rumor. The skirt is a lint trap. The cushions walk.

If a house wants a sofa because families photograph it, write a **firm, high, cleanout, healthcare-cover sofa with real arms**, or write two chairs and a table. Two chairs and a table will do more sit-to-stand than a $4,000 sinkhole.

I am not anti-soft. I am anti-hole. A person who cannot stand from the day-room sofa will live in the geri chair. That is a package.

The skirt is how a residential collection announces itself. EVS cannot wipe a skirt. A void under a skirt is a smell that becomes a chair that lives in the courtyard. I have refused skirts on room chairs. I refuse them twice on a sofa that six people will share.

Cushions that zip are a promise. Cushions that zip and have no barrier under the zip are a reservoir. Ask what is under the cover. If the answer is “foam,” you do not have a healthcare sofa. You have a sponge with a photograph.

## Mix the seats

A good day room is a **height mix**. Some 19. Some 20. Some 21. A rocker only if it cannot walk and cannot pinch. A recliner only if it has a parking place that is not the egress. A wheelchair parking place that is a place, not a leftover in front of the TV.

One SKU is a warehouse. The channel loves one SKU. The bodies do not.

Weight ratings belong on every object, not only the “bariatric chair” in the corner. The corner chair becomes a stigma. Mix capacities without making a throne that announces a diagnosis.

The resident-chair draft already said compressed height is the number. Day rooms are where purchasing forgets that sentence because the JPEG is a lounge. A 16-inch fashion seat in a day room is a hole with a lamp. I have sat in them. I have stood from them with a lunge I will not have at 86. If I need a lunge, the chair is a restraint that no one documented.

Rockers soothe and rockers travel. A rocker that walks toward the television is a bruise machine. A glider with a pinch at the side is a finger story. If the program wants motion, specify the stop and the pinch, or specify a stationary chair and let therapy own the motion.

## Tables that work

Activity tables take craft glue, communion cups, and a 200-pound lean. They want the same radiused corners and honest bases as dining, plus a top that can take a cut from a scissors program.

Height again: wheelchair and chair. Flip-top if the room is also something else. Storage for the bingo blower that is not a trip.

A coffee table in front of a sofa is a shin and a walker fight. If you need a surface, make it high enough to push on or light enough to move. The residential coffee table is a trap.

Craft programs are honest about chemistry. Glue, marker, glitter, a communion spill — the top is a lab bench that wants to look like a kitchen. Laminate with a real edge will take that. Open-pore oak with a showroom silk will not. The bleach draft is the wood half. Here: **the activity calendar is a finish spec**. If Tuesday is glue, do not buy a face that is a stain map.

A table that tips when someone stands from it is a dining problem that moved rooms. People will pull on whatever is in reach. Weight the base or fix it in a way maintenance can undo when the room becomes a service on Sunday.

## Television geometry

The TV is the fireplace. If it is high, necks suffer. If it is the only light, glare on a glossy top becomes F584. If the only chairs that can see it are the sinkhole sofa, you have specified a vision program with furniture.

Write a seating arc, not a sofa and a hope. Leave a wheelchair slot in the arc. Do not put a table in the only path to a chair.

Sound: a hard day room plus a television plus hearing aids is how a building fails comfortable sound while passing a finish inspection.

I will not invent a mounting height as a federal number. Lighting and sound live in F584 as comfortable and adequate, not as a millimetre. `[CITE NEEDED: any AHJ or company television-mounting dimension an editor wants named as policy rather than shop habit.]` Shop habit: if a seated person must tip their head to see the screen, the screen is furniture in the wrong place. If the only lamp is the screen, the room is a cave at 4 p.m. and a glare at noon.

Closed captioning does not fix a shout. A hard box of a room will shout regardless of the caption. Soft objects that EVS can still wipe — a covered panel, a curtain with a named flame story — do more than a louder set.

## The program is the occupancy

A day room is not a bedroom. CMS square-footage numbers — **80** per resident in a multiple, **100** in a single — do not tell you how many chairs this common room can hold. Occupancy, egress, and the adopted NFPA 101 edition tell you that, and the edition moves. I will not tattoo a headcount onto a day room I have not measured.

What I will say: furniture that eats the path is how a living room becomes a trap. A recliner opened into the only aisle is an egress object. A craft cart parked at the door is an egress object. A piano bench behind a walker is a fall.

Write a furniture plan that still works when the room is full. Full is not the brochure photograph with four people and a lamp. Full is bingo, a family, two wheelchairs, and a person who stands in the middle because standing is the program.

Memory-care day rooms add contrast, climb, and a chair that does not look like a toilet if that confusion is real on the floor. The memory-care draft sits with locks and mirrors. Here I only need this: a day room that is all one value — beige chair, beige floor, beige table — is a fall wearing a palette. Homelike wanted calm. Calm that erases edges is a clinical failure.

## Pianos, birds, and the homelike pile

Homelike wants texture and personal objects. Activity wants a birdcage and a pile of dolls. Infection control wants neither.

I will not pick the bird. I will say that **furniture has to survive the program you actually run**. If the program is glue and dirt, specify tops accordingly. If the program is a piano, specify a bench a person can stand from, or do not specify a bench.

Dolls and stuffed animals will live on the chairs. That is a belonging. It is also a cover that will not wipe. Plan a wipe that is not a war.

F584 allows personal belongings to the extent possible. That sentence is why the day room collects a life. It is also why EVS and activities will fight. Furniture people who pick a side and call it taste are lying. Write a wipeable chair and a place for the doll that is not the seat. Write a piano bench with arms you can push, or write no bench and a chair that can live at the keyboard.

Fish tanks and birdcages are program objects with water, seed, and a stand that tips. I am not your infection-control committee. I am the mill who will not build a stand that becomes a climb. If the stand has shelves a person can use as stairs, you have specified a fall in a costume.

## Recliners, geri chairs, and the hallway overflow

Day rooms fail into hallways. The geri-chair draft already named the hallway address. The day-room version is a recliner that would not fit the arc, or a sofa nobody can leave, or a television nobody can see from a wheelchair.

If the package includes recliners, write where they park when they are open. Open is a different footprint than closed. I have seen a house specify four recliners for a room that could hold two open and still keep a path. The other two lived in the corridor and became architecture.

A tilt-in-space chair in a day room is a clinical seating program visiting. It needs a place, a wipe, and a staff member who owns the assessment. It does not need a floral throw that stands in for a flame report. TB 117-2013 / 16 CFR 1640 is a smolder floor, not a permission slip for a quilt.

## Channel notes

Medline-era day rooms were often a dump of leftover resident-room chairs plus a sofa from an office catalog. The leftover chairs were usually the better objects.

A special-buy “residential living room group” was how you got the sinkhole. If you inherit one, the first repair is not a new cover. The first repair is a firm chair someone can leave.

Bradley Brand’s wood tables belong here when the edge is sealed and the base is honest. They do not belong here as a coffee table in front of a walker path.

I will not invent a living-room SKU. I will say the channel treated this room as hospitality because families photograph it. Hospitality is how you buy a sofa with a skirt and a coffee table with a sharp corner. Ask for sit-to-stand on the same page as the fabric name.

Attic stock the vinyl. A day-room chair dies in public. A dead cover in a lobby is a marketing problem and an infection-control problem on the same afternoon.

## Walkthrough

I sit in every type. I stand. I roll a chair to the TV. I listen. I look for the condemned sofa in the courtyard. I look for the recliner in the egress. I look for the coffee table’s bruises.

I watch whether a wheelchair has a place in the arc or only a leftover in the glare. I look at the underside of the activity table. I look for the skirt. I look for the doll occupying the only firm chair.

If I cannot stand, the day room is a waiting room for the hallway.
