# CAL TB 117 and NFPA 101

A flammability label is not a fire program.

The Medline-era binder loved “meets CAL 117.” Sometimes the speaker meant the old open-flame 117. Sometimes they meant **TB 117-2013**, the smolder test California moved to, the one that later became the federal upholstered-furniture floor under 16 CFR part 1640. Sometimes they meant TB 133, the full-scale open-flame test that California later walked away from. Sometimes they meant nothing.

If you specify seating for a skilled-nursing dining room and you cannot name **which test, which edition, and which occupancy**, you are specifying a sticker.

## 117-2013 is a smolder test

California Technical Bulletin 117-2013 is *Requirements, Test Procedure and Apparatus for Testing the Smolder Resistance of Materials Used in Upholstered Furniture*. It is a component / mockup smolder test in the ASTM E1353 family, a cousin of the old UFAC cigarette work. It is **not** an open-flame room test.

Furniture manufactured after January 1, 2015, for the California market had to carry the 117-2013 label language the Bureau published. The federal story: the COVID-19 Regulatory Relief and Work From Home Safety Act (Pub. L. 116-260) made TB 117-2013 a flammability standard under the Flammable Fabrics Act, effective as a federal floor on **June 25, 2021**, now 16 CFR part 1640.

That is a big deal for a sofa factory. It is **not** a healthcare occupancy analysis. A SNF chair that meets 117-2013 has met a smolder floor. It has not thereby met the Life Safety Code your fire marshal is holding.

## NFPA 101 is a different house

NFPA 101, the Life Safety Code, as adopted by a state or a CMS survey, has its own furniture language. Newly introduced upholstered furniture is expected to resist a cigarette ignition (NFPA 260 Class I or NFPA 261 char-length language, depending on the edition and the sentence). In some occupancies, in buildings **without** a sprinkler system, a heat-release test (ASTM E1537, the TB 133 cousin) still shows up: peak heat release and a ten-minute total that people quote as **80 kW** and **25 MJ**. In a fully sprinklered building the open-flame furniture requirement often drops. I am paraphrasing a moving code. **Open the edition your building is surveyed on.**

CMS uses NFPA 101 for life safety in certified houses. The edition has moved. I will not tattoo 2012 or 2015 or 2021 on this page as if it were eternal. Ask the life-safety guy which edition is on the last survey.

## 133 is a ghost that still talks

TB 133 was the California full-scale open-flame furniture test. Specifiers still say “133” the way they say “homelike.” California repealed that regime for its own public occupancies and pointed back at 117-2013. The **test method** lives on in ASTM E1537 and in 101’s unsprinklered sentences.

If a vendor says “meets 133,” ask for the report, the edition, and whether they mean E1537. If they say “we don’t need it, we’re 117,” ask whether the building is sprinklered and which 101 edition applies. Those are not the same answer.

## Mattresses are another book

Mattresses have 16 CFR 1632 / 1633 in the residential world and other documents in the healthcare world. Do not put a chair’s 117 label on a mattress. Do not put a mattress label on a cubicle curtain. Textiles are a stack of books.

The curtain draft will sit with NFPA 701 and the track. This draft stays with upholstered furniture, because that is what a mill and a vinyl program get blamed for.

## What a specifier should write

Write three lines:

1. 16 CFR 1640 / TB 117-2013 (smolder floor for covered upholstered furniture).
2. NFPA 101, **named edition**, furniture ignition / heat-release sentences as they apply to **this occupancy and this sprinkler status**.
3. Any state or AHJ extra.

Then ask for reports, not adjectives.

Do not write “healthcare fire rated.” That is not a test.

## Channel notes

A punchout that says “CAL 117” in 2026 without an edition is a time traveler. A special-buy from 2009 that said “117” might mean the old bulletin. Do not inherit the words without inheriting the report.

I have seen vinyls that met a smolder mockup and chairs that failed a house because the foam was a different foam than the mockup. Component tests are component tests. Ask whether the **built chair** is in the report.

## What I will not do

I will not tell you a floral throw is fine. I will not tell you to skip a report because the building has sprinklers unless the AHJ and the edition say that sentence. I will not turn a label into a sermon.

Fire is a program. Furniture is a component. Keep them in that order.
