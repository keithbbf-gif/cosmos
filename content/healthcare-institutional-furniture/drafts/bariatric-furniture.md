# Bariatric furniture

A plus sign is not a test.

The Medline-era punchout loved “bariatric” as a checkbox next to a chair that had a thicker stretcher and the same 22-inch seat. A person of size is not a thicker stretcher. They are a **width, a capacity, a toilet, a lift, a bed deck, and a dignity problem** if the only chair that will hold them sits in the hallway with a different vinyl.

I have built wider seats and I have refused to stamp a number I could not stand behind. Capacity is a test or it is a wish.

## Capacity is a number you can fail

Write the pounds. Write how they were tested, or write that you do not know. BIFMA has chair tests. They are not a magic healthcare stamp — the BIFMA draft will sit with that — but they are better than a plus sign.

A 250-pound chair is a common residential-contract number. A 350 is a common “healthcare” number. A 500 or 750 or 1,000 is a different frame, a different caster, a different floor load, a different door. Do not mix them in a sentence.

If the house has a bariatric room, the **whole package** has to mean it: bed, mattress, commode, shower chair, recliner, visitor chair that a spouse can use, a toilet that is not a joke. A wide patient chair and a standard bed is how you buy a transfer injury.

I will not tell you which number is “enough.” That is a clinical census and a human conversation. Furniture’s job is to **not fail the people the house already has**. If purchasing bought a 350 because the catalog said healthcare, and the floor has people who need a different frame, the catalog is not the failure. The specification is.

Shop cruelty is not a lab. I will not pretend a mill floor is ISO. I will pretend even less that a thicker apron is a rating. Ask for the failure mode: rack, fastener pull, caster punch-through, a weld that talks. A chair that fails quiet is worse than a chair that fails loud.

## Width is dignity

A wide chair that will not go through the door is a hallway address. A wide chair that will not fit under the table is a tray. A wide chair that is the only different color on the floor is a sign.

Specify door width, elevator, bathroom clearance, and table clearance **before** you congratulate yourself on the seat. The ADA draft will sit with clearances. Here: if the object cannot enter the room, it is not a room object.

Seat width that is honest may look enormous in a rendering. Renderings are not hips.

Arm width is the forgotten dignity. Arms that clamp a person are a restraint conversation wearing oak. Arms that flare so wide the chair will not pass a door are a hallway conversation. I want a width a person can sit in without becoming a project, and a path that chair can take without an audience.

Vinyl color as a code — “the blue one is the wide one” — is a warehouse habit that became a stigma. Mix covers. Attic stock the wide cover the same way you attic-stock the standard one. A dead wide chair is a landfill that still photographs as a diagnosis.

## The room is the first object

A bariatric program that starts with a chair is already late. The room decides whether the chair is furniture or a hallway resident.

CMS still writes resident-room floor as **80** square feet per person in a multiple and **100** in a single. A wide bed in an 80-square-foot double is a leftover problem before it is a seating problem. The working side shrinks. The visitor disappears. The wardrobe becomes a wall. I will not tell you every wide program needs a private. I will tell you that a wide deck in a tight double will eat the aisle you thought you had.

Doors, elevators, and bathroom clearances are architecture. Furniture people who ignore them will specify a chair that lives at the nurses’ station because that is the only floor left. That chair is then a dignity tag and an egress object.

Measure the door. Measure the elevator. Measure the toilet room. Then measure the chair. In that order. A plus sign does not open a 32-inch leaf.

## The bed is the expensive half

Bariatric beds are devices with a capacity and a deck width that will laugh at your standard nightstand. The leftover shrinks. The working side becomes more important, not less. Zone 3 does not get kinder because the mattress is wider; a mismatch still makes a canyon.

FDA’s 2006 hospital-bed guidance still applies to the system: about **691** entrapment reports from January 1, 1985, to January 1, 2006; **413** deaths; **120** injuries; **158** near-misses. A wide deck with a standard mattress, or a wide mattress on a standard deck, is still a gap conversation. Width is not a waiver.

I do not specify those beds. I refuse a nightstand depth that ate the working side on a standard deck, and I refuse it twice on a wide deck.

Low-bed programs and bariatric programs fight. A low wide bed is a lift problem. Write both programs in the same meeting or admit which one won.

The nightstand, the overbed, the wardrobe — they all inherited a 36-inch-class deck. A wider deck makes the bedside a stub and the overbed base a fight. Specify the leftover as inches, not as “we will make it work.” Making it work is how a CNA twists.

## Reinforcing is not a style

People ask for a “heavier look.” Weight that is theater — thick posts, chunky corbels — is not capacity. Capacity is joinery, fasteners, a stretcher that is in the right place, a frame that does not rack, a glides-or-casters story that does not punch through a vinyl floor.

A hardwood mill can build a strong chair. A hardwood mill can also build a heavy weak chair. I have seen both. Ask for the test, the rating, and the failure mode, not the look.

Floors: a small caster under a 500-pound load is a dent. Specify the caster diameter or specify glides and a staff that will not love you.

A stretcher in the wrong place is a footplate fight and a rack. A stretcher that is only a visual bar under the front rail is decoration. I want the load to go to a joint I would sign. If I would not sit and rock and stand from it, I will not put a number on it.

Glides mark. Casters dent. Both fail on a floor that was specified for a 250-pound chair. Tell the flooring person the load. A furniture meeting that never invites flooring is how you buy a wave in the vinyl and a chair that walks.

## Dining, day room, and the visitor

A wide chair that cannot pull to the dining table is an overbed sentence. The dining draft already said apron and base. Repeat it here because purchasing will buy the room chair and forget the meal. Measure the table gap. Measure the base. If the only place the wide chair fits is the end cap by the steam table, you have specified a tray line for one person.

Day-room sofas fail people of size first. A sinkhole that was already a transfer disaster becomes a hole that will not give the person back. Two firm chairs of mixed capacity will do more than one throne sofa.

F917’s visitor-comfort language does not disappear because the resident is large. A spouse who cannot sit in the room will stand in the aisle or sit on the bed. Standing in the aisle is an egress. Sitting on a wide deck you did not rate for two is a device conversation I will not fake. Specify a visitor chair that is not a stacking banquet leftover and not a stigma.

## Channel notes

Punchout “bariatric packages” often meant one chair and a hope. Special-buys for a chain sometimes did it right: a room type with a door, a bed, a chair, a commode. Medilodge-era floors I walked were mixed. I will not assign a grade to a name.

If you inherit a plus-sign chair, look at the frame, not the sticker. Stickers lie. Racked stretchers do not.

Attic stock the vinyl. A wide chair with a dead cover is a wide landfill.

I will not invent a Medline plus-sign SKU or a Medilodge room count. I will say the channel taught buyers to treat “bariatric” as a checkbox because checkboxes photograph and tests do not. A grown-up special-buy asked for door, deck, chair, commode, and a visitor seat in the same paragraph. A late punchout asked for a thicker stretcher and a different vinyl.

Bradley Brand can build a wider hardwood chair. Width is not a religion and it is not a plus sign. If I could not stand behind the rating, I wanted the sentence off the page.

## What I will not do

I will not joke. I will not use a photograph of a person. I will not tell you a BMI. I will not tell you who “counts” as bariatric. That is a clinical and a human conversation. Furniture’s job is to **not fail the people the house already has**.

I will not hide the wide chair in a corner so the brochure stays pretty. I will not paint it a warning color. I will not call a heavy look a program.

If you do not know who the house has, you are not ready to buy a plus sign.

## Walkthrough

I measure the door before I admire the chair. I sit. I stand. I roll the chair to the table and to the toilet-room door. I look at the leftover beside the deck. I look at the caster and the floor. I look at the vinyl for a color code.

If the chair cannot enter the room, it is not a room chair. If the only wide seat is in the hallway, the package is a checkbox.
