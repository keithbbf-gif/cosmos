# Bariatric furniture

A plus sign is not a test.

The Medline-era punchout loved “bariatric” as a checkbox next to a chair that had a thicker stretcher and the same 22-inch seat. A person of size is not a thicker stretcher. They are a **width, a capacity, a toilet, a lift, a bed deck, and a dignity problem** if the only chair that will hold them sits in the hallway with a different vinyl.

I have built wider seats and I have refused to stamp a number I could not stand behind. Capacity is a test or it is a wish.

## Capacity is a number you can fail

Write the pounds. Write how they were tested, or write that you do not know. BIFMA has chair tests. They are not a magic healthcare stamp — the BIFMA draft will sit with that — but they are better than a plus sign.

A 250-pound chair is a common residential-contract number. A 350 is a common “healthcare” number. A 500 or 750 or 1,000 is a different frame, a different caster, a different floor load, a different door. Do not mix them in a sentence.

If the house has a bariatric room, the **whole package** has to mean it: bed, mattress, commode, shower chair, recliner, visitor chair that a spouse can use, a toilet that is not a joke. A wide patient chair and a standard bed is how you buy a transfer injury.

## Width is dignity

A wide chair that will not go through the door is a hallway address. A wide chair that will not fit under the table is a tray. A wide chair that is the only different color on the floor is a sign.

Specify door width, elevator, bathroom clearance, and table clearance **before** you congratulate yourself on the seat. The ADA draft will sit with clearances. Here: if the object cannot enter the room, it is not a room object.

Seat width that is honest may look enormous in a rendering. Renderings are not hips.

## The bed is the expensive half

Bariatric beds are devices with a capacity and a deck width that will laugh at your standard nightstand. The leftover shrinks. The working side becomes more important, not less. Zone 3 does not get kinder because the mattress is wider; a mismatch still makes a canyon.

I do not specify those beds. I refuse a nightstand depth that ate the working side on a standard deck, and I refuse it twice on a wide deck.

Low-bed programs and bariatric programs fight. A low wide bed is a lift problem. Write both programs in the same meeting or admit which one won.

## Reinforcing is not a style

People ask for a “heavier look.” Weight that is theater — thick posts, chunky corbels — is not capacity. Capacity is joinery, fasteners, a stretcher that is in the right place, a frame that does not rack, a glides-or-casters story that does not punch through a vinyl floor.

A hardwood mill can build a strong chair. A hardwood mill can also build a heavy weak chair. I have seen both. Ask for the test, the rating, and the failure mode, not the look.

Floors: a small caster under a 500-pound load is a dent. Specify the caster diameter or specify glides and a staff that will not love you.

## Channel notes

Punchout “bariatric packages” often meant one chair and a hope. Special-buys for a chain sometimes did it right: a room type with a door, a bed, a chair, a commode. Medilodge-era floors I walked were mixed. I will not assign a grade to a name.

If you inherit a plus-sign chair, look at the frame, not the sticker. Stickers lie. Racked stretchers do not.

Attic stock the vinyl. A wide chair with a dead cover is a wide landfill.

## What I will not do

I will not joke. I will not use a photograph of a person. I will not tell you a BMI. I will not tell you who “counts” as bariatric. That is a clinical and a human conversation. Furniture’s job is to **not fail the people the house already has**.

If you do not know who the house has, you are not ready to buy a plus sign.
