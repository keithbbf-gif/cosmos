# BIFMA is not a healthcare stamp

BIFMA is a trade association with a test culture. That culture is mostly **office**. X5.1 for office chairs, X5.4 for lounge, X5.5 for desks, a durability language that a contract buyer already trusts.

A Medline-era punchout that said “BIFMA approved healthcare chair” was speaking a dialect. BIFMA does not approve a nursing home. BIFMA publishes test methods. A lab can run them. A chair can pass. A SNF can still fail the chair for height, wipe, flame, or a survey tag that never heard of X5.1.

I like tests. I do not like stamps that launder a job.

## What a pass is

A BIFMA seating test is a story about loads, cycles, and a failure definition. It is a good story if you want to know whether an arm will walk off in an office. It is a partial story if you want to know whether a CNA can stand a person from the chair three times a night, whether bleach will open the vinyl, whether the seat height is 17 inches of fashion.

If a vendor has a report, read the **standard, the edition, the product, and the pass/fail**. If they have a logo in a catalog and no report, they have a costume.

Healthcare furniture sometimes shows up in BIFMA’s world as lounge or as a special test. I will not fake a “BIFMA healthcare” standard number I cannot pin this week. `[CITE NEEDED: any current BIFMA healthcare-specific standard or white paper an editor wants named.]` Until that pin, treat a BIFMA mention as **office durability language**, then add the SNF list yourself.

A pass is also a date. Editions move. A report from 2004 on a chair whose foam changed in 2011 is a cousin. Cousins are useful. They are not the chair on your dock. I want the model, the cover, and the year to match, or I want the sentence to say they do not.

Cycle counts impress a binder. They do not impress a hip that cannot flex. A chair can survive a hundred thousand office sits and still be a hole for a person who needs a 20-inch compressed front edge. Durability is not geometry. Geometry is the SNF job BIFMA did not write.

## What the letters actually name

BIFMA is the Business and Institutional Furniture Manufacturers Association. Institutional in that title is closer to office and campus than to a certified nursing home. The word is a trap for a DON who hears “institutional” and thinks CMS.

X5.1 is the office-chair book people mean when they say “the chair is BIFMA.” X5.4 is lounge. X5.5 is desk and table products. Confirm current editions before you tattoo a year on a spec. I will not pretend I opened this week’s list. `[CITE NEEDED: current BIFMA edition years if an editor wants the numbers on the page.]`

Lounge is how a day-room sofa gets a logo. Lounge is not sit-to-stand. A lounge pass on a sinkhole sofa is how you buy a stamp and a transfer disaster. The activity draft already argued the sofa. Here: the stamp did not make the sofa a healthcare object. The stamp made the sofa a contract object.

Desks are how a nurses’ station that is actually millwork gets an office-furniture cousin on a punchout. X5.5 will not give you a seated-to-seated conversation height or a bumper that meets the vinyl. It may tell you whether a work surface survives a load. That is not nothing. It is not a bunker.

## The SNF list BIFMA will not write

- Seat height, compressed, for sit-to-stand.
- Arm shape you can push.
- Cleanout and moisture barrier.
- Chemical resistance to the wipe you use.
- TB 117-2013 / 16 CFR 1640, and NFPA 101 as adopted.
- Weight capacity that matches the room, not the office.
- Tip and climb in a rail-free room.
- Whether the chair fits the leftover.

You can have a chair that passes X5.1 and fails all eight. I have sat in them.

Add two more that the office does not have to care about. Whether the chair can live under a dining top without a six-inch slump. Whether a wide frame will pass the door of an 80-square-foot double. Dining and bariatric drafts sit with those inches. BIFMA will not call the DON and ask who is on the floor.

F917 wants a chair for the resident and a thought for a visitor. Appendix PP will name uncomfortable furniture as an institutional leftover under the homelike cluster. Neither tag has ever asked me for an X5.1 logo. They have asked me, in so many words, whether a person can use the thing.

## Capacity is not a durability cycle

A plus sign and a BIFMA adjective are the same disease when they stand in for a number you can fail.

Bariatric frames need a capacity, a test story, and a failure mode. A cycle test on an office chair is not a 500-pound rating. Do not mix them in a sentence. The bariatric draft is the long version. Here: if the report’s load is an office load, it is an office load. Write the pounds the house needs, then ask whether anyone tested toward those pounds. If nobody did, the logo is a decoration.

I have refused to stamp a number I could not stand behind. I will refuse a BIFMA sentence that is doing the same job as a plus sign — laundering a wish.

## ANSI/BIFMA vs. a mill’s shop test

A hardwood shop has its own cruelty: sit, rock, drop, wipe. That is not a lab. I will not pretend a shop floor is ISO. I will pretend even less that a lab report on a different foam is our chair.

If Bradley Brand said a chair was strong, I wanted the joinery to look like it, and I wanted a rating I would sign. If I could not sign it, I wanted the sentence to go away.

Shop cruelty finds the stretcher that racks and the arm that walks. Lab cycles find a failure definition written for an office. Both can be true. Neither replaces a compressed seat height. I have watched a beautiful oak frame pass every shop insult and still fail a short woman because we cut the seat like a lounge. Strength is not mercy.

A mill that hides behind “we are not a lab” and then prints a BIFMA logo is hiding twice. Pick a report or pick a sentence that says shop observation. Do not pick a logo.

## Other stamps that are not the job

GREENGUARD and its cousins are indoor-air stamps. Useful. Not a sit-to-stand. FSC is a forest stamp. Useful. Not a wipe. “Healthcare grade” is a marketing stamp. Useless.

The only stamps I want on a SNF chair sheet are **tests that map to a failure the building has**. Durability cycles map to a failure. A leaf logo does not.

TB 117-2013 / 16 CFR 1640 maps to a smolder failure. NFPA 101, named edition, maps to a life-safety failure. A chemical chart that names the wipe maps to a Tuesday failure. Those are not BIFMA. They can live on the same page as BIFMA. They cannot be replaced by BIFMA.

LEVEL and other sustainability scorecards had their years in contract furniture. I will not fake a score. I will say a score that does not mention seat height is a score. Put it in the file if the owner wants it. Do not let it eat the inch.

## Channel notes

Punchouts stack logos because logos photograph. A special-buy that asked for BIFMA plus 117 plus a wipe chart was the grown-up version. A special-buy that asked for “all applicable standards” was a lawyer’s nap.

If you inherit a chair with a BIFMA badge and a 17-inch seat, keep the badge in the file and replace the chair.

Medline-era sheets loved a row of marks: a leaf, a flame word, BIFMA, a healthcare adjective. The row is a sales object. A mill shop drawing with a compressed height and a wipe note is a build object. I would rather have the drawing.

I will not invent a Medline BIFMA SKU. I will say the channel used BIFMA the way it used “homelike” — as a word that made a residential object feel purchased. Sometimes the chair underneath was a real contract chair. Sometimes it was a 17-inch fashion seat with a logo. Sit in it. The logo will not help you stand.

Attic stock does not care about BIFMA. When the vinyl dies, the report on last year’s foam is a keepsake.

## How to write it

“Durability: BIFMA [standard, edition] report attached. Healthcare performance: seat height __ compressed; arms; cleanout; barrier; chemical chart for [product]; 16 CFR 1640; NFPA 101 [edition] as applicable; capacity __ lb.”

If the vendor cannot fill the blanks, they are selling an office chair to a nurse.

I read a sheet the way the spec-sheet draft taught: start with what is missing. If BIFMA is present and compressed height is missing, the sheet is an ad with a test. If BIFMA is missing and the eight SNF lines are present, I can live with a shop sentence. I prefer both. I will not trade the inch for the logo.

## Walkthrough

I sit. I stand. I look for a report, not a mark. I look at seat height compressed. I look at the wipe chart. I look at 1640 and the 101 edition on the last survey.

If the only healthcare sentence is a BIFMA logo, the chair is an office visitor.
