# BIFMA is not a healthcare stamp

BIFMA is a trade association with a test culture. That culture is mostly **office**. X5.1 for office chairs, X5.4 for lounge, X5.5 for desks, a durability language that a contract buyer already trusts.

A Medline-era punchout that said “BIFMA approved healthcare chair” was speaking a dialect. BIFMA does not approve a nursing home. BIFMA publishes test methods. A lab can run them. A chair can pass. A SNF can still fail the chair for height, wipe, flame, or a survey tag that never heard of X5.1.

I like tests. I do not like stamps that launder a job.

## What a pass is

A BIFMA seating test is a story about loads, cycles, and a failure definition. It is a good story if you want to know whether an arm will walk off in an office. It is a partial story if you want to know whether a CNA can stand a person from the chair three times a night, whether bleach will open the vinyl, whether the seat height is 17 inches of fashion.

If a vendor has a report, read the **standard, the edition, the product, and the pass/fail**. If they have a logo in a catalog and no report, they have a costume.

Healthcare furniture sometimes shows up in BIFMA’s world as lounge or as a special test. I will not fake a “BIFMA healthcare” standard number I cannot pin this week. `[CITE NEEDED: any current BIFMA healthcare-specific standard or white paper an editor wants named.]` Until that pin, treat a BIFMA mention as **office durability language**, then add the SNF list yourself.

## The SNF list BIFMA will not write

- Seat height, compressed, for sit-to-stand.
- Arm shape you can push.
- Cleanout and moisture barrier.
- Chemical resistance to the wipe you use.
- TB 117-2013 / 16 CFR 1640, and NFPA 101 as adopted.
- Weight capacity that matches the room, not the office.
- Tip and climb in a rail-free room.
- Whether the chair fits the leftover.

You can have a chair that passes X5.1 and fails all eight. I have sat in them.

## ANSI/BIFMA vs. a mill’s shop test

A hardwood shop has its own cruelty: sit, rock, drop, wipe. That is not a lab. I will not pretend a shop floor is ISO. I will pretend even less that a lab report on a different foam is our chair.

If Bradley Brand said a chair was strong, I wanted the joinery to look like it, and I wanted a rating I would sign. If I could not sign it, I wanted the sentence to go away. A plus sign and a BIFMA adjective are the same disease.

## Other stamps that are not the job

GREENGUARD and its cousins are indoor-air stamps. Useful. Not a sit-to-stand. FSC is a forest stamp. Useful. Not a wipe. “Healthcare grade” is a marketing stamp. Useless.

The only stamps I want on a SNF chair sheet are **tests that map to a failure the building has**. Durability cycles map to a failure. A leaf logo does not.

## Channel notes

Punchouts stack logos because logos photograph. A special-buy that asked for BIFMA plus 117 plus a wipe chart was the grown-up version. A special-buy that asked for “all applicable standards” was a lawyer’s nap.

If you inherit a chair with a BIFMA badge and a 17-inch seat, keep the badge in the file and replace the chair.

## How to write it

“Durability: BIFMA [standard, edition] report attached. Healthcare performance: seat height __ compressed; arms; cleanout; barrier; chemical chart for [product]; 16 CFR 1640; NFPA 101 [edition] as applicable; capacity __ lb.”

If the vendor cannot fill the blanks, they are selling an office chair to a nurse.
