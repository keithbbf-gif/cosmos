I use the National Center’s **fig jam without added pectin**. I do not reprint it here as if I wrote it, and I do not change the bones and keep their process time.

What I will say, as a pointer, so a picker knows which page:

- About **2 quarts chopped fresh figs** (they say about **5 pounds**)
- **¾ cup water**
- **6 cups sugar**
- **¼ cup lemon juice**
- They have you pour boiling water over the figs, stand **10 minutes**, drain, stem, chop
- Cook with water and sugar until thick, add lemon, cook **1 minute** more
- Hot pack, **¼-inch** headspace, boiling-water process: **5 minutes** at 0–1,000 ft, **10** at 1,001–6,000, **15** above 6,000, half-pints or pints

Read the page. Read *Using Boiling Water Canners* if you have not. I am a fig grower with a pot, not your county extension.

Yield they list: about **10 half-pints**. My pot will wander if my chop is wetter. I do not force a yield.

I already “improved” a tested jam with extra fruit and less sugar and I kept the five-minute process. That is the kind of improvement that looks like cooking and acts like guessing. I do not improve the bones.

Flavor add-ins after the fact — a scraped vanilla bean, a strip of lemon zest — I treat as a fridge experiment first. If I want them in a canned jar I look for a tested path or I accept I am making a different product and I freeze it.

South Arkansas elevation is why I use the short time. [VERIFY] yours.

I do not can quarts of this jam because their table is half-pints or pints.

Lids, rings, headspace, bubbles, a clean rim: the boring steps are the steps.

There is a pectin-added fig jam on their site with different lemon and sugar. I do not mix the two pages in one Saturday.

This is the citation draft. The live URL lives with NCHFP. If they move it, I follow them, not my memory.

I print the page. I do not cook from a social-media card that stole the title.

If my jam is thin I do not put it back in the canner for a bonus five minutes I invented. I fridge that batch and I cook the next one longer *before* I fill, or I use it as a sauce. Process time is not a thickener.

If my jam is dark and stiff I cooked it too far. We still eat it. We do not add water and recan it as a new product.

Cite. Measure. Process. That is the whole restage I will allow — a pointer, not a new recipe with their minutes glued on.

I weigh fruit when I can. “About five pounds” is their language. My wet chop can be a liar. A scale keeps me from inventing a sixth pound I did not cook with their sugar.

I do not double the pot on a burner that cannot hold a boil I can stir. A double batch that scorches is two losses. Two single pots are boring and done.

If the page is down I do not cook from memory. I cook a fridge pot or I wait. Memory is how minutes move.

The printed page gets fig spots. Fine. A clean page in a sleeve is better. I have cooked from a spotted page and misread a 5 as a 15. That Saturday was long. The sleeve is cheaper.

I do not cook from a screenshot of a screenshot. The live page has the table. The screenshot loses a footnote. I have lost a footnote. I did not like the Saturday that followed.
