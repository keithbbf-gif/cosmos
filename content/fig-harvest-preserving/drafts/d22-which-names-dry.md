Sugar and size do the drying. Water does the fighting.

Celeste is the Southern drying fig I will stand behind. Small, sweet, closed eye, less of a puddle. It takes time because it is small and proud, but it finishes. If I only dry one name, it is this one.

Black Mission, when it actually ripens here, dries like the grocery story you already know. Dark, rich, a little seed crunch. I do not plant a row of Mission for 8a humidity. If I have fruit, I will dry some.

Brown Turkey is the jam mule. It will dry. It is wetter, bigger, milder. You get a larger leather that tastes like a polite fig. Fine in oatmeal. Not the jar I hide for myself.

LSU names vary. Gold can be a honey chip if I slice it. Purple can be wet. I treat them as halves and I do not promise a Mission clone.

Alma late in a humid September is a maybe. If the fruit is honestly ripe and the machine is free, yes. If I am tired, the freezer gets it. Late wet air plus a fat fig is a long night.

Open-eye watery names are the puddle. They can dry. They often sour before they dry if I was slow in the yard. I do not start a twelve-hour load with fruit I did not trust at 7 a.m.

I already dried a mixed “pretty tray” for a photo. The Celestes were done. The Turkey halves were soup. The photo was a liar. One name per load when I am trying to learn.

Jack’s GDD note still stands. A name that barely ripens will not dry into a miracle. Underripe fruit dries into a tough coin with no point.

Zone 7 people dry Hardy because that is what they can ripen. Steal their honesty. Zone 9 people can talk Kadota and Calimyrna if their climate allows. We can talk Celeste.

I do not chase a “best drying fig” list from a California board and then plant ten. I dry what shrugged this morning and I write the name on the jar. Two summers of jars is a list. A blog is a rumor.

If a name puddles two years in a row, I stop giving it tray space. The pot wants that fruit.

A grocery “dried fig” is often a Mission from a dry valley, picked for that job. Comparing our Turkey leather to that bag is how we get sad. Different fruit, different air. I compare our Celeste to last year’s Celeste.

White or green figs can brown as they dry. That is not a failure. Lemon in the ice water slows it. I do not bleach a fig to please a photo.

If I am short on tray space I dry the small sweet ones and I freeze the big mild ones. That is a kitchen decision, not a ranking of souls.

Seed crunch is louder when the fruit is dry. People who hate seeds will hate dried figs more than fresh. I do not have a seedless 8a speech. I have a jam speech for those people.

I will dry a name once as a test before I promise a jar line. One test tray, one jar, one winter. If we eat it and ask for it, it earns space next August. If it sits, the freezer gets that name.

Kadota and Calimyrna are words I read, not words I plant for this humidity. If a cutting of something pale and dry-climate shows up, I treat it as a guest fruit. Guest fruit can dry. Guest fruit does not rewrite the row.

A “dried fig” from a bag in town is a climate I can taste and cannot copy. I stop trying. I dry ours.
