The harvest job in August is not heroic. It is early.

I walk before the sun has a lane through the row. Fruit is cooler. Necks are easier to read. Wasps are slower. Birds have not finished their first pass. My own patience is better because I have not already spent it on the shop.

A ripe fig in 8a heat at 2 p.m. is a different object than the same fig at 6:30 a.m. The afternoon fruit is soft, leaking, covered in sugar ants, and half-owned by something with wings. You can still take it. You will not be glad you waited.

Routine I will actually keep:

- Bowl or shallow tray, not a deep bucket. Bottom figs crush.
- Knife only for a stubborn stem I should have left yesterday.
- Gloves if the latex is running. It runs more when I am sloppy and the day is already hot.
- A second container for splits I will cook today, not serve tonight.
- A pass around the back of the bush. That is where I get proud and miss.

I do not strip a tree because I am already out there. I take what shrugs. I leave what salutes. Twenty minutes, most mornings, in the glut weeks. If I skip two days I am donating.

Birds here like dark fruit and the first soft one they find. Netting is a whole other argument. A morning walk is the cheap net. You will still lose some. You will lose fewer than the person who harvests on Saturday like it is peaches.

Wasps find splits. I do not fight a wasp for a fig. I leave that one. There are others. A sting in the ear is not a yield strategy.

Zone 7 mornings can be cold enough that you wait an hour. Fine. Zone 9 mornings can already be a furnace in August. They still pick early. Steal that.

I already tried “evening harvest” because mornings were a shop mess. The figs I wanted were gone or drunk. The ones I took needed the pot the same night. Evening is a rescue pass after a storm, not a plan.

If you only have weekends, you still pick Friday evening and Sunday morning and you accept that Monday–Thursday belongs to the yard. A fig tree is not a CSA box. It does not hold.

Shop pots get the same morning. I do not say “they’re by the door, I’ll grab them at lunch.” Lunch is when the glass has cooked the ripe ones and the ants have filed a route.

Write the date on the bowl tape if the kitchen is about to jam. PapaFig wants to know if this fruit sat. Morning fruit can wait a few cool hours. Afternoon fruit wants the freezer sheet or the pot before you eat your own breakfast.

I drink coffee after the walk, not before, in July and August. If I sit down first I negotiate with the heat and lose. The row does not care that I have email.

Kids can help if I have already taken the wasp figs. I give them a shallow tray and the rule: if it does not come off easy, it stays. They will still pick one hard one. I let that be the lesson instead of a speech.

A dog in the row is how you find dropped fruit with your shoe. I leave the dog on the porch for the glut weeks. Dropped fruit is still a pick if it is clean and the ants have not claimed it. I do not serve porch-drop figs to guests. They go in the pot.

Rain in the night does not cancel morning. It makes morning mandatory. Splits start then. See the rain draft. The only morning I skip is lightning. Fruit can wait an hour. I cannot grow a new habit if I am stubborn in a storm.
