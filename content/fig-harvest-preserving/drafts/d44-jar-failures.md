A ping is a good sound. It is not a personality.

**No seal.** I fridge that jar and I eat it first, if the food still looks and smells like the pot. I do not put a new lid on and give it a process I invented the next morning unless I am following a reprocess note I actually have. Most nights I just eat it.

**Siphoning.** Jam in the water, sticky rings, low headspace after. Often a too-full jar, a too-hard boil, or a yank from the canner. I leave ¼ inch on the jam page. I do not stack jars. I do not tilt a hot jar to wipe it like I am polishing a trophy.

**Weak set.** Syrup. We already talked. Eat it. Do not recan as a thickener.

**Dark band / scorch.** House jar. Not a gift.

**Mold on a fridge jar.** Throw it. Do not scrape.

**Mold on a sealed jar I trusted.** I do not taste around it. The jar is done. I look at my headspace, my fruit, my process, my lid. I do not look for a folk save.

**Fizzy, off, bulged.** Out. I do not photograph it for the site.

I already kept a pretty unsealed jar on the shelf because I was sure I heard a ping. I heard the dishwasher. The jar was a science project. I tell that story so I test seals when the jars are cool: lift by the lid, look at the center.

Rings come off for storage. Rings can hide a failed seal and rust in our humidity.

Zone 8a steam in a small kitchen makes rims dirty. I wipe with vinegar if I need a clean rim. I do not skip the wipe.

Chips on a rim: that jar is a glass, not a canner jar.

I do not reuse a lid for canning. I reuse a ring if it is not rusty.

Failure is part of a real set. A set that never fails is a catalog.

If a whole load fails I stop and I read the page again before I light the next burner. Two failed loads is a habit, not bad luck.

I keep a notebook of batches: date, name, set, seals. That notebook has saved me from repeating a shallow headspace month.

Guests do not need a tour of the failure shelf. The failure shelf goes to the chicken bucket or the trash. The success shelf can be a tour.

A sticky jar in the cupboard is an ant letter. I wash jars when they are cool. I do not store jam in a film of sugar.

This is the unromantic sister of the plate test. The plate decides the pot. The lid decides the shelf.

I do not store jars with rings cranked as a “just in case.” Case is rust and a hidden fail. Rings off, clean, stacked with a card.

A jar that weeps sugar on the shelf gets washed and watched. Weep can be siphon leftover. Weep can be a seal that is leaving. I do not ignore a sticky ring of ants.

If I am unsure whether a jar sealed, I treat it as fridge food that night. Unsure is not a cupboard.

I keep extra lids. I do not wash a used canning lid and “try it.” Trying is a hobby I retired after one pretty fail.

Broken glass in a canner is a stop-the-day event. I do not fish and continue. I dump, I inspect, I start over or I quit. Fruit is cheaper than a trip I will not describe.
