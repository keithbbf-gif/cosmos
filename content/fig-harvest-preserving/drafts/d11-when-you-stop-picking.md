You stop when the fruit stops shrugging, or when the night says the argument is over.

In our 8a pockets the first real freeze is often early November. Some years it is mid-October on a hill. Some years a late Alma is still honest in the first week of the tenth month. [VERIFY] the night you actually get, not the zone card.

Green figs on the tree after a freeze are not “almost.” They are done. The texture goes wrong. The flavor goes nowhere. I do not pick a tree of rocks the morning after 28°F and call it a kitchen day. I pick the ones that were already ripe and I clean the rest off so they are not mummies in April.

Before the freeze, I stop chasing fruit that has been hard for three weeks. Late varieties have a window. After the window they are decorations. Alma will tell you. A name that wanted July will not become Alma because you waited.

Souring increases as nights cool and days stay humid. Open-eye leftovers are a science project. I stop leaving “one more day” on those in October. I take the shrugs and I let the rest go.

Birds get hungrier as other food thins. A late fig is a beacon. Morning walks get shorter and more necessary, then they end.

I do not fertilize in September to “push the last crop.” Soft wood is January’s kill. The pruning calendar already yelled about that. Harvest does not get a veto.

Zone 7 stops earlier and should. Steal their October honesty. Zone 9 can keep a bowl into December some years. Do not copy that onto a South Arkansas forecast.

I already stripped a tree of green figs before a “maybe freeze” that did not come. I had a bucket of rocks and then a week of perfect weather. The tree would have finished a few more. I do not strip on a rumor. I strip after the night, or I pick ripe and wait.

If a pot has to come in, I pick the ripe and I leave the hard if the shop is only a few nights. If the pot is in for the winter, the hard figs are not a winter crop. They are compost in a warm room. I take them off so they do not rot on the branch over a bench of clean plants.

Stopping is part of harvest. The person who never stops is the person who fills January with regret and a dehydrator full of nothing.

I walk once after the first frost with a bucket for mummies, not for jam. Fruit on the ground is next year’s beetle hotel. Fruit hanging brown is the same hotel in the air.

A light frost that only nips the tips is not always the end. I taste one. If it is still a fig, I keep walking a day or two. If it is wet cloth, I stop. The difference is not a forum chart. It is that fruit in my hand.

A pot I already shuffled in for the winter does not get a “last harvest” in the shop under a shop light. Shop light is not August. Fruit that was close can stall and then rot on a bench of clean plants. I take the ripe the day we shuffle. I take the hard off and I compost it if the pot is in until spring. I have tried to ripen three green figs on a #5 in January. I got three wrinkled arguments and a fruit fly in a month that did not need one.

When the walk is over I hang the bowl. I do not leave it on the porch “in case.” In case is how you find a moldy souvenir in December. The kitchen can still open a jar. The tree is off the clock. I write STOP and the date on the shop board next to the first-ripe date. Two dates make a season. One date makes a story I will inflate in January.
