People want a ranked list. I have a walk order.

This is not a catalog and not a flavor wheel. It is the order fruit shows up in our 8a dirt and pots when the wood lives. Your wall will move it. A cool June will move it. A name from a different cutting source may not be the name on the tag. “Brown Turkey” in commerce is a crowd. We still have to pick something.

**Celeste first.** Small, closed eye, brown-violet, honey-sugar, the Southern yard fig that actually behaves in humidity. Main crop often July here. Breba is a maybe. If you only plant one tree for fresh eating, this is the one I point at. It dries if you are patient. It jams if you have enough, which you might not, because you will eat them.

**LSU names next or overlapping.** Purple and Gold if we have the GDDs. They were bred for this kind of sticky heat. Color lies. Neck does not. Good pot plants. Good in-ground if the hole is not a clay grave.

**Brown Turkey in the middle of the loud weeks.** Bigger, milder, the jam mule. Heavier yields when it is happy. Easy to pick early because it looks finished. Wait for the neck. This is the fruit that fills the pot when Celeste is already in your mouth.

**Chicago Hardy when it wants to.** Useful north of us. Here it is a spare, not a religion. Can be dull if you pick it like you are scared. Can be fine if you wait. Treat winter like it can still die back. Harvest follows the wood you actually have, not the Facebook hardiness badge.

**Alma late.** Green-gold, honey, humid-South manners, still thinking in early September when you are tired. Do not prune it like an early fig and then wonder why October is full of rocks. The pruning pack already said that. I am saying it at the bowl.

**Black Mission as a guest, not a plan.** Fine flavor in the right year. Not the Southeast default. If it ripens, eat it. If it sulks, do not rewrite the yard around it. California is a climate.

I do not harvest “the row.” I harvest the name that shrugged today. Mixing Celeste and half-ready Turkey in one colander is how a jam batch tastes like nothing and a fresh plate has rocks.

Zone 7 will see Celeste and Hardy more than Alma. Steal their early names. Zone 9 can keep Mission in the conversation. We can keep it in a pot and tell the truth.

Jack’s live FigRoots note still stands: GDDs, water, tight eyes, pots versus dirt. I am not restaging that post. I am telling you the walk. If a name is not on this list it is not an insult. It is not what I meet before breakfast.

Write your own order for two summers before you buy ten more trees. The first summer is a rumor. The second is a draft. The third is a harvest.

I keep the tags readable from the path. Paint pen, two sides, the way we do cuttings. A harvest in a mixed row without tags is how “Celeste” becomes a story you tell about a fig that was not Celeste.

If two names ripen the same morning I still keep two bowls. The kitchen can mix later on purpose. The row should not mix by accident. PapaFig can decide a Turkey-Celeste pot. I should not decide it with a lazy colander.

A nursery tag that says “everbearing” does not give you fruit twelve months. It means breba plus main if the wood lives. In 8a that is still a summer walk. I do not sell a guest on year-round figs. I sell them on July and August if they will show up.
