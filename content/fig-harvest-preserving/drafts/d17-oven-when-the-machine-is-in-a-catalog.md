A dehydrator is the right tool. An oven is the tool many kitchens already have. I have used both. I do not pretend they are equal.

Oven drying wants the lowest heat the box will hold and a way for water to leave. I aim near **170°F** if that is as low as the oven goes, or as close to **140–150°F** as a “warm” setting gets. I prop the door with a wooden spoon. I run convection if I have it. I rotate pans. I do not walk away for a nap.

This is a Saturday method, not a “set it and sleep” method. An oven in August also heats the house. In our climate that is not a free side effect. I do it when the glut has already filled the fridge and the machine is full or absent.

Pans: parchment or a rack over a sheet. Fruit not sitting in its own syrup puddle. If juice collects, I pour it off and I cook it later or I drink it. I do not let halves swim. Swimming is candy, then charcoal.

I already burned a batch because I answered a phone in the shop and the oven was “only on 200.” 200 with the door shut is a roast. The smell told the house before I did. We ate the less-burned ones as a bitter snack and we did not call them dried figs.

Halves only in the oven if I can help it. Whole figs take longer than my patience and longer than a safe “I think the oven is fine.” Checking whole fruit is for a dehydrator I trust or a day I am actually home.

Zone 7 winter oven-drying has a charm I will not sneer at — the house wants the heat. Our August house does not. I freeze the glut and I dry in a cooler week if the oven is the only path.

A toaster oven can do a tiny tray. A tiny tray is a gift of six Celestes, not a harvest plan.

If the oven will not hold below 200, I use it to start and I finish in front of a fan in the air conditioning, or I admit I am making roasted fig halves for the freezer. Roasted is a product. Dried is a product. Mixing the words is how people get disappointed in January.

I do not line the oven floor with foil as a personality. I line the pan. I keep the door propped. I set two timers: rotate, then check.

This method exists so a person without a machine still has a way that is not a humid screen on the porch. It does not exist so I can skip buying a used dehydrator when the trees are actually producing.

If I am oven-drying I do not also bake bread in the same afternoon. The oven is a dryer that day. Sharing it with a casserole is how you get onion figs.

I open windows if I can stand the humidity trade. Wet kitchen air slows the work. I would rather dry at night when the house AC is already fighting and I am awake enough to rotate.

A convection roast setting is not a dry setting even if the fan sounds right. Roast wants a crust. I want leather. If I cannot get the number down, I stop and I freeze. Frozen figs make jam. Burnt figs make a story I do not need a second of.

A pizza stone in the oven does not make this a dehydrator. It makes a hot floor and a burnt bottom. I have tried the stone because someone on a board was sure. I went back to a rack and a propped door. The board was not in my kitchen in August.
