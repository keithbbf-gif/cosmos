In-ground figs in our clay run on the yard. Pots run on the pot.

A #5 against shop glass can color a week early because the wall is a radiator. The same name in the row can sit dull while you wonder if you bought a dud. You did not. You bought two climates.

I pick pots by neck, same as dirt. I do not pick them by the in-ground calendar I wrote on the board. The board is for the row. The pots get a walk of their own, usually when I water, which in July is not optional.

Pots also stop earlier if we shuffle them inside for a cold night. Fruit that was three days from a shrug can stall in a dim shop. Sometimes it finishes. Sometimes it sits. I do not run a heater to ripen a pot of figs. I pick what is ready before the shuffle if a freeze is actually coming. I do not strip green fruit because the forecast is rude.

Water is the other clock. A dry pot will drop fruit or turn it into cork. An overwatered pot in a saucer will drop fruit and sulk. In-ground clay does the same more slowly. If a pot is ripening unevenly, look at the hose before you look at the variety.

Root-bound pots ripen a little, then tease you forever. That is a pot-up problem, not a harvest problem. PapaFig already wrote pot-up before June. I am telling you a July bowl will not fix a plant that needed a #5 in April.

Zone 7 pots spend more of the year in a garage. Their harvest clock is even less like the yard. Zone 9 pots can live outside and still run hot on a patio. Steal the separate walk. Do not steal a patio Mission schedule for our in-ground Celeste.

I already mixed pot fruit and row fruit in one jam “because it was all Turkey.” The pot fruit was a week riper and the row fruit was a week prouder. The pot was jam. The row was toast. Two bowls.

Black pots cook roots on a July afternoon. Fruit can look ready and taste cooked. I move those pots to morning sun / afternoon shade in the loud months. Harvest improves because the plant is not in a fever.

If you only have pots, you still have a season. You do not have “the 8a in-ground calendar.” You have a set of buckets with their own frost date — the night you forgot to shuffle.

Write the name and the pot size on the tape. “Turkey #3 shop” is not the same plant as “Turkey east row.” Harvest notes that forget that will lie to you next year.

I water pots in the morning so I am already there for the pick. Night watering in our humidity is how you get wet foliage and a late walk you will skip. The fruit does not care about my schedule. The ants do.

A pot on a wagon at a sale is a third clock. Travel, dry mix, a day in a parking lot. I do not harvest that plant for a customer photo. I send it home and tell them to wait a week. If a ripe fig is going to drop in the truck, I pick it and eat it. I do not put a stressed sale plant into a jam story.

In-ground after a rain is mud and splits. Pots after a rain can still be dry an inch down if the canopy shed the water. I check both. I do not let the weather app pick for me.
