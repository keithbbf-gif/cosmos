Northern blogs give you one harvest sentence. California gives you two as a birthright. Zone 8a gives you a main crop you can plan on if the wood lived, and a breba you can talk about if January was kind.

I walk it like this, South Arkansas, not a map app.

**May.** Watch. Pea-sized breba on last year’s tips if the winter left those tips. Do not pick. Do not prune while you are standing there with hope. Hope is not ripe.

**June.** Breba, if you have it. Earlier on a south wall, later in a windy open row. Small pass. Eat them standing up. This is not a jam month unless you have a tree that actually carries a real breba. Most years our Celeste is polite and our Brown Turkey is a rumor. [VERIFY] your own wood. The pruning pack already said it: 8a is the dishonest middle.

**July.** Early main crop. Celeste is the alarm clock. Closed-eye, small, sweet, easy to miss because the fruit hides. This is when guests say “already?” and I say “walk every morning.” LSU names start to show. Brown Turkey is coloring and lying about being ready.

**August.** The glut month. Heat, humidity, rain arguments, birds. Brown Turkey comes honest. LSU Purple and Gold if the GDDs were there. This is when the kitchen stops being optional. If you only eat fresh you will waste a tree. If you only jam you will hate August. Split the fruit: bowl, sheet pan, pot.

**September.** Late names. Alma thinking about it. Leftover Turkey. First cool nights in the colder 8a pockets. Flavor often better, wasps still employed. Stop fertilizing. Stop wishing a green fig into October.

**October.** Last polite fruit on the late ones. First frost in the mean pockets. Green leftovers are not a second crop. They are winter decoration. Pick what shrugs. Leave what salutes. After a real freeze the remaining figs are compost, not “almost.”

**November.** The tree is done. You are not “finishing the harvest.” You are raking fruit mummies so next year’s souring has less to stand on.

Last spring frost here is often early to mid-April. First fall freeze often early November. Those two dates are the box. [VERIFY] your county. A river-bottom 8a and a hill 8a do not share a Tuesday.

Zone 7 compresses this. They may never see a June breba and their August is shorter. Steal their urgency in October. Zone 9 stretches it. They can still be picking when we are wrapping pots. Do not copy their “leave it another week” in our first-frost week.

Container trees on the shop side run a different clock — warmer wall, earlier color, sometimes a later drip of fruit if we shuffle them out of a freeze. I still pick by neck, not by the month I wrote in January.

I do not publish a “peak week” for the whole yard. The yard is a mix on purpose. Celeste in July is how we have fresh fruit while the jam names are still swelling. Alma in September is how we are not bored. If you plant one variety you get one panic. If you plant three ripening times you get a season.

The calendar is a walk, not a festival. Anyone who tells you “fig harvest Saturday” owns one tree or a lie.

I write the first ripe Celeste on the shop board. That date moves. Some years it is the second week of July. Some years a cool June pushes it toward the end. I do not move jam day to match a Pinterest week. I move jam day to match the board.

Pots along the glass can show a fig in June that the in-ground row will not match. I do not let that pot rewrite the yard calendar. I eat the pot fig. I wait on the row. Mixing those two clocks is how a person says “harvest started” and then has nothing for three weeks.

If a late April freeze burned the breba, June is quiet. Do not invent a harvest to keep the calendar pretty. Let July be July. The main crop on this year’s wood does not owe you a makeup June.
