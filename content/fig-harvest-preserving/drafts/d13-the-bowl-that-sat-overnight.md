A ripe fig in our kitchen is a same-day fruit.

I can keep a firm-ripe fig in the fridge for a short sit if the air is dry and the bowl is shallow. I cannot leave a glut on the counter overnight in August and expect breakfast to be proud of me. Humidity here is a second harvest. It harvests quality.

What overnight does: skins weep. Eyes sour. Fruit flies file paperwork. The bottom layer becomes jam without sugar or heat. You will still eat some. You will throw some. You will swear you will process next time.

Next time is the same day. Fridge if you must, single layer, not a washed wet pile. Wet fruit in a closed box is a sauna. Dry the skins, then chill. Use tomorrow. Do not start a collection.

I sort at the sink the hour I come in.

- Table figs: intact, closed eye, no bruise. Eat or give today.
- Cook figs: splits, soft necks, bird pecks I am willing to cut. Pot or freezer sheet today.
- Out: fizzy, fermented, ant-filled, mystery mush.

That sort takes five minutes and saves a batch.

I already left a beautiful bowl out “for the house to admire.” The house admired it. The morning after, the house had fruit flies and a smell. Admiration is a plate at supper, not a still life until Thursday.

Zone 7 houses can be drier. They get an extra night sometimes. Steal nothing from that. Zone 9 air can be worse than ours. They already know the same-day rule.

If we are going to dry, the fruit goes on trays the same afternoon. If we are going to freeze, the sheet pan goes in before I sit down. If we are going to jam, the pot is not a weekend plan unless the fruit is already frozen. Frozen is how you buy time. The counter is not.

A cooler with an ice pack in the truck is for a long morning at a second property, not for a week of storage. I have seen people “keep them cold” in a cooler that is actually a wet box. Wet box is worse than a fridge.

Give fruit away the day you have too much. A neighbor with a ripe fig today is a friend. A neighbor with your tired figs tomorrow is a review.

The bowl is a transfer, not a warehouse. I keep two bowls so I stop stacking. Stacking is how the bottom layer pays for my optimism.

I do not wash fruit I am about to fridge unless I dry it well. I wash what I eat now. I wash cook fruit as I cook. A wet overnight bowl is a fog chamber.

If someone brings us a grocery box of half-ripe imports, that box can sit because it was picked like a peach. Our tree fruit cannot. I do not let that grocery box teach the house bad manners.

Write the pick time on a piece of tape if more than one person is in the kitchen. “Tuesday 7 a.m.” is a fact. “Figs” is how they sit until Thursday.

A hotel ice bucket is not a plan if we are traveling with fruit. Melt water is a bath. I have watched a “careful” bucket become soup in a truck cab. If fruit must ride, shallow tray, towel, AC, and a same-day kitchen at the other end.

I would rather process an extra sheet pan at 9 a.m. than perform a rescue at 9 p.m. Rescue jam from tired fruit is possible. It will not be the jar you give. It will be the jar you hide behind the pickles. The hide-behind-the-pickles jar is how a house learns to distrust the good jars. I stopped making it.
