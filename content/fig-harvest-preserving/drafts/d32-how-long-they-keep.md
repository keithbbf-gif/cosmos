Frozen figs keep as food a long time. They keep as a good fig for a shorter time.

I aim to use a bag within a year. I prefer the next glut not to meet last year’s brick at the back. Quality slides: more ice, more dull, more brown. Safe is not the same as worth a cake.

I will not print a government slogan as if I measured it in this chest freezer. Home freezers open. They frost. They sit through storms. A sealed commercial box is a different object.

Rotation is the method. New bags to the back. Old bags to the cook list. A strip of tape on the lid of the freezer that says “Turkey 8/24” is more useful than a paragraph.

I already found a bag with no date and a color I did not trust. I threw it. Mystery meat is a joke. Mystery fig is just sad.

If a bag is freezer-burned I can still jam it if it smells like fig. If it smells like a freezer it is out. Burn is quality. Off is off.

Power outages: keep it shut. When in doubt, cook what thawed or throw what sat warm. I am not writing a chart for every hour. I am writing “do not guess to save a dollar of fruit.”

Zone 7 chest freezers in a cold garage can be kind. Zone 9 garages can be mean. Our 8a shop-adjacent freezer works if I do not treat the door like a television.

Dry-pack halves at the year mark are still jam. Sugar-pack may look prettier longer. I still date both.

I do not keep a “forever” jar of frozen puree. Forever is how you own a tub of brown ice.

The next August is the real deadline. If last August is still here, we cook a week of fig meals and we make space. Space is a harvest tool.

I write the year, not just the month, after the first January I confused two Augusts.

A small freezer should not pretend it is a warehouse. If I cannot name the bags without opening the door, I have too many bags. Eat them.

Friends who say “just freeze it, it lasts forever” are talking about fear, not about flavor. I freeze to use. I do not freeze to hoard a feeling.

When the new Celeste starts, I look at the old Celeste first. That is the rotation. That is the only clock I trust more than a printed range.

I do not keep a “emergency” bag I never touch. Emergency is how a bag becomes three years old and a dare. If it is food I put it on a list. If it is not on a list it is a ghost.

A frost-choked bag I open in March gets a smell test and a small cook, not a gift. Gift fruit is the newer bag.

I mark the freezer door with painter’s tape when a storm is coming so nobody stands there deciding on ice cream. Shut is the method. Ice cream can wait. Figs can wait. The cold cannot be replaced by hope.

If I buy a second-hand freezer I check the seal before I fill it with August. A cheap seal is a quality killer that looks like “figs don’t freeze well.” Figs freeze. The box failed.

I do not keep opened bags half-used without pressing the air out again. A half-used bag is a frost factory. I roll it, date it again, and I put it where I will see it next week.
