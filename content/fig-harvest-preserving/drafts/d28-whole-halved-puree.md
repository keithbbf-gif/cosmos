I freeze for a job, not for a museum.

**Whole.** Small, sound, closed-eye fruit I might want to thaw for a cake or a stew where a fig should still look like a fig. They take more space. They take longer to thaw. They hold a little more pride. I do not freeze wholes that are already split. Splits belong to halves or the pot tonight.

**Halves.** The default. They stack after the tray-freeze. They go straight into jam, into a skillet, into a muffin batter. I can see if a center was wrong before I bag, if I was paying attention. Most of our Turkey glut is halves.

**Puree.** Soft fruit, ugly fruit, the end of a bowl. I mash, I add a little lemon if I want color, I freeze in ice-cube trays or in small tubs. Cubes are a gift on a Tuesday in January when I want fig in yogurt or in a sauce and I do not want a brick. I do not freeze puree in a half-gallon tub unless I like to thaw a church supper.

I do not freeze peel-off projects. Peeling a fig for the freezer is a hobby I have not chosen.

Stems off. Grit off. Ants off. The freezer will not launder a dirty fig. It will only pause it.

I already froze a puree without labeling the name. It was “dark.” In March I put it in a cake that wanted Celeste and I got a mild nothing. Label the mash too.

Zone does not change the cuts. Kitchen space does. A small freezer should favor puree cubes and halves. Wholes are a luxury of a chest freezer.

If I know the fruit is for jam, I freeze halves or a rough chop. I do not need wholes I will smash later. If I know the fruit is for a holiday plate, I freeze the prettiest wholes and I accept they will still be softer than July.

Three products, three bags. Mixing them in one bag is how you thaw a puree leak over a whole fig you wanted to keep.

A food processor is fine. A potato masher is fine. A blender and a lot of water is how you freeze fig juice. I want fruit, not a slush I have to reduce for an hour.

I do not add sugar to puree unless I am making a sweet pack on purpose. Sugar is the next draft. The cut is this draft.

Kids can mash the ugly bowl. That is a useful job. I still look for a fizzy fig before it hits the mash. One sour fruit in a puree is a whole tub I will not use.

If I am freezing for someone else I ask what they cook. Bakers want halves. Jam people want halves or mash. People who “just want figs” get halves and a note that thaw is not fresh.

I do not freeze a puree I watered to make the blender happy. Water is a tax I will pay again in the pot. A mash that stands on a spoon is enough.

Seeds in puree are still seeds. A blender does not make a seedless product. If someone wants smooth they can press through a mill. I rarely do. Time is the glut’s enemy.

A cube tray needs a lid or a bag over it or the freezer tastes like onion. I have made onion fig cubes. I threw them. The next tray got a bag.

Whole figs with a bird peck can be halves if I cut the peck out. They cannot be pretty wholes. Pretty is a sort, not a hope.
