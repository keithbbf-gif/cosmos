Figs sit on the wrong side of a line.

Most fruits are clearly acid enough for a boiling-water canner. Figs wander around pH **4.6**. NCHFP said it in plain language: they can go above the line, so you add acid, and the amounts were researched. I am not going to restage that research as a kitchen experiment. I am going to buy bottled lemon juice and I am going to measure.

For their **fig jam without pectin**: **¼ cup lemon juice** on about **5 pounds** chopped figs (with the sugar and water they list). For **whole canned figs**: **1 tablespoon bottled lemon juice per pint**, **2 tablespoons per quart** — or citric acid **¼ teaspoon / ½ teaspoon**. I cite those numbers. I do not shrink them because the fruit “tastes tart.” Taste is not a pH meter.

Bottled. Not a pretty bowl of fresh lemons I squeezed until I got bored. Bottled is consistent. The bulletins ask for bottled for a reason.

This is food safety, not a wellness add-in. I will say the word botulism once, the way NCHFP does, as the reason the line exists. I will not write a clinic. I will not write a scare poster. I will write a measuring cup.

I already made a “low sugar, just fruit” canned spread because a magazine made it look rustic. I did not process it like a puree. I will not serve that memory. If I want low sugar I use a pectin made for that and I follow the insert, or I freeze, or I fridge. I do not subtract lemon from a tested jam.

Citric acid is allowed in the amounts they give for whole figs. I keep a jar of it. I still like lemon in jam for flavor. I do not swap randomly without the bulletin in front of me.

Vinegar is not a casual substitute in a sweet jam I invented. If a tested pickle wants vinegar, that is a different page.

Zone 8a elevation here is low. Process times still follow the table for **your** elevation. [VERIFY] yours. Ours is under 1,000 feet. I do not lend that sentence to a mountain kitchen.

Fridge jam can be a different contract. Canned jam is this contract. Do not blur them on a lid.

If an editor cuts one jam draft, they do not cut this one.

I put the lemon in when the recipe says. NCHFP’s no-pectin jam adds it near the end and cooks one more minute. I do not skip that minute.

A guest who “never uses lemon in fig preserves” can tell me about their grandmother after I have measured mine. Grandmother may have been lucky, or tart, or feeding a fridge. I am feeding a shelf.

The pot does not get a personality override. The line is the line.

I do not use lime from a bottle I cannot confirm is the same acidity as the lemon the bulletin named, not as a casual swap. If a tested page says lemon, I use lemon. Curiosity is a fridge pot.

I keep the lemon on the counter before I turn on the burner so the pot cannot start without it. Out of sight is how I tell myself I will add it “in a minute.”

A guest who wants “just figs and sugar, like the old folks” can have a fridge pot. The shelf is not old folks. The shelf is a line a lab drew. I can respect a grandmother and still measure.

If I lose the bottle mid-batch I stop. I do not finish a canned load on a squeeze of one fresh lemon and a hope. I fridge what I have or I wait. Stopping is a method.
