Dried figs are food. I put them on a board next to cheese if we have cheese. I chop them into oatmeal. I stew a handful in a little water and pour that over pork or over biscuits. I bake them into a cake that can stand a sticky fruit. I eat two standing at the cupboard. That is the use list.

I will not tell you they are a fiber protocol. I will not tell you they are a sugar strategy. I will not tell you they are ancient medicine because a history post mentioned fig cakes. PapaFig wrote a history post. It was history. It was not a dose.

If someone needs a medical plan they already have a person for that. This jar is not that person.

In the kitchen, dried is a different fruit than fresh. Fresh is jammy and perishable. Dried is concentrated and portable. I do not expect a dried Celeste to taste like the July bowl. I expect it to taste like July remembered.

To cook: I cover them with hot water, or tea, or orange juice if I want to be fancy, and I wait until they take a bend. Then I chop. If I throw hard coins into a batter I get rocks.

I do not sugar them again unless the recipe is a candy. They already did that work on the tree.

Kids who will not eat a fresh fig will sometimes eat a dried one because it looks like a store snack. Fine. I still do not call it a vitamin.

Gifts: a pint of named, dated figs with a note that says “stew a few in water if they are stubborn.” I do not include a health card.

Zone does not change the ethics. 8a just means the jar exists because we had a glut and a machine.

I already wrote a caption once that wandered toward “good for you.” I cut it. The fruit is good. That is a taste sentence.

If I want a savory use I stew them with onion and a splash of vinegar. If I want a simple use I put them on the table and I get out of the way.

The freezer bag of halves that never got dried is for jam and for baking. The dried jar is for the cupboard. I do not mix the jobs in my head.

A restaurant will wrap bacon around a dried fig and charge you. We can do that at home if we want. We can also not. Nothing about 8a requires a passed hors d’oeuvre.

I keep a small jar by the coffee because that is where I will remember it. The big jar stays dark. The small jar gets refilled. That is the whole hospitality plan.

If a fig is too seedy for someone, jam was the better gift. I do not apologize for seeds. I offer a different jar.

This draft exists so an editor does not add a nutrient table to a harvest set. The table would be a different site. FigRoots is a growing site that also puts fruit in a kitchen. Growing and cooking. Not a clinic.

I will stew dried figs with a strip of orange peel for a pork roast. I will not call that a pairing guide. It is supper.

I will chop them into a stuffing if we have a bird and I have a jar. I will not write a holiday essay around it.

If a baker wants a weight, I give a handful and a shrug. Our leather is not a commercial dice. They can chop to the cup they need.

The only claim I will repeat is that the jar is from this yard, this year, this name if I wrote it. That is a place claim. That is enough.
