I would sun-dry figs if I lived in a sentence that has a dry season. I live in South Arkansas 8a. August air here is a wet blanket. A screen of halved figs in that air is a fly tray with ambitions.

People send me California porch photos. Brown Mission halves leathering in three hot days. Good for them. Our three hot days come with dew at night and a thunderstorm on the fourth. Dew undoes a day of work. Rain finishes the joke. I have brought in a tray that looked promising at 6 p.m. and looked like a science fair at 7 a.m.

Sun-drying needs dry air, all-day sun, and nights that do not put water back. Extension notes that mention sun-drying usually mention sulfur or screens and a climate that cooperates. I am not running sulfur on the porch for a magazine still. I am not fighting raccoons for a screen. I plug in a box with a fan.

If you still want to try sun, I will not stop you. I will tell you what I already did wrong. I put halves on a window screen over two sawhorses, cheesecloth on top, south side of the shop. Day one: hopeful. Night one: cloth wet, fruit tacky. Day two: yellow jackets. I finished those halves in the dehydrator and I called the sun a preheat. That is the honest use of our sun — knock the surface wet off before the machine, if the day is actually dry. A “dry day” here is a rumor you verify with your skin, not the weather app icon.

Zone 7 can have a drier August some years. They still get dew. Zone 9 Gulf is not invited to this method at all. A high desert 8a — if that is your 8a — is a different conversation. Inland dry 8a is not South Arkansas 8a. The number on the map is not the water in the air.

Flies, yellow jackets, and a neighbor cat will find a sun tray faster than you will find the motivation to bring it in at dusk. If you cannot commit to in-at-dusk, out-after-dew, you do not have a sun-dry. You have a buffet.

I keep the sun story in the set so nobody “improves” a dehydrator draft by adding a porch method as if it were equal. It is not equal here. It is how you teach yourself to hate drying.

What I will do with a truly dry September stretch: maybe. I still prefer the box. The box does not care that a front is coming at 2 a.m.

Dried figs are a climate product as much as a fruit product. We can make them. We make them with a fan and a number, not with a postcard.

A car dashboard “oven” is the other internet trick. I tried a tray on the dash once. The cab hit a heat I did not measure and the fruit cooked on the bottom and stayed wet in the middle. Cooked-wet is not dry. It is jam with a tan. I do not use the truck as a dehydrator. The truck is for moving bowls.

If a guest from a dry county wants to sun-dry at our place, I give them the machine and I let them argue with the dew themselves if they insist. I do not argue long. The tray will.

I have no published sun-dry success rate to quote. I have a memory of cheesecloth and yellow jackets. That is enough. The live site does not need a fake rustic method to sound Southern. Southern here is humidity. The honest Southern method is electricity or a pot. The porch can hold a chair. It cannot hold a climate we do not have.
