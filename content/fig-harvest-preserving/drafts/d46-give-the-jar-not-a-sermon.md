A jar is a good gift. A lecture is a bad ribbon.

I write the name of the fruit, the word jam or fridge or freezer, the year, and my name if they will not remember. I do not write a fiber count. I do not write “heart.” I do not write a Bible verse about figs unless the person already lives in that language and asked. PapaFig wrote a history note that figs show up in old stories. That is history. It is not a dosage.

If the jar is fridge or freezer I say so twice — lid and mouth. If the jar is canned I do not pretend it is medicine that lives forever. I say “cupboard, dark, eat this year.”

I do not give a jar that failed a seal. I do not give a science-project split. I do not give a mystery mix I would not eat.

I already printed a cute label with a leaf and a claim I copied from a candy box. I threw those labels out. Cute can stay. Claims go.

If someone is careful about sugar I give dried fruit or a bag of frozen halves and I let them cook. I do not make a “lite” canned jam I cannot defend.

Kids can decorate a ring. The facts stay mine.

Zone does not change manners. 8a just means the gift exists because the tree was loud.

Jack can hand a cutting and a rule. I hand a jar and a noun. Neither of us hands a clinic.

If they ask how to grow the tree, that is a different folder. If they ask whether figs will fix a problem in their body, I say I grow fruit. That is the end of my authority.

The set ends here on purpose. Harvest, dry, freeze, jam. Human voice. No sermon.

A card can say “Celeste, September, our row.” A card can say “keep in the fridge.” A card cannot say a fig will do a job a doctor does.

I like to give a jar with a loaf or with nothing. The jar does not need a costume.

If they bring the jar back empty I refill it if I have it. If they bring it back full I do not take offense. Not every kitchen wants sugar. The gift was the offer.

I will take a jar to a porch and I will sit. I will not stand in a doorway and explain pH unless they are going to can. Then I will hand them the NCHFP page, not my speech.

This is the last draft. If an editor cuts, they can cut a spice note before they cut this fence.

We picked. We dried. We froze. We jammed. We labeled. We gave. We did not pretend a fruit was a treatment. That is the harvest I want on the site.

I will not put a QR code to a blog post about “benefits” on a lid. If they want to grow a tree, FigRoots has pages for that. If they want to can, NCHFP has pages for that. If they want a plan for a body, they already know that is not a fig site.

A jar can travel to a porch in town. A jar can sit on a table with cornbread. A jar cannot carry a claim I would not say to Keith out loud in the shop.

If I run out of Celeste I do not relabel Turkey. The gift is the truth. The truth is enough sugar and enough fruit and a name we actually picked.
