Not every pot is a canner day.

A saucepan of chopped ripe figs, sugar to taste I can defend for a fridge, a measured splash of lemon because I like it and because I am in the habit, cooked until it sheets or until I say “this is a spoon jam,” into clean jars, into the refrigerator. We eat it in a short stretch. I write FRIDGE and a date. I do not put that jar in the cupboard. I do not give it as a shelf gift.

This is how a Tuesday bowl becomes toast. This is how I do not open the canner for four cups of fruit.

I still sort fruit. Fridge is not a compost. Fizzy figs do not become a cute saucepan story.

I do not give fridge jam a water-bath time I invented to “be safe.” If I want shelf, I use a tested recipe and a real process. If I want tonight, I use the fridge.

How long: I treat it like a cooked leftover with sugar, not like a canned jar. A couple of weeks is the neighborhood I live in. If it looks wrong, smells wrong, or grows a hat, it is done. I do not scrape a hat.

I already found a “fridge” jar in a cupboard in October. I threw it. The lid had been helpful. The label had not been bold.

Zone 7 kitchens do this all season. Zone 9 kitchens should remember the fridge is not a cave. Our 8a fridge is busy in August. I make space or I freeze the fruit instead.

Vanilla, ginger, black pepper: fridge is where I play. Canner is where I copy. Play in a shelf jar is how people invent a process.

A small batch can still burn. I stir. I use a pot bigger than I think. Fig sugar climbs.

This draft keeps a person cooking when they do not have rings and a rack. It does not replace d37.

I like a wide pan so water leaves without a half-hour scorch. A deep pot is a trap for a small batch.

If I use pectin in a fridge jam I still follow the paper. I just skip the fiction that the fridge turned it into a canned product.

Kids can stir a small batch with a long spoon and a warning about steam. They cannot decide the jar goes on the shelf.

I serve fridge jam colder. I tell guests it is fridge jam. Honesty is part of the flavor.

When the small batch works I write what I did on a card. When I want to can that idea I go find a tested path that is close, or I stay in the fridge. I do not promote a card to a bulletin.

I use a pot I can see the bottom of. A dark pot hides a scorch until the smell tells the house.

A stick blender in a small batch is optional. I like some pieces. I do not want baby food unless I said baby food. If I blend I watch steam. Hot sugar on a wrist is a kitchen fact, not a clinic, and not a joke I repeat.

Fridge jam goes to the back of the fridge where I will see it when I hunt butter. Hidden behind a cabbage is how it grows a hat.

If I am going to be gone a week I freeze the small batch or I give it. I do not leave a fridge jam as a surprise for a house-sitter who thinks all jars are cupboard jars.
