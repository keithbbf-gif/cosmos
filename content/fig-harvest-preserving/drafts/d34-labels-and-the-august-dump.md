Jack writes names on pots with a paint pen because labels leave. I write names on bags because memory leaves faster.

A freezer after an 8a August dump is a graveyard of “figs” if I am lazy. In January I want:

- name (or “mix” if I meant it)
- cut (whole / half / puree)
- pack (DRY / SWEET)
- date
- COOK if it was splits or tired fruit

That is enough. I do not write a paragraph. I do not write a heart.

Painter’s tape on a bag sticks until it does not. I write on the tape and I write on the bag. Two places. Frost eats ink.

I already thawed a bag of “figs” into a pale gold jam I sold in my head as Celeste. It was late Alma and a little Turkey. Fine jam. Wrong story. I do not like lying to a lid.

Mix is allowed if I write MIX. Mix is how a last colander becomes cubes. Mix is not how I hide that I did not sort.

Shop habit and kitchen habit should match. If the pot says LSU Gold, the bag says LSU Gold. “Gold fig” is how two golds become one argument.

Zone does not change handwriting. Volume does. The dump is the test of the habit.

I keep a paper list on the freezer: tallies, not poetry. When I pull a bag I mark it. When I add a bag I add it. This is not inventory software. This is a card.

Kids like to decorate bags. I let them draw after I write the facts. Facts first.

If a bag loses its tape I open it when I have time and I relabel or I cook it. Mystery is a cook bag.

The August dump will happen again. The labels are how I still like the dump.

I use the same abbreviations every year. HALF not H. PUREE not mash/pulp/smash depending on my mood. Future me is not a decoder.

A neighbor who borrows freezer space gets their name on the bag too. I have cooked someone else’s fruit into my Saturday. That was a friendship tax I did not enjoy.

If I freeze for a gift I write FOR and a name. I do not want to open that bag for a Tuesday sauce.

This is the least romantic draft in the set. It is also how the romantic jars stay honest.

I write on bags before they go in so I am not labeling a frozen film with a dying marker. Ink on frost is a smear. Tape on a dry bag is a sentence.

If two people pick, two people label or one person owns the pen. Shared memory is how “Turkey” becomes “the dark ones.” The dark ones are three names.

I keep a roll of painter’s tape in the kitchen and a roll in the shop. The shop roll is how I label a pot. The kitchen roll is how I label a bag. Same handwriting on purpose. Winter-me can read both.

A bag that only says 8/12 is a problem in two years. I write 8/12/26. The extra two digits are cheaper than a mystery.

If the dump is so loud I skip a name, I write MIX-COOK and I do not pretend later I sorted. Honesty on a bag is a kind of harvest.

I do not use a red marker on a red bag. I cannot read it in January. Black on tan tape. Boring. Legible. I have wasted a January morning holding a bag to the shop light. I do not miss that morning.
