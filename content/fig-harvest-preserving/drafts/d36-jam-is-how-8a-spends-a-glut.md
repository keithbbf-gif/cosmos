Fresh is the best fig. The tree does not stop at best.

In this climate the ripe fruit arrives in a heap. Friends can take some. The dehydrator can take some. The freezer can take a lot. Jam is how the heap becomes a shelf I can give away without saying “cook this bag.”

I do not jam first-year souvenirs. I do not jam tired fizzy fruit. I jam ripe fruit I already sorted, or frozen halves I already trusted. The pot is not a laundry.

South Arkansas 8a has the heat to ripen and the humidity to ruin. Jam is not nostalgia. It is logistics.

A case of jars is a decision about sugar, time, and who we are feeding. I do not owe the internet a pantry that looks like a store. I owe the fruit a path that is not the compost.

I already made jam because I thought that is what fig people do, when I only had a bowl. The jar was lonely and too sweet. I wait for a real pile now, or I make a fridge half-pint and I call it that.

Zone 7 jam weeks are shorter. They should freeze more and jam once. Zone 9 can stretch pot days. We spike. We freeze. We jam on purpose.

The `Fig Jam Article` folder on the drive exists because this kitchen already lived this. I am not going to invent a new origin story.

Jam sits next to drying and freezing, not above them. A person who only jams will hate August. A person who never jams will watch a tree rot. I do both. I sleep.

If we only had one method, I would freeze. Frozen fruit can still become jam. Jam cannot become a half on a tray. Order of operations is a kindness.

This draft is the door to the acid draft. Do not skip the door and do not skip the acid.

I like the sound of lids pinging. I like it less than I like a method I can defend. A ping is not a test of a recipe I invented at 10 p.m.

Neighbors get jars. The shop does not get a sales pitch about wellness. The jar is fruit and sugar. That is plenty.

If the glut is small I skip the canner and I make toast jam. If the glut is loud I schedule a Saturday. The tree does not get to schedule me every night.

A Southern fig jam is a real thing. A Southern fig jam made from sour fruit is a real mistake. The climate gives us both. I pick the first.

I do not jam because a calendar said “preserving season.” I jam because a tape list said I have enough halves and a Saturday. The calendar is a zone card. The tape list is the crop.

If the year is thin I make fewer jars and I do not apologize to a tradition. A thin year is a freeze-and-eat year. Tradition can have toast from a fridge pot.

If the year is loud I still do not owe the internet a wall of jars. I owe the fruit a path. Some of that path is neighbors with empty bowls. Some of it is this pot. Both count as harvest.

A person who only knows fig as a cookie filling can meet a jar and like it. I do not need them to meet a lecture. I need them to meet a spoon.

If I am tired of sugar I stop making jars and I keep freezing. Tired sugar is how a pot burns and a person swears off a tree that did nothing wrong.
