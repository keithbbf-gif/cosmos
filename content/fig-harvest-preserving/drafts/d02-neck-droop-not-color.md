Color is how catalogs sell figs. Neck droop is how you pick them.

A Celeste can be brown and still a rock. A LSU Gold can be pale and already honey. Brown Turkey in our humidity will show a bronze shoulder for days before the neck gives up. If you pick by the postcard, you will fill a bowl with pretty disappointments.

I look at the neck first. The little stem between the fruit and the wood goes from stiff to a shrug. The fig hangs under its own weight. On a windy afternoon you can see the ready ones nod. The hard ones stay at attention like they have a job.

Second: give. Not a squeeze that bruises. A fingertip on the side. Ripe fruit yields and comes back a little. Overripe fruit yields and stays dented. Hard fruit laughs at you.

Third: skin. Ripe often loses the hard shine. A faint wrinkle at the neck is a gift. Splits after rain are a clock, not a variety flaw — see the rain draft. A sour smell at an open eye is a no, even if the neck drooped yesterday.

I do not use “days from set” as a harvest date. A fig can sit marble-hard through a cool spell and then finish in two hot days. Write the weather, not a spreadsheet of “July 18 every year.” July 18 is a rumor we tell in January.

Zone 8a mornings help. Cool fruit shows the neck better. Midday sun makes everything look ready because you are hot and the birds are already working. I pick before breakfast when I can. I pick again after a storm because that is when the splits start.

What color is good for: finding the fruit in the leaves. Dark figs hide. Green figs hide worse. I still walk the bush with my hand, not just my eyes. The ones I miss are usually on the back side, neck already bent, wasp already invited.

I already trusted a photo of a “ripe” Black Mission from a California board and waited for our fruit to go that purple-black. Ours never did. The necks had been telling the truth for a week. I left them. The birds filed a claim.

Variety notes I will actually use, not a flavor wheel:

- Celeste: small, closed eye, brown-violet when honest. Neck droop is obvious because the fruit is light. If it is still sticking straight out, leave it.
- Brown Turkey: bigger, slower to droop, easy to pick early because it looks finished. Wait.
- LSU Purple / LSU Gold: color fools guests. Neck and give do not.
- Chicago Hardy: can look dull and still be early. Taste one. If it is flat, wait two days.
- Alma: late, green-gold, easy to call unripe until it isn’t. The neck will tell you in September when you are tired of walking.

Zone 7 growers sometimes never see a full color show because the season ends. They learn neck faster. Steal that. Zone 9 growers can wait for a jammy collapse on the branch. Steal that only if you like sharing with hornets.

If you need a teaching fig for a kid or a guest, pick one that is already hanging, split it, and let them see the jam inside. Then hand them a hard one of the same color. The lesson sticks better than a paragraph.

I keep a bad habit of photographing the pretty ones and picking the ugly ones. The ugly ones with a bent neck are the crop. The pretty upright ones are next Tuesday. If I only take what looks like a magazine cover I leave the actual harvest for the wasps.

A split after a rain can still be a good fig if you get it the same morning and the smell is fig, not wine. A split that sat is compost. Color does not sort those two. Your nose and the clock do.

When I am teaching the row I make people put their phone away. A camera flattens the neck. In person the hinge is obvious. In a photo everything looks like a plum. Walk. Touch one. Leave the rest that do not shrug.
