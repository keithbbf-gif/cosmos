Size decides the knife.

NCHFP says leave small fruit whole and cut larger fruit in half. That is the sentence I use. Celeste often goes whole after a check — a 30-second boil until the skin splits, then ice, then dry. Brown Turkey gets a slice from stem to eye and opens like a book. A giant name I do not grow on purpose would get wheels.

Whole fruit that is not checked becomes a leather bag with jam inside. The skin tightens. The middle stays wet. You will think it is done because the outside wrinkles. You will jar it. You will find a wet center in November and you will meet mold.

Halves dry honestly. You can see the center. You can pull the thin ones. You can leave the thick ones. The cost is more tray space and more surface for flies if you are still pretending to sun-dry.

Slices — coins, wheels — dry fastest and look like a product from a bag. They also turn to chips if you forget them. I slice when I want a cookie add-in or a trail mix that is not a whole sticky bomb. I do not slice a fancy name into coins unless I have a lot of it. Coins erase the fruit a little.

I do not peel. Peeling a glut of figs is a church you can attend without me. Skins hold the thing together. If a skin is tough after drying, the fruit was picked early or dried too hot.

Stem on or off: I take the stem if it is woody. I leave a tiny bit if it helps me handle a whole Celeste. I do not owe anyone a stemless aesthetic.

I already dried a mixed tray of wholes and halves because I was in a hurry. The halves were done when the wholes were still puddings. I ate the halves. I put the wholes back. The mixed tray was a time tax.

Zone does not change the geometry. Humidity changes how much the whole fruit fights you. In 8a I default to halves unless the fruit is truly small and I am willing to check it.

A puree smear on a liner makes fig leather. That is a fifth product. I do it with leftovers that are too soft to look at. I do not call leather “dried figs.” I call it leather.

Pick one shape per load when you can. Your future self, standing at the jar, should know what the contract was.

I keep a paring knife for the tray and I keep it clean. Latex on the board is fine. Ants on the board are not. I work in small piles so fruit does not sit cut in the heat while I chase a phone.

Seeds are not a defect. A fig is a hollow flower with crunch. If someone wants seedless they want a different pantry item. I do not sieve a glut to please that person.

Wheels from a Turkey can look like dried tomatoes if I slice them too thin and I run the heat too high. I have served those. They were fine on a cracker. They were not what I meant. I go thicker on wheels now, or I stay with halves.

If the fruit is split already, it is not a whole and it is not a pretty half. It is a cook piece. I freeze those or I jam them. I do not give splits a tray as if the dehydrator will forgive a fermented eye.

I do not dry a fig I would not eat over the sink. The machine concentrates what you already had. A faint wine note becomes a loud wine note. That is not a variety. That is a skip I did not take.
