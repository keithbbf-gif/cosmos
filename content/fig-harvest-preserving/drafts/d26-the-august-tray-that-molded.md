I have thrown a tray. I will throw another if I have to. Mold is not a seasoning.

How it happens here: fruit a little too wet, kitchen a little too wet, machine off overnight because I thought we were “close,” lid closed, August. In the morning the fuzz is in the glue spots where two halves touched. Sometimes it is only one coin. I do not excavate the good ones. The tray is a loss.

Prevention is ugly and simple. Ripe, sound fruit. Space on the tray. Heat and fan until the bend test, not until I am sleepy. If the power blinks, spread the fruit in the fridge or the freezer. Do not leave a warm wet box to think about its life.

Conditioning catches the almosts. If the jar sweats, we go back to the machine. If I skip the jar week I meet the fuzz later, in a cupboard, which is worse, because I already gave some away in my head.

I already tried to save a tray by picking off the white and running the rest an hour hotter. That is how you get a story you should not tell at a table. I tell it here so I do not do it again.

Sun trays mold faster. That is the California-sentence draft. Oven trays mold if you leave them in a dead warm oven overnight. Same sin, different box.

Zone 7 winter mold is less common and then someone gets sloppy and it is common. Zone 9 summer mold is our cousin. Nobody is special.

Flies on a cooling tray are not mold. They are a different reason to throw food. Screens, timing, a clean counter.

If a finished jar shows one bad fig I do not play surgeon. The jar goes. I like figs. I like my house more.

The glut will tempt you to keep everything. The trash can is part of preserving. So is the freezer when the machine is behind.

I clean trays with hot water and I let them dry before the next load. A damp tray in this air is a head start I did not want.

This is the failure draft on purpose. A set that only wins is a catalog.

I keep the machine where I will hear it. A dehydrator in a closed spare room is a machine I will forget to rotate. Forgotten is the first word in a mold story.

If I smell sour in the first two hours I stop and I sort. Sour fruit should not have made the tray. The yard walk failed first. The machine cannot launder a fermented eye.

A white bloom on a truly dry, conditioned fig can be sugar. I know the difference because I have been fooled and I have been right. Sugar bloom is dry and sandy. Mold is wet and fuzzy and wrong. If I have to argue with myself, I throw it. The argument is the tell.

I do not photograph mold for the site. The caption would turn into a joke. The joke would get shared. Someone would try to save a tray because the joke made it folksy. Folksy is how you keep a bad habit.

Empty clean trays are the still. The lesson stays in prose.

I do not run a bleach bath on fruit I plan to keep. If I am at bleach I am at throw. Clean the trays. Do not launder the figs.

A load that smelled fine and looked dusty might be sugar bloom. A load that smelled sweet-wrong is mold. I have stood in the kitchen arguing. The argument is the cue to throw. I can dry another tray. I cannot unsay a gift I already worried about.

If the machine has a foam gasket that stays wet, I dry the gasket. A wet gasket in 8a is a petri dish with a fan. I learned that after a load that failed at the rim of every tray. The fruit was not the only damp thing.
