NCHFP will tell you you can freeze fruit with sugar, with syrup, or without. Sugar is not what makes the freezer safe. Cold is. Sugar helps color and texture. That is the whole sermon.

I dry-pack most of our figs. Tray-frozen, bagged, no sugar. They go to jam and to cooking. I do not need a syrup when I am going to cook them in sugar later anyway.

I sugar-pack when I want a thaw that looks more like fruit and less like a collapse. A light toss of sugar on halves, or a light syrup poured over, then headspace in a tub. This is a dessert pack. It is also sticky. It takes more freezer space because of liquid.

I do not invent ratios as a tested bulletin. If I syrup, I use a light syrup the way fruit-canning notes describe light — more water than a heavy candy. I cool it first. I do not pour hot syrup on a plastic bag.

Lemon in a sugar pack is still a color note.

I already sweet-packed a bag I later dumped into a jam pot and then had to guess the sugar. The jam went too far. I now keep sweet packs labeled SWEET and dry packs labeled DRY. That is not cute. That is how I do not ruin a Saturday.

Artificial sweeteners in the freezer: the bulletins say they can sweeten and they do not do what sugar does for texture. I do not build a diet product line in this set. If someone needs that, they already have a package insert. I am not writing one.

Zone 7 and Zone 9 freeze the same way. Our 8a problem is volume, not physics. Dry-pack is how I move volume.

A syrup pack that leaks in the freezer is a glue drawer. I double-bag or I use a tub.

I do not sugar fruit that is already tired and call it an improvement. Sugar does not fix a sour eye.

If I am giving a bag away I dry-pack and I say “for cooking.” If I want to give a nicer thaw I say so and I sugar-pack a small tub.

Quality is a word I will use. Safety is the freezer staying cold. I do not mix those words to sound official.

A medium syrup is for people who want sliced peaches. Figs already weep. A heavy syrup is candy. I stay light or I stay dry.

I do not pack figs in honey as a freezer method I will repeat. Honey stays messy at these temps and I cannot measure it the same way twice when I am tired.

If a guest asks whether sugar “preserves” the bag on the counter, I say no. The counter is the enemy. The freezer is the method. Sugar is makeup.

Makeup is allowed. Makeup is labeled.

I do not pack figs in leftover pancake syrup and call it a light syrup. Grocery syrup is a different sugar and a different flavor. If I syrup, I make a syrup from white sugar and water I measured, cool it, and I pour.

A dry-pack bag that leaks juice later was packed too warm or too wet. I tray longer. I do not fix it with sugar after the fact.

If I am teaching a person who is scared of sugar I still tell them the freezer is the safety. I do not let them leave fruit on a counter because they did not want sugar. Fear of sugar is not a preservation method. Cold is.

Sweet pack tubs get more headspace than I think. Sugar plus fruit plus freeze expansion is a lid pop. I have cleaned that shelf. I leave room now.
