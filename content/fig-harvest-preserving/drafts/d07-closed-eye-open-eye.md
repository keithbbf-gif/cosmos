Jack already wrote the humidity sentence on FigRoots: if your climate is wet, look for tight eyes and smaller fruit. I am not going to turn that into a catalog. I am going to tell you what it means at 7 a.m. in August.

The ostiole is the eye at the bottom of the fig. A closed or tight eye does not give beetles, rain, and yeast an easy door. An open eye does. In a dry California week an open eye is a detail. In South Arkansas it is a fermentation project.

Celeste is the teaching closed eye. Small fruit, tight ostiole, the reason aunts called it a sugar fig and not a science project. Magnolia / Brunswick is the teaching open eye. Bigger, wetter, fine in the right week, sour in the wrong one if you blink.

Harvest does not change the eye. It changes how fast you have to move. Open-eye names I pick the morning they shrug, not “when I get a minute.” I do not leave them to “fully collapse” like a Mission postcard. Collapse here is sour.

Closed-eye names still split. Tight is not waterproof. It is a better door. I still walk daily in the glut.

Souring smells like wine that sat open. The pulp looks wet and wrong. The eye may show yeast or a dried foam. That fruit does not go in a jar. It does not go in a dehydrator. It goes off the property or into a hole. One sour fig in a jam pot is how you teach a kitchen to hate you.

Nitidulid beetles — the little picnic beetles — love an open ripe fruit. I do not have a spray speech. I have a pick speech. Fruit on the ground is a beetle hotel. I rake.

Zone 7 dryness can make an open-eye name look easy. Then a wet August arrives and the forum post appears. Zone 9 Gulf growers already sorted this and planted Celeste, Alma, the LSU set. Steal their list if you are tired of wine figs. Do not steal a California “wait for the honey drop” on an open eye in our weather.

I already kept a pretty open-eye fig an extra day for a photo. The photo is fine. The fig was not. I do not trade a bowl for a still.

What I tell a guest who wants “big grocery figs”: those are often a different climate and a different eye. We can grow large fruit here. We cannot grow careless fruit here. If they want size and a lazy harvest, they want a different county.

Air in the canopy helps leaves and helps you see fruit. It does not close an eye. You cannot prune a Magnolia into a Celeste. You can pick it like you mean it.

If a name sours three years in a row in this yard, I stop making excuses for it. I keep one plant for curiosity or I do not. Curiosity is allowed. Jam is a closed-eye job in August.

When I sort a morning bowl I turn each fig and look at the eye. Tight and clean goes to the table or the dryer. Open and wet goes to the pot the same day. Open and fizzy goes out. That sort takes two minutes and saves a batch.

I will still grow an open-eye name if the flavor is worth the walk. I will not recommend it to a person who goes to the lake for a week in August. They need Celeste and a neighbor with a key.

A grocery Mission is a climate I do not have. I stop comparing our August bowl to that plastic box. The box was picked firm and shipped. Ours is picked ripe and has to move. Different fruit. Same word. That is the whole confusion.
