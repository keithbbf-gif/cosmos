Fig sap is not a personality test. It is latex. It runs when you break a stem, snap a leaf, or harvest like you are angry. In sun it is worse on skin. I am not going to write a clinic. I am going to write a glove.

I wear thin nitrile when I am on a loaded bush in July. I take them off to wash fruit. I do not rub my eyes. I do not wipe sweat with the same hand I just used to twist a stem. That is the whole medical section. If your skin complains, stop and wash. If it keeps complaining, you already know to talk to a person who is not a fig blog.

The white milk on a green stem is the same milk we wait to not see when we cut dormant wood. Harvest wood is awake. It will bleed. A ripe fig on a shrugging neck often lets go with less of a fight and less of a mess. A fig you wrench will paint you.

Clothes take spots. I do not harvest in the shirt I like. Latex marks do not always come out. That is a laundry note, not a tragedy.

Kids helping: gloves or a long sleeve and a short session. I do not make a child prove they are tough on a Celeste bush. There will be other mornings.

I already harvested a hot afternoon without gloves because the box was in the shop. My wrist looked like I had been painting. I finished the bowl. I did not finish the day comfortable. Morning, gloves, done.

Zone does not change sap. Heat changes how much you notice it. 8a July is a notice.

Tools: I do not need pruners to pick ripe fruit. If I need pruners, I am probably taking wood, not figs. Keep the harvest bowl and the cutting bag as two jobs. Sap on a clean dormant cutting is a different mess.

Wash the fruit at the sink, not in a tub you also use for cuttings unless you like grit in the jam. Hose the row dirt off the skins. You do not owe the latex a ritual. You owe your hands a barrier.

If a guest has a known problem with fig sap or with sun and sap together, they can hold the bowl. They do not need to prove they are part of the farm.

I will not list home remedies. I will not tell you sap is good for you. I will not tell you it is a medicine. It is a plant leaking. Wear the gloves.

A broken leaf in my shirt collar is how I get a line I did not want. I button up or I wear a cheap long sleeve over a t-shirt and I peel it off at the porch. Heat is real. A rash from being a hero is also real. I choose the sleeve.

Dogs do not need fig sap on their fur. I keep them out of the row when I am snapping stems. This is not a vet paragraph. It is a porch paragraph.

At the end of the walk I turn the gloves inside out and I wash my hands before I touch the shop door, the phone, or my face. That sequence has saved me more times than a clever variety list.

If the sap is on a knife, I wash the knife. I do not lick a taste off the blade to “see if it’s sweet.” That is how you meet latex the stupid way. Taste the fruit, not the tool.
