Dry is not the same as even.

I put cooled figs in a clean, dry glass jar, not quite full, lid on, and I shake them once a day for a week. That is conditioning. The drier pieces pull a little water from the thicker ones. If beads form on the glass, the load was not done. I dump it back on the trays. I do not wipe the glass and call it a day.

This is the step people skip because the fruit already took all afternoon. Skipping is how a pretty jar turns in week three.

I use glass so I can see. A decorative tin is a hiding place. A plastic bag is a condensing tent in a warm kitchen.

A week is the habit. If the weather is ugly and the kitchen is a swamp, I condition in the AC and I look harder. 8a August can put moisture back through a cheap lid. I want a lid that actually seals after the week, not a cork from a craft drawer.

I already stored a load the night I pulled it, no wait, because company was coming and I wanted a rustic jar on the table. Company was fine. The jar later had a white spot I did not serve. I threw the jar. I do not throw that story.

If I find one wet fig in the shake, I pull it and I re-dry the rest if the glass is sweating. One wet fig is a sponge.

Pasteurizing or freezing the finished jar for a few days is a bug insurance some old notes mention. I will freeze a questionable load. I will not invent a pest I did not see.

Zone 7 dry pantries forgive more. Ours do not. Steal the week.

After conditioning I move them to a storage jar, full enough to limit air, dark cupboard, not over the stove. The stove cupboard is a second dehydrator you did not want.

Label the name and the month. “Figs” is how you eat the good Celeste first and leave the bland Turkey until you resent the cupboard.

Conditioning is boring. Boring is why it works.

I keep the jar on the counter where I will see it. A jar in the pantry during the week is a jar I will not shake. Out of sight is how you skip Wednesday.

If the house is full of kids I still shake. I let them shake. I do not let them snack from the conditioning jar until the week is done. A missing handful is a missing data point.

Beads on day two are a gift. They told me early. Beads on day seven after I ignored day two are a lecture.

I do not add rice or a silica packet as a personality unless I am desperate and I know the packet is food-safe. The real fix is more tray time. Packets hide a wet load. I would rather run the machine again.

When the week is clean I write “OK” and the date on the lid. That mark is for winter-me. Winter-me does not remember how humid August felt.

I do not condition in a warm truck cab or on a porch because the kitchen is crowded. The week wants steady indoor air. A porch in 8a is a dew machine at night.

If I open the jar to snack during the week I restart the look. A hand in the jar is moisture and a crumb. Crumbs are not a method.

A second week is fine if I am nervous. A third week on the counter is how I forget and eat the experiment. After a clean week I store. Conditioning is a pause, not a lifestyle.
