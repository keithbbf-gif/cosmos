“Checking” is not a personality. It is a 30-second boil.

NCHFP: dip whole figs in boiling water about **30 seconds** or until the skins split, then ice water, then drain hard, then dry. The skin needs a door. If you do not open a door, the inside stays a swamp and the outside becomes a drum.

I do this for small Celestes I want to keep whole. I do not do it for halves. Halves already have a door.

The pot is not a blanch you wander away from. Thirty seconds is thirty seconds. I use a small batch in a strainer so I can lift them all at once. A crowded pot gives you some split and some still tight and a few that cooked.

Ice bath stops the cook. Then I spread them on towels until they are not dripping. Water on the tray is time you buy for mold.

I already skipped the check because the fruit “looked thin-skinned.” The jar in January had two that were perfect and six that were wet pockets. I threw the wet ones. I did not throw the lesson.

Lemon water after the check is a color habit some bulletins like. I will splash bottled lemon in the ice water if I remember. I will not turn it into a ceremony. Acid here is not a canning safety step. Drying is a water step.

Do not check splits and tired fruit and then expect a clean tray. Checking is for sound small figs. Tired fruit belongs in the pot.

Zone 8a water is fine. I do not need a special water story. I need a rolling boil and a clock.

If the skins never split, I nick them with a knife and I admit the boil was shy or the fruit was thick. I do not boil them for three minutes to force it. Three minutes is a poach. Poached figs can be a dessert. They are a worse dry.

I dry checked wholes the same day. A bowl of checked figs in the fridge overnight is a wet handshake.

This is the one pretreatment I will defend as useful. Sulfur I will not run on this porch. Honey dips I will talk about later as theater. The check is a door.

I keep a kettle just for this in the glut weeks so I am not arguing with a pasta pot. The strainer, the bowl of ice, the towels — I set them before I pick the small fruit. If I pick first and then hunt ice, the fruit sits and I skip the check.

A guest who sees the boil thinks I am starting jam. I let them think that until the ice goes in. Then I say the word checking and I do not make it mystical.

If I am drying only halves that day I put the kettle away. Mixing a checked-whole story into a half-tray day is how I scald fruit I meant to slice.

The skins will look uglier after a check. Good. Ugly is the door working. Pretty tight skins are the ones that lie in the jar.

I do not check fruit I already halved. That is two methods fighting. I do not check a split. The split is already a door, and often a dirty one.

A boil that is not rolling is a warm bath. Warm baths do not split skins. I wait for the pot to mean it. Then I count. Then I lift. Then I ice. The sequence is the method. Talking through it while the fruit sits in hot water is how I poach.

If I am drying for a gift I still check. Pretty tight skins on a gift jar are a wet-center letter I will get in January. I would rather send an ugly leather than a pretty lie.
