Breba is last year’s wood keeping a promise. In Zone 8a the promise is conditional. A quiet winter, a south wall, a name that actually carries an early crop — then June has fruit. A 12°F night with wind at the crown, or a February heading you took because a bulletin said “one-third,” and June is leaves.

I do not plan jam on breba. I plan a bowl. If the bowl fills two mornings in a row, we eat well. If it doesn’t, I do not write a failure report. The main crop is still the crop.

What breba looks like in May: small figs already sitting at the nodes near the tips of last year’s growth. Not this year’s green shoots. Those are main-crop babies and they are not your June breakfast. If you cannot tell last year’s wood from this year’s, wait until the new shoots are obvious and look at what was already there.

Harvest signs are the same as main crop. Neck, give, smell. Breba can be a little drier, a little less jammy, depending on the name. Desert King and some San Pedro types live and die on this crop. We are not a Desert King orchard. We are a common-fig yard that sometimes gets a bonus.

The winter that steals it is not always dramatic. Two nights in the low teens after a warm spell will burn the tips while the trunk looks fine. You walk out in April to “breba wood” that is brown past the first three nodes. Those peas were already dead. You can still have a main crop on the new wood that pushes below the burn. That is the 8a deal.

I already counted on a June box for a market morning because the previous August had been loud with fruit and the tips were loaded in January. Then we had the kind of cold the zone card admits is legal. June was polite foliage. I looked stupid in the notebook. The notebook now says “breba = maybe.”

Zone 7 should treat breba as a story they tell when a wall is kind. Zone 9 can put it on the calendar in ink. 8a writes it in pencil. Steal the Zone 9 appetite if your wood lived. Steal the Zone 7 shrug if it didn’t.

Do not pick breba early to “beat a freeze.” A May freeze that kills ripe fruit is rare here. A May freeze that burns soft new leaves is less rare. The fruit that is still hard will not ripen in the fridge to save you. Leave it. If it dies on the branch you lost a snack, not a year.

After a late April freeze that takes the breba, do not “make up for it” by stripping new shoots in May. You already lost the early bowl. Let the new wood run. That is now your August.

I will take a picture of a real breba morning. I will not use last year’s June photo as this year’s proof. The folder is called `Breba 2025` for a reason. Years have names.

When someone on a forum says “figs in June,” I ask which wood and which winter. If they cannot answer, they are selling a climate we do not have.

I walk breba wood in January with my hands in my pockets. Pea figs at the tips are a note, not a promise. I write “tips loaded” on the board. I do not write a jam date. If March is ugly I cross the note out. If June is kind I eat.

A guest who tasted a California breba will call ours small. Ours is small. I do not apologize for a bonus fig. I also do not let that guest talk me into heading the bush in February “to make bigger fruit.” Bigger is not the point of a maybe crop. The point is a bowl before the main crop exists.

If we want breba on purpose we leave tips on the names that carry it and we accept the winter tax. If we want a simpler August we can shorten wands and stop talking about June. Both are choices. Pretending 8a is Pasadena is not a choice. It is a story.
