Harvest timing is also other animals’ timing.

Birds find the first soft dark fig and then they teach each other. You can net. You can hang flashy nonsense. You can plant a mulberry as a bribe. What I actually do most weeks is pick earlier than they do and accept a tax. A clean morning bowl beats a fortress I will not maintain.

Wasps and hornets clock in on splits and overripe eyes. I do not harvest a fig with a wasp on it. I do not swat over my own head. I move on. There is another fig. If a bush is a wasp tree this week, I take the far side and I come back. Heroics are how you spill the bowl and get stung.

Ants farm the sugar. A line up the trunk is information. It says fruit is open. I follow the line and I pick. I do not soak the trunk in a product I will not name here. I keep ripe fruit off the ground so the line has less to report.

Ground fruit is a cafeteria. I pick it up whether I will eat it or not. Clean drops can cook. Dirty drops can leave. Leaving them is how beetles move in and how the next fig sours against a mummy.

Dogs and harvest: I do not let a dog invent a fig habit in the row. Fruit and pits and a sick porch are a bad morning. This is not a vet paper. It is a gate.

Zone 7 birds are just as employed. Zone 9 has the same wasps plus more months. Nobody is unique. 8a humidity just makes the damaged fruit go wrong faster, so the race is louder.

I already built a weekend net after the crop was half gone. The net caught my own hat and three ripe figs I could not reach. I pick first. I net if a single tree is a project I will actually tend. I do not net the whole place as a personality.

Green fruit is less interesting to birds than ripe fruit. That is not a reason to pick green. That is a reason to stay on the ripe ones.

If you want less bird pressure, mix names and colors. A yard of one dark fig is a billboard. A yard with Celeste hiding in leaves loses less to the first flock. Jack already said a green fig can be less obvious. True. Still pick it ripe.

The race does not mean spray. It means walk. It means same-day splits. It means you are not the only one who knows the neck drooped.

A fake owl fools a person longer than it fools a crow. I have one in a box. I do not put it back out as a plan.

If a child wants to help, I give them ground cleanup with gloves and I take the wasp zone. That is not unkind. That is how they still like figs tomorrow.

I do not harvest at dusk to “beat the birds tomorrow.” Dusk is when I cannot see the eye and I smash fruit I should have left. Morning is the race I can win part of.

A pecan orchard next door changes the bird week. When those trees are loud, our figs get a little peace. When those trees are quiet, we are the diner. I do not control the pecans. I control the 6:30 walk.

If I am gone two days I tell a person who already knows the shrug test. I do not tell a house-sitter “pick anything brown.” Anything brown includes sour, pecked, and the fig that needed one more day. A bad helper is worse than a tax. I would rather lose fruit to birds than lose a tree’s reputation to a bucket of rocks.
