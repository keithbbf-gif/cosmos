Jam does not erase a name. It cooks a name.

Celeste jam is honey and small seeds and a color that is not purple. It is the jar I hoard. You need more fruit than you think because the fruit is small and you ate half of it in July.

Brown Turkey jam is the workhorse. Milder, darker, more of it. It takes lemon and it takes a little patience in the telling. I do not apologize for it. I label it.

LSU Gold can taste like a different kitchen — lighter, floral if the fruit was honest. Purple goes darker. I do not mix them if I want to learn.

Alma late is a September pot if I still have the will. Honey again, a little different. Worth it if the fruit is ripe.

A mix pot is allowed. I write MIX and the week. I do not call a mix Celeste because I want the lid to sell.

I already made a “mystery dark” from the last bags of the year. It was fine. It taught me nothing for next year. One-name pots teach.

Underripe fruit makes a jam that tastes like a green argument. Overripe fizzy fruit makes a jam I will not gift. The pot is not a blender for mistakes.

Spices: a little ginger with Turkey is a house thing. I test in a fridge batch. Cinnamon can shout. I am careful. Vanilla is easy to like and easy to overdo.

Zone 7 Hardy jam is their jar. I will eat it. I will not pretend it is Celeste. Zone 9 Mission jam is a story I like and do not copy onto a tag we cannot support.

Jack’s flavor words on the live site — honey, berry, sugar, melon — are tasting words, not jam recipes. I steal the words for the lid, not a new chemistry.

If a name is bland in the pot, it was bland on the tree or I picked it early. I do not add more sugar to invent a personality. I cook it and I tell the truth on the lid.

A guest who wants “fig jam” and no name can have Turkey. A guest who wants the July memory can wait for Celeste.

I do not put five names in one pot to use them up unless the lid says so. Using them up is a puree cube job.

The pot is a portrait if I let it be. It is a dump if I do not.

I taste the fruit before I sugar it. If the bowl is flat, the jar will be flat. Sugar is not a personality transplant. Lemon will brighten. It will not invent Celeste.

A pot of shop-pot fruit and row fruit of the same name can still disagree. I have learned to keep them apart until I know. The shop fruit can be sweeter and earlier. The row fruit can be louder. Mixing is a choice I write down.

If a name is seedy and a guest hates seeds, I do not sieve a glut to please them. I hand them a different jar or a different gift. Sieving is a church I attend only for a tiny show pot.

I smell the bag I thawed. Freezer-dull fruit makes a dull jam. I can still eat it. I do not gift it as the July jar.

A single-name pot is also how I learn a cutting was mislabeled. If the jam does not taste like the tag, I write that down for Jack. The pot is a witness. The tag is a claim.
