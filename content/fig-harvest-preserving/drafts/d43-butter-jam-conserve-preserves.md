The words are not interchangeable. Lids that lie confuse a gift and a process.

**Jam.** Chopped fruit, spreadable, seeds and skin welcome. The NCHFP chopped-fig page is a jam.

**Butter.** Cooked darker, smoother, spread thinner. Apple butter is the mental picture. Fig butter I make as a long cook I mash. If I can it, I look for a tested fruit-butter path or I treat it as a fridge/freezer product. I do not glue jam minutes onto a butter I invented.

**Conserve.** Fruit plus extras — nuts, another fruit, raisins if you are in that century. I like a fig-and-pecan fridge conserve. I do not can a nutty pot unless I have a tested recipe in my hand. Nuts change a process I am not going to guess.

**Preserves.** In the Southern sense NCHFP means **whole figs in a heavy syrup**, not a mash. Different sugar story. They have warned that Splenda is not a swap in those short-process preserves because the sugar is doing work. I do not strip the sugar and keep the short time.

**Syrup / topping.** Thin on purpose. Useful. Label it.

I already sold a friend “preserves” that were jam. She expected whole fruit. She got a spread. She was polite. I was sloppy.

Pickles exist. Fig pickles are a tested NCHFP item. That is a vinegar page. I am not stuffing it into this jam folder as a personality.

Zone does not change definitions. Markets do. A farmers-market “fig butter” might be jam. I write the word I cooked.

If I mix strawberry into fig I look at their strawberry-fig note and I do not invent a third fruit. Mixing is how people wander off a page.

The lid gets the true noun. The true noun picks the bulletin.

A guest from California may say “preserves” for any jar. I still write jam if I mashed it. Two dialects. One process.

I do not can dairy with figs. Fig and cheese is a plate, not a jar.

When I am tired I make jam or I freeze. Tired is how butter scorches and preserves collapse.

This draft is a dictionary so the rest of the set can stay specific.

If an editor wants a whole-preserves Saturday they can follow NCHFP’s preserves page and they can not steal jam minutes. If they want butter they can stay in the fridge until they have a page. The dictionary is the fence.

I do not call a scorched long-cook “butter” to make it sound on purpose. Scorch is scorch. Butter is a cook I mashed on the way down, not a save.

A conserve with pecans is a South Arkansas temptation. I make it small. I fridge it. I like it on a biscuit. I do not put it in a water bath I cannot cite.

Whole preserves want fruit that still has a shape. Tired splits are jam. I sort before I pick a noun.

If a lid says “fig spread” I am hiding. I write the noun. Hiding is how a process gets borrowed from the wrong page.

I will not can a “fig honey” I invented from honey and mash. Honey is a different water story. That pot stays in the fridge or it stays unborn.

A chutney with onion and vinegar is a pickle-adjacent pot. I do not file it under jam minutes. If I do not have a page, I do not have a shelf. A noun without a page is a fridge noun. I can live with a fridge. I cannot live with a guess on a shelf.
