# Fig harvest & preserving — Zone 8a, staged

Staged magazine set. Not published copy. Not a recipe blog. Not a medical page.

These pieces are for a person who already has ripe figs, or is about to, and wants
the next twenty-four hours told in a human voice. The center of gravity is **USDA
Zone 8a** (average annual extreme minimum **10–15°F**), rural South Arkansas weather,
common figs only. Zone 7 and Zone 9 sit in the room because 8a people borrow methods
from both and lose fruit when they borrow the wrong one.

Four jobs, four stages:

| Stage | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `pick` | Harvest timing: ripe signs, calendar, rain, birds, latex, the bowl |
| `dry` | Drying: humidity, dehydrator, oven, checking, conditioning, mold |
| `freeze` | Freezing: tray-pack, sugar, thaw, glut week, freezer jam |
| `jam` | Jam and the canner: acid, NCHFP, set, failures, the Saturday |

Drafts live in `drafts/`. Editorial status on every file is `status: staged` —
writer voice pass done (`voice_check: written`). Not live. Not SEO-published.
`INDEX.md` is the read order. `PHOTO_NOTES.md` is the shoot/pick brief for stills
on **KC-PC** under `D:\FIGS`.

- A substitute for your county extension or the National Center for Home Food
  Preservation (NCHFP)

  Figs are fruit. Jars are food. That is the whole health section.
- A second tested canning recipe. Cite NCHFP. Do not invent a process time.
- Permission to sun-dry in August humidity
- A promise that a green fig will finish on the counter

## Numbers we already use (do not restage as a new trial)

- USDA 8a: average annual extreme minimum **10–15°F**
- Figs do **not** ripen off the tree. Green in the bowl stays a green problem.
- NCHFP fig jam without pectin (cite, do not reprint as ours): about **5 lb**
  chopped figs, **¾ cup** water, **6 cups** sugar, **¼ cup** lemon juice;
  hot pack half-pints or pints **5 min** at 0–1,000 ft, **10 min** at
  1,001–6,000 ft, **15 min** above 6,000 ft
- NCHFP whole canned figs: **1 Tbsp bottled lemon juice per pint** or
  **2 Tbsp per quart** (or citric acid **¼ tsp / ½ tsp**); pints **45 min**,
  quarts **50 min** at 0–1,000 ft
- South Arkansas yards in this set sit well under 1,000 ft. [VERIFY] your
  elevation before you steal the short process time
- NCHFP drying: fully ripe fruit; small whole or large halved; whole fruit
  “checked” in boiling water about **30 seconds**; dry **6–12 hours** as a
  starting window, not a promise
- Freezing does **not** require acid for safety. Lemon or ascorbic is a
  color/quality note. Sugar is quality, not a preservative
- Last spring frost here is often early to mid-April; first fall freeze often
  early November. [VERIFY] your county, not ours
