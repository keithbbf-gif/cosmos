# Sources — fig harvest & postharvest (staged)

House first. Paper second. Do not paste extension PDFs into a draft.

## House (FigRoots)

- *An Introduction to Figs* — fully ripe fruit of an excellent variety; year-one
  fruit is possible; established is closer to year three
- PapaFig, *Pick The Right Fig For You* (2025, updated on the site 2026) — GDDs,
  water split vs drought, clay that still has to drain, South shade + water,
  humid climate → tight eyes / smaller fruit, birds vs dark skins, pots vs
  in-ground, breba as a question, flavor families
- PapaFig, *A Brief History of Figs* (2025) — late summer crop dried or pressed
- Fruit reviews — live scores and ripening dates. Cite the review. Do not invent
  a new date for a name we already plated

## Arkansas / 8a

- Janet Carson / UAEX Democrat-Gazette fig columns — August season; Celeste
  before Brown Turkey; tight eye vs souring; Brown Turkey / Texas Everbearing
  name pile; Celeste slow to rebound after winter kill or a hard prune; breba
  rarely overwinters; leave the stem; latex; preserving-firm pick as a *kitchen*
  habit, not a fresh-eating rule
- UAEX White County fig note — drought drop, wet-August souring after heat
- USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map — 8a = 10–15°F average annual extreme minimum

## Postharvest paper we will not overclaim

- Kader / UC Davis *Figs: Recommendations for Maintaining Postharvest Quality*
  — commercial fruit harvested almost fully ripe and still firm; cold room
  about **−1 to 0°C (30–32°F)**, **90–95% RH**; drying finished near **17%**
  moisture; dehydrator about **60°C (140°F)**; fruit shows a climacteric
  *respiration* pattern. That is not permission to pick a green rock.
- Home boxes: we talk **135°F** because that is the fruit preset most people
  actually run. We do not pretend a kitchen fridge is a forced-air 32°F tunnel.

## Variety climate (not identity)

- LSU AgCenter / Mississippi State fig notes — Celeste closed eye, July start
  in the humid South; Brown Turkey types run longer
- Name confusion is the point. Do not crown a nursery tag.

## What we refuse

- A second A/B trial with invented percentages
- “Figs never ripen off the tree” as a slogan, and “figs ripen like bananas”
  as the opposite slogan
- Sun-dry instructions written for 20% RH
- Medical or nutrition claims
