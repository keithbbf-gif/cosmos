A fig can go soft and still taste like a wet sponge. Soft is a texture. Sugar is a history.

On the tree, the plant moves what it made into the syconium. Heat, water, leaves, time — that is the history. Off the tree, the fruit can rearrange what it already has. Pectin loosens. Aroma shows up. Water sits there. Brix does not climb like a peach in a paper bag. If the history was thin, the rearrange is just a softer thin.

I have been fooled because I wanted to be. A bronze fig, pretty, yielding, empty. That plant had a dry week or too much shade or a tag that lied. The counter did not fail me. I failed the week.

This is why the taste check is on the tree. If the one you ate was already honey, the almosts from the same plant can finish softening in the house and still be worth it. If the one you ate was nothing, a night on the counter will give you a softer nothing.

People say “it sweetened up overnight.” Sometimes they mean aroma. Sometimes they mean they were hungry. Sometimes they picked closer to ready than they admitted. I will allow aroma. I will not allow a fantasy about a green marble becoming Celeste.

A rain fig can be soft and watery. That is dilution, not ripeness. Those go to the now tray. They do not go to the “ripen” tray. There is no ripen tray for soup.

I do not own a refractometer I trust in a humid shop. I own a mouth. If you own a meter, use it as a note, not as a religion. Numbers on a late watery fig will make you sad. Sad is not a method.

Kitchen-firm fruit for preserving is a Janet Carson habit: pick a few days before fully ripe so it holds in the pot. That fruit is *not* trying to sweeten on the counter. That fruit is trying to stay in one piece in a saucepan. Different job. Do not mix the jobs in one bowl and then write a confused paragraph.

If I need dessert, I wait for the neck. If I need a jar, I pick closer on purpose and I process. If I need a story about how I “ripened” a hard fig, I need a hobby that is not this.

Zone 9 fruit that hung through a dry week has a different history. Our stalled heat-wave fruit has a thinner one until the water comes back. Softening a stalled fig off the tree is how you bottle the stall.

A peach on a counter is a different contract. People bring peach habits into this shop and then they write angry notes about figs. I let them. I also keep the almosts and the marbles in different plates so I do not catch the habit myself at 9 p.m. when I am tired and want the story to be easy.

If you need a number, you needed a tree week with water. The counter does not sell degrees Brix. It sells time, and time without sugar is just rot getting dressed.

Honey on a bad fig is a cover, not a ripening. I have watched people do it at a table and then say the fruit “woke up.” It woke up because you added honey. The plant did not get a second shift.

A sprinkle of sugar on a plate is the same cover as honey. I will do it for a child who needs a bridge. I will not do it and then write a review.

Leave sugar on the plant. Borrow texture for a night if you had to pick. Do not confuse the loan for a raise.
