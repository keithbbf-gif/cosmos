Storage starts before the twist. A pot you can walk is the only preharvest storage I trust.

I move a loaded #5 off the west slab and under the eave when the fruit is in the almost week. The plant cools. The fruit does not cook on one shoulder. The birds get a slightly worse angle. I get a day I might not have had.

That is not ripening off-tree. That is changing the tree’s weather. People will call it cheating. I call it having a handle.

In-ground does not get this. In-ground gets water, mulch, and a closer pick if the week is ugly. Do not be jealous. Be a pot person for the names you care about finishing clean.

I do not move a pot every hour. I move it when the week changes. Morning sun, afternoon shade is a good 8a sentence. All-day shade is how you get bland. All-day west is how you get raisins that are still sour inside.

A pot on a wagon is a luxury I will not sneer at. A pot you drag and scrape is still better than a pot you leave to die on concrete because you wanted to be “in-ground serious.”

Nematode pots stay pots. Harvest does not graduate them into a clay grave in August. August moves cook a root ball. The other set already said that. I will say it again because people get brave when they see fruit.

The eave is also where I park a pot for the last two days before I leave town. I pick what is ready, I walk the rest into kinder light, I do not ask a neighbor to water a skillet.

Kids can help roll a wagon. They cannot help by spinning a pot for fun. Spinning is a broken cane.

Zone 7 pots come inside for winter. Our pots come under a roof for August. Different season, same idea: the plant is portable, so the harvest is portable.

If you only grow in the ground, this piece is a reason to keep one name in a #5. Not a collection. One handle. One plant you can save from a week.

A pot that is too heavy to walk is a pot you sized like an in-ground and then put on a slab. I have done that. Then I had a harvest I could not save from the west wall without a hand truck. Hand trucks are allowed. Pride is how you leave it and lose the week.

Wheels on a nursery tray are not cute. They are how a block of #3s goes under the eave in one trip. I will not sneer at wheels. I sneer at a dead plant I could have rolled.

I water after I move, not before, if the pot is already a hernia. A wet #5 is a back injury with fruit on it. Move, then water in the new shade. I have learned this the expensive way.

A pot on a metal table is still a skillet. Walk it to wood or dirt or a board. I have “shaded” a pot by putting it on a trailer bed and cooked the root from below. Shade has a floor.

I do not walk a pot with fruit rubbing a doorframe. Tilt, clear, then roll. I have knocked off a day’s ripe on a jamb and then written a poem about clumsiness. The poem was not food. The jamb is still there. Look up.

Walk the pot. Then pick. Then store what you picked. The walk is the cheapest cold chain in the yard.
