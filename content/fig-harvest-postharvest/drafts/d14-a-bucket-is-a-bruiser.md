A five-gallon bucket is for water and for dirt. It is not for ripe figs.

The first layer in a bucket is fine. The second layer is a press. The third layer is jam you did not vote for. By the time you walk from the back stool to the shop, the bottom is a wound and the top is a lie.

I want a shallow tray. A nursery flat. A low cardboard that I can stand to throw away. Something that holds one layer, maybe a gentle second if the fruit is on the firm side of ripe. Peak soft fruit is one layer. I am not running a packing house. I am trying to get food to a table.

Stem on, not stacked like cordwood. If they must nest, they nest on their sides with space. They do not stand on each other’s shoulders.

I do not pour a tray into another tray from a height. I hand them across. Pouring is how you write bruises you will blame on “figs being delicate,” as if the fruit jumped.

A metal bowl in the sun is a cooker and a bruisers’ den. Dark plastic on a tailgate is the same. If the tray has to wait, it waits in shade, off the steel.

Commercial clamshells exist because someone else already bruised the fruit for you and then hid it. We do not need a clamshell for a fifty-foot walk. We need not to pretend we are hauling apples.

If I have a glut, I make two trips. Pride is how the third layer happens. Two trips is cheaper than a tray of soup.

The walk matters. I do not take the shortcut over the hose tangle with a loaded flat. I have worn more fruit than I picked that way. Set the tray down to move a hose. Feel like an old man. Keep the figs.

Kids want to fill a bucket because a bucket looks like a harvest. Give them a small tray and a compliment when they do not heap it. A heaped bucket is a photo. A flat tray is a method.

Wounded fruit does not ride with keepers. I eat it, I cook it, I give it to the chickens. One leaking fig in a tight tray is a contagion of stickiness and mold later.

After the walk I sort on the table. Keepers to a clean tray or a towel-lined sheet pan. Soft-now to the mouth or the freezer bag that night. I do not store the harvest in the same dirty flat I walked through the clay.

Zone 7 people with three figs can use a cereal bowl and be fine. Zone 9 people with a real orchard can talk lugs and foam. We are a shop with pots and a few in-grounds. The right vessel is boring and wide.

If all you have is a bucket, put a folded towel in the bottom and stop at one layer anyway. The bucket is then a tall tray with bad press. I have done that on a walk-over when I forgot the flat. I still made two trips. The towel was the apology. The second trip was the method.

I have used a cardboard six-pack holder as a field tray when I was already in the row and the flat was in the shop. Six figs, not twenty. Ugly works. Deep-and-proud does not.

If you only remember one hardware change, throw the harvest bucket back at the dirt pile. Buy a tray you can wash. Pick into that. The fruit you already waited August for should not die in the last forty feet.
