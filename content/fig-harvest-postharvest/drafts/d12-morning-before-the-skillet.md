Pick before the yard is a skillet. In 8a August that means morning, not a theory about “any time they’re ripe.”

Cool fruit is firmer in the hand. It twists cleaner. It does not collapse into the tray on the walk back. Warm fruit is already starting its postharvest clock while it is still on the plant. You can pick it. You will have less of it by supper.

I want the necks I can read. Afternoon heat wilts everything — leaves, necks, your judgment. I have stripped a plant at 4 p.m. because it all looked ready and then looked at the tray in the kitchen and seen a lot of firm interiors. Heat is a costume.

Birds also prefer dawn. If you wait until the coffee is a second pot, you are picking their leftovers. I am not going to romanticize 5:30 a.m. I will say 7 is better than 11, and 11 is better than 4, and 4 is how you donate.

Latex runs more when the plant is pumping in the heat. Morning sap is still there. It is less of a fountain. If you are one of the people who welt, morning is mercy.

A wet morning after a night rain is a different problem. Fruit is slick. Eyes may already be compromised. I still go early. I pick what is honest. I do not wait for “it to dry off” until noon and then act surprised the wasps clocked in.

Shade the tray as you walk. I said it in the daily piece and I will say it until someone stops leaving a metal bowl on a tailgate. The harvest can be correct and the next twenty minutes can ruin it.

If mornings are impossible, pick the night before a hot day rather than the afternoon of one. Evening fruit is warmer than dawn fruit, cooler than 3 p.m. fruit. Take the compromise. Do not take the skillet.

I bring fruit inside to a table, not to a sunny counter. The counter in an 8a house in August is a second skillet. A dining table away from the window is a receiving dock. Sort there. Eat the wounded. Tray the keepers. Fridge what you must.

Commercial rooms blast figs down toward 32°F with forced air. We do not have that. We have morning and shade and a crisper that is not the door. Morning is the part of the cold chain we can afford.

Zone 9 people will pick in the evening because their evenings are the living hours. Fine for them if the fruit is dry and the nights are kind. Our evenings in August can still be 88°F and wet. I would rather sweat at 7 a.m. than pick soup at 7 p.m.

A thunderstorm at 2 p.m. does not make 2 p.m. a harvest. It makes a mess. If fruit is splitting in the storm, you can do an emergency pass. That is a save, not a routine. Routine is morning.

Coffee can ride in the other hand. The tray cannot ride on a hot hood. I have set a flat on a truck hood “for a second” and come back to a cooked bottom layer. The second was long enough.

If the only morning you have is Sunday, pick Saturday evening and process Saturday night. Do not invent a Sunday dawn you will not attend. The skillet does not honor intentions.

I drink the coffee after the first walk, not before. If I drink it before I get philosophical and the birds get practical. Philosophy is for winter cuttings. August is a tray and a clock.
