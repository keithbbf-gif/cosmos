A split fig in August is not your fig. It is a wasp shop. You can visit. You do not own it.

Yellowjackets find sugar faster than you find your shoes. An open eye after rain is a sign on the highway. A sugar crack is a door. I do not stand there and negotiate. I decide from a step back: is there a safe fruit next to it, or is the whole cluster a convention?

I pick around them in the morning when they are slower. I do not breathe on a knot of them at noon and hope I am the main character. A sting on a ladder is how harvest ends.

Split fruit I want for the kitchen — if it just opened and they have not clocked in — gets taken with a tray, not with my chest against the cane. If they are already in the cavity, that fig is theirs. I will not wash a wasp out of a fig and call it supper. I will not put that fig in a dry box.

Kids get a clear sentence: we do not grab the ones with bugs in them. We do not swat at the plant. We walk away and tell me.

I do not spray the fruit. This is food. A yard spray on a ripe fig is a different hobby. I will knock a nest that is in a pot rim if it is a problem for hands, and I will do that like an adult, not like a hero in shorts.

Split after drought-then-rain is predictable. That week I pick closer and I process the same day. Jam and dry and freeze are how you keep ahead of the shop. Leaving splits “to finish” is how the shop comes to you.

A closed-eye Celeste still splits if you water like a panic after a dry spell. Tight eyes reduce souring. They do not repeal physics.

I keep a second tray for “now.” Splits, pecks, soft-shoulders. That tray does not ride under the keepers. That tray goes to the table or the pot. Mixing them is how the keepers get wet and popular.

If I have to harvest a plant that is a wasp magnet, I wear the long sleeves I already own for latex and I do not wear perfume like I am going to town. Sweet on you plus sweet on the tree is a meeting I did not schedule.

Zone 9 dry hang can tree-dry and still look clean. Our splits ferment. Different air. Do not copy a California photo of fruit hanging open and brown and call it “on-tree raisin” here unless you like vinegar.

I have eaten around a clean split at the plant. I have also learned the smell of the ones you should not. Vinegar and wine-gone-wrong — that is souring, not “complex.” Spit, walk, do not be brave for a blog.

A fallen split under the plant is how a nest finds the address. I pick those up with a tool, not with a speech. The hole gets them. The tray does not. If I am allergic, I do not harvest that plant alone. That is not toughness. That is a Tuesday I want to finish.

A soda can on a fence post is not a trap program. It is a snack for the same shop. I do not farm wasps. I farm the pick. If the can makes you feel better, fine. The tray still decides.

Wasps are not the enemy of the orchard. They are the accountants of your delay. If they own the splits, you were a day late or a storm was on time. Pick the honest fruit. Leave the shop to the shopkeepers.
