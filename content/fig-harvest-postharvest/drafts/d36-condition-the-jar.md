Hot fruit from the box into a sealed jar is how you build a terrarium.

Conditioning is an old, boring word: cool the fruit, then hold it in a loose jar or a crock for several days, shake once a day, and let the leftover moisture even out. The wetter pieces share. The drier pieces take. If you see beads, or you smell a wine, or a shoulder spots, you dump that piece back in the box or you dump it in the trash. You do not “stir it in.”

I use a big jar I can see through. I do not use a dark crock I forget in a corner. Seeing is the method.

A week of shakes is enough for a home batch. If everything still looks leather and nobody wept, then I pack for keeps. If somebody wept, I re-dry the wet ones. I do not punish the whole batch unless the whole batch lied.

Cool first. Sealing warm fruit is a condensation trick. The lid rains. The lid rain is a mold starter.

I do not add sulfur. I do not add a whiskey splash because a comment said so. If you want a splash, that is your jar and your story. My jar is fruit.

A silica packet is for a tool box. I have used a clean, food-safe desiccant when a winter pantry was a swamp. I would rather the fruit be dry enough that I do not need a gadget.

Label. Year, name I believe, “dry.” August me thinks I will remember. January me does not remember.

If a jar spots in October, I do not pick the good ones out and keep going like a hero. I compost the jar’s reputation. A little mold in a sugar pack is not a treasure map.

Kids love the shake. Give them the job. It is the only part of drying that feels like a game and is still useful.

Zone 9 dry houses condition less because the air already finished the job. Our houses sweat. Condition.

I keep the conditioning jar on the table I walk past, not in a closet. Out of sight is how you skip the shake and then you seal a lie. The table gets sticky. Wipe it. The shake is still cheaper than a furry lid.

If I am gifting a jar, I condition first and I gift later. A warm jar of yesterday’s box is a present that can turn. I like my neighbors.

A paper bag week is the old orchard version of conditioning. I will use a bag if I am out of big jars. I still shake. I still look. A bag in a dark cupboard is easy to forget. The table jar is harder to ignore, which is why I like it.

I do not condition in the same jar I will gift if the gift jar is tiny. Tiny jars hide wet pieces. Big jar, shake, then pack small. Pretty is the last step, not the first.

If the batch is tiny — a plate, not a glut — I still wait a couple of days in a small jar I can see. Tiny is how people skip the week. Tiny mold is still mold. The rule does not retire for a cute amount.

This is the last drying rule I will keep repeating: the box gets you close, the week in a see-through jar tells the truth, the sealed jar is a privilege you earn. Skip the week and you can still get lucky. Lucky is not a method. I have been unlucky. The science project was educational. I did not need the education twice.
