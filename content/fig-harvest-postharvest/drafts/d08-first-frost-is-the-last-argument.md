The last argument in this climate is a night in the 30s. Not a chart. Not a variety description that says “ripens into fall.” When the first real frost is on the page, you stop waiting for a neck that is never going to wilt.

Most years South Arkansas gives us into October. Some years it doesn’t. I will not write a date like it is a rail schedule. I watch the forecast like I watch a pot on concrete. If Thursday night is 31°F and Friday is 28°F, Thursday morning is a harvest of what can still be food.

What can still be food: fruit that has color and some give, even if the neck is stubborn. That is kitchen fruit, freezer fruit, a tray for the dehydrator if the insides are already turning jam. What is not food: hard green marbles. Those will not become dessert on a windowsill because you got sentimental. They can go to the compost or the chickens. I have tried the sentimental route. I ate a bowl of nothing.

Jack will write the off-tree piece. My job here is the calendar feeling. Frost does not ripen. Frost ends. A light frost may only burn the tips and leave fruit that was already close. A hard one blackens the leaves and the last figs look boiled on the branch. Pick before the boil.

Pots I can walk. That is the whole advantage in a late October warning. In-ground I cover if the plant is young and I care about the wood. I do not build a tent over a mature stool to save twelve hard figs. I pick the close ones and let the wood take the night. Wood is next year. Green marbles are not.

Celeste is usually done before this conversation. The late berries and the slow pots are who we are talking about. We already scored fruit on October 1. That does not mean October 20 is owed to you.

Zone 7 people live in this panic from Labor Day. I borrow their urgency when the forecast turns, and I do not borrow it in August. Zone 9 people will tell you to leave fruit up because “it’ll be fine.” Their fine is not our 8a river-bottom night.

After a frost I still walk. Sometimes a fig that was under the canopy is untouched and perfect. Sometimes everything is slush. I do not put slush in a jar to be brave. Slush is done.

The first frost is also when people finally pick the tree they ignored all August because they were busy. That fruit is not a treasure. That fruit is a museum of what they missed. Eat the good ones. Do not write a review off a neglected October plant and decide the name is poor.

I keep a sheet or an old blanket that has already lived a life. It goes on a young in-ground if I have warning. It does not go on a bowl of hard figs in the kitchen. The blanket is for cambium.

If you want less of this argument, plant earlier windows and stop collecting late names like they are baseball cards. If you want the argument, grow the late plates on purpose and be home on the first cold Thursday.

I text myself the forecast the night before a close call. Not a group. Myself. If I do not pick in the morning, I have no one else to blame, and the blanket on a young stool is still for the wood.

The season ends when the night says so. I would rather have a full dehydrator on Wednesday than a story about the frost I thought I could talk out of it.
