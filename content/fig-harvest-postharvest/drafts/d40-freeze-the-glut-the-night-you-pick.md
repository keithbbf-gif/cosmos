If I cannot eat it and I cannot dry it, I freeze it the night I pick it. Not “this weekend.” The night.

A ripe fig in an 8a house does not wait for your ambition. Ambition is how a beautiful Tuesday becomes a Thursday smell.

Whole, on a sheet pan, not touching, into the cold until they are firm, then into a bag with the air out. That way they do not become a brick of fused shoulders. If I am tired, I bag them as-is and I accept a brick I will thaw for a pot. Tired is allowed. Delay is not.

I do not sugar them unless I am doing a specific kitchen job. I do not syrup them for a 1950s magazine. Fruit, bag, date, name I believe.

Puree is for the ones that are too far for a pretty freeze — soft, a little torn, still honest. Blitz, bag or a box, flat if I can so it thaws like a book. Flat is a gift to January.

I do not freeze sour. I do not freeze a wasp shop. The freezer is not a priest either.

Thaw in a bowl, not on a white shirt. They weep. That weep is jam waiting. I use it. I do not pour it down the sink and then wonder why the fruit is sad.

Texture after a freeze is not a fresh fig. Do not hold a tasting. Hold a crumble, a pot of oatmeal, a sauce, a batch of things this set is not going to recipe. The harvest job is to get honest fruit into a state January can use.

Label. I have eaten a mystery bag that was either figs or overripe plums. I was not in danger. I was annoyed.

A chest freezer is better than a door that slams all day. If all I have is the kitchen box, I still freeze. I just use it faster.

Kids love the sheet-pan step. Give them the space-the-figs job. Then you do the bag. Then you do the date. Dates are how August stays true.

Zone 7 people freeze because the season ends. We freeze because the season peaks too hard. Same bag.

I have left a sheet pan in the freezer and then needed the pan for Wednesday supper. Bag them once they are firm. The pan is a tool, not a home. A bag in a chest is a home.

If the power flickers in an 8a storm, I do not open the chest to check. Checking is how you lose the cold. The figs will tell you later if they thawed and refroze into a sad brick. Then they are pot fruit, not pretty halves. Still food. Still better than the smell of a tray I left on the table because I was waiting for “a good time to freeze.”

I keep a stack of bags in the shop so the glut does not have to walk to town for storage. A glut that has to shop first will sit on the table. Sitting is the failure.

I do not freeze in glass from hot fruit. Glass and a swelling puree is a shop floor event. Bags or plastic boxes with room. I have cleaned that floor. I still hear the pop.

I do not freeze a fig I would not eat tonight. The freezer is a pause on food, not a jail for doubt. Doubt belongs to the hole. January me cannot sort what August me would not swallow.

Same night. That is the whole piece. If you remember it when you are tired, the set did its job.
