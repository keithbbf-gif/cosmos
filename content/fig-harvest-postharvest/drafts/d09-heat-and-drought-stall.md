A fig can sit at “almost” for ten days in a 98°F week and make you feel crazy. You did not miss the harvest. The plant closed up and waited.

When it is that hot, the leaves stop trading air like they did in May. Photosynthesis slows. The fruit that was swelling yesterday just holds. If the hole is dry, the plant will drop fruit rather than finish it. UAEX said it: too dry, they drop. I have swept green figs off clay and been mad at the wrong thing. I was mad at the name. I should have been mad at the hose.

This is why I will not let a July calendar boss an August bowl. July is when we stop the late fertilizer in 8a — mid-July is plenty — and start treating water like the crop. Deep, not a sprinkle. Pots on concrete may want water every day in a stretch. In-ground wants a long drink and mulch that is still actually there. Mulch thins. Check it.

Afternoon wilt that recovers by evening is a warning. Afternoon wilt that is still there at dusk is a bill. Pay it that night. Do not wait for morning “just to see.” Morning you will be picking droppers.

Shade in the South is part of harvest timing. I already wrote it: figs can take a lot of sun, full in the North, but here they want some shade in the summer and lots of water. A pot I can walk to the east side of the shop will finish fruit that the same pot cooked on the west slab. That is not a variety secret. That is moving the skillet off the burner.

People pick the stalled “almost” because they are tired of looking at it. Tired is not a ripeness test. If the neck is still standing and the fruit is firm and the week was a furnace, water, wait, walk again in three mornings. If you pick now you will write a bad review of a good name.

Split after a drought-then-storm is the other half. The fruit gulps. The skin cannot. You get cracks and an open invitation. That week I pick closer to ready and I cook or dry what I cannot eat. I do not leave cracked fruit up to “finish.” Finish is how souring clocks in.

A #3 in August is a harvest tool because I can change its weather. An in-ground is a negotiation with clay. Clay that holds a puddle in April can still be a brick at two inches in August. Water long enough that it is not a brick.

I do not fertigate a stalled plant to “push it through.” Salt plus heat is how you buy leaf burn and more drop. Food was for spring. August is water and shade and patience.

Zone 9 dry-heat people have their own tricks — whitewash, more aggressive shade cloth. I will steal shade cloth for a pot. I will not copy a desert irrigation schedule onto a humid 8a week and then be surprised by fungus. Our heat comes with wet air. The stall is heat. The rot is the wet. Treat them as two problems.

A rain barrel that went dry in July is a harvest problem in August. I have stood there with a pretty plant and an empty barrel and picked nothing worth a bowl. Fill the barrel in June if you want a neck in August.

When the stretch breaks and the nights ease, the necks that were waiting will move. That is the week the bowls get heavy. Be home. The stall is not the season ending. The stall is the season holding its breath. If you pick the breath, you do not get the sentence.
