Celeste is a sugar marble with a closed door. That is a drying plant.

It comes in a heap, it is small, it is already sweet if you waited, and the eye does not invite a science project as often as a cave. In a wet August I would rather fill a box with that heap than with a few giant bronzes I am nervous about.

I still sort. A sour Celeste is just a smaller sour. Tight eye is odds, not a halo.

Whole works more often on this fruit than on a turkey. I still cut one to check a tray. I do not assume the name dried itself because the name is famous in Arkansas.

Flavor in the jar is honey-sugar, not a berry circus. If you want a berry jar, dry a berry name that you actually ripened. Do not expect Celeste to become a review plate from October.

Because the harvest window can be short and heavy, this is the plant that makes a box earn its shelf. Daily walk, daily sort, nightly box if you have to. A Celeste glut left “until the weekend” is how you turn a gift into a smell.

I do not under-dry these because they are small. Small can still hide a wet core if you piled them. One layer. The rule does not retire for cute fruit.

People will say Celeste is “just a drying fig” like that is an insult. I grow fruit to eat standing up first. The box is the overflow. If a name is good fresh and good dry, that is a farm, not a slight.

The turkey pile dries too. It takes more knife and more faith. Late berries we plated in October can dry if they made it to honest ripe before the cold talk. I will not dry a late marble to punish a frost.

Name confusion: if your “Celeste” is large and open, it will not dry like this paragraph. Believe the fruit.

Zone 7 Celeste is a survival name. Ours is a harvest name. Same plant, different job in the year. In 8a the job includes a jar.

I keep a jar that says the year and the name I believe. I do not keep a jar that says “mixed” unless I want a surprise in January. January me is less romantic than August me.

A year Celeste froze back, the box stayed hungry. That is when I learned not to talk like the jar is owed to me. The plant has to show up first. The box is second. I still keep the empty jars. Empty jars are a winter prune reminder: do not butcher the name that fills them.

I do not mix Celeste leather with a late berry in one unlabeled jar. January me will call the mix “fine” and then be annoyed. Two jars. Two dates. The heap earned its own lid.

A year I had almost no Celeste, I dried more turkey and I chewed more leather. That is fine. It is also why I still protect that tight-eye stool in a bad winter. The box has a favorite even if I pretend I am fair.

I do not wait for Celeste to wrinkle on the tree and call it California. On-tree wrinkle here is often a sour start. Pick ripe, then the box. The plant’s job was sugar. The box’s job is water. Do not make the plant do the box’s job in a wet week.

Celeste was not built for a catalog photo. It was built to show up. The box is how the showing-up lasts.
