The ostiole is a hole with a job. On a fig it is the eye. On a humid 8a week it is a weather report you can read with your thumbnail.

A tight eye — Celeste is the Arkansas example UAEX keeps using — keeps rain and picnic beetles out of the flower chamber. An open eye is a gutter. Water goes in, heat follows, and the inside ferments. That is souring. It smells like a cider you forgot. It is not “tangy.” It is ruined.

PapaFig already wrote the climate line: if you are humid, look for tight eyes and often smaller fruit. That is not a style preference. That is how you still have a bowl after a wet August. We do not “rarely have a wet August” every year. When we do, the open-eye names teach the class.

I do not wait for a fig to be dead-ripe on an open eye if a two-inch rain is on the night forecast and the fruit is already at color and starting to give. That is a bird-save and a rain-save. It is not the same as picking a green rock. The fruit has to be *almost*. The eye is why you move the date one day toward you.

After the rain I walk first. I smell. I look for a drop that is not nectar. Nectar at the eye of a ripe fig can be a good sign — a little honey bead. A watery seep with fruit flies is a funeral. Those do not go in the keep tray. They do not go in the dry box. They can go to the chickens if you want, or the hole.

Heat after rain is the ugly combination. UAEX said souring is worse when the rain is followed by high temperatures. That is our August. The fruit warms up like a jar in a car. Closed eyes still win. Open eyes need you that morning.

I will not pretend every “Brown Turkey” has the same eye. The name is a crowd. Some wear a moderately closed eye and behave. Some are a tunnel. Learn the plant you have, not the PDF.

Beetles find an open eye before you do. If you see them working the ostiole, you are already sharing. I pick what is ready, I do not spray a fantasy. This set is harvest, not a spray program.

Zone 9 dry-summer people can hang an open-eye fig longer and talk about “tree-dried.” We are not them. A fig that starts to dry on the tree here often starts to sour on the tree. If you want dry fruit, pick a good ripe one and use a box. I will get to that.

A closed eye is not a closed mind. Celeste still splits if you drown the roots and then the sky opens. Tight is not magic. Tight is odds. Water even through the week so the fruit does not gulp a storm.

When I teach a new set of hands, I make them find the eye before they find the color. Is it a pinprick? A slit? A cave? Then I make them look at the forecast. Then I let them touch the neck. That order keeps more fruit in the bowl than “it looks brown.”

If the eye is already a cave and the fruit is still hard, you have a name that opens early. Those names are poor friends in a monsoon and fine friends in a dry stretch. Do not judge them in the wrong week.

The eye does not ripen the fig. The eye decides whether the last two days are safe. Harvest timing in 8a is half physiology and half weather. The neck is physiology. The ostiole is weather. Read both.
