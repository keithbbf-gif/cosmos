A zip bag on top of the fridge is a warm, light, sweaty apartment. Dried figs do not want that apartment.

After they have conditioned and told the truth, they go in a clean jar, lid that actually seals, in a dark cupboard that is not over the stove. The stove cupboard is an oven with dishes in it.

If the summer pantry is a swamp, the jar can live in the fridge. Cold and dark and boring. If I want them through a long warm stretch, the freezer is legal. Frozen dried figs are not pretty. They are safe.

I do not store a half-finished bag rolled “tight.” Rolled tight is a hobby. Air and a kitchen’s humidity will undo a box’s work.

Light fades a jar on a windowsill. We are not decorating.

Six months in a good cupboard is a reasonable hope if you dried them honestly. A year if you were thorough and the house was kind. I do not write a guarantee on a lid. I write a date. I look. I smell. I taste one in winter before I serve a bowl.

Bugs like dried fruit. A clean jar is the fence. A bag in a drawer is an invitation. If I see a moth, I do not pick around a moth. I lose the jar and I clean the shelf.

I do not oil them. I do not sugar-coat them for storage. If they stuck, they were a little wet or the weather was a thief. Eat those first.

Kids will open the jar and leave it open. The lid is part of the job. Put it back.

A pretty swing-top that does not seal is a vase. Use a lid you trust.

Zone 9 dry pantries are easier. Our 8a kitchens in August will steal moisture back into a weak package. Seal.

I do not store dried figs next to onions, garlic, or a box of soap. They are a sponge for a kitchen’s personality. A dull cupboard with flour and lids is a better neighbor than a spice riot.

If a lid rusts, I change the lid, not the religion. Rust is a leak. A leak is August coming back in.

I have given a jar at Christmas that I dried in August and I have watched it get eaten like it was a trick. It is not a trick. It is a Tuesday you finished.

A vacuum sealer is allowed if you already own one and you dried honestly. It is not required. A lid you trust is required. I have vacuumed a wet batch and made a compact science project. The machine does not fix a wet center.

I keep one jar as “open now” and the rest sealed. The open jar lives in the fridge in summer. The sealed ones stay dark. Opening every jar “to check” is how you check them into 8a air.

I wipe the rim before the lid. Sugar on a rim is a leak and a bug signal. A clean rim is a boring twenty seconds. I have skipped it. I have then met a rim of ants in October. Boring is cheaper.

I do not store a jar in the truck “for later.” A truck in 8a August is an oven with a lid. Later is a science project on the seat. The cupboard is fifty feet away. Walk.

The box, the week of shakes, the jar, the dark. That is the chain. Skip a link and you can still get lucky. I have been lucky. I have also opened a fridge-bag in November that tasted like the onion drawer. The jar would have been cheaper than the lesson.
