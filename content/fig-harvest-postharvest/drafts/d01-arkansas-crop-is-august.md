August is when the bowls fill. Not a website calendar from California. Not a Zone 9 June brag. South Arkansas, Zone 8a, common figs: the crop you eat is the one on this year’s wood, and it comes late in the summer when the shop is already a skillet.

Janet Carson has been saying it in the Democrat-Gazette for years. I am not going to write a cleverer month. If your trees did not take a hard winter to the ground, there should be fruit ready in August. Celeste first, if you have a real Celeste. The commerce Brown Turkey — the name that also wears Texas Everbearing on a tag — a few weeks later and for longer. That is the Arkansas sentence. I will not move it because a forum thread in Georgia started picking in the first week of July.

We still get earlier fruit some years. A pot against the east wall can show color in late July. A young in-ground stool that froze back will be later, because it is building wood and then fruit on the same flush. A late name we already plated — Red Sicilian, LSU Jack Lily — sat on the review table October 1, 2024. That is not “fig season.” That is a late plate after the main rush. Do not build your mental year on the last fruit you photographed.

Zone 7 people talk like harvest is a race against September. Zone 9 people talk like harvest is a lifestyle from May to November. Both leak into 8a advice. Steal the hurry from 7 if you are growing a late berry that still looks like a marble on September 20. Steal nothing from the person who has ripe brebas in May and then tells you your July tree is “behind.”

GDDs are the other half of the month. I already wrote it on FigRoots: you can have a wonderful fig and not enough heat to finish it. 8a has the heat. What we run out of is patience in July, when the fruit is there and still hard, and people start picking because they are tired of waiting. Hard is not a date. Hard is not ripe.

I walk the in-ground set in the morning in August because the afternoon lies. A fig that looks dull and tired at 3 p.m. can still be a day away. A fig that already dropped a neck at 7 a.m. will be on the ground by supper if I get proud and “give it one more day.” The month is August. The unit is the day.

Pots are on a different clock and I will say that again in this set. A #5 on concrete in full south is a week ahead of the same name in clay under a high canopy. That is not a variety difference. That is a skillet. I move pots. I do not rewrite the month because a pot on the driveway colored up on July 22.

Winter is the silent editor of the calendar. A year the wood dies to the crown, you do not have last year’s stubs holding a June surprise. You have new canes and a later main crop, and Celeste will make you wait longer than the Brown Turkey pile, because Celeste does not rebound from a hard cut the way the commerce turkey does. UAEX already said that. I have watched it. I will not tell you to prune Celeste like a shrub in March and then ask where August went.

If you only remember one thing from this piece, remember it in the kitchen: put the bowls out in August. Check every day. Do not wait for a chart. Do not wait for the fruit to look like a grocery Mission. Grocery fruit is picked for a truck. Ours is picked for a mouth.

I still write dates on the tape on a harvest tray. Not because I am building a spreadsheet. Because next July I will forget how early the east-wall pot was, and I will almost pick a hard fig to make the memory true. The tape is cheaper than a lie.

Zone 8b Gulf people get a longer tail. We get a tail if October stays kind. We do not get to call June the season because a magazine did. June is for watching wood and watering a pot before it sulks. August is for a bowl.
