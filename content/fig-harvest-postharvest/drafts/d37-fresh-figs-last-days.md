A ripe fig is a short story. People want a novel. I will not write them one.

On the table in an 8a August house, dessert fruit is tonight and maybe tomorrow. In the crisper, if you did everything kindly, you might get two or three more days. A paper cold room at 32°F and wet air can talk about one to two weeks. That room is not your Kenmore. Do not buy groceries like you have Davis in the garage.

I plan the walk around the meals. Morning tray is lunch, supper, a neighbor, a freezer bag. I do not plan a Sunday market on Wednesday’s harvest.

Bruised fruit is already on the clock. Soft fruit is on the clock. Intact, stem-on, morning-picked fruit is the only fruit I will try to hold.

If I have more than we will eat, I change the job the same day. Freeze, dry, cook. Holding a glut “to enjoy them fresh all week” is how you enjoy them less and smell them more.

A guest bowl sits out for an hour, not an afternoon in the sun on the porch. Porch is a wasp shop.

I have kept a firm almost in the house overnight and a ripe one in the fridge and I still lost the ripe one first. Ripe is the point and the limit.

Kids will hide a fig in a lunchbox for Friday. Friday is a science project. Pack the lunch the morning you pick, or pack something else.

Grocery clamshells last because they were picked firmer and kept colder. Your tree fruit is better and ruder. Treat it like fish, not like apples.

Zone 7 September kitchens buy you a little more table time. Our August kitchens do not. Translate.

I have tried to stretch a pretty bowl for a Saturday guest by picking Wednesday “a little early.” Saturday I had a bowl of almosts that never became the fig I wanted to serve. I should have picked Friday morning and served Friday night, or frozen Wednesday and cooked Saturday. Hospitality is a clock, not a display.

A church potluck is the same trap. People want a platter that sits. Figs do not sit. Send a dry jar, a crumble, or a bowl that gets eaten in twenty minutes. Do not send a platter that commutes.

When the plant is in peak, I cook more dinners that want fruit on them so the tray has a job. When the plant is slow, I buy bananas like a normal person and I do not pretend the tree owes me a week.

The short story is the point. If you need a novel, dry or freeze. Fresh is a Tuesday. I like Tuesdays. I do not ask them to be a month.

I tell guests the bowl is for now. If they want to take some home, they get a small container and a sentence: tonight. If they look disappointed, I offer a dry jar. Disappointment is how fruit dies in a hot car on the way to a fridge that will not save it.

A hotel minibar fridge is not a plan for fruit you drove four hours. Eat at the plant, or dry, or freeze. I have been the person with a sad clamshell in a cooler. I did not need to be that person twice.

I do not buy grocery figs the same week the tree is in peak to “have extra.” Extra is how you ignore the tree. Eat the tree. The clamshell can wait until October when the late names are a maybe.

Days. Not a week. If you need a week, you need a different job for the fruit. That is not a failure of the plant. That is the plant being a fig.
