If it has to go cold, it goes in the back of the crisper, not on the door next to the ketchup.

Paper wants 30–32°F and 90–95% humidity. A home fridge is a dry box that thinks 35–40 is a personality. We work with what we have. Drawer closed. Fruit in a shallow container. Paper towel under. Lid on loose or vented so they do not stew, closed enough that the fridge’s dry air does not turn them into sad raisins.

A sealed sweat bag is a rain cloud. A naked plate in the main box is a wind tunnel. The middle is a towel and a vent.

I do not wash first. Jack will write that. Wet fruit plus cold is a slime.

Door shelves are a slap. Every juice run is a warm wave. Figs do not like the wave. Neither do I, but I am not the one who rots.

I keep them away from a cut onion and a leftover fish. Figs take on a fridge. A fridge takes on figs. I would rather they met less.

Do not freeze-burn them against the back wall if your box runs cold. Pitting on firm fruit is a cheap fridge tax.

I stack nothing. One layer in the container. If I have two layers I have too many for the fridge and some of them need a new job tonight.

Take out what you will eat and let it sit twenty minutes if you want aroma. Eating ice-cold figs is legal. It is also how you miss the honey. I am not your priest.

If the towel is wet tomorrow, change it. You are not making a marsh.

Zone 9 people with a real extra fridge in a garage have a better shot at the paper week. Our kitchen box is doing leftovers and a school project. Be humble.

I wipe the drawer before a glut week. Crumbs and a leaky berry box are how a fig picks up a smell that is not its own. A clean drawer is not holiness. It is cheaper than throwing a tray.

If the fridge is already packed with a lake trip, I do not wedge figs behind a casserole. I process. Wedges bruise. Casseroles win the space war. The tree does not care that you have lasagna.

A second cheap dorm fridge in the shop would be a luxury I have talked about and not always had. When I had one, I set it as cold as I dared and I still used it for keepers, not marbles. The luxury did not change the religion. It just gave the keepers a quieter room.

I do not store figs in the same drawer as apples if I can help it. Apples are ethylene loud. Figs do not need a roommate that wants them softer and older. A separate tub on a shelf is better than a fruit party.

I set a reminder on the phone for “figs in drawer” because I am the person who will forget a keeper under the celery. The reminder is ugly. The forgotten slime is uglier.

A thermometer in the drawer once is worth it. If your “crisper” is 48°F because the fridge is stuffed, you do not have a crisper. You have a cool closet. Process faster or fix the box. I have trusted a stuffed fridge and lost a Thursday.

The crisper is a pause for keepers. It is not a warehouse. Fill it like you will look again tomorrow, because you will, or you should.
