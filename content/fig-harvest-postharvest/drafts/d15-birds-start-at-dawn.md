Birds do not wait for you to finish breakfast. They have been to the plant while you were looking for the other shoe.

PapaFig on FigRoots already said a green fig might get eaten less than a dark one. That is odds, not a force field. A ripe green honey fig still goes. A dark berry that colored early goes first. If you only grow dark names and you only walk at noon, you are running a buffet.

I pick the ripe ones in the morning because that is the only “control” that does not involve a war. Netting works if you build it like you mean it and you do not leave a hole at the bottom for a cardinal to write a review. A sloppy net is a trap, and I will not harvest a trapped bird to save a fig. If you net, check the net.

CDs on strings, plastic owls, tin pie plates — I have tried the yard-sale theater. It works until it doesn’t, which is about three days after the birds get bored. I will not sell you a shiny object.

A dog helps if the dog is actually out at dawn. A dog on the couch is décor.

I do not poison. I do not shoot into a pot block. This is a food yard, not a campaign.

What I do: walk early, pick what is ready, pick a bird-save if the fruit is almost and the pecks have started on that plant, accept some loss. Loss is part of an 8a August. A clean plant with zero wildlife is a greenhouse story.

Pecked fruit does not go in the keep tray. I cut away the peck if I am standing there and the rest is honest, and I eat it. I do not fridge a peck. A peck is a hole with a future.

If a plant is being hammered, I pick closer for a couple of mornings and I finish that name’s ripe fruit into the kitchen. I would rather have a full dehydrator than a tree that is a bird classroom.

Green-skinned ripe names are a small strategy. So is a pot I can walk under the eave for the last three days of ripening. The pot is the tool. The owl is not.

Kids love the drama of “the birds ate our figs.” Let them. Then let them come on the morning walk. The lesson is clocks, not villains.

Zone 7 people with two figs will net a whole shrub for six fruit. Fine. Zone 9 people with orchards have a different math. We have enough plants that some fruit is rent. I pay rent. I do not pay the whole farm.

A single ripe fig left as “payment” does not buy protection. That is a story people tell so they can sleep in. The birds take the payment and then the rest. I have left the payment. I have still walked out to pecks. Rent is loss you already counted, not a treaty.

If a plant is young and only has six fruit, I will net that one like it is a baby. A mature stool with a glut can pay rent. Do not run the same rule on both and then feel noble. Noble is a six-fig plant with a clean net and a morning walk.

A mocker will sit on the shop roof and laugh. I still walk. The laugh is not data. The pecked necks on the ground are data. Tomorrow I set the alarm earlier. That is the program.
