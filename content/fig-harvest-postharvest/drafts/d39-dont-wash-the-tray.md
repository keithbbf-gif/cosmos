Water is for the minute before you eat, not for the minute after you pick.

A washed fig is a wet sponge with a sugar coat. In a tray it sweats. In a fridge it slimes. On a counter it invites every spore that was already bored. I brush clay. I pick off a leaf. I do not bathe a harvest.

The dull film on a ripe fig is not dirt. Sometimes it is just the skin being itself. I do not scrub for a photograph.

If they are actually dirty — a drop, a splash, a pot that got knocked — I rinse those few and I eat them or I dry the skins before they join a keep tray. The few. Not the flat.

A salad spinner is a joke I have watched. Do not.

Kids will wash everything because we told them to wash everything. Good. Then we eat those. We do not store those.

For the box, wet skins are steam. If I must wash before drying, I wait until the shine is gone. Patience on a rack. Then heat.

Commercial fruit is washed in a system that then blasts it cold. You do not have the system. You have a sink. The sink is not the system.

I do not use soap on a fig. I do not use a produce wash with a story. Water, if anything, and a hand, and then a mouth.

Latex on the skin is not dirt. It is sticky. Water does not make it less of a day. Gloves at pick are easier than a bath at the sink.

Zone 7 people with three figs can wash and eat. That is the whole storage. We have trays. Trays stay dry.

Clay dust on a dark fig looks worse in a photo than it eats. I brush it. I do not panic. Panic is a sink full of fruit that then will not keep. If you need a photo, brush, shoot, eat. Do not bathe for the camera and then try to store the models.

A spray bottle “to freshen” is a forum idea I have hated in person. Fresh is a neck on a tree. Water is weight and a clock.

When fruit comes in from a muddy drop, I rinse that one at the hose, eat it there if it passes, and I do not let it drip on the keepers. Isolation is not rude. Isolation is how the tray stays a tray.

A salad bowl of washed figs on ice is a restaurant move. Eat it. Do not put the ice melt back in the drawer. I have watched that melt become the week’s smell.

A damp paper towel under fruit is a hold trick, not a bath. Damp is a towel you wrung. Wet is a puddle. I have turned a damp towel into a puddle by being generous. Generous is a slime.

I wash the tray, not the fruit. A dirty flat from the clay row does not become a storage dish. Dump, wash the plastic, move keepers to a clean pan. People wash the figs and leave the clay tray. That is backwards. The tray is the farm. The fruit is the food.

If pollen or dust bothers you, a dry brush is a tool. A sink is a clock. I keep a clean paintbrush in the shop for fruit the way I keep one for labels. Two brushes. One story.

If you remember one kitchen rule from this set, remember this one when you are being “helpful.” Helpful is a dry tray and a washed plate at supper. Helpful is not a bath for fruit you wanted to keep until Thursday.
