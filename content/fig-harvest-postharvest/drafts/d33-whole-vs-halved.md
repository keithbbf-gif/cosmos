A small Celeste can go in whole and come out a raisin with a job. A big bronze cave will sit in the box like a water balloon with a tan.

I cut what needs cutting. That is most fruit that is larger than a walnut, and any fruit I do not trust in the center. Halves, cut side up, so the wet looks at the fan. Quarters if it is a monster or if the week was rain.

Whole is prettier in a jar. Pretty is not dry. I will put a few wholes in for the people who want to stuff them later. I will not let pretty set the timer for the rest of the tray.

A slit from the eye toward the stem is a compromise on a medium fig. It lets steam out. It is not as honest as a half. I use it when I am tired of sticky boards.

Seeds and jelly will leak on a half. That is sugar on the tray. It will cook into glue. Parchment. Rotate. Do not scrape with a knife that then goes into the next fig like a science experiment.

Kitchen-firm fruit halves cleaner than dessert-soft fruit. Dessert-soft smears. If I want neat halves, I pick the canning-firm tray for the box and I eat the dessert. If I want the best flavor in the jar, I dry the dessert and I accept the smear.

I do not peel. Peeling a fig for the box is a hobby from a different century.

Stems: off if they are woody, on if they help me flip a whole. No sacrament.

Mix sizes on one tray and the small ones will be glass before the large ones are leather. Separate trays. Separate pull times. This is not hard. This is why the box has more than one shelf.

Kids can cut with a table knife on the firm ones. Soft ones are mine. Blood in a dehydrator is a story I do not need.

A puree on a liner is fruit leather, not a dried fig. I will do leather with a glut that is too far gone for halves but still honest. I will not call leather a fig in a jar.

Zone 9 tree-shriveled fruit can go in whole because the tree already cut the water. Our fruit did not get that favor. Cut it.

I dry seeds-and-all. I do not scrape a fig hollow to make a fancy chip. If someone at the table hates crunch, they can have a leather or a puree. The half is still a fig. A scraped skin is a project I do not run on a Tuesday.

A mandoline is how you donate a fingertip to a Tuesday. Table knife. Board. Done. Blood is not a rustic note.

I dry cut-side up even when the fruit looks like it will leak a lake. The lake is sugar leaving. The swamp is water staying. I will mop a tray. I will not jar a swamp.

A whole fig that looks done and still rolls heavy is a wet center wearing a costume. Cut it. If I am too tired to cut, I am too tired to jar. Tired fruit goes back in or goes to a pot. I do not reward a costume with a lid.

I also do not quarter everything into confetti because I saw a chip recipe. Confetti dries fast and tastes like the tray. Halves are still food. Chips are a snack I did not ask August to become.

If you only remember one cut: cut-side up, like it still has something to say to the fan. Face down is how you hide a swamp.
