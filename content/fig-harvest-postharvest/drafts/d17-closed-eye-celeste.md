Celeste is the Arkansas lecture. Small, brown, tight eye, early, cold-hardy, slow to forgive a hard prune. UAEX has been giving that lecture so long I will not pretend I invented it.

In a wet week I pick Celeste like I have time. I pick an open-eye name like I do not.

That is the whole craft difference. The neck still rules. The eye changes how much “one more day” you can afford. On a pinprick eye I will let a fig hang from first nod to a softer nod if the hornets are busy elsewhere. On a cave I will take it at first honest give if the radar is green.

PapaFig’s climate line is the buying line: humid place, tight eyes, often smaller fruit. Harvest is where that sentence becomes a tray. The small sugar figs come in a heap and they keep better on the plant in rain. The big bronze caves come in a parade and they dare you.

I still like a good open-eye fig in a dry stretch. I will not trash a name because August 2024 was a monsoon. I will trash a plan that only planted caves and then went to the lake for the weekend.

Celeste after a hard winter is a later, lighter crop. Do not pick it like it is “early” on the chart if the plant is restarting. The eye is still tight. The calendar moved.

Commerce will hand you “Celeste” that is not. Name confusion is the sport. If your “Celeste” has a tunnel and a long hang, believe the fruit. Write what it is on the pot. Harvest the plant you have.

I do not wait for Celeste to get huge. It will not. People who want a turkey-sized fig will pick Celeste late looking for size and get a raisin or a sour if the week turns. Size is not the test. Give is the test. Small and giving is the point of that plant.

Open-eye week protocol: morning walk, smell, first honest give, into the now tray if the skin already cracked, into the keep tray if it is intact. Do not leave a cracked cave “for sweetness.” Sweetness is already there or it is not. Bacteria will add a flavor you did not order.

Closed-eye week protocol: same walk, more patience, still no yank. Tight is not permission to go on vacation.

I will pick a mixed row in that order: caves that are ready, then the tight-eyes that are ready, then I leave. I do not “finish the tree” because I am already out there. Finishing the tree is how hard fruit gets into the bowl and then into a lie about the variety.

Zone 7 growers love Celeste because it lives. Zone 9 growers sometimes call it ordinary because they can ripen circus names. We are 8a. Ordinary that shows up in a wet August is not ordinary. It is the farm.

I still grow caves. I like a big bronze on a dry week. I just do not plan a lake weekend on their peak. The tight-eye heap is what lets me leave town on a Friday if I picked Thursday morning and processed the rest. That is the only luxury the eye buys you.

When a guest asks “which one should I plant,” I point at the tight eye first if they have a lake weekend personality. I point at the cave if they like a long parade and they will be home. Personality is climate.

If you only have room for one in-ground in clay, I would rather you had a tight eye than a romance. If you have pots, you can play. Play is for names you can walk out of the rain.
