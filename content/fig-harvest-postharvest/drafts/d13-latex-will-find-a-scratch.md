Fig sap is not a rustic charm. It is latex. It will find the scratch on your wrist you forgot about and turn the afternoon into a reminder.

Some people never notice. Some people welt like they touched a jellyfish. I will not guess which one you are. I will tell you to find out on one fruit, not on a three-hour harvest in a short-sleeve shirt.

Janet Carson said you may want gloves. I want gloves if I am doing a loaded plant, or if I already know I am a welter, or if I am cutting kitchen-firm fruit on purpose. A clean twist of a truly ripe fig leaks less. A yank leaks more. Technique is PPE.

Long sleeves in August sound like a joke until the cane slaps you. The leaves are rough. The sap is worse. I would rather be hot than decorated.

If it gets on your skin, soap and water sooner is better than a story later. Do not rub your eyes. Do not be the person who wipes sweat with the sap hand. I have been that person. The shop does not need a second verse.

Clothes take the stain and the itch. I do not harvest in the shirt I like. I harvest in the shirt that already lost.

Kids and latex: I do not make it a scare. I make it a rule. Hands off the white stuff. Wash when we go in. If a child is a welter, they carry the shaded tray and eat washed fruit at the table.

The sap is in green parts — unripe fruit, fresh stems, a broken leaf. Ripe fruit still has some at the wound. A tray of torn shoulders is a sap tray. That is another reason the stem stays on.

I keep a rag that is only the sap rag. It lives near the hose. It does not live in the kitchen towel stack. Cross-contamination is a fancy word for “I wiped a knife and then I ate.”

People will tell you to use the sap as a curdle for milk or a folk wart story. This set is not a folk medical page. Harvest is enough. Do not put fig sap on a project because a comment said so.

If you get it in a cut, it burns. That is the plant being a plant. Rinse. Done. If you get a real reaction — swelling, trouble breathing — that is a person problem, not a fig-forum problem. I am not your doctor. I am the person who will say I have seen enough rashes to keep a box of cheap nitrile in the shop.

Nitrile beats cotton gloves for sap. Cotton soaks and then rubs it in. Nitrile gets slick when the fruit is wet, so I still want a dry morning if I can have one.

A ladder plus sap plus heat is how people fall. Keep the plants where your feet are on the ground. That is pruning philosophy leaking into harvest, and I will let it leak.

A shower after a long pick is not luxury. It is how you do not take the day to bed. If sap dried on a wrist, it will remind you when the sheet rubs. I keep a grease-cutting soap at the shop sink for that reason, not because I am precious.

The latex is why I am boring about the twist. Every torn fig is a little chemical event in the tray and on you. Pick like you respect it. The fruit is sweet. The sap is not trying to be.
