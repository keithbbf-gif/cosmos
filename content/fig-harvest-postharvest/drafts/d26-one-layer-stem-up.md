If the fruit is off the tree and not in a mouth, it gets a single layer. That is the whole interior design.

A pile is a bruise factory and a sweat lodge. The bottom fig pays for the photo of a full bowl. I like a full bowl on the table at supper. I do not like a full bowl at 9 a.m. on a counter that still has work to do.

Stem up is how I park a fig I care about until tonight. The stem is the lid. The shoulder is the bruise. Sitting a fig on its wound is how you print a wet circle onto a towel and then onto the next fruit.

A sheet pan with a towel is enough. A plate is enough. A fancy riser is a magazine. I do not need a magazine. I need to see them.

Space is a kindness. They can touch. They should not stack. If I am out of pan, I am out of “hold.” I am in “eat” and “freeze” and “neighbor.”

I turn them once if I am holding overnight and I remember. I do not build a schedule. A turn is so I see a weep I missed.

The now fruit — splits, pecks, torn shoulders — do not share the overnight pan. They share a smaller plate that will be empty by noon or they share a pot. One wet fig trains the rest.

Kids will build a pyramid because it looks like a market. Markets have foam and a cold case. We have a table. Pyramid is for oranges.

If you must bowl them for a guest, bowl them at the last minute from the pan. The bowl is a serving tool, not a storage tool.

I do not cover with plastic wrap. Plastic wrap is a rain cloud. A light cloth if there are flies. Flies in an 8a kitchen in August are not a hypothetical.

Night kitchen temps here are not a cellar. If the house is still 80°F at 10 p.m., the overnight hold is shorter. Eat more. Fridge the true keepers. Do not invent a pantry.

Zone 9 dry counters can hold a layer longer. Our layer is a night. I would rather say a night and be wrong toward the mouth than say three days and be right toward the trash.

A picnic basket with a checked cloth is a pile with a costume. I have taken that costume to a table and watched the bottom layer arrive as paste. If you are going to a neighbor, take a flat with a cloth over it, not a basket you saw in a magazine.

Stem down for a minute while you wash your hands is not a crime. Stem down overnight is a wet ring. I am not precious about a minute. I am precious about a night. The ring is how mold finds a door.

I reuse a bakery box with a lid cracked if I am taking fruit in a car. The lid keeps the sun off. The crack keeps the rain cloud from forming. A closed foam cooler is a stew. I have opened the stew. I now crack the lid.

If a fig rolls, the towel has a job. A slick pan is how fruit gathers in a corner and becomes a pile you did not vote for. I would rather wash a towel than unstick a corner of jam.

Stem up, one layer, see them. If you cannot see them, you cannot decide. Decision is the whole postharvest. A pile is how you delay a decision until it is made for you.
