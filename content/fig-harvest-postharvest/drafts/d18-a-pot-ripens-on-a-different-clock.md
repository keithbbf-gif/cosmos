The same name in a pot and in the clay will not keep the same calendar. I have a lot of pots. I have a smaller in-ground set. I have been lied to by both.

A #5 on concrete is a skillet with roots. Fruit colors early. Fruit also stalls when the mix hits the 90s and the plant shuts down. You can walk that skillet into morning sun and afternoon shade. That walk is a harvest tool. It is not décor.

In-ground in our clay is slower to start, steadier in a heat wave if you watered, and later to finish a late name. You cannot walk the hole. You can mulch it and not plant it in a puddle. Jack already wrote the clay-hole sermon in the other set. Here the sermon is: do not pick the in-ground because the driveway pot went yesterday.

I harvest pots first in the morning loop. They are the ones the birds can see from the wire. They are the ones that dry out. They are the ones I can save from a west wall at 2 p.m. by moving them before I go in.

A pot that is too small will set fruit and then drop it like a bad habit. That is not a variety. That is volume. If you are harvesting droppers off a #2 in August, the pick is not your problem. The pot is.

I do not strip a pot because I want to “let it rest.” Ripe fruit comes off. The rest stay. A pot is not a bowl just because you can lift it.

Shade cloth on a block of pots in a 100°F stretch keeps more necks honest than a prayer. I have used it. I still water. Cloth is not a well.

When a pot is loaded and the forecast is ugly, I walk it under the eave for the last days. That is cheating and I recommend it. In-ground people have to pick closer. Pot people have a hallway.

Nematodes are why some of us are in pots at all. FigRoots already said it. Harvest does not change that. You still pick ripe. You still do not eat a sour. You just do it at waist height, which is the one kindness.

A greenhouse pot in March is a different climate than a greenhouse pot in August. August glass is a punishment. I do not finish fruit under glass in our summer unless I like cooked leaves. The shop eave is enough of a room.

Zone 7 pots come inside for winter and their harvest clock is whatever you gave them under lights. Zone 9 pots live outside and get cocky. Ours live outside most of the year and still get walked like livestock in August.

Write the pot size on the tape next to the name. When you compare dates next year, compare #5 to #5, hole to hole. A chart that mixes them is how rumors start.

Write the side of the shop on the pot if you have to — east, west, eave. Next year you will move it and forget why last year’s dates jumped. The handle is only useful if you remember what you did with it.

A saucer under a pot in August is a swamp. Fruit will stall and sour while the roots drown. Harvest timing includes dumping the saucer. I have forgotten a saucer and then written a bad week on the wrong name.

The pot is not a lesser tree. It is a tree with a handle. Use the handle. Then pick what is actually ready, not what the handle made convenient.
