The number on the dial is not the fig. The center is the fig.

I run the box around **135°F** because that is the fruit preset and I am not trying to invent a laboratory. Paper will say 140. I will not fight 5 degrees. I will fight “high,” I will fight a box with no fan, and I will fight the urge to pull fruit because the skins look done.

Skins lie. A wrinkly skin can hide a jam pocket. That pocket is how a jar grows fur in October. Cut one from the middle of the tray, not the edge. Edge dries first. Middle is the truth.

Leather, not soup, not glass. Bend it. A little tack is fine. A wet bead is not. If it snaps like a chip, I still keep it, and I use it chopped, and I turn the dial down next time or I pull earlier.

Rotate trays. Cheap boxes have a hot floor and a dumb ceiling. I swap top to bottom once. I do not open the door every twenty minutes to parent the fruit. Every open door is 8a humidity clocking back in.

Time is a range. Eight hours, twelve, eighteen. Small Celeste halves are not a turkey whole. A wet August fig is not a September fig. I write the start time on tape on the box so a helper does not “check” by turning it off.

I do not dry overnight on a guess if I cannot smell the shop in the morning. A box can finish at 3 a.m. and then sit in residual heat. That is usually fine. A box that stalls because someone unplugged it to run a saw is not fine. Then you have warm wet fruit in a dark plastic cave. That is a compost story.

Oil the trays if your fruit welds. Parchment if the trays are a nightmare. I have welded a fig to a mesh and lost the argument.

If the center is wet and the skin is tough, I drop the heat a little and give it more time. Crusting is how you trap a swamp. Patience is cheaper than a second batch.

Davis wants about 17% moisture for a commercial dry fig. I am not selling a pallet. I am filling jars for a house. Cut, bend, done. If I am storing a long time in a warm pantry, I dry a little further and I still condition. Next piece.

Zone 7 winter houses can finish a box easier because the air is dry. Our August box works harder. Let it.

A cheap analog dial can be 20 degrees wrong. I learned that on a tray that browned too fast. A twenty-dollar oven thermometer in the box for one run is not science theater. It is how you find out your 135 is 155. Then you turn it down and you stop blaming the fruit.

I write “cut me” on a piece of tape on the door so I remember the middle fig is a tool, not a snack I steal at hour three. Stealing the middle is how you lose the truth.

If two boxes disagree, believe the fruit, not the brand. I have run a “good” box that ran hot and a cheap box that ran true. Tape the true number on the door after you measure once.

I pull trays that are done and leave the wet ones in. Mixing done and wet in a closed box is how the done pieces pick up steam and soften again. Separate the finished. Let the stubborn ones have the fan to themselves.

The dial is a starting sentence. The wet center is the last word. Believe the last word.
