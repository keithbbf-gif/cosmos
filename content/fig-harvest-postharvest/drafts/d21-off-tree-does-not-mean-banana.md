The forum has a slogan: figs do not ripen off the tree. The warehouse has a different slogan: figs are climacteric, they make ethylene, they soften after you pick them if you picked them at the commercial stage. Both slogans have wrecked a bowl.

Here is the shop version. A hard green fig is a vegetable with ambitions. It will not turn into the fruit on the intro page. It will not get the sugar it never made. You can put it in a paper bag with an apple. You can put it in a sunny window. You can talk to it. You will still eat a polite nothing, or you will throw it away when it rots from the inside while the skin looks brave.

A fig that already had color, a starting neck, and some give — the commercial “almost fully ripe and still firm” fig — will soften on a counter. It may smell more like itself. It may eat better on day two than at 7 a.m. when you were in a hurry. That is not a banana. A banana is a starch factory that planned this. A fig is mostly done building sugar on the tree. Off-tree is a short courtesy, not a career.

UC Davis will tell you commercial fruit is picked near ripe because that is the only eating quality that works, and that a cold room at 30–32°F with wet air can hold it a week or two. We do not have that room. We have an 8a kitchen in August. Our courtesy is one day, maybe two, one layer, out of the sun. Then the fridge if we must, or the mouth.

I will not let a paper word — climacteric — talk you into stripping a plant of marbles. I will not let a forum slogan talk you into throwing away a fig that was honest-almost and got a little softer overnight. The mouth is the referee. The slogans are the crowd.

PapaFig’s intro line still stands: fully ripe, excellent variety. That is the farm. Off-tree is the emergency door and the shipping door. We use the emergency door for birds, rain, and a frost night. We do not live in the shipping door.

If someone brings you a grocery clamshell, that fruit was picked for a truck. It may soften. It may never be the fig that made you plant a stick. Do not grade your backyard name against a clamshell that had a different job, and do not grade the clamshell against a tree fruit as if someone cheated you.

I have left a tray of almosts on the shop bench overnight because a storm was coming and I picked close. Morning, some were dessert. Some were still stubborn. The stubborn ones did not become dessert because I waited until lunch. They became tired. Eat the ones that moved. Cook the ones that didn’t. Do not wait for a miracle at 3 p.m.

Zone 7 people pick closer because frost. Their counter is a tool. Zone 9 people can hang fruit and get smug. Our tool is the tree plus one honest night if we had to be early. That is the whole religion.

I will put the two slogans on the shop wall if I have to: not a banana, not a never. Then I will still use the mouth. Walls do not eat.

Off-tree is a word I will use in this set. I will not let it mean banana. I will not let it mean never. I will let it mean: sugar is mostly a tree job, softening can finish in the house, green rocks are compost.
