A dehydrator is not a priest. It will not bless a sour fig. It will concentrate the sour and then you will have a jar of regret that lasts all winter.

I dry fruit that was already a good ripe fig, or an honest almost that made it on the plate. I do not dry vinegar. I do not dry a wasp house. I do not dry a marble and hope it becomes a raisin with a personality. A dried marble is a bullet.

PapaFig already wrote the old story: late summer crop dried or pressed so people could travel. That fruit was ripe enough to be food. They were not drying homework.

Sort on the table before the box ever gets plugged in. Keepers that are dessert-soft can dry. Kitchen-firm can dry if you accept a tougher chew. Splits that just opened and still smell like fig can dry if you cut to the split and you are fast. Splits that smell like a cider experiment go to the hens.

I cut out a peck. I do not dry a peck and call it rustic.

Wash only if they are dirty, and then dry the skins before they go in the box or you are steaming. I would rather brush clay off a dry fig than bathe a tray.

Stem on or stem off: I usually take the stem if it is stubborn and leave it if it is a little lid. There is no sacrament. There is chewing.

If the glut is more than the box, I freeze and dry later, or I freeze and do not dry. Frozen puree is a winter tool. I will write that. Do not overload a box because you are stubborn. Overload is wet centers and a 14-hour night.

Commercial California fruit is often tree-shriveled and then finished at about 140°F to around 17% moisture. That is a dry climate’s head start. We do not have their head start. We have a ripe wet fig and a box. Start with good fruit or do not start.

I have dried a mediocre fig because I hated waste. I have then not eaten the jar. Waste is also a jar you do not want. The compost is allowed to win.

I have dried a tray of “fine” figs because I hated looking at them. The jar was fine too. Fine is how a pantry fills with food nobody reaches for. Eat the excellent fresh. Dry the excellent overflow. Fine can be a pot the same night and then it is done being a decision.

A fermented smell in the box means I missed a sour. Stop the box. Sort again. One bad fig will perfume a run. I have thrown a whole shelf of leather because I was stubborn about one cave. Stubborn is expensive.

A fig that sat in a hot car for an hour is not automatically a dry candidate. It may be a now candidate. Heat is not a dehydrator if the center is already a ferment. Smell it. If you hesitate, the hole does not.

I do not dry fruit I would not serve a neighbor tonight. That sentence has saved more jars than any temperature. If I hesitate to hand it over, the box does not get it.

A “seconds” basket from a neighbor is not automatically a box batch. Seconds can mean split, pecked, or just ugly. Ugly and honest dries. Seconds that smell like a storm do not. Sort their basket like it is yours. Politeness is how a science project starts.

Zone 9 people tree-dry and finish. Zone 7 people may never have a glut worth a box. We have August. August is why the box exists. Use it on food, not on a rescue fantasy.
