A rain does not just change the picking. It changes how long anything you picked will behave.

Fruit that took on water is thinner in sugar, easier to split, quicker to sour, quicker to slime in a box. I do not put a rain harvest on the same “three days in the crisper” plan as a dry Wednesday. Rain fruit is tonight and tomorrow. Then a job.

I dry the skins if they came in wet — a towel, a fan in the room, not a sunbeam. Then I decide. I do not fridge them wet and hope the drawer is a miracle.

Open-eye names after a rain are on the shortest clock. Closed-eye names get a little more, not a vacation.

If the rain was the drought-breaker, expect cracks. Cracks are now. Now is a pot or a mouth or a box the same day. I have tried to “hold the cracked ones for Saturday.” Saturday was a shop.

A rain week is when I say no to a neighbor’s “just bring me a bowl on Sunday.” I can bring a jar. I can bring a frozen bag. I should not bring a Sunday of Monday’s rain fruit.

The yard after rain is slick. I still walk. I do not store what I slipped on.

Kids will want to pick everything because everything looks shiny. Shiny is water. Feel the equator.

I run the box more in a rain week, not less. People think wet weather is a reason to wait. Wet weather is a reason to change jobs faster.

Zone 9 can have a rain and still have dry air the next day. Our next day is still a blanket. Translate.

A rain that hits at 4 p.m. after a 98°F day is the ugly one. Fruit is warm, water is in the eye, night is still hot. I pick what I can before dark and I process that night. Sleeping on that tray is how Wednesday starts with wings.

I do not fertilize after a rain to “help them recover.” They do not need a meal. They need you to stop making soft tips and to deal with the fruit that already existed.

If the week is rain, rain, rain, I pick closer every morning and I stop dreaming about a long hang. The hang is for a dry stretch. The clock is the weather’s, not mine.

A wet week is when I cancel a “fig tasting.” Tastings want dry skins and time. Rain wants a pot and a box. I have hosted a tasting after a storm and I have served watery fruit I was too stubborn to reframe. Stubborn is not hospitality.

I cancel a sale or a trade pickup after a monsoon if the fruit is the trade. I will trade a jar or a bag. I will not trade a wet Tuesday and call it professional. Weather is allowed to change a plan.

I dry my hands before I pick after a rain. Wet fingers tear necks. A towel on the belt is not precious. It is how the fruit still has a shoulder when it hits the tray. Weather already did enough.

I write “rain” on the tape on a rain-week tray so a helper does not apply the dry-Wednesday plan. The word is ugly. The wrong plan is uglier.

Shorten the clock on purpose and you still like figs after a storm. Keep the dry-week clock and you will tell someone “figs don’t keep,” which is a half-truth you earned the hard way.
