In the peak I walk every morning. Not because I am holy. Because a ripe fig in 8a August is a 24-hour object.

Yesterday’s “tomorrow” is on the ground. Tomorrow’s “almost” is still a marble. The day you have is the day you pick. Extension will say every two or three days. That is a polite sentence for a plant with three figs. A loaded Celeste in a warm wet week is a daily job. A long-running turkey pile is a daily job with a longer plot.

I do it in the morning. Jack will write why. My reason is simpler: I am already out with water, and the fruit is cooler, and I can see necks without the 4 p.m. theater. If I miss a morning I do an evening pass and I am gentler, because heat-soft fruit tears.

A weekend grower in August is a donor. Birds work Saturday at dawn. Wasps work Saturday at 10. The droppers work Saturday all day. If you can only come Saturdays, pick a little sooner on Friday night if you have a neighbor, or grow fewer plants, or accept that you are farming for wildlife. I am not judging the wildlife. I am done pretending a Sunday bowl is a method.

Write a time if you have to. 7:15, walk, tray, shade. It takes longer to think about than to do once the hands know the plants. New names take longer. Old names I can read from the gate.

Daily does not mean strip. Daily means judge. Most fruit stays. A few come. The tray should look like a meal and a leftover, not a panic.

When the stall week hits, daily is still the walk, but the tray is lighter. That is information. When the stall breaks, daily is how you do not lose the glut in two afternoons.

I put the tray in the shade as I go. A bowl on a tailgate in the sun is a cooker. By the end of the row you have jam you did not ask for.

Kids will want to “help” by grabbing every colored fig. Give them one plant you already picked or a pot of something you can afford. A child who learns to wait for the neck is a better harvest than an extra quart of hard fruit.

If I am gone two days, I pick closer before I leave and I do not romanticize what I will find when I get back. I will find vinegar, wings, and a few miracles under the leaves. The miracles go in the mouth on the spot. The rest is a cleanup.

Zone 7 peak can be a short panic in September. Zone 9 peak can be a lifestyle. Ours is August, with a tail. Put it on the real calendar, the one with dentist appointments. The figs do not care that you have a meeting.

If I am going to town, I walk before I shower. Vanity is how a tray becomes a ground pile. The shop can wait. The necks cannot. I have come home at 6 p.m. to a plant that was perfect at 7 a.m. and I have not forgiven the errand.

A paper calendar on the fridge that says “figs??” in August is not a joke if you live here. Put a real time on a real day. I have missed a dentist because I was in the row, and I have missed the row because I was at the dentist. Only one of those can wait a week.

Daily is not content. Daily is how you still like figs at the end of the month. A glut you ignored becomes a smell. A glut you walked becomes a freezer bag, a jar of dry fruit, a bowl for a neighbor. That is the calendar I will keep writing down until the first frost argues.
