When the neck gives up, you can pick. That is the sentence I want on the tape, not a color chart.

A fig holds itself out on a little stem like it is still a leaf. For weeks it is cocky. Then the neck wilts, the fruit leans, and the whole thing looks slightly embarrassed. That lean is not disease. That lean is the plant saying the sugars are in and the water balance has shifted and you may have this one.

I do not mean floppy rotten. I mean the posture change. Upright is homework. Nodding is supper.

People wait past the nod because they want “one more day of sweetness.” One more day in 8a August is how you get a sour eye, a bird bite, or a fig that drops into the clay and becomes a wasp house. The nod is not early. The nod is on time.

Touch confirms it. A nodding fig should give like a ripe peach, not like a water balloon and not like a golf ball. Balloon is late or rain-split. Golf ball is a liar with a bent neck from drought. Drought will wilt a neck and still leave the inside empty. If the week was dry and the leaves were tired, water first, then judge tomorrow. Do not strip a thirsty tree because the fruit sagged.

The twist should finish the argument. Nod plus give plus a stem that lets go — that is a harvest. Nod plus a fight is a fig that got sunburned on one side or a variety that always hangs. Learn the name in *your* yard. A review plate from October 1 does not tell you how that name hangs in your clay.

I pick with two hands when I can. One hand lifts the fruit so the neck does not tear the shoulder. One hand twists. Stem stays on the fig. A torn shoulder weeps latex and rots in the tray. A clean stem is a lid.

Morning necks are easier to read. Afternoon heat makes everything look tired. I have walked a row at 4 p.m. and thought the whole plant was ready, then come back at 7 a.m. and found half of them standing up again. Heat wilt is not harvest wilt. If you only have evenings, pick the ones that give, not the ones that merely hang.

Zone 9 people sometimes pick firmer because they have a longer, drier hang and they are thinking about a box. Zone 7 people pick anything that changed color because frost is in the forecast. We are 8a. We have time in August and we have humidity. The neck is how you use the time without donating the week to souring.

A fig that nods on the tree and then sits in a bucket will keep nodding into mush. The sentence is for the tree, not for the counter. Once it is off, you are in postharvest, and postharvest is a short story.

I still eat one at the plant. If the neck said yes and the mouth says no, I have the wrong name or a water problem. I do not rewrite the neck rule. I fix the week.

Watch a single fruit for three mornings if you are new. Same fig. Monday upright, Tuesday a hint, Wednesday a nod. That is your teacher. A tray of mixed names will confuse you because each name has a different idea of posture. Learn one plant. Then steal the habit for the rest.

The neck is not folklore. It is the plant moving water out of a fruit it is done building. You can argue with a calendar. You cannot argue with a wilted neck that already gave and twists off in your fingers. That fig was never going to get better on the tree. It was only going to get stolen.
