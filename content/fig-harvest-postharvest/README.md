# Fig harvest & postharvest — Zone 8a, staged

Staged magazine set. Not published copy. Not a catalog. Not a ripening chart
you can tape to the fridge and obey.

These pieces are for a person who already has fruit on a common fig and wants
the next week told in a human voice. The center of gravity is **USDA Zone 8a**
(average annual extreme minimum **10–15°F**), rural South Arkansas weather,
common figs only. Zone 7 and Zone 9 sit in the room because 8a people borrow
methods from both and get hurt when they borrow the wrong one.

Five jobs, five stages:

| Stage | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `time` | Harvest timing: August crop, breba as bonus, neck, eye, heat stall, frost |
| `pick` | Picking: twist, morning, latex, trays, birds, wasps, pots, drops |
| `ripen` | Off-tree: softening is not sugar, bird-save, windowsill, fridge lies |
| `dry` | Drying: ripe fruit only, 8a humidity, sun vs box, 135°F, jars |
| `store` | Storage: days not a week, crisper, freeze the glut, sour stays out |

Drafts live in `drafts/`. Editorial status on every file is `status: staged` —
ready for a human editor, not live, not SEO-published. `INDEX.md` is the read
order. `PHOTO_NOTES.md` is the shoot/pick brief for stills on **KC-PC** under
`D:\FIGS`.

## Numbers and house facts we already published (do not restage)

- Best eating is a **fully ripe** fruit of an **excellent variety** (FigRoots
  intro). A grocery fig is not the argument.
- **GDDs** decide whether a late name finishes. A wonderful fig with not enough
  heat is a green ornament (PapaFig, *Pick The Right Fig For You*).
- Humid climate: look for **tight eyes** and often **smaller** fruit.
- South summer: some **shade** and **lots of water**. North advice that says
  “full blast, no shade” leaks into 8a internet and cooks a pot.
- Birds hit dark fruit first. A green-skinned ripe fig is not invisible. It is
  just later on their list.
- Flavor families we already name: berry, honey, sugar, melon, peachy.
- Review stills already carry dates. A Red Sicilian and an LSU Jack Lily were
  scored **October 1, 2024**. Another dark berry sat at **August 15, 2024**.
  Cite the live review. Do not invent a new date.
- Dried and pressed cakes are how people kept a late summer crop. PapaFig
  already wrote that history. This set is the shop version, not a second
  archaeology post.

## Local calendar we will not argue with extension about

Janet Carson / UAEX, said in plain Arkansas: **August is fig season**. Celeste
usually before Brown Turkey. Celeste has the tight eye. Brown Turkey (the
commerce name that also wears Texas Everbearing) runs longer and rebounds
faster after a hard winter or a hard prune. Celeste does not. **Breba rarely
persists** through an Arkansas winter. The crop you eat is on **this year’s
wood**. Wet August after heat sours an open eye. Drought drops fruit.

Field practice still outruns paper. The drafts say so.
