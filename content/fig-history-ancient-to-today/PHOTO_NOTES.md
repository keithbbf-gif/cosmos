# Photo Notes — History of the Fig

How this pack uses pictures. The series is **not** a fruit-catalog shoot. A reader should leave remembering a Senate fig, a wasp, a drying yard, a fresco, and a crate stencil — not only a split Black Mission.

## Three image classes

### 1. License-clear historical and scientific images (preferred)

Museum open-access files and Wikimedia PD / CC reproductions of:

- tomb and villa painting (Egypt, Pompeii, Herculaneum)
- botanical plates (Ehret, Köhler, Gottorfer Codex, Le Moyne, Bimbi)
- historic maps and port views (Smyrna, Cádiz, San Diego, Fresno valley)
- later documentary photographs that are PD or CC (packing sheds, USDA plates, 19th-c. orchards)

Every such file is logged in `IMAGE_SOURCES.md`. If the license is not on the log, the image does not ship.

## Caption rules

- Credit + license in the caption or in a one-line credit under it.
- “After Ehret, 1771” is a plate. “Photograph of a 1771 tree” is a lie.
- “Schematic; not a surveyed map” on every original map.
- People in orchard photos: consent, or crop to hands/work.
- Do not use Priapus / erotic Pompeii frescoes as cheap color. The Casa dei Vettii Priapus is a fertility account; if used, say so and keep it in the art/labor piece, not as a thumbnail.

## Balance test (per article)

Before staging, count figure types:

- at least **one** non-fruit image (site, map, plate, diagram, document, portrait, packing)
- fruit photographs, if any, **do not outnumber** the rest
- one figure that could run in a history magazine with the fruit cropped out and still make sense

## What we will not do

- Claim a model-generated “tomb painting” is from Thebes.
- Hotlink a museum file whose license is All Rights Reserved.
- Build a hero image that is three figs on linen and a serif headline.
- Put a Calimyrna beauty shot on the Gilgal article.
