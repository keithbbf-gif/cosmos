# Bibliography — History of the Fig

Working bibliography for the staged pack. Entries are sources actually used or checked for the articles. Page numbers are given only when verified in a public text or a standard numbering. This is not a dump of “further reading.”

If a claim in an article cannot be walked back to a line here, cut the claim.

## Ancient and late antique texts

- **Hebrew Bible.** Standard chapter/verse. Especially Genesis 3:7 (fig leaves); Numbers 13:23 (Eshcol cluster with figs); Deuteronomy 8:8 (the seven species); 1 Kings 4:25 / Micah 4:4 (“under his vine and under his fig tree”); 2 Kings 20:7 / Isaiah 38:21 (cake of figs as poultice); Amos 7:14 (dresser of sycomore-figs — *F. sycomorus*, not *carica*).
- **Qurʾān 95:1.** “By the fig and the olive” (*wa-l-tīn wa-l-zaytūn*). Do not over-interpret; the oath is the fact.
- **Theophrastus.** *Historia Plantarum* (*Enquiry into Plants*), esp. books 1–2 on roots, propagation, and the remark that gall insects from wild figs make cultivated figs swell (standard Hort Loeb numbering).
- **Cato the Elder.** *De Agri Cultura* 8.1 — marisca on chalky/open ground; African, Herculanean, Saguntine, winter, and long-stalked black Telanian on richer or manured ground. (Quoted by Pliny.)
- **Columella.** *De Re Rustica* — villa fig culture, preservation, and the later Roman working orchard. Use for practice, not for a variety census.
- **Pliny the Elder.** *Naturalis Historia* 15.19 (twenty-nine varieties; Chalcis, Chios, Lydia, African, Alexandrian, Livian, Pompeian, marisca, Herculanean, Tarentine *ona*, Cato’s list, Moesian winter figs by art); 15.20 (Cato’s Carthage fig in the Senate). Latin: LacusCurtius / Rackham Loeb. English: Bostock & Riley on Perseus.
- **Plutarch.** *Solon* 24.1 — oil-only export; he reports, with caution, the story that fig export was also forbidden and that the informer was called a sycophant. Treat as Plutarch, not as an Archaic inscription.
- **Strabo.** *Geography* 2.1.14 — Hyrcanian figs (productivity topos; cited via Iranica).
- **Dioscorides.** *De Materia Medica* — fig as food and simple; the trunk from which Galenic and later Arabic writers hang.
- **Geoponica** (Cassianus Bassus compilation) — Byzantine agricultural digest; a major citation pool for Ibn al-ʿAwwām.

## Medieval Islamic and Persianate

- **Ibn Waḥshiyya** (attrib.). *al-Filāḥa al-Nabaṭiyya* (*Nabataean Agriculture*). Quoted heavily by later agronomists; treat attribution as traditional.
- **Ibn Baṣṣāl.** 11th-c. Toledo–Seville agronomist; *Hāʾiṭ al-Sulṭān* garden. Survives in part; reconstructed through later quotation.
- **Ibn Ḥajjāj.** *al-Muqniʿ fī l-filāḥa* (1073) — olive, vine, fig extracts survive; extensively used by Ibn al-ʿAwwām.
- **Abū l-Khayr al-Ishbīlī.** Seville, c. 1070–75.
- **Ibn al-ʿAwwām.** *Kitāb al-Filāḥa*, late 12th-c. Seville / Aljarafe. Banqueri Spanish ed. 1802; Clément-Mullet French 1864–67. Filāḥa Texts Project: http://filaha.org/author_ibn_al_awwam.html
- **Al-Muqaddasī, Ibn Ḥawqal, al-Masʿūdī, Ibn al-Balkhī.** Medieval notices of Fārs and Jibāl fig districts (via *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Fig”).
- **Rashīd al-Dīn Faḍl Allāh.** *Āthār wa aḥyāʾ* — fig in Tabrīz; grafting claim on mulberry (report, do not certify).
- **Abūnaṣrī Heravī.** *Irshād al-zirāʿa* — four color classes of fig.
- **Ibn al-Bayṭār.** *al-Jāmiʿ* s.v. *tīn* — Dioscorides/Galen plus Arabic physicians.
- **Ibn Sīnā.** *al-Qānūn* book 2, fig monograph.
- **Bundahišn** 16.26 (Anklesaria) — Middle Persian *anjīr* among fruits edible outside and inside.
- **Houshang Alam.** “Fig,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, 15 Dec 1999 / updated 28 May 2013. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fig/

## East and South Asia (checked, not inflated)

- **Laufer, Berthold.** *Sino-Iranica* (1919), pp. 410–12 — Chinese *a-ži* and Iranian names; Babylonian *tittu*.
- **Flora of China** 5: 52. *Ficus carica* — *wúhuāguǒ*; “introduced to China during the Tang Dynasty (618–906 CE)”; cultivated especially in Xinjiang. http://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200006351
- **Morton, Julia.** *Fruits of Warm Climates* (1987) — England 1525–1548; Chinese gardens by 1550; New World 1560 (use as a compilation; prefer primary where we have it).
- **Wang Lianju et al.** “The Production and Research of Fig (*Ficus carica* L.) in China.” *Acta Hort.* 605 (ISHS). Commercial cultivation recent; introduction “about one thousand years.”
- **Kang, Choon-Ki.** “A Historical Study on Fruits in Korea.” *J. Korean Soc. Food Culture* 5.3 (1990) — figs around the 16th century.
- **Kang, Choon-Ki.** “A Historical Review on Economic Plants from Other Countries in Korea.” *Korean J. Plant Resources* 2.2 (1989) — *F. carica* around the 16th c. A.D.
- **Dymock, Warden, Hooper.** *Pharmacographia Indica* III — *anjīr* in Indian languages; older claim of Muslim introduction (contradicted, cautiously, by Iranica / Laufer).
- Do **not** treat Purandar (Maharashtra) commercial dates from trade blogs as primary; say “local industry histories place commercial Purandar figs in the early 20th century” when that is all we have.

## Archaeology and archaeobotany

- **Kislev, Mordechai E., Anat Hartmann, and Ofer Bar-Yosef.** “Early Domesticated Fig in the Jordan Valley.” *Science* 312, no. 5778 (2 June 2006): 1372–74. doi:10.1126/science.1125910. Gilgal I, 11,400–11,200 cal BP; nine carbonized figs + 313 drupelets; parthenocarpy argument.
- **Lev-Yadun, Simcha, et al.** Comment on Kislev et al., *Science* 314 (2006): 1683a — wild parthenocarpic gathering vs. cultivation.
- **Kislev, Hartmann, Bar-Yosef.** Response, *Science* 314 (2006): 1683b.
- **Denham, Tim.** “Early fig domestication, or gathering of wild parthenocarpic figs?” *Antiquity* (discussion of the same problem).
- **Zohary, Daniel, Maria Hopf, and Ehud Weiss.** *Domestication of Plants in the Old World.* 4th ed. Oxford, 2012 — founder fruit trees; wild distribution maps.
- **Zohary, Daniel, and P. Spiegel-Roy.** “Beginnings of Fruit Growing in the Old World.” *Science* 187 (1975): 319–27.
- **Fuller, Dorian Q., and Chris J. Stevens.** (and related) — fourth-millennium finds outside the wild zone, including southern Iran and Nile Delta (summarized in Fuller/Stevens-adjacent syntheses; see Miller, Naomi F., and the 2019 *Vegetation History and Archaeobotany* arboriculture paper).
- **Miller, Naomi F., et al. / Fuller circle.** “Between domestication and civilization…” *Vegetation History and Archaeobotany* (2019). doi:10.1007/s00334-019-00727-4 — *F. carica* vs *F. sycomorus*; Old Kingdom differentiation in art; Postgate 1987 for Mesopotamia.
- **Postgate, J. N.** “Notes on Fruit in the Cuneiform Sources.” *Bulletin on Sumerian Agriculture* 3 (1987): 115–44.
- **Powell, Marvin A.** Fruit-tree notes in cuneiform / “Tree List” discussion (Uruk III: date, vine, fig, apple as a lexical set).
- **Galil, J., and Gidi Ne’eman.** “Pollen Transfer and Pollination in the Common Fig.” *New Phytologist* 79 (1977): 163–71; and “Seed Set in the ‘Male Syconia’…” *New Phytologist* 81 (1978): 375–80.
- **Galil, J.** “An Ancient Technique for Ripening Sycomore Fruit in East-Mediterranean Countries.” (gashing / ethylene; tomb fruit with circular cuts). Purdue Hort 306 reprint.

## Egypt (keep the two figs apart)

- **Gad, Magdi, et al. / IJHTH 2020.** “The Common Figs ‘Ficus Carica’ in Ancient Egypt until the New Kingdom.” doi:10.21608/ijhth.2020.153621 — offering lists, Idu G 7102, Tehuti-Hetep, TT 40 Huy, TT 112 Menkheperraseneb.
- **Azzazy, Mohammed, and Azza Ezzat.** “The Sycamore in Ancient Egypt.” (Nut / Hathor / Isis; garden pools; Tell el-Dabʿa palynology.)
- **Brewer, Douglas J., Donald B. Redford, and Susan Redford.** *Domestic Plants and Animals: The Egyptian Origins.* Warminster: Aris & Phillips, 1994 — visual distinction of the two figs from the Old Kingdom.

## Genetics and wild relatives

- **Khadari, Bouchaib, et al.** Mediterranean fig genetic structure (mtDNA and later SSR work; 2003ff.).
- **Aradhya, M. K., et al.** “Genetic structure and differentiation in cultivated fig (*Ficus carica* L.).” *Genetica* 138 (2010): 681–94. PMC2860561.
- **Khadari, Bouchaib, Cinderella Grout, Sylvain Santoni, Finn Kjellberg, et al.** “Diffuse and regionally structured domestication of the common fig…” *Horticulture Research* / bioRxiv 2025. Three gene pools: Moroccan–Algerian, Northern Mediterranean, Levantine. *F. carica* s.s. distinct from *rupestris* / *F. colchica*.
- **Browicz, Kazimierz.** Moraceae, *Flora Iranica* 153 (1982) — *F. carica* subsp. *carica* / *rupestris*; *F. johannis*.
- **Berg, C. C., and J. T. Wiebes.** *African Fig Trees and Fig Wasps.* 1992 — *F. palmata* as a species complex.
- **PLOS ONE** (2022). “Re-evaluation of NCGR Davis *Ficus carica* and *palmata* SSR profiles.” doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0263715 — mislabels, Chicago Hardy ≈ Abruzzi, etc.

## Protected names and official specifications

- **Aydın İnciri (PDO).** EU original registration 17 February 2016; UK scheme 31 December 2020. Spec: dried Sarılop; Büyük / Küçük Menderes valleys. GOV.UK: https://www.gov.uk/protected-food-drink-names/aydin-inciri — PDF specification hosted by DEFRA.
- **Fico Bianco del Cilento (PDO).** Commission Regulation (EC) No 417/2006 — sun-dried Dottato, Salerno / Cilento.
- **Fichi di Cosenza (PDO).** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 596/2011; UK listing. Dried Dottato, Crati valley, 0–800 m.
- **Xera Syka Kymis / Ξερά Σύκα Κύμης (PDO).** Greek ministerial decision 313420 (1994 national); EU PDO. Variety Traganikó of Euboea; doubled “askada” drying.
- **Figue de Djebba (Tunisian GI).** First fruit GI in Tunisia, 2012; Bouhouli of Djebba, Béja. PAMPAT / UNIDO notes (use as institutional, not folklore).
- **Figue sèche Nabout de Taounate (Moroccan GI).** Arrêté 1680-15, 25 May 2015 (WIPO Lex MA167).

## Production statistics

- **FAOSTAT.** Crop: Figs. Cite year. 2022 world total c. 1.24 million tonnes (FAOSTAT 2024 as quoted in Ferrara et al. 2024). 2023 Türkiye 356,000 t (FAOSTAT 2025 as quoted in *Mediterranean Journal of Agriculture / Veterinary / Life* 2025 review). Rank order in recent years: Türkiye, Egypt, Morocco, Algeria, Iran, then Afghanistan / Syria / Spain / USA — **re-check the year before publishing a table.**
- **Ferrara, Giuseppe, et al.** “The fig (*Ficus carica* L.): varietal evolution from Asia to Puglia…” *CABI Agriculture and Bioscience* (2024). doi:10.1186/s43170-024-00262-x
- **Aksoy, Uygun.** Turkish fig industry reviews (2017, 2021) — dried-export share; do not lift brochure percentages without FAOSTAT.

## Iran, heritage orchards

- **FAO / GIAHS file.** “Estahban Rainfed Fig Orchards Heritage System.” Open-knowledge PDF. Rainfed slope orchards of Fars; the file’s deep-time origin remarks are weaker than its local agronomy — use the latter.
- **Farshī, M.-H.** “Anjīr-e Īrān.” *Īrān-e ābād* (1960) — via Iranica.

## England and the northern garden

- **Lambeth Palace / Archbishop’s office (2014).** White Marseilles associated with Cardinal Reginald Pole, planted (Palace tradition) 1556; cutting to Pope Francis 24 June 2014. Loudon (1836) said 1548 and found the old trees gone, young cuttings remaining. Do not collapse 1525 / 1548 / 1556 into one date.
- **Garden History Blog** (2017). “Who gives a Fig…especially in Sussex?” — Matthew Paris 1257 mention; Pole chronology.
- **Jefferson, Thomas.** *Garden Book* / Monticello documentation — Marseilles figs; French cuttings 1789. Use Monticello’s published notes, not souvenir copy.
