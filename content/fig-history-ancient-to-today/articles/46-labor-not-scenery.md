# Hands on the Tray

Caprification is hanging fruit in a tree at the right week. Drying is turning fruit before it molds. Packing is grading with the eyes until the eyes are tired. None of this is a landscape photograph.

Slow Food’s Nabout note says the drying traditions of Taounate are the work of women. Italian and Aegean yards, in every honest memoir, are family yards in August. California’s industry used hired crews, including, in the twentieth century, the same migrant labor the rest of the valley used. Condit’s circulars assume a grower and “help.” They do not linger on names.

This article will not invent a wage series. It will insist that every GI spec’s “traditional method” is a labor spec. Cilento’s stuffing clause is kitchen labor written into Brussels English. Kymi’s doubling is a pair of hands. Aydın’s slope figure is a body on a hill.

Villa Rome used enslaved labor. That belongs in article 09 and should not be laundered out of the still lifes in article 12. A loaf and two figs on a sill do not show who baked or picked.
