# Seven Hundred Names, One Monograph

Ira J. Condit spent decades as the University of California’s fig man. *The Fig* (Chronica Botanica, 1947) is the book. “Fig Varieties: A Monograph” (*Hilgardia* 23, 1955, pp. 323–538) is the list — more than 700 cultivars, some from his own eyes, some from Eisen’s unfinished papers, some from a European book he trusted and some he did not. A 2024 *HortScience* project pulled agriculturally useful attributes from that monograph into a table of 717 names. The monograph is now a database. It was already a database, written in prose.

He inherited Eisen’s four types and the Italian crop names. He tried keys for caprifigs and admitted that keys fail when a clone changes size in a new valley. He fixed, as far as a campus can fix, the commercial California five: Mission, Adriatic, Calimyrna, Kadota, Brown Turkey — and then spent pages saying Brown Turkey is a pile.

## How to read him now

As a primary source for mid-century identity, not as a DNA court. The PLOS ONE re-evaluation of NCGR profiles is what happens when the monograph’s world meets a sequencer: some pedigrees hold, some “Kadota” is not Excel’s mother, some Adriatic is Milco’s. That does not retire Condit. It puts a clerk beside him.

Read him also as a writer. He is clearer than his period’s bulletins. He is willing to say he has not seen a fruit. That tone is this series’ tone.
