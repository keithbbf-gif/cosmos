# The January Fig

Ferrara et al. (2024) give a fresh-import map that is mostly Europe — Germany, France, Austria, Switzerland, Britain — then the United States, Russia, the UAE, and a world fresh market they quote in the hundreds of millions of dollars (Global Market Insights / related compilations in the same neighborhood). Dried remains the bigger tonnage trade; Turkey still owns much of it. The new historical fact is not the dried crate. It is the winter clamshell.

A January fig in a northern supermarket is a common type, picked early or exactly on time, flown or trucked, a variety chosen for skin and a harvest window. Brazil, Peru, Mexico, a Mediterranean greenhouse, a Californian late hold. It will never taste like a tree-ripe Mission in August on a Sacramento night. It exists because someone decided the fruit should be a year-round grocery, not a month.

## Cato’s stunt, without the Senate

Cato’s African fig (article 11) was a two-day crossing as a threat. The January fig is a two-day crossing as a habit. Same physics — a fruit that can still look like itself after a journey — different morals. Pliny asked whether we should admire the speed of the passage. A logistics manager answers with a routing.

Smyrna types are almost never this tray. They need a wasp, they bruise into a different story, they belong to the dried ledger. The Common box (article 39) owns the air.

## What the tray erases

Season. Place. The yard. A child who only knows the January fig does not know August (article 52). A history series that only showed the January fig would be a produce newsletter. This article exists so the newsletter has a date.

Counterseason is a geography (article 37). It is also a labor in someone else’s summer. The hands are still on a tray, even if the tray is a packing line.
