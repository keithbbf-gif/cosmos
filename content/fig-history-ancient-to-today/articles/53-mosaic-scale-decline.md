# The Orchard’s Other Insects

The wasp is not the only small animal in this story. Fig mosaic — a virus complex, ugly leaves, a reason nurseries went looking for clean wood — sat in California collections for decades and then sat in collector mail (article 58). Endosepsis rode *Blastophaga*; Caldis found the fungus in caprifigs and showed the insect as the ride; Hansen, another UC plant pathologist, worked controls in the 1940s. Scale and dried-fruit beetles ate the pack. Birds ate the fresh fruit. A history that stops at 1900’s first seeded crop has not walked an August orchard.

Condit’s circulars treat pests in period language, as implement problems. They are also why a yard closes.

## Area down, yield up

Ferrara et al. (2024), using FAOSTAT, note world fig *area* falling from roughly 622,000 hectares in 1960 to about 300,000 in 2022, while tonnes fell less brutally because yields rose — from about 2.7 to 4.2 tonnes per hectare in their reading of the same tables. That is intensification and abandonment in one sentence. Italy’s national harvest, in their ISTAT note, is a shadow of a former countryside. California’s bearing acres are not the 1920s. Aydın still looks like a republic of trays. Many other districts look like a memory.

Do not blame a single villain. Urban land, cheap imports, labor, mosaic, taste, water. The tree is easy to plant and easy to neglect. Article 02’s stick cuts both ways.

## The collector bill

Clean wood is a late 20th-century obsession because dirty wood became a global post. A forum cutting can move mosaic faster than Eisen moved a bulletin. The PLOS ONE re-evaluation of NCGR labels is a naming hangover; mosaic is a biological one. A series that celebrates the mailbag (article 58) has to pay this invoice in the same breath.
