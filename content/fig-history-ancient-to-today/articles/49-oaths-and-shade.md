# Oaths and Shade, Not a Universal Myth

Qurʾān 95:1 — *wa-l-tīn wa-l-zaytūn* — is an oath by the fig and the olive. Tafsīr traditions argue about whether the words are the fruits, the trees, or places. This pack will not pick a tafsīr. It will say the Book thought the pair worth swearing by, which is already a cultural fact, and that Hadith collections (Alam’s Shiʿi set, and others) then loaded the fruit with virtues. Report as tradition.

Micah’s vine and fig (article 05) is politics. John 1:48 — Nathaniel under the fig tree — is recognition. The barren fig in the Synoptics is a judgment image that has been preached into the ground; the species is *carica* in the usual telling, and the point is unfruitfulness, not a cultivar. Luke 19:4 is a sycomore-fig, a different tree. Keep the split.

Roman Priapus and fertility gardens are a third register (do not thumbnail). Andalusi and Persian verse will praise fruit the way they praise wine; do not scrape a couplet you cannot attribute.

The rule is the style guide’s rule: if the text is not in the bibliography, it is not a myth we “found.”
