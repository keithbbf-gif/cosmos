# Painted Rooms, Painted Lists

Article 12 kept the ash. This one keeps the job of pictures.

Egyptian offering walls (article 03) are production and eternity. Campanian still lifes are portion and house pride. A serpent in a painted fig tree is narrative, not pomology. Tissot’s biblical shade (article 05) is 19th-century piety using a tree the painter could see in Palestine and in his head.

Then something changes in the late Medici world. Bartolomeo Bimbi, 1696, Poggio a Caiano: figs as a labeled set, a painted cultivar list for a court that already collected citrus as politics. That painting is the ancestor of Condit’s monograph and of every fig-forum photo dump. It is also, unlike a forum photo, public domain.

Ehret (1771) and Holtzbecher’s Gottorfer sheet are Enlightenment and court science: the plant as a specimen. Le Moyne is a field artist’s watercolour. Köhler’s medicinal plate puts the tree next to a dose. None of these is a photograph of a Roman breakfast. Caption them as plates.

The Met’s open-access still lifes (MET-001) are a later import reserve: use CC0 records only, fruit-in-a-painting as European appetite, not as “historic orchard photography.”
