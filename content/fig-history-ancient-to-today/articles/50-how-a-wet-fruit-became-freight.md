# How a Wet Fruit Became Freight

A fresh fig is a clock. A dried fig is a ledger. The techniques are few and they keep being renamed. The long roads (article 18) are this article wearing a camel. The box (article 56) is this article wearing a stencil. The yard (article 52) is this article wearing a back.

## The short list of methods

**Sun.** Fruit ripens to a sugar that can stand on a cane tray. Aydın, Estahban, Cilento, the Maghreb. Weather is a partner and a thief. A rain at tray-time is a year that becomes something else.

**Doubling.** Kymi’s askada: two faces, one cake, written into a PDO. Other yards have pressed and flattened without a stamp.

**Pulling / Lerida / layer / garland / protoben.** Packing-house English and Spanish for how the dead fruit is arranged in a box. The names traveled farther than the towns. A grocer in 1955 could say Lerida without knowing a Spanish city.

**Sulfur.** A 19th–20th-century house to bleach and to kill what would eat the pale pack. Condit and Eisen treat it as ordinary industry. A later organic yard treats it as a problem. Both are dated positions, not eternities.

**Oven / tunnel.** Cilento’s spec allows adjuvant heat and a salt dip. Factory dryers are the same idea with a power bill. Moisture caps in the Cosenza spec (24 percent, or 28 if conditioned) are a lawyer’s hygrometer.

## The old object

Casa dei Cervi’s painted dried figs (WC-006) show that the keeping-fruit was already a still-life object in 79. An Old Assyrian “brick of figs” (article 06) is older than that painting. The factory is new. The wish — sugar that will travel — is not.

Eisen’s 1901 bulletin is, among other things, a curing book written at the moment California thought it could join the wish. The circulars that follow are refinements. They are not a new physics.
