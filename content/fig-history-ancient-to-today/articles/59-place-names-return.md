# The Place Comes Back: PDOs, GIs, and the End of ‘Smyrna’

For a century the profitable name was a port or a California stitch. Smyrna on the crate, Calimyrna on the stencil, “Turkish figs” on a bin. Then the legal weather changed. A village could hire a lawyer.

Greece wrote Xera Syka Kymis — Traganikó, doubled, a KY code. Italy wrote Fico Bianco del Cilento (Commission Regulation 417/2006) and Fichi di Cosenza (Implementing Regulation 596/2011), both Dottato, both dried, two valleys that refused to be one south. Türkiye wrote Aydın İnciri, EU 17 February 2016, dried Sarılop, the clone behind the port name. Tunisia wrote Djebba in 2012, first fruit GI in the country, Bouhouli of a mountain village. Morocco wrote Figue sèche Nabout de Taounate, arrêté 1680-15, 25 May 2015.

The documents are slope, variety, moisture, a control code. They are also a political wish: that a village not be laundered into a generic “Mediterranean fig.”

## What the wish can and cannot do

It can move a price. PAMPAT’s institutional note on Djebba claimed supermarket and export sales and a better return when the mark stuck. Treat that as a development-agency report. It can punish a substitution, if anyone is watching. It can force a spec to admit labor: Cilento’s stuffing clause is kitchen work in legal English.

It cannot make a buyer care. It cannot put a wasp in a common-type orchard or take one out of a Sarılop orchard. It cannot revive a closed yard (article 53). California did not get a Calimyrna PDO. The word remains a trade name for a Turkish clone in irrigation dirt. That is the old world, still.

Pliny’s place-names (article 10) were elite appetite: Chalcis, Chios, Lydia, Tarentum. These names are rural development and a fight with the port. The appetite is different. The instinct — to make a fruit wear a town — is the same.
