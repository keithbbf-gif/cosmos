# Dioscorides on the Table

Dioscorides’ *De Materia Medica* puts the fig among foods that are also simples: softening, nourishing, useful on a swelling, useful in a cough as a kitchen might be useful. Galen hangs later commentary on that trunk. This article will not translate a full monograph into a supplement label. It will say what the genre is. A simple is a single-substance remedy. A fig cake on Hezekiah’s boil (2 Kings 20:7) is the same genre in a different library: kitchen plus hope.

Roman moralists liked the fig as a modest food — the Herculaneum loaf-and-figs still life is the picture of that sentence. Physicians liked it because people already ate it. The overlap is the point. A drug nobody eats is a different market.

## What we will not claim

We will not say figs cure cancer, diabetes, or a modern named disease because a first-century sentence mentioned softening. We will not print milligrams. We will not collapse humoral hot/cold language into a calorie table (that fight belongs in the nutrition-history piece).

## Food already in the mouth

A simple that is also lunch is a simple the physician cannot ignore. Dioscorides writes that situation. Hezekiah’s cake is the same situation in another library. A supplement brand that cites either for a modern disease has left both libraries.


## A simple that is already lunch

Dioscorides writes fig as food and as simple because the patient is already eating it. Softening, nourishing, a use on a swelling, a use in a cough — kitchen verbs. Galen hangs commentary. Celsus and Pliny’s wider medical books mention figs among foods. This is not a lost oncology.

2 Kings 20:7 is the same genre in Hebrew: a cake applied. Later physicians will have opinions. The verse has a cake. The Wellcome plate (V0044761) is a later pharmacy seeing of stem and halved fruit, CC BY 4.0, useful as a picture of the simple, useless as a first-century autograph.

No milligrams. No modern disease names. The nutrition article holds the other desk. This one holds the trunk the Arabic monographs will graft.
