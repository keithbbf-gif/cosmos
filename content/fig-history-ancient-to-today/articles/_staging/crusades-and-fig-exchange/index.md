# Cargo, Not a Relic

The crusader states sat on a coast that already dried figs. They did not need to invent a crop. They needed to tax, to eat, and to let Italian merchants do what Italian merchants were going to do anyway. David Jacoby’s work on Venetian Acre — chronicles, public documents, private charters, and the late-thirteenth-century merchant manuals — is the ground. Dried fruit is a line in that ground, not a holy object.

The *Racione de Alexandria* (Venice, Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana, It. XI.87), a late-thirteenth-century manual Jacoby flagged in 1986 and Beatrice Saletti has since described and edited, is a book of routes, measures, and duties. Acre and Alexandria dominate its attention. It is not a fig monograph. It is proof that a clerk in that world expected to need numbers for a Levantine run. Dates from Alexandria into Romania show up in an unpublished Acre manual of about 1270 (Jacoby’s note, as cited in later Constantinopolitan food-trade work). Figs travel in the same class of cargo: dense, sweet, keepable.

## What “exchange” can mean

Cuttings move with people. A Frankish household in the kingdom of Jerusalem could plant a Levantine stick. A returning ship could carry a dried sack and, sometimes, wood. That is how plants move in every war. It is not a “Crusade variety” you can hang a tag on. Do not invent a Templar fig. Do not credit Richard I with a cultivar. Do not read a modern Israeli or Syrian market name back onto a twelfth-century *casale*.

Negroponte, in later thirteenth–fifteenth-century regional syntheses, re-exports dried figs from Chios. Ligurian dried figs sit in Pera in 1290, bound toward Caffa. The Black Sea is already in the dried-fruit map before anyone calls it a spice route. The crusade is one political weather over a sea that had been carrying trays for centuries.

Pegolotti’s *Pratica della mercatura* (c. 1340) is the next generation’s clerk. Majorca and Catalonia appear in the western dried-fig notes that later Constantinopolitan studies cite. The Frankish states are mostly gone by then. The fruit is not.


## Clerks, not relics

Acre’s Venetian quarter is a legal and commercial fact. Jacoby’s unpublished-then-published charter trawl is how you add knowledge: one more document, one more duty. The *Racione* counts ports, not miracles. A crusader who planted a stick planted a stick. Call it exchange if you must. Call it a relic and you have left the manuals.


## Tax, eat, let the Italians count

Crusader states sat on a drying coast. Jacoby’s Acre — charters, quarters, manuals — is the ground. The *Racione* (Marciana It. XI.87) counts ports and duties. An Acre manual c. 1270 notes Alexandrian dates into Romania. Figs ride the same cargo class. Cuttings move with people in every war. No Templar cultivar. No Richard I clone. Pegolotti is the next clerk after the Frankish flags fade.
