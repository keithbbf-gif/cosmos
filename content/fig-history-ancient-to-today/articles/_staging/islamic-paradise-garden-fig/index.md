# By the Fig and the Olive

Qurʾān 95:1 — *wa-l-tīn wa-l-zaytūn*, by the fig and the olive — is an oath, not a monograph. Do not over-interpret. The pairing is the fact: two Mediterranean trees a Hijazi audience already understood as blessings you can eat. Later tafsīr will argue whether *al-tīn* is a place, a fruit, or both. This series takes the fruit as sufficient and leaves the place-names to the commentators.

Islamic garden culture — courtyard, canal, shade, the fourfold *chahār-bāgh* in Persianate spaces — uses the fig as a dooryard and orchard tree, not as the geometric star. Dates, citrus, and plane do more monumental work. The fig does household work: a fruit for the fast’s end, a dried sweet for travel, a tree that asks for water and returns shade.

## Agronomists, not angels

Ibn Waḥshiyya’s *Nabataean Agriculture* (attribution traditional) sits behind later writers as a quarry. Ibn Baṣṣāl in eleventh-century Toledo–Seville, Ibn Ḥajjāj’s *al-Muqniʿ* (1073), Abū l-Khayr al-Ishbīlī — Andalusi names that the Aljarafe article will take up. Ibn al-ʿAwwām’s *Kitāb al-Filāḥa* is the great surviving Seville book, a third of it Byzantine, a third Near Eastern, a third local. He does not write paradise. He writes when to plant the cutting.

## Courtyard, not tile star

A *chahār-bāgh* can hold a fig without being about a fig. Dates and citrus take the monumental jobs. The fig takes the dooryard and the traveler’s bag. Qurʾān 95:1 remains an oath. Tafsīr arguments about whether *al-tīn* is also a place can be footnoted; they do not change the fruit.

Ibn al-ʿAwwām’s cutting calendar is the anti-romance. Paradise metaphors work because someone already irrigated a basin.

## An oath is not a tile pattern

Qurʾān 95:1 needs a fruit an audience could eat. Later commentators may
need a place. This series needs the fruit. *Chahār-bāgh* geometry hires
water and axis, not a fig as star. Dooryard work — shade, basin,
traveler’s bag — is the hire. Agronomists write cuttings. Ibn Sīnā
writes lunch. Do not merge the desks into a paradise proof.


## Fast’s end is household work

A dried fig at sunset is not a tile star and not a proof of paradise.
It is why an oath about fruit already worked. Dates and canals take the
monument. Agronomists take the basin. Tafsīr may take a place-name.
This page takes the dooryard.


## Rivers in the text; a basin in the court

Paradise is gardens with rivers. A fig asks for a basin and returns
shade. That is enough metaphor. Do not hire the tree as a geometric
star. Dates and axes already have the job.
