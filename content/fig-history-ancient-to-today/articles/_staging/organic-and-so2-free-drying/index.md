# Sun, Sulfur, and a Label

Sulfur was a trade fact before it was a villain. Eisen and Condit describe it as how a pale dried fig meets a market that learned paleness from a Smyrna box. Organic certification and SO2-free branding are a later revolt: keep the sun, drop the cylinder, accept a darker or more variable skin, charge for the honesty.

This is not a return to a pure past. The past had mold, theft, and a buyer who still wanted the pale box. SO2-free is a technical choice (process, hygiene, faster handling, sometimes a different clone). Organic is a paper choice (inputs, inspection). They overlap. They are not synonyms.

## What a label cannot do

It cannot make a Common-type dried fig taste like a seeded Sarılop. It cannot make rainfed Estahban into a California tunnel. It cannot make a supermarket honor a PDO. It can make a packer keep a log.

Do not print a health claim about sulfur. Do not print a purity sermon. Print the industrial history: color was taught, then untaught, for a price.


## Two papers, one tray

Organic is an input log. SO2-free is a process choice. Sun is weather. A label can stack them. A history should unstack them. Pale was taught. Dark can be sold again if the price holds.


## What paleness cost

Eisen and Condit’s sulfur chapters assume a buyer who learned “Smyrna” as a pale, even skin. Burning sulfur in a closed room is how a valley that is not Aydın sat for that exam. Mold and insects were the other exam. Chemistry was a tool, not a sin, in those books.

Organic certification arrives as a paper trail: what went on the tree, what went in the house, who inspected. SO2-free branding arrives as a process claim: we did not use that cylinder. A packer can be one and not the other. A supermarket can print both on a bag that still came through a tunnel. Unstack the words.

Sun-only drying is weather-honest and labor-heavy. It is not Eden. Women still sorted. Dew still ruined a night. The label’s gift is a log. The label’s lie, when it happens, is “traditional” as if tradition had no grades and no theft. Sell the log. Do not sell Eden.
