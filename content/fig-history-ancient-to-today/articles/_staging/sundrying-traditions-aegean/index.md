# How a Wet Fruit Became Freight

A fig on the tree is mostly water and a short clock. A fig on a reed tray in a dry wind is a store. Every country in this series that ships has some version of the same afternoon: pick at the right softness, split or not, lay out, turn, guard from dew and from theft, sweat in a box so the skins even, grade. Eisen’s 1901 curing chapters are still the English prose of that afternoon. He was teaching California to copy a quay. The quay had copied a yard.

Aegean sundrying is not a folk dance. It is a climate machine. The Menderes valleys and the Euboea face have a summer that will take water out of a fruit without a coal fire. That is why Sarılop and Traganikó became legal names and why a wetter coast becomes a jam coast.

## Steps that look simple

Harvest. Sort. Lay. Turn. Stack. Box. The pack diagram numbers them because writers skip them. Sulfur, when it appears, is a later industrial habit (tunnel-dryer article). Askada doubling is a Kymi signature, not a universal. Lerida’s pull and other western Mediterranean tricks belong in the same family and not in a single recipe.

## Weather as a co-author

A yard that cannot see a dew coming is a yard that will sell mold. Reed and plastic, old cloth, a pull-under roof — the tools change, the fear does not. Eisen’s sweating step is chemistry and time: fruit in a box evening out so a grade can be honest. Skip it and the top layer lies.

Women’s seasonal labor is the unprinted majority in every drying district this pack names. A photograph without consent does not fix that. A sentence that says “the packer” as if it were a machine does not either. The diagram’s human gate is the minimum.

## A climate machine with hands

Menderes summers and Euboea faces take water out of a fruit without coal. That is why Sarılop and Traganikó became legal names and why a wetter coast becomes jam country. Harvest, sort, lay, turn, stack, box: the diagram numbers them because writers skip them. Sulfur is a later branch. Askada doubling is a Kymi signature. Lerida’s pull is a western cousin. One recipe card for the whole sea is a lie.

Dew is the nightly enemy. Theft is the human one. Eisen’s sweat is the evening-out that makes a grade possible. ORCH-05 waits for consented hands on trays. Until then the schematic is more honest than a stock photo of three figs on linen.

## What the sun actually does to a fig

A fresh fig is mostly water held in a thin skin. Left on a tray, that water
has to leave through the ostiole, through micro-cracks, and through the skin
itself. The first day is evaporation. The second day is sugar concentration:
the pulp darkens, the skin wrinkles, the fruit stops looking like a fruit and
starts looking like a store. A third day in a dry wind finishes the job. A
third day in a humid wind starts a ferment. That is why every serious
sundrying culture — Aydın, Estahban, Kalamata, the California interior —
talks about *wind* as much as sun.

The chemistry is not mysterious. Invert sugars rise as water falls.
Polyphenol oxidase browns the cut or cracked surface. A sulfur burn (the
California and some Aegean twentieth-century habit) bleaches that brown and
knocks back insects. Organic packs skip the burn and accept the darker face,
or they use a tunnel with filtered air. Neither method is “ancient.” Both are
answers to the same water problem the *Geoponika* already knew.

## Industry, not cottage

Call sundrying an industry when the fruit leaves the family and enters a
grade. Aydın’s *sardes* / *natural* / *lerida* grades, California’s “choice”
and “fancy,” Estahban’s export sorts — those are industrial words. They
imply a floor, a sorter, a scale, and a buyer who will reject a moldy tray.
The ancient industry is harder to see because the grade names did not survive
on ostraca the way wine amphora stamps did. What survived is the volume.
Egyptian offering lists, Roman *ficedula* jokes, Venetian *fichi* cargoes,
Ottoman *incir* customs — none of those are a bowl on a windowsill.

The labor is gendered in a way the manuals underplay. Sorting and stringing
are often women’s work in the Aegean and Maghreb ethnographies; picking can
be mixed; packing houses in twentieth-century California hired seasonal crews
whose names did not make the variety bulletins. A history that only cites
Condit will miss the hands. Eisen’s sweating step — fruit boxed so moisture
evens — is the packing-house version of a rack pulled under a roof when dew
threatens. Skip it and the top layer lies.

## What did not change

A fig still dries from the inside out if you leave the skin intact. It still
case-hardens if you rush the heat. It still takes a second sweat in a bin if
you want a uniform pack. Tunnel dryers and solar tents changed the weather
risk. They did not change the fruit. That is why this essay sits next to the
Aydın and Estahban pieces instead of replacing them: the industry is local,
the physics is shared.

Kymi’s *askada* pairing is handwriting on the same physics: doubled fruit
that sells as a unit. Spanish *pan de higo* is a press. Some hill towns
string. Universalizing the string is laziness. Weather remains a co-author.
A yard that cannot see a dew coming is a yard that will sell mold.

## Dew is a co-author

A fig case-hardens if you rush the heat: a tough skin, a wet center, a
grade that lies. It ferments if the third day is humid. It finishes if the
wind is dry. That is why Aydın, Estahban, Kalamata, and the California
interior talk about wind as much as sun. Eisen’s sweat — fruit boxed so
moisture evens — is the packing-house version of a rack pulled under a roof
when dew threatens. Skip it and the top layer is a liar.

Grades make an industry: *sardes*, *natural*, *lerida*, California “fancy,”
Estahban export sorts. A scale and a rejected tray are the difference
between a windowsill and a custom-house. Sorting and stringing are often
women’s work in the ethnographies; California floors hired seasonal crews
the variety bulletins did not name. Kymi’s *askada* is handwriting on the
same physics. Spanish *pan de higo* is a press. Some towns string.
Universalizing the string is laziness. Tunnels changed weather risk. They
did not change the fruit. Sulfur bleached a brown the grocery had been
taught to refuse. Organic packs untaught the exam for a price. The hands
remained.
