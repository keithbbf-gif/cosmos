# *Anjīr* Among Native Figs

Pipal, banyan, cluster fig — South Asia’s *Ficus* flora is a civilization of its own. *Ficus carica* is not that civilization. It is a guest with a Persian-Arabic name, *anjīr / anjeer*, in the drug and fruit books. Dymock, Warden, and Hooper’s *Pharmacographia Indica* treat it as such. An older claim of “Muslim introduction” sits in that Victorian desk; Iranica and Laufer complicate the word-history. Do not pick a century to please a narrative. Say: the common fig is not native in the way *F. religiosa* is native, and commercial *anjeer* districts are late as industries.

Purandar (Maharashtra) and other Deccan stories appear in trade writing as early-twentieth-century commercializations. This pack will not treat a blog date as a gazetteer. It will say local industry histories place commercial Purandar figs in the early twentieth century when that is all we have.

## A continent of other figs

*F. religiosa* and *F. benghalensis* do not become *carica* because a grocer said anjeer. Word-history (Laufer, Iranica, Dymock) is the honest trail. Purandar commercialization stays marked as local industry history. Import boxes in a sweet shop are allowed and should be admitted.


## Anjeer is a word with luggage

Laufer’s *Sino-Iranica* pages on fig names are a trail of sounds: Babylonian *tittu*, Iranian *anjīr*, forms that wander east. Alam’s Iranica article is the modern crosswalk. Dymock, Warden, and Hooper put *anjīr* in the Indian drug-and-fruit lexicon and repeat an older “Muslim introduction” line. Print the line as theirs. Print Iranica’s complications next to it. Do not pick a century to please a communal story.

Pipal and banyan are not this crop. A writer who uses a Bodhi-tree photograph to illustrate *anjeer* has left the species. Cluster fig (*F. racemosa*) is another native economy. *Carica* is the guest that sweet shops also import already dried from the Aegean. Admit the import. A Mumbai box can be Turkish fruit in a Marathi sentence.

## Cluster fig is another economy

*F. racemosa* has its own markets and medicines. It does not become
*anjeer* because a grocer is in a hurry. Pipal and banyan keep their
gods. Laufer and Alam keep the word-trail. Dymock keeps a Victorian
line you quote. Purandar keeps a hedge. Import jars keep the Aegean
in a Marathi sentence. Admit every desk. Crown none.


## Anjeer is luggage

Persian-Arabic word, Victorian drug book, Iranica crosswalk, Deccan
industry hedge, Aegean jar in a sweet shop — that is the guest’s
suitcase. Pipal keeps the gods. Do not crown the Deccan. Do not pick
a century for a communal story. Admit the import.


## Sweet shops import; forests do not

A Mumbai jar can be Turkish fruit. A pipal grove cannot. Say both.
Purandar stays hedged. Laufer stays a sound-trail. Dymock stays
quoted, not crowned. Guest crop. Other *Ficus* continent.
