# Late Arrivals

Kang Choon-Ki’s 1989 paper on introduced economic plants (*Korean Journal of Plant Resources* 2.2) and his 1990 paper on fruits in Korea (*Journal of the Korean Society of Food Culture* 5.3) put *Ficus carica* around the sixteenth century. “Around” is a scholar’s word. It is better than a 1520 ship this pack cannot source. The tree is late on the long arc and early enough to matter. It is not a Jōmon carbonized *carica* and not a Silla temple origin.

Japanese *ichijiku* is the common fig as a guest. Edo-period horticultural weather and later honzōgaku consensus treat it as introduced, not as a native tree. This pack will not invent the first priest, the first Nara courtyard, or a landing-party diary. If a dated honzōgaku entry is added later, it should come with an edition. Until then: late, named, cultivated, not native.

It does not pull Kang’s Korean “around” backward into the Heian, and it does not make *ichijiku* a Jōmon snack.

The China essay next door holds the Tang / 1550 / “thousand years” seam. This one holds two later desks that refused to become legends. Do not collapse the three countries into one “East Asian fig.” Flora’s Xinjiang note is climate. Kang’s sixteenth century is a Korean economic-plant history. Edo *ichijiku* is a Japanese guest name. Three sentences. Three hedges.

## Luxury January, imported August

Modern Tokyo and Seoul groceries sell fresh figs as a short seasonal luxury and dried figs as import. That is allowed. It is the present tense of a late crop. It is not an ancient industry hiding under a shrine. A magazine that needs a temple story to justify a grocery display has left Kang and the Edo consensus for costume.

## Two papers, one hedge

Kang’s 1989 piece is a historical review of economic plants from other countries in Korea. Kang’s 1990 piece is a historical study on fruits. Both put *F. carica* around the sixteenth century. That double mention is why this pack trusts the hedge. It is still a hedge. It is not a landing at Busan in a dated log. A later Korean agricultural history with an edition and a first orchard can thicken the desk. Until then, “around” is the honest word.

Honzōgaku is the Japanese class of text that would have to supply a first dated *ichijiku* entry. This pack has not printed that entry because it has not sat with an edition. Edo-consensus guest is the weather, not a footnote. Inventing a priest to fill the gap is the temple origin this dek banned.

Modern luxury fresh figs in January windows and imported dry figs in August bins are the present tense. They do not owe a shrine a backstory. They do not owe Flora’s Tang a Korean sequel. The China seam stays next door. The syconium stays a room. WC-013 stays reserved until a live import re-checks the Naturalis tag.

## Guest gardens, not a drying belt

Edo-consensus *ichijiku* is a dooryard and a horticultural curiosity before it is a packing house. This pack will not invent a Tokugawa drying yard that taught Europe a taste. Japan and Korea sit north of the painted horseshoe on the teaching map. Winters are the point. A wall, a sheltered court, a short fresh season — the northern-garden essay’s theft, in another language. Fresh luxury in a Tokyo January window is that theft succeeding for a month. Imported Aegean dry in an August bin is the honest freight the home trees will not make.

Korea’s sixteenth-century “around,” in Kang, sits in the same century as Morton’s English compilation range and Lambeth’s disputed Palace dates. That is coincidence of guest years, not a shared fleet. Do not write a Lisbon-to-Busan fig galleon. Do not write a Jesuit first orchard unless an edition is in the bibliography. Late arrivals can share a century and still be separate desks.

Jōmon archaeology has many plant stories. *Ficus carica* is not one this pack will add. A carbonized native *Ficus* would be another species and another paper. Silla or Nara temple origins remain banned because they are how a grocery display borrows antiquity. The China seam — Tang, 1550, a thousand years — stays next door as a different guest’s argument. This page’s argument is late, named, cultivated, not native.

A honzōgaku line with an edition can thicken the Japanese desk later. Until then Kang’s Korean “around” and Edo-consensus *ichijiku* are enough: late, named, cultivated, not native. Luxury fresh and imported dry are the present tense, not a shrine industry.

## Two vernaculars, one late guest

Chinese *wúhuāguǒ* describes the room — flowerless fruit. Japanese *ichijiku* names the guest. They are not one East Asian fig story. Kang’s 1989 economic-plant paper and 1990 fruit paper both put Korean *carica* around the sixteenth century. That double mention is why this pack trusts the hedge. It is still a hedge. It is not a Busan log and not a Jesuit first orchard.
