# Figs at the Table, Not on the Altar

A Greek dinner that has come down to us in literature is mostly wine, bread, fish, and argument. Figs appear at the edges: dried, cheap, sweet, a poor man’s dessert and a rich man’s extra course, a fruit you can throw, a fruit you can insult someone with. That is already a lot. It is not a liturgy.

The temptation, in a magazine series, is to dress every classical fruit in a god. Dionysus gets the vine honestly. Athena gets the olive honestly. The fig gets household use and the occasional comic phallus, which is a different kind of honesty and not a temple program. This article stays at the table.

## Fresh, dried, and out of season

Fresh figs in the Aegean are a short, greedy season. Dried figs are the winter fact. That split organizes Greek mention the way it organizes every later Mediterranean kitchen. A symposium that wants to show off can serve the fresh fruit when it exists. A household that wants to last until spring serves the dried. The *Geoponica*, centuries later, will still be fussing about preservation. The fuss is older than the compilation.

Athenaeus and the antiquarian diners who fill the *Deipnosophists* quote earlier comic and lyric scraps about fruit. Use those scraps as proof that figs were eaten and joked about. Do not build a menu for 420 BCE out of a second-century CE quotation machine. Theophrastus remains the cleaner witness for the tree; the diner-collectors are witnesses for talk.

## What Solon does not prove

Plutarch, *Solon* 24.1, says Solon allowed olive-oil export and — Plutarch reports the story with his own caution — that fig export was forbidden and that the informer was called a sycophant. The *Oxford English Dictionary* and classical philology have long marked the fig-informer etymology as unsubstantiated. Attica grew figs. Athens cared about food and about talking. Those facts do not require a lost inscription about fig smuggling. The Attica economy article takes the oil law seriously and leaves the pun as a pun.

Dried figs moved. They moved inside the Aegean long before they moved as a named crate through a medieval quay. Chios and other islands will later be exporters of types as well as of freight (Pliny still knows a Chian fig). That is dining becoming trade. The banquet is where the taste is trained.

## Leave the altar alone

There are fig trees in sanctuaries because there are fig trees in Greece. A sacred grove that includes a fig is not a fig cult. Priapus is a Roman garden statue with a fig in the joke more than once; he is not a Greek dinner guest you need on this page. If a vase shows fruit, say which vase and what the catalogue calls the fruit. This pack does not embed an uncleared kylix. The pending raster `greek-vases.fig-banquet` stays pending until a rights check lands.

The Greek contribution to the long arc is looking (Theophrastus) and eating (everyone). Rome will name varieties as if they were conquests. The Aegean will keep drying. The table is the hinge.

bibliography can name.

Theophrastus remains the looking. He writes difference, propagation, the
swell. A banquet article that only quotes jokes has left the tree. A banquet

the fence the folklore essay built.
