# Cilento and Cosenza Dry the Same Clone

Commission Regulation (EC) No 417/2006 registers Fico Bianco del Cilento: sun-dried Dottato in the Cilento / Salerno country. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 596/2011 registers Fichi di Cosenza: dried Dottato in the Crati valley, 0–800 m. Two PDOs, one clone name, two legal maps. That is the Italian opposite of Aydın’s single-valley fence: the wood is shared, the paperwork is not.

Dottato (Kadota is a related commercial conversation in American lists; do not collapse without Condit in hand) is a pale fig that dries and eats. Cilento’s white dried fig and Cosenza’s dried fig are district methods and reputations. Substitution is the reason for the stamps.

## Two maps, one pale fruit

Cilento’s 2006 regulation is a Campanian sun-dry: Dottato, white, a coast-and-hill district that already sold a reputation. Cosenza’s 2011 regulation is a Calabrian sun-dry: the same clone name, Crati valley, an elevation cap (0–800 m) that is a lawyer’s climate. Official maps live in the EU PDFs (EU-002 in the image ledger). This pack’s Mediterranean schematic is a teaching drawing. Do not treat it as the specification.

Why two stamps? Because substitution is a business. A pale dried fig from somewhere cheaper will wear a famous Italian word if no one fences the word. Aydın learned the same lesson with “Smyrna.” The Italian answer was to fence twice, by district, not once, by clone. That is a political choice about place over wood.

Kadota, in American canning lists, is a related conversation Condit knew and did not finish for every reader. Do not collapse Kadota and Dottato in a grocery caption unless the matching paper is on the table. The PDOs do not mention California. They mention sun, a variety name, and a map.
