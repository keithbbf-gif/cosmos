# Cilento and Cosenza Dry the Same Clone

Commission Regulation (EC) No 417/2006 registers Fico Bianco del Cilento: sun-dried Dottato in the Cilento / Salerno country. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 596/2011 registers Fichi di Cosenza: dried Dottato in the Crati valley, 0–800 metres. Two PDOs, one clone name, two legal maps. That is the Italian opposite of Aydın’s single-valley fence: the wood is shared, the paperwork is not.

Official maps live in the EU specification PDFs (EU-002 in the reserved image list). This pack’s Mediterranean schematic is a teaching drawing. Do not treat it as the specification. Do not scrape the official maps into WordPress without the publication’s reuse terms.

## Place over wood

Why two stamps? Because substitution is a business. A pale dried fig from somewhere cheaper will wear a famous Italian word if no one fences the word. Aydın learned the same lesson with “Smyrna,” a port name that escaped its valley. The Italian answer was to fence twice, by district, not once, by clone. That is a political choice about place over wood.

Cilento’s 2006 regulation is a Campanian sun-dry: Dottato, white, a coast-and-hill district that already sold a reputation. Cosenza’s 2011 regulation is a Calabrian sun-dry: the same clone name, Crati valley, an elevation cap that is a lawyer’s climate. Both specs are drying specs. The sundrying essay holds the tray. This one holds the lawyer.

## Eating fruit, drying fruit, one pale wood

Dottato is not only a lawyer’s drying adjective. Italian growers have long eaten it fresh and dried it when the tray cooperated. The PDOs that reached Brussels chose the dried face because that is the face substitution can wear on a northern grocery shelf. A fresh pale fig from Campania or Calabria can still be Dottato wood without wearing the stamp. The stamp is the sun-dry plus the map. Confusing the two is how a tourist caption becomes a lie.

Aydın’s specification counts fruits to the kilo and demands seeds filled. Cilento and Cosenza demand a variety name, a district, and a sun. They do not reprint the Turkish count. Different fences, different fakes to stop. A pale dried fig from a cheaper hill will wear “Cilento” as happily as it once wore “Smyrna” if no one reads the PDF. That is why two Italian stamps exist instead of one national Dottato denomination.

## Two PDOs, two climates, one pale bet

Cilento’s 2006 file is Campanian sun-dry in a district that already sold a white dried reputation before Brussels numbered it. Cosenza’s 2011 file is Calabrian sun-dry in the Crati valley with a lawyer’s climate written as 0–800 metres. A fruit that grows above the cap does not get the name. A fruit that dries outside Cilento’s map does not get the other name. That is why substitution needs two fences, not one national Dottato sticker.

The Crati valley’s elevation cap and Cilento’s coast-and-hill district are two climates written as law. A pale fruit that meets one map does not get to wear the other name. That is place over wood in a sentence a grocer can violate and a lawyer can read. Official maps stay in the EU PDFs (EU-002 reserved). This pack’s teaching schematic is not a specification, and a tourist layer called Magna Graecia is not an ingredients list.
