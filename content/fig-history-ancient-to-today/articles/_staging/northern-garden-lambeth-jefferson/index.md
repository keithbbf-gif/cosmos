# A Wall, a Palace, a Garden Book

Lambeth Palace associates a White Marseilles fig with Cardinal Reginald Pole, planted — in the Palace tradition — in 1556. A cutting went to Pope Francis on 24 June 2014. John Claudius Loudon, in 1836, said 1548 and found the old trees gone, young cuttings remaining. Popular garden writing sometimes prints 1525. Do not collapse 1525 / 1548 / 1556 into one date. Print the disagreement. Matthew Paris’s 1257 English fig notice, as garden historians cite it, is a medieval northern sighting, not a Palace tree.

Thomas Jefferson’s *Garden Book* and Monticello’s published notes put Marseilles figs among the fruits he wanted, with French cuttings associated with 1789. Use Monticello’s notes, not a souvenir spoon. A Virginia wall is a different climate bet than a London palace wall, and both are bets: heat the brick, shelter the stick, hope the summer is Mediterranean for a month.

## Brick climate

1548 (Loudon), 1556 (Palace tradition), 1525 (popular range), 1257 (Matthew Paris as cited), 1789 (Jefferson/Monticello). Print the spread. A wall is how you fake a belt for a month. White Marseilles is a name that traveled; the trunks did not all survive Loudon’s visit.


## Dates that refuse to be one date

Lambeth Palace’s public note on the 2014 cutting to Pope Francis repeats a Palace tradition: Cardinal Pole, White Marseilles, 1556. Loudon in 1836 said 1548 and found the old trees gone. Popular garden pages still print 1525, a range Morton also uses as a compilation. Matthew Paris in 1257, as garden historians cite him, is a medieval English fig notice that is not the Palace tree. Print the spread. A collapse into “the 1525 Lambeth fig” is how a palace becomes a brand.

Jefferson’s *Garden Book* and Monticello’s published notes are the Virginia desk: Marseilles figs, French cuttings associated with 1789, a wall and a hope. Use those notes. A souvenir spoon is not a source. A brick wall in London and a brick wall in Virginia are the same bet in two climates: fake a Mediterranean month, shelter the stick, do not expect Aydın.

Holtzbecher’s Gottorfer plate shows what a northern court wanted the species to look like when fruit was a rarity. It is a seeing, not a harvest total.
