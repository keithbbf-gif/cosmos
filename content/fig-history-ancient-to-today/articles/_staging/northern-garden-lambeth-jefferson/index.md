# A Wall, a Palace, a Garden Book

Lambeth Palace associates a White Marseilles fig with Cardinal Reginald Pole, planted — in the Palace tradition — in 1556. A cutting went to Pope Francis on 24 June 2014. John Claudius Loudon, in 1836, said 1548 and found the old trees gone, young cuttings remaining. Popular garden writing sometimes prints 1525. Do not collapse 1525 / 1548 / 1556 into one date. Print the disagreement. Matthew Paris’s 1257 English fig notice, as garden historians cite it, is a medieval northern sighting, not a Palace tree.

Thomas Jefferson’s *Garden Book* and Monticello’s published notes put Marseilles figs among the fruits he wanted, with French cuttings associated with 1789. Use Monticello’s notes, not a souvenir spoon. A Virginia wall is a different climate bet than a London palace wall, and both are bets: heat the brick, shelter the stick, hope the summer is Mediterranean for a month.
