# Aljarafe Ink

Ibn al-ʿAwwām farmed and wrote in the late twelfth century in the Seville countryside — the Aljarafe, a rise of olive, vine, and fruit west of the city. His *Kitāb al-Filāḥa* is the book later Europe would translate (Banqueri 1802; Clément-Mullet 1864–67) when it wanted an Islamic agriculture that looked like a system. The Filāḥa Texts Project’s source-mix — roughly a third Byzantine, a third Near Eastern, a third Andalusi — is the right warning. This is not a folk notebook. It is a learned farm.

Ibn Baṣṣāl before him had the *Hāʾiṭ al-Sulṭān* garden and a reputation that later quotation has to reconstruct. Ibn Ḥajjāj’s *al-Muqniʿ fī l-filāḥa* (1073) survives in extracts; Ibn al-ʿAwwām used him heavily on olive, vine, and fig. Abū l-Khayr al-Ishbīlī is another Seville name of the 1070s. Together they are a city talking to its hinterland in Arabic about a tree the Visigoths and the Romans had already planted.

## Names without a modern key

Andalusi books name figs by color, season, place, and eating quality. Matching those names to Dottato, Málaga, or a modern Spanish nursery list is a Condit-class grief. Do it only when a later scholar has done the philology. This article will not invent a “Seville Black” and call it Mission.

Christian conquest did not evict the tree. It evicted some of the libraries. The fig stayed in the huerta and in the later Spanish Americas as a cutting in a friar’s bundle. Sicily’s Arab-Norman garden (extra article) is the other western archive. Aljarafe is the one we can still walk as a landscape name.


## After the libraries

Christian Seville still ate figs. The huerta did not wait for a new agronomist of genius. What thinned was the Arabic bookshelf, not the tree. Spanish American cuttings are the next chapter of the same wood. Matching a twelfth-century color-name to a twenty-first-century nursery tag remains a philological job this pack will not fake.

## What conquest evicted

in a new court language the way Sicily did. Spanish American cuttings
are that wood on another ocean. Andalusi color-names remain a
philological job. This article will not invent a Seville Black to make
a nursery happy. The Filāḥa thirds are the warning that the ink was
already international. Aljarafe is still walkable. Walk it as olive,
vine, and basin — the machine, not a clone list.
