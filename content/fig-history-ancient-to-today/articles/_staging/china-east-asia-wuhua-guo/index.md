# A Guest Tree Called Flowerless Fruit

*Wúhuāguǒ* — flowerless fruit — is a good name for a syconium. *Flora of China* (5: 52) says *Ficus carica* was introduced during the Tang (618–906 CE) and is cultivated especially in Xinjiang. Julia Morton’s compilation says Chinese gardens by 1550. Those dates do not agree. This article will not pick a winner to please a timeline graphic. It will print both and move.

Laufer’s *Sino-Iranica* hangs Chinese *a-ži* and related forms on Iranian names and, behind them, a Babylonian peg. That is a history of words riding west-to-east. It is not a packing list and not a Tang imperial orchard you can book a ticket to. Wang Lianju and colleagues, in an ISHS *Acta Horticulturae* paper, treat commercial cultivation as recent and introduction as “about one thousand years.” Another seam. Commercial is not botanical introduction.

## Asia as guest rule

Native East Asian *Ficus* species are not this crop. Do not write a Buddhist fig that is actually a pipal and call it *carica*. Do not give the Tang a Smyrna industry. Xinjiang cultivation makes climatic sense (dry summers, irrigation cultures). It does not make Gilgal Chinese.

Ishizaki Yūshi’s *F. carica* in the Naturalis / Cock Blomhoff collection is a later Japanese seeing (Japan–Korea article). It is not a Tang witness.


## Print the seam

Tang (*Flora of China*). Gardens by 1550 (Morton). Commercial recent / introduction “about a thousand years” (Wang et al.). Xinjiang as a cultivation note. No walkable Tang orchard. No pipal substitution. *Wúhuāguǒ* remains a good name for a room without a flower show.


## Three dates, one guest

*Flora of China* 5:52: Tang introduction, cultivation especially in Xinjiang. Morton 1987: gardens by 1550. Wang et al., *Acta Hort.* 605: commercial recent, introduction “about one thousand years.” Print all three. A timeline graphic that picks one to look clean has left the seam this series promised to keep.

Laufer’s *a-ži* and Iranian pegs are word-history. They are not a Samarkand invoice. Native East Asian *Ficus* are other crops and other religions. A pipal is not a *wúhuāguǒ*. Xinjiang cultivation makes dry-summer sense. It does not make Gilgal Chinese. Ishizaki’s later Japanese plate is a seeing, not a Tang witness.

*Wúhuāguǒ* — flowerless fruit — remains the best vernacular this series has for a syconium. The name saw the room.
