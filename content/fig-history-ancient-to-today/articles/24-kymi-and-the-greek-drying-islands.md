# Askada: The Doubled Figs of Kymi

Kymi sits on the east of Euboea, looking at a sea that is not the tourist Aegean of the postcards. Its dried fig is a made object. The fruit of the Traganikó (Traganió / Traganikà) type is split, opened, and two halves are pressed inner face to inner face until they become one cake — *askada* in the local commercial speech. The PDO name is Xera Syka Kymis / Ξερά Σύκα Κύμης. The Greek ministerial decision 313420 recognized the designation; the EU list followed. The zone is a roll of communities: Kymi, Vitala, Oxylithos, Platana, the two Kourounia, Taxiarches, and the rest of the official parish list. Packs carry a KY control code.

Qualigeo’s English abstract repeats a charming Archilochos notice (7th century BCE) that this pack has not independently lined up to a fragment number. Until a classicist on the desk does, treat “Archilochos mentioned a tasty fig” as secondary color, not a footnote. The method and the variety are the facts.

## Greece is larger than Kymi

Kalamata’s olive fame has a fig shadow; island types run from the Cyclades to the Ionian; Attica’s old reputation (article 07) is not this PDO. A magazine that says “Greek figs” and shows only a Kymi cake has done the port trick in reverse: made one method into a nation. This article will not. It will say Kymi industrialized a doubling that other places may have done by hand, and then wrote it down for Brussels.

Oven-dried “white” Kymi figs are a second commercial face: paler, a different heat. The spec allows more than one finish. The constant is the variety and the place.
