# After Condit: Hardy Figs, LSU, and a Nursery Passport

W. B. Storey and the later UC / USDA fig people did what a monograph cannot: they crossed. The aim was not always a better dried Sarılop. It was often a tree that would set in humidity, or come back from a freeze, or carry *palmata* blood without tasting like a wild joke. Handbook chapters (Storey 1975 and after) are the paper trail. Pedigrees in GRIN are the next trail, and they are noisy (PLOS ONE 2022).

Louisiana State University’s releases — names that show up in Gulf gardens — are a humid-south answer to California’s dry-west monopoly on English fig talk. Chicago Hardy, which profiles near Abruzzi, is a northern answer with an Italian ghost. Desert King (a San Pedro type in many lists) is a Pacific Northwest answer: a breba that can finish when the main crop cannot. This pack will not certify a nursery catalog as Condit. It will say the *problem* changed: from crate to yard.

James Doyle and other late breeders appear in grower talk; print them when a release note exists, not as folklore heroes. The collector century (article 58) is what happens when breeding and swapping outrun the experiment station.
