# What the DNA Will Allow

Aradhya’s 2010 SSR work on cultivated figs found structure that was real and also leaky — clines, gene flow, a crop that has been moved as wood and as seed for a long time. Khadari’s earlier mitochondrial work had already hinted that “natural” Mediterranean populations remember geography more fiercely than orchards do.

The 2025 Khadari–Grout–Santoni–Kjellberg sampling (949 accessions, microsatellites, fourteen countries) is the paper this series has to live with now. *F. carica* sensu stricto is a group. *Rupestris* and *colchica* are kept off to the side. Inside *carica*: three pools — Moroccan–Algerian, northern Mediterranean, Levantine. The Levant is the most differentiated. The other two are closer to each other, a west-and-north block from Portugal to Aegean Turkey and across to the Maghreb. Cultivated and spontaneous trees in the same region look like family. The authors call this diffuse, regional domestication, not a single Levantine export of one tamed type.

Fossils of *F. carica* in France and Italy at 60,000–100,000 years (as they cite) are the rude guest at the “introduced from the east last Tuesday” party.

## What this does to Gilgal

It does not erase nine carbonized figs. It stops a headline from turning those figs into the mother of every tree in Fez and Empordà. Gilgal can still be an early Levantine experiment. It cannot be, on present genetics, the only workshop.

## What this does to condit’s names

It explains the synonym pile. Clones move; seedlings come home; a “Adriatic” in one collection is Milco’s Adriatic in another (PLOS ONE 2022). DNA is a clerk for the monograph, not a replacement for tasting.
