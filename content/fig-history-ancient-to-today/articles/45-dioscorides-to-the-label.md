# A Simple, Not a Miracle: Figs from Dioscorides to the Box

Dioscorides put the fig in the *Materia Medica* as food and as a simple — softening, useful, not mystical. Galen lengthened the note. Arabic and Persian physicians — Ibn al-Bayṭār collecting the Greeks and the Baghdadis, Ibn Sīnā’s *Qānūn*, Abū Manṣūr Muwaffaq, the 10th-century Persian *Hidāyat* of Akhawaynī — hung their own uses on that trunk. Alam’s Iranica entry is the map: Akhawaynī rarely gives fig alone; he puts it in decoctions and enemas and poultices with other drugs, as if the fruit were a useful citizen of a formula, not a star.

Shiʿi collections (Majlisī gathering earlier men) preserve sayings: the fig as near to the plants of paradise, useful against piles and gout, a fruit without a stone. Qurʾān 95:1 is an oath, not a monograph. Report the sayings as sayings. Do not print them as clinical results.

## What remains

Alam: nowadays the only general medicinal use is as a laxative. A Kurdish decoction of dried figs for piles is a local survival. Hezekiah’s cake (article 05) is the same kitchen idea without Galen.

Wellcome and Köhler plates (WC-015, WC-002) show how Europe kept the tree in the pharmacy picture-book after the theory moved on.

Wang’s Chinese processing paper (article 20) includes mouse extract work. That is a lab note. It is not a reason to write “figs fight cancer” on a FigRoots page. Scar of placation, in another chair: a claim is not evidence.
