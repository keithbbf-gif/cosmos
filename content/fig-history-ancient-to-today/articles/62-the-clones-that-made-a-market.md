# Five (or So) Clones That Made a Market

Condit said California had fixed names for five commercial varieties: Mission, Adriatic, Calimyrna, Kadota, Brown Turkey. Each is a history lesson if you refuse the grocery blur. Two of them are other countries wearing California passports. One of them is not a clone at all.

## Mission

A black common fig. Spanish-mission wood (articles 34–35). Sets without a wasp, which is why it could found a New World orchard. Dries dark, eats rich, now also a fresh-market face. Not a generic “mission fig” of all Christendom. Not every black fig. The name is a California cultivar name with a colonial walk behind it.

## Adriatic

A pale common type. The 1889 eastbound dryer, called inferior to imported Smyrna in the UC circulars, still a workhorse because it set when the wasp year was ugly. Condit says that when you caprify it the pulp goes deeper and more acid — type is destiny of set, not of flavor. The name is a pile. The PLOS ONE re-evaluation thinks the NCGR “Adriatic” is likely Milco’s Adriatic. Say which one or say you do not know.

## Calimyrna

Sarılop in a California suit. Lob Injir, Loh Injir, Sari Lop: Condit says identical with the principal drying fig of the Smyrna district, introduced more than once. Wasp or nothing. Drops at an inch. The tasting standard that made the San Joaquin want to be Aydın. Aydın İnciri is the legal name for the same idea at home (article 23). The stencil lied about the place and told the truth about the fruit.

## Kadota

Dottato’s American industrial cousin: pale, canning, drying, no wasp. The Mezzogiorno’s solution (article 25), renamed. Cilento and Cosenza wrote Dottato into Brussels. California wrote Kadota onto a can. Same temperament. Different lawyer.

## Brown Turkey

A warning label. Many clones. Condit spent pages saying so. A nursery tag that says Brown Turkey has told you a color and a country and almost nothing else. Ask for a synonym, a source, a picture of the ostiole in a known week. Or walk away.

## The home names

**Sarılop / Aydın İnciri** and **Dottato / Cilento / Cosenza** are the two ideas at home, with lawyers. Panache, Violette de Bordeaux, White Marseilles, Celeste: garden fame, not crate fame. They belong in the mailbag (article 58). They made no valley.

Bimbi painted more names than a market needs. Markets simplify. That simplification is how a fruit becomes a cupboard word, and also how a village disappears.
