# Cousins on the Dry Edge: Palmata, Rupestris, Johannis

*Ficus palmata*, sometimes called Indian fig in older English, runs a wide, unfriendly map — Berg and Wiebes treated it as a species complex from Ethiopia toward India and Nepal, arid to wet. It is not the orchard crop. It will hybridize in a collection. NCGR Davis took SSR profiles of *carica* and *palmata* together around 2009; a 2022 PLOS ONE paper had to say which accessions were mislabeled, which “descendants” did not match the pedigree book, and that Chicago Hardy sat next to Abruzzi.

Browicz, in *Flora Iranica* 153 (1982), is the other adult voice: *F. carica* subsp. *carica* and *rupestris*; *F. johannis* and its Afghan subspecies. Alam’s Iranica vernaculars sit on that skeleton. Khadari 2025 keeps *rupestris* and *colchica* off the *carica* sensu stricto pile.

## Why a history series needs the cousins

Because “wild fig” in a travel sentence is often one of these, or a spontaneous *carica* seedling, or a sycomore. Because breeding programs (Storey, later work) have used *palmata* blood. Because a repository error becomes a nursery error becomes a forum argument.

Ehret’s 1771 plate is *F. carica*. Do not paste it on a *palmata* paragraph.
