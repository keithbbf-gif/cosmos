# The Wasp, Without Folklore

The insect’s living name is *Blastophaga psenes* (older papers: *B. grossorum*). She is not “the fig fly.” She is not a blessing you can swap for a honeybee. She is the partner of *Ficus carica*’s sexual cycle, bred in the short-styled flowers of the caprifig, dusted with pollen at the profichi ostiole, and then, if a grower has hung those profichi in a Smyrna tree, lost in a room of long styles where she can pollinate and cannot easily breed.

Theophrastus saw the swell (article 08). Eisen described the year for California (1901). Condit and UC workers described it again until the Italian crop names were “household words.” Schwarz, on Roeding’s place in 1900, said the year looks complicated only if you do not know it: mamme overwinter inhabited; profichi are the pollen crop; mammoni keep the insect through late summer.

## What folklore gets wrong

That all figs need wasps. (Common types do not.)

That caprification “fertilizes the tree.” (It pollinates a crop of flowers inside a stem.)

That the insect was a Turkish secret. (It was a Mediterranean farm practice; the secret was California’s unread books.)

That more wasps are always better. Caldis showed *Fusarium* / endosepsis riding the same insect; Hansen worked controls. A history of caprification that stops at “success in 1900” has not paid the disease bill (article 55).

## What the biology gets right

Mutualism is not kindness. It is a door, a style length, a generation time. Kjellberg and Khadari’s later genetics sit on this insect as the reason seeded fruit leak genes across orchards.
