# August Is a Factory Without Walls

Go to a drying country in the right week and the farm has no inside. Fruit on cane, on cloth, on concrete, on rock. Dogs, wasps, dust, a plastic sheet against a threat of rain. Someone’s back, not in the brochure. This is Aydın and Estahban and Taounate and a hundred unnamed yards that will never get a PDO. The GI texts that mention “traditional method” are pointing here without showing the heat.

A yard is a factory that uses the sky for a roof and the calendar for a foreman. If the week is wrong, the factory closes and the year becomes jam, or feed, or a loss.

## What the specs are actually describing

Aydın İnciri’s lawyerly slopes are a drying machine: southwest faces, a height band, a fruit that sugars in that air. Cilento’s sun-and-tunnel-and-salt-dip clauses are a yard in Brussels English. Kymi’s doubling is a pair of hands making one cake from two faces. Nabout’s Moroccan arrêté is a variety and a place; Slow Food’s Ark note, which is not the law, adds the sentence the law often skips: the drying is women’s work.

Condit’s California circulars talk about trays and sulfur houses as if they were implements. They are implements. They are also a social order: who turns, who grades, who owns the fruit when it leaves the dirt. Crew labor in the San Joaquin is not the same as a Taounate household, and pretending they are both “tradition” is how a magazine goes soft.

## What a photograph usually refuses

The picture that shows only golden fruit has chosen a side. It has chosen the crate’s afterlife, not the week that made the crate. This series uses a diagram (AS-DIA-05) instead of a stolen tourist snap. If `D:\FIGS` holds a yard picture with consent, that is ORCH-05. Until then the diagram is the honest picture: a set of jobs.

Birds and beetles are workers too, unpaid. So is the wasp, if the type is Smyrna and the profichi were hung in time (article 51). The yard is downstream of that hanging. A common type skips the hanging and still needs the week.

## Why the yard is a historical source

Because this is where the long freight story (article 18, 50) becomes physics you can smell. Because a “brick of figs” in an Old Assyrian text (article 06) is a yard’s grandchild. Because Casa dei Cervi’s painted dried fruit (article 12) is a yard’s afterimage on a silver tray. Because when world acreage falls (article 53) it is yards that close, not adjectives.

August, in a drying country, is not a season. It is a shift.
