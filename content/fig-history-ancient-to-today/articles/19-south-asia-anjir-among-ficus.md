# Anjīr Among the Ficus: South Asia’s Imported Fig

South Asia does not need *Ficus carica* to have a fig religion. It has *F. religiosa*, *F. benghalensis*, *F. racemosa*, and a literature that already knew how to make a tree cosmic. The common fig arrives into that crowded genus as a fruit plant with a borrowed name: *anjīr* / *anjeer*, Iranian in dress (article 17). Dymock, Warden, and Hooper, in *Pharmacographia Indica*, recorded the name and an old sentence that Muslims brought the cultivation. Alam, with the *Bundahišn* and Laufer in hand, says the move east could be Sasanian or earlier. Neither side hands us a dated orchard in the Deccan.

The honest South Asian chapter is therefore a guest story, not an origin story.

## What the guest found

A climate that is often too wet, or too monsoon-broken, for the Mediterranean drying trick. Figs in India are more often fresh-market and local-processed than a Smyrna crate. Where a dry season and a slope cooperate, a district can specialize. Trade writing about Purandar in Maharashtra likes a commercial start around 1904. That is local industry memory, not an imperial gazetteer we have checked in this pack. Say it as memory. Karnataka districts and a Kashmiri reputation appear in the same kind of writing. Do not turn them into FAO ranks they do not hold.

Mughal gardens loved fruit and water. The *Āʾīn-i Akbarī* is a book that can describe a kitchen and a fruit store without being a planting date for *carica*. Persianate court culture would have recognized *anjīr* as a familiar word. Recognition is not introduction.

## Do not steal the native Ficus

A pipal is not a Mission fig. A banyan is not a caprifig. Magazine copy that says “India has worshipped the fig for millennia” and then shows *F. carica* has committed a species theft. This series will not. The guest deserves its own plates: a leaf that is palmate and scabrous, a syconium that looks like the Mediterranean, a name that still sounds like Shiraz.

*Ficus palmata*, the “Indian fig” of some older English, is a wild relative with a wide dry-land range (article 41). It is not the orchard crop. NCGR Davis mixed the two in SSR work and then had to unmix them. South Asia is one of the places that confusion was born.
