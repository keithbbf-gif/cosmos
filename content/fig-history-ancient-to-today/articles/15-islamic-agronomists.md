# The Filāḥa Bench: How Arabic Writers Farmed the Fig

Ibn al-ʿAwwām of Seville, late twelfth century, is the name that stuck because his *Kitāb al-Filāḥa* was translated into Spanish in 1802 and French in the 1860s, and because the book survived nearly whole. He is not the first. He is the one who kept the others.

The Filāḥa Texts Project has done the census. About 1,900 citations: roughly a third Byzantine (mostly the *Geoponica* line), a third Near Eastern Arabic (mostly the *Nabataean Agriculture* attached to Ibn Waḥshiyya), a third Andalusi — Ibn Baṣṣāl, Abū l-Khayr, Ibn Ḥajjāj. He names 112 authors. He also farmed, in the Aljarafe west of Seville, and says so. Ibn Khaldūn, who mentions almost no agronomist in the *Muqaddima*, mentions him.

## The bench in the south

Eleventh-century Seville and Toledo had a circle. Ibn Baṣṣāl, who had been in Toledo, is tied to the *Hāʾiṭ al-Sulṭān*, a garden that was a research plot before we had the word. Ibn Ḥajjāj wrote *al-Muqniʿ fī l-filāḥa* in 1073; what survives directly is extracts on olive, vine, fig, and garden plants, but Ibn al-ʿAwwām quoted him so hard that much of the rest can be felt. Abū l-Khayr al-Ishbīlī, “the tree-planter,” wrote around 1070–75. Al-Ṭighnarī belongs to the same weather.

They argue about soil, manure, grafting, water, and the sympathies of plants — some of that is antique theory wearing a new cloak. They also argue about things you only learn with your hands in the Aljarafe. The fig is in both layers: artificial pollination listed with date, pomegranate, pistachio; preservation of fruit; pests. Clément-Mullet’s table of contents, as later summaries report it, puts pollination of the fig among the crafts.

## Nabataean Agriculture

The *Filāḥa al-Nabaṭiyya* is a problem child: a huge, strange book, Greek and Mesopotamian ghosts, an attribution to Ibn Waḥshiyya that scholars fight. Ibn al-ʿAwwām used it anyway. For this series the point is not to settle authorship. It is to say the Islamic agronomic fig has an eastern memory as well as an Andalusi one. The tree is being thought about in Baghdad’s long shadow and in Seville’s hills at once.

## What the bench changed

It made fig culture *writable* at a length Rome did not bother with. Cato gave a soil note. These men gave a system. When Christian Iberia later kept the huerta (article 16), it was keeping a landscape these books describe. When a modern GI specification talks about slope and drying, it is, without knowing, in their debt.
