# Cuttings in the Mail: The Collector Century

The California Rare Fruit Growers and their cousins in other climates turned Condit’s list into a swap meet. A clone that had been a village tree in Calabria became a forum name with three misspellings and a proud photograph. Virus traveled with the wood (article 53). So did generosity. This is not a USDA story. It is a postal and then a digital one.

Article 02 said the fig travels as a stick. After 1970 the stick got cheap postage and then a tracking number. Airmail did what a mission cart did, faster, with worse labeling.

## The hangover in a spreadsheet

The 2022 PLOS ONE re-evaluation of NCGR Davis SSR profiles is the hangover: repositories and donors over decades without a way to authenticate. Seventeen mislabels in one collection is not a scandal. It is the collector century in a table. Chicago Hardy sitting next to Abruzzi, an Adriatic that is probably Milco’s, an Excel whose “Kadota” mother does not match the Kadota on the bench — these are what happens when names outrun clerks.

Do not print a forum handle as a cultivar authority. Do not claim a “lost Roman fig” recovered in a Facebook group. Do print the structural fact: vegetative travel sped up again, and Condit’s synonym pile grew a second life.

## Where FigRoots sits

This content lane is for people who already know a cutting is a decision. The series is the long memory those cuttings forgot they had — Gilgal as an argument, Pliny as a list, Aydın as a legal valley, Roeding as a man who waited. The backyard is not a failure of the industry. It is what the industry left standing, plus a thousand names the industry never needed.

Bimbi, in 1696, wanted names on a canvas. The collector wants them on a map pin. Same appetite. Different postage.
