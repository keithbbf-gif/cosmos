# Four Kinds of Work the Tree Will Do

Gustav Eisen, at the end of the nineteenth century, and Ira Condit, in the middle of the twentieth, gave English-speaking horticulture a four-part drawer that still works.

**Common** — parthenocarpic. Mission, Adriatic, Kadota, Dottato, White Marseilles, Violette de Bordeaux. The mission cart, the palace wall, the Italian PDO.

**Smyrna** — must be pollinated. Sarılop / Calimyrna, Bardacık, Kassaba. The dried-fig empires. Drop at an inch if the wasp fails.

**San Pedro** — breba sets alone; main crop needs the wasp. A tree with two religions. Commercially minor, intellectually expensive.

**Caprifig** — short-styled, wasp house, pollen in the profichi. Not dessert. Named crops: mamme, profichi, mammoni.

Condit is clear that when you caprify a common Adriatic the pulp goes deeper and more acid. Type is not destiny of flavor; it is destiny of set.

## Why the drawers are history

Because California’s lost decade was a type error. Because a GI that names a common Dottato is making a different bet than a GI that names a Sarılop. Because a backyard grower in Ohio who buys a “Smyrna” on the internet has bought a disappointment unless a wasp and a caprifig come too.

Bimbi’s 1696 painting is a Medici appetite for more drawers than four — named clones, painted as individuals. Condit’s monograph is 700-plus names stuffed into the four drawers. Both are the same hunger.
