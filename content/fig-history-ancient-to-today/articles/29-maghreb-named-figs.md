# Nabout and Bouhouli: The Maghreb Writes Its Figs Down

Morocco’s ministère put it in an arrêté: 1680-15, 25 May 2015, *Indication géographique « Figue sèche Nabout de Taounate »*. WIPO Lex files the Arabic and the French. Slow Food’s Ark of Taste, which is not a law, adds the kitchen: 24 rural communes in Taounate and Rhafsai, a gold-yellow dried fig, women’s drying work, festivals at Bouhouda and Sebt Mtiwa, a national fig festival in September, a fruit that shows up at Ashura and in Ramadan and in a tagine. New varieties, the Ark says, are pushing the old one. That is a living political sentence.

Tunisia’s Djebba fig — Bouhouli, a mountain village in Béja — became the country’s first fruit geographical indication in 2012. PAMPAT / UNIDO’s institutional note: at first no farmer was labeling; an external audit through INNORPI; by their count a quarter of eligible fruit certified; a local association later given legal charge; supermarket and Gulf and Canadian sales; prices up when the mark stuck. Use this as a development-agency report, not as folklore. The variety-and-place claim is the farmers’ claim the state agreed to write.

## Algeria, without a stamp in this pack

Algeria sits in FAOSTAT’s front rank (2022: about 112,000 tonnes) and in Khadari’s Moroccan–Algerian gene pool. This pack does not have an Algerian GI text in hand. Do not invent one. Say the trees are there, the production is large, the legal name-work is less visible in our sources than Morocco’s and Tunisia’s.

Egypt’s tonnage is larger still (article 60’s atlas) and is a Nile-and-coast story, not a Maghreb GI story.

## Why the stamps matter

They are the opposite of “Smyrna.” They try to make the village the brand before the port is. They also record labor: Nabout’s file and the Ark both say women dry. Article 52 will keep that.
