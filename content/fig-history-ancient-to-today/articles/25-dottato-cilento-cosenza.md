# Dottato: Two PDOs and One Southern Italian Clone

Italy’s two fig PDOs are not two fruits. They are two addresses for dried Dottato (Ottato): Fico Bianco del Cilento, registered 2006, sixty-eight comuni in the Salerno hills between Agropoli and the Bussento, much of it inside the national park; and Fichi di Cosenza, registered 2011, Crati country in Calabria, 0–800 meters, slopes under 35 percent, a fruit the spec praises for small empty achenes you hardly chew. Both are *Ficus carica* “domestica,” both honey-sweet, both a common type — they set without a wasp, which is why a California nurseryman could love them.

Condit’s Kadota is the commercial American name tied to this family: a pale, all-purpose fig, canning and drying, the opposite temperament from Calimyrna. The south of Italy had already solved the wasp problem by choosing a clone that does not need her.

## Methods the specs bother to write

Cilento: harvest very ripe if the skin stays on; earlier if the skin comes off. Sun, optional plastic tunnel, optional two-percent salt dip, optional hot-air darkening. Stuffing with local nuts and fennel and citrus peel, under ten percent, cut lengthwise. That last clause is a kitchen entering the law.

Cosenza: moisture caps (24 percent, or 28 if conditioned), sugar ranges that change with sun versus greenhouse. The fruit is the same idea in a different valley’s weather.

Italy has, in trade writing, something like 240 named cultivars. Two legal dried names. The rest is village. Sicily’s later chapter (same south, different island memory) should not be collapsed into Dottato.
