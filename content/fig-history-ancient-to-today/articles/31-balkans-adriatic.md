# The Cool Fig: Balkans and the Adriatic

Pliny calls the callistruthiae the coldest figs by nature, and he knows winter figs in Moesia as a product of art. The Balkan and Adriatic shore is where those sentences still have weather. Dalmatian stone, Albanian production that shows up in FAOSTAT’s second tier, Greek mountain types that are not Kymi, a Croatian or Montenegrin courtyard tree that dies to the ground some years and comes back from the root — this is fig culture as stubbornness.

The fruit is often a common type. The wasp is a fair-weather friend this far north. Drying happens in a short, fierce autumn or not at all. Fresh and rakija-adjacent kitchens do the rest. Venice’s ledger (article 28) ate some of this coast’s surplus when there was one.

## Do not make a single “Balkan fig”

There isn’t one. There is a climate band. Names are local and Condit only half-caught them. Modern hardy-fig nurseries in the American Midwest raid this band for blood (article 57) and then give the clones new passports — Chicago Hardy, which profiles near Abruzzi in the NCGR re-evaluation, is the kind of rename this coast knows in reverse.
