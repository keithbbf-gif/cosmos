# Sarılop Country: Aydın İnciri

The specification is not shy. Aydın İnciri is dried Sarılop — a female fig, the drying type of the Aegean, whitish-yellow, thin-shelled, seeds filled, not more than ninety fruits to the kilo. The geography is a lawyer’s mountain walk: slopes of the Aydın Mountains and Bozdağlar, Büyük and Küçük Menderes, towns named as if in a freight book: Sultanhisar, İncirliova, Germencik, Nazilli, Ödemiş, Tire, Selçuk. Elevation 0–900 meters, southwest faces, a slope figure the EU text bothers to give. Registration: EU 17 February 2016; a Turkish GI earlier (2005 in some national summaries). The UK scheme lists it from 31 December 2020 because of the exit paperwork.

This is the clone California named Calimyrna — Condit: identical with the principal drying fig of the Smyrna district, introduced more than once. Lob Injir, Loh Injir, Sari Lop, Sarılop: the same pale, seeded, wasp-needy fruit. Without caprification the crop drops at about an inch. With it, the crate that taught Europe what a dried fig was.

## *Ficus carica* and Caria

The specification’s bow to the botanical name — Caria, the ancient country of the southwest coast — is fair as etymology and risky as a continuous-orchard claim. “Thousands of years” in a GI text is a tone, not a pollen core. What is not a tone: the conventional sun-dry, the public Fig Research Station under the ministry, the fact that Türkiye still leads world production (FAOSTAT 2022: 350,000 tonnes; 2023 reviews: 356,000) and dried export.

İncirliova means fig-plain. A town that calls itself that is not waiting for a Brussels stamp to know its job.

## What the PDO fixes

It fixes the lie of the port. The fruit may still leave through İzmir. The name on a protected pack is the valley. It also fixes, or tries to fix, the substitution problem: dried figs from elsewhere wearing an Aegean reputation. Whether a supermarket in Düsseldorf honors that is a different police.
