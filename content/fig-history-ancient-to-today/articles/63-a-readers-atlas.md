# A Reader’s Atlas: Where Figs Still Matter

Print the year. 2022, FAOSTAT as compiled by Helgi and Ferrara: world about 1.24 million tonnes. Türkiye 350,000; Egypt 188,000; Algeria 112,000; Morocco 110,000; Iran 68,000; Spain 43,500. 2023, as compiled in a 2025 Türkiye review: world about 1.30 million; Türkiye 356,000; Egypt 193,000; Morocco 119,000; Algeria 116,000; Iran 73,000; Afghanistan 61,000; Syria 41,000; Spain 40,000; then Saudi Arabia, United States, Uzbekistan. Rank order is stable at the top and jittery below. Re-download before a live table.

**Legal names in this pack:** Xera Syka Kymis (GR), Fico Bianco del Cilento (IT), Fichi di Cosenza (IT), Aydın İnciri (TR), Figue de Djebba (TN), Figue sèche Nabout de Taounate (MA).

**Places that matter without a stamp:** Estahban’s rainfed slopes; California’s remaining valley; Purandar as a local Indian industry memory; Xinjiang as China’s dry-land address; Lambeth as a northern relic; the Maghreb’s unnamed majority of trees.

**Places this series refused to invent:** a Chinese Tang orchard we could walk; a Mughal introduction date; a Solon stone about figs; a single Eden; a “fig goddess of Israel”; an Algerian GI we do not have; a forum’s lost Roman clone.

The atlas is a humility device. Tonnage is not culture. Culture is not tonnage. Egypt’s second-place harvest and Aydın’s dried-export fame are different jobs. A Chicago backyard and a Germencik slope are different jobs. The tree does both because of article 02.
