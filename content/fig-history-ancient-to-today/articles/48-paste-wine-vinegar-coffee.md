# After the Fresh Fruit: Paste, Wine, Vinegar, ‘Coffee’

A fresh fig is a rumor that spoils. Everything after that sentence is processing. The first processed product is the dried fruit (article 50). The second is the pressed brick — Akkadian *libittum ša tīnātim* (article 06). The third is a string of half-dry fruit in Iran, *anjīr-reshta*, which Alam treats as an ordinary market form. The fourth is Cilento’s stuffed cake, nuts and fennel written into a PDO. Then the factory, which is the same impulse with a motor.

## What the factory named

Paste for bakery filling. Diced fruit for the cereal aisle. Jam, which Wang’s Chinese industry paper lists among the products that actually move. Juice. A wine that is not grape. A vinegar. A roasted powder sold as coffee in more than one cupboard language — wartime, poverty, a Central European shelf, not a tasting note to brag on.

California and İzmir packers list paste and diced as SKUs. Eisen’s curing chapter is the ancestor of those SKUs: get the water out, keep the sugar, do not let the beetle finish the winter. The industrial names are new. The physics is Pliny’s and Columella’s keeping problem with a different invoice.

## What we will not romanticize

Do not claim an ancient “fig wine culture” to match the grape. Ferments happen. They did not build a religion here, and they did not build a *terroir* literature we can cite. A fig vinegar is a use. A fig coffee is a substitute. A fig paste in a cookie is how a drying country survives a year when the grade is ugly.

Casa dei Cervi’s painted dried figs and dates on a silver tray (WC-006) are already a keeping-board. The factory is the unpainted continuation.

Wang’s paper also wandered into mouse extracts and tumors. That belongs in article 45’s warning, not on a crate.
