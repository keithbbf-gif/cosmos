# Style Guide — History of the Fig

Staged pack for FigRoots / buyfigs. Not live-site copy. Not COSMOS.

## What this series is

A long-arc magazine history of the edible fig — *Ficus carica* and the trees people have mistaken for it — from the Pre-Pottery Neolithic to the supermarket tray. Each piece stands alone. Read in order, they form one argument: the fig is not a Mediterranean cliché. It is a vegetative technology, a trade good, a wasp problem, a naming maze, and a household crop that outlasted the states that first wrote it down.

## Voice

Write as a human who has sat with the sources and still has dirt under a nail.

- **Concrete first.** Open on a site, a text, a cultivar, a crate stencil, a fresco inventory number. Do not open on “since ancient times.”
- **Names over atmosphere.** Gilgal I, Pliny *NH* 15.19, Sarılop, George C. Roeding, Ibn al-ʿAwwām, Estahban, Xera Syka Kymis. If a name is not in the bibliography, do not invent a local legend to fill the gap.
- **Admit the seam.** Kislev’s 2006 claim is an argument, not a settled origin story. Plutarch’s sycophant etymology is disputed. Flora of China says Tang; Morton says gardens by 1550. Put the disagreement on the page.
- **Magazine-serious, not academic-stiff.** Short sentences earn their keep next to long ones. One good verb beats two adjectives. Humor is allowed if it is dry and earned.
- **No AI prose habits.** Ban: *delve, tapestry, rich heritage, vibrant, moreover, furthermore, it’s important to note, in conclusion, throughout history, nestled, boasts, showcases, a testament to, the fascinating world of, let’s explore, in today’s fast-paced.* Do not start three sentences in a row with a participial phrase. Do not stack rhetorical questions. Do not close with a TED-talk moral.
- **Do not flatten people.** Growers, women on drying yards, mission gardeners, Ottoman packers, and USDA entomologists are not extras in a fruit romance.

Front matter on every article must include `voice_check: human`. That field is a pledge, not decoration. If a draft reads like a model summary, rewrite it before staging.

## Facts and citations

- **No invented citations.** If you cannot point to a text, excavation report, monograph, gazetteer, or official specification, leave the claim out or mark it as tradition.
- Ancient sources: cite book/chapter where the standard numbering exists (Plutarch *Solon* 24.1; Pliny *NH* 15.19–20; Theophrastus *HP* 2.8; Qurʾān 95:1).
- Modern science: author, year, journal. Kislev, Hartmann, and Bar-Yosef is *Science* 312 (2006), not *Nature*.
- Geography: use the name the source uses, then the modern one in parentheses once — Smyrna (İzmir), Caria, Aljarafe, Büyük Menderes.
- **Two trees in Egypt.** *Ficus sycomorus* (sycomore / sycamore-fig) is not *F. carica*. Say which one the tomb, text, or offering list means, or say we cannot tell.
- **South and East Asia.** Native *Ficus* species (pipal, banyan, cluster fig) are not the common fig. *Anjīr / anjeer / wúhuāguǒ / ichijiku* are loan-crop stories unless a source says otherwise.
- Production numbers: prefer FAOSTAT year + country. Round and date them. Do not invent a “50% of world supply” figure from a packer’s brochure.

## Structure of an article

1. YAML front matter (see `WP_IMPORT.md`).
2. Title + dek (one or two sentences; the dek is not a teaser, it is the thesis).
3. Body: 1,400–2,200 words typical. Flagship pieces may run longer. Nothing under ~1,200 unless it is a map-note or glossary companion — those are not counted toward the 40.
4. **Figure plan** in-body: numbered figures with captions, credits, licenses. Mix art, sites, maps, diagrams. Fruit close-ups are supporting players, not the show.
5. **Sources for this piece** — short list pointing into `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`. No fake page numbers.

## Images

- Prefer museum open access, Wikimedia PD / CC, and original pack graphics under `assets/`.
- Never caption a generated or redrawn image as a photograph of a historical object.
- Document every external file in `IMAGE_SOURCES.md` (URL, license, credit, article IDs).
- Orchard photographs from `D:\FIGS` are **optional slots only**. Mark them `orchard_slot:` and do not depend on them for the argument.
- Coordinate maps, timelines, and botanical redraws with the graphics lane via the paths in `assets/`.

## House spelling

- *fig* lowercase except in titles and cultivar names.
- *caprifig*, *caprification*, *syconium*, *breba*, *profichi*, *mamme*, *mammoni* — keep the Italian crop names when discussing the wasp cycle; define once.
- *Calimyrna* (California trade name) ≠ *Sarılop / Lob Injir / Sari Lop* (Aegean drying fig) — say they are the same clone when Condit says so.
- *Mission* is a California cultivar name with a Spanish-mission origin story; it is not a generic “mission fig” of all the Americas.
- *Brown Turkey* is a name-pile. Specify the clone or say the name is unreliable.
- Use *İzmir*, *Aydın*, *Fars*, *Estahban* with proper characters.

## What this pack will not do

- Edit figroots.com or any live WordPress.
- Invent a folk tale and call it ethnography.
- Treat Condit 1955 as the last word on identity (it is the last great printed word).
- Claim medical efficacy beyond what Dioscorides, Ibn al-Bayṭār, or a modern label actually says.
- Mention COSMOS, the mesh, or the writer’s other chairs.
