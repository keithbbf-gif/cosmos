They call it a cake because English is lazy. It is a press.

Dried figs go through a grind or a hard chop. Grape brandy — *lozovača* — or a herbal rakija goes in until the mass agrees to be a dough. Almonds on some islands, walnuts on others, fennel where the wind says so. On Vis the fennel is not a garnish; it is the accent that makes *hib* *hib*, a round, compact cake that people wait to “frost” with its own sugar, *zastrižio*, a crystallization the island treats as a verdict.

Cres likes a cone. Krk likes a loaf. Vis likes a little wheel. These are not branding meetings. These are pans and hands and the way a grandmother’s wrist turns. The shaped mass dries again — a week, two, under bay and rosemary, or an hour in a low oven if the terrace is no longer a workplace. Then paper. Then a bag. Then a pocket, if you are going to a field, or a souvenir shelf, if you are the later economy.

Vinodol’s old walk inland, baskets of dried figs traded toward the snow, is the prehistory of the cake. The cake is what you make when you want the trade to be even smaller and sweeter: a slice for a glass, a gift that looks like a decision.

I have eaten *hib* that was too wet and tasted of alcohol in a way that was not charming, and *hib* that was old and proud and sliced like a salami of August. The proud one had bay in the paper. The wet one had a tourist label and a story about “energy.” You can guess which I would walk for.

Istria will make a spicier cousin and call it by the same family name; let them. Dalmatia will insist the fennel is the island. Both are allowed if the fig was dry enough to begin with. A cake made from tired fruit is a cake that will taste of the brandy and nothing else.

Serve it with a cheese if you are on Pag or if you know Gligora’s door. Serve it alone if the night is already loud. Do not serve it as an energy bar. That sentence is a theft of context. It was a worker’s sweet, a sailor’s sweet, a walk inland. It is now a holiday sweet and a souvenir if the island needs the cash. Both are better than a gym, and both still want bay in the paper.
