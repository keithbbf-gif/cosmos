A lamb shoulder, a cinnamon stick, a handful of the fruit that kept.

This is not “fusion.” This is a pot that has always known that meat and dried fruit can share a lid. Prunes get the fame in some Moroccan dishes, the ones the cookbooks already photographed. Apricots in others. Figs take the job when the orchard says so, or when the cook likes a seeded chew against the grain and a sweetness that is not a prune’s. Tunisia will put the fruit in a couscous that is a Friday, a platter that is a table, not a side dish next to a grilled thing. Fassi houses will have their own spice and their own refusal to be called Tunisian. Algiers will have its own argument about cinnamon and who is allowed to add sugar at the end. I will not flatten them into one “North African tagine” with a stock tent.

What repeats is the logic. Fresh figs are too wet and too proud for a long pot; they collapse into a rumor and make the sauce cloudy. Dried figs hold. They take the sauce and give back a sweetness that is not sugar dumped at the end by a person who panicked. They come out looking like they meant to be there, darker, a little swollen, a prize.

A feast couscous with fruit is a landscape on a platter: meat, grain, the sweet, the almond if the house is showing off, chickpeas if the house is feeding a crowd, the broth in a bowl because someone will want to ruin the arrangement on purpose. The fig hides and then is found. Children hunt it. That hunt is a review. An adult who will not hunt is performing a cuisine they did not grow up with.

I have eaten a pot where the figs were added too late and tasted like a mistake from another dinner, still themselves, sulking. I have eaten a pot where they were added too early and vanished into a rumor you could only smell. The middle timing is a family secret only because families do not write minutes. Ask, if you are a guest. They may lie and then you will watch the clock anyway.

If you serve this to people who want their savory and sweet in separate rooms, let them be unhappy. The Mediterranean south did not build its feast around their rooms. Give them bread. Give them the broth. Leave the fig for the people who came to eat the year.
