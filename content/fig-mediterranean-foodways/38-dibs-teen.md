Not every fig will take the tray. Some are too far gone in sweetness, too soft, too many, the ones that would sour if you asked them to be a shape and a grade. Those go to the pot and become *dibs*. The word is the same family as other Levantine molasses: a reduction, a winter, a bottle that does not need a story on the glass.

Fig molasses is a cousin of carob molasses and grape pekmez, the dark liquids that Levantine and Anatolian kitchens use when sugar was dear or when sugar was boring. You cook the overripe fruit with a little water, you strain the pulp if you want a pour and not a jam, you reduce until a spoon leaves a slow letter on a cold plate. The letter is for tahini, for a drizzle on a white cheese, for a stew that wants a brown note without a stock cube, for a drink cut with water if the house still does that in summer.

Villages used to make it because the tree does not ask how many mouths you have. A glut is a problem that looks like luck. *Dibs* is how luck is stored. Syrian kitchens have their own pace and their own bottles; Lebanese ones have been talked about more lately because projects needed a product with a name. Both are older than the project.

AUB and various campaigns have tried to give this syrup a modern public life, which is fine if it pays a grower. The older public life was a shelf in a pantry that smelled of other reductions. A bottle without a label, a mark of a year that had too many figs, which is a blessed complaint. You open it when the yoghurt is too proud or the guest is staying.

Do not call it a superfood. Call it a surplus. The word surplus is more honest and more interesting. A landscape that can make *dibs* is a landscape that sometimes has more fruit than mouths. That is a kind of luck the recipes remember even when the market forgets and the shop wants a detox caption.

I like *dibs teen* on yoghurt that is too sour, or on a grilled thing that is too proud of its char. I like it in a cookie if the baker is not showing off with gold leaf. I do not like it when it is poured in a store like a performance, from a height, for a phone. It is a winter liquid. Treat it like one: cap, dark, a teaspoon, another if the first was true, a shelf that does not face the sun.
