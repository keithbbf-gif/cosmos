Open a proper Cosenza tin at Christmas and you will not find a pile. You will find carpentry.

*Crocette* — *crucetti* — are four opened figs arranged as a cross, a nut in the crossing, citrus peel if the house likes a bitter thread, then a bake that turns the edges gold. The shape is not a branding exercise. It is how you make a gift that looks like a gift without icing. *Jette* are plaits. *Corolle* are wreaths. There are knots, balls, a salami of figs and almonds that slices like a joke about butchers. The Dottato that dried on the *cannizza* is the lumber.

Amantea likes to be called the capital of this carpentry. Belmonte Calabro has the older family stories, the 1930s workshop, the secret that is not a secret: good fruit, a nut, heat, a box that closes. Corso Telesio in Cosenza Vecchia still has shops that will sell you the year in small, medium, and large, October to December and again after the holidays when the leftovers become ordinary.

Chocolate is the late guest. Dark, a full dip, a half dip, a drizzle that photographs better than it eats. People who want a medieval Calabria in every bite can sulk. Cocoa came when it came. The feast absorbed it because the feast is a living mouth, not a museum. A chocolate fig is still a fig if the fruit was honest before the coat.

*Pitta ’mpigliata* — *pitta ’nchiusa* — is the other Christmas where the dried fig goes into a pastry that is all spirals and walnuts and a honeyed dark. It is a pie that wants a knife and a neighbor. The tin of *crocette* is for the visits that do not sit down. Both are the same crop in different furniture.

I have been in a kitchen where three generations argued about orange peel. The youngest lost, which is how you know the peel matters. The cross was assembled on a tray that had a burn mark from a previous year. Nobody replaced the tray. The burn was a witness.

If you send these as a gift, send them in a tin that can be reused for screws. That is not a joke. The foodway includes the second life of the container. The figs will be gone by Epiphany if the house is functioning. The tin will see Easter.

Amantea at Christmas smells like sugar, citrus, and a workshop that has been hot since October. That smell is the drying year finishing its sentence. If you only meet the fruit in a gift shop at the airport, you met the last clause. The *cannizza* was the first.
