If you cut a proper drying fig from the Menderes, you meet seeds. Not the ghost seeds of a parthenocarpic table fig. Real ones, a little grit, the reason the flesh tastes finished.

Those seeds are a wasp’s errand.

*Blastophaga psenes* is a small life with a large job. She is born inside a caprifig — the male tree, the one that does not make the fruit you want to eat — and she leaves looking for another fig to enter. Growers hang caprifig fruits, *male* fruits, in the female orchards at the right week. The wasp goes in. The fig you will dry gets pollinated. The wasp does not get a statue.

This is caprification. The word sounds like a thesis. The practice looks like someone decorating trees with ugly ornaments. In Aydın it is still timed, argued over, blamed when a year comes out hollow. A Sarılop that misses the wasp can swell and look like a fig and then dry like a disappointment: empty, light, a skin around a rumor. The PDO text is almost shy about it and then suddenly botanical, subspecies stacked on subspecies. The yard is not shy. No caprifigs, no crop.

The same biology crossed into California when people tried to grow “Smyrna” figs in a new valley and could not understand why the fruit aborted. They imported the wasp. They imported the male trees. They coined Calimyrna as if a portmanteau could hide the shipping documents. The drying yards of the San Joaquin are not Mediterranean geography. They are Mediterranean method under a different sky. The foodway traveled because the insect did.

Catalogs prefer a photograph of golden fruit in a wooden bowl. They do not prefer a paragraph about a female wasp dying in a flower that is pretending to be a fruit. Fair. It is a hard sell at Christmas. It is also the reason a dried Smyrna-type fig has that particular crunch, the one tasters call “seeds” as if seeds were a garnish and not a proof.

There are figs that do not need her. Dottato, many Greek table types, the black ones you eat off a Roman wall. Those have their own drying lives. The great drying economies of the eastern Aegean built themselves on the types that do need her. Taste a hollow fig beside a caprified one and the argument ends. One is candy. One is a landscape.

I will not make the wasp a mascot. She is not cute. She is a condition, like slope and August humidity. When a writer calls a dried fig “simple sun and fruit,” smile and ask who hung the caprifigs that week. If the writer does not know the word, the piece is about a bowl, not a foodway.
