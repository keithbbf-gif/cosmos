*Uyutmak* is to put to sleep. *İncir uyutması* is what happens when dried figs and milk share a pot long enough to forget they were separate.

The method is homely. Chop or slit the figs — the ones that were always going to be the house bag, not the gift box — and warm them with milk. Some kitchens add a spoon of rice flour or cornstarch if the house is impatient. Some trust only the fruit’s own body, which will thicken if you gave it time and a low heat and did not wander off to take a call. The mixture rests. It sets in the cool of a pantry or a fridge that has known worse leftovers. You eat it with a spoon. Walnuts if you have them. Cinnamon only if you cannot leave a brown surface alone.

There is a version that looks almost like a baked pudding and a version that looks like a thick soup that changed its mind overnight. Both are correct if the figs taste like figs and the milk tastes like milk. A restaurant will try to set it in a glass and put a crumb on top. Fine. The crumb is not the dish.

This is not a pastry-shop dessert in most of its life. It is a way to make a bag of last autumn’s fruit become a bowl for children who will not eat “just fruit” and for adults who want a night that is not baklava. It belongs next to *muhallebi* and *sütlaç* in the family of milk sweets, with the fig as the reason the bowl is brown instead of pale. Aegean houses make it because the drying crop is in the cupboard. Central Anatolian houses make it when a bag from Aydın has been sitting long enough to need a second career.

I like the name more than is professional. A language that will say the figs are sleeping is a language that still thinks of food as a set of beings. You can call that rustic. I call it accurate to a pot. The fruit went still. The milk went still. In the morning they are one weather.

Serve it cold. Serve it the next day, when it has tightened and the walnuts have something to push against. Do not serve it as a wellness pudding and do not put a calorie line under the title. Serve it as what it is: a cupboard trick, a lullaby, a brown bowl on a table that has already been wiped once.
