A whole dried fig is a performance. It has a shape you can grade. It has a face for a box. A great many figs will never be that person.

They split on the tray. They took a bruise from a fall. They were the small ones at the end of the count. They fermented a little at the eye. None of this is tragedy in a house that still cooks. It is the beginning of paste.

In Gaziantep and along the same latitude, fruit leathers — *pestil* — are a winter grammar usually written in grape or apricot. Fig does the work when the orchard says so: cooked down, spread, dried again into a sheet you can roll and slice. The sheet is uglier than the whole fruit and more useful. A child can take it to school. A cook can fold it into a stuffing. A traveler can keep it in a pocket without performing heritage.

Calabria turns the lesser fruit toward *miele di fichi*, a dark, slow syrup that is not honey and does not need to apologize, and toward the fillings that go into *crocette* when the pretty Dottati have already been reserved for the gift tins. Sicily knows a fig *mostarda* that will stand next to boiled meat the way a grape mostarda does in the north: sweet, sharp, a condiment that remembers it was a preserve. The Levant cooks the broken ones into *teen matboukh*, a jam that wants sesame and anise, and stops calling them broken.

Paste is also how a culture invents a new object. Spanish *pan de higo* is a paste that got a shape and a spice. Dalmatian *smokvenjak* is a paste that got rakija. Turkish *incir ezmesi* is a paste that got a breakfast plate. The whole fruit is a luxury of intactness. The mass is a luxury of time.

Factories now make paste on purpose, from good fruit, for bakers who want a uniform filling. That is a legitimate trade. It is not the same as the winter store, which is a moral system: nothing that can still be food leaves the yard as waste. The industrial vat does not have a grandmother’s other basket. The industrial vat has a spec.

If you write about drying and only photograph the perfect discs, you are writing a catalog. Write the knife that takes the damaged ones to the pot. Write the sheet on the cloth in the spare room. Write the jar that will be opened in a wet February when the whole figs are being saved for guests. That jar is the foodway when no one is watching.
