Do not go to a fig festival looking for silence. Go looking for the place where the fruit is still being sold while someone is making a speech.

The Gold Fig events around Aydın and İncirliova have the usual kit: a stage, a queen if the year wants one, a panel, a plate of the best lot arranged as if figs were medals. Children get sugar. Officials get photographs. Growers get a day when the city has to say their crop’s name correctly.

Under the bunting, the market continues. A festival that does not include a stall is a brochure. The useful stalls are the ones that look slightly bored: bags of seconds, paste in unlabeled tubs, a man who will talk to you about tunnels versus open yards until your friend tugs your sleeve. That man is the archive.

Festivals are also how a valley teaches its own children that the work is visible. A kid who only sees figs as a thing that makes the house smell in August may, on a festival Friday, see a buyer, a banner, a map. Some of those children will leave anyway. Some will stay because they saw the crop become a public fact, not a private chore.

There is always a tension between the gold and the dust. The gold is the extra class, the gift box, the fruit a minister can hold without wiping his hand. The dust is the actual week: ants, dew cloths, a cousin who did not come to turn the trays. A good festival lets both onto the grounds. A bad one is only gold, and then the photographs go to a tourism site and the growers go back to a price that did not attend the party.

I am not against the brass band. A harvest that feeds a republic’s export column is allowed a parade. I am against the sentence that says the festival “celebrates ancient tradition” and then shows a plate that could have been styled in a studio in another country. Show the *kerevet*. Show the count. Show the woman who can grade in her sleep. If the band must play, let it play where she can hear it and keep working.

A festival week is also when a price gets said out loud in front of people who do not usually hear it. That can be more useful than a queen. Listen for the number. Write the number. Leave the costume in the caption if you must, but put the number in the text.
