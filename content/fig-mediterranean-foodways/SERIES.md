# Fig Mediterranean Foodways — staged magazine series

Desk copy for a cultural food-history series on the common fig (`Ficus carica`) around the Mediterranean basin. This is **not** a wellness vertical. The fruit is treated as a crop, a trade good, a feast food, and a kitchen memory.

## Status

Every draft in this folder is **`status: staged`**. Staged means:

- accepted onto the desk for edit, fact-check, and art
- not scheduled, not published, not syndicated
- claims stay inside food history, technique, markets, and ritual tables
- medical, supplement, and “superfood” language is out of scope

Do not promote a staged file because the folder looks finished. The folder is a pile.

## Voice

Human magazine, not encyclopedia and not brand journal.

- Open on a place, a tool, a smell, or a quarrel. Do not open on a thesis sentence.
- Prefer verbs that belong to work: split, string, grade, cover, press, crate, bargain.
- Name the tray, the town, the cultivar, the feast. Vague “Mediterranean tradition” is a failure.
- Short sentences earn the long ones. Let a paragraph breathe, then cut.
- First person is rare and only when the narrator is clearly a guest at a table.
- Do not flatter the reader. Do not lecture the reader. Do not sell the fruit.

Banned furniture: *rich tapestry, delve, embark, journey (unless a ship is actually moving), it's important to note, in conclusion, moreover, furthermore, unlock, superfood, packed with, boost, support digestion, ancient wisdom* as a slogan.

## Claims policy

Allowed: cultivation, drying, packing, grades, prices as history, feast menus, regional dishes, literary and liturgical mentions, humoral *history* clearly framed as old theory.

Not allowed: disease claims, nutrient-as-medicine copy, weight-loss framing, modern supplement talk, invented clinical studies.

If an older source praises figs as a remedy, quote the source as a period voice. Do not convert it into advice.
