This piece exists to save an art desk from a lawsuit with botany.

The common fig is *Ficus carica*, a tree fruit, a hollow flower pretending to be a fruit, the object of every other draft in this folder. The prickly pear is *Opuntia*, a New World cactus that the Mediterranean adopted with enthusiasm after the sixteenth century and then named, in several languages, as if it were a fig. *Fico d’India*. *Higo chumbo*. *Bajtra* in Malta. *Kermus* and other local words along the Maghreb. It has a season, a market cry, a way of staining a shirt a color that will not come out, a harvest that requires gloves and a fire or a scrape to get the glochids off so a child can eat. It is a real foodway — jam, a summer stall, a landscape of pads that holds a dry hill together. It is not this series.

Why the theft of a name? Because peasants and merchants name by use and look, not by lineage. A sweet fruit you can dry or jam or eat with wet hands will pick up the nearest famous noun. The common fig was already famous. The cactus moved in and borrowed the fame, the way a new neighbor borrows a cup and then keeps it.

Malta in summer is a bajtra island; the common fig exists but does not own the cry. Sicily in late summer is a red and orange landscape of pads that photographers love because they look like the island “being itself.” Those photographs are beautiful and wrong under a headline about Aydın *kerevet* or Cosenza *crocette*. I have seen the mistake in otherwise careful magazines: a cactus fruit, a caption that says fig, a PDO name that belongs to a tree. The comments, for once, were right.

Ballarò will sell both. A good seller will let you ask which. A tired seller will shrug. The shrug is how errors enter print.

If we ever run a sister series on *Opuntia*, it should have its own IMAGE_SOURCES, its own feasts, its own gloves. Until then, the rule is simple: no spines in the fig well. No pads. No “Sicilian figs” caption on a cactus. The common fig has enough work, and the cactus deserves not to be a stand-in.

A reader who came for *carica* and got a cactus has been toyed with. Do not toy. The Mediterranean is already confusing — two shores, a dozen names for one tray. Our job is to keep the names honest so the dishes can be, and so an art desk does not have to apologize to a botanist in the letters page.
