The fifteenth of August is a religious date and a horticultural one, and the two have been shaking hands for a long time.

Ferragosto empties cities and fills tables. Rome leaves itself. Calabria receives cousins. A coastal apartment that was a closet in March becomes a republic of mattresses. In fig country the table wants the first real crop, not the early teasers that tasted like a rumor in July. The *breba* — the first fig, the one on last year’s wood — has its fans, the people who like a slightly grassy sweetness and a story about waiting. The feast wants the *forniti*, the main crop, the ones that taste like the tree meant it and will stain a napkin in a way that cannot be negotiated.

Assumption processions and beach traffic do not care about your orchard. The orchard cares about the day because guests will come, or you will go, and a house without figs in the bowl looks like a house that forgot the month. You put out the fruit that will not keep. You put out cheese that can sit. You argue about whether to light a stove. Usually you do not. Heat is already the cook. A tomato, oil, bread, the fruit. If someone insists on a roast they are performing city manners in a month that does not want them.

On the Adriatic the same week is a drying anxiety. Fruit that will be *smokvenjak* is not yet dry; it is still a tray and a superstition about rain. Fruit that will be eaten tonight is in a bowl that cannot be near the tray or a child will confuse the two economies. The split is a household split: which tree is for now, which rack is for December. Children understand now. Adults pretend the tray is more virtuous, then eat from the bowl anyway.

I have eaten figs on the fifteenth that were perfect and figs that were a week early because someone was proud of a tree. The proud ones are a little crisp at the skin and a little hollow at the center, like a speech. They still disappear. A feast is not a tasting. A feast is a permission to be inaccurate. Someone will say the ones in September are better. Someone will be right. Someone will still have emptied the bowl.

If you photograph Ferragosto, photograph the empty bowl at four in the afternoon, the wasps doing their own procession, the nap that is not optional. The full bowl at noon is advertising. The empty one is the foodway completing a sentence. Leave the procession to the church and the traffic to the coast. The fig’s feast is the stain.
