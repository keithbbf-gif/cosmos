Egypt is a fig country in the tables that count tons, and a fig country in a more complicated way than a PDO map.

The common fig — *teen* — grows, sells fresh in its week along the coast and in gardens that still have room, dries, becomes a jam a grandmother will call by a name that is only a verb, becomes a winter bag in a Cairo shop that also sells dates because of course it does. Alexandria will give you a market morning that feels like the rest of the sea: crates, shouts, a fruit that will not last the tram if you are proud. The Delta gardens will give you a quieter tree and a child who eats standing up. None of this needs a pharaoh costume. The costume is how a food gets stolen from its shop.

The sycamore fig, *Ficus sycomorus*, is the relative that gets the tomb paintings and the shade in the village square. It is a different species. It is not the drying fig of Aydın and it is not a substitute in the jam pot if you were expecting *carica*. The fruit is a different sweetness, a different seed, a different story about who sat under what. It is part of the Egyptian plant memory and should be named so we do not smash two fruits into one caption. A food-history series that cannot tell them apart is not yet a series. It is a mood board.

Winter *teen* in a city shop is often an import as much as a local, because cities eat more than their trees. Turkish lots, Levantine lots, a bag that does not confess. That is allowed. A foodway includes the bag that came from elsewhere and became breakfast anyway. Istanbul does the same with Aydın. Marseille does the same with a Maghrebi bin. Honesty is naming the crate, not pretending every fig is a Nile fig.

I like a simple Egyptian plate: dried figs, a white cheese, bread, tea that has been poured more than once. No spice lecture. No “ancient Egyptians ate…” unless you have a text and even then put it in a footnote. The lecture is the light on a table that has held the same idea in other decades. If a writer must mention antiquity, let them mention a market tax or a painting of fruit, then come back to the shop. The shop is where the living chew is. Buy the bag. Eat it before you write the dynasty.
