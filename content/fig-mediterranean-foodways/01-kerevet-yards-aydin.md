By late August the valley smells like a bakery that forgot to close. Not bread. Something darker, almost tobacco at the edges, honey if you stand in the wind. The fig trees on the slopes above the Büyük Menderes have already done the impolite part of harvest: they drop the fruit. Nobody climbs for the drying crop the way you climb for a table fig. The good ones let go.

What happens next looks, to a visitor, like laundry. Wooden or plastic frames — *kerevet* — sit in a yard that gets sun and a little air and as little dust as a farmyard can manage. The figs go down in a single layer. That rule is not folklore. Stack them and you make a bruise that later becomes a grade argument. Leave a wet pocket and you make a smell that later becomes a rejection.

Sarılop is the name the trade uses. Thin skin, a pale that wants to be gold, a center that reads as honey if you are being kind and as jam if you are being honest. In the EU paper that protects Aydın İnciri, the fruit is allowed a degree of poetry and then a count: too many pieces per kilo and you are no longer talking about the same object. The yard knows this before the lawyer does. Hands sort while the sun is still a worker and not a tourist.

The drying is short if the weather holds. Two days, three. Moisture walks down from the wet, half-dry state the fruit had on the tree — locals will call some of those fallen ones sour figs, *ekşi*, not as an insult but as a stage — toward something that can sit in a crate without collapsing. Twenty-two to twenty-four percent water is the number the extension agents like. The yard does not carry a lab. It carries a pinch, a bend, a look at the skin.

You can tell a serious yard from a tired one by the evenings. A tired yard leaves the fruit out for dew to find. A serious one piles and covers, then spreads again at first light. That rhythm is older than the plastic tunnels that now crop up along the same roads, netted against rain and wasps and the kind of humidity that turns a week of work into a shrug. The tunnels are not a betrayal. August has been failing people in new ways. The *kerevet* is still the idea: air under the fruit, sun on the fruit, a person who will turn it.

Children learn the job by being in the way. A tray is a good height for a small hand to ruin. Someone older will put the ruined fig in a different basket without a speech. There are always other baskets. Paste, livestock, the neighbor who makes pekmez and does not mind a split. The export tray is a narrower church.

Stand in İncirliova — the name is not subtle; it means the fig valley — and the geography starts to explain the taste. Alluvial ground, a slope that drinks the afternoon, winters that are wet enough and summers that are rude enough. The same cultivar planted in a damper pocket comes off the tray duller, as if it had been thinking about something else. This is why the PDO language bothers to draw a map: Sarayköy to Söke, then the Küçük Menderes cousins at Ödemiş and Tire. A fig is a local argument that got lucky.

The factory, if you need the word, is this rectangle of light. No stainless, no shift horn. A radio, maybe. A dog that has opinions about the cover cloth. When the fruit is ready it leaves the yard the way it arrived, in someone else's hands, toward a shed where the counting starts. The yard does not follow it. The yard is already thinking about tomorrow's drop.
