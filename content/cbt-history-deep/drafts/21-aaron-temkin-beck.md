**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Aaron Temkin Beck was born on 18 July 1921 in Providence and died on 1 November 2021. The survey pack already has a figure essay that walks the life. This page is a deepen: three objects he left that a workforce still lives inside — an inventory (1961), a book (1967), an institute (1994) — and the tape that made students fail in public.

He trained in medicine at Yale, in psychiatry, and in psychoanalysis at the Philadelphia institutes, and he spent the working decades that matter at the University of Pennsylvania. The path is hospital-shaped. Cognitive therapy's manners come from that path. So does its later ease with a score.

## 1961, a paper that escaped

"An Inventory for Measuring Depression," with Ward, Mendelson, Mock, and Erbaugh, *Archives of General Psychiatry*, 1961. Later revisions are a publishing history. The historical object is the first public instrument. It let a clinic talk to itself in a number. It let a later waiting room make a ritual of a number. Beck knew a score was not a person. The later industry of outcomes sometimes forgot.

The 1963 and 1964 "Thinking and Depression" papers are the theory's first clothes: idiosyncratic content; a therapy that might address it. A deepen pack keeps those clothes on the table so the inventory cannot talk over them. A number without a theory of thinking is a clerk's tool. A theory without a number, in a 1960s psychiatry department, was a philosophy. He wanted both.

## 1967 and 1979, two social classes of book

*Depression* (Hoeber, 1967) is a colleague's volume. *Cognitive Therapy of Depression* (Guilford, 1979), with Rush, Shaw, and Emery, is a workforce technology. *Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders* (1976) sits between them as a widening. Burns's *Feeling Good* (1980) is a bookstore object. Judith Beck's *Basics and Beyond* (1995) is a teaching-shop hardback. Confusing these classes is how a brochure writes "Beck" as if it were one pamphlet.

He kept seeing patients into old age. Biographies and the 2021 obituaries describe a man more interested in a single belief than in a keynote. That temperament is why a session could be taped. Visibility invited imitation, including the worksheet that never looks up. The imitation is not his best student.

The Cognitive Therapy Scale (Young and Beck, 1980) made the tape a number. Competence as a number is how a school becomes a guild that can fail you. It is also how a school becomes a franchise. He lived long enough to see both.

## 1994, a shop with his daughter

He and Judith S. Beck founded the Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy in 1994 in the Philadelphia area. The institute's own dated pages say the early years were still mostly clinical hours; training grew. A founder shop has to decide, after the founder, how much curiosity survives a barcode. CT-R — recovery-oriented cognitive therapy, late work with Paul Grant, Ellen Inverso, and colleagues — is one late answer: take the model into rooms the 1979 depression sequence was not written for. Reception was mixed. Late extensions are.

He did not claim biology was a fiction. He claimed a person could still work with what the mind was doing while the biology was being addressed, or while it was not. Rush's name on 1979 is that handshake. STAR*D is a later graph in the same family, not a conversion story.

## Not Ellis, not a kinship title

Ellis had already been interrupting musts. Beck's temperament was quieter. American hospitals preferred the tester. This pack will not crown a single inventor. It will keep 1955 and 1967 on the same shelf.

Obituaries reached for "grandfather." The style guide usually bans the kinship. In his case the nickname at least names a lineage that now includes people who have never opened 1967. Open it. Then notice when a rural hour uses a thought record *instead of* a relationship. The 1957 Rogers paper and the 1979 manual were never a forced choice.

WOW Therapies does not inherit his license. This page does not teach a triad. The sibling era essays (`beck-leaves-the-couch`, `depression-book-1967`, `depression-manual-1979`, `after-beck-2021`) hold the years. This figure holds the man who wrote them down.

Providence, a Jewish household, a brother who died in the 1918 influenza — mention without a key. Yale, Cushing, Penn: a medical path, not a California weekend. He left an analytic hypothesis about hostility under sadness because the sentences did not match. He did not leave the idea that another person is in the room. Students who leave that idea have left him even if they keep his initials.

## A hallway at Penn, and a tape

Students remember a man who would stop a tape to ask what the hot sentence was. Stopping a tape is a pedagogy. Pedagogy is how a 1967 book became a 1979 workforce and then a 1994 shop. This deepen page exists so the pedagogy does not become the only memory. He also wrote. Writing is slower than a tape. The 1963 and 1964 papers are still slower than a waiting-room form. Read slowly.

He kept seeing people into old age. That fact, repeated in obituaries, is not a protocol. It is a temperament: the work was still a Tuesday, not a keynote. Keynotes flattened him into a kinship title. Tuesdays did not. A rural clinician who still takes one sentence seriously, and still looks up, is closer to the hallway than a slide that says grandfather.

Judith Beck's shop had to keep the hallway's curiosity after the hallway closed. CT-R colleagues had to keep it in rooms the 1979 sequence never drew. Both keepings are collaborative. Collaboration is the 1979 lesson. 2021 should not unteach it. Providence remains the birth city, not a key to the triad. Yale remains a medical school, not a weekend. The leaving remains a hypothesis about hostility, not a leaving of the other person in the chair.
