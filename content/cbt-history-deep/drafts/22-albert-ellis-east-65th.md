**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Albert Ellis was born on 27 September 1913 in Pittsburgh, grew up in New York, and died on 24 July 2007. The survey pack already walks the life. This deepen stays with the address: a Beaux-Arts house on East 65th Street, Friday-night demonstrations, a 2005 board removal, a 2006 court file that put him back on the board without making the house kind again.

He began practicing a named therapy in 1955. He published the Adlerian-debt paper in 1957. He wrote *A Guide to Rational Living* with Robert Harper in 1961 and *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy* in 1962. He founded an institute in 1959 and bought the house not long after. The house is why the naming year has a stage.

## A parlor that taught America a volume

For decades the Friday-night live demonstrations were the public ritual: a volunteer, a demand, an audience that laughed or winced or learned. Theater is democratic. It is also a risk. Humiliation as technique is the risk. He believed people could stand a direct hit to their favorite sentence. Some could. A history that only quotes the one-liners is doing his worst work for him.

He liked Epictetus and liked to say so. He liked sex research and wrote about it in a decade that punished that writing. He liked to swear in session. Students either heard the precision under the volume or they fled. The early sexological career is not prurience for this page. It is the temperament: he was already fighting American respectability before the therapy had a three-letter name.

The ABC diagram is what students remember. This page will not reprint it as homework. It is easy to teach as if the activating event were a pebble. A person in a violent home is not suffering only from a sentence. On his better days he knew that. On his worse days the volume won.

## September 2005, a vote without a notice

On 18 September 2005 the board of the Albert Ellis Institute voted, after stepping into another room, to remove him as a trustee. He was ninety-two. He lived upstairs. The Friday nights had already been suspended. Benedict Carey's *New York Times* piece (11 October 2005) and a *New York* magazine feature made the fight public: medical costs, "excess benefits," a tax-exempt fear, a founder who would not go quietly.

In 2006 a New York Supreme Court justice held the removal invalid for want of notice and a chance to be heard (*Ellis v. Broder*). Reinstatement on paper is not the same as a parlor. He died the next year. Founder shops eat founders. The clinical idea does not need East 65th Street. It needs a clinician who can hear a demand pretending to be a fact, and who can say so without becoming a second tyrant.

This pack will not do gossip. The court file and the 2005 reporting are public objects. Health speculation is banned. The historical use of 2005 is modest: an address is not a method, and a method that lived as theater can become, in a quieter room, either a relief or a new shame. Training is the difference. This page is not the training.

## What traveled, what stayed in Manhattan

Named REBT remained smaller than the CBT workforce that took Beck's manners and some of Ellis's targets. The Albert Ellis Institute survived as a training shop with a myth attached. Like every founder shop, it has to decide whether the myth is the method. The useful remainder does not require his vocabulary of insults. It requires an ear.

A Friday-night demonstration is not a rural Arkansas hour. The transplant problem is real. Rural hours may borrow the question — where is the must? — without the roast. Borrowing the roast is impersonation. Impersonation is not history's job to teach.

WOW Therapies does not, by dating the house, claim an REBT service. The sibling era essay (`ellis-names-rebt-1955`) holds 1955. The Stoic and Adler essays hold the timber. This figure holds the parlor and the court file: a New York story that explains why a loud American clinic became a myth, and why the demand he named outlived the subway stop.

He wrote too many books. The 1962 volume is the one this pack names first. Later editions restated the same fight with new cultural examples. The fight did not get quieter. The American "must" did not either.

## A 1962 page, and a 2006 order

Open *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy* to the places he thanks Adler and quotes a Stoic line. Those pages are the timber essays in this pack. Then remember that the same man ran a parlor that taught America a volume. Timber and volume are not the same object. Histories that keep only the volume make him a clown. Histories that keep only the timber make him a footnote to Beck. He was a founder with an address, a court file, and a demand that outlived the address.

The 2006 order that invalidated the September 2005 removal is a dry piece of not-for-profit law. Dry law is how a ninety-two-year-old man got his board seat back on paper. Paper is not a Friday night. He died in 2007. This pack will not turn the last two years into a soap. It will say founder shops eat founders, and that the eating is in the *Times* and in a docket, and that the demand he named does not need the parlor in order to be heard in a quieter room — if the quieter room has an ear and not a second tyrant.

Pittsburgh is the birth city. New York is the weather. East 65th Street is the stage. 1955 is the name. Keep the four. Refuse the roast as a homework.
