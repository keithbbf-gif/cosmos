**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

George Alexander Kelly was born in 1905 and died in 1967, the year Beck's *Depression* appeared. He taught at Ohio State. In 1955 W. W. Norton issued *The Psychology of Personal Constructs* in two volumes. Ellis, that same year, began naming REBT. Beck was still an analyst. Three cognitive doors, one year. American textbooks later kept two.

The era essay (`kelly-personal-constructs-1955`) holds the year. This page holds the man who would not write a pamphlet.

## A psychologist who treated a person as a scientist

His fundamental postulate — that a person's processes are psychologically channelized by the ways they anticipate events — is a sentence that refuses a slogan. Constructs come in pairs. They have a range of convenience. They can be tight or permeable. A clinician, in his telling, tried to understand the system well enough to invite an experiment.

Fixed-role therapy asked a person to live briefly as if they were someone written on a sheet. This page will not teach it. The dare was philosophical: identity as a draft. Departments that wanted a trial could not have one in 1955. List-makers in the 1990s could not photocopy a 1979-shaped manual out of the two volumes. Personal construct psychology kept a society, a journal, and a British afterlife (Bannister, Fransella) larger than its American textbook share.

Beck's later "collaborative empiricism" is a cousin one can hear across a hallway. Both men asked a person to treat a belief as a hypothesis. Kelly got there from a theory that wanted to replace trait psychology. Beck got there from depressions that did not match a hostility script. Cousin is not citation. This style guide will not make Kelly a grandfather.

## Ohio State, not a parlor

He was not Ellis. There is no East 65th Street myth. There is a Midwestern department and a book that needed two volumes because the psychology would not fit one. Students remember a man who assigned more than a test. The Role Construct Repertory Test is the instrument that escaped. Instruments escape. Theories wait. American clinical psychology often keeps the instrument and wallpapers the theory. This pack files the theory so the wallpaper does not win.

He did not live to see DSM-III, the 1979 manual, or Division 12's lists. Dying in 1967, he missed the decade that would have asked him for a sequence a camera could see. Whether he would have written one is a parlor game. This pack does not play it. It says the 1955 volumes remain a way to see what a cognitive clinic might have sounded like if it had stayed a psychology of anticipation.

## How a practice site should use him

As a neighbor, not as a service name. WOW Therapies does not offer personal-construct therapy by publishing this essay. No grid. No "try a new self." If a graduate seminar still assigns him, it is often as "context." Context is a polite word for a book the workforce did not need. Need is not truth.

The British afterlife matters. IAPT did not staff Kelly. Occupational psychologists and a set of UK clinicians did. "What counted as CBT" is a later administrative category. He did not apply for it. Filing him as precursor is a courtesy. He asked for a psychology. Courtesies should not erase the ask.

A rural reader who has never heard his name has still met the weather: the idea that a person might be wrong in a patterned way, and that the pattern might be testable. Beck wrote that weather down as data. Ellis shouted at it as a demand. Kelly wrote it as a system of constructs. 1955 is crowded if you look. Looking is this URL's job.

He wrote other papers; the two volumes are the object this pack will keep naming. Later PCP handbooks are a guild's afterlife. Afterlives are allowed. They are not 1955. Date the book. Refuse the handout.

## 1905–1967, a life that missed the list-world

He was born before Ellis, died the year Beck's depression book appeared, and never saw DSM-III or Division 12's tables. A psychology of anticipation did not apply for those later tickets. Later courtesy filed him as precursor. Courtesy is not what he asked. He asked for two volumes and a person treated as a scientist of their own forecasts.

Bannister and Fransella's British afterlife is the proof that 1955 did not need Penn in order to stay alive. IAPT did not staff him. Occupational psychologists did. "CBT" as an administrative category arrived after he was gone and did not invite the two volumes to the procurement meeting. This plaque is the invitation a procurement meeting will not send. Come for the year. Do not leave with a grid.

Ohio State remains the working address this pack will print. Other campus years, if a later editor verifies them, may be added. Do not invent a parlor he did not run. He ran a department and a book. Departments are quieter than East 65th Street. Quiet is not absence.

A 1960 doctoral student at Ohio State who underlined "range of convenience" and never ran a trial is the 1955 book's real first audience. Audiences like that do not make lists. They make a society that outlived him in Britain. This plaque is for that audience, not for a procurement meeting. Two volumes. A quiet department. No parlor. No grid.

A 1955 Norton invoice for two volumes is a comic historical object and a serious one: someone had to pay for a psychology that would not pamphlet. Payments like that do not impress list-makers. They impress this pack. Quiet department. Dead in 1967. British afterlife. No grid on this URL.
