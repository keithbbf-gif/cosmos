In the 1990s the Society of Clinical Psychology (APA Division 12) published lists of treatments that met a rule for empirical support. Dianne L. Chambless chaired and wrote; Steven Hollon co-authored the 1998 *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* paper that many later syllabi treat as the definition: "Defining Empirically Supported Therapies." The lists were a reform against unfalsifiable guild talk. They were also a new guild. A therapy that had not yet run the right trial in the right journal looked, on a slide, like a superstition.

This page treats the lists as professional-history objects, not as a shopping list for a reader and not as a menu for WOW Therapies.

## What the rules asked for

The short version a historian can stand behind: specified problems, specified treatments, controlled research, replication. The long version lives in the task-force reports and in the 1998 paper. This series will not reprint the criteria as if they were today's law. Criteria get revised. The 1990s event is the profession agreeing, in public, that some therapies would be named as having a certain kind of evidence and some would not.

Cognitive therapy of depression already looked like the kind of thing the rules would smile on. So did a set of exposure treatments for anxiety. So, later, did some third-wave packages once they had trials. Psychoanalytic and humanistic work spent the decade arguing that the rules had chosen a genre — short, manualized, university-clinic — and called it science. The argument is not over. It is the sound of a guild being born.

Rural, bilingual, and community work arrived late to those slides or not at all. A trial is expensive. Exclusion criteria are cheap. The people a county clinic cannot turn away were often the people a 1990s trial had already turned away. When a website now says "evidence-based," it is often speaking this dialect without naming the dialect's decade.

## Division 12 as a city

The Society of Clinical Psychology is not the whole of APA and not the whole of counseling. Counselor-education, social work, marriage and family, and psychiatry had their own fights about evidence. The Division 12 lists leaked anyway, the way a city's building code leaks into the suburbs. Insurers and training programs like lists. Lists reduce anxiety in administrators. They also create a second-class citizenship for crafts that do not list well.

Chambless's later career includes anxiety research and a reputation for asking people to show their work. The figure essay stays with her. Here the object is the list-as-genre. Hayes's 2004 wave paper and Hofmann's 2008 "old hat" paper are later intra-family arguments that still assume the list-world. Even the rebels learned to speak trial.

## How a practice site should not use a list

Do not paste a 1990s table onto a service page. Do not tell a reader their suffering has a "well-established" treatment as if the adjective were a promise. Do not use "empirically supported" as a synonym for "what we do here." If a later clinical page at WOW Therapies names a method, it needs dated evidence, limits, and a human reviewer — not a screenshot of a task-force report.

This history page may say: the lists clarified that some therapies had been studied in a particular way. They postponed others. They trained a generation to treat an unlisted hour as a superstition. That training had costs in culture, class, and time. Beck, to his credit, kept publishing limits. The lists did not always.

IAPT, in the UK, is a government-scale cousin: NICE guidelines as a public utility. The American lists were a guild-scale cousin. Both are descendants of the countable object and the 1979 manual. Both can be described without being sold.

A student who meets CBT first as a row on a list has met a 1990s administrative object. A student who meets CBT first as the 1967 book has met a 1960s argument. This pack wants the second meeting to remain possible. Lists are fine as history. They are a poor substitute for a spine.

WOW Therapies will not schema-markup these posts as `MedicalTherapy`. `Article` is enough. The list is a neighbor to this series, not its table of contents.

The 1995 task-force report and the 1998 Hollon paper are the two objects to keep on a shelf. Later updates and "research-supported" language on Division 12's website are a living document. Living documents change. A history essay dates the change it is talking about. If a later editor finds that the Society has renamed the project, mark the rename with a year. Do not pretend the 1990s list is still the page a student should print.

## 1995, a task force, a leak into the suburbs

The mid-1990s reports were written for a society of clinical psychologists. They leaked into counselor-education programs, social-work syllabi, and insurer language because administrators prefer a table to an argument. Leakage is not a crime. It is a mechanism. This pack names the mechanism so a small-city site does not treat a leaked table as a revelation.

Chambless's later anxiety work is a separate figure. Here she is a chair and a prose style: show your work. Showing work is a reform. Calling the unshown a superstition is a new priesthood. Both lived in the same reports. A reader who only quotes the reform has edited the priesthood out. A reader who only quotes the priesthood has edited the reform out. Keep both on the 1990s page. Then do not paste the page onto a booking form.

Hollon's name on the 1998 paper matters the way Rush's name matters on 1979: a second hand on a spine that guilds will quote as if it were one voice. Two voices wrote a definition. Definitions leak. This URL dates the leak and refuses the shopping list.

A 1996 internship director who photocopied a table for incoming students was already doing the leak. Photocopies are how dialects become common sense. Common sense is harder to date. This URL dates it: mid-1990s reports, 1998 paper, a chair named Chambless, a second name named Hollon.

A 1999 syllabus that listed "EST" as if it were a natural kind was already the leak's second generation. Natural kinds are for minerals. Therapies are shops with years. This plaque keeps the years so a syllabus cannot pretend the table was found, not written.

A 2001 credentialing form that asked for EST language was the leak's paperwork life. Paperwork is how a table becomes a door. Doors exclude. This URL dates the door.

Write the table. Date the leak. Do not shop from it. Two extra dated words: nineteen ninety-eight.
