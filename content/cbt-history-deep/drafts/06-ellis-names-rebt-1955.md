**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

1955 is a naming year, not a thunderclap. Albert Ellis, already a psychologist who had written about sex in a decade that punished that writing, began to practice a therapy he would call Rational Psychotherapy, then Rational-Emotive, then Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy. The 1957 paper in the *Journal of Individual Psychology* is the early public object. *A Guide to Rational Living* (with Robert A. Harper, 1961) is the public book. *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy* (1962) is the professional book. The later institute on East 65th Street is a New York address with a myth attached.

The sibling figure essay in this pack stays with the address and the 2005 board fight. The survey pack's Ellis essay is the biography. This page stays with the naming: what it meant, in Eisenhower's America, to tell a patient that a *must* was not a fact.

## Before the name, a quarrel with respectability

Ellis had already been in trouble with American manners. The early sexological writing is not a footnote for prurience. It is the temperament: a man who had decided the culture's demands were the problem before he had a three-letter therapy. REBT's bluntness is that temperament in a new room. Students who meet only the ABC slogan miss the earlier fight.

He trained as a clinician in a city still dominated, in its prestige clinics, by analytic furniture. He did not wait for a trial to authorize a sentence. That impatience is why later CBT — which did wait, and filmed the waiting — could take his targets and decline his manners. The 1955 naming is the moment the impatience became a school, even if the school was still one man and a set of phrases.

Kelly's two volumes landed the same year. Wolpe's book was three years away. Beck was still listening for hostility under depression. 1955 is crowded if you look. Ellis made it look like a one-man show. Showmanship is part of the historical object. It is also the part that travels worst into a quiet rural hour.

## Two books, two distortions

The 1961 Harper collaboration made REBT sound easy: a reader, a chair, a set of arguments with a demand. The 1962 book made it sound like a theory. The Friday-night demonstrations, once the institute had a parlor, made it sound like a roast. Between those three distortions sat a clinician who could hear a preference pretending to be a law of the universe.

This series will not reprint an ABC worksheet. The diagram — activating event, belief, consequence — is easy to teach as if the event were a pebble. A person in a violent home is not suffering only from a sentence. Ellis, on his better days, knew that. On his worse days the volume won. The 1955 naming does not settle which day you get. Training does, and this page is not training.

The later CBT mainstream preferred Beck's medical-school quiet and some of Ellis's enemies (the catastrophic *must*, the demand for love). Named REBT remained smaller, stubborn, still offering workshops. A history that treats REBT as "early CBT" has performed a takeover. A history that treats CBT as "late REBT" has performed the inverse. They are neighbors who did not share a supervisor.

## Why a practice site needs the naming year

Brochures like origin stories. 1955 is a true year and a misleading one. True: Ellis began practicing a named, argumentative therapy then. Misleading: as if the later workforce, the BDI, the 1979 manual, and IAPT were already in the room. They were not. The naming is a New York event. The workforce is a later American-British administrative event.

WOW Therapies does not, by dating 1955, claim an REBT service. If a later clinical page names a method, that page needs review. Here the claim is only: before Penn wrote the thoughts down as data, a psychologist in New York was already interrupting them as demands. The interruption had Adlerian timber and a Stoic slogan. It did not yet have a hospital manners.

A reader who has only met "CBT" on an insurer form can be forgiven for thinking there was one invention. There were at least two, and they disliked each other's tone. The dislike is useful. It reminds a clinic that a sentence can be tested quietly or attacked loudly, and that neither tone is the same thing as a relationship. Rogers's 1957 paper — another 1950s object — sits on the same decade's shelf. The empirical turn did not erase it. Slide decks did.

## 1959, a house, and a long Friday

Ellis founded an institute in 1959 and bought the Beaux-Arts house on East 65th Street not long after, with royalties that biographies tie to *The Art and Science of Love*. The house is why the naming year has a later stage. For decades the Friday-night live demonstrations were the public ritual: a volunteer, a demand, an audience. When the board suspended those nights in 2005, they were not only cutting a budget item. They were turning off the ritual that had taught America what REBT sounded like.

The 1955 naming did not require the house. The house required the naming. That order matters. A therapy can begin as a practice in a smaller room and become an address. Addresses eat founders. The 2005–2006 court file is the late proof. The 1955 year is the early one: a man deciding that American "musts" were a clinical object. The object outlived the address. Rural hours still meet it. They do not need the parlor to hear a demand. They do need a clinician who will not become a second tyrant in the room.

This page will not give that clinician a script. It will leave the year on the table: 1955, a name, a quarrel already in progress.

## 1957, a journal still willing to print Adler's name

The *Journal of Individual Psychology* paper is the object a historian can put in a footnote without the house. It admits the timber. It also shows that in 1957 there was still a readership for Adler's remaining American guild. Ellis used that readership and then outgrew it. Outgrowing is not betrayal. It is how a school leaves a smaller room. The 1961 public book and the 1962 professional book are the next rooms. The Friday-night parlor is a later room. 1955 is the hallway.

A reader who meets REBT only as "early CBT" has skipped this hallway. Skipping is how takeovers happen. This deepen pack exists to keep the hallway on the calendar: a naming year, a debt, a volume that hospitals would not prefer. Beck's notes were already being written. They were not yet a school. Two schools can share a decade without sharing a supervisor. 1955 is Ellis's year. 1967 will be Beck's book-year. Both years remain true if you do not flatten them.
