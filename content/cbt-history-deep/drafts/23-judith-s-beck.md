Judith S. Beck is living (verified 2026). This page uses public books and the Institute's own dated institutional history. No health speculation. No scraped portraits. No invented credentials.

She is a psychologist, a teacher, and — with her father, Aaron T. Beck — co-founder of the Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy (1994). *Cognitive Therapy: Basics and Beyond* (Guilford, 1995; later editions retitled *Cognitive Behavior Therapy: Basics and Beyond*) is the hardback a generation of students actually opened. The 1967 Hoeber volume stayed the book you were supposed to have read. The 1995 book was the book in the bag.

## A teaching shop, not a personality cult

Second-generation stories are easy to sentimentalize (the daughter) or to dismiss (not the inventor). Both moves are lazy. A school that wants a workforce needs someone who can write the hour for a stranger who did not sit in Penn in 1963. She did that writing. She also kept saying, in public teaching, that a relationship is what makes a thought testable. Students who treat her book as a form have skipped the sentences that refuse the form.

The Institute's own history says the 1990s began with more clinical hours than training hours, then inverted as demand grew. That inversion is the occupational story of CBT after the lists and after managed care: the world asked for trained people faster than a single department could mint them. A nonprofit shop in the Philadelphia area became an international address. International addresses have barcodes. Barcodes are not a sin. They are a test of curiosity.

She has served as the Institute's president. Allen Miller and others appear in later institutional pages as staff. This pack will not invent an org chart. It will say a founder shop that outlives one founder still has a living one, and that living people are not public property.

## What 1995 changed

The 1979 manual assumed a certain reader. The 1995 teaching book assumed a student who needed the hour broken into something a supervisor could watch. Young and Beck's 1980 Cognitive Therapy Scale had already made the tape a number. *Basics and Beyond* made the number's world portable. Portability is how a rural trainee meets Penn without moving to Penn. It is also how a worksheet travels without a face.

Later editions added "Behavior" to the title as the wider house demanded the wider initials. Title changes are historical objects. They show a daughter managing a brand the obituaries would later over-kin. This style guide bans "mother of" as quickly as "father of." She is a writer and a shop-runner. That is enough.

CT-R and other late extensions involved colleagues (Grant, Inverso) as well as the family name. Second-generation figures who share credit are doing the 1979 lesson: spines have more than one name. This page will not unteach it.

Without a booking button attached to her name. Without a downloaded headshot. Without a claim that WOW Therapies "trains in the Beck method" unless a later clinical page can show the training. This history page may say: a 1995 book and a 1994 institute are why "CBT" in a 2020s brochure does not require a dead man's hallway.

The critiques still apply. A teaching shop can export a worksheet that cannot hear a neighborhood. Her better chapters refuse that export. Slide decks that skip those chapters are not her best students either.

WOW Therapies does not, by naming her, claim a supervision relationship. Living people get extra care. Public objects only: the book, the Institute's dated about-pages, the fact of the 1994 founding. If she later publishes a memoir or a dated interview this pack needs, add it to the bibliography with a year. Do not embroider.

A rural supervisor who assigns *Basics and Beyond* and also assigns Rogers 1957 is closer to the 1960s leaving than a supervisor who assigns only a form. She has said versions of that in public teaching. This page will not quote her as if a website had a transcript it does not have. It will say the teaching reputation is the historical object: a second generation that tried to keep the look-up in the hour.

## 1994–1998, hours then training

The Institute's own history says clinical hours dominated the first years and training grew by the late 1990s. That inversion matches the decade: lists, managed care, a world asking for people who could run a grandchild of 1979. She wrote the 1995 book that made the grandchild portable for a student who would never sit at Penn in 1963. Portability is the mixed gift. A rural trainee can meet the hour. A form can travel without a face. Her better chapters refuse the form-without-face. This URL exists so a website that only remembers the form can be told it has skipped the teacher.

Living people get extra care. We have the book, the 1994 founding, the public role as president. We do not have a right to a photograph, a health story, or a fake supervision relationship with WOW Therapies. If she publishes a dated memoir, add it. Until then, the portable hardback and the shop are enough. Second generation is a job, not a sentiment. She did the job. Date it.

A 1996 student who put *Basics and Beyond* in a bag and left Philadelphia without meeting him is the reason the 1995 book exists. Bags are a social fact. This plaque honors the bag without turning it into a form-only memory. She kept writing the look-up into chapters a slide can skip. Do not skip them on this site either. Public objects only: the book, the 1994 shop, the public presidency. Nothing else.
