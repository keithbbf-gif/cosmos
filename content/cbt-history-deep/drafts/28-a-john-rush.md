**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A. John Rush is living (verified 2026). Public papers and books only. No health speculation. No campus scrape. No dosing advice.

His name sits on the spine of *Cognitive Therapy of Depression* (1979) with Beck, Shaw, and Emery. He is a psychiatrist who knew what a "response" looked like on a ward graph. That is why the name matters. The 1979 book is not only a psychologist's sequence. It is a handshake with a medicine that already expected Tuesdays to move a number.

## The 1979 handshake

Pharmacologists and cognitive therapists were sharing depressed inpatients and arguing about the same weeks. Rush's presence on the manual is the argument written as a co-authorship. Later popular histories that say "Beck wrote the manual" have dropped three names. Dropping Rush drops the pill-graph neighbor. This deepen pack will not drop him.

STAR*D — Sequenced Treatment Alternatives to Relieve Depression — reported in the mid-2000s, including a 2006 *American Journal of Psychiatry* paper among others, is a later federal object with his name on it. It is not a CBT trial. It is a sequenced antidepressant program with a controversy about how remission was counted. It belongs here as a reminder that the shared graph did not end in 1979. The appetite to sequence, to step, to define response, continued. IAPT stepped care is a British cousin. STAR*D is an American pill-side cousin. Rush's career sits on the furniture that made both cousins thinkable.

This page will not re-litigate STAR*D's critics. It will say a graph that starts argument is doing a 1980s–2000s job. A website that turns the graph into a shopping list is doing a brochure's job. We are not a brochure.

## Why a CBT pack needs a pharmacologist

Because the empirical turn is unintelligible if talk and pills are written as enemies. They were colleagues and rivals on the same wards. TDCRP (Elkin 1989) put imipramine on the same page as CBT and IPT. Rush's 1979 name is earlier furniture for that page. Enemies make better copy. Colleagues make better history.

He also stands for a limit this pack keeps repeating: a score is not a person. Outcome cultures forget. Psychiatrists who live with scores know the forgetting and still need the score. That tension is the honest one. This URL will not resolve it with a slogan.
