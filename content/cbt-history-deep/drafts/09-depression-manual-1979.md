Guilford published *Cognitive Therapy of Depression* in 1979. The authors were Aaron T. Beck, A. John Rush, Brian F. Shaw, and Gary Emery. The book is the object a later profession means when it says "the manual." It can be taught, supervised, and compared. That is a political fact as well as a clinical one. A therapy that can be written as a manual can be granted. A therapy that cannot is, in a managed system, a hobby.

This page will not reprint the sessions. It will not walk a thought record. It will treat 1979 as a date when a 1967 book grew a workforce technology.

## Four names on a spine

Rush's name matters. He was already a psychiatrist who knew what a "response" looked like on a ward graph. Pharmacologists and cognitive therapists were sharing depressed inpatients and arguing about the same Tuesdays. The later STAR*D trial (Rush et al., 2006, among the reports) is a descendant of that shared graph, not a conversion story in which pills defeated talk or talk defeated pills. The 1979 spine is the earlier handshake.

Shaw and Emery are the other hands: the people who help a model become a sequence someone else can run. Manuals have that sociology. Founders supply the ear. Co-authors supply the week-by-week. Students remember the founder. Historians should remember the week-by-week. Without it there is no trial, and without a trial there is no 1980s federal graph.

The Cognitive Therapy Scale (Young and Beck, 1980) arrived on the manual's heels: a way to watch a tape and say the student missed the cognition. Competence as a number is how a school becomes a guild that can fail you. It is also how a school becomes a franchise. Beck lived long enough to see both. Judith Beck's later *Basics and Beyond* (1995) is the teaching-shop hardback that made the 1979 object portable for a new generation.

## What the manual made possible

The NIMH Treatment of Depression Collaborative Research Program could put cognitive therapy on the same page as interpersonal therapy and imipramine because someone had written the hours down. Elkin et al. (1989) is the graph. 1979 is one reason the graph has a column.

Managed care in the 1990s did not invent the thought record. It recognized a cousin it could authorize in six sessions. University clinics that had spent the 1970s arguing about manuals spent the 1990s arguing about how many sessions a depression trial needed. A history that treats the Penn object as the whole country has repeated the slide deck's error. Rural, bilingual, and community hours arrived late to those arguments or not at all.

The Society of Clinical Psychology's later lists of "empirically supported treatments" needed manuals the way a librarian needs a call number. Chambless and colleagues wrote rules. The 1979 book already looked like a call number. That is luck and design. Design is the four authors. Luck is a profession that had decided, after DSM-III, that countable objects were the kind of objects it would fund.

## What the manual postponed

Meaning. Culture. A childhood that is not a "core belief" page. Psychosis as more than a thought to be tested. The body. The political situation that makes a "negative thought" into a description. Third-wave work would put acceptance and mindfulness back into the behavioral house without returning the keys to 1910 Vienna. Schema therapy would try to house the longer story. None of that erases 1979. It answers it.

There is also the marketing hangover. "Evidence-based" became a brand. A trial in a university clinic with English-speaking outpatients is not a trial in a county with comorbidity and a two-hour drive. The 1979 book is not to blame for every slide that followed. It is the book the slides claimed to have read.

## How to read 1979 on a practice site

WOW Therapies does not sell a protocol here. If a later service page names CBT, that page needs its own review. This page names a turn in the profession's furniture: from a 1967 argument to a 1979 sequence a stranger could be trained to run.

A rural hour that uses the manual as a map is in the building. A rural hour that uses the manual *as* the patient is in a parody. The 1957 Rogers paper is still on the shelf. The 1979 book did not take it off. Training cultures did.

Guilford's brown and later jackets are copyrighted objects. Do not scrape them. The historical honor is to know the year and the four names, and to notice when a waiting-room brochure says "CBT" as if 1979 were a personality.

Brian Shaw's later career as a teacher of the model, and Gary Emery's as a writer of anxiety-adjacent books, are second-generation stories this page will not balloon. The load-bearing fact is the shared spine. Four people agreed to write the hours in an order a camera could see. Cameras invite imitation. Imitation includes the hour that never looks up from the order. The 1979 authors cannot be blamed for every imitation. They can be read as the people who made imitation possible. That is the mixed gift of every manual.

## Guilford, 1979, a brown object

The physical book — later reprints, later jackets — is a copyrighted object this pack will not scrape. The year is the thing we can print. 1979 sits one year before DSM-III and a decade before Elkin's graph. It sits sixteen years before Judith Beck's teaching hardback and fifteen years before the Institute. Those intervals are how a manual becomes a culture. Cultures forget intervals. This URL exists to remember one: the year the hours were written down for strangers.

A rural supervisor who photocopied a chapter in 1991 was already in the culture. A rural supervisor who only ever saw a worksheet was in the culture's thinner end. Both supervisors used 1979. Only one of them had met the four names. Meet the four names. Then look up.

Twelve months later DSM-III would issue the ticket. The manual and the ticket are a pair, not a single invention. Readers who start "CBT" at an insurer form have started at the pair's grandchild. Start at the four names. Then, if you must, look at the ticket. Then look up from both.

A 1981 trainee who watched a tape with the Cognitive Therapy Scale in the other hand was already living in the 1979 building's annex. The annex is 1980. The building is four names. Do not let the annex eat the building on this URL.
