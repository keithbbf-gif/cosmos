**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Stefan G. Hofmann is living (verified 2026). Public papers and the 2018 volume only. No health speculation. No campus scrape. This page will not teach a process list.

With Gordon J. G. Asmundson he wrote "Acceptance and Mindfulness-Based Therapy: New Wave or Old Hat?" (*Clinical Psychology Review*, 2008). The paper asked whether Hayes's 2004 geology was a rupture or exposure-and-cognition with better manners. In 2018 he co-edited *Process-Based CBT* with Hayes. Ten years from skeptic-of-the-last-name to co-owner of a new last name. The era essay holds the fight. This page holds the skeptic who stayed in the family.

## 2008, a review, not a roast

Intra-family arguments can sound like wars on Twitter and look like reviews in *Clinical Psychology Review*. Hofmann and Asmundson wrote a review. Reviews that get cited as takedowns have been flattened. This pack will not flatten. The 2008 question is still a good question: how much of "acceptance" is a new school and how much is a second-wave shop in new clothes? A practice site does not need to pick a champion. It needs to know the fight exists.

Asmundson's name is easy to drop. Do not drop it. Two authors asked the question. Later Hofmann branding is a career. Careers are not 2008.

His day job includes anxiety research in a Barlow-adjacent city of labs. Day jobs keep list-makers and wave-namers honest. A man who runs trials and also writes "old hat" is harder to cartoon as a reactionary. Cartooning is for slide one.

## 2018, a shared spine

Sharing a catalog with Hayes is the historical surprise. Surprises like that are how you know a family is still a family. The 2018 book tried to retire brand-name churches in favor of processes. Processes can become a new church. He knows that; the book is still a syllabus, not a revelation. Syllabi are allowed. Homework is not.

New Harbinger / Context Press is not Guilford. The publishing address is part of the meaning: a contextual house and a broader CBT house agreeing to share chapters. Chapters are not protocols on this URL.

## 2012, a meta-review between the ask and the catalog

Hofmann, Anu Asnaani, Imke J. J. Vonk, Alice T. Sawyer, and Angela Fang published "The Efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy: A Review of Meta-analyses" in *Cognitive Therapy and Research* in 2012. It sits in the interval this plaque exists to keep visible: four years after "New Wave or Old Hat?" with Gordon Asmundson (*Clinical Psychology Review*, 2008) and six years before *Process-Based CBT* with Hayes (Context Press / New Harbinger, 2018). Brochures hate intervals. A 2009 journal club that assigned "old hat" as if it ended a wave was already flattening a review.

The 2012 co-authors are easy to drop from a "Hofmann review." Do not drop them. Meta-analyses are group objects too. Institutional biographies move among Boston and other addresses; verify if you print one.

Living wave skeptic, public papers and the 2018 volume only. No process menu. No Venn diagram as science. No downloaded face. If a later paper changes the slogan, date the change. Do not treat 2018 as the end of the argument.

The 2012 meta-analysis paper is easy to misread as a victory lap for initials. It is closer to an inventory of inventories — a profession counting its own counting. Counting is what happens when a school grows up inside universities that speak in effect sizes. Effect sizes are not a parish sermon. They are a committee language. Hofmann's later co-editorship with Hayes is stranger if you forget the 2012 inventory: the skeptic and the namer shared a shelf after both had spent decades inside the same anxiety city. Cities forgive strange roommates when the lease is tenure. Asmundson's 2008 co-authorship is the earlier tell: the question was always whether a new label hid an old exposure shop, not whether Hayes had invented a new continent. Reviews that ask that question politely are still reviews. Politeness is a genre choice, not a verdict. A 2010 syllabus that assigned only the 2018 catalog and skipped the 2008 review was teaching brand loyalty, not family history. Brand loyalty is a business major. This pack is a calendar major. Calendars start with years, not slogans. Start with 2008. End with a question, not a purchase link.
