*Mind Over Mood* (Guilford, 1995), with Dennis Greenberger, is the workbook a generation of clients and trainees actually touched. The same year Judith Beck issued *Basics and Beyond*. 1995 is a teaching-shop year: two portable objects, two coasts (Huntington Beach; Philadelphia). ## A workbook is a social class of object

The 1967 Hoeber volume was a colleague's book. The 1979 manual was a workforce sequence. *Mind Over Mood* is a book a person in a waiting room might be handed. Handing is a moral choice. It can be a collaboration. It can be a way to send the person home with a task instead of a relationship. Padesky's public teaching has long insisted on collaborative empiricism: two people testing a sentence, not a form testing a person. Students who skip that insistence have skipped her.

Greenberger's name is easy to drop. Do not drop it. Two authors made the 1995 spine. Later editions are a publishing history. Quote the year you mean. Workbooks get written in after a life has already happened. Afterlives include coffee stains. Stains are not a license to reprint the pages here.

She trained clinicians in Socratic questioning as a craft. This page will not teach the craft. Crafts that become YouTube are already too thin. The historical object is a California shop that tried to keep the look-up in an hour a stranger could learn.

## 1995 beside managed care

A portable workbook looks, to a reviewer, like something that can happen in six sessions. Sometimes it can. Sometimes the portability is the muzzle. This pack's managed-care essay holds the payment shape. This figure holds a teacher who wrote a book the shape could recognize — and who kept saying the recognition was not the point.

The critiques still apply. A mood workbook can treat a neighborhood as a cognition. Her better teaching refuses that. Slide decks that keep only the forms are not her best students.
