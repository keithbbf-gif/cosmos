*Cognitive-Behavior Modification: An Integrative Approach* (Plenum, 1977) is the spine this pack names. It sat two years before the Guilford depression manual and a long way from Ellis's parlor. Waterloo — the Canadian university address attached to his working life — is not Penn and not East 65th Street. Another door.

## Self-talk as a research object

He treated inner speech as something a clinician and a researcher could hear without becoming an analyst of dreams. Stress-inoculation training is the named method later syllabi attach to him. This page will not teach it. The historical claim is that a 1970s behavioral world already had a cognitive vocabulary that was not only Beck's triad and not only Ellis's must.

Integrative, in his title, is a 1977 mood: the lab and the sentence might share a clinic. Later brand names made sharing look like betrayal or conversion. 1977 is earlier and calmer. A deepen pack keeps calm objects on the shelf so the brand fight of the 2000s does not rewrite the 1970s.

He later wrote in a more narrative and constructive weather. Late weather is allowed. It does not cancel 1977. Students who meet him only through a workshop title have met a product. Meet the year.

## Why he is easy to drop

Slide decks that run Wolpe → Beck → Linehan have no spare minute. Meichenbaum is the spare minute. Dropping him makes "cognitive-behavioral" look like a single invention plus a third wave. It was already a conversation among neighbors who did not share a supervisor. Waterloo talked to a literature. Penn talked to a ward. New York talked to a parlor. All three talks mattered.

The 1979 manual won the workforce. Winning is not the same as being the only 1970s book. This URL exists to keep 1977 visible as a neighbor. Neighbors prevent churches.
