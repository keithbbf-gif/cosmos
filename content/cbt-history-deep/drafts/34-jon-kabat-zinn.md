**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jon Kabat-Zinn is living (verified 2026). Public book and dated clinic history only. No health speculation. No UMass scrape. This page will not teach a body scan.

In 1979 he opened a mindfulness-based stress reduction clinic at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center. *Full Catastrophe Living* (Delacorte, 1990) is the public object. MBSR was a medical-behavioral program for people whose lives already included pain, illness, and stress. It was not, by itself, a psychotherapy school. The later CBT house borrowed the cushion. This pack files the lender.

## 1979, a hospital, not a sangha

A medical center is a particular kind of room. It already speaks graphs. It already has patients who will not be well in the way a surgical schedule wants. Putting a sitting practice in that room was a 1979 bet: attention might be something a hospital could offer without calling it insight. The bet traveled. Travel is how a hospital program becomes a book, then a workshop industry, then a word on yogurt.

Buddhist teachers have had opinions. Some are grateful. Some see ethics stripped for a medical market. A counseling history can admit the debt without pretending a county-clinic group, or a phone, is a sangha. Kabat-Zinn has written as a person who wanted the hospital to be able to say "mindfulness" without saying "convert." Wanting is not the same as controlling the afterlife. Afterlives include apps. This URL will not recommend one.

## Why a CBT deepen pack includes him

Because Segal, Williams, and Teasdale's 2002 book is unintelligible without 1979 and 1990. Because Hayes's 2004 nickname put "mindfulness" in a wave that already had a medical cousin. Because the 2010s boom flattened a hospital program into a commute. Flattening is the occupational hazard of a technique that can be described. Description is how techniques get staffed. Staffing is how they get sold.

He is not Beck. He is not Linehan. He is not a third-wave founder in the 1991-trial sense. He is a neighbor whose cushion the family took. Neighbors who get taken deserve a plaque that says what they actually opened: a 1979 clinic, a 1990 book.
