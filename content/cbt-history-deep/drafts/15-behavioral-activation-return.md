**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1996 the *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* printed a component analysis by Neil S. Jacobson and colleagues. Cognitive-behavioral treatment for depression had been taken apart. A behavioral-activation piece was compared with a fuller cognitive package. The paper asked an impolite question: how much of the furniture does the room need?

Sona Dimidjian and colleagues, in the same journal in 2006, ran a trial of behavioral activation, cognitive therapy, and antidepressant medication. The first wave, which Hayes would nickname in 2004, had never fully left. It came back inside the depression literature as a reminder that some of the older house still stood when you carried the cognitive chairs out.

This page will not assign an activity schedule. It will treat 1996 and 2006 as dates in a family fight.

## What a component analysis is for

Manuals accumulate. A 1979 sequence grows techniques the way a kitchen grows gadgets. A component analysis asks which gadgets do work. Jacobson's group was not vandalizing Beck. They were doing what an empirical school had promised it would do: test its own furniture. The results were argued, reanalyzed, and absorbed into a smaller named therapy — behavioral activation — with its own later manuals (Martell and others). Those later manuals are copyrighted training objects. They are not this page's handout.

The impolite question remains useful even if you dislike the answers. A depressed person on a Tuesday may be able to test a thought. They may only be able to get out of a chair. A school that cannot hear the second sentence will keep handing over the first. Rural hours know the second sentence without a JCCP subscription.

Jacobson died in 2003. The figure essay stays with him and will not embroider the death. The 1996 paper is the object this era needs. Dimidjian's 2006 trial is the object that made the 1996 question harder to treat as a local Seattle quarrel.

## How this sits beside the third wave

Behavioral activation is not ACT and not DBT. It is, if you like geology, a first-wave crest inside a second-wave shoreline. Hayes's 2004 nickname did not own it. Hofmann's later process-based work would try to name processes across packages rather than defend packages. BA is a good test case: a process (approach, schedule, contact with contingency) that can live inside several brand names.

The family fight — how much "cognition" a treatment needs — is not a branding problem. It is a question about what a person can stand to do this week. University clinics argued it with arms and effect sizes. County clinics argued it with transportation and childcare. Both arguments are real. Only one of them got into JCCP.

## What a practice site should not do with 1996

Do not tell a reader to "activate." Do not print a weekly grid. Do not imply that thinking work is a luxury or that behavioral work is for people who cannot think. Those are insults dressed as science. The historical useful sentence is smaller: inside the CBT house, some of the best empirical work was a willingness to take the house apart.

WOW Therapies does not, here, offer behavioral activation as a service name. If a later clinical page ever names it, that page needs training, limits, and review. This page names two papers and a return. The return embarrassed some cognitive therapists and relieved others. Relief is allowed. Homework is not.

A rural hour that helps a person do one thing they had stopped doing is standing in a building older than 1979. Wolpe would have recognized the temper if not the diagnosis. Beck would have asked what the person thought about the thing. A living hour might need both questions. This series will not script them.

Dimidjian's later writing on training and on the gap between efficacy and the room a person can actually reach belongs with the 2006 trial as a second chapter. Trials that stay in universities have one moral. Trials that ask what a community clinic can staff have another. BA's later life in public systems — including as a cheaper step in some stepped-care designs — is part of the IAPT-adjacent weather. Cheap is not the same as simple. Simple is not the same as kind. The 1996 paper did not settle kindness. It settled that a profession which had promised to test its furniture would, at least once, test it.

## Seattle, 1996, a dismantling

Jacobson's group was working in a behavioral-couples and depression world that already liked components. The 1996 paper is Seattle furniture as much as it is JCCP furniture. Local groups take houses apart. Journals make the taking-apart national. National taking-apart becomes a named therapy with later manuals this page will not reprint. The interval between a component analysis and a brand is the interval in which a question becomes a product. 1996 is still a question. 2006 is a trial that made the question expensive to ignore. Keep the interval. Brochures hate intervals.

A rural clinician who has never run "BA" as a title may still have asked a person to do one thing they had stopped doing. Titles are for guilds. Tuesdays are for people. This series will not confuse them, and will not assign the thing.

Martell and later writers turned a 1996 question into teachable sequences this page will not reprint. The interval between JCCP and a workshop title is the interval in which a dismantling becomes a product. Keep 1996 as a dismantling. Let 2006 remain a trial. Let the workshop remain somewhere else, with consent.

A 1997 lab meeting in Seattle that argued about which component was "active" was doing the 1996 paper's job in a smaller room. Smaller rooms are where questions stay questions. Journals make them national. This pack wants the smaller room visible so the later brand cannot pretend it was always a product.

A 2007 community clinic that heard "BA" as a cheaper step and not as a 1996 question was already the product life. Cheaper steps can be kind. They can also be a muzzle. This pack will not assign the step. It will keep 1996 audible under 2007's procurement word.

A 1996 methods appendix that listed what was stripped from the fuller package is the dismantling's inventory. Inventories become brands. Keep the appendix audible. Do not print an activity sheet.

Strip the furniture in 1996. Trial the question in 2006. Do not assign the Tuesday. Seattle first. Journal second. Brand last if at all.

Question first. Product later if at all. 1996 remains a dismantling in Seattle before it is a title anyone can procure.

Seattle, 1996: a question with an appendix. That is enough furniture for this URL.
