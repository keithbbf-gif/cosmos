**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Arnold Allan Lazarus was born in 1932 in South Africa and died in 2013. He was younger than Wolpe, trained in the same behavioral weather, and less willing to let a physiology be the whole clinic. *Behavior Therapy and Beyond* (McGraw-Hill, 1971) is the title that already admits the argument. *The Practice of Multimodal Therapy* (1981) is the later inventory of a life — BASIC ID, in the jargon students remember and this page will not teach.

## A household, not a conversion

American slide decks like conversions: behaviorist sees the light, becomes cognitive. Lazarus is messier. He stayed in a behavioral house and kept adding rooms: sensation, imagery, cognition, interpersonal, drugs as a letter in an acronym, affect. Adding rooms is how a clinician refuses a single gadget. It is also how a school looks, to a list-maker, like a mess.

Wolpe's 1958 book is teachable because it is narrow. Lazarus's later books are teachable only if you accept a map of seven or so domains. Maps of domains become, in careless hands, a checklist that never looks up. His better writing is a protest against narrowness, including the narrowness of a thought-only hour. That protest sits beside Beck, not under him.

The South African origin belongs on the same map as Wolpe's. Johannesburg exports are easy to drop once Temple and Penn have the microphone. Put them back. The first wave is not a Mid-Atlantic invention. The widening of the first wave is not a California weekend. It is a household argument that went to press in 1971.

## 1971, before the 1979 workforce

*Behavior Therapy and Beyond* is eight years before Guilford's depression manual. The "beyond" is already cognitive in a broad sense and already impatient with a clinic that would treat a person as a single channel. Impatience is a temperament this pack keeps meeting: Ellis, Lazarus, later Hofmann. Temperament is not a protocol.

He worked in the United States for the long middle of the career (Rutgers is the address many obituaries used). Addresses are not methods. The method, if you must name one, is a refusal to let one modality bully the hour. Refusals are hard to randomize. Lists were not kind to multimodal work. That unkindness is part of the 1990s story, not a verdict on 1971.
