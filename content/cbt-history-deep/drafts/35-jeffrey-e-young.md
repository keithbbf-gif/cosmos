**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jeffrey E. Young is living (verified 2026). Public manuals and the 2003 book only. No health speculation. No institute scrape. This page will not teach a schema list or an imagery exercise.

With Aaron Beck he wrote the Cognitive Therapy Scale (1980), the tape-as-number that made a student fail in public. With Janet Klosko and Marjorie Weishaar he wrote *Schema Therapy: A Practitioner's Guide* (Guilford, 2003). The first object is how the 1979 workforce learned to watch itself. The second is a later bet that some lives need a longer story than a twelve-week sequence.

## 1980, a scale, a guild

Competence as a number is how a school becomes a guild. It is also how a school becomes a franchise. Young’s name on the CTS is easy to drop when obituaries want one genius. Do not drop it. Scales have authors. Authors who later want a longer story are allowed to have wanted the short story first. The 1980 object trained a workforce to see a missed cognition. The 2003 object asked what to do when the missed thing is older than a Tuesday.

## 2003, a longer story

Schema therapy tried to house early patterns, modes, a relationship that could stand more heat than a 1979 sequence always asked for. It sits beside the third wave as a different answer to the same restlessness: the thought record is not enough for every life. It sits beside psychoanalysis as a neighbor who still wants a formulation and still wants data. Neighbors make brochures nervous. This pack likes neighbors.

Klosko and Weishaar are easy to drop. Do not drop them. Three names made the 2003 spine. Later schema-mode workshops are a guild's afterlife. Afterlives are not 2003. Date the book. Refuse the handout.

Critics said schema work could become another endless therapy with cognitive furniture. Defenders said some people had been failed by short sequences. Both sentences can be true in different hours. A rural clinic that has never taken a schema workshop may still have asked about a long pattern. Asking is not a license to claim the brand.
