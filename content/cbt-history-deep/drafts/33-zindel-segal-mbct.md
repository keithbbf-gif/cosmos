**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Zindel V. Segal is living (verified 2026), as are, at last check, his 2002 co-authors J. Mark G. Williams and John D. Teasdale. Public book only. No health speculation. No campus scrape. This page will not teach a sitting practice or a relapse handout.

*Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression* (Guilford, 2002) is the object. The aim, in their telling, was to keep a passing sad mood from restarting an old cognitive loop in people who were between episodes and who already knew the thought record. Their earlier collaboration with Beck's cognitive model is the bridge: they were not converting to a religion. They were trying to stop relapse.

## A trio, not a lone inventor

Slide decks say "Segal" because first authors are easier. The book has three names. Williams and Teasdale are British cognitive scientists of mood and mind; Segal has been the Toronto-addressed name American workshops often keep. This pack will not flatten a trio into a brand. Three people wrote a 2002 sequence this page will not reprint.

Kabat-Zinn's MBSR clinic at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center (1979) and *Full Catastrophe Living* (1990) are the borrowed cushion's public objects. MBSR was a medical-behavioral program, not a psychotherapy school. MBCT is the psychotherapy-adjacent adaptation. Buddhist teachers have had opinions about the borrowing. Some are grateful. Some see a technique stripped of ethics. A counseling history can admit the debt without pretending a county-clinic group is a sangha.

## 2002 beside 2004

Hayes's nickname arrived two years later and put MBCT in a wave. The 2002 book did not ask for a geology. It asked about depression relapse. Those are different asks. The app boom later detached the cushion again and sold it to commuters. The trio's better pages keep a problem attached: people who already knew the record and still relapsed. Apps prefer a vibe. This URL prefers the problem's year.

The critiques still apply. Mindfulness, taught badly, can sound like a muzzle. A woman whose "loop" is a correct reading of a room does not need a cushion as a first answer. The 2002 book is not that first answer for every life. It is a 2002 object for a specific relapse question.
