Dianne L. Chambless is living (verified 2026). Public papers only. No health speculation. No Penn scrape. This page will not paste a list as a menu.

She chaired and wrote for the Society of Clinical Psychology (APA Division 12) task force that named empirically supported treatments in the mid-1990s. With Steven Hollon she wrote "Defining Empirically Supported Therapies" (*Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, 1998). The era essay holds the lists as a genre. This page holds the prose style: show your work.

## A chair, a leak, a dialect

The reports were written for a society of clinical psychologists. They leaked into counselor-education, social work, and insurer English because administrators prefer a table to an argument. Leakage is a mechanism. This figure is one of the people who wrote the table. Writing a table is a reform against unfalsifiable guild talk. Calling the unshown a superstition is a new priesthood. Both lived in the same pages.

Hollon's name is easy to drop. Do not drop it. Two voices wrote a definition. Definitions that leak get quoted as one voice. This pack keeps the second name.

Her later career includes anxiety research — a day job beside the list-world. Day jobs matter. List-makers who also run trials are harder to cartoon as bureaucrats. Cartooning is for slide one. This URL prefers a 1998 paper and a 1990s chair.
