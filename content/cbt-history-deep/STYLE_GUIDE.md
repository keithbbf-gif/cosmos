# Style guide — WOW Therapies, CBT history deepen

This pack is educational history for [WOWTherapies.com](https://wowtherapies.com): how cognitive and behavioral therapies were named, written, filmed, funded, and argued with. It is **not** a protocol library and **not** the SLPWOW speech-pathology series.

It deepens four sibling essays in `content/wowtherapies-therapy-history/`:

- `cognitive-therapy-empirical-turn`
- `third-wave-mindfulness-clinic`
- `aaron-beck`
- `albert-ellis`

Do not retell the whole of Freud, Rogers, or Bowlby here. Point at those slugs if a reader needs the wider house. Do not retell Van Riper, articulation science, or dysphagia. Those belong on the speech lane.

`voice_check: human` means the writer draft passed this file. `voice_check: edited` means a later editor read the piece aloud. Neither value is a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Who is speaking

A historically literate magazine writer who has sat in enough consulting rooms to know that a manual is not a person. Warm without being pastoral. Precise without being a textbook. The reader may be a curious client, a family member, a student, or a clinician. Do not talk down. Do not talk shop as if the room were empty of patients.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas practice brand. These essays are **heritage education**, not a menu of services and not a claim that the clinic practices every school named here.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" as a metaphor for a field
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- three-beat synonym piles ("heal, grow, and thrive")
- "the father of…" unless a contemporary actually used that phrase and you are quoting the myth, not endorsing it
- "Freud discovered the unconscious"
- "gold-standard treatment" as marketing
- invented quotes
- AI-generated faces or "period photographs" that never existed

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "empower," "in this article we will explore," "journey of healing" as filler.

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a room, a document, or a named quarrel. The first sentence should be checkable.
2. Mix sentence length. A short sentence after a long one. Do not write every paragraph as topic sentence + three examples + wrap.
3. Name the book, paper, hospital, or society. *Cognitive Therapy of Depression* (1979). The 1961 BDI paper. East 65th Street. The 2004 Hayes address in *Behavior Therapy*.
4. Mark uncertainty. `[CITE NEEDED]` or a plain "historians disagree" beats a clean falsehood.
5. Put Beck, Ellis, and the later names in their decade. Do not offer them as current treatment manuals.
6. Give women, British, South African, and later international work full essays, not a closing paragraph titled "also."
7. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target 1,000–1,800 words for figures, 1,200–2,000 for era essays. Cut the recap paragraph if it only restates the headings. `check_pack.py` fails an era under 1,200 words or a figure under 1,000. Deepen with dated scenes, not with recap.

## Claims posture

See `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`. History may describe a method. History may not teach a reader to self-administer it. No session scripts, no skill worksheets, no "try this at home" protocols. Do not reprint an ABC diagram as homework. Do not list DBT modules. Do not walk a thought record.

Efficacy sentences, if any, stay historical and sourced ("Linehan's 1991 trial reported…") and never become "CBT will help your depression."

Living people get extra care: no gossip, no unsourced health claims. Linehan's 2011 public disclosure may be mentioned because she chose the *New York Times*. Do not embroider it. Do not diagnose a founder.

## Portraits

Public domain or Creative Commons only. Document the file in `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. If there is no clean license, use type or a period object — never a generated face. See `PHOTO_NOTES.md`. Almost every twentieth-century CBT figure in this pack is `no-portrait`. That is correct.

## Series voice, in one paragraph

Write as if *The New Yorker* and *Psychotherapy Networker* had to share a desk, and the desk was in a small-city clinic that still answers its own phone. The past is allowed to be strange. The present is not allowed to be sold. A thought record is a historical object. It is not this page's homework.
