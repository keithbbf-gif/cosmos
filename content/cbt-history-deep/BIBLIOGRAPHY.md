## Precursors

- Epictetus. *Enchiridion*. (Oldfather, W. A., trans., Loeb, 1928, is a convenient English; the line Ellis liked is a tradition of translation, not a clinical manual.)
- Marcus Aurelius. *Meditations*.
- Adler, Alfred. *Über den nervösen Charakter*. Wiesbaden: Bergmann, 1912.
- Adler, Alfred. *What Life Should Mean to You*. Boston: Little, Brown, 1931.
- Kelly, George A. *The Psychology of Personal Constructs*. 2 vols. New York: Norton, 1955.
- Bannister, Don. "Personal Construct Theory: A Summary and Experimental Paradigm." *Acta Psychologica* 20 (1962): 97–117. [VERIFY] volume/pages against the issue before a printed footnote treats this line as gospel.
- Bannister, Don, and Fay Fransella. *Inquiring Man: The Theory of Personal Constructs*. Harmondsworth: Penguin, 1971. British afterlife; after Kelly's death.

## First-wave behavior, gauntlets

- Eysenck, H. J. "The Effects of Psychotherapy: An Evaluation." *Journal of Consulting Psychology* 16, no. 5 (1952): 319–324. Historical gauntlet; not a last word.
- Watson, John B., and Rosalie Rayner. "Conditioned Emotional Reactions." *Journal of Experimental Psychology* 3, no. 1 (1920): 1–14. Little Albert — cite as ethics history, not as a method to copy.
- Jones, Mary Cover. "A Laboratory Study of Fear: The Case of Peter." *Pedagogical Seminary* 31 (1924): 308–315.
- Wolpe, Joseph. *Psychotherapy by Reciprocal Inhibition*. Stanford: Stanford University Press, 1958.
- Skinner, B. F. *Science and Human Behavior*. New York: Macmillan, 1953.
- Lazarus, Arnold A. *Behavior Therapy and Beyond*. New York: McGraw-Hill, 1971.
- Lazarus, Arnold A. *Multimodal Behavior Therapy*. New York: Springer, 1976.
- Lazarus, Arnold A. *The Practice of Multimodal Therapy*. New York: McGraw-Hill, 1981.

## Ellis / REBT

- Ellis, Albert. "Rational Psychotherapy and Individual Psychology." *Journal of Individual Psychology* 13 (1957): 38–44. (REBT practiced from 1955.)
- Ellis, Albert, and Robert A. Harper. *A Guide to Rational Living*. Englewood Cliffs, NJ: Prentice-Hall, 1961.
- Ellis, Albert. *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy*. New York: Lyle Stuart, 1962.
- Carey, Benedict. "A Psychologist, 92, Is at Odds With the Institute He Founded." *New York Times*, 11 October 2005.
- *Ellis v. Broder*, New York Supreme Court, 2006 (removal without notice held invalid). Public docket; not gossip.

## Beck / cognitive therapy

- Beck, A. T., C. H. Ward, M. Mendelson, J. Mock, and J. Erbaugh. "An Inventory for Measuring Depression." *Archives of General Psychiatry* 4 (1961): 561–571.
- Beck, Aaron T. "Thinking and Depression: I. Idiosyncratic Content and Cognitive Distortions." *Archives of General Psychiatry* 9 (1963): 324–333.
- Beck, Aaron T. "Thinking and Depression: II. Theory and Therapy." *Archives of General Psychiatry* 10 (1964): 561–571.
- Beck, Aaron T. *Depression: Clinical, Experimental, and Theoretical Aspects*. New York: Hoeber, 1967. Later *Depression: Causes and Treatment*.
- Beck, Aaron T. *Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders*. New York: International Universities Press, 1976.
- Beck, Aaron T., A. John Rush, Brian F. Shaw, and Gary Emery. *Cognitive Therapy of Depression*. New York: Guilford, 1979.
- Young, Jeffrey, and Aaron T. Beck. *Cognitive Therapy Scale*. Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania, 1980 (unpublished manual; later CTS-R revisions are a publishing history).
- Beck, Judith S. *Cognitive Therapy: Basics and Beyond*. New York: Guilford, 1995. Later *Cognitive Behavior Therapy: Basics and Beyond*.
- Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy. Institutional history: founded 1994, Philadelphia area. Use the institute's own dated pages as institutional history, not as a license to scrape photographs.
- Beck, Aaron T., et al. Recovery-oriented cognitive therapy (CT-R) papers and the later Guilford volume with Paul Grant and Ellen Inverso — late-career object, not a rewrite of 1979.

## Neighbors on the same graph

- Meichenbaum, Donald. *Cognitive-Behavior Modification: An Integrative Approach*. New York: Plenum, 1977.
- Meichenbaum, Donald. *Stress Inoculation Training*. New York: Pergamon, 1985. Named later method; cite, do not teach.
- Rush, A. John, Aaron T. Beck, Maria Kovacs, and Steven Hollon. "Comparative Efficacy of Cognitive Therapy and Pharmacotherapy in the Treatment of Depressed Outpatients." *Cognitive Therapy and Research* 1 (1977): 17–37.
- Burns, David D. *Feeling Good: The New Mood Therapy*. New York: Morrow, 1980. Public book; not the 1979 Guilford manual.
- Burns, David D. *The Feeling Good Handbook*. New York: Plume, 1989. Later public book; still not a treatment plan.
- Greenberger, Dennis, and Christine A. Padesky. *Mind Over Mood*. New York: Guilford, 1995.
- Padesky, Christine A. "Socratic Questioning: Changing Minds or Guiding Discovery?" Address, European Congress of Behavioural and Cognitive Therapies, London, 1993. Unpublished teaching object; not a worksheet.
- Elkin, Irene, et al. "National Institute of Mental Health Treatment of Depression Collaborative Research Program: General Effectiveness of Treatments." *Archives of General Psychiatry* 46, no. 11 (1989): 971–982.
- Klerman, Gerald L., and Myrna M. Weissman. Interpersonal psychotherapy of depression — named here as the third school on the TDCRP graph, not as a CBT manual.
- Rush, A. John, et al. STAR*D reports, including *American Journal of Psychiatry* 163, no. 11 (2006): 1905–1917. A graph and a controversy, not a shopping list.
- American Psychiatric Association. *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders*. 3rd ed. Washington, DC: APA, 1980.
- Decker, Hannah S. *The Making of DSM-III*. New York: Oxford University Press, 2013.

- London School of Economics, Centre for Economic Performance, Mental Health Policy Group. *The Depression Report: A New Deal for Depression and Anxiety Disorders*. London: LSE, June 2006. (Layard, chair; Clark among authors.)
- Clark, David M., Richard Layard, and Rachel Smithies. "Improving Access to Psychological Therapy: Initial Evaluation of the Two Demonstration Sites." CEP Discussion Paper 897, November 2008. Doncaster and Newham opened late summer 2006.
- Clark, David M. "Realizing the Mass Public Benefit of Evidence-Based Psychological Therapies: The IAPT Program." *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology* 14 (2018): 159–183. First full IAPT services: September 2008.
- Proudfoot, Judy, et al. Computerized CBT papers of the late 1990s–2000s (*Beating the Blues* as a historical product name).
