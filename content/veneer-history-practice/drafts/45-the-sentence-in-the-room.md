I say it before the shine. Quartered oak face, stable core, both sides, solid band. Veneer. The client has time to hate the word while the panel is still a dull leaf on a bench. If I wait until the film is a mirror, they will hear the word as a confession I delayed. I do not delay.

This pack started with a leaf that is not a confession. It ends with the sentence that keeps it from becoming one.

## What they often mean

They mean the motel nightstand. They mean a peeled tape. They mean a sister’s desk and a rental sander. They mean a tag that stole. I agree with them about those objects. I do not agree that a bookmatched door with a note on the back is those objects. I show the edge. I show the backer. I show page 17 next to page 18. I let them decide if they want a glue-up instead. A glue-up is furniture. A leaf is furniture. A photograph of oak is a catalog.

I do not win everyone. I do not have to. I have to not steal.

## The mixed piece

Solid legs, a tenon, a hip. A faced top, a picture, a climate. Solid stiles, a flake panel. This is a lot of the good furniture in the last four hundred years and a lot of what I want to build in this county. The ébéniste and the menuisier in one room. I say both jobs. I do not say “solid oak dining set” if the top is a face.

If they want all solid, we talk propellers and width and winter. If they want all leaf, we talk bands and backs and a latch. If they want the mix, we talk the mix. The mix is not a compromise. It is a decision about which argument each part should win.

## The tag and the invoice

The invoice says species, face, core, glue, both sides. The tag, if there is a tag, says the same. [VERIFY] house language. I will not write “oak” as a color. I will not write “solid” as a mood.

A sales channel that cannot say the sentence is a sales channel I will not feed a door. I can lose the job. I have lost the job. I still have the county.

## What I will not do

I will not distress a leaf to look more solid. I will not dye a core and call it a blend. I will not iron-on a dining arris. I will not fake a listed wood. I will not call a Director plate a cut list or a Ruhlmann caption a shopping list. I will not shuffle a flitch. I will not start at 80.

I will not sneer at a factory that tells the truth. I will not defend a factory that steals. I will not use Grand Rapids as a slur or as a halo. I will not hang a Japanese word on an Arkansas loaf. I will not put a Dutch moth on a Biedermeier idea unless the client is buying a moth.

## The job I lost on the word

I said veneer on a kitchen that wanted the magazine word *solid*. I showed the edge, the backer, pages 17 and 18. They left. I still have the county. I would rather lose a kitchen than write “solid oak” as a mood on a faced door.

A sales channel that cannot say the sentence does not get a door. [VERIFY] house language on tags. I will not write oak as a color. I will not write solid as a feeling.

## How I show the edge

Before the shine I walk them to the arris. Solid band, leaf, core. I knock the backer. I let them hold a leftover page. The motel nightstand is in their mouth. I agree about the motel nightstand. I do not agree that this door is that object. They decide. A glue-up is furniture. A leaf is furniture. A photograph of oak is a catalog.

If they want all solid we talk propellers and winter. If they want the mix we talk which argument each part should win. The mix is most of the good furniture I know. Legs that rack. A top that is a picture. Stiles that take a hinge. A panel that takes a flake.

I have a sample door that is rift stiles and a flake panel, note on the back, band you can thumb. I set it next to a peeled tape from a closet I keep as a villain. The two objects do the talking. I do not give a speech about four hundred years unless they ask. If they ask, I start with Egypt and I end with the motel. I do not start with the motel and hope they forgive the word.

## What the next person finds

The note on the back is the sentence when I am not in the room. Both faces, date, glue, core, face species, flitch and pages, band, who scraped. I write it as if they have a belt. I sand as if they will not read.

I will not distress a leaf to look more solid. I will not dye a core. I will not iron-on a dining arris. I will not fake a listed wood. I will not shuffle a flitch. I will not start at 80.

## The note on the back

Both faces. Date. Glue. Core. Face species. Flitch and pages if I have them. Band. Who scraped. The note is the sentence the next person gets when I am not in the room. I write it as if they have a belt in their hand. I sand as if they will not read.

## This county

We are a hardwood shop in a place that cut oak for a long time. Veneer does not make us less of one. A mill that sliced faces would be family to a mill that sawed boards. [VERIFY] before a brochure claims a slicer. What I have is a leaf that lets me put rift stiles and a flake panel in one door without a propeller. The hip meets a band. The book stays a book. The word is older than the motel and older than the apology.

Egypt spent an import on a face. Rome spent a fortune on a skin. A duke spent a wall on a lute that would not play. A Dutch shop spent a moth. A French shop spent brass and a name. An English shop spent an oyster. A New York shop spent a fold. A factory spent a catalog. A motel spent the word. A law spent some trees back.

I spend a flitch I can buy, on a core I can defend, with a glue I write down, in a room where I say the word first.

When the press releases, I listen. I tap. I look at the seam. I ease the band. I write the note. I say the sentence again if they come to pick up the shine. Quartered oak face, stable core, both sides, solid band. Veneer. If they still want a glue-up, I have boards. If they want the door, they have the door. The flake stays a flake. The rectangle stays a rectangle. That is enough craft for the word to stop being an apology. The rest was the syllabus. The panel is the critic. I let it be.
