The bag sighs and the platens become one thought. I watch the tape, the edge, the place a leaf likes to crawl. I do not walk away in the first minute. A fold in the bag is a line in the face. A crumb under the platen is a hill in the face. The pump is not a personality. It is even pressure on a sandwich I already built.

I veneered doors with cauls and a pile of weights before I had a bag. I still use cauls. The bag did not make me smarter. It made me more even.

## What the bag will not do

It will not joint a seam. It will not flatten a chip you should have flattened last night. It will not add a backer. It will not fix a dirty ground. It will not make PVA into hide. I have asked it to do all of those. It glued my mistakes evenly.

A one-sided panel in a bag is a cup you applied pressure to. Both faces, or a mill-balanced sheet, still.

## Platens and crumbs

A flat platen above and below, thicker than a hope. I scrape the platen. I look for a dried glue hill from Tuesday. I look for a stray tape ball. I look for a leaf corner that folded under. The bag will print all of it.

A curved form in a bag is a different platen: the form is the caul. I cover the show face with a clean sheet so the bag’s texture does not become the finish. I have printed a bag weave into a softwood leaf. I sanded. I should not have had to.

## Glue in the bag

PVA, a urea the label allows, a hide if I can work the window: I use what the job wants and I write it down. I do not print a secret mix. I do not cook a resin. I ventilate urea. I do not bag a contact cement job and call it a bag job; contact is a different religion — a kiss, not a press.

Spread is even and not a puddle. A puddle is a telegraph. A starve is a blister. Figured leaves get size if they look thirsty. I have starved a crotch in a bag that looked perfect until October.

## The caul I already had

A flat door, a few clamps, a caul that is actually flat: this still faces a small field. A clamp without a caul is a dent I will find under a satin finish. I use a caul. I use wax or a tape so the caul does not become the work.

Sandbags on a curve, a hot hide, a team: older than my pump. I do not sneer at sandbags. I sneer at a clamp on a leaf with no caul.

## Time

The bag does not cure glue faster because I am impatient. I leave it as the bottle says, in a shop that is not a fridge. A cold shop is a gelled hide and a sleepy PVA. I have opened a bag early. The leaf lifted at an edge I then had to fight.

I crack the bag and I wait a minute before I peel a tape that wants to lift a corner. I tap. I look at the seam. I write the note. Then I stack the panel on stickers so both faces see air. A panel on a plastic sheet on one face is a climate I applied after the press.

## Safety, short

The pump is electricity and a bag that can hold a crimp. I keep fingers out of platens. I do not modify a pressure vessel I do not understand. I do not write a homebrew vacuum from a fridge compressor as a how-to. Buy a kit that is sold for this, or use cauls.

Urea formaldehyde systems: I read the label, I ventilate, I do not make them from raw chemistry. If the job wants a hot-press industrial resin, the job wants a shop with a booth I do not have. I send it or I change the glue.

## The dining top the bag cannot rush

A dining field is a size I can bag and a time I cannot hurry. I jointed the seams, flattened the crotch pages two nights, spread PVA, laid platens, and I watched the first minute. A corner folded under. I cracked the bag, lifted, flattened the corner, closed again. If I had walked to the phone I would have printed a fold into a field I cannot hide with a scarf.

I left the bag as the bottle said. I opened early once on a smaller door because I needed the pump. The edge lifted in a line I then fought with weights and a speech. The dining top stayed in until the clock agreed. I stickered both faces. I did not set it on a plastic sheet.

Urea on a field that size is a ventilation decision, not a bravery. I read the label. I do not cook a resin. [VERIFY] what we actually put in the bag before a house mix gets printed. I write the glue on the back. The next heat needs to know.

## A form, a weave, a sanding I should not have needed

I bagged a leaf onto a curved form for a door lite — a small radius, a clean walnut. I forgot the slip sheet. The bag’s texture printed into the face. Soft, even, a weave you could see in a raking light after oil. I sanded. I met nothing of the core, luck, and I still should not have had to sand a print I applied.

The form is the caul. The slip sheet is the finish. The bag is even pressure, including even pressure of its own skin. I cover the show face. I scrape the form for Tuesday’s glue hill. I look for a tape ball. The bag will print all of it, and a curve will print it as a stripe you cannot belt off.

## When the pump is down on a Tuesday

The pump died on a kitchen run. Eight doors. I used cauls, clamps, bricks I keep for this Tuesday. The seams were already jointed. The chips were already flat. The doors came out. A little less even at a center I tapped and lived with because it was not a hollow, it was a thickness.

A clamp without a caul on one of those doors would have been a dent under satin. I used cauls. I waxed them. I did not invent a new glue because the pump was down. PVA still needed time. Hide still needed a gel window I did not have on eight doors with two hands. The tool is the last thing. The sandwich is the first.

Sandbags on a repair at a client’s house are a different religion and I do not practice it. The bag and the caul live in the shop. The dining room gets a finished door. The crate in the corner can wait. The leaf on the platen cannot wait for a fold I walked away from.

## What I take from both tools

Even pressure. A sandwich I already made well. Time. Both faces. A note.

The hammer is for a carcase the bag cannot swallow. The bag is for a field the hammer cannot finish in a gel window. The caul is for a Tuesday when the pump is down. I have had that Tuesday. The door still had to be a door. The weights were bricks. The bricks were enough because the seam was jointed and the chip was flat. The tool is the last thing. The book is the first.
