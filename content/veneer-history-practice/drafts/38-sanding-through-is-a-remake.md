I found the core on a quartered oak door at 11 a.m. A wide-belt, a proud seam I should have scraped, a pass I did not need. The wound was a lightning bolt of pale, a ply stripe, a thing that would not take dye like the flake. I remade the panel. I did not paint a lightning bolt and hope the room was dark. I have hoped. The room was not dark.

Sand-through is a remake. Patch if the wound is a coin and the folder has a sibling. Remake if the wound is a weather system. Do not “blend.”

## Why it happens

The leaf is a business card. The machine is a board eater. A ridge at a seam is a place the belt hits first. A quilt’s high spot is a place the ROS hits first. A tape residue is a place you sand extra to “get it clean.” A helper who thinks 80 is how you start furniture.

I start with a scraper. I use a block and a paper I can count. I do not start at 80 on a 0.6 mm face unless I am trying to meet the core.

Sawn veneer has meat. I still do not belt it like a tabletop glue-up. I have meat. I do not have a board.

## The scraper is the tool

A cabinet scraper, a hook I turned, a whisper. Flake likes a scraper. Crotch likes a scraper. Burl likes a scraper and then a paper I change before it loads. The ROS is for a ground or for a solid. On a leaf it is a vote I often lose.

I mark the thickness in my head. I mark a pencil scribble on the field and I sand until the scribble is shy, not gone. If the scribble is gone and I am still proud of a ridge, I scrape the ridge, I do not chase it with a machine.

## Dye is not a patch

A wound dyed dark is a wound dyed dark. It photographs in a dim feed. It sits in a dining room as a shadow that moves when you move. I do not dye a ply and call it oak. I patch a leaf from the folder, or I remake.

A patch is a butterfly of the same page if I have it, cut to the figure, hide if I can, a scrape, a hope that is smaller than a dye hope. I date the patch on the back. I do not hide the fact in the invoice if the client is paying for a face.

## Wide-belts and shops that have them

A wide-belt is how factories meet cores. If I use one, I use a light pass, a sharp belt, a thickness I measured, a sacrificial setup piece. I do not “just kiss” a leaf I have not measured. The kiss is a wound.

I would rather be a shop without a wide-belt on leaves than a shop with a story about a kiss.

## The kiss I measured after

I had a wide-belt for a year. I used a light pass on a quartered oak panel I had measured with a caliper at four corners. I still kissed through at a proud seam I had not scraped. The machine was even. The panel was not. Even pressure on a ridge is a wound at the ridge.

I remade the panel. I sold the habit. The belt still lives for solids and for grounds. Leaves see a scraper. If a future house rule invites a belt on a leaf, it will invite a setup piece, a sharp belt, a number I write down. [VERIFY] house grit and whether a belt is allowed at all. I will not print a kiss as a method.

The sacrificial setup piece is not optional. I have skipped it. The first panel became the setup piece. That is a tuition I already paid.

## A paper-backed birch that felt like meat

A closet door, paper-backed birch, paint-grade, a helper who sanded as if the paper were a board. The wound was paler than a wood sand-through — a fuzz of fiber that would not be birch. Paint hid some of it. Raking light did not. I remade the door. Paint-grade is not a license to meet paper.

I caliper a cutoff of every paper-backed I buy. The wood thickness is the only thickness. The paper is a feeling. I treat the feeling as a lie. I scrape. I count paper. I do not start at 80.

## When a coin is a weather system

A coin wound from a ROS on a high spot can take a sibling patch if the folder has the page. A weather system from a wide-belt pass is a remake. I have tried to patch a weather system. The field became a quilt of good intentions. The quilt sanded through at the next high spot.

I show the client the pale. I use the word remake. I do not say blend. Dye on a ply stripe is a shadow that moves. I have hoped a dining room was dark. Dining rooms have four p.m.

A jewelry lid I sanded through because I held it in my lap and a paper wrapped my finger. Lap sanding is a coin flip. I remade the lid from page 11. The folder is why I still have a lid. The note on the back says *scraped, no belt* or it says *kiss, remake*. I have written *kiss, dye, hope*. I remade it later for free. The scraper is cheaper.

I keep a pale oak cutoff with a lightning bolt of core on the sample wall. Clients who want me to “just blend it” get to hold the cutoff in the window. The ply does not become flake in afternoon light. The conversation ends. We remake, or we patch a coin if the wound is a coin, or we refuse the dye. I would rather lose an afternoon than sell a shadow that moves.

## Paper-backed

The wood is thinner than you feel. The paper is the meat you feel. Sand through and you are in fiber that will not be oak. The wound is paler. The remake is the same. I caliper a cutoff. I treat the wood thickness as the only thickness.

## What I tell a client about later

If you refinish this, scrape. Do not rent a belt. Do not start at 80. There is a note on the back. I still sand as if they will not read it. I still write it.

If they want a piece that can take a future belt, we talk sawn leaf or solid. I do not sell a 0.6 mm face as a refinishing hobby.

## What I write

*Scraped, no belt, date. If a kiss happened, remake.* I have written *kiss, dye, hope* once. I remade it later for free because the hope failed. The free remake was the tuition. The note is cheaper.

Knives are next: how to cut a leaf without a saw that chews a business card. Then the crate in the corner, the air that turns a book into a chip or a sponge. The remake is the most expensive sentence in this stage. I scrape. I stop. I remake when I have to. I do not dye a ply. The flake is a church window. The core is a closet. I know which one I sold.
