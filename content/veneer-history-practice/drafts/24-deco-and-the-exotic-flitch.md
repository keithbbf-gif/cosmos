A sunburst on a Deco cabinet is a clock of leaves. Each wedge is a piece of a flitch, pointed at a center you only get to choose once. Émile-Jacques Ruhlmann’s shop and the Paris names around him used macassar ebony, amboyna, shagreen, ivory, a chrome, a lacquer. [VERIFY] a named piece before those woods go in a caption. I look at the center. If the center is tight, the shop could lay out. If the center is a wad, the shop hoped the finish would hide a layout.

Art Deco is veneer as geometry. Biedermeier wanted one thought in a rectangle. Deco wants a thought in a circle, a chevron, a lightning stripe. The slicer made the wedges possible. The port made the species possible. The later law made some of those species a stop.

## Sunburst is a layout religion

I have laid a sunburst in oak and in walnut. The math is a pie. The wood is a flitch that has to take a taper without the figure going stupid at the narrow end. Crotch wants to be a sunburst and will fight you at the heart. Straight ribbon can be a sunburst and look like a fan. Both are allowed. Mixing leftovers from three bundles is how you get a sun that looks like a repair.

Tape is structural. Dry-fit the pie on the bench. Number the wedges. The center disk — a contrasting wood, a bone in the old shops, a legal hardwood now — is a cap on a mistake or a period on a sentence. I cut the disk last, after I know the hole I actually made.

## Exotic as a period word

Macassar, palisander, thuya, amboyna: Deco captions read like a port ledger. Some of those trees are restricted now. I will not write a substitute recipe that says “use this, it looks the same.” It does not look the same, and the look is not the only issue. The CITES essay is the stop. This essay is the appetite.

Ivory and shagreen are faces I will not add. Chrome and lacquer are other benches I will hire. A walnut sunburst with a maple disk is a Deco sentence I can stand next to in 2026. A “macassar look” stain on sapele is a sentence I will not write.

## Ruhlmann and the factory cousin

Ruhlmann is a luxury name. A Grand Rapids Deco suite is a factory cousin. Both used leaves. Both used geometry. The difference is flitch versus grade, mounts versus stamped metal, a shop versus a line. I can admire a Ruhlmann photograph and still refuse to fake his materials. I can also refuse to sneer at a factory Deco dresser that told the truth on its tag and still runs its drawers.

## Chevron and the book

A chevron is a bookmatch on a bias. It is a herringbone that got ambitious. The seam is a zigzag that shows every ridge. I scrape. I do not belt. Pale woods in a chevron show glue stain like a map. I test hide and I test PVA on the actual leaf.

Deco liked a dark/light contrast. I can do that with walnut and maple. I do not need a banned dark wood to get a stripe.

## The walnut sunburst I filled twice

I laid a walnut sunburst for a round occasional table — a second object next to the museum cabinet — sixteen wedges, a maple disk, a flitch that had a quiet ribbon. I dry-fitted on the bench. I numbered. I still cut the center hole by the first wedge’s idea of a circle, not by the hole I had actually made. The disk sat on a wad. I filled the wad with a ring of leftovers. The ring photographed. In the room it was a nest.

I warmed what I could, lifted the disk, recut the hole to the pie I had, and cut a second disk. The second disk is tight. The first disk is in the folder with a note that says *do not*. The table is legal walnut and maple. It is not Ruhlmann. It is a layout religion I had already written down and then ignored at the last cut. The center is a decision you cannot undo. I undid it anyway, once, and paid with an afternoon.

Crotch wants to be a sunburst and will fight you at the heart. I have pointed crotch wedges at a center and watched the figure go muddy where the tree had already argued with itself. Straight ribbon made a cleaner fan. I tell a client that before I promise a crotch sun. The tree does not owe us a firework.

## A cocktail cabinet in a legal stripe

A cocktail cabinet is the Deco object that shows up in emails as a photograph of macassar and chrome. I built one in walnut and maple: chevron doors, a maple interior, a solid walnut lip, chrome I hired out. The chevron is a bookmatch on a bias. Every ridge showed. Pale maple showed a hide stain like a county map. I switched the doors to a glue I had tested on the actual leaf. I scraped. I did not belt. The cabinet is a geometry I can stand next to in 2026. It is not a port ledger.

Ivory and shagreen stay off the brief. I will not add a bone disk to get a period caption. The maple disk on the sunburst table is enough punctuation. If a client wants the photograph’s woods, we look at the CITES essay and we look at whether they want a museum visit or a new object. A new object gets woods I can buy. A museum visit gets a ticket.

Palmwood, amboyna, thuya: period words that need a catalog before they need my mouth. [VERIFY] a named cabinet’s woods. I will not write a substitute recipe. I will write walnut, maple, oak flake, a legal dark hardwood I can invoice. The appetite is the history. The shopping list is the later law.

## Paper before the knife

I draw the pie on paper the size of the top. I mark the flitch direction on each wedge. I cut paper first if the client is choosing between crotch and ribbon. Paper is cheap. The flitch is a book I cannot reprint. I transfer the numbers from paper to the leaves with the same corner habit I use on a crate. Then I dry-fit with tape on the bench and walk around it. North light. A warm lamp. I do not trust the phone.

If a mineral streak becomes a pinwheel, I start the sequence on a quieter page or I move the streak to a wedge that lives at the back of a cabinet. I do not hope the finish will hide a pinwheel. Deco finish is often a high shine. High shine is a magnifier. The factory cousin — a Grand Rapids Deco suite — could hide a grade under a toner. I cannot, and I do not want to. The geometry is the flex. The toner would be a motel habit in a nicer dress.

I leave the habit of calling every sunburst Deco as if a pie were a decade. A pie is a layout. The decade is a taste and a port and a later appendix. The sunburst I will sell is the one I have already taped down, in a wood I can buy, with a center I have already recut in my head.

## What I take

I take the sunburst as a layout test. I take geometry as a reason to veneer. I take the period appetite as a warning, not as a shopping list.

I leave ivory. I leave a fake macassar. I leave the habit of calling every sunburst “Deco” as if a pie were a decade.

Danish teak will take the geometry out and leave a quiet field on a flat door. That is the other modern. Deco is the loud modern. I need both in the pack because clients use “modern” to mean both, and the leaf has to know which room it is walking into. The exotic flitch stays in the museum and in the invoice file. The sunburst, in a wood I can buy, stays on the bench if the client can live with a center they only get once.
