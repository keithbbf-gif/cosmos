I run a thumb along a dining edge and I want to meet oak, not a photograph and not a 0.6 mm arris. The band is a strip of solid, mitered or butted, thicker than a thumbnail, glued to the core, then flushed with a tool that will not dive into the leaf. A hip will find this edge. A vacuum will find this edge. A child will find this edge. The leaf does not get to be the edge.

Iron-on tape is for a closet. I have said it. I will say it on a drawing if I have to.

## Why solid

A veneer wrap around a particle arris is the motel object. A veneer band on a plywood arris is better and still thin at the hit. A solid band is meat. You can ease it. You can dent it and still have wood. You can refinish it without finding a gray core in the first hour.

The band can be the same species as the face, or a quieter cousin, or a contrast if the room wants a lip. I match the face when the piece is quiet. I contrast when the piece is a table that will take a beating and I want the lip to read as a lip.

## Miter versus butt

A miter at the corner is a picture. It also opens. I biscuit or I spline or I just glue a long grain if the band is narrow enough to get away with it. A butt — long grain of one band meeting the end of the other — is stronger and uglier until I ease it. I pick based on the room. A dining top I miter if I can back it up. A shop table I butt.

I glue the band to the core, not to the leaf. The leaf is flush to the band after, or the band is proud and I plane toward the leaf with a tool I trust. I do not plane from the leaf toward the band with a hungry mouth.

## Flush without a dive

A block plane, a scraper, a flush-trim bit that is set and not hungry. A bearing that rides the leaf is a bearing that can chew the leaf. I have a crescent of core from a bit that took a dip at a knot in the band. The crescent would not stain like the face. I patched a leaf. I should have used a plane.

I tape the leaf at the edge as a witness. If the tape shreds, the tool is too hungry. If the tape is fine and the band is flush, I peel and I scrape.

## Thickness

Thicker than a thumbnail is a feeling and a rule I like. [VERIFY] a house millimeter if we have one. Too thick and the top looks like a butcher block lip on a thin face — which can be handsome if I mean it. Too thin and I have built a tape with extra steps.

The band should be thicker than the leaf by enough that a future scrape of the face does not make the band a proud frame. Faces lose meat. Bands should have meat to lose too, or I leave the band a hair proud on purpose and I say so.

## Corners and the kid

A sharp corner on a solid band is a bruise. I ease it. A roundover that eats the leaf is a different bruise. I ease the band and I stop at the leaf, or I ease the whole arris if the leaf is set back. I think about the kid before I think about the photograph.

## Iron-on, honestly

I use iron-on in a closet, a shop cabinet, a place a hip is not invited. I say tape. I still try to put it on a core that will not swell into a mushroom. I do not iron-on a dining top even if the client wants the price. I would rather lose the job.

## The closet tape that lifted in year two

A linen closet I edged with iron-on in a hurry. The core was a particle that later took a drip from a plumbing chase. The arris mushroomed. The tape lifted in a brown line you could see when the door opened. I did not dye the line. I pulled the tape, let the core dry, sealed the edge, and put a thin maple band on with hide and tape as a clamp. Still not a dining lip. Better than a peel.

The procedure I use now on closet tape: sealed core, slip sheet under the iron, trim with a knife, ease the arris so a sleeve does not catch. I say tape on the ticket. A child’s chair and a desk where a belt buckle lives do not get this procedure. They get a solid band. I have priced a whole kitchen at tape money and then I have not written that number on a proposal. Year two is cheaper to refuse than to peel.

## The crescent at the knot

A flush-trim bit, a knot in a white-oak band, a dip. The bearing followed the dip into the leaf. The crescent was a ply stripe the length of a thumb. It would not take stain like the face. I patched a leaf from the folder. The patch was a sibling. The hallway still found it if you knew. I knew.

I use a block plane now for the last of the flush. The bit can hog the waste if the bearing rides a sacrificial tape on the leaf, not the leaf. If the tape shreds, I stop. I have a crescent I did not need. The plane takes a whisper toward the leaf, never a dive from the leaf into the band.

## Setting a leaf back from the lip

On a dining top I sometimes set the leaf shy of the band and I ease only the solid. The hip meets oak. The photograph meets a small step you can feel if you hunt. I say the step. Clients who want a single flush plane get a flush I do with a plane, not a bit I do not trust.

The band should have meat to lose when a future scrape takes the face down a whisper. If the band and the leaf start flush and the face is scraped twice in a life, the band becomes a proud frame. I can leave the band a hair proud on purpose. I write that on the back so a refinisher does not chase the proud into a crescent.

A mitered band on a table I cannot spline is a picture I back up with a long glue surface and a clamp I can leave overnight. A butt on a shop table is a joint I can defend with a hip. I pick in the room, not in a catalog mood. [VERIFY] a house millimeter if we have one. Thicker than a thumbnail is still the test I trust when the drawing is quiet.

## What I write

Band species, thickness, miter or butt, glue, whether the leaf was set back. If a crescent happened, I write the repair. A future refinisher will start at the edge. They should know what they are about to meet.

Sand-through is next: the other way a leaf dies, not at the hip but in the field, under a machine that does not know a business card from a board. The band will not save a field. The band will save a hip. I want both saved. I ease the corner. I set the bit. I keep the tape for closets. The thumb test is the test. If I meet a photograph, I remake the edge.
