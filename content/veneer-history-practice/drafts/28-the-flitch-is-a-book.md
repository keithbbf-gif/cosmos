I fan a flitch on the bench and the cathedral walks. Leaf 14, 15, 16: the peak of the grain moves a finger’s width each time, a movie of a tree. That walk is the only reason a bookmatch looks like a thought instead of a fight. The mill cut the pages in order. My job is not to get clever with the order. My job is to read.

A flitch is a book. I have shuffled a book once because I wanted a prettier cover on the first door. The second door was a stranger. The set looked like a clearance aisle. I taped the numbers back as well as I could and I still lost a page. I do not shuffle.

## How a flitch comes

A cooked log, sliced, stacked in the order of the knife. Tight face, loose face — one side a little fuzzier, one side a little burnished. The mill may stamp a flitch number and a leaf number. They may not. If they do not, I number before I make coffee. Pencil on the back, same corner, same hand. If the leaf cannot take pencil, a tape flag that I will hate later is still better than a shuffle.

I open the crate like a binding. I do not flip a random leaf to “see the color.” Color is a stack problem. One leaf in the window is a liar.

## Tight and loose

Bookmatching flips every other leaf so the tight face and the loose face can meet as mirrors. That flip is why a bookmatch can look like a Rorschach and why it can barber-pole if the faces take stain differently. I look at the pair under finish on a cutoff before I commit a dining top.

Slip-matching keeps the faces the same way up, a current, a repeat, less mirror, less barber risk. The next essay is the religions. This essay is the book they are cut from.

## Defects walk too

A mineral streak that is a mustache on leaf 20 is a thinner mustache on leaf 21 and a memory on leaf 24. If I start the dining top at 20 I have bought a mustache. If I start at 24 I have bought a quieter field and I have spent the mustache on a side I can hide or on a sample. Walking the defect is reading.

Bark inclusions, a knot, a pitch pocket: same walk. I do not put a pitch pocket at an elbow on a dining top because it was “in sequence.” I start the sequence where the elbows are clean. The leftover leaves are still the book. They become a door, a drawer, a sample.

## The crate is part of the job

I keep the flitch together until the set is cut. I do not lend three leaves to another job because they were on top. I have done that. The other job looked good. The set looked robbed.

Offcuts go in the job folder, not in the burn barrel. The repair stage will beg for them. The book is not only the leaves I used. It is the pages I did not.

## Buying a book, not a look

When I buy, I ask to see the flitch, not a sample leaf. A sample leaf is a cover. The book may be a disappointment after page ten. If I cannot see the flitch, I buy a grade and I tell the client it is a grade. I do not promise siblings.

Width and length of the flitch decide the door. A pretty book that is too short is a pretty scarf I do not want. I measure the book before I fall in love with a cathedral.

## The hurry job that robbed a dining set

I lent three leaves from a dining-set flitch to a hurry job because they were on top and the hurry job was a single door and the client for the door was in the building. The door looked good. The dining set looked robbed. The cathedral that should have walked from the top onto the leaves of the table had a gap, a missing chapter. I bought another bundle in the same grade and pretended a cousin was a sibling. The cousin was a cousin. I told the dining client late. I should have told them when I reached for the top pages.

I do not lend from a book that is assigned. I buy the hurry job its own grade or I say no. The crate is part of the job. Offcuts go in the folder, not in a favor. I have told this story to myself enough times that it should have become a lock on the crate. It is a habit. Habits fail on Tuesdays. The photograph of the fan, taken before I cut, is what proved the robbery. Keep the photograph.

## A wardrobe that needed eight feet of agreement

A pair of wardrobe doors is the second object that taught me to measure the book before the crush. Birch, one thought, Biedermeier-manner, eight feet if you count the pair as a facade. The flitch I loved in the first ten pages ran out of width at page eighteen and ran out of quiet at page twenty-two. A mineral streak that was a whisper became a mustache. If I had started the doors at page one I would have bought a handsome left door and a stranger on the right.

I walked the defect. I started the pair at a quieter page. The mustache became a sample and a drawer that lives in the dark. The doors are a current, slip-matched, a seam I spent a morning on. Pale birch showed every ridge. Hide can stain. I tested. The book was long enough once I stopped asking the cover to be the whole novel.

If the book is too short, I do not scarf two flitches and hope the finish unifies them. I buy a longer book or I change the design: a rail, a reveal, two thoughts I admit are two thoughts. One thought is a buying decision. It is not a finishing trick.

## When the mill’s stamps skip

Some crates arrive with a flitch number and no leaf numbers. Some arrive with both, then skip a 7. Some arrive with a stamp that sanded off in the mill’s own handling. I number before coffee. Pencil, back, consistent corner, a box around the flitch number so a block does not take the only 7. If the back will be glued down forever, I photograph the stack with a card that says the order, then I copy the map onto the crate lid and into the job book.

A helper who tidies a stack is a weather event. I have come back from lunch to a “neater” pile. The movie of the tree was still there if you knew the walk. I did not know it for twenty minutes. I had to reread the cathedral the way you reread a book you dropped in the bath. I photographed the next stack before lunch. I still tell the helper the stack is a book. I still photograph.

House habit when stamps are missing is a [VERIFY] if we print an SOP. What I do is: number, map, photograph, do not shuffle to see if I like a prettier cover on the first door. I shuffled once. I said that already. The clearance-aisle set was the cost. I will keep saying it until the habit is cheaper than the set.

## Arkansas light, again

I read a flitch in the light the piece will live in if I can. Cool shop LEDs make walnut green and oak flat. A late window makes walnut gold and oak a church. I have approved a match under LEDs that died at 6 p.m. in a dining room. The book was fine. I had read it in the wrong library.

## What I write

Flitch number, leaf range, which door got which pages, both faces, glue, date. If I flip for a bookmatch, I write *book, 17/18*. A future me sanding a repair will not have to reconstruct the movie.

The next essays are how to bind the pages: book, slip, diamond, the diamond you should not promise, sequence you cannot replace, the leaf that will not lie flat, crossbanding, rift stiles around a flake panel. All of that is reading. If you shuffle first, the rest is fiction.
