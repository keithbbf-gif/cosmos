I raised the grain on a sliced oak leaf with a waterborne I liked and then I sanded the raise as if I had a board. I met the core in a pale freckle. The finish was not the villain. The thickness was the villain and I was the hand. A leaf is not a board. The finishing-chemistry pack can tell you what a film is. This essay is what a film does to a business card of wood.

Scrape more. Sand less. Seal with a purpose. Do not start at 80.

## Grain raise is meat

Waterborne finishes raise grain. Water raises grain. A wet hide wipe raises grain. On a board, you cut the whiskers and you still have a board. On a 0.6 mm face, the whiskers can be the face. I raise on a cutoff first. I knock the whiskers with a paper I can count, or I pick a system that does not need a hard raise, or I use a dewaxed shellac as a diplomat the chemistry pack already named.

I do not “just water-pop” a sliced oak dining top because a forum said so. I can water-pop a solid. I can destroy a leaf.

## The water-pop I will not repeat

A forum said water-pop oak. I popped a sliced dining leaf I had already jointed and bagged. I sanded the raise as if I had a board. I met the core in a pale freckle near a seam I should have scraped first. The finish was a waterborne I still like on solids. The thickness was the villain. I remade the top from pages 12 and 13 of the same book. The folder is why I still had a dining field. Honest on a board can be a wound on a business card.

## A halo on birch

Hide squeeze-out on a Biedermeier-manner birch field — shop birch, not a museum attribution — stained a halo I discovered after the film. I had not finished a cutoff with the same pot. I remade the field. Pale woods remember the pot, the iron, the wet rag.

I now finish the cutoff with the same pot and the same rag before I promise the door. If the cutoff halos, the door will halo. I change the glue or I size or I change the species. I do not discover the halo at delivery.

## Oil on one face, a cup on a calendar

A tabletop I oiled on top and left raw below cupped toward the film in a wet week. I had said both faces in the balance essay. I had rushed the back. I oiled the back. The cup eased. I still should have filmed both when I filmed one.

A finish on a blister is a sealed tent. I tap before I film. I have filmed a shine that was a drum. The fingernail found it at delivery. The spot-repair showed. Tap. Then film.

A jewelry lid I filmed before I tapped. The coin was under a gloss. I cut the film, seated the hide, spot-finished. The lid shows a weather if you hunt. I keep it as a sample. The next lid gets a tap.

I do not strip a leaf with a belt. Chemistry the finishing pack allows, or a remake. I do not write a stripper recipe. [VERIFY] house finish names before a brochure prints a brand. I will print the habit: scrape, stop, both faces, cutoff in the folder. I will not print *blended*.

Crotch drinks in patches under a film as much as under a dye. I size the heart on a cutoff first. If the cutoff shows a raccoon after one rag, the door will show a raccoon after three. I change the schedule or I change the page. I do not flood a dining field and then sand the shine off as if I had meat to spare.

A scraped oak flake under oil is the church window I sold. A ROS swirl under the same oil is a weather the hallway will find at four p.m. I stop when the scribble is shy. I film when the tap is solid. I write the system on the back so a future rag is not a guess.

## Dye and the tight face

Sliced leaves can be burnished by the mill knife. Dye sits on a smeared wall. I wash, or I scuff, or I pick a pigment that does not need a deep drink. I test the book pair. Barber pole is a finish problem as much as a match problem. I do not chase a blink with more dye. I change the system or the layout.

Crotch drinks in patches. I size. I do not flood a heart and starve a wing and then call the raccoon “figure.”

## Film on a tent

A finish on a blister is a sealed tent. Tap before you film. I have filmed a tent because the shine looked like success. The fingernail found a drum at delivery. I spot-repaired and the spot showed. Tap. Then film.

Both faces of the panel should see a similar film if the film is a moisture control. A tabletop oiled on top and raw below is a cup you can schedule. I oil the back. I said it in the balance essay. I say it at the rag.

## Scrape, then a count of paper

I scrape the field. If I paper, I paper with a block, a grit that erases the last scratch, and I stop. [VERIFY] house grit if we have one. I will not print 180 as a religion. I will print: stop.

A wide-belt after a film failed is how factories meet cores twice. I do not strip a leaf with a belt. I use a chemistry the finishing pack allows, or I remake. Methylene chloride is a class I leave in the can or send to a booth. I do not write a stripper recipe.

## Pale woods and hide stain

Birch, maple, a Biedermeier field: hide can stain, water can stain, an iron can stain. I finish a cutoff with the same pot and the same rag. I do not discover a halo on the door.

## What I tell a client

This face cannot take a rental sander. If you want to refinish in ten years, call someone who will scrape, or choose a thicker leaf now. I write it on the back. I say it in the room. They will still rent a sander sometimes. I still write it.

## What I write

Finish system, whether I raised, whether I filmed both faces, cutoff in the folder. If a freckle of core happened, I write remake or patch. I do not write *blended*.

The last essay is the sentence in the room: the client who hates the word, the tag that must not steal, the mixed piece that is most of the good furniture. Finish will make them shy. I say veneer before the shine. I say core. I say band. I say both faces. Then I let the flake be a church window without an apology. The film is a use. The leaf is a face. The word is older than the motel. I can stand next to all three if I did not start at 80.
