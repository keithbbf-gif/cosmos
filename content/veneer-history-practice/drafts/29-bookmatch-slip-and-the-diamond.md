I tape a bookmatch and the oak makes a butterfly I did not draw. The seam is a spine. If the joint is proud, the butterfly has a ridge like a bad binding. If the faces take stain differently, the butterfly blinks: barber pole, a stripe that is tight then loose then tight. I look at that before I promise a dining top. I have promised a dining top. I have then spent a day trying to make two faces agree. I should have promised a slip.

Book, slip, diamond, four-way, radial, a sunburst: these are layouts, not morals. The flitch is the book. The layout is how you bind it.

## Book

Every other leaf flipped. Mirror. Crotch loves it. Cathedral oak loves it and can also look like a Rorschach someone did not ask for. Quiet rift can book and still look quiet if the stripe is straight.

Barber pole is the tax. Tight face versus loose face, a finish that bites differently. I finish a cutoff pair. If they blink, I slip instead, or I pick a finish that does not bite, or I accept a mild blink and tell the client. I do not hope.

The seam must be jointed. A light box, a shooting plane, a veneer jointer if you have one. I have taped a gap and filled it with glue and dust and called it a seam. It photographed. At 4 p.m. it was a river.

## Slip

Leaves the same way up, edge to edge, a repeat, a current. Less mirror, less barber, a quieter door. Architectural panels often slip because a wall of butterflies is a lot of butterflies. I slip rift stiles in my head even when the panel is a book: the stiles should not fight the panel’s mirror.

Slip is how you spend a flitch economically on a run of drawers that should look like a family, not like a stack of inkblots.

## Diamond and four-way

A diamond is four triangles, figure pointing in, a kite. Four-way is a book in both directions, a center that is a cross. Radial is a sunburst without the Deco name. These are drawings. They spend the flitch fast. They spend the afternoon faster.

I dry-fit on the bench with tape and I walk around it. I do not sell a diamond from a pencil sketch. The sketch does not show the mineral streak that becomes a pinwheel. The sketch does not show the center wad.

Centers are where diamonds go to die. I cut a disk or a small square of a quieter wood, or I accept a tight meeting and scrape for a week. I do not fill a hole with a waffle of leftovers.

## Running match and the room

A running match is a slip that continues from door to door, a wall that reads as one flitch. It is handsome and it is a hostage situation: one door gets damaged and the replacement is a cousin. I tell the client. I keep leftover leaves. I number the doors on the back as a map of the wall.

A balance match is a book or a layout centered on each door so each door is a complete thought. Safer for a future repair. Less “one tree walking.” I ask which room they want.

## What I will not promise

I will not promise a diamond on a flitch I have not opened. I will not promise no barber pole on a book I have not finished a test of. I will not promise a running match on a grade bundle that is not a flitch.

I will promise a layout I have dry-fitted, a seam I have jointed, a story about repair. That is enough promise for a face.

## The four-way I sold from a sketch

I sold a four-way walnut coffee table from a pencil sketch because the client nodded and I liked the nod. The sketch was a cross. The flitch, when I opened it, had a mineral streak that became a pinwheel and a tight face that blinked against the loose face in two of the four leaves. I had not finished a cutoff. I had not dry-fitted. I had a sketch.

I dry-fitted late, on a bench that was already paid for in the job book. The pinwheel was a fact. I moved the center to a quieter page and I slipped two of the quadrants instead of booking them so the barber pole would shut up. The table is a four-way that is not a pure four-way. I told the client. They kept it. I kept the sketch in the folder with a line through the promise. A diamond or a four-way is a drawing. The drawing does not show the wad at the center. I now cut a small square of a quieter wood for a center if the meeting is a wad. I do not fill a hole with a waffle of leftovers. I did that on a Deco sunburst. I do not need two waffles in one career.

Centers are where diamonds go to die. I walk around the dry-fit. I do not sell from a smile.

## A box lid that is a herringbone, not a dining dare

A jewelry-box lid is the second object I will promise more often than a diamond dining top. Herringbone is two leaves, opposite grain, a band or a field. It is a cousin of the chevron and a cousin of the English face. On a lid the size of a book I can joint, tape, scrape, and stop. I have belted a herringbone lid because the tape hid a ridge and I was proud. The proud pass went through at the seam. I remade the lid from the next pages. The first lid is a sample of a remake.

I shoot the edges on a light box. A gap I taped and filled with glue and dust photographed at noon and became a river at 4 p.m. I said that about a bookmatch. It is truer on a herringbone because the grain keeps changing its mind and the eye follows the zigzag. The joint is the edge you shot. The tape is not the joint.

Slip on a run of drawers is how I spend a flitch as a family. Book on a dining top is how I spend it as a thought. Diamond on a table I have not taped is how I spend an afternoon as fiction. The box lid keeps me honest. If I cannot joint a herringbone the size of a book, I have no business selling a kite the size of a parlor.

## The wall I numbered backward

I numbered a run of closet doors left to right on the bench and hung them right to left from the window. The current ran the wrong way. The cathedral walked into a corner instead of along the hall. I pulled two doors and swapped them. The map on the back — *door 3 = leaves 30–33* — was correct for the shop and wrong for the house. I now write *as hung, from the window* on the map. The room is the library. The shop is a draft.

A running match still photographs well. I do not assume they want the hostage because of the photograph. I ask. If they want it, they pay for leftover pages or they accept a cousin later. The backward hang was my error, not the flitch’s.

## Tape is not the joint

Veneer tape on the show face, or a stitch, or a good tape on the back: it holds the drawing until the glue does. The joint is the edge you shot. I remove tape with moisture and patience if it is gummed, or I sand lightly if I like living dangerously. I have sanded through at a taped seam because the tape hid a ridge and I was proud. Scrape the ridge. Do not belt the drawing.

## Light, again

A bookmatch in north light can be a church. The same book in a warm dining lamp can be a butterfly that is too much butterfly. I take a phone photo in both and I still do not trust the phone. I ask the client to stand in their room if I can. I bring a sample board. I do not bring a sketch of a diamond and a smile.

Rift stiles around a flake panel is a later essay and a layout I promise more often than a diamond. It is a door that stays a door. The diamond is a table that stays a dare. I like both. I only sell the one I have already taped down on the bench.
