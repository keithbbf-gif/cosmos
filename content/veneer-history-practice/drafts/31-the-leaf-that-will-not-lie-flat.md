The crotch came out of the crate like a wave. I set it on the bench and it rocked. Sequence numbers were on the back, a neat 8 and 9, a book I wanted. If I had bagged it that afternoon I would have bagged a wave. The press would have looked like success until the first hollow tap. I weighted the corners, misted the back, put a caul on, and I waited. The wave became a lake. Then I glued.

Figured leaves do not owe you a plane. Tension wood, a fork, a burl of buds: they remember the tree. Flattening is not a personality. It is a climate you apply on purpose, smaller than a mill’s cook, kinder, slower.

## Dry chip, wet rag

A leaf that is too dry will shatter when you unroll it. A leaf that is too wet will grow in the glue and then shrink into a telegraph. I want a leaf that is limp enough to take a caul and dry enough to not be a sponge. I do not have a pin-meter religion for a 0.6 mm face. I have a hand and a shop that is not a swamp or a kiln.

A plastic-wrapped bundle that sweats is a wet rag. I open it and let it even out under weights, not in a fan. A bundle that crackles is a chip. I mist the back — the back — and I weight. I do not soak the show face and then wonder why the figure raised into a mess.

## Weights, cauls, time

A sheet of plywood, a few bricks, a night: the old method. A vacuum bag without glue, a dry run, can pull a wave down if the leaf can stand the bend. I have pulled a wave into a crack. The bag is not gentler than I am. It is more even and more willing to break a dry burl.

I tape splits as I flatten so they do not run. Veneer tape is a stitch. A split that runs through a crotch heart is a raccoon I will not finish.

## Baths I will not make a religion

Glycerin and water recipes live in shop folklore. Some people swear. Some leaves later take finish in a blotch. [VERIFY] any house bath before it becomes SOP. I have not made a bath the house standard. I have used controlled moisture and time. If an editor later cites a tested bath, it can go here with a date. Until then I will not print a mix.

Heat helps a little and burns a little. I do not iron a show face. I have ironed a show face. The shine was a burnish I then had to sand, and sanding a crotch is how you meet the core.

## Flatten the pair together

Bookmatched pairs should see the same hours, the same mist, the same caul. If I flatten 8 and not 9, I have two climates. They will not agree in the glue. The book is a climate as well as a picture.

## When to refuse the leaf

Some burls will not flatten without a bath I do not trust or a crack I do not want. Some crotches are a sculpture. I can use them on a small box, a jewelry lid, a sample. I will not force them onto a dining field. The client can have a quieter page from the same book, or a different book, or a solid turning if they wanted a sculpture.

A paper-backed burl is already flattened by someone else. The tax is the paper and the sanding limit. I use it when the sculpture is a price I will not pay in hours.

## Glue after, not during

Flattening is a dry operation. Glue is a wet operation. If I combine them I am hoping the glue will act as a clamp on a shape that is still moving. Sometimes it works. Sometimes I get a blister that is a memory of a wave. I flatten first.

The shop-stage bag essay will talk about glue in the bag. This essay is the night before.

## The jewelry lid I allowed

A walnut crotch that would not become a lake sat on the bench for three nights. The heart still sprang. I could have bagged it onto a dining field and hoped the core would argue it down. I have hoped. The hollow tap in October was the argument coming back.

I cut a jewelry-box lid from that page instead. Small field, a caul I could cover with bricks, a client who wanted a sculpture they could lift. The wave became a shallow dish I could live with because the object was allowed to be a dish. The dining table got pages 4 and 5 from the same book — quieter, flatter, the pages I should have offered first.

The object decides the leaf. A chip that will not lie flat is not a moral failure of the tree. It is a leaf that wants a smaller sentence. I keep a sample board of refused pages so a client can see the sculpture and the door in the same light. They usually pick the door. Sometimes they pick the lid. I do not glue the lid’s climate onto a table to save a sales conversation.

## A split that ran under the caul

I misted the back of an oak burl and weighted it with a plywood caul. In the morning the wave was kinder and a split had run from the heart to a sequence stamp. I had not taped. Veneer tape is a stitch I skip when I am tired. The stitch would have been a night. The raccoon was a week.

I taped what was left, flattened another day, and used the page on a box where the split could become a line in the figure. I did not put it on a door. A door is a rectangle that has to stay a rectangle under a knuckle. A split through a burl heart is a line that will take finish differently and then look like a crack the client caused.

Now I tape first. Then mist. Then weight. The order is a lesson I paid for in a page I cannot reprint. The mill will not send page 9 again. The folder got the offcut. The crate got a note: *split in flatten, taped, not for a field.*

## How I mist without making a sponge

The back only. A spray bottle that fogs, not a stream. I want the leaf to slump, not to drink. I wait until the fog disappears into the fiber, then I put the caul on. If I fog the show face I raise the figure into a nap I will later sand, and sanding a crotch is how I meet the core.

I do not stack a wet leaf on a dry sibling. Bookmatched pairs see the same fog, the same minutes, the same plywood. If 8 is limp and 9 is still a chip, I wait on both. A bag will not iron a climate disagreement into a butterfly. It will glue the disagreement down.

A paper-backed burl skips this night if the paper is already a flatten someone else paid for. I still weight it overnight in this shop so it meets our air. I do not assume a crate that crossed the country and a July in this county are the same climate. [VERIFY] any house bath before it becomes a printed mix. Until then the procedure is fog, weight, tape, wait. Then glue.

I knock the leaf after the wait. It should sound like a leaf, not like a chip. If it still rocks on the bench I give it another night. The numbers are patient. A potato chip with a sequence stamp is still a potato chip.

## What I write

*Flattened, dates, mist or not, still a little spring at the heart.* The next person should know this leaf has a history of being a wave. They should not belt it to “true it up.”

I knock the flattened leaf. It should sound like a leaf, not like a chip. I dry-fit the book. Then I tape. Then I glue. The numbers waited. They are patient. I should be. A potato chip with a sequence stamp is still a potato chip. The book can wait a night. The door cannot wait a remake.
