The mill stamped 1147 on the back of a walnut leaf, then a small 22. I wrote 1147-22 in the job book and I cut the dining top from 22 and 23, bookmatched, and I put 24 and 25 in the folder because the client had a child and a future leaf of water. Two years later the child happened. I had 24. The repair was a sibling. If I had burned 24, the repair would have been a story.

Sequence numbers are a one-time map. The tree is spent. The slicer will not pass that way again. I treat the numbers like a cut list I cannot reprint from memory.

## When the mill does not stamp

I stamp. Pencil, back, consistent corner, a box around the flitch number so a sanding block does not take the only 7. If the back will be glued, I photograph the stack with a card that says the order, then I transfer numbers to the crate map. I have relied on memory. Memory is a shuffle.

A helper who “tidies” a stack is a weather event. I tell them the stack is a book. I still photograph.

## Sets, suites, walls

A dining table and two leaves: one flitch if I can. A wall of doors: running match or balance match, numbered on the back as a map, *door 3 = 30–33*. A suite of bedroom pieces: either one book spent in a planned order or a grade I have admitted is a grade.

I do not start the headboard with the prettiest pages and give the nightstands the dregs unless the client chose that. The prettiest pages might belong on the piece they touch least. I ask. I do not assume the table gets the glory. Sometimes the table gets the elbows and the dresser gets the quiet.

## The folder is the reprint

Offcuts, a full spare leaf, a cutoff with the finish schedule on it: the folder is the only reprint. I write the finish on the cutoff because “walnut, oil” is not a schedule. I have matched a repair to a memory of an oil and been wrong by a year of light.

The folder lives with the job, not in a scrap barrel that becomes kindling. I have burned a reprint. I do not tell that story to a client. I tell it to myself when I reach for the barrel.

## Reorders

“Can you get more of that flitch?” is a question with one honest answer. No. I can get that mill, that grade, that species. I cannot get 1147-26. If the client may want a later piece, we buy the leaves now. Storage is cheaper than a cousin.

I store labeled. An unlabeled crate of pretty walnut is a novel with the binding gone.

## The headboard that took the glory

I started a bedroom suite with the prettiest pages on the headboard because the headboard is what photographs. The nightstands got the dregs: a mustache, a quieter pore, a color that had walked. The client touches the nightstand every evening and looks at the headboard from the door. The nightstand told. The headboard glowed. The set looked like a clearance aisle that had kept one good piece.

I should have asked. Sometimes the table gets the elbows and the dresser gets the quiet. Sometimes the nightstand is the face they live with. I now lay the suite out as a map before I cut: headboard, dresser, pair of stands, which pages, which thought. I do not assume glory belongs on the largest rectangle. The child-and-water dining top in the lede is one map. A bedroom is another. Both are one-time books.

I remade one nightstand from leftover pages I had almost burned. The reprint was the folder. The mill was not going to print 1147 again. The client saw a sibling. I saw a barrel I did not use. I still tell myself the barrel story when I reach for scrap that has a number on it.

## Pencil the glue smear ate

I numbered a stack in pencil and then smeared hide across the back of leaf 22 in a hurry. The 22 became a weather stain. I had a photograph of the fan. I did not have a photograph of that leaf next to 23 after the smear. I reconstructed the walk by the cathedral and I was one page off. The dining top I cut from the reconstructed pair was a cousin bookmatch, not the sibling I had promised. I recut from 24 and 25. I had 24 because of the child. I used the child leaf on a pride mistake. I bought more leaves from the same mill and the same grade. I did not get 1147-26. I said that already. I am saying it again because a smear is how numbers die.

I now box the flitch number, keep the leaf number away from the glue edge, and photograph 22 next to 23 after I number and before I spread. If the back will be hidden forever, the photograph is the map. Phones lie about color. They lie less about order. I do not put permit numbers or client IDs on photos that might wander.

A helper who tidies remains a weather event. A glue smear is a weather event I cause. Both want the same reply: photograph, map, do not trust memory. Memory is a shuffle.

## Extra leaves as a line item

If the client can already imagine a later piece — a second table, a child’s desk, a repair — I write spare pages as a line on the ticket: *spare leaves, flitch 1147, stored labeled*. I store them flat, weighted, away from the vent that cooked a cherry sofa table in July. Species, flitch, leaf range, a job name I can stand. No cute trade name on the crate.

I have opened my own unlabeled crate of pretty walnut and spent an hour rereading a tree I had already read. The binding was gone. The line item is how a client joins that fact without a lecture, and how I keep the binding.

## The insert leaf that broke the walk

A dining table with two removable leaves is a sequence problem wearing a hinge and a pin. The main top was 22 and 23. The inserts should have been 24–27, in order, so the cathedral kept walking when the table grew. I cut the inserts from 28 and 31 because those pages were quieter and I told myself no one would open the table. They opened it at Thanksgiving. The walk jumped. A mineral streak that had left the main top came back as a mustache on the far insert. The set looked like two thoughts pretending to be one.

I now dry-fit the grown table on sawhorses before I glue any of it. Main top, insert, insert, in the order of the meal. I number the underside of each insert *leaf A, toward the window* so a helper cannot rotate a quiet page into a stranger. Pins and clips go into the core, not through a face I cannot replace. If the book is not long enough for a grown table, I do not promise a walk. I promise a balance match on each piece and I say so.

The child-and-water spare is still in the folder. It is not an insert. It is a reprint for a wound. Do not spend the reprint on a holiday you have not dry-fitted.

## Digital numbers

I photograph the fan, the crate, the map. I put the photos in the job folder too. Phones lie about color and they do not lie as much about order. A photo of 22 next to 23 has saved me when a leaf lost its pencil to a glue smear.

I do not put permit numbers or client IDs on photos that might wander. Species, flitch, leaf, job name I can stand.

## What I tell a client

This face is from a book that will not be printed again. If you want a later leaf, we keep pages. If you damage a running-match door, the replacement will be a cousin unless we have the pages. They can pay for pages. They can accept cousins. They cannot have a reprint from the tree.

CITES made some books unprintable for another reason. Sequence made all books unprintable once the knife passed. Both make me careful. Only one is a law. The other is a habit. I can choose the habit every Tuesday.

The next essay is the leaf that will not lie flat: burl, crotch, a potato chip that still has numbers. The numbers do not flatten it. They just tell you which chip is the sibling of which chip. Flatten first. Then bind. Then glue. I have glued a chip. I do not recommend the novel that follows.
