The nightstand in the motel had an oak photograph wrapped around a box that drank a glass of water and let go. The edge was a tape. Under the tape was a biscuit-colored core that swelled into a mushroom. The tag, if there had been a tag, would have said oak. That object is what a lot of people mean by veneer. I do not defend it. I will not put a sequenced flitch in the same sentence without a period between them.

The twentieth century did not ruin the method. It ruined the sentence. Print grain, vinyl wrap, a paper that peeled, a particle core, a “solid oak” that was a picture of oak: those are products. Veneer is a leaf. When the market used one word for both, the word went to the motel.

## Print is not a species

A printed film can be handsome in a laboratory kitchen. It is not oak. I will install a printed panel if the brief is a closet and the sentence is honest. I will not stand next to a printed dining table and say hardwood. The print has no flitch number. It has a roll number. That is a different shop.

Transfer papers, photo-grain, a laminate with a registered emboss: the industry got good at pictures. The studiolo was a picture made of wood. The motel was a picture made of ink. I will not pretend they are the same just because both are pictures.

## Peel is an edge religion

Iron-on tape, a thin wrap folded around a particle arris, a hot-melt that lets go in a humid week: peel is how the word died in the hand. A solid band, thicker than a thumbnail, glued as wood, flush-trimmed without a dive — that is an edge that can take a hip. I have said this and I will say it until the pack ends.

If I use an iron-on, I use it in a closet and I say tape. I do not say band.

## The tag

“Solid oak” on a faced top is a theft. “Oak veneer on a hardwood plywood core” is a sentence. “Oak-look” is a sentence. The century preferred the theft because the theft sold. I prefer the sentence because I have to live in the county after the sale.

I will not name a living retailer here without a tag in my hand. [VERIFY] or cut. The habit is enough. The habit is the ruin.

## Sand-through as a public teacher

A homeowner with a rental sander and a desk from 1984 is how the word died in the afternoon. They wanted to “refinish.” They found a pale core. They told their sister veneer is cheap. Their sister believed them. The desk may have been cheap. The lesson was sloppy.

I write a note on the back and I still sand as if no one will read it. I tell a client: if you ever refinish this, scrape, do not start at 80. I cannot follow them home. I can make the face thick enough to have a chance, or I can use sawn leaf on a piece that will be loved by a sander.

## Particleboard drinks

The motel nightstand failed at the edge because the core was a drinker. Particleboard swells into a mushroom when a glass finds the raw arris. A leaf on that swell is a skin on a bruise. I do not face particleboard and call it furniture unless the brief is a painted box I am honest about and the leaf is not invited. A hardwood-plywood core can still drink at an unbanded edge. The difference is speed and shame. Particleboard is fast. Plywood is a slower tell.

I have been asked to “re-veneer” a mushroomed nightstand. There was no nightstand left at the arris, only a crumble. I declined. A new box in a core I trust, a leaf if they want a leaf, a solid band, both faces — that is a new object. Wrapping a photograph around a crumble is how the word died. I will not be the shop that buries the crumble under a better photograph.

If I use an iron-on tape, I use it in a closet and I say tape. A kitchen arris that takes a hip and a splash is a solid band or it is a job I refuse. Peel is an edge religion. I have said that. The drink is the reason the religion matters.

## A kitchen I would not call oak

A kitchen arrived as a brief with a sample door: oak photograph, registered emboss, a box construction that was a price. The owner wanted me to “make the island real oak to match.” Match a photograph with a flitch and you will lose in the noon light. I said so. We built the island as quartered oak, a leaf on a balanced core, a solid band, and we let the perimeter doors stay a print with a sentence that said print. Two religions in one room. The island does not apologize. The perimeter does not pretend.

I will install a printed closet. I will not stand next to a printed dining table and say hardwood. The studiolo was a picture made of wood. The kitchen print is a picture made of ink. I will not pretend they are the same just because both are pictures. The island is the second object in this essay: a leaf job that had to live next to a roll number. The honesty was the period between them.

I will not name a living retailer without a tag in my hand. [VERIFY] or cut. The habit is enough. The habit is the ruin.

## The desk from 1984 I was asked to save

A homeowner brought a desk from 1984 with a pale wound the size of a hand at the writing edge. A rental sander, 80, an afternoon, a sister who now hates the word. They asked me to stain the core so it would “go back to walnut.” The core was not walnut. The face had been a walnut leaf the thickness of a business card. I could have dyed the wound and sold a fog. I put a dated patch from a legal walnut I had, scraped to the surrounding film, and wrote on the underside *repair, year, not original face*. The desk is still a desk. It is not a proof that veneer is cheap. It is a proof that a belt is a remake.

I offered a new top: sawn leaf, a band, both faces, a note. They kept the wound and the patch. That is allowed. What is not allowed is me agreeing that the afternoon was the method’s fault. The method did not fail. The sentence failed, and then the grit failed.

## What I will not do in response

I will not stop veneering. I will not call every leaf a confession. I will not compete with the motel object on price. I will not distress a good face to look “more solid.” Solid has tool marks and an end grain. A leaf has a band and a backer. They should look like themselves.

I will not sneer at every factory. I sneer at the tag. A factory that says “walnut veneer, engineered core” is in the same honest family as my note. A factory that says “solid walnut” on a photograph is in the motel family.

## The word I still use

I say veneer in the room. I say it early, before the finish shine makes people shy. I say what the core is. I say both faces. I say the band is solid. I let the client hate the word and then I show them a bookmatch that a slab could not stay flat for. Sometimes they come over. Sometimes they want a glue-up. Both are furniture.

The ruined word and the restricted tree are different problems. One is a sentence. One is a law. I can fix the sentence in a shop. I cannot fix the law with a stain.
