A crossband around a walnut field is a frame that also changes direction on purpose. The grain of the band runs around the picture, a border that can hide a seam and can take a little hit at the edge if it is thick enough. A stringing is a thin line — holly, maple, an ebonized pear — that makes the frame read. I like both. I sand through the stringing if I am proud. I have. The line becomes a dotted line, then a memory, then a core.

This is layout as carpentry. The field is the book. The band is the binding you can see.

## Crossband

I cut strips across the grain of a quieter leaf, or I buy banding. The corners are miters if I am awake, or butts if the band is so cross that a miter is a fight. A miter in a crossband is two end grains meeting. They open if the glue is a wish. I tape. I dry-fit. I do not fill a daylight with dust and call it a miter.

Width: wider than a string, narrower than a stile. A band that is too wide becomes a second field and wants its own match. A band that is too thin is a string that thinks it is a band. [VERIFY] house default if we have one. I cut to the piece.

Species: a contrast that is legal and local if I can. Maple on walnut. Walnut on oak. An ebonized line if the room wants punctuation. I do not need a listed dark wood to get a border.

## Stringing

A groove, a strip, hide or a tight PVA, a scrape. Commercial stringing is even and a little dead. Shop-cut stringing is alive and a little crooked. I pick based on the room. A Federal-manner drawer wants even. A shop box can want alive.

Holly is pale and dents. Ebonized stringing chips on an arris. I ease the arris after, not before I lose the line.

Sanding is the enemy. I scrape the field and I avoid the line with a block that has a hole in my attention. A ROS on a stringing is a coin flip. I have lost the flip.

## Herringbone as a band

Two leaves, opposite grain, a chevron that runs as a border. English, handsome, a place the grain changes every quarter inch. A belt loves it. A scraper can handle it. I joint the two leaves first as a pair, then I cut the band. I do not assemble a herringbone out of leftovers at the corner. The corner is where leftovers show.

## Movement

A band around a large field is a frame of wood that wants to move the other way from the field. On a leaf, the movements are small and the glue is the argument. On a big table, a wide crossband can check. I keep bands modest on big tops, or I use a solid lip that is a different system.

The band is not a substitute for a solid edge that takes a hip. A crossband of 3/4 inch on a plywood arris is still a thin face at the hip if the band is veneer. A solid oak lip is a lip. I use both: lip for the hit, band for the picture, or a lip that is the band if the species agrees.

## Inlay kits and the cartoon

A laser-cut cartoon of a shell in a kit is a studiolo joke. I can use a kit if the client wants a kit and I say kit. I will not call it intarsia. The groove still has to be clean. The kit still sands through. The religion is the same: layout, then knife, then glue, then scrape.

## The Federal drawer that taught the arris

I built a Federal-manner drawer for a sideboard — not a Phyfe attribution, a shop drawer in that grammar. Crossband, a holly string, a walnut field. The stringing was even because the room wanted even. I sanded the field with a block that wandered onto the line. The holly became a dotted line at the pull. I remade the drawer front.

The remake taught the arris. A stringing sits proud of nothing. It is a groove and a strip and a scrape that stops. I now tape the string after glue, a sacrificial ribbon, and I scrape the field up to the tape. I peel. I ease the arris with a single pass that does not have a vote.

Bands I have measured on pieces I could handle were often thicker than a modern inlay kit. [VERIFY] any named collection piece before a caption. What I trust is the feeling: old stringing had meat you could ease. A laser kit is a cartoon with no meat. I can use a kit. I say kit. I still tape the line.

## How I cut a shop string

A quiet maple leaf, a straightedge, a knife, several light passes. Width: a thought, not a ruler religion. Too wide and it is a band pretending to be punctuation. Too thin and it disappears in the first scrape. I cut more than I need and I pick the straightest run.

I groove with a cutting gauge or a small router with a fence I trust, depth shy of the core. If I meet the core in the groove I have built a canal that will telegraph. I dry-fit the string. Hide on a small line, or a tight PVA I have tested on a pale cutoff. I wipe. Dried hide in holly is a stain I have made.

Commercial stringing is even and a little dead. I use it when the drawer is one of eight and the eight must agree. Shop-cut stringing is for a box that can be alive. I do not mix them on one face. The hallway will find the one that is even next to the one that wandered.

## When a miter opens after the film

A crossband miter is two end grains meeting. They take glue poorly. They take climate after the film even more poorly. I had a walnut band around an oak field on a writing box. The miters were tight on Tuesday. In October they were hairlines that took oil like a splinter.

I had not taped across the miter. I had not sized the end grain. I had filled a daylight with dust on one corner and called it closed. The film found it. I lifted the band at that corner, recut, taped, glued, and I lived with a small color mismatch because the band had already seen a year of light.

Now I dry-fit under a raking lamp. I size end grain with a thin hide. I tape. I do not fill. If the miter will not close, I recut the band. A butt corner on a shop table is stronger and I use it when the room can stand a butt. A dining lip that is a solid oak band is a different system — this essay should not steal the hip. The hip is meat. This is a picture frame.

## A herringbone I assembled from leftovers

I once built a border from leftover chevrons because I had run out of the paired leaves. The corners were a meeting of three directions. Handsome in a photograph. A belt later found the high spots. A client later found the place the leftover did not continue the chevron. I remade the border from a fresh pair.

Joint the two leaves first. Then cut the band. Then miter. Leftovers go in the folder, not into the last corner of a field I have already sold. English herringbone is a grammar. Leftovers are a shuffle. I do not shuffle a border I have asked a room to read as one thought.

## What I write

Band species, string species, width, glue. If the stringing is ebonized, I write ebonized, not ebony. A future stain job will thank me.

The last matching essay is rift stiles around a flake panel: the door layout I trust more than a diamond, the one this pack has been aiming at since the foundation. Bands and strings can sit on that door too. They should not replace the stiles. The stiles are structure in the picture. The band is a frame. The flake is the thought. Keep the jobs apart.
