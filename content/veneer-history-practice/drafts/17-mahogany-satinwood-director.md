The *Gentleman and Cabinet-Maker’s Director* (first edition 1754) is a book of suggestions that got treated like a law. Thomas Chippendale sold plates of Chinese, Gothic, and modern — meaning French — taste to gentlemen and to the shops that wanted their money. [VERIFY] which edition a plate comes from before a caption. I have used a Director plate as a conversation with a client. I have not built a plate. A plate has no flitch number.

The faces of the decades that used the book were often mahogany, then satinwood, then a pictorial top that wanted to be a painting again. The carcases stayed oak or pine. The leaf did the fashion. The joint did the furniture.

## Mahogany as a trade, not a color

*Swietenia* from the Caribbean and Central America is the historical furniture mahogany. What a bundle says now may be sapele, khaya, “African mahogany,” a stained something. I write the Latin when the client is paying for a period face or when a finish will tell on me. CITES and harvest rules are a later essay and a current invoice problem. [VERIFY] what we actually buy this year.

Mahogany crotch is why veneer stays in the dining room. A solid crotch top of that width is a museum dare. A matched pair of leaves is how a lot of London dining happened. The Director does not tell you how to tape the seam. The shop does.

Ribbon-stripe mahogany, plain, pommele: the words are figure words. I buy the figure I mean. I do not buy “mahogany” and hope for pommele. Hope is not a flitch.

## Satinwood and the lighter room

West Indian satinwood (*Zanthoxylum*, the furniture one) and later Ceylon satinwood are the pale, silky faces of Hepplewhite and Sheraton plates — books that are also not laws. [VERIFY] species on a named piece. Bee’s-wing figure in satinwood is a quiet flex. It sands through if you are proud. It finishes like a gem if you scrape and stop.

A satinwood crossband around a mahogany field is an English sentence I still like. The two woods move differently. The band is small, so it can get away with it. A satinwood door in a mahogany case is a larger argument. I balance. I do not assume the Director thought about my July.

## Gothic, Chinese, modern

The Director’s tastes are costumes. Gothic tracery in a mahogany door is a carving and sometimes a fret that is a thin wood, which is a cousin of veneer. Chinese fret is a lattice that can be solid or applied. “Modern” is Paris. None of these costumes excuse a cup. I can cut a fret. I cannot cut a climate.

When a client brings a plate, I ask which part they want: the leg, the fret, the face, the mood. If they want the mood, I may refuse the fret and give them the mahogany field and a quiet band. If they want the fret, we talk about how a thin lattice gets knocked by a chair. The plate does not show the chair.

## Oak underneath

London carcases in oak or pine, faces in the fashion wood: the habit holds. I have repaired a “Chippendale” revival whose carcase was a softwood that had been eaten and whose face was a mahogany that had been belted through at the handle. The plate was innocent. The belt was not.

A revival is allowed. A revival that calls itself 1754 is not allowed. I date my work with the year I made it. I can say “after a Director idea” if the idea is real. I cannot say Chippendale made it in Wilmar.

## A plate is a conversation, not a cut list

A client emailed a JPEG of a Director plate with the contrast blown out and a note that said they wanted that desk. The plate had a fret, a carved knee, a leather top, and a scale that was a suggestion. I printed it larger and still could not read a thickness. I asked which part they wanted. They wanted the mood and the fret. I refused the fret for a hallway desk that would take a chair back every winter. I gave them a mahogany field, a satinwood crossband, and a solid lip on the writing edge. The plate stayed on the table as a conversation. It never became a cut list.

I have also tried to scale a Gothic traceried door from a plate by counting the voids and dividing a width. The plate lied about the rail that has to take a hinge. I built a sample rail in oak, applied a thin fret, and hung a hinge as a test. The fret cracked at the first swing. The plate had not shown the swing. I keep that sample. It is a Director lesson I can hold.

When I use a plate now, I write on it: *leg / face / fret / mood*, and I cross out the ones we are not buying. [VERIFY] the edition before a caption. The book is public. The flitch is not in the book.

## The dining top the plate never mentioned

Chippendale plates are full of chairs and case pieces. The dining top that actually used the mahogany trade is often a leaf job the book does not bother to draw: a crotch bookmatch, a band, a thickness that can take a gravy boat. I laid a pair of mahogany-crotch leaves for a dining table in a house that already owned a Director reprint. The reprint sat on the sideboard. The flitch sat on the bench. They did not automatically meet.

The crotch wanted to be a butterfly. The tight face and the loose face took the oil differently. I finished a cutoff pair. They blinked. I slipped the field instead of booking it and I told the client why. A slip of crotch is a current, not a Rorschach. The plate on the sideboard still showed a carved leg. The leg we built was solid mahogany I could name, or a species I could defend. [VERIFY] the invoice Latin. The face was the leaf. The plate did not tape the seam. I did.

I banded the top thicker than a thumbnail because a dining arris is a hip that sits down. A Director drawing will give you a profile. It will not give you the band.

## Sapele in a crate marked with a color

I bought a bundle labeled mahogany because a finish sample had been approved under that word. The leaves were sapele. Ribbon, handsome, a different smell, a different pore. I could have stained them toward the sample and hoped. I called the client and said the crate was sapele. We kept the crate and changed the sentence in the room. The dining chairs — a second object in that job — were already a solid I had bought as khaya and written as khaya. The table face and the chairs did not have to be one Latin. They had to be two honest lines.

Hope is not a flitch. A trade name is not a species. The Director sold taste to gentlemen who were not going to open a crate. I open the crate. If the leaf is *Swietenia*, I write *Swietenia*. If it is sapele, I write sapele. CITES and the current invoice are a later essay and a folder I keep with the job. [VERIFY] what we actually buy this year. I will not stain a ribbon and say Cuban.

## What I take

I take the publicness of the book. Patterns travel. A good shop can use a plate without becoming a plate. I take mahogany crotch as a leaf job. I take satinwood as a scrape-and-stop wood. I take the oak carcase.

I leave the habit of calling every ball-and-claw a Chippendale as if the man invented a foot. I leave the habit of staining sapele and saying Cuban. I leave the plate on the table as a conversation, not as a cut list.

Phyfe will take this language to New York. Biedermeier will refuse some of the costume and keep the leaf. The Director sits in the middle: a shop that advertised, a face that was a trade wind, a book that still shows up in my email as a JPEG with the contrast blown out. I ask for a better picture of the plate. Then I ask for the flitch.
