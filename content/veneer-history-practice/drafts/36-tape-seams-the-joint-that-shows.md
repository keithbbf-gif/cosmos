I run a fingernail across a bookmatch and I stop. There is a ridge. The glue is already set. The ridge is now a river I can scrape toward, not a river I can unsay. I scrape. I do not belt. I should have shot the edges until the fingernail was bored.

The seam is the only confession on a quiet field. Tape is not the confession. Tape is a clamp you will remove.

## Shooting the edge

A light box, or a raking lamp, two leaves, a plane or a jointer made for this. I want no daylight and no overlap. Overlap is a ridge. Daylight is a fill. I have used both as a hope. Hope photographs. Hope does not survive 4 p.m.

Figured leaves chip at the edge when the plane is dull. I hone. I take a smaller bite. I tape the face near the edge if the figure is a suicide. I do not smash a crotch edge into a jointer and then say the mill sent a bad page.

## Which tape

Gummed veneer tape on the show face: traditional, a lick, a hold, a damp removal. It can stain a pale wood if I flood the damp. It can tear a fiber if I rip it. I damp, I wait, I pull low.

Filament or a modern veneer tape on the back: holds the drawing, stays out of the scraper. I like it on a bag job. I still joint the edge. The tape on the back will not hide a gap. It will hold a gap in place.

Blue painter’s tape is a temporary for a dry-fit. I do not glue with it as my only clamp on a seam I care about. It lets go in a bag sometimes. Sometimes is enough.

## The stitch

A few stitches of tape across a split in a crotch, like a doctor, then the long tape on the seam. I have watched a split run during a flatten and I have stitched it before it became a raccoon. The stitch stays until the glue says so.

## Glue in the seam

A little glue in a tight seam is a line. A lot of glue in a gap is a river that takes stain differently. I do not dye a river and call it oak. I remake the joint. If the glue is already a river, I scrape and I accept a scar or I patch a leaf from the folder.

Hide in a pale seam can stain. PVA in a pale seam can shine. I test. I size. I wipe.

## Removing tape

After the glue has a right to hold. Not after I have already started a wide-belt. I remove tape, then I scrape the field, then I decide if a sander is invited. The sander is often not invited.

Tape residue is a finish fisheye later. I clean. I do not invent a solvent recipe here. I use what the tape maker allows and I ventilate. I test on a cutoff.

## Barber pole is not a seam problem

If the seam is perfect and the faces blink, that is tight/loose, not a ridge. I do not scrape a blink into a valley. I change the layout or the finish or the promise. I have scraped a blink into a valley. The valley blinked and was a valley.

## The light box and the bored fingernail

I inherited a light box from a sign shop that closed in Warren — not a romance, a fluorescent tray with a sheet of plastic. I lay two leaves on it, edges together, and I look for daylight. Daylight is a fill I will refuse. I shoot until the light is bored. Then I shut the box and I run a fingernail. The fingernail is the room. The light box is the shop.

Without the box I use a raking lamp on the bench. Same test. I do not joint by the overhead fluorescent alone. Overhead lies about a hairline. Raking tells.

Figured leaves chip when the plane is dull. I hone more often than I want to. I tape the show face near the edge on a crotch so the plane does not pluck a flake of heart. I take a smaller bite. I do not smash the pair through a power jointer I set up for boards. A board jointer on a leaf is a chew I have paid for in a shortened page.

## Blue tape in a bag I trusted

I dry-fitted a bookmatch with blue painter’s tape because the gummed roll was empty. I left the blue on for the bag. The tape let go on one end. The seam crawled. I opened a panel with a step I could feel in the dark. I scraped toward it. I remade the field.

Blue tape is a dry-fit guest. Gummed veneer tape on the show face is the clamp I remove with damp. Filament on the back is the clamp that can stay out of the scraper. I keep the gummed roll in a can so it does not dry into a relic. I do not glue a seam I care about with a tape I know lets go. [VERIFY] a house tape brand before a caption names one.

The crawl taught me to look at the seam in the first minute of the bag, not at the pump gauge. The gauge is even pressure. The seam is the drawing.

## A river I dyed once

A gap I should have recut took a river of PVA. I dyed the river to match oak. In a dim photograph it disappeared. In the dining room it was a darker vein that moved when you moved. I patched from the folder later, for free, because the dye was a hope.

A little glue in a tight seam is a line I can live with if I tested it on a cutoff. A lot of glue in a gap is a different species. Hide in pale maple is a stain. PVA in pale maple is a shine. I test. I wipe. I remake the joint if the light box still shows weather.

## Shooting a crotch pair as one thought

Pages 8 and 9 from a walnut book, a butterfly I wanted. I shot each edge separately and they looked fine. Together they made a ridge because I had shot them to two different straightedges on two different days. I shot them again as a pair, one pass philosophy, same plane, same hour. The fingernail went bored.

Bookmatched pairs are a climate and a joint. Flatten them together. Shoot them together. Tape them as a drawing. If I treat 8 as Tuesday and 9 as Thursday I have two shops. The room will see one river.

When I fill, I write filled. A future stain will find it. The band essay is the hip. This essay is the eye. I owe the eye a bored fingernail before I owe the bag a sigh.

## What I write

Seam: book 17/18, shot, tape type, glue in the line or not. If I filled, I write filled, because a future stain will find it.

The next essay is the band: the seam at the hip, the one a body finds. The bookmatch seam is for the eye. The band is for the hip. Both are joints. Only one of them gets a fingernail from a stranger who is not looking for a butterfly.
