I lay a straightedge on a walnut leaf and I pull a knife, several light passes, a break that is a click. The edge is clean enough to shoot. A circular saw on the same leaf is a chew and a fuzz and a page I have to shorten. I have used a saw on a stack of leaves with a sacrificial board and a sharp blade and a score. It can work. It is not my first hand.

A leaf wants a knife. A saw wants a board. The mill already used a knife the size of a door. I can use a small one.

## The knife

A veneer knife, a marking knife, a blade I replace before it tears. Light passes. The straightedge must not walk. I tape the show face along the cut if the figure will chip. I cut from the show face so the break-out, if any, is on the back.

I do not snap a leaf like a cracker. I have snapped a $200 crotch like a cracker. The cracker did not follow the straightedge.

## The veneer saw

A small saw with no set, or almost none, a fine tooth, used against a straightedge. It is a knife with teeth. I like it on a stack of two, a book I am cutting as a pair. I do not like it dull. Dull is a chew.

I have a cheap veneer saw I should have replaced. I keep honing it because I am stubborn. The leaf does not care about stubborn. It cares about the tooth.

## Shears and the shop hack

Some shops use shears. I have used kitchen shears on a paper-backed birch in a closet job. I will not use kitchen shears on a sequenced oak. The hack is a closet hack. The book is a book.

## Saws, when I mean it

A stack of leaves, a sacrificial fence, a sharp fine blade, a score pass, a climb that is controlled if I know the saw: I will do this for a production of backs, a rotary backer, a paint-grade. I will not do this as my only method on a diamond I have dry-fitted for a day.

A bandsaw with a fence and a zero-clearance and a helper is how some people cut a loaf. I am not cutting a loaf every week. When I do, I treat it like yosegi: the loaf is the work, the slice is the leaf, the numbers go on immediately.

## Safety, short

A knife pulled toward a straightedge can leave the edge and find a palm. I keep the off hand past the end of the cut or behind a fence. I do not cut in the air. I cut on a bench I can sacrifice.

A saw on a leaf wants to throw a chip and a finger. I use a board on top. I unplug when I change a blade. I do not write a clever jig that becomes a story in an ER.

## Chips and the recut

A chipped edge is a shorter leaf. I recut. I do not fill a chip on a seam edge with dust and call it jointed. I can fill a chip in a field if the folder has a sibling and the chip is not at a butterfly spine.

Dull knives make chips. Dull saws make fuzz. I hone before I blame the mill.

## The cracker I paid for

A walnut crotch, a price I still remember without printing it as a catalog, a day I was late. I scored once and I snapped. The break ran with the figure, not with the straightedge. I had a shorter leaf and a story. I recut. I lost the butterfly I had dry-fitted. The door got a quieter page. The crotch became a box lid with a river I did not want.

I do not snap a leaf like a cracker. Several light passes. A click that is a cut, not a hope. Tape on the show face if the figure is a suicide. Cut from the show face so break-out, if any, is on the back.

## Scoring a stack of backs

Rotary birch backs, a kitchen run, a stack I needed to size. Sacrificial board on top, a sharp fine blade, a score pass, then through. The backs were not a diamond. They were a climate on the other face. I still numbered the stack as a bundle. I still did not use kitchen shears.

A bandsaw loaf is rare in this shop. When I cut one I treat it like the yosegi essay said: the loaf is the work, the slice is the leaf, the numbers go on before I stack the slices in a crate that can wander. I have cut a loaf and then shuffled the slices to find a better face. That is a flitch I unpublished. I do not unpublished a book.

## Tape, hone, and the suicide figure

Crotch, burl, a tight flake: I tape the show face along the cut line. I hone. I take light passes. I do not blame the mill for a chew I applied with a dull veneer saw I should have replaced. Stubborn is not a grit. [VERIFY] a house knife or veneer-saw preference before a caption names a brand.

I keep the off hand past the end of the cut or behind the straightedge. I cut on a bench I can sacrifice. A knife that leaves the edge finds a palm. I have a scar that is not a figure. I do not write a clever jig that becomes an ER story.

A chipped seam edge is a shorter leaf. I recut. Dust in a chip is a fill that will take stain as a river. I can patch a chip in a field from the folder. I cannot patch a seam edge and call it shot. The fingernail will find it. The room will see a river.

## When a saw is the honest tool

Paint-grade backs, a long rip, a production I would not finish with a knife before the glue gelled on a different job: I use the saw. I write *saw on backs* so I remember the show face still wants a knife. The exception is a stack I can defend. The diamond I dry-fitted for a day is not that stack.

A circular saw on a single show leaf is a chew I have already named. I do not do it because the knife is in the other room. I walk to the other room.

## What I write

How I cut the field, whether I stacked the book, whether a chip cost me a page. If I used a saw on a show face, I write it so I remember not to on the next diamond.

The crate is next: air, storage, the climate that turns a book into a wave before the knife is invited. A perfect knife on a chip that should have been flattened is still a chip. The tools in this stage only work on a leaf that arrived as a page. The crate decides a lot of that. I keep the crate off the slab. I keep the numbers up. I keep the knife sharp. I keep the saw for boards and for the exceptions I can defend.
