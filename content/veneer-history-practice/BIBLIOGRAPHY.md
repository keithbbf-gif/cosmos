# Bibliography — Veneer History & Shop Practice

Working list for editors. A caption that names an object should be checked against the current collection page, not only against this file. Dates and accessions in the drafts that are remembered rather than re-read stay `[VERIFY]`.

## Shop and method

- Fine Woodworking and similar shop literature on hammer veneering, vacuum bags, and matching — use as practice notes, not as history citations.
- HPVA / ANSI architectural veneer grade language — [VERIFY] current edition before a thickness or grade name goes in a caption.
- Hide-glue practice: traditional cabinetmaking manuals (Roubo as a distant ancestor; later English and American shop books). Do not invent a pot temperature from memory.

## Ancient and early

- George A. Reisner and the MFA Boston documentation of Hetepheres I furniture (reconstruction vs original members).
- Howard Carter object cards / Grand Egyptian Museum labels for Tutankhamun furniture woods — [VERIFY] a named box.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* — citrus tables and luxury wood; [VERIFY] book and chapter against Loeb or Oxford before quoting a price.
- Herculaneum carbonized furniture: site reports and museum labels, not a stand-in for Pliny’s citrus.
- Victor Chinnery, *Oak Furniture: The British Tradition*.
- Penelope Eames, *Furniture in England, France and the Netherlands from the Twelfth to the Fifteenth Century*.

## Renaissance to cabinet centuries

- Metropolitan Museum of Art, Gubbio studiolo (accession 39.153) — current collection page and conservation notes.
- Palazzo Ducale, Urbino, studiolo monographs.
- Rijksmuseum collection entries for Dutch floral marquetry (van Mekeren and circle) — prefer Open Access pages when they exist.
- Wallace Collection, cabinet F16 and related Boulle entries — current materials line and attribution sentence.
- Jean-Pierre Samoyault, *André-Charles Boulle et sa famille* (1979).
- William Rieder, entry on Met 1982.60.82 in *The Jack and Belle Linsky Collection* (1984); current Met label.
- Pierre Verlet; Alexandre Pradère — French ébénistes, stamps, and the menuisier / ébéniste split.
- Adam Bowett, English walnut- and mahogany-period furniture.
- Thomas Chippendale, *The Gentleman and Cabinet-Maker’s Director* (1754; later editions 1755, 1762) — cite the edition of any plate.
- Peter M. Kenny, Michael K. Brown, et al., Duncan Phyfe literature around the 2011–12 exhibitions (Met / MFA Houston).
- Winterthur and Met collection pages for Federal / Phyfe-shop card tables.

## Machines, factories, modern

- Mill monographs on rotary peeling (Immanuel Nobel and earlier lathe claims) — [VERIFY] before a caption date.
- U.S. plywood industry histories (Portland Manufacturing Co. / 1905 fair demonstration as commonly cited) — [VERIFY].
- Grand Rapids furniture histories and period catalogs — PD plates where the scan is PD; factory postcards `[VERIFY rights]`.
- Charles and Ray Eames molded-plywood chairs — manufacturer and date on the specific object photographed.
- CITES appendices and U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service guidance — check on the day of edit. Listings move.
- Ruhlmann and Deco catalogs — museum pages, not shopping lists.

## What this list is not

It is not a complete scholarly apparatus. It is the shelf the history drafts leaned on. An editor who adds a firm date or a materials line should add the page they read.
