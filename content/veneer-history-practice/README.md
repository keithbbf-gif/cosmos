# Veneer history and shop practice

These are **drafts**. They are staged on purpose: you can read them in order and
build a mental model before you unroll a flitch, or you can jump a stage when
you already know why the last door cupped.

This is shop writing with a memory. History is here because a leaf is not a
trick invented by a particleboard catalog. Practice is here because a Wallace
cabinet will not tell you how to tape a seam on a Tuesday in Bradley County.

## What this series is

Seven stages, one folder:

1. **Foundation** — what a leaf is, how it was cut, what it sits on, why both
   faces matter.
2. **Ancient** — Egypt, Rome, the medieval board, intarsia, Antwerp, and a
   Japanese cousin that is not a footnote.
3. **Cabinet** — Dutch flowers, Boulle, the ébéniste, English walnut,
   mahogany, Biedermeier, Federal America, and the glue that held them.
4. **Machines** — slicer and lathe, plywood, Grand Rapids, Deco, Danish teak,
   the ruined word, and the woods that left under CITES.
5. **Matching** — the flitch as a book, bookmatch and slip, sequence numbers,
   buckled figure, crossbanding, rift stiles around a flake panel.
6. **Shop** — hide and hammer, bag and caul, tape, solid bands, sand-through,
   knives, storage air.
7. **Repair** — flattening, blisters, the job-folder offcut, finish on a thin
   face, and the sentence you owe a client who hates the word.

## Shop-safe fence

This series stays inside **legal furniture-shop practice**.

It will talk about:

- hide glue, PVA, urea, contact, and vacuum bags as they are sold
- knives, tape, moisture, and ordinary shop air
- museum objects and published catalogs
- CITES as a stop, not a puzzle

It will **not** teach:

- how to cook, distill, or synthesize adhesives
- how to dodge CITES, customs, or species rules
- how to pass veneer as solid in a sales tag
- how to strip lead or dump solvent

If a draft mentions a dangerous commercial class (formaldehyde-heavy
hot-press resins, solvent contact in a closed room), it names the class and
the reason a small shop should leave it or take it to someone with the booth.
It does not become a formulation notebook.

## Voice

No "comprehensive guide." No "whether you're a beginner or a pro." If a
sentence could sit on the back of a big-box panel, it does not belong here.
A claim without a shop or object consequence is cut.
