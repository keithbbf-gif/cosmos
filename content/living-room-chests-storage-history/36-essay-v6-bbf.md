# Draft 36 — BBF mouth

The living room is younger than the box.

Chest: a lid on a well. Six boards when the shop is ordinary. Iron at the corners because the nails will fail if you rack it. Seat, table, cupboard if the room has nothing else. Europe ran this without a special name for the room.

Money walked other chests through streets. Tuscan marriage pairs, 1400s: a month of paint each, *forzieri* in the books, *cassoni* in ours, garbage-statute in Florence 1415. Then the bedchamber. No living room exists yet.

Drawer, England, around 1600. Till, drawing box. Stop unstacking the well. Hybrid lid-plus-drawers is seventeenth-century. “Mule chest” is c. 1911. Restoration: a case that is all drawers. You look at storage instead of sitting on it.

Commode = convenient. Cressent for Louis XV, La Muette bedroom, August 1738. Not a salon story. Tallboy / highboy: chamber height. A bonnet top next to a television has lost.

Connecticut Valley oak, late 1600s–early 1700s. Hadley group: tulip-and-stem, 100+ pieces, several shops. “Sunflower” group: Wethersfield-area rosettes, Blin 1681 credit, not a monogram on the field. Initials ≠ maker. No living room in those houses.

Living room: American winning name. In decorating copy in the 1890s. Bok does not coin it. He sells plans from 1895 with the parlor deleted. Bric-a-brac as a nervous condition. Drawing room: “too much money and no taste.” Magazine preference by ~1910. Permission, not invention. Tuesday needs a box. The parlor had been a face. Faces do not store blankets.

No period type “living-room chest.” A young room inherits old boxes. Catalogs sand the jobs off. Accent is the leftover word.

Lane, Altavista, 1912. Cedar / moths. WWI ammo boxes; speed stays. “The gift that starts the home.” Girl Graduate Plan official 1930. Miniatures as the hook. Company counts later: tens of millions; half to two-thirds of girl graduates at the height. Treat as company counts. Funnel is real. WWII uniform ads. Gateway to the rest of the line. Plant closed 2001. Lids remain. Script thinner than cedar.

Radio wears a cabinet because the room still has parlor manners. Ozarka “Credenza,” 1928. TV keeps the long face. ESU, Herman Miller, 1950–55, copy names the living room. Five-year product. Lid survives as trunk-table and storage ottoman.

Tansu: Genroku record; wheeled chests in 1657 fire; storehouse manners; jobs not moods. Bandaji: half-front, Korean, say the name. Not “Asian chest.”

Contents now: leftover narrative. Lid postpones. Drawer files.

Do: right word, clearance for handles, top you can clear with one hand, Tuesday. Do not: wet glass on carved oak, a parlor staged inside the box.

Room’s winning name: ~130 years. Box: does not care.
