# Opening lines — a shop file

The first sentence has to carry the gap. If it sells, it is wrong. If it explains itself, it is weak. These were tried.

## Keep

1. The living room is younger than the box it keeps trying to hide.
2. The living room is younger than the box.
3. A chest is a lid on a well.
4. The parlor was a face. Faces do not have lids if they can help it.
5. There is no period type called living-room chest.

1 is the candidate. 2 is 1 after BBF got to it. 3 is the object start if the room start gets tired. 4 is the parlor essay’s spine. 5 is a door you can only use if the next sentence is the relocation.

## Almost

6. Tuesday needs a box.  
   — True, but it needs Bok first or it sounds like a cross-stitch.

7. You sit on the lid to pull your shoes on.  
   — Good once you are inside the six-board. Not a lead.

8. Edward Bok did not invent the living room.  
   — Correct and sour. A correction is not an opening. Use it in the middle, where corrections live.

9. Lane understood the inheritance as a product.  
   — Good paragraph start. Not the piece.

10. The box does not care.  
    — An ending. It already is one.

## Dead

11. Throughout history, humans have needed a place to store their belongings.  
    — Kill on sight.

12. From ancient cassones to today’s accent chests, the story of storage is a story of us.  
    — Kill twice.

13. Whether you’re a collector or just looking to warm up your seating group—  
    — The sentence is already a cart.

14. In an age of open-plan living, storage solutions have never been more vital.  
    — Vital is a hospital word. Open-plan is a brochure.

15. Let’s take a journey through the rich history of living room furniture.  
    — There is no journey. There is a lid.

## Order of battle

If the final opens with 1, the second sentence must not repeat younger / older. Go to the six-board or to the well. If it opens with 2, the second sentence has to add the hide, or the well, or the parlor — one image. Do not stack slogans.
