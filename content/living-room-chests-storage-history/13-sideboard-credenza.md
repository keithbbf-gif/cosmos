# Sideboard, credenza, and the word that sounded richer

A sideboard is dining furniture. Late eighteenth-century England gave it a classic form — a serving height, drawers for the silver and the cloths, sometimes a cellaret, sometimes a rail to keep the plates from committing suicide. Shearer, Hepplewhite, Sheraton: the names on the plates. American Federal copies and improves. You stand at it with a platter. You do not watch a serial there.

A credenza is, in Italian, belief. The medieval and Renaissance habit of tasting food against poison — *credenza* as an act — sits on a table, then names the table, then names the cabinet. English *credence* once held the same cluster of meanings and then dropped them. About 1883 English starts borrowing *credenza* again for “an Italian sideboard.” About 1920 it is in domestic interiors as a smarter word than sideboard. Dining room first. Office later — the low cabinet behind the desk. Living room by drift, because the living room is where extra words go to get a lamp.

Why the living room wanted this object: height. Thirty-two to thirty-six inches is lamp height, drink height, photograph height. A chest of drawers can be that if you stop stacking. A sideboard already is. The wall opposite the sofa is a dining-room wall that lost its table.

The mid-century American “credenza” is often sliding doors, a long box, tapered legs or a plinth, maybe a drop-front for a record player. Danish teak sideboards did the same job with a better oil and a more honest name. They are not cassoni. They are not commodes. They are sideboards that moved rooms when the dining room shrank and the living room took the records.

I use credenza in these drafts the way a person in 1962 used it: the long low cabinet in the room where you sit. I do not use it for a Renaissance tasting table unless I am in that century. I do not use it for a file cabinet unless I am tired.

If your living-room chest has a marble top and cabriole legs, you are in commode country. If it has a serving height and a drawer that wants silver, you are in sideboard country. If the listing says credenza either way, the listing is thirsty.
