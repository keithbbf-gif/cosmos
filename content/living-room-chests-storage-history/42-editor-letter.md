# Letter to the editor who gets this folder

You asked for a history of living-room chests and storage furniture. You asked for a human voice, BBF, staged, quality over speed, forty drafts or more.

The piece is `41-final-candidate.md`. The hed is the first sentence. Everything else in the folder is how that sentence got earned.

What I will not do for you:

I will not add a subhed about “timeless storage solutions.” I will not caption the cassone as a weekend project. I will not make Bok a lovable uncle. I will not give Peter Blin a monogram he did not own. I will not turn Lane into a folk song. I will not write “Asian-inspired.” I will not end on a shopping list.

What you can cut without killing it:

The highboy/television joke, if your art is solemn. The router in the mouth, if you think it is cute — I do not, but I can hear the complaint. The company-numbers caution on Lane, if you replace it with a footnote, not with a fake census.

What you cannot cut:

The 1911 mule-chest name. The La Muette bedroom (not salon). Bok not coining the term. The relocation thesis — young room, old box. Tuesday.

Length: `41` is a long magazine feature if you run it straight. `36` is the brief if you need a box in a review section. Do not split `41` into a slideshow of types. The types are already in `05`–`29` as stand-alones.

Art: a six-board with real straps; a cassone in a gallery, not a reproduction in a loft; a Lane ad, which is a primary source; an ESU in a room that is not a museum; a bandaji with the metal readable. No bowl of lemons on a carved lid.

BBF: if a sentence could sell a sofa, it is already out. If you put one back, you are not editing this piece. You are replacing it.

The folder stays with the piece. A later writer who only gets `41` will flatten the contested bits. `40` and `01` are the floor.
