# Read-aloud — draft 35 against the ear

Read in a room, not on a stage. If you need to act, the sentence is dressed.

**The living room is younger than the box it keeps trying to hide.**  
Clean. Stress on younger, box, hide. Do not smile at hide.

**A chest is a lid on a well.**  
Also clean. Stop after well. Do not rush the six.

**If the shop is ordinary the count is six: ends that run down and become feet, sides nailed on, a bottom, a cover.**  
The colon works if you let each board land. Cover is a quieter word than lid; that is on purpose — we already spent lid.

**The corners want to tear when you rack the box on a cart.**  
Want is the human verb. Keep it.

**Iron straps are not a look.**  
People will want to say “just a look.” Do not.

**Florence, in 1415, forbade throwing stones or garbage at the couple’s house, which is a statute you write after the garbage.**  
The last clause is the point. If you rush it, you get a factoid.

**We call it a mule chest. The joiners did not. The name is about 1911.**  
Three hits. Do not merge them.

**You stop sitting on your storage. You start looking at it.**  
This is the essay’s hinge. Pause.

**Put a bonnet-top highboy in a room with a television and the bonnet will win.**  
Comic without a wink. If you wink, it becomes draft 31.

**Those houses had no living rooms.**  
Short enough to fail if you embroider.

**He did something more bossy.**  
Bossy will bother a certain editor. Keep it. Promoted is the museum word.

**Those are company numbers. The shape of the funnel is not.**  
Do not soften.

**The cabinet stays, confused, with a router in its mouth.**  
If it feels cute, cut confused. The final keeps it; the BBF pass would not.

**“Asian chest” is how a menu avoids a language.**  
Let it be rude.

**A lid postpones a decision. A drawer files.**  
Second hinge.

**The box does not care.**  
End. Do not add a moral after. Draft 32 tried. It got weaker.
