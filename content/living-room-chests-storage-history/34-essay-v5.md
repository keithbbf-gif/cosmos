# Draft 34 — still finding the sentence

I keep coming back to the same uneven pair.

The box is old. A lid, a well, six boards if no one is showing off, iron where the nails will fail. People sat on it. People put the household’s movable wealth in it because the house might not last. In Florence the expensive ones walked down the street. In the Connecticut Valley a joiner carved a tulip and a set of initials that were not his. In England someone got tired of digging for a stocking and invented, or rather installed, a drawer. None of that needed a living room.

The room is young. Not sitting, not talking — those are ancient. The *name*, the American winning name for a front room you use on a weekday. Magazines in the 1890s. Bok’s plans in 1895 with the parlor missing. By about 1910 the word is the preferred one if you learn your house from paper. The parlor had been a face. Faces do not have lids if they can help it. When the face has to live, it needs a box, and the box comes in looking a little ashamed — runner on the lid, lamp on the runner, as if storage were still a guest.

That shame is the interesting part. We did not invent a new furniture type for the living room. We inherited chests, commodes, sideboards, presses, ship cabinets, factory cedar, radio furniture, steel units that lasted five years, and we sanded the jobs off until a catalog could say accent chest. The accent is our embarrassment.

Lane did not pretend. Altavista, rails, moths, a graduation list. The miniature first, the full lid after, the rest of the house if the funnel worked. A hope chest is an ad you can sit on. I would rather live with that honesty than with a “Tuscan” box made last year in a place that is not Tuscany.

The machine was honest too, in its way. It wore wood because the room still had parlor manners. Then the screen went flat and left us the empty wooden mouth.

I want a living-room chest that knows which of these it is. Lid or drawer. Travel or wall. Factory or shop. Tuesday or shrine. If it knows, the room gets quieter. If it does not, you get a bowl and a stack of books that have never been read in that county.
