# Commode — convenient, and not a living room yet

The French called it *commode* because it was convenient. That is the whole manifesto. The coffer, the big lidded chest, had become a nuisance in rooms that were learning to be small and curved. Louis XIV’s cabinetmakers already owed the king a case with drawers. Under the Régence and Louis XV the case grew a belly, long S-legs, bronze that behaved like vine. Charles Cressent, sculptor first, gave the type its early nerve.

The dated example I will stand on is not a salon piece. On 4 August 1738 a commode by Cressent was delivered — through the *ébéniste du roi* Gaudreaus — for Louis XV’s new bedroom at La Muette. Marble top matched the chimney. Inventory number on the back. Bedroom. The later royal-bedroom commodes with two drawers and side cupboards take this as a pattern, or so Daniel Alcouffe argued from the Garde-Meuble. I am not going to pretend I have the journal in my lap. I am going to keep the moral: the famous convenient chest stood where the king slept, then the type walked into the smaller salons because the furniture of that reign was built to be moved.

English took the word and split it. A commode is a French case in a dealer’s mouth. A commode is also the bedroom cupboard that hid the pot. If you write “commode in the living room” without a country, you will get a laugh you did not budget for.

What the living room actually inherited is the *low, finished-all-around case with a hard top*. Marble, or wood pretending to be stone, or now a sealed walnut you can set a wet glass on. The commode was often meant to be seen in the round, even to stand off the wall. That is a salon habit. American living rooms shove the piece to the wall and put a lamp on it, which is closer to a credenza’s life. The bronze mounts become “hardware.” The bombé front becomes a shipping problem.

I have a softness for a plain provincial commode — cherry, three drawers, short feet, no ormolu. It does the convenient thing without the court. It will live under an American window and not apologize for having been French adjacent. The court commode wants a different room and a different insurance rider.

Convenient is still the right test. If you have to empty the sofa to open it, it is not a commode. It is a monument.
