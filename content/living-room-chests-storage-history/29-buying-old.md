I am not an appraiser. I am a person who has watched listings lie.

**Name.** If they say mule chest, they are speaking after 1911. Fine, if the object is a lidded chest with drawers. If they say Hadley and the carving is a router mandala, walk. If they say tansu and the case is a Korean bandaji, they cannot read their own photograph. If they say lowboy and there is no dressing history in the form, they mean “short.”

**Feet.** Assume they are later until the underside argues. Especially walnut buns. Especially campaign chests that have “traveled.”

**Back and bottom.** A finished back on a piece that will live on a wall is a nice extra. An unfinished back is normal. A new plywood back on a “1670” chest is a sentence. Look at oxidation. Look at saw marks. Circular saw on a claimed seventeenth-century boarded chest is a joke at your expense.

**Drawers.** Dovetails can be faked and can be machine. The story is in the pins: regularity, tool marks, wear at the runners, secondary wood. A chest of drawers with perfect maple plywood sides and “antique” brasses is a new box.

**Smell.** Cedar should be wood, not a spray that fades in a week. Camphor too. If the listing mentions the smell three times and the photos show a painted interior, believe the paint.

**Hardware holes.** Extra holes mean a life or a refresh. One set of fresh bright screws in old brass means a morning in a garage.

**Cassone fronts.** If the “chest” is a panel glued to a new box, you bought a picture. Sometimes that is what you want. Pay picture money.

**Lane.** Serial numbers and paper labels exist; collectors track them. The living-room value is usually use, not a number. The miniature graduate chests are their own market. Do not let anyone tell you a jewelry-box Lane is a rare court object.

**Price vs. carry.** If you cannot get it through the door in the parts it was built to become (campaign, tansu stacks, chest-on-chest), you are not buying furniture. You are buying a problem.

Pay for joinery you can see. Pay for a lid that still fits. Do not pay for the word *authentic* without a date range and a place. A place is a town or a valley or a factory. “Europe” is not a place.
