# Voice brief — BBF, human, no furniture-ad throat

Keith BBF asked for a human voice. That is not “add contractions and a joke.” That is: write as a person who will still be in the room if the sentence is wrong.

## Mouth

Short when the fact is short. Long only when the object is complicated. One picture per paragraph if you can steal it. Dates that can be checked. Names of towns. Woods. The sound of a lid.

I, sometimes. Not a brand *we*. Not a seminar *one*.

If I did not open the chest, I do not describe the smell as if I did. Cedar is easy to fake on the page. “I have opened Lane chests that still smell like a closet in August” is allowed only if it is true. For this set it is not claimed as a personal inventory. The factory history is. The smell is general knowledge about red cedar and can be said that way.

## Refuse

- Throughout history / since time immemorial / from ancient times
- Tapestry, delve, landscape (unless there is dirt)
- It’s important to note / it’s worth mentioning
- Whether you’re a collector or a first-time buyer
- Statement piece, conversation starter, elevate, nest, curated, vibe
- Timeless elegance, rich heritage, stunning example
- The perfect blend of form and function
- In today’s fast-paced world
- Unlock / unleash storage potential
- Any sentence that could be swapped onto a grill or a mattress

If a paragraph is selling, it has failed even if the facts are right.

## BBF constraints that apply to furniture

Same as the rest of the house:

- A claim is not evidence. Museum accession numbers and dated catalogs are evidence. “Everyone knows” is not.
- Contested attributions stay contested. Peter Blin does not get a clean maker’s label.
- Later names stay later. Mule chest, accent chest, hope chest as a national product.
- Do not flatten another culture’s furniture into a mood board.
- Do not write marriage as destiny when the factory wrote it as a funnel.
- Improvement is not bloat. A later draft that is longer and vaguer has gone backwards.

## The human test

Read the paragraph out loud. If you hear a docent who is afraid of the donor, cut. If you hear a listing, cut. If you hear a person who has carried a chest through a doorway and hated the person who built it without handles, keep.

`38-read-aloud.md` is that test applied to the final candidate.

## What the piece is allowed to want

It is allowed to want the reader to look at the box in their own room and know whether they are looking at a lid, a drawer, a cupboard, or a machine in wooden clothes. It is not allowed to want a click. It is not allowed to want a sale. It is not allowed to want Keith soothed.
