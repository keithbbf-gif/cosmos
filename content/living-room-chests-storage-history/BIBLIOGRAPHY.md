# Bibliography — living-room chests and storage

Working shelf for the staged drafts. Prefer the monograph or the catalog page over a blog that ate the monograph. Popular surveys may orient; they may not carry a date alone.

## The room

- Edward W. Bok, *The Americanization of Edward Bok* (New York: Charles Scribner’s Sons, 1920) — parlor vs living-room advocacy; do not credit him with inventing the term.
- *Ladies’ Home Journal* house-plan years under Bok (1895–) — living-room labeled on plans; parlor avoided. Cite the issue when a draft leans on a plan.
- Katherine C. Grier, *Culture and Comfort: Parlor Making and Middle-Class Identity, 1850–1930* (Washington: Smithsonian Institution Press, 1988).
- Elizabeth Donaghy Garrett, *At Home: The American Family 1750–1870* (New York: Abrams, 1990).
- Jessica H. Foy and Thomas J. Schlereth, eds., *American Home Life, 1880–1930* (Knoxville: University of Tennessee Press, 1992).
- Gail Caskey Winkler and Roger W. Moss, *Victorian Interior Decoration* (New York: Henry Holt, 1986) — parlor suites, etageres, the formal front room.
- Anna Tobin D’Ambrosio, work on American parlor suites; University of Delaware M.A. thesis tradition on factory parlor suites, 1871–1901 (UDspace).

## Medieval and early chests

- Penelope Eames, *Furniture in England, France and the Netherlands from the Twelfth to the Fifteenth Century* (London: Furniture History Society, 1977).
- Chris Pickvance, papers on English medieval chest construction, the Canterbury arcaded group, and Westminster Abbey dendrochronology (*Regional Furniture* and related).
- Westminster Abbey, Coronation Chair and fabric-roll notes; Long Chest / Hutch-type Chest tree-ring ranges as cited in Pickvance.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, Chest with Iron Banding, 13.205.2 (Westphalia, 15th c.); Cloisters Chest 16.32.20.
- Charles Tracy, *English Gothic Choir-Stalls* (Woodbridge: Boydell, 1987) — church furniture adjacent to parish chests.

## Cassone, cupboards, credenza

- Metropolitan Museum / National Gallery catalogs for cassone panels (Apollonio di Giovanni and workshop; the *spalliera* problem).
- Graham Hughes and later Italian Renaissance furniture surveys; prefer museum object pages for a named cassone.
- Met, Credenza, The Cloisters 53.95 (walnut and intarsia, 1440–50), attrib. Lorenzo and Cristoforo Canozi da Lendinara.
- *Encyclopædia Britannica* 1911, “Credence”; John Gloag, *A Short Dictionary of Furniture* (London, 1977) — credence / credenza.
- OED, *credenza* (2014 online update) and *credence*.

## English and American joined work

- Benno M. Forman, *American Seating Furniture, 1630–1730* (New York: Winterthur / Norton, 1988) — for joined thinking; chests by analogy.
- Dean A. Fales, Jr., *American Painted Furniture, 1660–1880* (New York: Dutton, 1972).
- Patricia E. Kane, *Furniture of the New Haven Colony* and related Yale / New Haven Colony catalogs.
- Frances Gruber Safford, *American Furniture in The Metropolitan Museum of Art, I: Early Colonial Period* (New York: Met / Yale, 2007) — Hadley 10.125.682; related boxes.
- Philip Zea and others on Hadley chests (Historic Deerfield; the Erving 1883 purchase story).
- Robert F. Trent and Peter Blin / Wethersfield sunflower-chest literature; Connecticut Historical Society.
- John T. Kirk, *American Furniture and the British Tradition to 1830* (New York: Knopf, 1982).
- Morrison H. Heckscher, *American Furniture in The Metropolitan Museum of Art, II: Late Colonial Period* (New York: Met / Random House, 1985) — high chests, chests-on-chests.
- Newport and Philadelphia high-chest literature: Michael Moses, *Master Craftsmen of Newport*; Heckscher on Goddard-Townsend; Philadelphia Chippendale catalogs (PMA, Met).

## French commode and English names

- Francis J. B. Watson, *Louis XVI Furniture* (London: Academy / St. Martin’s, 1973).
- Geoffrey de Bellaigue and Wallace Collection catalogs — Oeben / Riesener, *bureau du Roi*.
- Thomas Chippendale, *The Gentleman and Cabinet-Maker’s Director* (London, 1754; 3rd ed. 1762), “French Commode Tables.”
- Ince and Mayhew, *Universal System of Household Furniture* (London, 1759–62).
- OED / etymonline on *commode* (headdress; chest; close-stool). Do not collapse the three senses.

## Kas, press, court cupboard, sideboard

- Peter M. Kenny, Frances Gruber Safford, Gilbert T. Vincent, *American Kasten: The Dutch-Style Cupboards of New York and New Jersey, 1650–1800* (New York: Met, 1991).
- Victor Chinnery, *Oak Furniture: The British Tradition* (Woodbridge: Antique Collectors’ Club, 1979, rev.).
- Benno Forman and Winterthur on press cupboards and court cupboards.
- Christopher Gilbert, *Furniture at Temple Newsam House and Lotherton Hall*; English sideboard / shearer / Hepplewhite literature.
- Thomas Sheraton, *The Cabinet Dictionary* (London, 1803); Hepplewhite, *The Cabinet-Maker and Upholsterer’s Guide* (1788).

## Campaign, trunks, cedar

- Nicholas A. Brawer, *British Campaign Furniture: Elegance Under Canvas, 1740–1914* (New York: Abrams, 2001).
- Christopher Claxton Stevens and others on Ross & Co. of Dublin; Thomas Butler.
- Virginia Museum of History & Culture, “Lane Cedar Chest”; Lane Company history (Standard Red Cedar Chest Co., Altavista, 1912; Lane name 1922; Girl Graduate Plan).
- FundingUniverse / Interco acquisition notes for the late corporate chapter — use as orientation, mark `[VERIFY]` on employment and sales figures.

## Painted chests

- Monroe H. Fabian, *The Pennsylvania-German Decorated Chest* (New York: Universe / PMA, 1978; later reprints).
- Beatrice B. Garvan, Philadelphia Museum of Art Pennsylvania German catalogs.
- Norwegian rosemaling and immigrant-chest literature: Vesterheim; Norsk Folkemuseum. Mark tourist attributions `[VERIFY]`.

## Asia

- Ty and Kiyoko Heineken, *Tansu: Traditional Japanese Cabinetry* (New York: Weatherhill, 1981).
- Kazuko Koizumi, *Traditional Japanese Furniture* (Tokyo: Kodansha, 1986).
- *Musashi Abumi* (1661) and Genroku-era first uses of the word *tansu* — cite Heineken, not a dealer page.
- Edward S. Morse, *Japanese Homes and Their Surroundings* (Boston, 1886) — tatami, tansu in the house, with period eyes.
- Korean *bandaji* / *jang*: Ho-Am / National Museum of Korea catalogs; Met Korean furniture pages.
- Craig Clunas, *Chinese Furniture* (London: V&A, 1988); Wang Shixiang, *Connoisseurship of Chinese Furniture*.
- Camphor export chests: treaty-port and later commercial literature; do not romance “the Chinese chest” as one type.

## Shaker, Arts and Crafts, mid-century

- June Sprigg and David Larkin, *Shaker: Life, Work, and Art*; Hancock and Pleasant Hill site catalogs — built-in cupboards.
- Gustav Stickley, *Craftsman Homes* and *The Craftsman* magazine; David Cathers, *Furniture of the American Arts and Crafts Movement*.
- George Nelson, Comprehensive Storage System (Herman Miller, 1959) — Herman Miller archives.
- Charles and Ray Eames, ESU (Herman Miller, 1949–50) — Eames Office / Herman Miller.
- Nisse Strinning, String shelving (1949).
- USM Haller (1963) — company history; do not invent a living-room origin myth.
- Danish credenza makers: cite a named piece (Wegner, Vodder, Juhl) from a museum or maker catalog, not a “Danish modern” mood board.

## Television furniture and wall units

- Marilyn Hoffman, “Entertainment systems behind closed doors,” *Christian Science Monitor*, 24 December 1981 — Hokanson “entertainer,” Pulaski, Lane, Hammary, Stanley.
- Chicago Tribune, “Systematic,” 1 May 1988; “Media Room,” 25 December 1988 — wall systems, armoires as TV cabinets, case-goods growth.
- James Larson, Audioholics critique of entertainment centers (speaker and ventilation) — useful as a later dissenting voice, not as 1981 evidence.

## Shop and words

- John Gloag, *A Short Dictionary of Furniture*.
- Charles F. Montgomery, *American Furniture: The Federal Period* (Winterthur / Viking, 1966).
- Helen Comstock, *American Furniture* (New York: Viking, 1962).
- OED entries: chest, coffer, bureau, dresser, sideboard, ottoman, cellaret / cellarette.
