**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

August in this county will add a little across a top. January heat will take it back. A breadboard that was proud in the shop will look shy in a week and honest in a year. None of that is a cracked joint. A cracked joint is a cracked joint. This piece is how you tell a living board from a bad job before anyone says warranty (piece 38).

## Movement is the material

Solid wood changes width with moisture. Length, much less. That is why breadboards, framed panels, and slotted screws exist — the craft pack next door. If the RFQ demanded a solid top glued to a rigid apron with no room, the shop should have said no (piece 42) or designed the joint. If they built the trap, the trap is theirs.

Seasonal gaps at a breadboard, a panel that rattles faintly in January, a top that is flatter in June — those can be the piece working. A split through the glue-up, a joint that opened on day one, a door that never fit — those are not the seasons. Write the difference on the care card so the household does not call the first gap a failure.

## Lots and color

Two oaks are two trees. A leaf stored in a garage will not match a top that lived in the dining room (piece 11, piece 36). A new sideboard next to a ten-year table will be younger (piece 31). UV will close the gap some and open it in other ways. "It does not match the phone" is usually true and usually not a defect.

Plywood and veneer move less across the face and have their own tells. If the quote said solid and the edge says core, that is not movement. That is a spec fight (piece 04, piece 08).

## The phone

We already buried the phone as a color witness (piece 07, piece 31). It is also a flatness witness. Wide lenses and overhead shots make tops look longer and deader. A raking light in the real room will show every sanding skip. That raking light is the truth. The phone's beauty light is not.

## What to do on delivery

Walk the piece in the room light you approved (piece 37). Compare to the sample board, not to the inspiration JPEG. Look at joints, not at grain that is "not like the picture." Grain that is not like the picture is wood.

If something is wrong — a size, a finish miss vs the board, a broken joint — write it that day. Concealed damage vs obvious damage is a freight conversation (piece 34). Do not wait a month and then send a filtered photo.

## Warranty vs education

Magnuson-Moss cares about written warranties to consumers. If you give a card, the card should not call ordinary movement a covered failure, and it should not hide a real structural failure behind "all wood is different." Those are opposite sins. `[VERIFY]` house card language.

This essay is education. It is not a waiver. A shop that uses "wood moves" to explain a door that was never fitted is using a true sentence as a costume.

## A leaf that lived in the wrong room

Self-storing failed, so the leaf lived in a garage. In December the leaf was a different width and a different color than the top. That is climate and UV, not a mill that "switched oak." Store leaves in the house, flat, away from a stove (piece 18, piece 36). Write that on the care card so the call in December is shorter.

## Day-one gap vs January gap

A gap that was there when the crew was still in the driveway is a shop problem until proven otherwise. A breadboard that looks shy in January and proud in June is the design working, if the design allowed it. Write both sentences on the care card. The punch list catches day one (piece 37). The card catches January.

## The RFQ lines

Solid vs veneer. Movement-friendly joinery: yes/no. Sample board ID. Care card: will ship.

The table will keep living after the truck leaves. You wanted that when you did not want plastic. Let it live. Call when it breaks.
