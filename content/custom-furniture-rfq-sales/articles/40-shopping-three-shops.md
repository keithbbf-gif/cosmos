**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

Three PDFs, three integers, one room. The lowest had no leaf, no white glove, and a customer measure the household had not taken (piece 17). The highest had a site visit, a crate, and a finish sample in the room (piece 31). The middle had a date it could not defend (piece 25). Shopping is allowed. Comparing integers without scopes is how you buy a fight.

This is not a script for getting a shop to "beat" a number. It is how to run a fair RFQ so the number means something (piece 02, piece 41).

## Send the same packet

Same inches, same path, same finish rules, same service level (piece 34), same want-date labeled WANT (piece 24). If you learn something from shop A — a soffit, a scribe — send it to B and C. A secret extra given to one shop is not a comparison. It is a favorite.

Dealers: do not send three mills three different lengths because three salespeople guessed. Pick a length. Then shop.

## Compare the stack

Queue, lumber, finish, buyouts, freight (piece 19). A shop that is cheaper because the oak is in the rack is honestly cheaper (piece 20). A shop that is cheaper because they skipped the crate is not (piece 35). A shop that is cheaper because they will not visit the stair is a porch risk (piece 14).

Write a little grid: site measure, template, crate, service, sample, date kind, deposit steps. Then look at the integer.

## What you may share

You may share another shop's *scope* ("they included a split base and a room-of-choice delivery"). You may share a budget ceiling (piece 06). You do not owe anyone the other shop's unit price. Some people share it anyway. If you do, do not demand a beat-without-scope. That demand is how you get a different piece (piece 41).

## What you should not do

Use a ballpark as a bid (piece 03). Collect free design from A and hand the drawing to B as if B invented it — say where the drawing came from. Promise three shops they "have the job" to hurry a quote. Ghost two after you pick one without a thanks. Manners are not a close. They are how you can call next year.

## Time

Three real quotes take weeks, not a weekend. If the holiday is fixed (piece 24), start earlier or shop stock (piece 04). A rush bake-off is a lie bake-off (piece 23).

## After you pick

Send the others a short no. Accept the winner in writing. Get the ack. Deposit after the drawing (piece 26, piece 08). Do not keep shopping after the cut list and then cancel like a SKU (piece 29).

## A grid you can fill in an evening

Rows: site measure, path, crate, service, sample, date kind, deposit steps, object integer, freight integer, tax note. Columns: shop A B C. Fill from the PDFs, not from memory. The lowest object integer with three empty rows is a costume (piece 41). The middle integer with a full row is often the job.

## Close the loop

A short no to the shops you did not pick is manners and a clean whiteboard for them. Ghosting two shops after they priced a packet is how you get slower quotes next year. The packet was work for them too. Thank them. Then deposit with the winner after the drawing (piece 26), not while you keep shopping.

## The household sentence

"We are asking three shops the same job. We will pick on scope and date, then price." Shops can live with that. Shops cannot live with a moving job and a single integer contest.

Piece 41 is the cheap quote. Piece 43 is when you walk. This piece is only the grid. Use a grid. Then the integer can speak.
