Two tapes, two numbers, one wall. The nephew had 96. The shop, a week later, had 94½ and a baseboard the nephew had treated as air. The table was already 96 in the rack. That is not a mystery. That is ownership.

Someone has to own the inch. The RFQ and the ack should say who. "We are not responsible for customer measurements" is a sentence shops love. It is incomplete if the shop also refused to visit, did not explain the risk, and then built the wrong object with a smile.

## Shop measure

The shop comes, hooks the tape (piece 09), walks the path (piece 14), notes the 91-degree corner (piece 10), photographs the outlet (piece 18). The shop owns the number. If the piece does not fit because the number was wrong, the shop's problem — remake, recut, or a hard conversation under UCC ideas about tender and cure (UCC § 2-601, § 2-508). This pack is not your lawsuit. It is the reason shops take measuring seriously.

Shop measure costs time. Time is money. Put the fee or the inclusion on the quote. Do not hide a two-hour drive in "free."

## Customer measure

You hook the tape. You own the number. The shop will still ask questions. The shop should still refuse numbers that are obviously a listing (piece 09). The shop should not silently "fix" your number and then pretend you owned the fix.

Write "customer measure, [date], [name]" on the drawing (piece 08). If the piece fails the room, the first question is whether the inch was true. If it was true and the shop built wrong, that is the shop. If it was false, that is you.

Dealers: your salesperson's tape is a customer tape as far as the mill is concerned unless the mill sent the body.

## Shared and sloppy

"We both looked at it." Nobody owns it. Pick.

A designer measure the shop never saw is a customer measure with better shoes. Field-verify or write the owner.

## When the shop should refuse the job

If the path is ugly and you will not allow a visit (piece 14). If the wall is a belly and you will not template (piece 16). If two people in the house send two widths. If the only number is "about eight feet." Refusal is a kindness (piece 42).

## Warranty language that is a shrug

A footer that says the shop is never responsible for fit, even after a shop measure, is a sentence counsel should see. A footer that says customer-supplied inches are customer-owned, after a warning and a chance at a site visit, is ordinary. This pack will not draft your footer. It will say: the sentence and the conduct have to match.

## Cure, not pride

If the inch was wrong and the piece is close, a shop may recut, scribe, or split a top (piece 14). That is cheaper than a lawyer and cheaper than a one-star story. Say in the ack whether field adjustment is included. Surprise labor on install day is how deposits get ugly (piece 26, piece 37).

## What a site-measure visit should include

The room, the path, the live plates, the heights, a photo of the tape on the long wall, and a sentence on out-of-square. If the visit was "we stopped by and looked," it was not a measure. Looking is a ballpark (piece 03).

Fee or inclusion belongs on the quote. A shop that drives two hours and then eats the drive is a shop that will hurry the tape. Pay for the tape you want.

If the household declines the visit to "save money," write declined. Then the inch is theirs when the landing vetoes. That sentence feels cold in the office. It feels fair on the porch.

## Shared custody is nobody

"We measured together" without a name on the drawing is how both sides point. Pick a name. If the shop's apprentice held the tape and the household called numbers, the shop still owned the method if they ran the visit. If the household's nephew held the tape and the salesperson watched, the household owned it. Write the name. Watching is not measuring.

## The RFQ lines

Measured by. Date. Shop visit: scheduled / declined / not offered. Path included: yes/no. Template included: yes/no.

The tape is a witness. Put a name on the witness. Then the inch has a home.
