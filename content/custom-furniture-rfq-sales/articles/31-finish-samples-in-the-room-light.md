The board sat on the table overnight, north window in the morning, cheap overhead at dinner. In the morning it was brown. At dinner it was orange. Both were true. The phone photo of the same board was a third color that does not exist.

Finish approval is a light problem before it is a taste problem. An RFQ that says "like the photo" (piece 07) is asking the shop to match a lamp and a filter. The shop can match a board. Put the board in the room.

## Why the showroom lies

Showrooms are bright, even, and often cooler than a dining room at 6 p.m. A sample under that lamp will look calmer than it will under your warm bulbs. Dealers: do not let a household sign a finish in the store without sending a board home. If they refuse the board, write "approved under showroom light" on the ack (piece 08). That sentence will earn its keep.

## Why the phone lies

White balance, wide lens, a filter you forgot, a dirty screen. Process photos from the spray room are worse: dust, mixed bulbs, a wet film that will amber. Ask for a dry board. Ask what light. Better: take the board.

## Why the old table lies

A ten-year-old top has UV, oil from hands, and a different species lot (piece 20, piece 32). "Match the table" is a direction, not a promise. A good shop will get in the family. A shop that promises a twin is selling a fight.

Floors are worse. Floors are dirty on purpose. Do not use them as a color standard.

## How to run a sample

Same species, same sanding grit, same system as the job. A pine board is not an oak answer. Leave it 24 hours if you can. Look at breakfast and at night. Look with the pendant on (piece 15). Write: approved, too red, too dead, more grain. One sentence. Two people in the house, one sentence (piece 06).

If you need a second board, that is a day (piece 21). Budget it. It is cheaper than a strip.

## Sheen

Sheen changes color. A satin and a gloss of the same stain are not the same object. Approve sheen in the room too. A glossy top under a window is a mirror. A dead-flat top will look dusty in a showroom and honest in a farmhouse.

## Dealers

Tag the sample with the RFQ number, not "oak #3." Oak #3 will drift. The RFQ number will not. Keep a chip in the job jacket. When the household says the delivered top is "different," you will want that chip in the same room as the top.

## When to freeze color

After the board, before the mill if the color will not change the cut list. After the mill, before the first coat if you are still fighting. Do not freeze in a parking lot (piece 30).

## How to reject a board without a fight

"Too red at dinner under the pendant. Morning is fine. We want less orange, same grain." That is a finish room instruction. "We hate it" is not. "Make it richer" is not.

If two people disagree, do not average two boards in the shop. Average them in the room or pick a decider. A shop that stains a top to a compromise nobody signed will remake for both of them.

## Keep the chip in the jacket

When the truck arrives, the board should still exist. Compare that night (piece 37). A shop that throws the board away is a shop that wants a JPEG fight. A household that loses the board is a household that will use the phone. Label the chip with the RFQ number, not "oak #3."

## The RFQ lines

Sample in room: required / declined. Light notes. Match existing: family / twin / no. Sheen. Who signs.

The north window will not care about the Pinterest. The dinner bulbs will not care about the showroom. Approve the room you have.
