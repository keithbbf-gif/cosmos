The cut list was on the door, yellowed by glue-pot heat, her name in the corner. She called to cancel. The boards were already her length. That is not a restocking fee on a boxed chair. That is a pile of oak that wants a dining room that no longer wants it.

Cancellation is timed. Before a substantial beginning, a shop can often unwind: return a deposit minus a quoted design fee, or return all of it if the ack says so. After a substantial beginning — manufacture or procurement commitments, before notice of repudiation — UCC § 2-201(3)(a) is the neighborhood people mean when they say "custom is different." The goods may not be suitable for sale to others in the ordinary course. The shop may have a price action in ugly cases (UCC § 2-709). This page will not decide your case. It will say: do not cancel like you canceled a SKU.

## Before the saw

RFQ only: there is nothing to cancel. Quote only: let it expire (piece 03). Ack + deposit, lumber not pulled: the ack should say what comes back. Write it while you are still friends (piece 26).

A shop that refuses any refund the morning after a deposit, with no labor and no lumber, should have a reason on paper. "Because custom" is not a reason if nothing custom has happened.

## After procurement, before the saw

Hinges ordered, stone PO out, a slab trucked in (piece 22, piece 20). The shop has commitments. Unwinding may cost restocking at the *vendor*, not a vibe percent. Pass those costs through with copies. Do not invent a round number because it feels like justice.

## After the cut list

The object is becoming unsaleable as a general table. A 96-inch top in a rare green with a name in the stretcher is the textbook. A 41-inch height for a specific radiator is the same idea. A shop that can flip the piece to the floor as a sample may offer a smaller hit. A shop that cannot should say so now, not after a fight.

Payment already accepted can also matter under UCC § 2-201(3)(c) as part of a formation story. Again: counsel, not a blog.

## Remote cancel-and-refund

16 CFR Part 435 can require a cancel-and-refund option when a remote seller cannot ship on time (piece 25). That option and a specially manufactured goods story can tension. Do not pick a winner in a caption. Call counsel. Do not ghost the customer while you do.

## Dealers

Your household may cancel you. You may still owe the mill. Align those two contracts in week one. A store that refunds a household and leaves the mill holding the oak is why mills want deposits from stores too.

Do not write "100% refundable until delivery" on a custom special unless the mill wrote it first. You will eat a table.

## Homeowners

Life happens: moves, deaths, a job loss. Say the truth early. A shop can sometimes stop before the next coat (piece 21). Early is money. Late is oak.

If you are canceling because the shop slipped a date, that is a different sentence (piece 25). If you are canceling because you want a different table, that is this sentence. Do not dress one as the other.

## Talk on the day the life changes

Move, death, job loss — call the same day. A shop can often stop before the next coat or before the hardware PO. A call in week six after a finished top is a different pile of oak.

Ask what is already identified to the job. Ask what can be sold as a sample and at what hit. Ask what cannot. Get it in email. Do not accept a round "custom, no refunds" if the saw has not run. Do not demand a full refund if your name is in the stretcher and the film is cured. Those are different days. The ack should have named the days while you were still friends.

## Sample the leftover oak if you can

A shop that can put the top on the floor as a sample may reduce the hit. Ask. Do not demand. A 96-inch rare green with a name in the stretcher will not become a floor leftover. A plain 84-inch oil top might. The ack should have said which world you were entering when the species and the carving were chosen (piece 04).

## The ack lines

A schedule: before lumber / after procurement / after first machine pass / after finish. What money does at each gate. Who decides the piece can be sold as a sample. No invented percents in this essay.

The yellow cut list is a person. Treat cancellation as if the boards can hear you. They cannot. The shop can. Call before the next pass.
