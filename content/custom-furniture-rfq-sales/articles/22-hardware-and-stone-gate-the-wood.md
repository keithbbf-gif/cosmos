The table was finished. The hinges were on a boat. The shop sat on a perfect top and an open mortise. The customer saw a photo of a done table and asked why it was not in the truck. The photo was the wood. The job was the hinge.

Buyouts are other people's queues. They belong on the RFQ next to the oak (piece 02, piece 19). UCC § 2-201(3)(a) even cares about procurement commitments — the shop that has already bought *your* stone is in a different legal neighborhood than the shop that has only talked. This pack will not litigate that. It will say: name the buyout before anyone stamps a week.

## Hardware

Hinges, slides, pulls, table locks, equalizers, casters, levelers. House hardware in the bin is days. A specified European hinge in a finish the distributor does not keep is weeks. A "soft-close like the ones we saw" is a hunt.

Write the series if you know it. If you do not, write the duty: kitchen, furniture, overlay, inset. Do not write "nice."

A change of pull after freeze is a change order and sometimes a new boring pattern (piece 30). New boring on a finished door is a remake.

## Stone and solid surface

Who templates (piece 16). Who fabricates. Who installs. A wood shop that promises a dock date before the stone template is promising a ghost. Sequence: base set or field-verified, template, fabricate, set, then sometimes wood finish touch-up. Put the sequence on the quote.

If the customer supplies the stone (piece 39), the shop still needs the hole and the timing. Late stone is storage and a finish that sits too long in a dry room or a wet one (piece 36).

## Glass and mirror

Tempered, sized, edged. A local glass shop is a week when they are empty. A beveled oval is not a week. Write the size after the wood is real — glass cut to a drawing that then scribes is scrap.

## COM and COL

Customer's own material, customer's own leather. Those words are a calendar and a liability. When does the fabric arrive. Who inspects it. What happens if it is short or flawed. Upholstery shops have rules about yards and repeats. Wood shops that also upholster still have those rules. Write them or do not take COM.

## Appliances and inserts

Islands (piece 13) and media cases eat spec sheets. The sheet is a buyout even if the customer bought the appliance at a box store. Wrong cutout is a finished box with a hole in the wrong county.

## Dealers

Your special is often a house wood and a specified pull from a brand the mill does not stock. Send the pull *with* the RFQ, not in week eight. If MAP or a finish match requires a specific SKU, put the SKU on page one (piece 05).

## Homeowners

You can save a week by using the shop's bin. You can spend a month on a brass that photographs well. Both are fine if they are chosen. A surprise specified pull in a text after the doors are bored is a change order.

## Parallel vs sequential, written

Mill can run while hinges are on order — unless the hinge boring is the first machine pass. Stone cannot template until the base is real — unless you accept a wood hole that may be wrong. COM cannot sew until the roll is inspected.

Draw two columns on the quote: PARALLEL and GATE. Put each buyout in a column. The date on the ack is the longer column plus the gates (piece 25). A shop that puts everything in PARALLEL is optimistic. A shop that puts everything in GATE is slow and sometimes right.

## The photo of a done top is not a ship date

Process photos of a finished table with an open mortise waiting on a hinge are how households think you are lying. Send the photo with a sentence: wood done, hinge PO #, ETA, date on ack still this / moved. Silence plus a pretty top is worse than a plain delay notice (piece 25).

## The RFQ lines

Buyouts listed. On hand / to order / customer-supplied. Template sequence. What can mill in parallel. What cannot.

The wood is only half the whiteboard. The hinge house has one too. Quote both boards, or you photographed a table and shipped a wait.
