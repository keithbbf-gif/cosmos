**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The house was new and proud and still drying. The table had lived in a shop that had been running HVAC for years. In two weeks the top had a belly. Someone said the shop rushed the kiln (piece 20). Someone else said the house was a swamp. Both could be true. Only one of them was on the RFQ.

Site conditions are a spec. AWI installation language has said for a long time that woodwork expects a building that is closed-in and conditioned. A furniture shop that skips that sentence on a new build is optimistic. A household that wants the table the week the drywall is taped is optimistic. Write the climate.

## Lived-in rooms

Ordinary houses in this county swing from wet August to dry winter heat. The piece was designed for that if the joinery allows movement (piece 32). Still: do not park a finished top in a closed car in July. Do not store leaves in a shed. Do not run the piece next to a stove (piece 18).

If the house has been empty with the HVAC off, turn it on before the truck — days, not an hour. `[VERIFY]` a house number of days if you publish one. This pack will not invent a meter reading.

## New construction and remodels

Wet slab, wet plaster, a crew still spraying. Refuse the date or store the piece (piece 27, piece 42). Touch-up after other trades is a different ticket. White glove into a job site is how you fund a scratch that is not the mill's.

Commercial openings (piece 46) are famous for this. The ribbon is Monday. The HVAC is Thursday. The furniture is already in the hall. Do not be the hall.

## Acclimation on site

Some specs want the piece in the room unboxed, days before final set. That is calendar (piece 19). It is also a security and a dust problem. Write who watches it.

Do not acclimate a crate in the rain. The crate is not a kiln.

## Who owns the climate

If the shop knew the house was new and shipped anyway without a warning, they own part of the story. If they warned and you took the truck, you own part. Put the warning on the ack. A footer nobody reads is weaker than a sentence in the confirm email.

## Dealers

Your warehouse climate is not the household. A piece that sat in a hot back room and then a cold truck and then a wet foyer has had three climates. Minimize the tour.

## A readiness sentence on the confirm

"Building lived-in, HVAC on, no wet trades in the room, path clear, someone home." If any clause is false, the shop may store or refuse (piece 42). New builds replace "lived-in" with a closed-in date and a humidity note. Facilities replace it with a dock window and a no-spray note (piece 46).

## Touch-up after other trades is a ticket

Painters, stone, floor sanders — they will hit a finished table if it is in the room early. Either store (piece 27) or write a touch-up ticket. "White glove" is not a shield against a sander. Facilities are famous for this (piece 46). Households remodeling a kitchen are the same story with a smaller dock.

## The RFQ lines

Building: lived-in / remodel / new. HVAC on as of. Jobsite: yes/no. Storage if not ready. Warning acknowledged.

The kiln can only dry the board you had. It cannot dry the house. Get the house ready, or get the table later. The belly does not care which pride you chose.
