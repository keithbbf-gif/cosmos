**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The PO said "same as last time" and the last time was a different stain, a different leaf, and a different store manager. The shop priced the memory. The dealer invoiced the customer on a different memory. Two invoices, one table, no friends.

A dealer RFQ can be one page. It should be. The shop is not your warehouse system. It is a floor with a queue. Give it the fields that price, in an order a human can read without opening six apps.

## Header

Store name, account number if you have one, buyer name, phone that answers, email that is not a black hole. Today's date. Your RFQ number — yours, so when you call you can say it. The shop will add theirs.

If this is a revision, say revision and what changed. Silent revisions are how the wrong hardware ships.

## The line

One line per object. Model or shop drawing number. Kind: stock / made-to-order / custom (piece 04). Qty. Size in inches, not "queen" unless the shop's queen is a published number. Finish system and color name *as the shop spells it*, not as your POS spells it. Hardware. Options that change the cut list: leaves, self-storing, felt, glass, stone cutout.

House vs floor vs sold special. The shop needs to know if this is a sample that will be abused or a piece that will be delivered to a living room. Floor samples get different edges, sometimes different tops, sometimes a different crate.

## The ship story

Ship-to, not the store's billing address unless they are the same. Dock, back door, or upstairs. Liftgate. Residential. A requested dock week labeled REQUESTED. Your customer's "need it for Thanksgiving" is not a shop promise (piece 24, piece 25).

If the piece must arrive with another vendor's stone or a carpet install, write the dependency. The shop cannot hold a finished top in the spray room for six weeks because the granite is late — not without a storage conversation (piece 22, piece 36).

## The paper fight, named early

Your PO will have terms. The shop's quote will say "subject to acknowledgment." UCC § 2-207 is waiting. Do not hide a 10-day ship, an open-ended warranty, or a chargeback clause in 8-point type and then act surprised when the ack strikes it.

If your chain requires a vendor packet — insurance, W-9, routing guide — send it *with* the RFQ, not the week the truck is loaded. Routing guides that appear on Friday for a Monday pickup are how crates get a wrong PRO.

## What to attach

One photo of the room if the special is site-sensitive. The path if the customer is residential (piece 14). A finish sample ID, not a screenshot (piece 31). The prior ack number if it really is "same as last time."

Do not attach your entire vendor manual. Do not attach a mood board of twenty tables. Do not attach a customer argument. The millworker is not your therapist.

## What to leave off

Retail price. The shop does not need your margin to cut oak. They need it if you are asking them to MAP-check or to print a tag. Say which.

Threats. "We have three other shops." Good. Send the scope you want matched (piece 40). A race to the lowest integer produces a different piece (piece 41).

A deposit. You do not fund the RFQ. You fund the ack (piece 26). If your accounts payable needs a prepay invoice, ask for that document after the quote, with the right name on it.

## Cadence

Send the page. Wait for the quote. Ask questions on the quote, in writing. Accept. Get the ack. Then cut the customer ticket. Dealers who sell the table on Saturday and RFQ on Monday are selling a calendar they do not have.

If you need a ballpark to hold a customer, label it (piece 03). Do not write the ballpark on the sales slip as if it were the ack.

## The one-page test, dealer edition

Hand the page to someone in the warehouse who does not sell. Can they tell what is coming, how many, what color, and where it goes? If they have to call you, rewrite the page. The shop is that warehouse, farther away, with a saw.

Piece 06 is the house version of this letter. Piece 44 is the checklist. This page is the habit: one job, one page, no voicemail required.
