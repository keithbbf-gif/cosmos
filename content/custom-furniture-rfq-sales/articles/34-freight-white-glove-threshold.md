**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The quote said "delivered." The driver said "end of driveway." Both thought they were honest. The table sat on gravel in the rain for twenty minutes while someone found a dolly that did not exist.

Freight is a menu. Write the item. A custom piece with a vague "delivered" is how you fund a curb argument after you already fought the deposit (piece 27). The path still has veto (piece 14). The service name does not override the landing.

## Dock

The truck, a receiving door, a pallet jack if you are lucky. Commercial default. Residential "dock" is a fantasy unless you have one. Dealers: your warehouse dock is not the household.

## Liftgate

The truck can lower a crate to the ground. It will not carry it up a stoop. People buy liftgate and think they bought help. They bought a gate.

## Threshold

Across the sill, into the first dry place, often not upstairs, often not assembled, often not through a turn. Better than gravel. Not the dining room.

## Room of choice / white glove

Pads, a crew, assembly, placement, debris out. Still not a wider rail. Still not a crane. Still not an HOA elevator reservation you forgot to book. If the crew arrives and the path fails, you have a return or a split conversation, not a miracle.

White glove books up in holiday weeks (piece 24). Reserve it when the deposit clears (piece 26), not when the film cures (piece 21).

## Inside delivery vs install

A built-in that must be scribed (piece 10, piece 16) is install, not freight. A different ticket, sometimes a different trade. Do not hide scribe labor in "white glove."

## Who quotes freight

Sometimes the shop. Sometimes a third-party carrier. Sometimes the dealer. Write who. A shop that says "freight extra" without a class or a weight is not done quoting. Ask for an estimate with assumptions: crate size, residential, stairs, wait time.

NMFC class and the bill of lading are the carrier's paper. Concealed-damage windows live there. This pack will not recite a carrier's manual. It will say: photograph the crate before you sign, and write exceptions on the BOL if the corner is crushed (piece 35, piece 37).

## Dealers

Your routing guide is a buyout of attention (piece 22, piece 33). Attach it. If you want a specific carrier, say so on page one. A mill that ships "best way" will pick cheap. Cheap will look like your quality.

## Homeowners

If you live on a dirt road, a low tree, or a cul-de-sac a 53-foot trailer cannot enter, say so. A smaller truck is a line. A refused trailer is a redelivery fee.

Be home. Have help if the service is threshold. Empty the path you measured. A crew that moves your piano is a different quote.

## Words to ban on the quote

"Delivered." "Shipping included." "They'll get it in." Replace with the menu item and the assumptions: residential, stairs, wait time, redelivery if refused. If freight is a third-party estimate, say estimate and the weight/class guess. A wrong guess is a change order on money, not a shop sin, if you wrote "estimate."

## Be home, empty the path

A crew that moves a piano to place a table is a different quote. A path you measured and then filled with boxes is a path you lied about. Redelivery is a line (piece 45). The shop is not late if they booked the window you asked for and you were at work (piece 25).

## The RFQ lines

Service: dock / liftgate / threshold / room. Stairs. Residential. Who quotes. Who books. Path attached.

"Delivered" is a warm word. The gravel does not care. Pick a colder word and a measured path. Then the hello at the door can stay a hello.
