The sideboard kissed the outlet. Not a metaphor. The plug for the lamp had nowhere to go, and the only other outlet was behind the door swing. The piece was the right length for the wall in the photo. The wall in the photo had hidden the receptacle with a plant.

Case pieces look simple: a box against a wall. The box is easy. The wall is a pile of trim, power, and doors. The RFQ that prices only the box will deliver a fight.

## Baseboard and shoe

Measure the height and the thickness of the base. A plinth that sits on the shoe will kick the case forward. A back that dies to the plaster will need a notch or a scribe rail. Write the detail you want: sit on the shoe, notch over it, or float a shadow.

Old shoe is chewed. New MDF base is proud of the plaster. Both steal depth. A 16-inch deep dresser in a catalog is not 16 inches in the room if the baseboard ate an inch and the drawer hardware ate another.

## Width is not the wall

Leave the door swing. Leave the window trim. Leave a hand on each side if someone has to make the bed. A dresser that is "the width of the wall" is a dresser that cannot be pulled out when a sock falls behind it — and a dresser that will show every out-of-square sin (piece 10).

Center on the window or center on the wall. Pick one. They are not the same.

## Height

Ceiling, crown, and the fan (piece 15). A bookcase that "goes to the ceiling" needs a scribe to the joist dip and a plan for the crown. A bookcase that stops short needs a number, not "pretty tall."

Top drawers and the mirror you already own: measure the mirror. A jewelry drawer that hits the mirror frame is a Tuesday problem.

## Drawers and doors on the case

Full-extension slides need clearance to the wall or the next piece. A door on a sideboard needs a swing that does not hit the dining chair. Write the chair. Write the slide type if you care. Soft-close is a hardware lead time (piece 22), not a personality.

## Power and data

Outlets, switches, thermostat, alarm keypad. Photograph them with a tape (piece 07, piece 18). A case that covers a thermostat is a service call. A case that covers the only outlet is a power strip story.

If you need an outlet in the case — a charging drawer, a lamp pass-through — that is custom, and it is electrical, and it may be a licensed job in your town. Do not hide it in "a little cutout."

## Soft walls

Plaster waves. A tall bookcase will rock. A low dresser will show a wedge. Scribe or a thicker back rail is labor. Rocking is a review.

Carpet: the case will sink. Measure to the slab if you can, or admit the sink. A file drawer that was proud in the shop will drag on a thick pad.

## Dealers

Bedroom suites are sold as widths that fit a plan view. The plan view did not include the baseboard or the outlet. Send a photo of the wall at outlet height. It is unglamorous. It prevents a truck roll.

## Depth is where case pieces fail

A catalog "18-inch" dresser is often 18 to the front of the drawer, not to the pulls, and not after a ¾-inch baseboard and a proud shoe. Add the pull. Add the base. Then see if the remaining aisle is a person (piece 13). Bedroom suites sold from a plan view die here: the plan used inside wall to inside wall and forgot trim.

If the piece must sit in an alcove, measure the alcove at the front and at the back. Alcoves taper. A case built to the front number will jam. A case built to the back number will show a gap at the face. Pick the number you mean, or template (piece 16).

File drawers and printer shelves have extra depth needs. Write the machine. A "desk" RFQ without the printer is how you fund a return.

## Soft-close and the last inch

Full-extension plus a 1¼-inch pull plus a baseboard is how a "fits" dresser fails the closet door. Draw the depth stack on the RFQ: box, face, pull, trim, remaining aisle. If the remaining aisle is a child, you are done. If it is a shoulder, you are not. Hardware lead time still applies (piece 22); do not add soft-close in week eight and discover the drawer now hits the door.

## The RFQ lines

W × D × H. Plinth vs notch. Drawer count and slide. Door swing. Outlet map. Baseboard height. Floor finish. Path (piece 14) — a dresser is often the piece that will not turn the stair even when the table will, because the dresser does not split.

The wall is not a backdrop. It is half the joint. Measure both halves.
