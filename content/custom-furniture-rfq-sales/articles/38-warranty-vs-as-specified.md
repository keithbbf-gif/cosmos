The card said "lifetime" in a font that liked itself. The drawing said 84 inches, oil, no leaf. The household wanted a different life than the drawing. Those are three documents. Only one of them was the job.

Custom work is as-specified work. The spec is the approved drawing and the ack (piece 08). A warranty that ignores the spec is poetry. A spec that pretends nothing can fail is a shrug. Hold both.

## What should be on the card

What is covered: structure, joinery failure in ordinary use, finish failure that is a film fault (not a hot pan, not a puppy). What is not: movement within the design (piece 32), color change in sun, customer measure errors (piece 17), site climate you were warned about (piece 36), a phone match.

How long. Who honors it if the shop name changes. How to make a claim. Magnuson-Moss and 16 CFR Parts 700–703 care about written consumer warranties — full vs limited, what you must disclose if you use certain words. `[VERIFY]` counsel before you print "full" or "lifetime." This pack will not draft the card.

## As-specified is not a dodge

If the drawing said 84 and the piece is 80, that is not a lifestyle complaint. If the board was approved and the top is a different system, that is not "wood varies." If the path required a split and you shipped a one-piece crate that failed the landing, that is not the customer's romance with oak (piece 14, piece 35).

As-specified cuts both ways. The household cannot demand a leaf they did not buy. The shop cannot hide a miss behind poetry.

## Heirloom

A true sentence: a well-built piece can last. A false sentence: heirloom is a warranty. Do not put it on the card. Do not put it in a blog as if it were coverage. The craft pack can talk about joints. This pack talks about paper.

## Commercial vs household

Healthcare and hospitality (piece 46) often want a spec warranty and a parts term that matches a facility, not a family. Do not paste a consumer "lifetime" onto a day room. Do not paste a BIFMA test onto a farm table as if it were a hug.

## Dealers

Your store card and the mill card will disagree. Read both before you sell. If you add a store warranty, you own it. If you only pass through the mill, say so. Chargebacks that invent coverage the mill never wrote are how friendships end (piece 33).

## How this sits next to the punch list

Delivery day is for misses against the spec (piece 37). Year three is for a joint that let go. Do not save a size error for year three. Do not call a January gap in a breadboard on day two a structural failure without looking at the design.

## Words to keep off the card

Heirloom. Lifetime, unless counsel blessed the disclosures. Match any photo. Never moves. Full, unless it is a full warranty on purpose. "We stand behind our work" without a how-to-claim. Put the how-to-claim. Put the as-specified pointer. Put the movement paragraph. That card will feel less warm and will fail less often in year three.

## Two cards will disagree

Store card and mill card. Read both before you sell. If you add coverage the mill never wrote, you own it. If you only pass through the mill, say so on the ticket. Chargebacks that invent years are how the next special gets a deposit the mill will not waive (piece 26, piece 33).

## The ack lines

Warranty card attached: yes/no. As-specified pointer. Consumer vs commercial. Counsel still to read.

The drawing is the promise. The card is the backup if the drawing was built and then failed. The phone is neither. Keep the pile that small.
