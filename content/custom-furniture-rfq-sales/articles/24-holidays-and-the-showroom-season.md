**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

In June the whiteboard already had Thanksgiving written in a corner, half joke, half grave. By September the joke was gone. Every RFQ wanted the same Thursday. The oak did not care. The queue did.

Holidays are not surprises. They are a pile-up you can see from the solstice. Showroom seasons and market weeks are the dealer version of the same pile-up. Write them as calendar facts, not as emotional leverage.

## The household holidays

Thanksgiving tables, Christmas deliveries, Easter, a graduation in May. The want is real. The stack does not compress because the turkey is in the oven (piece 19, piece 23). A shop that takes a September RFQ for a custom table at Thanksgiving is either empty, lying, or talking about a made-to-order object already solved (piece 04) with lumber in the rack (piece 20).

Put a cutoff on the wall if you have one. `[VERIFY]` before publishing a date as house policy. "RFQs after [date] are not promised for Thanksgiving" is a kindness. It will make people angry in the office. It will save them on the porch.

A want-date belongs on the RFQ labeled WANT (piece 06). A need-date belongs with a reason, so the shop can refuse. "Need" without a reason is a WANT in a louder font.

## The dealer seasons

Spring and fall markets, Fourth of July sales, a store's anniversary. Dealers load specials into a window and then ask the mill to be a warehouse. The mill is a mill. If you need floor samples for a date, RFQ them like a commercial opening (piece 46): early, with a path, with a finish freeze.

Do not sell "in time for the sale" on a custom line you have not acknowledged. Your circular is a representation to a lot of households. The mill's ack is a representation to you. They should match.

## Freight in the pile-up

Trucks get scarce. White-glove books fill. A piece that is finished on the 10th and delivered on the 26th is a freight story (piece 34), not a shop-slack story. Book the window when you book the deposit (piece 26), not when the film is cured (piece 21).

Weather in this part of the country will ice a week in January. Build a spare week if the holiday is immovable. Spare weeks are how adults calendar.

## Remote promises

A website that says "guaranteed by Christmas" on a custom object is a 16 CFR Part 435 conversation if the order is internet or phone (piece 25). Guarantees without a stack are ads. This pack does not write ads.

## How to keep the dinner

Use the table you have, or buy stock (piece 04), or take a made-to-order in a shop stain. Commission the custom for January, when you can enjoy it. That sentence feels like a loss in September. It feels like wisdom in December.

Shops can say this without being cruel. Customers can hear it without being sold. Dealers can write it on the ticket and still keep the relationship.

## The RFQ lines

Want-date. Holiday-driven: yes/no. Accept January if the stack says so. Floor-sample window, if dealer.

The turkey will come whether the table does. The whiteboard knew in June. Ask it in June.
