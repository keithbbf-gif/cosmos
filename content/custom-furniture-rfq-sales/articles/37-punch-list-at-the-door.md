**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The crew was tired. The household wanted them gone. Someone signed a clean ticket. Two days later the raking light showed a dent the crate had advertised at the curb. The BOL said nothing. The claim got harder.

A punch list at the door is not hostility. It is how you still like each other in week two. UCC § 2-606 is the acceptance neighborhood — receiving can become acceptance if you act like an owner and wait too long. This pack is not your clock. It is a habit: look now, write now.

## Before they leave

Crate corners if a crate (piece 35). Count of pieces. Obvious finish hits, broken joints, a size that is not the drawing (piece 08). The path they actually used versus the path you measured (piece 14). Hardware in the bag.

Walk it in the *room light* you used for the sample (piece 31), not in the garage. Compare to the board, not to the phone (piece 32).

If something is wrong, write it on the ticket and on the BOL if freight is in the story (piece 34). Photograph with a tape. A crew that will not wait five minutes is a crew you still write after.

## What can wait

A grain that is not the JPEG. A seasonal tell that is not a split. A question about care. Those are emails, not a refused delivery.

A color fight versus the approved board should not wait a month. It can wait an evening if the light is wrong tonight — then write "color check tomorrow vs board RFQ-__."

## Cure and touch-up

A shop may have the right to cure a defect (UCC § 2-508 neighborhood). Field touch-up is ordinary on furniture. A full remake is not ordinary. Say which you think you are looking at. Do not demand a remake for a nick a tech can blend. Do not accept "wood moves" for a door that never latched (piece 32, piece 38).

## Dealers

Your receiver should punch-list before the household if the piece hits your warehouse. Do not pass a crushed crate to a living room and then act shocked. Your warehouse light is not the room light — still catch the crush.

## Homeowners

Be home. Have the drawing and the board out. Have a lamp. If you cannot do this on delivery day, do it the next morning and send the note. Silence is a kind of acceptance in the real world even when the statute is more careful.

Do not post a one-star before you send the punch list. The shop cannot cure a review.

## A ten-minute door script

Crate photos. Count. Drawing out. Board out. Walk joints and size. Write exceptions or "none seen in this light." Sign time. Crew can go. Evening raking-light check if needed, email same night.

If you are a dealer receiver, do this in the warehouse *and* tell the household to do the room-light pass. Two punch lists catch two classes of miss: crush, and color.

## Do not post before you send the list

A one-star that the shop has not seen is a remake you refused to allow. Send the list. Give a remedy window. Then talk to the internet if they ghost. Shops cannot cure a review. They can cure a nick (piece 38).

## The ticket lines

Exceptions: none / listed. Photos: yes. Board compared: yes/no. Freight note: yes/no. Signature and time.

The crew can go. The light can stay. Use the light the same day. The dent will not get more honest later.
