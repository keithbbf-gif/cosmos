**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The whiteboard in the office has job names and a column of weeks that keep getting erased. Someone wants a stamp: 12 weeks, as if the stamp were a kiln. The stamp is a guess about a stack. The stack is the truth.

Custom lead time is not a personality. It is six waits that sometimes run in parallel and often do not. If you only remember one sentence from this pack's calendar pieces, remember this: ask for the stack, not the stamp.

## The stack

**Queue.** Other jobs are already on the saw. Your deposit buys a place in that line (piece 26), not a teleport. A shop that is "12 weeks" when it is empty and "12 weeks" when it is slammed is lying to one of those rooms.

**Lumber.** The right boards may be in the rack, in a kiln, or on a truck from a yard (piece 20). Species, thickness, and grade change this wait. A 8/4 white oak slab is not a 4/4 red oak top.

**Mill.** Cut list, joinery, dry-fit, glue (the craft pack next door). A known made-to-order object is faster than a first-time custom (piece 04). A remake is a new mill wait.

**Finish.** Sealer, color, film, cure. Days you cannot stack chairs on the top (piece 21). Humidity in south Arkansas is not a footnote.

**Buyouts.** Hardware, stone, glass, COM fabric, a special caster (piece 22). The wood can be ready and the job can still be a box on the floor.

**Freight.** A truck, a crate, a window (piece 34). A dock in Dallas is not a turn stair in a bungalow (piece 14).

Add them like an adult. Some overlap: hardware can be ordered while the mill runs. Some do not: you cannot finish what you have not glued, and you should not glue what you have not approved (piece 08).

## Why "12 weeks" spreads

It fits in a catalog. It fits in a salesperson's mouth. It is sometimes true for a made-to-order oak table in a quiet month. It is a lie for a custom built-in that needs a template, a stone top, and a white-glove window at Thanksgiving (piece 24).

Dealers paste 12 weeks into the POS because the POS has one field. Homeowners hear 12 weeks because they asked "how long" in a parking lot (piece 03). Both should ask: 12 from what — RFQ, approval, or deposit? Those are three clocks.

## From when

A serious ack starts the calendar at a defined event: signed approval + deposit cleared, or lumber in the rack, or the later of the two. `[VERIFY]` the house start. An RFQ date is not a start. A verbal "we can do fall" is not a start.

If the customer sits on a quote for six weeks, they did not buy a six-week delay in the shop. They bought a stale quote (piece 03). Re-price and re-queue.

## Remote clocks

If the order is mail, internet, or telephone, 16 CFR Part 435 cares about the ship time you stated and the delay notice you owe (piece 25). A stamp on a website is a representation. A stamp you cannot defend is a Rule problem, not just a disappointed dinner.

Showroom acks are still honesty problems even when the Rule does not apply. Do not use "it's custom" as a way to skip calling when the stack slips.

## How to read a shop that knows its stack

They will say what they do not know (freight, a hinge house, a stone template). They will say what is sequential. They will not promise Thanksgiving in September if the queue is already Christmas. They will put a date on the ack and a sentence about what slips it (piece 25).

A shop that only has the stamp will also only have an excuse.

## Dealers

Put the stack in the special-order note, even if the customer only sees a week count. When the hinge house slips, you will need the note. When the customer asks why, you will have a sentence that is not "the shop is slow." Sometimes the shop is slow. Sometimes the stack was visible in week one and nobody wrote it down.

## Homeowners

Ask: what is already in front of me? Is the oak here? What hardware is a buyout? When do you want my approval? What happens if I change the stain after freeze (piece 30)? Those questions are not rude. They are the job.

## A stack written as a paragraph

"Queue is three jobs, about three weeks. Oak 8/4 is on a truck, yard says ten days, then rest. Mill on a known apron is a week after lumber. Finish is five working days plus cure. Pulls are in the bin. White glove is booked the week after cure if you book it at deposit."

That paragraph is a quote's calendar. "12 weeks" is a costume of that paragraph. If any clause is UNKNOWN, write UNKNOWN. A shop that will not write the paragraph will not call you when one clause slips (piece 25).

The whiteboard will still get erased. The stack is still the thing. Quote the stack. Stamp nothing you cannot point to.
