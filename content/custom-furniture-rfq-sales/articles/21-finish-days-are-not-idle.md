**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The top looked done on Thursday. A sock printed in it on Friday. The film was still a clock. The customer saw a quiet spray room and called it slack. The room was waiting on a recoat window that does not care about anyone's dinner.

Finish is labor you can see and time you cannot rush without lying. An RFQ that picks a color from a phone (piece 31) is also picking a calendar (piece 19). Oil, conversion varnish, lacquer, paint — different clocks. This pack will not name a house system. It will say: ask for the days, and do not crate a green piece.

## Recoat windows

Cans have windows. Too soon and you wrinkle or solvent-pop. Too late and you scuff-sand for adhesion. Humidity in this county moves the window. A shop that "always recoats the next morning" is either in a system that allows it or is gambling. Gambling is a remake.

If you change color after the first coat, you may have changed the window and the week (piece 30).

## Cure vs dry-to-touch

Dry-to-touch is a fingerprint test. Cure is when the film can take a crate, a seat, a vase. They are not the same afternoon. White-glove crews that stand a leaf on a green top will leave a story. Build cure into the pickup date (piece 34).

Oil finishes that "soaked in" still smear. Waterborne films that look clear still print. Winter shops and August shops are different rooms. The stack should say which room you are in.

## Color as calendar

A sample board in the room overnight (piece 31) is a day you should want. A second board is another day. A "surprise me" finish is how you fund a strip. Stripping is mill time plus finish time. It is not a free mood.

Matching a 10-year-old piece is sample time plus the admission that you will not hit it dead (piece 32). Budget the samples on the quote so nobody calls them stall.

## Paint and pigment

Paint hides and paint shows. More coats, more days. Two-tone is two colors and a mask. A painted case with an oil top is two systems and a tape line. Write it. Price it. Calendar it.

## "Non-toxic" is not a date

Do not buy a week of marketing. If you care about VOC or a named standard, name the product and the SDS and the rule. The calendar follows the product, not the slogan. See `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`.

## Dealers

Do not promise "in the truck Friday" because the mill ended Wednesday. Ask the finish room. The finish room is the truth. If your POS cannot hold two dates, hold the later one.

## Homeowners

You can ask for a simpler finish to buy a week. You cannot ask for a piano film on Monday and a Tuesday pickup. If the dinner is fixed, the finish has to be a finish that can exist by then — or the dinner uses the old table (piece 24, piece 42).

## What to ask the finish room, not the salesperson

Recoat window for this system in this weather. Cure before crate. Whether a color change after coat one is a strip. Whether the sample board used the same grit and the same sealer as the job (piece 31).

If the salesperson answers without walking to the finish room, you have a hallway date (piece 03). Walk. The room will smell like solvent and truth.

## August in this county

Humidity moves windows. A system that recoats next morning in March may need more hours in August. The finish room knows. The POS does not. If your date is a holiday in a wet month (piece 24), add a day before you sell the dinner. A sock print in a green film is not "custom character." It is a crate that left early.

## The RFQ lines

Finish system (or "shop house, sample in room"). Sheen. Paint vs clear. Match existing: yes/no. Pickup no sooner than cure — who decides.

The spray room at rest is not a nap. It is the film becoming a film. Put those days in the stack. The sock will thank you.
