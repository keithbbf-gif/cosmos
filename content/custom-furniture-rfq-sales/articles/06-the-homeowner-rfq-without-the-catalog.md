The email had seven photos of seven tables and the sentence "we want something unique." Unique is not a dimension. Unique is how you get a quote that is really a guess, then a table that is really an argument.

You do not need a dealer account to write an RFQ. You need the same job packet with fewer part numbers (piece 02). The shop will translate. It will not mind-read.

## One sentence for the problem

"We need a table for six that can become eight at Thanksgiving, in a room that is 11'4" by 13'2", with a stair that turns." That is a problem. "We want it to feel like Sunday" is not.

The problem is the object. Everything else is how you solve it. If the problem is a built-in that must clear a return-air, say the return-air. If the problem is a dresser that must die into a closet door swing, say the swing. Shops are good at problems. They are bad at vibes.

## The room, as it is

Measure it (piece 09). Write the inches. Photograph the corners that are not square (piece 10). Photograph the light at the time of day you will live with the piece — not the golden hour you wish you had (piece 31).

Note what already lives there and will stay: radiator, piano, dog bed, the door that slams. A table that fits the empty room will not fit the room you actually eat in.

If you cannot measure today, say that and ask for a site measure. Do not invent 12 feet because the listing said 12 feet. Listings lie. Tape does not, if you hook it (piece 17).

## The path

Walk from the street to the room with a tape in your hand (piece 14). Doors, turns, stair, landing, ceiling at the soffit. If the top will not make the turn, the RFQ must say the base and top ship apart — or that the piece cannot be this long.

Elevators in condos have a door and a car. Measure both. Building rules about pads, hours, and certificates of insurance belong in the RFQ if they exist. The shop cannot guess your HOA.

## Finish without a catalog chip

You can still be specific. Species you like in a piece you already own. A sample board you are willing to leave in the room. A photo of a table *as a direction*, with a sentence about what you are stealing (the edge, the thickness, the color) and what you are not (the carved lion).

"Match the floors" is a trap (piece 02). "Oil, not film, because we are messy" is a useful constraint. "No yellow" is a start; "this board, this window, this week" is a finish direction (piece 31).

If you have a can of leftover stain, say so now (piece 39). Old cans are not a finish system. They are a chemistry problem.

## Money and time, said like an adult

A range you can actually fund, labeled as a budget ceiling, not as a bid. "We cannot go past X including freight" helps the shop propose a smaller object instead of a prettier lie. A ballpark you already heard, labeled BALLPARK (piece 03). A date you care about, labeled WANT, not NEED, unless you truly need it — and then say why, so the shop can refuse (piece 42).

Do not send a card number in the first email. Do not ask the shop to "hold" a table that does not exist. Hold comes after a quote and an ack (piece 26).

## Photos that help

Three to six. The room empty-ish. The path's tight spot. The piece you like, if any, with a ruler in the frame. The wall outlet you will fight. Not your family. Not forty screenshots. Not a competitor's watermarked catalog page you expect the shop to copy mill-for-mill — that is a different conversation about design and honesty.

Piece 07 is the photo rules. This piece is only: send enough to build a takeoff, not enough to bury one.

## Tone

You can be warm. You cannot be vague. "We're easy" is how shops get a remake. "We will pick a stain from boards in the room, and we understand custom after the cut list costs money" is easy in the way that matters.

If two people in the house disagree, do not send two RFQs from two emails. Send one packet and say the decision is not final on finish. The shop can quote a structure and hold finish. It cannot quote two tables for the price of one argument.

## What happens next

The shop should ask questions. That is a good sign. Answer in the same thread so the packet stays one packet. Then you get a quote with a scope. Read the scope twice. The number is the least interesting line if the leaf is missing.

If the quote feels high, ask what in the object is costing. Thickness, species, split base, white glove, a finish that takes a week to cure — those are levers (piece 41). Asking "can you do it for less" with no lever is a script. This pack does not do scripts.

You do not need a catalog number. You need a problem, a tape, and a page the millworker can read. That is a homeowner RFQ. Piece 44 is the checklist before you send it.
