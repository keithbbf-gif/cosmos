**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The wall was a gentle belly, plaster proud in the middle, shy at the studs. The notebook had a single width. The case, built to the notebook, rocked like a chair. The second trip was cardboard. The third trip was a scribe. The first trip should have been the cardboard.

Tape is a straight-line tool. Rooms are not always straight-line problems (piece 10). When the job is a curve, a jog, a stone sink, a hearth, or a bay, make a template or admit you are guessing.

## When to template

A built-in against a wall that failed 3-4-5. A top that must follow a radius window. A hearth surround. An island that dies into a jog (piece 13). A stone cutout the wood shop must leave. A delivery path that is a sculpture of rails — sometimes a plywood blank of the crate is the only honest turn test (piece 14, piece 35).

If you can describe the shape in three numbers, tape. If you are using the word "sort of," template.

## What to make it from

Luan, cardboard, cheap plywood, or the shop's preferred sheet. `[VERIFY]` house material. Tape the seams. Write "TEMPLATE — not the piece" on it so nobody finishes it. Date it. Initial it. Photograph it on the wall with a tape in the frame (piece 07).

A paper rubbing is not a template for a case. It is a hint. Stone people have their own language; if they are templating the top, the wood shop should see that template or a file that came from it (piece 22). Two templates that disagree are two jobs.

## Who owns it

If the shop makes it, the shop owns the fit (piece 17). If the customer makes it, the customer owns the fit. A designer template is the designer's fit unless the shop field-verifies. Write the owner on the ack.

Templates walk away. They get wet. They curl in a truck. Store them like they are money. They are.

## Digital scans

A phone scan or a lidar visit can be a template if the shop will actually mill from it and if someone still checks two tape numbers. A pretty mesh that nobody verified is a expensive napkin. Say who signed the file.

## Lead time

Templating is a trip. Trips are calendar (piece 19). An RFQ that hides a required template will produce a quote that hides a week. Put "template visit required" on the first page (piece 02).

Stone templates often happen after cabinets are set. Wood shops that finish a top profile before the stone template are brave. Brave is a remake.

## Dealers

Do not send a napkin from the walkthrough as if it were a template. Do not promise "they'll figure it in the field" on a piece that will be finished before it sees the field. Field-fit and shop-finish are in tension. Finish after scribe, or scribe a raw edge. Pick one and price it.

## How a bad template wastes a top

A cardboard template taped with packaging tape will creep in a hot truck. A luan template left on damp concrete will banana. Date and photograph the template *on the wall* the day you make it, then again the day it arrives at the shop. If the two photos disagree, do not mill.

Label the room side, the floor, and the up. Templates get flipped. A flipped hearth surround is a pretty scrap.

If two trades template the same hole — wood and stone — pick a master. The master file or the master sheet wins. The other trade fits. Two masters are two holes.

## When digital is honest

A lidar scan the shop will mill from, signed, with two tape checks in the file, is a template. A pretty mesh nobody opened in the CAM is a brochure. Ask who will click "toolpath." If the answer is "the designer has a model," you still need a shop sign-off. Models are square. Rooms are not (piece 10).

## The RFQ lines

Template required: yes/no. Who makes it. What it controls (back, side, top cutout, path). Date of visit. Who signs.

The tape is still the first tool (piece 09). The template is what you reach for when the tape starts lying by being too simple. Cardboard is cheaper than pride.
