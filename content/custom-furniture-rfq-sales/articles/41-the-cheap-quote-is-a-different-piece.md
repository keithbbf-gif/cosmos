**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The integer was pretty. The scope had holes you could carry a leaf through — because the leaf was gone. So was the site measure. So was the crate. The household thought they had shopped (piece 40). They had collected costumes.

Cheap is not a sin. A shop with oak in the rack (piece 20) and a known object (piece 04) should be cheaper than a hunt. A shop that wins by deleting the job is not cheaper. It is otherwise employed.

## Common deletions

**The visit.** Customer tape only (piece 17). Fine if you own the inch. Not fine if you thought they did.

**The path.** No split, no crate-out, "white glove will handle it" (piece 14, piece 34). White glove will handle a refusal.

**The leaf / the hardware / the stone hole.** Buyouts left to "later" (piece 22). Later is a second quote.

**The finish sample.** Phone match (piece 31). A remake is not cheap.

**The date kind.** Ship-from-shop sold as in-your-room (piece 25). Two Fridays.

**The kind of work.** Custom sold at made-to-order math, or stock language on a first-time object (piece 04).

**The paper.** No ack, deposit to "hold a vibe" (piece 03, piece 26).

## How to test a low number

Ask the shop to initial your packet and say "priced as written." If they mark up the packet, those marks *are* the quote. If they will not initial, you do not have a bid. You have a hook.

Ask what happens if the stair fails. Ask what happens if the can of stain fails (piece 39). Ask what happens if you cancel after the cut list (piece 29). A cheap quote that goes silent on those is not cheap. It is incomplete.

## Dealers

A mill that underbids to win a chain will get even on freight, on a finish, or on a chargeback war (piece 33). You will feel it in month four. A mill that explains why they are higher is easier to sell than a mill that lies low.

If your buyer demands the low integer and the full scope, you are the one who will eat the gap. Write that for yourself before you write the PO.

## Homeowners

You can choose the smaller object on purpose: no leaf, shop stain, threshold not room, made-to-order not custom. That is a good cheap. It is honest. The grid in piece 40 will show it. The costume will not.

## When cheap is a walk

If the only way the number works is a lie about the stack (piece 19) or a deposit that vanishes into a drawer with no drawing (piece 26). Walk (piece 43). There will be another shop. There will not be another landing.

## Initial the deletions

If you accept a cheap quote, write the deletions in your own hand: no visit, no leaf, threshold, shop stain, customer tape. Sign it. Then you cannot be surprised. If you will not sign the deletions, you do not want the cheap quote. You want the other integer with the full packet.

## Honest cheap is a smaller object

No leaf, shop stain, threshold, made-to-order, oak in the rack. Write those as choices. That integer is a bargain if you wanted that object. A costume integer that pretends to be the full packet is not a bargain. It is a later fight. The grid (piece 40) is how you see which you have.

The pretty integer can stay pretty. The job has to stay the job. If they parted, you did not get a deal. You got a different table. Quote the table you meant.
