The ack said the week of the 14th. The hallway had said "before the holiday" (piece 24). The customer remembered the hallway. The floor remembered the ack. Only one of those documents was going to the truck.

A date is a representation. Put it on the acknowledgment, start it from a defined event, and say what slips it. If you sell by mail, internet, or telephone, 16 CFR Part 435 has already written part of this for you. If you sell in a showroom with a signed ack, you still owe honesty. You may not owe that Rule. Do not guess which channel you are in — write it.

## What the date is

A week the shop has a reasonable basis to hit, given the stack (piece 19). Not a wish. Not a salesperson's courage. Reasonable basis is the Rule's language for remote orders (16 CFR 435.2). It is also a decent shop word for every channel.

Name the kind of date: ship from shop, deliver to dock, deliver to room. Those are three different Fridays (piece 34). A customer who hears "the 14th" will hear it in their dining room.

## When the clock starts

Signed approval (piece 08) and cleared deposit (piece 26) is a common pair. Lumber in the rack is another (piece 20). The later of the two is safer. An RFQ is not a start. A ballpark is not a start (piece 03). A heart emoji is not a start.

If the customer delays approval, the date moves. Write that. If the shop delays because a buyout slipped, the date moves and someone has to talk (piece 22).

## The 30-day default people misread

If a remote seller states no time, Part 435's default is generally thirty days after a properly completed order (fifty if the buyer applies to the seller for credit at the time of order). Custom furniture people hear "30 days" and either panic or joke. The useful sentence is: if you are in the Rule, either state a time you can defend or you have borrowed a 30-day clock that most custom stacks cannot keep. State the stack's time. Do not hide from the default by saying nothing on a website that takes the order.

Showroom custom with a 16-week ack is a different animal. Still write the 16 weeks. Still call if they slip.

## When it slips

Call before the date dies. Say the new date if you have a reasonable basis, or say you do not. Offer the choices the Rule requires when the Rule applies: consent to delay or cancel for a prompt refund — details in § 435.2 and the FTC's business guide. Silence is not always consent; later notices get stricter. Read the Rule. This page is not your delay notice.

On channels the Rule does not cover, still call. A shop that goes quiet is a shop that will meet a lawyer or a one-star, and both cost more than the call.

Refunds on custom after a substantial beginning are a fact pattern (piece 29), not a slogan. The Rule's "prompt refund" and a shop's specially manufactured goods story can tension. Counsel. Do not blog your way through that knot.

## Dealers

Your PO date and the ack date will fight (piece 02, UCC paper). The floor will build the ack. If you promised your customer the PO date, you promised a date you may not have. Align them before you sell the Thanksgiving (piece 24).

When the mill calls a slip, you call the household the same day. You are the mouth they bought.

## Homeowners

If the only date you have is a hallway, ask for the ack. If the ack has no date, ask why. If the shop will not put a week on paper, you do not have a calendar. You have a vibe.

If a delay notice arrives, read it. A cancel-and-refund option on a remote order is a legal object. A "we'll try" text is not.

## The RFQ / ack lines

Channel: showroom / dealer PO / internet / phone. Start event. Date kind: ship / dock / room. Date. Slip triggers. Who calls whom.

## A delay call that is a document

"Ack A-22 said room-of-choice week of the 14th. The hinge house slipped; we do not have a reasonable basis for the 14th. We can offer week of the 28th if the hinge lands Friday, or cancel per the ack. Please reply."

That is a notice. "Running a little behind" is not. On a remote order, read Part 435 for the extra sentences you may owe. On a showroom order, still send the paragraph. Copy the dealer the same hour if a store sold the date (piece 33).

## Ship vs room is two Fridays

A shop that writes "the 14th" without ship / dock / room is writing a fight. Freight after the 14th is not the mill being late if the ack said ship. A household that heard room will not care about your vocabulary. Put the vocabulary on the paper they signed. Then the delay notice, if you need one, has a thing to delay.

The hallway can stay friendly. The truck reads the ack. Put the week where the truck can see it.
