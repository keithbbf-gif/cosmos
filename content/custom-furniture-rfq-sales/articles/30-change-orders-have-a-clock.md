**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

She wanted the overhang a little longer. The tenons were already home. A little longer was a new rail, a new cut list, a new week. The email said "quick tweak." The door said REV D or a remake.

Spec freeze is the day the drawing stops being a conversation (piece 08). After that, a change is a change order: money, calendar, sometimes a piece that is already the wrong piece. UCC § 2-209 is the modification neighborhood. This pack will not litigate consideration. It will say: write the change like the first quote, or do not pretend the old date still holds (piece 25).

## What counts

Anything that moves a machine: length, thickness, joinery, leaf, hardware boring, a different stone hole (piece 22). Anything that moves a finish system or a color after the first coat (piece 21). A path split you did not mention (piece 14). A chair count that forces a longer top (piece 11).

What does not count: a delivery window that freight can still slide, a phone number, a misspelled street. Still write those. Do not call them change orders.

## The clock

House gates vary. Approval date. Lumber pull. First machine pass. First color coat. `[VERIFY]` the gate. The earlier you freeze, the cheaper a change is. The later you "tweak," the more you are canceling (piece 29) and starting a cousin job.

A shop that never freezes is a shop that never keeps a date. A shop that freezes before you have seen a sample in the room (piece 31) is a shop that will remake the color. Sequence: sample, approve, freeze, mill.

## Paper

REV letter, date, who asked, what moved, new price, new week, new deposit if the object got more expensive. Same seriousness as the first ack. A text that says "sure add the leaf" is how two people remember two prices.

Dealers: your customer will ask you, you will text the mill, the mill will need a form. Be the form. Do not be the shrug.

## Price and time

A leaf is not "a little more wood." It is a mechanism, an apron, a store, a crate (piece 35). A stain change can be a strip. A hardware change can be a new door. If the change saves money, say so. If it does not, say so. Do not hide a week because you are embarrassed you asked.

Rush plus a change order is two lies stacked (piece 23). Pick one.

## When to refuse the change

If the date is a holiday (piece 24) and the change kills the stack. If the path cannot take the new length. If two people in the house do not agree (piece 06). Refusal is a product (piece 42).

## A change-order that kept a friend

Household wanted 2 inches more overhang after dry-fit, before glue. Shop wrote REV D, added a day and a small integer, household signed the same afternoon. Date slipped one week, labeled. Dinner still happened.

A change-order that did not: "just stain it darker" after coat two, holiday week, no new ack. Shop tried. Strip failed. January plus a scar. The tweak was a second job. Write the second job or keep the first color.

## Dealers: be the form

The household will text you. You will text the mill. The mill needs REV, date, who asked, money, week. If you skip the form, you are the shrug. Your customer will remember you promised a tweak. The mill will remember a hallway. Hallways are not acks (piece 03).

## The ack lines

Freeze gate. How to request a change. That the old date dies until a new ack exists.

The tenon does not know it was a tweak. The calendar does. Write REV D while the glue is still a choice.
