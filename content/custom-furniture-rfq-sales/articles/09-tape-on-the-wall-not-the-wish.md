**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The tape hung on the baseboard hook, yellow blade across a floor that sloped toward the porch. The listing said twelve feet. The blade said eleven-seven. The listing is not coming to dinner.

Measuring for a quote is not interior design. It is a record of a room on a day, with a tool, by a person who will still answer the phone when the piece does not fit. If that sentence feels heavy, you have measured with hope before.

## The tool

A tape you trust, 25 feet or longer. Hook that actually hooks. A second tape or a folding rule for the short verticals. A laser is fine as a check, not as the only witness — lasers lie on glossy paint and across curtains. A notepad. A date. Initials.

Dealers: do not send a salesperson with a cloth sewing tape and a smile. The shop will price the smile and build the inch.

Phones will do a "room scan." Treat the scan as a sketch. Treat the tape as the number. If you submit a scan, say scan.

## The habit

Hook on a hard edge. Pull tight. Read the blade, not the memory of the listing. Write feet and inches the same way every time — 11'7" is not 117, and 11-7 is not 11.7 feet. If you mix notations on one page, you will donate an inch to the saw.

Measure each wall twice. If the two numbers disagree, you have a story (piece 10) or a sloppy hook. Measure height at more than one place. Floors sag. Ceilings belly. A tall case that fits the photo will kiss a dipped joist.

Note the date and the HVAC state. A house in August is not a house in January. You are not moisture-metering the room yet (piece 36). You are admitting the room is alive.

## What to write besides the span

Baseboard height and thickness. Window stool. Door swing. The outlet you will cover if you are careless. The vent. The radiator. The return. The fan. The pendant (piece 15, piece 18).

The path is a separate walk (piece 14). Do it the same day if you can. A beautiful room measure with a dead stair is a porch delivery.

## Rounding

Do not round to the pretty number. Do not "call it 12." If you need a clearance, subtract after you write the true wall. NKBA and Architectural Graphic Standards publish circulation numbers — use them as *targets*, then see what the room will give. A dining clearance of 36 inches behind a chair is a common minimum in those books; 42 to 48 is the comfortable pass. `[VERIFY]` the edition you are citing before you print a number as NKBA gospel. The room may only have 34. Then the table gets smaller, or the chair count drops. The wall does not grow because you wanted eight people.

Dealers: rounding up is how you sell a table that blocks a door. Rounding down is how you sell a table that looks cheap in a big room. Write the inch. Decide later.

## Who holds the tape

If the shop holds it, the shop owns the number (piece 17). If you hold it, you own it. Shared holding — "my nephew is pretty good" — is still you.

A site measure is a service. If it is billed, say so. If it is in the quote, say so. "Free quote" that includes a two-hour drive is not free; it is in the overhead. Honesty here prevents the feeling that the shop is stalling.

## The page you leave behind

A sketch with inches, a date, a name, and a photo of the tape on the long wall. That page goes in the RFQ. It is not pretty. It is enough.

If you lose the page and keep the memory, you do not have a measure. You have a wish with a ghost of a number.

## South rooms, old rooms

Wilmar and Warren houses, and the farmhouses that sit on the same dirt, are not drywall boxes. Plaster waves. Floors drop a half inch to the hearth. A shop that works this county already expects that. A dealer sending a special into a 1920 bungalow should expect it too. The tape is how you stop expecting and start writing.

## A measure you can hand to a stranger

Sketch the room as a box. Write each wall on the line, feet and inches, twice if you are kind. Mark north. Mark the door swing with an arc. Mark the outlet as a circle and a height. Put the date and your name in the corner. Photograph the sketch on the floor next to the tape on the long wall.

That packet survives a lost phone. A "notes" app with 11.7 and 11-8 in the same list does not.

If you use a laser, still write one hooked-tape number per long wall. When the two disagree by more than a quarter inch, you have either a sloppy hook or a wall that is not a wall (piece 10). Do not average them in your head. Measure a third time.

Piece 10 is the out-of-square room. Piece 11 is the table. This piece is only the habit: hook, write, date. The wish can come to dinner after the inch is on the page.
