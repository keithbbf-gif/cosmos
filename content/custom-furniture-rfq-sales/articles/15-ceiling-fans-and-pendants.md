**Disclaimer.** Educational shop talk. Not a quote, not a contract, and not legal advice. Deposit terms, lead times, and cancellation rules live on the acknowledgment you actually sign. A shop in another state may run a different clock.

The fan blade ticked the dried flowers on the second dinner. Not a ghost story. A thick top, a vase someone loved, and a fan that had been there since the kitchen remodel. Nobody put the blade in the RFQ. The shop built the table it was asked to build.

Horizontal inches get all the poetry. Vertical inches cancel jobs. Fans, pendants, soffits, crown, low beams in a farmhouse — write the air.

## Pendant to table

Designers often talk about 30 to 36 inches from the tabletop to the bottom of a pendant over a dining table. That is a lighting conversation, and editions differ, and a low industrial cage is not a glass globe. `[CITE NEEDED]` if you publish a house target. The RFQ still needs two numbers: table height (top, not floor to apron) and the lowest point of the fixture as it hangs *today*.

If the fixture is not bought, say so. A table built to a phantom pendant will lose when the electrician shortens the stem — or does not.

A two-inch thicker top steals two inches of air. People order thick tops from photos and forget the fan they live with.

## Fans

Measure from floor to the lowest blade. Subtract table height. Subtract the centerpiece you actually use, not the one in the photo. If the remainder is a few inches, you have a tick or a wind problem. Winter and summer matter; some fans drop on a rod.

A case piece under a fan is less dramatic unless the case is a tall breakfront. Still measure. Blades wander.

## Soffits and beams

Stair soffits are a path problem (piece 14). Room beams are a placement problem. A table that must sit under a beam needs to be shorter or off-center. A bed with a tall post needs the same honesty.

Photograph the beam with a tape from the floor (piece 07). Beams look higher in wide-lens photos.

## Crown and "to the ceiling"

Bookcases that die at the ceiling need the lowest point of the ceiling and the crown profile. A level case under an unlevel joist shows a wedge (piece 10). Scribe the top or stop short and write the gap.

## Lights over islands

Islands have their own aisle math (piece 13) and their own pendants. A bar-height island under a fixture hung for a 36-inch counter is a forehead. Write both heights.

## Dealers

Showrooms have 12-foot ceilings and no fans. Customers have 7'10" and a Hunter. Do not sell a 40-inch-tall hutch from the showroom photo without a ceiling number. The RFQ is the ceiling number.

## How to measure the air without a sermon

Stand a tape from the floor to the lowest blade or to the bottom of the pendant. Write that number. Write table height from the same floor, same shoes. Subtract. Subtract the vase you actually use — measure the vase. If the remainder is under a hand-span, you are in tick territory or you need a shorter fixture, a thinner top, or no flowers.

If the fixture is on a chain, measure it as it hangs today and as it would hang two links shorter. Electricians will shorten a stem more willingly before a dinner than after a table exists. Put "fixture to be shortened by others" on the RFQ if that is the plan. Do not assume the shop is the electrician.

Beds with posts, breakfronts, and canopy thoughts get the same subtraction against the fan and the soffit. A post that clears the showroom will not clear a 7'10" farmhouse with a dropped beam. Write the beam.

## Thick tops steal air on purpose

People order 8/4 and 10/4 from photos and keep the fixture they have. Each extra quarter-inch of top is air you do not have. Write the thickness next to the floor-to-fixture number so the household can see the subtraction. If they still want the thick top, they are choosing a shorter stem or a shorter vase. Put the choice on the drawing. Do not discover it when the flowers tick.

## The RFQ lines

Floor to fixture / blade / soffit. Table or case height. Top thickness. Centerpiece or none. Who is moving the fixture, if anyone. Date measured.

The chair conversation is real (piece 11). The air above the table is real too. Quote both, or the flowers will tell you.
