I'm not going to start with a table.

If I start with a table, you will look at the wood, and that is a good way to spend an afternoon and a bad way to understand a century of retail. The wood is the easy part. The hard part is the door the wood walked through to meet a person who would pay for it.

A handmade piece of furniture has always had the same problem. It is large. It is heavy. It is slow. It does not fit in a tote bag. It does not survive a joke return policy. And it costs more than the room thinks it should cost until someone sits at it, or opens the drawer, or runs a hand down a finished edge and goes quiet for a second. That quiet is the whole business. Every selling system we are going to talk about is just a different machine for manufacturing that quiet — or for skipping it.

For a long time the machine was a gallery. Then it was a craft fair. Then it was a designer showroom that would not sell to you unless you came with a designer. Then it was a market building in a North Carolina town that turns into a city twice a year. Then it was a photograph on a website that called itself handmade. Then it was a photograph on a website that did not even bother to call itself handmade — it just called itself a deal.

Those are not the same doors.

The BBF channel is going to walk them in order. Galleries. The Etsy years. Trade showrooms. The wholesale past that ran through Overstock and Wayfair and a thousand shops that said yes because the purchase order looked like oxygen. I have walked showroom floors and trade markets for years. I watched the platform pipes from this bench in southern Indiana. I will tell you which is which.

This is educational on purpose. I am not here to make you hate a platform. Platforms are pipes. Pipes do what they are designed to do. A dropship catalog is designed to show you seven million things and ship you one of them from someone else's warehouse. A craft gallery is designed to put twelve objects in a white room and ask you to take one of them home like a decision. A trade showroom is designed to let an interior designer spend your money with a professional discount and a sample program. If you do not know which room you are standing in, you will use the rules of one room inside another, and you will get hurt. Or you will hurt a shop that cannot absorb your return.

I opened a custom shop in 1999. That year is not magic. It just happens to sit on a hinge. The American craft-fair system was already mature. The designer-showroom system was already mature. Overstock was renaming itself and learning how to sell other people's inventory without owning it. Etsy did not exist. Wayfair was still three years from a website about speaker stands. If you want to understand why a dining table on a phone looks cheap and a dining table in a showroom does not, you have to stand on that hinge and look both ways.

I will use ordinary words for money, because the fancy words are how the rooms hide. Consignment means the shop did not buy the piece; they took a cut when it sold. Wholesale means a buyer took title at a discount and accepted the risk. To-the-trade means the public was not the customer; the designer was. Dropship means the website never touched the object; we did, and they kept the relationship. Those four sentences will do more for you than a year of furniture ads.

If you make furniture, this series is a map of doors you can still use and doors that will waste a year of your life. If you buy furniture, it is a map of why the same oak can be a commission, a lifestyle brand, or a carton with a one-star review about a chipped corner. If you just like history, it is a retail history that never got a clean textbook because the people who lived it were busy sanding.

We will go slow. We will name dates when the dates are public. We will not invent a contract this shop did not sign. We will not pretend a website invented craft. And we will not end with a coupon.

The next time you see the word handmade on a listing, I want you to ask a ruder question than "is it pretty?" I want you to ask: which door is this, and who is standing in it with me?
