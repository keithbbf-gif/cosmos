I need to take four words to the bench and mill them flat. If we do not, the history will sound like taste, and it is not taste. It is vocabulary doing cover work.

Handmade.

In a shop, handmade is a labor claim. Hands held the work. Machines may have helped. A tablesaw is not a moral failure. A CNC is not automatically a lie. The question is whether a person was responsible for the object as a made thing — layout, joinery, finish, the decision when a board is too wild to use and when it is the whole point. In a gallery in 1985, handmade meant that. On Etsy in 2005, it still mostly meant that, and the site was built around the promise. In 2013 the company said out loud that a seller could hire and could apply to use a manufacturer if they remained the author of the design. That is a real policy with a real date. After that, handmade on a website might mean "a person had an idea." That is a different job. Both jobs can be honest. They are not the same word.

Custom.

Custom used to mean the object did not exist until you and the shop agreed. Size, wood, finish, the weird corner of the room, the leaf that has to hide, the fact that one diner is six-foot-six and the table cannot look like a boardroom. We still do that work. A lot of websites now say custom when they mean you picked walnut from a dropdown. Configuration is not custom. Configuration is a catalog with manners. I do not hate a dropdown. I hate the theft. When a buyer thinks they bought a commission and they bought a SKU, the disappointment lands on the first shop that later tells them the truth.

Wholesale.

Wholesale is a contract. Title moves. Risk moves. Price moves down because the buyer is not a civilian. After the platforms, wholesale started meaning "we have a portal and a spreadsheet." A shop would get an email that said wholesale partner, and what it meant was: load your catalog, accept our retail price, accept our return window, ship under our name. That is not wholesale. That is door four in a door-two hat. Real wholesale still exists. A store that writes a deposit and takes the floor risk is doing the old job. A website that never sees the carton is doing a new job. Call them by different names or you will sign the wrong PDF.

Artisan.

This one I almost do not want to use, because it has been perfumed to death. Artisan used to sit next to a trade. A person who had hours in the material. Then it sat on a bread bag. Then it sat on a sofa that came out of a container in a color called artisan taupe. I will use maker, shop, bench, craftsman, because those still point at a building. If I say artisan, I mean a person whose hands would be recognized by another person in the same trade. I do not mean a mood.

There are smaller thefts.

Bespoke — a tailor's word, now a filter on a dining table.
Limited edition — sometimes true; sometimes a leftover production run with better lighting.
American made — sometimes the shop; sometimes the final screw; sometimes the brand office in Ohio and the work overseas. I will do a whole draft on that word later, because it changed between 1999 and now.
Designer — a licensed or practicing interior designer with a trade account, or a person who picked a SKU. Only one of those can open a showroom order.
Vintage — on Etsy, a policy bucket with an age test. In a mall, a vibe.

I am not the word police. I am a shop that has to live with what the words do to the incoming phone call. When a person says they want something handmade and custom and wholesale-priced, they have been trained by three different rooms and they think they are asking for one object. My job on that call is not to win. It is to slow the language down until we are talking about a table.

This channel will keep putting the old jobs back on the words. You will hear me repeat myself. That is not because I ran out of stories. It is because the ads will be on the next video, and the ads do not care.

If you write product copy, try a week of saying only what you can defend to a finisher. If you cannot tell a finisher why the word is on the page, take it off. The finishers are harder to fool than the algorithm, and they remember.
