I am not going to tell a morality play where the shop refuses the internet and the heavens open.

Walking away from a portal is a budget.

You lose orders you had gotten used to.
You lose the feeling of being busy, which some owners confuse with being alive.
You may lose a finish that only existed because the portal asked for it.
You gain hours.
You gain the right to say no to a stair.
You gain a name that is not a ghost.
You gain a designer who is not competing with a screenshot.

Whether the budget works depends on the other doors. If you have showrooms that can eat the hours, walk. If you have a commission practice that can eat the hours, walk. If you have nothing else, you are not a coward for staying. You are a shop in a pipe, and you should run the pipe like a job, not like a brand story.

I can say, for us, that the showroom door and the commission door are the living. The platform pipe was never going to be our personality. That is a privilege of a certain kind of object and a certain kind of map. A stool maker in a town without designers may not have our privilege. I will not lecture them from a loft above a loading dock.

What I will lecture, gently:

Do not stay in a pipe because of fear you have not written down. Write the number. How many pieces. How much margin after the hospital wing. What percent of the year. If the number is a no and you stay, you are now making a different decision — a decision about addiction to busy.

Do not leave a pipe in a tantrum. Finish the orders. Do not punish a buyer who already paid. Offboard like an adult. Then take the photographs you still own and put them in a thicker document.

Do not confuse walking away with becoming invisible. You still need a door. A website of your own that ends in a phone is a door. A showroom is a door. A fair is a door. Silence is not a strategy.

The industry will keep offering you the pipe with a new name. The name will sound like a partnership. Use the four doors. If they keep the customer, it is the same job.

For the channel, this walk-away is not a brand film. It is the reason we can teach the history without a checkout button at the end of the lecture. We are not above commerce. We sell tables. We are trying to sell them through a document that can hold a dining table.

If you are a buyer who only has the pipe, I am not asking you to pretend you have a designer. I am asking you to use the pipe with gallery questions. Who held the work. Who owns the customer. What happens if it is wrong. Who is coming to the house. Those questions make a hunter a little more like a student.

If you are a designer, help your clients walk away from the screenshot when the screenshot is a different object. That is your labor. It is worth the fee.

We close the platform stage with the job description still ringing. Door four is a job. Door three is a practice. Door two is a bet. Door one is a teaching room.

The last stage is shorter. It is how to live after you know this, including how to read four prices on the same table, and what "American made" meant when we opened versus what it means in a caption.

I will not tell you the platforms lost. They did not. They taught the country a way of buying a house in pieces. I will tell you the way has a cost, and the cost landed on shops that believed a portal was a store.

That is enough ugly. Next we read prices like grown-ups.
