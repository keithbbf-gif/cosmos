Etsy's clean public sentence, the one in the filings, was that goods should be handmade, vintage, or craft supplies.

Three buckets. One search bar. That is a hard architecture.

Handmade we have already taken to the bench. Labor or authorship, 2005 or 2013.

Vintage is a policy bucket with an age test. The number has been in the neighborhood of twenty years, and you should check the live rule before you swear to it on camera. The educational point is not the number. The point is that vintage is a different moral claim. Nobody made this last month. Somebody kept it, found it, repaired it, or at least recognized it. A vintage furniture seller can be a scholar or a picker or a person with a truck. They are not a shop like ours. They can be as honest as we are. They should not wear our apron unless they also made the thing.

Craft supplies are the back door of making. Wood, hardware, fabric, the stuff that becomes the other two buckets. A supplies listing is honest when it is selling a material. It becomes a fog when the material is staged like a finished life.

Furniture sits in all three buckets like a cat that does not care about your taxonomy.

A new bench-made table: handmade.
A 1985 dining table from a good factory: vintage, if the age test is met.
A slab, a set of legs, a finish kit: supplies.

The buyer types "dining table" and the storefront, which promised a kind of room, returns a flea market, a factory, and a shop. The search does not teach. The search dumps.

This is not unique to Etsy. Every general marketplace has the dump problem. Etsy's dump is more painful because the company's original gift was a room that felt taught. Three buckets were how they tried to stay not-eBay. The buckets leaked because furniture is a category that predates the buckets.

I have a soft spot for an honest vintage dealer. They keep objects in the world. They know a joint. They will tell you what is a repair and what is a story. The good ones are teachers. They are door one with a different inventory. I have a harder time with a listing that uses vintage lighting and a plant to sell a new object as if it had already lived a life. That is a photograph doing costume work.

Supplies listings taught a generation of hobbyists to make. That is a public good. They also taught a generation that making is a kit. A kit is a start. A kit is not a shop.

If you browse a handmade marketplace for furniture, filter on purpose. Decide your bucket before you fall in love with a picture. If you want labor, read the about page for a building and a process. If you want vintage, ask about repairs. If you want a project, admit you are buying a project.

If you sell, pick a bucket and stay in it. Crossing buckets in one shop is how a buyer decides you are a vibe.

The three-bucket architecture was a serious attempt to keep a word from meaning everything. 2013 stretched the first bucket. The other two stayed as alibis: we are still not a general store, see, we have rules. Rules that a search bar can ignore are decorations.
