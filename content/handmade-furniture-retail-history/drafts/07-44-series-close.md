I said at the beginning I would not start with a table. I will end with a house.

A house, in the way I mean it, is a shop that is still working while the weather changes. Galleries thinned in some towns and thickened in others. Showrooms still stand. Markets still open in April. Etsy still uses a word it had to redefine. Overstock and Wayfair still know how to be a storefront on other people's docks, even as their own mixes change. A shop that evacuates itself every time the weather shifts is not a shop. It is a mood.

This series was a map of doors so we do not evacuate.

Door one: a room that teaches, takes a cut, leaves title with you.
Door two: a buyer who takes title and risk.
Door three: a professional, a sample, a net sheet, a truck.
Door four: a website that keeps the stranger and hands you a ticket.

Galleries. Fairs. America House. The rude table. The art fork.
Design centers. Cold calls. Paper. Territory.
High Point. Tear sheets.
eBay. The camera. Freight.
2005. Jewelry weather. 2013. Ads. A wholesale dream.
Closeouts. A forest of nouns. Dropship as a job. The flood. The name on the box. The hospital wing. The walk-away.
Four prices. A building, not a caption.

If you make, use the map to stop sending a dining table into a pipe built for earrings. Price the door you are actually in. Keep a door that can hold a no.

If you buy, use the map to stop calling a showroom a snub and a website a shop. Ask who held the work and who owns the customer. Sit when you can. Stay until you are bored.

If you design, use the map to explain your fee without theater. You are a teacher and a shock absorber. That is a living.

If you just wanted history, you now have a retail history that usually gets told as a string of brand names. Brand names are weather. Doors are climate.

The BBF channel will show work. Of course it will. A finish, a leaf, a board that was supposed to be a defect. That work is not a catalog. It is evidence that a building still exists. The history is so the evidence has a sentence around it.

I live above the shop. I still like mornings before the crew arrives. I still think you should touch a table. I still think a photograph is a map. I still think a cart is a thin document. None of that requires you to buy our work. It requires you to keep the words honest in your own house.

I will change a date if a date is wrong. I will not change the doors to make a platform feel kind. Kindness is a teacher in a room. It is not a return policy.

When you see the next pretty listing, I want the rude question to arrive on time.

Which door is this?
Who is standing in it with me?
What living am I paying for besides the walnut?

If you can answer those, the history did its job. The house can stay open. The trucks can leave the front. The wood can keep coming in the back.

That is the series. Thank you for sitting through a lecture that tried not to sound like one. If you want the short version, it will not get shorter than the four doors. Use them. They still fit.
