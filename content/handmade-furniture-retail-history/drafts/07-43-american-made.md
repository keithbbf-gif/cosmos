When we opened in 1999, "American made" was not a caption. It was a map.

There was a building. There were people on a clock. There was a dock. The wood might have come from farther than the building — wood has always traveled — but the decisions that made it furniture happened in a place you could drive to. Southern Indiana has been that kind of place for a long time. So has a lot of North Carolina. So has Grand Rapids in its chapters. The map had holes. The map was still a map.

Then the work moved, in the larger industry, in the way everyone already knows and still pretends not to know. Containers. Specs. A brand office in one state and a factory in another country. A final bolt put on in a domestic warehouse so a label could breathe easier. I am not your customs lawyer. I am a shop. I can tell you what the phrase feels like when it has been through a caption machine.

It feels like artisan taupe.

In 2011, when a group put an award on us in Washington and talked about Made in America, I said the thing I still mean: I want to promote craft in this country. Our people like the traditions. We like putting furniture into the hands of designers. That is a payroll sentence. It is not a filter.

A caption can say American designed. American brand. Assembled in. Made with American steel. Each of those can be true. Each is a different map. The platform years loved the caption because the buyer loved the feeling and the SKU flood needed a story that was not "network of docks."

I use woods that are not all from the backyard. I have also used woods that were. The backyard sentence is pretty. The honest sentence is: we choose material for the work, we make the work here, we finish it here, we know the people. If a board traveled, I can say so. If a person here held the work, I can say so. That is the 1999 map, still.

What I will not do is wear the flag as a way to win a filter against a shop in another country that may be doing honest labor I have never seen. Nationalism is a bad finish. Labor is a good one. I am for buildings. I am for names on clocks. I am for a young person in Ferdinand being able to learn a trade that is not only a portal.

If you buy, ask:

Where is the building?
Who is on the clock?
What traveled?
Who do I call in year eight?

If the answers are a P.O. box and a story, you bought a caption.

If you make elsewhere and sell here, say so. There is a living in that. There is not a living in the costume, not a living I respect.

If you make here, do not assume the caption will save you. The caption is crowded. The building, the truck, the designer who has sat at the sample — those still work. They worked before the caption. They will work when the caption gets tired.

1999 and 2005 and 2011 and 2013 are just years on a hinge. The hinge is still moving. The phrase will keep being stolen. Put it back on a building when you say it on this channel.
