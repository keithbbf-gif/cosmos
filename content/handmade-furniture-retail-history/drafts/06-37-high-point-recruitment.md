Picture a family casegoods line that has been going to High Point since before the people in the booth were born.

They know how to talk to a store. They know a cutoff date. They know a finish list. They have a binder. They have a dock. They are door two in their sleep.

Now picture a person from a website, badge from a city that is not High Point, saying: we can be your biggest online retailer. Give us the catalog. Give us the photographs if you have them. We will handle the rest.

That conversation, multiplied by a thousand aisles, is how you get seven million products without seven million rooms.

The S-1 said it in polite language: small suppliers without easy retail access. The Guy Raz interview said it in story language: suppliers told them they had become the biggest online seller of the noun. Both are the same event. The market's original animal — the store buyer — was thinning in a lot of towns. The website buyer was thickening. A shop that needed orders followed the thickening.

I do not blame the family line. I have followed weather too. I followed designers to design centers because that was where the work was. They followed portals because that was where the orders were. The educational grief is what got lost in the translation.

A store buyer edits. They cannot take the whole binder. They have a floor. A floor is a spine. They choose.

A website buyer, in the extreme version, takes the binder. Completeness is the product. Completeness is also how a finish that should have died in committee lives forever as a SKU.

The family line learns to make what the portal can sell. The portal learns to sell what the family line can ship. Neither of them is in a room with a taught civilian. The civilian is home, hunting.

High Point did not become evil. It became bilingual. You can still write a thick order with a store. You can also leave with a portal login. The aisle does not label which language you just spoke. You have to label it yourself.

There is a particular sadness when a line that used to be proud of its dealer network becomes proud of its ranking on a site. Dealer networks were imperfect. They were also people. A ranking is not a person.

If you walk market now, listen for the verbs.

Write you up — old language, maybe door two.
Onboard you — new language, probably door four.
Feature you — could be either, ask who owns the customer.

If you are a shop that still only speaks store, you may feel invisible. You are not obsolete. You are picky. Picky is a living if you have doors that match.

If you are a shop that only speaks portal, you may feel busy. Busy is not the same as a practice. Ask what would happen if the portal changed its fee tomorrow. That question is your business plan.

Our kind of work — custom, showroom, trucks — was never the easiest recruit. We do not have a binder of a thousand SKUs. We have a family of forms and a yes that takes time. The websites did not need us to fill the house. They needed the family lines. Understanding that keeps me from making this a personal grievance. It is a structural history.

The next draft is about what completeness did to the idea of an edited floor — the SKU flood, and why more choice made looking harder.
