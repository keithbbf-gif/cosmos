June 2005. Brooklyn. Rob Kalin and a small group put a site on the web that said, in effect: we make things, and there is nowhere that feels like the right place.

That sentence could have been written by America House. The difference is the rent. Etsy socialized the rent across a million booths and charged a listing fee and a cut. Twenty cents and a small percentage, in the early telling. Cheap compared to a wall on 54th Street. Cheap compared to Rhinebeck. Cheap in dollars. Expensive, later, in the meaning of the word they built the company on.

I want to give the early site its due, because the later fights make people pretend it was always a mall.

It was a room that believed handmade was a category, not a flavor. That belief pulled people out of eBay's sport and Yahoo's loneliness. A maker could be in a context. Context is what a gallery sells. Etsy sold a version of context at network scale.

It taught a generation to photograph their work, write a story, pack a box, and talk to a stranger. Those are real skills. Some of those people now run shops that do not need Etsy. The site was a school whether it meant to be or not.

It made "I made this" a public identity again. For a while you could walk a digital aisle and feel the ACC weekend in it — the two buyers, even. A civilian with a cart. A small store looking for wholesale. The community language was thick. Too thick, sometimes. But thickness is not the enemy.

What it was not:

It was not a furniture system. A dining table is still rude in a booth built for earrings. The fees were designed around objects that ship as parcels. The culture was designed around objects you can make in an apartment. A shop with a loading dock was an awkward guest, the same way we were an awkward guest in a lot of galleries.

It was not a replacement for teaching. The buyer was still hunting. The jury was still a search bar.

It was not, in 2005, a factory door. That fight comes in 2013.

Our shop did not become an Etsy shop. I will not fake a shop-announcement memoir. I watched the word handmade get a new capital city. I felt the weather. Clients started using the word the way Etsy taught them: a story paragraph, a maker face, a promise of uniqueness. Sometimes that helped me. A person who believes in makers is easier to talk to. Sometimes it hurt me. A person who believes handmade means apartment-scale prices will not understand a dining table that took a team.

If you sold on early Etsy and it fed you, you are part of a real chapter of American craft. Do not let later bitterness steal that from you. If you sold on early Etsy and it trained you to race other makers on a heart icon, you are also part of the chapter. Both can sit in the same booth.

The educational point of 2005 is simple. A platform can start as door one — a room that presents makers to taught-enough buyers — and still be a company that will need more volume than door one can honestly provide. Volume is the snake in this garden. Remember the snake. We will watch it eat the word.
