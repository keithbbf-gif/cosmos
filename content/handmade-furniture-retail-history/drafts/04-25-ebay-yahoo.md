The internet did not arrive as Wayfair. It arrived as a messy row of doors that looked like classifieds.

eBay taught America that a stranger would send money for an object they had not held. That is a civilizational change. It is also a change that works better for a coin than for a sideboard. The auction format trained price as sport. Sport is a bad mood for a dining table. You do not want the person who "won" you. You want the person who understood you.

Yahoo and other early storefronts taught a simpler lesson: you could have a URL that was yours, a cart that was yours, a look that was almost a shop. Makers who were tired of a gallery split heard freedom. Sometimes it was. Sometimes it was a booth fee in a new costume — hosting, a payment cut, the hours of building a page that five people would see.

Personal websites were the proud version. A shop photographed the work, wrote a paragraph, listed a phone number. The phone number was the grown part. The page was a tear sheet that could be found at 2 a.m. by a designer who could not sleep. I respect that. I still respect a page that ends in a human.

What those early doors did not do:

They did not teach looking. They borrowed looking from the photograph.
They did not solve freight. They hid freight or they shocked people at checkout.
They did not replace a jury with something wiser. They replaced a jury with whoever could type.
They did not create a taught buyer. They created a hunting buyer.

There was a kindness. A maker in a town without a gallery could be seen. America House existed because rural work could not reach a city. The early web said the city could come to the driveway. For jewelry, for prints, for a certain chair that ships in a box, that sentence was true enough to live on.

For our kind of table, the early web was a brochure. The sale still wanted a call. I am not ashamed of that. A brochure that pretends to be a closer is how you get a person who thinks a dining table is returnable because the site had a button.

eBay also trained a generation of sellers to be anonymous. An anonymous furniture seller is a warning light. Furniture has a long life. You want to know who to call in year eight when a leaf misbehaves. A username is not a loading dock.

I did not build this company as an eBay shop. I will not fake a memoir of auctions. I watched the doors open. I watched some good makers learn photography and payments, which they needed. I watched some good makers learn to race to the bottom, which they did not.

If you are young and you think Etsy invented handmade online, give the earlier row its due. Etsy is a chapter. It is not Genesis. Genesis is "I put a picture on a network and a stranger sent a check." Everything after is an argument about who owns that stranger.

If you still sell on a general marketplace, use it as a door, not as a shop. Put your real name. Put your real terms. Refuse the sport if the object cannot survive sport.
