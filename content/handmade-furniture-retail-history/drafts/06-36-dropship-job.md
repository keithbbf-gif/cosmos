I am going to describe dropship as if I were hiring you for it, because that is the only way the word gets its job back.

You will keep the object until we tell you to move it.
You will photograph it to our taste, or we will photograph it and you will live with the lie.
You will accept a retail price we set, or a range we set, because we own the front of the house.
You will ship to a stranger in a window we advertised.
You will not call the stranger unless something is wrong, and sometimes not even then.
You will accept a return policy written for our brand promise.
You will be scored.
If the score slips, we will starve you of orders before we starve ourselves of listings.

We will take the payment.
We will take the email list.
We will take the review.
We will take the right to replace you with a cousin listing.

That is the job.

It can be a rational job. A factory that already runs SKUs, already has a dock, already lives on repeatable objects, can price that job and live. A shop that lives on commissions, names, and a few hundred pieces a year cannot price that job like a wholesale order and survive, because the job includes being a customer-service department for a person you are not allowed to know.

The confusion was linguistic. They said partner. They said vendor. They said wholesale. Your uncle who ran a store in 1985 heard those words and signed. Your uncle's store had taken title. This website did not.

Title, in dropship, is a lawyer's sport. Sometimes the platform never owns the piece. Sometimes they own it for a second. Sometimes a consumer-protection rule cares. You should care more about the relationship than the metaphysical second. If you cannot call the buyer, you do not have a buyer. You have a ticket.

Risk sits in strange places.

Damage in transit: often you, or you versus the carrier, while the buyer yells at the site.
Wrong size: often you, because the site allowed a civilian to order a custom-ish width with a parcel brain.
A photograph that implied a wood the shop does not use: you, because the buyer believed the site.
A late truck: you, in the score, even when the site promised a date you never saw.

I own trucks and still do not want that job at those terms. I want the person. I want the acknowledgement. I want to say no to a stair.

If you are going to take the job, take it like a job.

Price the service, not only the object.
Cap the percentage of the shop that lives on one portal.
Keep a finish that the portal does not get, so your name still has a home.
Read the offboarding paragraph. They can erase you. Can you erase them?
Do not list a one-off as a SKU.

If you are a buyer, dropship is why a website can show you a country of sofas without owning a sofa. It is clever. It is also why the person who answers the chat may not be able to tell you who finished the arm. Ask. If they cannot answer, you are buying a network.

If you are a designer, a dropship listing is usually not a specification. It is a gamble you are taking with someone else's house. Some of you have to gamble because the budget is the budget. Say that out loud. Do not call it custom.

I have no interest in abolishing dropship. I have an interest in stopping the costume. Door four may wear door one's words and door two's handshake. Take the costume off before you sign, and before you click.
