# Style guide — WOW Therapies, counseling skills & modalities history

This pack is educational history for [WOWTherapies.com](https://wowtherapies.com): how counseling **skills** were named and taught, and how **modalities** packaged those skills. It is **not** the founder-biography therapy-history series and **not** the SLPWOW speech-pathology series.

`voice_check: human` in front matter means the draft was written against this file. It is not a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Who is speaking

A historically literate magazine writer who has sat in enough rooms to know that a skill is not a personality and a modality is not a menu. Warm without being pastoral. Precise without being a textbook. The reader may be a curious client, a family member, a student, or a clinician. Do not talk down. Do not talk shop as if the room were empty of patients.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas speech-language practice. These essays are **heritage education**, not a claim that the clinic practices every school named here, and not a counseling-licensure page.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" as a metaphor for a field
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- three-beat synonym piles ("heal, grow, and thrive")
- "the father of…" unless a contemporary actually used that phrase and you are quoting the myth, not endorsing it
- "Freud discovered the unconscious"
- "gold-standard treatment" as marketing
- invented quotes
- AI-generated faces or "period photographs" that never existed
- session scripts, worksheets, "try this at home," or skill steps a reader could run as self-treatment

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "empower," "in this article we will explore," "journey of healing" as filler.

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a room, a document, a scale, or a named quarrel. The first sentence should be checkable.
2. Mix sentence length. A short sentence after a long one. Do not write every paragraph as topic sentence + three examples + wrap.
3. Name the book, paper, hospital, society, or coding manual. *Microcounseling* (1971). The 1957 Rogers paper. Truax's Accurate Empathy Scale (1961). Miller's 1983 drinkers paper. ASHA's 2016 Scope of Practice.
4. Mark uncertainty. `[CITE NEEDED]` or a plain "historians disagree" beats a clean falsehood.
5. Put famous names in their century. Do not offer them as current treatment manuals. If a figure already has a full essay in the therapy-history pack, point across the hallway; do not reprint the life.
6. Give SLP counseling, rehabilitation counseling, school counseling, and non-European work full essays, not a closing paragraph titled "also."
7. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target 1,200–2,000 words for skill and modality essays, 1,000–1,800 for figures. Cut the recap paragraph if it only restates the headings. `check_pack.py` fails a skill or modality under 1,200 words or a figure under 1,000. Deepen with dated scenes, not with recap.

## Claims posture

See `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`. History may describe a skill. History may not teach a reader to self-administer it. No session scripts, no OARS worksheets, no "try this reflection at home."

Efficacy sentences, if any, stay historical and sourced ("Miller's 1983 paper reported…") and never become "MI will help your drinking."

Living people get extra care: no gossip, no unsourced health claims. Public books, public lectures, and public interviews only.

## Portraits

Public domain or Creative Commons only. Document the file in `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. If there is no clean license, use type or a period object — never a generated face. See `PHOTO_NOTES.md`.

## Series voice, in one paragraph

Write as if *The New Yorker* and *Psychotherapy Networker* had to share a desk, and the desk was in a small-city speech clinic that still answers its own phone. A skill can be taught. A life cannot be reduced to a skill. The past is allowed to be strange. The present is not allowed to be sold.
