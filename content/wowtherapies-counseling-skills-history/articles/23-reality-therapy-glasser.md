**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1965 Harper & Row issued William Glasser's *Reality Therapy: A New Approach to Psychiatry*. The subtitle was a dare. A psychiatrist was offering counselors, teachers, and the general reader a present-tense method that downplayed diagnosis, played up responsibility, and treated the past as a poor workplace. The book sold. HarperCollins later put the figure near a million and a half copies. Sales are not a validation study. They are how a modality left the ward and entered the American school.

This essay is not a school-counseling manual. Essay 33 in this pack follows school counseling as a skill lineage of its own. Glasser's package is one weather system that blew through that lineage. It is also a clinic argument that made enemies in psychiatry and friends in in-service days.

## Ventura, Harrington, and a present tense

Glasser began work in 1956 as a psychiatrist at the Ventura School for Girls, a California Youth Authority institution, while also tied to UCLA and the veterans' services where G. L. Harrington was a tutor. Institute timelines record Harrington's welcome into a circle that did not "believe in mental illness" as the organizing fact of the hour. The phrasing is theirs, from later retellings. The 1965 book is the checkable object: a method that asked what the person was doing now, what the person wanted, and whether the present behavior was a reasonable way to get it.

Ventura is the room. A locked school for labeled girls. A young psychiatrist watching staff who refused punishment as the first move. Later Glasser Institute lore made "Mama G" and cottage kindness into origin scenes. Those scenes are oral history. The book is 1965. Historians can hold both without turning the cottage into a protocol.

Reality therapy's skill package, as trainers later listed it, was deceptively short: involve, present, evaluate, plan. Robert Wubbolding's later WDEP teaching device (wants, doing, evaluation, planning) is a dated training object from a second generation. This series will not teach WDEP as steps. It will say that a 1965 bestseller had already made *planning in the present* sound like the whole of counseling to people who were tired of origins.

## Schools Without Failure, 1969

*Schools Without Failure* (1969) carried the clinic into the classroom. Glasser had begun work in Watts with the Los Angeles Unified School District after the 1965 book. The 1969 argument was that failure, as a school practice, produced the behaviors schools then punished. Classroom meetings, involvement, and an attack on the grading-and-failure machine were the public proposals. Teachers heard a psychiatrist telling them that relationships outranked worksheets. Some were grateful. Some heard an outsider flattening instruction.

The Institute for Reality Therapy, established in 1967, became a training apparatus. In-service days multiplied. "Quality Schools" and "lead management" were later brandings. *The Quality School* as a 1990s object belongs to that apparatus. This essay will not evaluate a district. It will say that reality therapy became, in the United States, as much a school-reform dialect as a fifty-minute method. That is why this series files Glasser under a school-counseling modality without turning the file into a school manual. It is also why critics in counselor education asked whether a reform dialect had crowded out other skills — silence, cultural humility, grief — that other essays in this series treat as separate histories.

## Control theory, then Choice Theory, 1998

Glasser spent the 1980s attaching "control theory" (a borrowing and a renaming that irritated some readers of William T. Powers) to reality therapy as its psychology of needs. Basic needs — survival, love and belonging, power, freedom, fun, in the later lists — organized the posters. In the mid-1990s, after conversations that Institute timelines place with Irish instructors who disliked the word *control*, Glasser renamed the psychology Choice Theory. *Choice Theory: A New Psychology of Personal Freedom* appeared in 1998.

The rename is a historical event. It does not make 1965 and 1998 the same book. 1965 was a psychiatrist's dare to his own guild. 1998 was a popular psychology of internal control versus "external control," written after decades of workshops. Living trainers still teach under those trademarks. They are living people. This file uses the public books. It does not gossip.

What the 1998 book did for the skill package was to put *relationship* back in neon. Glasser argued that the problems people brought were, at root, relationship problems, and that coercive control — from a spouse, a teacher, a clinician — was the rival psychology. Counselors who had used 1965 as a blunt instrument ("what are you doing about it?") had to decide whether 1998 required them to slow down. Some did. Some kept the blunt instrument and updated the poster.

## The criticisms that are part of the record

Psychiatrists who kept the DSM on the desk heard Glasser's skepticism about mental illness as a category as a denial of suffering that medication and diagnosis sometimes name. Glasser's public stand against treating unhappiness as a brain disorder needing drugs made him famous and, in later decades, easy to caricature. This series will not referee a medication question and will not offer dosing talk. The historical fact is the quarrel: a psychiatrist who wrote for the general reader against the grain of his guild.

Feminist and social-justice counselors asked a different question. "Responsibility" in a 1965 vocabulary can land as a demand that a woman, a poor student, or a girl at Ventura invent a plan that ignores the conditions that planned her. A present-tense hour that will not look at the past can also refuse to look at the street. That critique does not require a composite client. It is a reading of the books' emphasis.

School critics asked whether "no failure" was a slogan that hid tracking, racism, and underfunding. Glasser was not the only 1960s writer to walk into that fight. He was one of the loudest with a counseling trademark.

Researchers asked for trials. Reality therapy produced fewer of the grant-culture objects that cognitive therapy's 1979 manual invited. Absence of a certain kind of trial is not a secret proof of failure. It is a fact about what got funded and what got workshopped. Counselor-education programs that still mention Glasser in a theories survey often do so in a week, with a chart of needs, and without Ventura. The chart without Ventura is how a modality becomes a cartoon.

## What the package actually packaged

As a skill package, reality therapy asked the helper to do several things this pack has already met under other names, then to refuse several others. Involvement is a cousin of the working alliance (Bordin 1979 sits in essay 09) and of Rogers's conditions, though Glasser did not present himself as a Rogerian. Present-tense questioning is a cousin of the questions essay, without the miracle question's overnight hypothetical. Evaluation of one's own behavior is a cousin of cognitive therapy's thought-testing, with less interest in a columnar record. The refusal of long excavation is a fight with the psychodynamic listening modality two files back.

The package's distinctive public sound was *plan*. Make a plan. If the plan fails, make another. The sound delighted guidance offices that needed a next step before the bell. It worried clinicians who had sat with grief that does not plan on a Tuesday. Luterman's CSD counseling, already in this pack, lives in that worry. A parent who has just heard that a child is deaf may not need a Glasser plan in the first hour. They may need a person who can stand the unfilled second (essay 05). Modalities that cannot admit another modality's hour become advertisements.

## Heritage, not a Glasser shop

WOW Therapies is a speech-language practice in Southeast Arkansas. It does not certify Quality Schools and it does not teach Choice Theory. School counselors in the region, like school counselors elsewhere, may have sat through a Glasser in-service in some decade. Families still hear "take responsibility" as a school sentence. Heritage education can say where that sentence got a 1965 binding.

ASHA's 2016 Scope of Practice does not mint an LPC. An SLP who asks a teenager what they are doing about a home-practice plan is not thereby a reality therapist. The question has a history. So does the risk of the question: that a communication disability will be heard as a failure of will. Glasser's useful stubbornness about hope can, in a speech room, become a way of not seeing the impairment. The 1965 book did not write that warning. A 2026 speech-site history can.

Glasser died in 2013 in Los Angeles. The Institute continues under later names. The books remain. A reader who wants a school-counseling history should wait for essay 33. A reader who wants a founder biography of mid-century psychiatry should look next door. This file keeps the dare of 1965, the 1969 school turn, the 1998 rename, and the criticisms that traveled with the trademark. No classroom meeting is assigned at the end.
