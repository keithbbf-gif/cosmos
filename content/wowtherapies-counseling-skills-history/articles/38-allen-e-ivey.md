## Colorado State, two-inch tape, a morning's units

Ivey directed the counseling center at Colorado State University in Fort Collins from 1963 through 1968. The microcounseling group there — his later site names Eugene Oetting, Weston Morrill, Max Uhlemann, Cheryl Normington, Richard Haase, and Dean Miller as original colleagues — used videotape when the decks were still a dare. The claim was modest and huge: isolate attending behavior (eye contact, body orientation, verbal following) as something a beginner could practice in a short, structured session rather than absorb as a personality.

The intellectual weather included social-learning psychology. Ivey has publicly recalled visiting Albert Bandura's early work and watching a microteaching framework associated with Dwight Allen at Stanford. Those are origin stories he has told in interviews. The 1968 paper and the 1971 book are the objects that do not depend on the interview. Microteaching in education had already atomized a teacher's performance. Microcounseling did the same to the helping interview. The tape stopped. The unit was named. The beginner tried again.

He had come through Stanford as an undergraduate and Harvard for a doctorate in counseling, with a Fulbright interval studying social work in Denmark — public CV facts. Colorado State, in his own later telling, was the high point: a counseling center that took student development seriously in the same years Division 17 was learning to care about journal placement. The microskills did not wait for the *Journal of Counseling Psychology* to bless them. They travelled by workshop, by Thomas's 1971 binding, and by the later firm Microtraining Associates.

## What a unit could and could not hear

Attending as a countable behavior let listening leave the Rogerian church without leaving the room. You could teach it to a medical student, a librarian, an agricultural extension agent. Ivey's later editions advertised those migrations. They are part of the skill's history. They are also how a clinic word became a corporate workshop. The tape did not choose its later audience.

Cross-cultural critique arrived on the skill itself. Eye contact as "good attending" is a North American classroom rule. Ivey's later work tried to write that rule down as a *choice*, not a law. The first papers were less careful. A history that loves the 1968 film should also say what the film assumed about faces. He has publicly dated a first anti-racism workshop to 1967, the same year as the microcounseling origin story. The two dates sitting together are the interesting fact. A training technology can travel faster than its ethics. Later editions had to chase the technology.

Norman Kagan's Interpersonal Process Recall, already named in the listening essay, used video for a different confession: stop the tape and ask what was thought and felt. Ivey's machine taught units. Kagan's taught recall. Historians who treat Ivey as the only video story miss a parallel. Both assumed a trainee could bear to watch. Not every trainee could.

## Mary Bradford Ivey, and a textbook that outlived the two-inch deck

Mary Bradford Ivey, a school counselor by professional identity in the public record, became central to extending the model; Ivey's site dates that partnership in the work to 1981. She is a co-author, not a footnote, and not a private-life anecdote. Together with Carlos P. Zalaquett they kept *Intentional Interviewing and Counseling* through Brooks/Cole and then Cengage, edition after edition. Cite the copy on the desk. Later printings folded in neuroscience citations Ivey was proud to have introduced to ACA audiences. Neuroscience in a skills book is a historical turn, not a license to print brain diagrams as treatment.

He became Distinguished University Professor emeritus at the University of Massachusetts Amherst. He is a diplomat in counseling psychology, a past president and fellow of the Society of Counseling Psychology, a fellow of the American Counseling Association. He has described being named a multicultural elder at the National Multicultural Conference and Summit as the honor he liked. Those are public professional facts. Developmental Counseling and Therapy (DCT), his later integrative theory, is a separate published system. Name it as a later ambition. Do not teach it here. The microskills essay in this pack is the place for the units. This page is the teacher who would not let interviewing stay "diffuse," his word in later prefaces, when a camera could make it particular.

## A speech clinic and a borrowed camera

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas speech-language practice. Its students and new hires still meet "attending" as a word, often without Ivey's name on the slide. The inheritance is the idea that a helping behavior can be filmed and named. The danger is the same one Ivey spent later editions qualifying: a North American attending rule offered as a human universal, a unit offered as the whole of care. ASHA's 2016 Scope of Practice names counseling in CSD. It does not name Colorado State 1967. The reel still reached the allied-health syllabus. Once listening was a technique, every later clinic had to decide whether to teach it, ignore it, or call it manners.

The therapy-history pack can keep Rogers the man. This pack keeps Ivey as a teaching problem: how small a piece of an interview you can name before you have named something that is no longer the interview.
