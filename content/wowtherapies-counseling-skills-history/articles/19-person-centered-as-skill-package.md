**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1957 the *Journal of Consulting Psychology* printed Carl R. Rogers's "The Necessary and Sufficient Conditions of Therapeutic Personality Change," a paper that offered a list rather than a memoir. Six conditions. Three of them — congruence, unconditional positive regard, empathic understanding — became a poster. A fourth, often forgotten on the poster, required that the client *perceive* those attitudes. The remaining conditions concerned contact and the client's incongruence. The paper is a skill-package claim: if these are present, change follows; if they are not, the rest of the therapist's ingenuity is beside the point. Whether that sufficiency holds is a research quarrel this essay will not settle. The historical fact is that a modality was bundled as conditions plus a method of looking (the tape) plus a prohibition (do not load the hour with advice and interpretation).

The life of Rogers belongs to the therapy-history pack. Kirschenbaum's 2007 *The Life and Work of Carl Rogers* is there if a reader wants the man. This pack keeps the *package*: what was to be done with the mouth, the face, the reel, and the impulse to fix. Person-centered work, in counselor education, became a set of teachable restraints. A speech-language pathologist who "is Rogerian" on a website is usually advertising manners. The 1957 paper was advertising a test.

## What is in the bundle

First, recording. Ohio State in 1940 had already made the hour heavier than a conscience; *Client-Centered Therapy* (1951) made the recordings public argument. The package needs the reel because without playback the conditions are a self-report. Supervisors who later "taught Rogers" without ever stopping a tape taught a reputation. The modality, as a skill package, is the reputation plus the machine.

Second, the conditions as work, not as traits. Congruence — the counselor's insides and outsides not running separate shows — is treated in this pack as a technical claim with its own essay. Unconditional positive regard is another. Empathic understanding, with the "as if" intact, is a third. The package is the three together, in the same hour, perceived. Split them and you have a kind person, or a technically accurate parrot, or a sincere person who cannot stand the client. Rogers's sufficiency claim was that the cluster, received, was the therapy. Later trainers who taught "the core conditions" as three items on a quiz were already unpacking the package into atoms. Ivey's microskills and Carkhuff's scales are what you get when a package is atomized so a beginner can fail in public. Those atoms have their own numbers in this pack. The modality is the claim that the atoms, together and received, are enough.

Third, non-advice. The older name was non-directive, a 1940s word Rogers later found inexact. Directiveness snuck in through the adjective in a reflection, through the timing of a summary, through the decision to record at all. Still, the package's public face was a counselor whose speech was small. *Client-Centered Therapy* is full of hours that embarrass people who wanted a clever therapist. The smallness was the method. Advice, in this bundle, is not a seasoning. It is a different modality sitting down uninvited.

Fourth, a theory of the other chair. The client is the one who evaluates the hour. Barrett-Lennard's 1962 Relationship Inventory at least asked. Many later courses asked only the supervisor. A package that forgets the last 1957 gate is a package that grades the helper and ignores the only person the 1957 paper said had to be convinced.

*On Becoming a Person* (1961) popularized the tone for a wider public. The book is part of the package's history because it taught a generation what the modality *sounded like* when it left the journal. It is also how the package became a personality: warm, Midwestern, allergic to diagnosis. The allergy is historical. It is not a rule this series will impose on a speech clinic that must, in fact, diagnose communication and swallowing disorders. Person-centered skills inside CSD are a borrowed restraint on the dump, not a replacement for an audiogram.

## Names the bundle wore

Non-directive counseling, client-centered therapy, person-centered: the same household, different decades. "Person-centered" as a preferred label belongs to Rogers's later years and to the wider applications (education, encounter groups, conflict work) that the therapy-history pack can narrate. Counselor-education programs kept "person-centered" as a unit in a theories course: one week, a tape, a list of conditions, a warning not to give advice. That week is the modality as it actually arrived for most American master's students.

The Wisconsin project of the late 1950s and early 1960s — Rogers, colleagues, a hospital population, Truax and Carkhuff coming out with scales — is the package under strain. A university clinic's manners were not a ward's manners. The package claimed sufficiency. A hospital asked for evidence. The scales tried to supply it. Critics later said the slices were too short and the raters too trained in the house style. The package survived as a teaching religion. The sufficiency claim did not, in most of the field, remain a law. Even person-centered trainers learned to speak of "necessary" more easily than of "sufficient." The 1957 title had used both adjectives. History is allowed to notice the drop.

Gendlin's experiencing work, attached to the same Chicago and Wisconsin weather, is a fork: the client's felt sense as the thing to follow, not only the counselor's conditions. *Focusing* (1978) is a later book with its own afterlife. For package history it matters that even inside the household, people disagreed about *whose* process the hour was tracking.

## What the package refuses

It refuses diagnosis as the first move of the hour — which is why a medical or CSD clinic can only ever take a *piece* of it. It refuses interpretation as the counselor's gift. It refuses the empty chair, the thought record, the miracle question, the miracle. Those belong to other packages in this calendar. A "person-centered" clinician who is also doing those things is practicing an integration, which is a later essay, not the 1957 bundle.

It refuses the idea that the counselor's theory of the person must be spoken aloud to work. The theory is enacted. That is why the tape mattered. A spoken commitment to the conditions, followed by a reel of advice, is the package failing in the way packages fail: on film.

Feminist and multicultural critiques — that "unconditional" regard often meant regard for a certain class of feeling-talk, that empathy tracked a white, middle-class vocabulary — are part of the package's later history. They are not a reason to skip the 1957 paper. They are a reason to read Wrenn 1962 and Sue, Arredondo, and McDavis 1992 as pressure on the same hour. A package that cannot hear those papers has become Wrenn's cocoon with a warm voice.

## How CSD carried a piece of the bundle

Luterman's CSD counseling books taught a humanistic temperature: the parent's affect is not an obstacle. Holland and Nelson's wellness texts (2007 and after) kept a person-sized frame while adding positive psychology's later fashion. ASHA's Practice Portal still lists humanistic theory among frameworks "particularly applicable" to audiology and speech-language pathology, citing Rogers 1951 and 1959 among others. That citation is a professional-history object. It does not mean ASHA adopted the sufficiency claim. The 2016 Scope still bounds the hour to communication, feeding, and swallowing, and still requires referral.

What traveled into CSD, then, was not the whole 1957 machine. It was the prohibition on the dump, the preference for a small counselor-mouth, and the idea that the other person is not a broken device. Those are skill imports. They can be practiced by an SLP without the SLP becoming a person-centered psychotherapist. The license essay in this pack exists to keep that sentence intact.

WOW Therapies does not, by hosting this history, claim to practice person-centered psychotherapy. The clinic's live job is speech-language pathology in Southeast Arkansas. The package is heritage: a mid-century way of bundling recording, conditions, and non-advice so that a helper's urge to fix had to justify itself on tape. A family who meets an SLP after bad news is not in 1957 Chicago. They may still meet a clinician who has been taught, in one graduate week, not to fill the silence with a home program. That restraint has a serial number. It is the package, thinned, surviving outside the institute that made it.

The therapy-history pack can keep the man, the Wisconsin disappointment, the later peace work. This essay's last job is to say what a *modality* is in this series' usage: not a founder, not a vibe, not a menu item on a clinic website. A package of skills with a dated claim about what is enough. Person-centered work claimed that the conditions, perceived, were enough. The rest of this calendar is, in one way or another, a set of disagreements with that sentence — or a set of attempts to teach its pieces to people who will never hang Rogers's photograph.
