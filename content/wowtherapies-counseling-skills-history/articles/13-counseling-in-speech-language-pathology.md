**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1979 Little, Brown of Boston issued David Luterman's *Counseling Parents of Hearing-Impaired Children*, a first edition of some two hundred pages with a foreword by Mark Ross. The title did something impolite. It named the parents as the people being counseled. The child's ear was the medical fact. The book's problem was the adults in the room who had just been handed a life they had not scheduled. Audiology and speech-language pathology already knew how to explain an audiogram. Luterman insisted that the explanation was not the hour.

This essay is the history of *counseling* as a word inside communication sciences and disorders. It is not a biography of Luterman — that figure essay comes later in this pack — and it is not the SLPWOW speech-science story of how the profession grew. It is about a skill claim that CSD borrowed, renamed, bounded, and still teaches in a single crowded graduate semester. The 2016 ASHA Scope of Practice would eventually list counseling among eight service-delivery domains. It did not mint a counseling license. The whole history sits in that gap.

## A Boston book that refused the information dump

Luterman wrote from a clinical tradition in which parents of deaf and hard-of-hearing children were often treated as students in a crash course. The professional talked. The parent nodded. The file showed that "counseling" had occurred because information had been transferred. *Counseling Parents* (1979) treated that transfer as necessary and insufficient. Grief, guilt, marital strain, and the wish that the professional would simply fix the child were not interruptions of the real work. They were the work that made the rest of the work possible.

The book is a historical object, not a homework sheet. It argued that the clinician's expertise about hearing did not automatically produce expertise about a family's inner weather. It also argued, against a certain medical briskness, that affect in the parent was not a side-effect to be waited out. Later CSD textbooks would still have to fight the same briskness, which tells you the 1979 argument did not win in a year.

In 1984 Little, Brown issued *Counseling the Communicatively Disordered and Their Families*. The clientele widened: not only hearing, not only parents. The later PRO-ED line, retitled *Counseling Persons with Communication Disorders and Their Families* (sixth edition 2017), is the same lineage under a less dated adjective. ERIC's ED583622 notes a 1979 first-publication claim for the line. Cataloguers can quarrel about which title is "first." The historical sequence a general reader needs is simpler: 1979 names the parents of hearing-impaired children; 1984 names the communicatively disordered and their families; later editions keep the family in the title because CSD counseling never convincingly became a one-person hour.

Luterman's living presence in the field — he was born in 1934 — is a fact for the figure essay. Here the books are the event. They gave graduate programs something to assign when "the counseling course" had to exist and the program did not wish to pretend that Ivey's microskills, taught next door in counselor education, had been written for an aphasia group.

## Informational and personal-adjustment: a portal's two drawers

ASHA's Practice Portal page "Counseling for Professional Service Delivery" still divides the work into two drawers that Flasher and Fogle's textbook had already used. Informational counseling — also called client and family education — is talk about the nature of a disorder, intervention options, prognosis, and resources. Personal-adjustment counseling addresses feelings, thoughts, and beliefs that show up because a communication or swallowing condition has entered the house. The portal cites Flasher and Fogle (2012) and, as a later object, Tellis and Barone (2018). Re-open the portal the week of upload; web pages move. The *distinction* is the dated professional object.

The two-drawer scheme is a peace treaty. It lets a profession say: we do counsel, and we know the difference between explaining a swallow study and sitting with the fact that meals are no longer easy. It also lets the profession say: we refer when the hour is no longer about communication, feeding, or swallowing. That referral sentence is the license border. A reader who hears "my SLP is my counselor" is hearing a kindness and a category error at once.

Luterman had already warned that information dumped into a shocked parent does not stay. The portal's "informational" drawer can still be practiced as a dump. History is not a guarantee of manners.

## Two later textbooks, two temperatures

Lydia V. Flasher and Paul T. Fogle's *Counseling Skills for Speech-Language Pathologists and Audiologists* (Thomson/Delmar, 2004; second edition 2012) did what counselor-education books had done since Carkhuff and Ivey: it treated counseling as *skills* a CSD student could be shown. Theories appear — humanistic, cognitive-behavioral, family systems, multicultural, existential — as frameworks "particularly applicable" to the professions, in the portal's later wording. The book is a training object. This series will not lift its exercises. The historical claim is that by 2004 a CSD student could be assigned a counseling-skills text with the professions in the title. That had not been true in 1950. It was barely true in 1979, when Luterman still had to explain why parents were the subject.

Audrey L. Holland and Ryan L. Nelson's *Counseling in Communication Disorders: A Wellness Perspective* (Plural, first edition 2007; second 2014; a later third edition — catalog records split 2018 and 2020, so the year wants a check on upload) changed the temperature. Holland had spent a career on aphasia and on what a life could still contain. The wellness frame, drawing on positive psychology as a research fashion of those decades, refused to treat the counseling hour as only a grief hour. People with communication disorders also had humor, stubbornness, and ordinary plans. Holland died in 2023. Write her in the past tense. The book remains a CSD object that argued against the clinic's temptation to see only the hole in the sentence.

Luterman's temperature was affect: do not skip the feeling to reach the home program. Holland's temperature was wellness: do not skip the person to reach the feeling. Graduate courses that assigned both books without saying they were in a conversation produced students who thought "counseling" meant whichever chapter they had underlined. The quarrel is the inheritance. A speech clinic that only cheers or only mourns has picked a chapter and called it a personality.

## ASHA 2016: a domain, not a second license

The 2016 *Scope of Practice in Speech-Language Pathology* (ASHA document SP2016-00343) reorganized the profession into eight service-delivery domains: collaboration; counseling; prevention and wellness; screening; assessment; treatment; modalities, technology, and instrumentation; population and systems. Counseling's paragraph is careful. SLPs counsel by providing education, guidance, and support. Individuals, families, and caregivers are counseled regarding acceptance, adaptation, and decision making about communication, feeding and swallowing, and related disorders. The SLP's role includes interactions related to emotional reactions, thoughts, feelings, and behaviors that result from living with those disorders. Then the document lists activities and, crucially, a referral duty: refer when counseling needs fall outside communication and feeding and swallowing.

That is a scope sentence. It is not an LPC. It is not an LMHC. It is not psychology or psychiatry. The American Counseling Association's *Code of Ethics* (2014, still the in-force code while a 2026 revision was being prepared) governs a different profession. CACREP, founded in 1981, accredits counselor-education programs, not CSD programs. ASHA's Certificate of Clinical Competence does not become a counseling credential because the word "counseling" appears in a domain list. The next essay in this pack will stay with families. The license essay after that will stay with the border. The 2016 PDF is the document that made the border visible to anyone willing to read past the word.

Earlier ASHA documents had not "always" included counseling in this shape. Cite the dated scope you have. The 2016 reorganization — eight domains, counseling named among them — is the object in hand. Preferred Practice Patterns for speech-language pathology (2004) and audiology (2006) had already listed counseling outcomes as professional expectations. Those are older objects. They still do not mint a second license.

## The one-semester problem

By the 2000s many master's programs in speech-language pathology required a counseling course, or a counseling unit stuffed into a professional-issues class. The syllabus tended to be a sampler: a Luterman chapter, a Holland chapter, a slice of Ivey or Egan borrowed from next door, a day on "difficult conversations," a slide that said refer. Students left able to name personal-adjustment counseling and still terrified of tears. The terror is historical. CSD training is built to make people competent at assessment and treatment. Counseling skills, in the Truax-and-Ivey sense, were invented to be taught to people whose primary identity was already "helper." SLP students are still becoming clinicians of speech, language, and swallow. The counseling course is a graft. Grafts take unevenly.

None of that is a reason to sneer at the course. It is a reason to read the 1979 book as a protest against the graft's thinness. Luterman wrote because information was being mistaken for care. Holland wrote because care was being mistaken for a funeral. Flasher and Fogle wrote because skills can be shown. ASHA wrote because a profession that uses a word without a border will be asked, in a deposition, what it thought it was doing.

WOW Therapies is a speech-language practice in Southeast Arkansas, not a counseling center. Heritage education on these pages does not mean the clinic practices Luterman's groups, Holland's wellness workshops, or any modality named in the sister therapy-history pack. It means that when an SLP in a small-city room explains a result and then stops talking, a forty-year CSD argument is in the pause: is this informational counseling, personal-adjustment counseling, or the moment to refer? The 2016 Scope named the domain. The 1979 book named the parents. The portal named two drawers. The license remains the one on the wall.
