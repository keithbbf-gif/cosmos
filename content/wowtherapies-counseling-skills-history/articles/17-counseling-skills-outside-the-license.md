**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 2016 the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association's *Scope of Practice in Speech-Language Pathology* listed counseling among eight service-delivery domains, in the same list as screening and treatment, and then drew a border the size of a license. SLPs counsel by providing education, guidance, and support about communication, feeding and swallowing, and related disorders. They refer when the need falls outside those. The PDF did not create a Licensed Professional Counselor. It described a piece of speech-language pathology and told the profession where to stop.

This essay is about that stop. Counseling *skills* — listening as a technique, reflection, a working alliance, even a named grammar like OARS — traveled into occupational therapy, physical therapy, nursing, medicine, and CSD because they could be taught to people who were already licensed as something else. The travel is a historical fact. The confusion it produces in a waiting room is also a fact. WOW Therapies is a speech-language practice. These pages are heritage education, not a claim that the clinic practices counseling as ACA members mean the word, and not a back door to a second profession.

## Two professions, two paper piles

The American Personnel and Guidance Association formed in 1952 from vocational guidance, counselor-trainer, student-personnel, and college-personnel groups. It became the American Association for Counseling and Development in 1983 and the American Counseling Association in 1992. CACREP, the Council for Accreditation of Counseling and Related Educational Programs, was founded in 1981 to accredit counselor-education programs. The ACA *Code of Ethics* (2014) — still the in-force code while a revision was being prepared for 2026 — is a document for that profession. It speaks to the counseling relationship, confidentiality, professional responsibility, distance counseling, and the rest of a licensed counselor's day. It does not govern an SLP by virtue of a shared English noun.

Speech-language pathology has ASHA, a Certificate of Clinical Competence, state licensure boards, and a 2016 scope that names counseling as a domain *about* communication, feeding, and swallowing. Occupational therapy and physical therapy have their own national associations and state boards, and their own documents that sometimes use "counseling" or "therapeutic use of self" for talk that helps a person live with a body that has changed. Those documents are not interchangeable. A PT who listens well after a hip fracture is not an LPC. An OT who addresses the despair that can follow a loss of a hand's function is not a psychologist. The skills can rhyme. The licenses do not.

Psychology and psychiatry sit further along the same hallway with still different paper. A psychologist's license, in U.S. jurisdictions, is a psychology license. A psychiatrist is a physician. Neither is created by an SLP course called "counseling." DSM and ICD diagnoses, medication, and psychotherapy as a declared service belong to those rooms when the law says so. History essays in the sister therapy-history pack tell the schools. This lane tells the skill names. Neither pack turns a speech clinic into a mental-health clinic by vocabulary.

## How the skills crossed the hall

Rogers's recordings, Truax's scales, Ivey's 1968–1971 microcounseling, Carkhuff's five-point sheets, Egan's *Skilled Helper* (1975), Hill's *Helping Skills* (1999): these were built to be taught to helpers, not only to people whose diploma said "counselor." Ivey advertised migrations into medicine, librarianship, agricultural extension. Miller and Rollnick's later editions watched motivational interviewing leave the alcohol clinic for primary care. Once a skill is filmable and countable, every allied-health syllabus can decide to include it, ignore it, or pretend it is "just being nice."

CSD's version of the migration is dated in this pack: Luterman 1979 and 1984, Flasher and Fogle 2004/2012, Holland and Nelson 2007 and after, ASHA 2016. The graduate "counseling course" taught skills. It did not confer ACA membership. Students who left that course able to reflect a feeling were still, legally, student SLPs. The reflection did not change the wall certificate they would eventually hang.

Occupational and physical therapy have parallel textbooks on "therapeutic communication" that this pack will not annex. The point for a general reader is the pattern. American universities trained thousands of people to do small, named interpersonal acts, then sent them into jobs whose *license* was about a different expertise: speech, occupation, movement, medication, the soul. The skills were portable. The title was not.

## What the 2016 referral sentence is for

ASHA's counseling domain includes discussion of emotions, thoughts, and behaviors that result from living with communication or feeding and swallowing disorders. That sentence is wider than "explain the test." It is still not a blank check. The same page of the scope tells the SLP to refer when counseling needs fall outside communication and feeding and swallowing. ASHA's Code of Ethics, in the version the Practice Portal was citing by 2023, requires members to stay within competence and to use referral. Preferred Practice Patterns from 2004 already listed counseling among expected professional acts. None of those documents authorize the treatment of a major mental illness as if it were a fluency side-effect.

The waiting-room confusion is understandable. A person in distress will use the word "counselor" for whoever is kind. A family who has no LPC within a two-hour drive will use the SLP as the only talker in the system. Southeast Arkansas geography makes that substitution tempting. History's job is to keep the temptation from becoming a claim. An SLP may, inside the 2016 domain, sit with the anger that follows a new aphasia. An SLP may not, on the strength of a microskills weekend, take over a trauma caseload. The skill names are shared. The fence is the scope PDF and the state board.

Arkansas, like other states, licenses speech-language pathologists through a speech-language-hearing board and licenses professional counselors through a counseling board. The two lists are not a hierarchy of virtue. They are two jobs. LMHC, in states that use that title, is another counseling license, not an SLP add-on. School counselors have yet another paper pile — ASCA's National Model (2003 and revisions) as a dated object in a later essay. Rehabilitation counselors have statute history of their own (Smith-Fess 1920 and later vocational-rehabilitation amendments). This pack will give those lineages their own numbers. The present essay's only duty is to keep them from collapsing into one warm word.

## Skills without a license, licenses without those skills

The inverse error is also historical. Licensed counselors can be clumsy at the very skills this pack has been naming. A license is a legal permission and a disciplinary hook. It is not a Truax score. Microskills training was invented partly because graduate titles did not guarantee an hour you could stand to watch on tape. So a reader should not hear this essay as a hymn to licenses. It is a hymn to *not lying about them*.

Freud's 1926 *Question of Lay Analysis*, treated as a listening-modality problem in a later essay, is an ancestor quarrel: who may do the work, the physician or the trained non-physician? American counseling, social work, psychology, and CSD each answered with a board. The answers do not agree. A speech clinic that quotes Rogers is not thereby under the Vienna rules of 1926. A counselor who quotes ASHA is not thereby allowed to do a swallow study. The comedy of borrowed quotations is part of the record.

Commercial workshops aggravate the blur. A weekend certificate in a named method can be honest as continuing education. It becomes dishonest when the flyer implies a new profession. Motivational interviewing's own later editions added compassion to "spirit" because the techniques could be used in self-serving ways. License law is one of the boring tools that still limits self-service. This series will not teach a reader to self-administer any method. It will say that the person who holds the hour should be the person the state named for that hour.

## A speech clinic, a website, a fence

WOW Therapies, on wowtherapies.com, is a Southeast Arkansas speech-language practice associated with Christina Chambers. Heritage essays on counseling skills do not convert the site into a counseling practice, do not add DSM specialties, and do not put `MedicalTherapy` schema on a history URL. The educational note at the top of each of these pages is the same fence in ordinary prose: not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, not a substitute for a licensed clinician. When the clinician in the room is an SLP, "licensed clinician" means licensed in speech-language pathology for that work, and a different licensed clinician when the work is something else.

The skills remain worth teaching inside CSD. Luterman was right that information is not care. Holland was right that a life is not only a hole in a sentence. Ivey was right that some acts can be shown on a tape. ASHA was right to name a domain and a referral. The error is the sentence that begins "and so we are also counselors." No. We are speech-language pathologists who, in a documented historical line, were taught some counseling skills for a bounded job. The bound is the point. Without it the word "counseling" on a speech site is either a kindness or a confusion. History prefers the bound.
