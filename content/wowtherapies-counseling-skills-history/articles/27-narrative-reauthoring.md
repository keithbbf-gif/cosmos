**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1990 W. W. Norton published Michael White and David Epston's *Narrative Means to Therapeutic Ends*. The Australian precursor, *Literate Means to Therapeutic Ends*, had come from Dulwich Centre Publications in Adelaide in 1989, with Karl Tomm associated in some catalog records as a foreword presence the Norton edition kept. 1990 is the date American counseling programs could assign a New York binding. Re-authoring, as a modality name, is that binding's claim: that a life is storied, that some stories are thin and problem-saturated, and that a helper might ask questions which thicken other stories. This essay will name externalizing as a move. It will not give a script for it.

Michael White died on 4 April 2008 in San Diego, after a teaching trip. He was fifty-nine. Dulwich Centre's public remembrance and the *New York Times* obituary are the sources for that date. Living colleagues remain. Public books, public lectures, public interviews. No private medical story beyond the public fact of his death.

## Dulwich, 1983, Kaurna Country

Dulwich Centre opened in Adelaide in 1983. Michael White and Cheryl White established it as a base for counseling, community work, training, and, from 1984, Dulwich Centre Publications, which Cheryl White founded as an independent house. The Centre's own later pages acknowledge the Kaurna people as Traditional Custodians of the land on which it stands. That acknowledgment is part of the institution's public history, not a decoration this file invented.

White had worked as a psychiatric social worker at Adelaide Children's Hospital after a 1979 social-work degree. Epston worked in Auckland. The Tasman is inside the 1990 book: letters, documents, and a shared taste for the text analogy. Foucault's arguments about power and knowledge are in the theoretical chapters. So is a distinction between a logico-scientific mode and a narrative mode that the book borrows and applies. This is not a philosophy seminar. It is a note that the 1990 skill package arrived with a library, unlike some workshop cousins that arrived with a chair.

White's later *Maps of Narrative Practice* (2007) is a subsequent teaching object. Essay 44 in this pack is the figure file. This file is the modality: re-authoring as a named package of questions, letters, and ceremonies.

## Externalizing, unique outcomes, letters — named, not assigned

Externalizing conversations, as the 1990 book names them, separate the person from the problem in the grammar of the hour. Workshop posters later compressed the idea into a slogan that pitted "the person" against "the problem." This series will not print a fill-in-the-blank ("how has [problem] been tricking you?") as a thing to try. The named move is historical. A runnable script is a treatment. The difference is the rule this series holds.

"Unique outcomes" — times the dominant story did not fully apply — are cousins of Milwaukee's exceptions (essay 26) and not the same bet. Milwaukee built a future from what was already working. Adelaide and Auckland asked how a neglected story could be *authorized* as a story, often with witnesses. Definitional ceremonies and outsider-witness practices, developed in White's later teaching and in related Dulwich projects, made an audience part of the skill. A ceremony is not a worksheet. Describing that a ceremony existed is not an invitation to stage one in a living room.

Letters are the 1990 book's most portable literate object: letters of invitation, prediction, reference, "counter-referral," documents that a person might carry as a record of a preferred identity. Epston's New Zealand practice made the file a craft. Counselors who later sent a summary email are not thereby narrative therapists. The 1990 letters were written inside a theory of documents as identity. A billing summary is not that theory.

This series will not reproduce a sample letter a reader could copy. Letter-as-history is a genre in the book. Letter-as-template is a protocol.

## Re-authoring as a fight with interiority

Cognitive counseling (essay 22) treated a thought as something to catch and test. Narrative re-authoring treated a *story* as something a culture and a family had helped to write, with the person as a character under descriptions they did not choose. The helper's skill, in this package, is not primarily a thought record. It is a line of questions that invite a different narration, then a community that will recognize it.

The fight with psychiatry's interior language is explicit in the 1990 theoretical chapters. A diagnosis can become the whole story of a life. White and Epston were not the first to say so; Chesler's 1972 *Women and Madness* (next essay) and a long anti-psychiatry literature sit in the same century. Narrative therapy's distinctive skill claim was that the alternative to a totalizing description was not a silence but another text.

Critics said the method could become clever, literary, and thin on suffering that does not want a better story yet. Others said it traveled into Aboriginal Australian and Māori collaborations in ways that later Dulwich publications documented as community work rather than a white clinician's office hour — and that those collaborations, like all collaborations across colonial histories, require the Centre's own later ethical writing, not a speech-site summary. This file will not annex that work as a trophy. It will say that narrative practice, as Dulwich published it, refused to stay only in a private office. Community gatherings and the *International Journal of Narrative Therapy and Community Work* are institutional objects. They are not a how-to for a reader in Arkansas.

Workshop culture, after White's international teaching of the 1990s–2000s, produced a second problem: narrative as a brand with maps, certificates, and a chair (again) for the visiting expert. White's own later writing tried to de-center the therapist. A de-centered therapist on a sold-out circuit is a paradox the field still lives with. Naming the paradox is not gossip about a dead man. It is a fact about how modalities travel.

## Skills that look like other skills until they don't

Ivey could film an "externalizing question" as a microskill. He would miss the politics. Egan could place re-authoring in an insight stage. The skilled-helper stages were not written for Foucault. Motivational interviewing can sound like it is evoking a preferred future; MI's home is ambivalence about a behavior, with a 1983 drinkers' paper as the door. Narrative's home is the politics of description. The resemblance in the mouth — both ask, neither lectures — is real. The library behind the mouth is not.

Feminist therapy (next) and multicultural competencies (the file after) are kin in the refusal of a supposedly culture-free interior. They are not the same package. A competencies document is a standards object for a profession. A 1990 Norton book is a method with letters. Keep the objects apart and the history stays honest.

## Heritage, no scraped workshop photo

This page will not use a scraped workshop photograph of White. Type, or a title page, is enough. That choice is the graphic version of this essay's refusal to turn a person into a method's mascot.

WOW Therapies is a speech-language practice in Southeast Arkansas. It does not practice narrative therapy as a trademark. Families already live in stories: the child who "won't talk," the stroke that "ended" a worker, the stutter that "is" a personality. Those folk stories are why a CSD clinic might care that 1990 named a way of separating a person from a description. They are not a reason to run externalizing questions as a speech technique. ASHA's 2016 Scope of Practice locates counseling in communication, feeding, and swallowing. Audrey Holland's wellness frame, in this pack's figure essays, already asked CSD to see a person besides a disorder. Narrative therapy is a cousin of that insistence, with a different passport.

The therapy-history pack can keep White as a founder. This pack keeps Adelaide 1983, *Literate Means* 1989, *Narrative Means* 1990, externalizing as a named move without a script, letters as documents, and a death date of 4 April 2008. Re-authoring is a word that sounds gentle. The 1990 book is not gentle about power. A history that keeps only the gentleness has dropped the book.
