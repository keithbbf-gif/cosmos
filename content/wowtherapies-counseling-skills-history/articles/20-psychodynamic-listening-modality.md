**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1912 Sigmund Freud's "Recommendations to Physicians Practising Psycho-Analysis" told the doctor not to direct notice to anything in particular, and to keep toward everything heard what English translators later called evenly suspended — or evenly hovering — attention. The paper is a technical memo, not a life. It says the physician who follows expectations will find only what was expected, and the physician who follows inclinations will falsify what is heard. The counterpart, on the other side of the hour, is the patient's obligation to say what comes to mind without selecting. Two disciplines, one pair: free association and a listening that tries not to prefer. Counselor education later lifted the listening half, thinned it, and taught it as a modality to people who would never become analysts. That thinning is this essay's subject.

The life of Freud, the institutes, the wars, the American medical capture of analysis — those belong to the therapy-history pack. This pack will not reprint them. It will stay with a *way of listening* that counselors were told they could practice as a package: hovering attention, a rule of abstinence (do not gratify, do not advise, do not become the missing person), and, much later, short-term dynamic adaptations that tried to fit the package into a semester's worth of hours. The package is not a discovery myth about a hidden mind. The 1912 memo is enough.

## Evenly hovering attention as a skill claim

The 1912 technique, in the Standard Edition's English, rejects special expedients, even note-taking, as the means of holding many patients' material. The analyst is to withhold conscious steering of attention and give over to what Freud called, in that paper, unconscious memory. Later he would, in correspondence, prefer "preconscious" for a more careful formulation. Historians can keep the nuance. Counselor-education handouts kept the slogan: listen equally to everything; do not pounce on the interesting bit.

That is already a fight with Ivey's 1968 attending behaviors and with MITI's later preference for some kinds of talk over others. Psychodynamic listening, as a modality, is a refusal to rank the next sentence in advance. Motivational interviewing's grammar ranks. Microskills rank. The 1912 memo tells the physician that ranking is how you miss. A counselor who "does psychodynamic listening" in a twelve-session agency is therefore in a contradiction the later short-term people tried to solve. The contradiction is the history.

Theodor Reik's *Listening with the Third Ear* (1948) is the popular English book that made the analytic ear a literary object. Reik, a non-physician, is also a character in the license fight below. The 1948 book taught a generation of American readers that the listener's own inner movement was data. That claim slides toward what later writers called countertransference as tool rather than contaminant. Counselor trainees who heard "trust your gut" in a psychodynamic week were getting a distant, flattened Reik. The flattening is not Reik's fault. It is what happens when a modality is taught without the rest of the institute (training analysis, supervision on the couch's terms, years).

## Abstinence, not as a moral advertisement

"Abstinence" in this technical sense is not a temperance poster. It is the counselor's refusal to supply the friendship, the advice, the love, the exemption the other person may want from the hour. Freud's papers on technique — including the 1915 discussions of transference love — treated gratification as the way the work dies. The analyst who becomes helpful in the ordinary social meaning of helpful has left the method.

Counselors who imported abstinence as "don't give advice" were already mixing packages. Person-centered non-advice has a different theory: the other person knows. Psychodynamic abstinence has a different theory: the wish is to be analyzed, not granted. The behaviors can look alike on a tape for thirty seconds. The packages disagree about why the mouth stays small. A theories course that taught both in adjacent weeks without saying so produced students who thought they had learned one skill called "not rescuing." The skill is real. The disagreement is the modality.

Abstinence also meant, in classical packaging, a limit on extra-session contact and on the analyst's self-display. Later relational and feminist analysts rewrote those limits. Counselor education sometimes kept the 1912 hush and sometimes kept the 1990s rewrite and rarely said which decade was in the room. This essay will not referee a century of analytic civil war. It will say that "psychodynamic listening" as a *counseling* modality is already a selection from that war: usually the hush, the hovering, the interest in what is not said, and a light version of transference ("you might be talking to me as if I were someone else"). The light version is a historical object. It is not an analysis.

## 1926: lay analysis as a license fight

*The Question of Lay Analysis* (1926) was Freud's book-length intervention in a Viennese prosecution of Theodor Reik for treating patients without a medical degree. The book argues, among other things, that psychoanalysis is not simply a branch of medicine, and that a trained non-physician might practice if certain precautions were observed. The American institutes, in the decades that followed, often went the other way, medicalizing training and leaving "lay" analysts in a fight that still has grandchildren in every state licensing statute.

Why it belongs in a counseling-skills pack: the 1926 book is an early, loud version of the question this pack's license essay asked with ASHA 2016. Who may do the listening? The physician? The trained non-physician? The person with the right institute diploma? The person with a state LPC? The SLP who has been taught to hover for a semester? Freud's answer in 1926 was not ASHA's answer, and not ACA's. The kinship is the *form* of the quarrel. American counseling, social work, and psychology each built boards rather than accept Vienna's settlement. CSD named a bounded counseling domain and a referral duty. The 1926 book will not decide those boards. It is the ancestor scene in which listening was already a jurisdictional object, not only a virtue.

Reik's later American career, and the New York fights over who could analyze, are institute history. This paragraph stops at the 1926 object. Living people are not at issue. The dead are not ours to gossip about. The book is public.

## Short-term dynamic: the package on a clock

Agency counseling and university training clinics could not offer a classical analysis. They could offer a psychodynamic *attitude* in twelve or twenty-five hours. Several named attempts exist as dated objects, not as protocols for a reader.

James Mann's *Time-Limited Psychotherapy* (Harvard, 1973) set a twelve-session frame and a central issue. David Malan's Tavistock work — *A Study of Brief Psychotherapy* (1963) and later *The Frontier of Brief Psychotherapy* (1976) — tried to make brief dynamic treatment researchable. Peter Sifneos's short-term anxiety-provoking psychotherapy, papers and books through the 1970s, selected for a certain kind of willing patient. Habib Davanloo's intensive short-term dynamic psychotherapy, papers in the late 1970s and a 1980 book, made the hour more confrontational than the 1912 hush would have sounded. Those names disagree with each other. What they share, for this pack, is the admission that hovering attention on an open-ended clock is not what most counselors sell. The modality, once it left the institute, had to learn a calendar.

Bordin's 1979 alliance paper, which this pack already treated as a skill essay, began by generalizing a *psychoanalytic* concept of the working alliance. That is a two-way borrow. Psychodynamic listening as a counseling modality often arrives now as alliance plus curiosity about the unsaid, without the rest of the couch. Whether that remainder is still "psychodynamic" is a quarrel for institutes. For counselor education it is already how the week is taught: listen for pattern, do not leap to advice, notice the relationship as data.

## What this modality is not, on a speech site

It is not a swallow treatment. It is not a method for a parent conference in early intervention, except in the thinnest sense that any professional can refrain from pouncing. It is not Freud's biography. It is not an invitation to interpret a child's lateness to therapy as a wish. CSD's 2016 border still holds. An SLP who becomes fascinated by the unsaid at the expense of the communication diagnosis has changed jobs without changing licenses.

WOW Therapies does not practice psychoanalysis, lay or medical, short-term or otherwise. Heritage education means a reader can see why "just listen" in an American helping profession is sometimes a 1912 technical object, sometimes a 1957 condition, sometimes a 1968 attending behavior, and sometimes a 1983 motivational grammar. The mouths can look alike. The packages do not. Psychodynamic listening, as counselors received it, is the package that tried not to prefer among sentences, and that paid for that refusal with a license fight in 1926 and a clock in the 1970s.

A small-city speech clinic in Southeast Arkansas will rarely need the 1912 memo. It may still need the warning the memo contains in ordinary language: if you hear only the finding you came to confirm, you will find it. That warning is older than ASHA. It does not require a couch. It does require a profession that knows which listening it is selling, and which license hangs on the wall while it sells.
