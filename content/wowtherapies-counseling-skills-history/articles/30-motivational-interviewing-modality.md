**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In April 1983 the British journal *Behavioural Psychotherapy* printed William R. Miller's "Motivational Interviewing with Problem Drinkers" (volume 11, pages 147–172). The paper argued that motivation is not a personality trait the drinker either has or lacks. It is an interpersonal process. Attribution, cognitive dissonance, self-efficacy, and a Rogerian empathic ear were the borrowed tools. Labeling was to be deemphasized. The helper's job, in that 1983 account, was not to wrestle the person into a program. It was to arrange a conversation in which the person's own reasons could outtalk the short circuits of shame and "denial."

Essay 12 in this pack is the OARS grammar — open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries — as a *skill* list later trainers could film. This essay is the *modality*: spirit, ambivalence, partnership. The grammar without the spirit is how MI became a bag of stems. The 1983 paper is stranger, and more social-psychological, than a stem list.

Living people. Miller (born 1947), Stephen Rollnick, Theresa Moyers, and colleagues still in public work are not public property. Public books, public lectures, public interviews. No gossip. No health speculation. Type, not a scraped headshot.

## Hjellestad, 1982, a Selectric, then a journal

Miller has told, in later public origin stories including a 2009 *American Psychologist* paper, how a 1982 sabbatical at the Hjellestad Clinic near Bergen put him in a room with Norwegian psychologists who stopped his role-plays and asked why he had just said what he said. The implicit model, he wrote, was not identical with the behavior therapies he had come to lecture on. A November 1982 draft, typed on A4 with an IBM Selectric in a barbershop office at the clinic, was longer than the 1983 article. Ray Hodgson, then editor of *Behavioural Psychotherapy*, took a reduced version.

That origin is Miller's own published account. It is not a private anecdote invented here. The checkable publication remains 1983. New Mexico, where Miller worked at the University of New Mexico and at CASAA, is the American home of the later coding shop. Norway is the accident of articulation. Both rooms count.

## 1991, Rollnick, and a book that outgrew addiction

Miller has also described, publicly, a 1989 sabbatical in Australia where Stephen Rollnick told him MI was already moving through United Kingdom addiction treatment and that more needed to be written. *Motivational Interviewing: Preparing People to Change Addictive Behavior* (Guilford, 1991) is the first book. Later editions — 2002, 2013, 2023 — widened the subtitle's world. The method left the drinkers' clinic and was claimed by health care, corrections, and a thousand workshops. Rollnick's later work with colleagues on health-care consultations is part of that widening. Living authors; the books are the source.

"Spirit" is the 1990s–2010s word for what the 1983 paper was already circling: a way of being with a person, not a trick. Later editions listed partnership (against the expert who installs motivation), acceptance (including autonomy), compassion, and evocation (the reasons are already in the person). Earlier printings used neighboring words: collaboration, evocation, autonomy. Historians can live with the paraphrase if they do not freeze one edition as scripture.

Ambivalence is the modality's subject matter. A person who both wants to change and does not is, in this package, in the normal condition of the hour — not a "resistant" character to be broken. The "righting reflex" — the helper's urge to leap in with the good argument — is a named problem in the later books. Naming it is not a worksheet. It is a historical admission that MI was built against a confrontation culture in addiction treatment that had treated argument as care.

## What this file will not redo from essay 12

OARS will not be drilled here. Complex reflection as a MITI category has already been mentioned in the restatement essay. This file points across the hallway. If a reader wants the grammar's history, that is skill essay 12. If a reader wants the package that made the grammar mean something besides clever listening, that is this file.

The distinction matters because workshops sold OARS to people who had never read 1983. A medical student can "do a reflection" and still run a righting reflex at full speed. The modality claim is that the spirit is the thing; the skills are how the spirit becomes audible. Critics said that was mysticism. Miller and Rollnick kept trying to specify it anyway, then to measure it.

## MITI, MISC, and the reel again

The Motivational Interviewing Skills Code (MISC) was the earlier, heavier coding system. Theresa Moyers and colleagues developed the Motivational Interviewing Treatment Integrity (MITI) as a shorter, one-pass system. A January 2003 MINT newsletter already described MITI as in development. Moyers, Martin, Manuel, Hendrickson, and Miller published reliability and validity indices in the *Journal of Substance Abuse Treatment* 28 (2005): 19–26. Later MITI versions (3.1.1, 4.2, and others) are dated coding manuals from CASAA. They are historical coding objects. This series will not print a coding sheet. A sheet is a homework protocol. A 2005 paper is a fact about how a modality tried not to become a rumor.

The reel is back. Essay 01's Truax scales tried to hear empathy. MITI tries to hear whether MI is happening: global ratings, behavior counts, in later versions a technical and a relational score. Same American bet: if you cannot count it, you cannot train it. Same American risk: raters learn a house dialect. Moyers's public work is part of that measurement culture. Living researcher; cite the manuals as objects, not as a personality.

## Project MATCH, MET, and a bounded efficacy sentence

NIAAA's Project MATCH, initiated at the end of the 1980s, compared twelve-step facilitation, cognitive-behavioral coping skills, and Motivational Enhancement Therapy over a twelve-week window. MET, in the public therapist manual, was a four-session relative of MI with structured assessment feedback. Primary results appeared in 1997. Matching effects were smaller than the matching dream. This essay will not say MI is the treatment of choice for alcohol problems. It will say that a federal trial made a brief motivational package *auditable* beside two other manuals, and that the audit did not crown a king. Later UK trials asked similar questions. Limits belong in the same paragraph as the acronym.

Confrontation-based addiction treatment is the rival historical object. MI's public story is a refusal of that rival. Historians of treatment should also say that "confrontation" was never one thing, and that some people got sober in rooms MI would have coded as MI-inconsistent. A modality's origin story is allowed to be partial. It is not allowed to be the only story on the ward.

## A speech clinic, a 2016 scope, no stems

WOW Therapies is a speech-language practice in Southeast Arkansas. It does not certify MI trainers. CSD rooms still meet ambivalence: a teenager who wants fluency and does not want the drill; an adult who wants a safer swallow and does not want the diet. Those ambivalences are not "problem drinking." They are why allied health borrowed MI language. ASHA's 2016 Scope of Practice locates counseling in communication, feeding, and swallowing. It does not mint an MI practitioner. Flasher and Fogle's CSD counseling texts, and Holland and Nelson's wellness texts, are this pack's nearer CSD objects. They may mention motivational ideas. They do not make this page a training.

Heritage education can say that a 1983 paper and a 1991 book taught helping professions to treat mixed feelings as the work, not as a character flaw. That is a skill-package history. It is not a permission to "MI your way out of" anything. This series already refused that sentence.

Do not take OARS stems from this page. Take the dates: 1982 Hjellestad draft, 1983 article, 1989 conversation as Miller told it, 1991 Guilford, editions 2002–2023, MITI in development by 2003 and in JSAT in 2005, MATCH results 1997. Spirit, ambivalence, partnership. The grammar lives next door.
