# Acclimate where you work

People "acclimate" lumber in a closed trailer
because the trailer was handy. Or in a
finishing room because that room is dry and
they like dry. Or in a house hallway because
the piece will live in a house. Then they
mill in the shop. The board learned the wrong
air.

Acclimation is not storage. It is the stock
coming to the EMC of the room where you will
cut the joint.

## The room the joint meets

If you mill and glue in the assembly shop,
the rack lives there. If you have a held
finishing room ten points drier, that room
is for film, not for the week before a
panel glue-up. A board that dried its skin
in the booth and then came back to a wetter
shop will move after you flatten it. You
will call it spring-back. It is a change
of weather.

If the piece will be assembled on site — a
built-in — the last acclimation is the
site, with time, with sticks or at least
with air around the parts. A built-in
milled in a dry shop and slammed into a
wet addition is a built-in that will show
you the addition's EMC.

## Time is thickness

4/4 on sticks in moving air can get close
in a matter of days if it was not far
away to start. 8/4 takes longer. A slab
takes a patience people put on posters
and then ignore. I will not print a
universal day-count. I will print this:
if face and witness still disagree, you
are not done. If the board just arrived
from a different climate, you are not
done because the calendar said a week.

A week is a habit that works when the
ΔMC is small. It is a superstition when
you brought river-shed oak into a
dehumidified room.

## Sticks, not a hug

A strapped bundle in the middle of the
shop is a bundle. Air cannot reach the
faces. Crack the straps, sticker, or at
least break the pile down so the boards
you will cut this month can breathe.
Leave the rest bundled if you must;
do not lie that the middle board
acclimated.

## The house-acclimation fantasy

"Let it sit in the house a week before
you install" is good for a built-in and
for a piece that arrived from a very
different shop. It is not a substitute
for shop acclimation. A dining table
that you finish in the shop and then
park in a foyer for a week will move
a little and then you will drag it
across a rug. Fine. It will not undo
a panel you glued at 12 percent MC.

Site time is a last correction. Shop
time is the job.

## What acclimation will not fix

A case-hardened board. A cup from
storage. A flatsawn cathedral you
should not have used as a slab door.
Pith. A species that moves like beech
in a design that moves like plywood.
Time in the right air is mandatory
and not magic.

## Ticket line

`In shop air from [date]. Assembly RH
[x]. MC in / MC glue.` If those dates
are the same afternoon as delivery
from the yard, you did not acclimate.
You unpacked. Unpacking is honest.
Call it that. Then wait where you
work, not where the trailer sits in
the sun.
