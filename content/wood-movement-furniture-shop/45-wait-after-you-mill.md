# Wait after you mill

The board came off the
planer shining. You could
glue it before lunch. The
core just met the room.
If there was a gradient —
and thick stock often has
one — you have opened a
new EMC problem and given
it a pretty face.

## Why the first flat lies

Jointing and planing
remove the skin that had
been talking to the shop.
A wetter plane underneath
starts exchanging. The
board can cup or move a
whisper by morning. A
glue-up of first flats is
a glue-up of boards that
are still settling into
the thickness you chose.

This is not mysticism.
It is the wet-core draft
wearing a cutterhead.

## A boring sequence

Mill oversize a little if
you can. Sticker in the
assembly air. Wait until
face and witness agree
better than they did.
Flatten again. Then cut
joints. Then glue.

How long: until the
board stops changing
enough to matter, not
until a poster says
"overnight." Overnight
works when the ΔMC is
small and the stock is
4/4. A slab can want
days. A board that
smiled when you ripped
it wants enough time
that the halves stop
arguing.

## Rush weeks

Wedding, holiday, a
client in town. You will
skip the wait. If you
must, mill thinner stock
that already lived in
your air, not a stranger
from the shed. Cut the
wide fields from the
rack that has been
honest for a week, not
from the bundle you
cracked this morning.
The rush is real. So is
the photograph.

## After a rip

A rip is a new face down
the core. Stiles cut from
a smiling 8/4 need sticks
and a second joint. If
you take them to the
mortiser the same hour,
you are mortising a
moving target. The
shoulder will look good
in the clamp and tired
in February.

## Glue-up morning

Meter. If the number
climbed overnight after
milling, you opened a
wet plane. Wait. If the
number held and the
boards stay flat on
sticks, glue. Write both
the mill date and the
glue date. When they are
the same date on 8/4,
you have a note for the
service call.

## What waiting is not

It is not a substitute
for travelers. A fully
settled oak top still
needs clips. Waiting
gets you a field that
is one climate, not a
field that is exempt
from the house.

The planer makes
furniture possible. It
also starts a clock.
Do not glue the first
ding of that clock.
Let the core finish
the sentence. Then
the clamps are holding
a board, not an
argument.
