# Maple, beech, and the pale shove

Pale wood photographs like
quiet. Hard maple, beech,
hickory — they look like
milk and they shove like
work. If you sized your
habits on walnut, these
species will humble a
frame.

## Maple

Sugar maple tangential about
0.00353 — in oak's
neighborhood. A 12-inch
flatsawn maple field, 4
points: about 0.17 inch.
Treat it like oak in the
groove. Treat it worse in
the finish room if you
burnish it and then raise
the grain with waterborne.
That is another series.
Here: do not give maple a
walnut gap.

Soft maple is not a holiday
either. It can cup, dent,
and take moisture in a
hurry. Meter it. Do not
grade movement by the
word maple on an invoice.

## Beech

Beech's tangential number
in the usual reprints sits
up around 0.00431. That is
a shove. European beech in
a chair factory is a whole
culture of drying and
design. A furniture shop
that buys a pretty beech
slab and builds an oak
breadboard habit will
split something.

If beech is on the ticket,
I want the product on the
scrap before I agree to a
width. I want quartered if
they insist on a slab. I
want a box if they insist
on a case opening that
cannot change.

## Hickory

True hickories sit loud —
about 0.00411 tangential
in the same tables. Hard
wearing, hard moving.
Hickory floors taught a
lot of people the
coefficient without
calling it that. Hickory
furniture wants the same
respect: travel, or a
narrower field, or a
different construction.

## Why pale tricks the eye

Oak shows pores and
cathedral drama. You
expect mood. Maple looks
even, beech looks even,
and even reads as
"furniture-dry behavior."
It is only furniture-dry
color. The Handbook does
not grade by photograph.

## Mixed pale and brown

A maple case with walnut
drawers: the case is the
loud opening if the case
is solid maple. A beech
worktop on a walnut base:
the top is the traveler.
Fasten for the shove, not
for the pretty brown
member you liked in the
yard.

## Shop habit

When the species goes
pale and dense, I size
up. I do not average
with the last oak job.
I write the coefficient
on the cut list. If I
cannot find a Handbook
line I trust, I treat it
as louder than oak until
a page says otherwise.

Pale is a color. Shove is
a number. The groove
believes the number.
So should you.
