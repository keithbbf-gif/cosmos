# Forty-two to eighteen

You held the shop. The logger sat in the
forties like a grown instrument. The oak
read eight-something. The condo you are
driving toward has been closed since
Thanksgiving, heat on, no pot on the
stove. A disc in that hall — if it is
honest — can sit in the high teens or
the twenties. `[VERIFY]` with a logger
if you are going to put their number
on a ticket. The story is the drop,
not the folklore.

Forty-two to eighteen is a ΔMC. At shop
temperature that RH drop is several
points of EMC. Several points across a
36-inch flatsawn oak top is a fraction
you can see. If you built as if the
condo were the shop, the condo will
edit the piece.

## Design for the drop you can name

If you know the house runs dry, you
either build drier — hold the shop
lower, acclimate longer in a dry
room — or you build more travel:
panel gaps, slotted tops, drawer
clearance that looks loose in your
forties and correct in their
twenties. I would rather the drawer
look easy on delivery than locked
in January. I would rather a winter
stripe at a panel than a split
stile.

If you do not know, say so on the
card and design a middle plus
mercy. Then offer a first-season
look.

## The truck is not a climate you own

A metal box on a January highway is
cold and dry. A metal box on an
August highway is an oven. Blanket
the piece. Do not leave it in the
sun in the parking lot while you
find the freight elevator. Do not
shrink-wrap a solid-wood piece for
a week "to keep it clean." Plastic
is a greenhouse and a sweatbox.
A day for rain is manners. A week
is a different EMC.

## What you say in the elevator

Not a lecture. "The house is drier
than the shop. The top may show a
little winter gap. Call us if
something opens or a drawer will
not pass." Then you look at the
vent over the sideboard. If the
vent is writing on the piece, you
move the piece or you move the
air. That is part of delivery, not
a later argument.

## Warranty at the drop

A drop you named is a drop you can
stand next to. A drop you ignored
is how "humidity" sounds like a
shrug. Write shop RH and house RH
if you measured it. Write the
fastener method. If the top was
pinned, the drop is your problem
even if the house is a desert.
If the top can travel and the
house is a desert, you are in a
care conversation, not a glue
conversation.

## Eighteen is not a moral failing

People live how they live. Some
cannot add a humidifier because
of a building, a child, a budget,
a landlord. You can still build
for them. You cannot still build
as if they lived in your rack
aisle. Forty-two to eighteen is
just a pair of readings. The
formula does not care which one
you liked. The width will follow
the house. Let the hardware
follow the width.
