# A warranty written in weather

The card that goes in the drawer is usually a paragraph about
oil soap and coasters. The crack does not read that paragraph.
The crack reads the house. If you never wrote the house into
the card, the warranty will be asked to do the writing in
January, in a voice nobody likes.

This is not a lawyer's draft. It is the shop half of a
sentence you owe before the truck leaves.

## What you can stand behind

You can stand behind a joint that was cut for movement and
then still failed because the fastener was wrong. You can
stand behind a finish that checked because you coated one
face. You can stand behind a drawer you fitted in a dry week
with no summer in the gap.

You cannot stand behind a climate you do not control. A
closed house at 20 percent relative humidity will pull
width out of oak. A sunroom that swings like a porch will
move a panel every week. A piece stored in a plastic wrap
in a storage unit is not in a house. If the card never
said those rooms are outside the job, January will put
them inside the job.

## The sentences that belong on the card

Short. Specific. Not a lecture.

Solid wood changes width with the moisture in the room. A
small winter gap at a panel edge, or a breadboard that
stands a little proud, is the piece working. Call if a
crack opens, a door will not latch, a drawer will not pass,
or a top pulls from an apron.

Keep the piece away from a heat vent, a wood stove, and a
damp slab. Do not wrap it in plastic. If you run the house
very dry or very wet, the wood will take that as an order.

Then the finish sentence you already had. Coasters. The oil
you actually used, not a brand you wish you had used.

That is enough. The card is not a course. It is a fence.

## What the ticket holds that the card cannot

Moisture content of the stock the week you milled, or the
week you glued, if those differ. Shop RH that week. Species
and cut of the wide fields. How the top is fastened. Where
the piece is going if you know. Those lines are for you
when the photograph arrives. They are also how you learn
whether your April shop and your August shop are the same
lie.

A warranty fight without a ticket is two memories. Memories
are how shops pay twice.

## "Normal movement" is not a phrase

Do not write "normal wood movement" and stop. Normal is
where arguments live. Write the costumes: proud breadboard,
panel stripe, a drawer that eases in October. Write the
failures: a split, a joint that opens and stays open, a
fastener that pulled. The first set is a call and a look.
The second set is a bench.

If you need a number, put it on the ticket as a shop
allowance — the gap you left in a 12-inch oak panel — not
on the public card as a promise of 3/16 inch in every
house. Houses do not sign your coefficient.

## Refunds, repairs, and the year

A seasonal look that you named on the card is a
conversation. A split you caused with a pinned top is a
repair you schedule. A piece that sat in a storage unit
through a Gulf summer and then went into heat is a
different job; you can still help, and you should say it
is help, not warranty theater.

Some shops offer a first-season visit. That is not
generosity. It is cheaper than a review that starts at
angry. You bring a screwdriver for the slots you should
have used, a plane for the drawer you fitted in a dry
week, and a hygrometer you leave behind if the hall is
the problem. You do not bring filler for a winter line
you intend to ignore in May.

## What you refuse to warranty

Outdoor use of an indoor finish. A painted porch bench
that was a dining chair. A solid top used as a
butcher block if you did not build a butcher block.
Modifications — a second shop that screwed the top down
"so it would stop moving." Plastic wrap. A basement that
failed the piece before the living room met it.

Write the refusals in the same calm type as the care
lines. Anger on a card reads like you expect a fight.
You are trying not to need one.

## Counsel, not this file

Anything that sounds like a legal promise gets a human
and, if the shop is a company, a person who is allowed
to write those sentences. This draft is the weather
half: the facts you will wish you had said. The card
can carry them in ordinary English. The January crack
will use whatever English you left empty.
