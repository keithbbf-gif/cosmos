# A repeatable meter protocol

A reading without a ritual is a mood. You cannot
compare moods across a week. You can compare a
protocol. This is the one I want on a furniture
rack. Steal it, rename it, but write it down so
two people in the same shop are not inventing
two oaks.

## The same meter

Pick a pinless for mapping and a pin for
arguments. Do not switch brands mid-job and
call the difference "the wood." If a meter
dies, overlap one rack with both old and new
before you trust the new numbers.

Batteries on a schedule, not when the display
dims in front of a client.

## The same setting

Species or specific gravity set before the
pad or the pins touch the board. Say the
setting out loud if you are tired. Write it.
Walnut after maple is where fiction starts.

## The same places

On a board you will cut up: both ends, middle,
waste face. On a glued panel: two corners and
a center, underside if the show face is
finished. On a rack map: one place per board
plus a second place on anything that looks
like a different bundle.

Chalk the wet ones. Do not trust memory of
"the dark board."

## The same words

`Date. Job. Species. Cut. Thickness. Meter
(pin/pinless). Setting. Face / end / witness.
MC high / MC low. Shop RH. Shop °F.`

That is one line, or two. It is enough. If
you add feelings ("seemed dry"), put them
after the numbers so they cannot replace
them.

## When to measure

When the stock comes in. When it has sat in
your air long enough to matter — days for
4/4, longer for 8/4. The morning you mill.
The morning you glue if those are different
mornings. After a storm on an open stack.
Before delivery if the finishing room is a
different climate from the assembly floor.

Do not measure once at the yard and live on
that number for six weeks of a wet shop.

## What you do with a fight

If pin and pinless disagree by more than you
can stand — I get restless at a point, and
suspicious at two — check setting, check
temperature, check whether you are on a glue
line or an edge. Then believe the wetter
honest reading for design. Gaps sized to the
dry fantasy are how stiles split.

If two boards in one glue-up disagree, the
wetter board is the one that will move after.
Sticker them together until they stop
arguing, or reject the stranger.

## Teaching it

An apprentice can run this protocol in a
week. What they cannot run is your hope.
Stand there for the first racks and make
them write the high number. Shops hide the
high number because it delays the job. The
job will wait either now or in January. Now
is cheaper.

## The book is the instrument

Meters get dropped. People leave. The book —
or a sheet in the job folder — is how a
service call in February can see what you
believed in October. Without the book, you
will remember eight. Everyone remembers
eight. Eight is the folklore MC of American
furniture. Some of those eights were eleven.
The protocol is how you find out which.

## A book that saved a kitchen

Six drawer boxes, solid
sides, fitted in a dry
week. The book said shop
38 percent RH, sides 7.1
to 7.4 pinless, maple
setting. August locked
two of them. We knew we
had fitted thin. We
planed runners and wrote
the month. Without the
book we would have
argued about "the wood."
With the book we owned
the fit and the client
stayed a client.

## What not to put in the book

Feelings first. A sketch
of a cathedral. A brand
loyalty paragraph. Put
those after the numbers
if you need them. The
protocol is boring on
purpose. Boring is
comparable. Comparable
is how two Tuesdays
talk.
