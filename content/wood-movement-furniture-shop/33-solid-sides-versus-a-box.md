# Solid sides versus a box

A chest can be four solid boards
and a back, grain running the
way the log did. A chest can be
a plywood box with solid faces
hung on it. Both are furniture.
They are not the same humidity
machine. Mixing their fastening
rules is how a pretty oak side
splits along a plywood web.

## The solid side

Grain usually runs vertically
on a case side you care about
looking like a tree. Then the
*depth* of the case is the live
width — front to back. A 16-inch
deep oak side through a few
points of moisture is a
fraction the back and the
web frames have to allow.
If the back is nailed hard
on four edges like a crate,
the side will bow or the
back will split, or both.

Grain running horizontally
makes the *height* live. Then
the face frame and the top
are the argument. I want the
arrow on the drawing before
anyone cuts dados.

## The box

Plywood, if it is honest
plywood, shrugs at the year.
You still give it a little
room for a back, you still
finish faces so a veneer
does not take water from
one side only, and you still
do not leave it on a wet
slab. But you can screw a
web in and expect the
opening to be there in
August.

A solid face on a plywood
box is a common, decent
peace: the climate lives
in the box, the tree lives
on the show. Tell the
client if they paid for
"solid oak chest" and you
built a box. The word
solid has been abused. Do
not add to that.

## Web frames and dust panels

In a solid case, a web
frame is a rectangle of
quiet members. Fasten it
so the sides can change
depth or height. Slots.
Buttons. A glue block
only at a centerline.
In a plywood box, a web
can be glued and you can
go home.

Dust panels in grooves
follow the same treaty as
door panels if they are
solid. Plywood dust
panels are why people
sleep.

## Backs

A solid back in a solid
case is a wide panel. Frame
it or float it. A plywood
back in a solid case is
wise if you let the sides
move — slots or a rabbet
that does not pin both
edges. A plywood back
nailed tight to a solid
case is a clamp.

## Drawers and doors

Openings in a solid case
change if the case changes.
Openings in a box mostly
do not. Fit drawers to the
construction you built,
not to the construction
you wish you had sold.

## The honest drawing

`Sides: solid oak, grain
vertical, depth live, back
floated.` or `Box: maple
ply, solid oak faces,
openings fixed.` Two
sentences. A shop that
will not write them will
meet them in a split
along a dado that was
cut as if both sentences
were true.

## A dado that split a side

Solid oak sides, grain
up, depth 16 inches.
Plywood web frames glued
into dados the full
depth. January pulled
the sides in. The dados
held. The side split
along a web. That is
not mysterious oak. That
is a box fastener in a
solid machine. The redo
used slots in the webs
and a floated back. Same
sides. Same year. The
machine matched.

## Selling the word solid

If the client wants to
see a tree on the side
and wants drawers that
never change, you can
do a solid show and a
box interior — or you
can do solid throughout
and size the openings
for travel. What you
cannot do is take the
price of solid and the
habits of a box. Write
the machine on the
quote so the word solid
does not have to do two
jobs.

Pick a machine. Build it.
The year only inspects
the one you actually
fastened.
