# Cup, bow, twist, crook

Someone says the top warped.
That word is a junk drawer.
Pull the four names out and
look at the board. The year
only owns some of them. The
log and the rack own the
rest.

## Cup

The face went hollow or
crowned across the width.
Rings, a moisture gradient,
a naked back. This is the
seasonal one shops mean
most often. Fix the climate
and the faces, or live
with a shallow cup, or
rebuild. Do not belt a
cathedral into a veneer.

## Bow

The face went curved along
the length. A long top
that rocks on the ends or
the middle. Storage on a
soft pile, a board that
was never straight, a
finish that treated a
long face differently
from its back. Moisture
can bow a board if one
end lived over a vent
and the other did not.
A bow is not a
coefficient across the
width. Slots will not
pull a bow out of a
stile. Jointing might,
if there is thickness
to spend.

## Twist

A propeller. Two corners
up, two down. Reaction
wood, a bad dry, a
board that should have
been shorter parts. You
can sometimes joint it
into a thinner flat
board. You cannot
humidity it away with a
logger. If a door
twists, check whether
the frame is a twist or
the slab is. A twisted
frame is a joint problem
or a case that is not
square.

## Crook

The edge left the chalk
line along its length.
A sprung edge. Ripping
a stressed board, or a
board that dried with
one edge open to air.
You joint the edge
again. You do not
spritz it and wait for
August unless you like
disappointment.

## How to look

Winding sticks for twist.
A straightedge across
the width for cup. A
straightedge along the
length for bow. The
edge against a known
straight for crook. Do
this in raking light.
Do it before you blame
the house.

## What the year owns

Cup from a naked back
and a dry hall. Cup
from a flatsawn field
that was always going
to move. A bow that
appeared after one end
sat on a cold slab.
A door that cups in
the field because you
finished one side.

## What the year does not own

A twist that was in
the rack. A crook from
a rip you took to the
tablesaw the same hour.
A bow in a board you
stored on two sawhorses
six feet apart. Those
are shop geometry.
Call them that.

## The wrong fix

Wetting a twisted stile
and clamping it to a
"correct" shape is how
you invent a spring
that returns in a week.
Filler in a cupped top.
A tighter screw on a
bowed lid. Name the
warp first. Then decide
if you are in a climate
draft or a milling
draft.

Four names. Use them.
Warp can stay in the
junk drawer. The bench
needs a noun it can
plane or a noun it
should not.
