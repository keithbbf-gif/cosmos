# Stickering like you mean it

A board left on a concrete floor takes the
floor's weather on one face. A bundle left
strapped takes almost no weather at all,
then a lot at the edges. Stickers are the
boring way to give every face the same air.
Boring is the point.

## Alignment is the job

Sticks of the same thickness, dry, not
infected with a stain you do not want on
oak. Stack them over each other, over
bolsters that are not a sagging 2x4 in
the middle of an eight-foot pile. If the
sticks wander, each board is a bridge on
a different pier. That is how you buy
bow you will later call "the wood."

Spacing: close enough that thin stock
does not sag, not so close that air
cannot move. Furniture 4/4 will forgive
a lot if the stack is plumb and the
sticks agree. A slab will not forgive a
soft middle.

## Air has to move

A stickered pile against a plastic
curtain with no path through is a pretty
diagram. A fan that stirs the aisle
helps. A fan that blasts one face of the
pile makes a gradient. Cross-aisle air,
not a leaf blower aimed at walnut.

Outdoors under a roof: same rules, plus
sun on one side is a climate. Shade the
stack or accept a color and moisture
story you did not meter.

## Sticker stain

Some species and some stickers leave a
shadow. Oak is famous for it if the
sticks are wet or the air is still and
the tannin has time. Dry sticks, a
little air, and do not use mystery
scrap that lived in a compost of
walnut dust. If a client is buying a
clear dining field, sticker stain is
not "character" unless you sold it
that way.

## Finished parts

Doors on sticks. Tabletops on sticks,
or on a rack that lets both faces
breathe. A stack of finished doors
kissing each other is how you print
the weather into a film and how you
trap moisture on a face you already
sealed. The finish slowed the trip.
It did not cancel the need for air.

## The hug

Straps for travel. Sticks for living.
If you must keep a bundle tight for
space, break out the boards you will
cut this month. The rest can wait in
a hug if you are honest that they
are not acclimated. A shop that
stickers the show pile for visitors
and hugs the working pile is a shop
that photographs well and glue-ups
badly.

## Floors and walls

Concrete wicks and cools. Sleepers
under the first layer. A board
leaned on a wall dries on one face
and bows. Short-term is life.
Overnight before a glue-up is how
a flat rail becomes a spring.

## A five-minute inspect

Walk the ends. Look for the board
that has already cupped in the pile
— dirty sticker line, new light at
the edge. That board is telling you
the stack failed or the board was
never a pile citizen. Pull it. Do
not hope the clamps will vote it
flat for a year.

Stickering is not a rustic craft
photo. It is furniture climate at
the scale of a stick. Do it like
you mean the EMC you wrote on the
wall. The wall cannot reach the
middle board without you.
