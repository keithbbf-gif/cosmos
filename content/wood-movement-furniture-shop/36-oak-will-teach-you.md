# Oak will teach you

This county grows oak and this
county builds with oak. That is
lucky and it is a school. Oak
moves enough that a sloppy gap
shows. It does not move so much
that you cannot build furniture.
If you learn the product on oak,
you will stop believing walnut
is "stable" in a way that lets
you pin it.

## The numbers you already met

Flatsawn white oak, tangential
about 0.00365. Red oak about
0.00369. A 12-inch width, 4
points of MC: on the order of
0.18 inch. Radial is roughly
half. Quartersawn oak is how
you buy a calmer width and
rays if the species has them.

Red and white are not the same
tree in a glue pot or a finish
room. They are close enough in
movement that I size gaps like
oak, not like pine, when either
is on the ticket.

## Why oak teaches

Because shops try to treat it
like a picture: cathedral
field, breadboard for looks,
screws at the ends. Oak will
open a glue line and not
apologize. Because clients
know the word oak and expect
it to behave like a table
their grandmother had — which
often *did* move, and they
forgot the winter stripe.
Because tannin and moisture
together stain iron, so a
wet shop and a cheap clip
become a black fingerprint
you will blame on humidity
if you are lazy. The clip
was the humidity's partner.

## White versus red, movement only

White is a little more radial
in some tables, a little
different in pores and in
how it takes finish. Do not
let that become a story that
white does not move. I have
seen white oak breadboards
blow the same as red when
the outer third was glued.
The teacher is the cut and
the fastening, not the
folk difference between the
two.

## Quartered as a tuition payment

You pay more. You get less
cup, less width change, a
face that reads as furniture
in a quieter voice. Use it
where a slab is stubborn —
a wide door you will not
frame, a lid you cannot
cleat honestly. Do not use
it as permission to skip
slots. Radial is smaller.
It is not a lock.

## Oak in mixed pieces

An oak case with walnut
drawers, a walnut top on
oak aprons — two
coefficients. Fasten for
the louder one. A walnut
field in an oak frame is
usually mercy in the
groove. An oak field in a
walnut frame is the
opposite. Do the product
for the field, not for
the pretty member.

## What oak will not teach

Softwoods. They move less
in the Handbook table and
then they cup, dent, and
take moisture faster
because they are a
different sponge. Beech
and hickory. They shove
harder than oak. If oak
is your only teacher, you
will undersize those
gaps.

Still: start with oak.
Write the 0.18 inch on a
12-inch field until you
can feel it without the
scrap. Then the quieter
woods stop tricking you
into locks. Oak is not
mean. Oak is audible.
Listen before you pin it.

## Tannin is not movement

A black ring around a
cheap steel clip on
oak in a wet week is
iron and tannin. People
file it under humidity
because both involve
water. Clean it as a
metal problem. Use a
clip that can live
there, or a wood
button. Do not add
gap to a door because
a clip stained. The
gap is a different
sentence.

## A 12-inch field until it is boring

Write `12 × 0.00369 × 4
≈ 0.177` on a scrap
until you can see
three-sixteenths
without the pencil.
Then a 24-inch field
is obviously a
three-eighths
conversation. Then a
walnut 12-inch is
obviously smaller.
Oak as a teacher is
that scrap, not a
speech about
character. Character
is what people say
after they skipped
the scrap.
