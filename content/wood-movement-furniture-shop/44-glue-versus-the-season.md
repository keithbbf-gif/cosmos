# Glue versus the season

A bottle on the bench will
take the blame for a January
hairline. Sometimes it
earned it: starved joint,
burnished cheek, open time
you ignored in August heat.
Often the bottle did the
job and the year undid the
drawing. Glue is not a
climate control.

## What glue can do

On a long-grain to
long-grain shoulder, a
good film is stronger than
the fiber next to it. That
is why a mortise stays a
mortise. On a panel glue-up
of matching grain, the
lines hold and the whole
width travels together.
That is a field, not a
fight.

Hide glue can let go with
heat and moisture in a
repair-friendly way. PVA
creeps a little under
long stress. Polyurethane
foams into gaps and then
you think a gap was a
joint. Epoxy fills and
holds and does not forgive
a dirty cheek. None of
that is a slot.

## What glue cannot do

It cannot make a
breadboard and a field
agree about length versus
width. It cannot pin a
top to an apron at both
ends and smile in
February. It cannot glue
a panel around its four
edges in a groove and
call the stile safe.
Creep is not a design
allowance. If you are
counting on PVA to ooze a
season, you are counting
on a mess.

## Open time is weather

August heat and wet air
change what the bottle
printed on the label.
January dry heat shortens
some films and fools you
into a starved joint that
looks closed. That is
glue work in a climate,
not climate work via
glue. Size the joint for
the bottle you have that
day. Size the *travel*
for the year.

## Glue in the groove

A dab that acts like a
bumper is not glue. A
squeeze that locks a
field is. If you use
space balls, they are
not adhesive. If you
pin a panel at one
center, that is a
location, not a
perimeter. The season
will test the perimeter.

## Repair instinct

The wrong instinct is
more glue in the outer
third. The right one is
to free the travel and
glue only the joint that
was supposed to be
glued. If the cheeks
show torn fiber, the
bottle held. If they
show a glossy film, you
never had a joint — fix
that, and still do not
use the bottle as a
clamp across the grain.

## Ticket grammar

`Glue: [which] on frame
shoulders only. Field
floated. Top travelers.`
If the ticket says
`glue well` you have
written a shrug. Well
is not a direction.

## Hide pot, PVA bottle, epoxy syringe

I like hide on chairs
and on work I may take
apart. I like PVA on
panels that are a
field, not a fight. I
like epoxy when I am
filling a void I have
already named as a
void, not when I am
trying to freeze a
width. Polyurethane
that foams into a
gap I should have
cut again is how
shops hide a bad
shoulder and then
blame January. The
season did not foam.

If two bottles are
on the bench, the
ticket says which
one met which joint.
"Glue" is not a
species.

## Clamp time is not a climate

Overnight in the
clamps does not
equalize a wet core.
It equalizes pressure
on a film. People
leave panels in
clamps for days
"to be sure" and
then unclamp into a
different RH than
the glue-up morning.
The film is fine.
The width is already
traveling. Unclamp
into the same aisle
you glued in, on
sticks, not into a
finishing booth that
is ten points drier.

## The bottle on the service call

If you open a joint
and see torn fiber,
say the glue held.
Then free the
travel. If you see
gloss, say the glue
never bit. Then
remake the joint
*and* check the
drawing for a
cross-grain span.
Two findings can
live in one piece.
The call should
name both.

The season always
shows up. Let it meet
a slot. Let the bottle
meet a shoulder. When
those two jobs get
swapped, January
writes the review and
it will use the word
glue whether the
bottle deserves it
or not.
