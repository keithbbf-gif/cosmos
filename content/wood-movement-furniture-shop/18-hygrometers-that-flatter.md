# Hygrometers that flatter

The disc by the door says 45 percent every day
you look at it. January. August. The week the
dehumidifier died. That disc is not broken in
the way that would make you throw it away. It
is broken in the way that makes you keep it.
It has learned your favorite number.

## Placement is half the lie

A hygrometer over a heater reads the heater. One
on a south wall in the sun reads a porch. One
in the office reads the office. The boards live
in the rack aisle, near the floor and the
doorway that opens on a wet afternoon. Put the
instrument where the sticks are. If you have
two rooms — assembly and finish — you have two
climates. One disc cannot cover a pride.

Height matters. Dry heat stacks up. A slab on
the floor can be in a different RH than your
face at the bench. I want a logger or a decent
hygrometer at rack height, not on a nail you
chose because the stud was handy.

## Cheap discs and honest loggers

Hair-element and unbranded coils drift. Some
are born optimistic. A small data logger that
writes a week of RH and temperature is worth
more than a pretty analog face you never
check against anything. The logger will show
you the night the shop goes wet after you
leave, which is the night the doors swell.

If you keep an analog because you can see it
from the saw, salt-check it or sit it next to
the logger for a week. `[VERIFY]` with a
saturated salt jar if you trust your hands
to run that test; if you do not, pair two
instruments and believe disagreement.

## Temperature is on the same card

EMC cares about temperature. A 45 percent RH
at 55°F is not the same destination as 45
percent at 80°F. Write both. A shop that
"holds 45" in a building that swings twenty
degrees is not holding an EMC. It is holding
a slogan.

## The vent, the open door, the wet slab

A bay door open on a Gulf afternoon spikes
RH in the front aisle and leaves the back
room alone. A concrete slab after a wash
throws water into the first two feet of air.
A finishing booth with exhaust can be drier
than the assembly floor by enough to matter
to a fresh film and to a board you moved
"just for the day."

One reading at 10 a.m. at the coffee pot is
a diary of the coffee pot.

## What to write on the wall

Not a single number. A range you actually
fight for, and the date you last checked the
instrument. `Target 40–50% RH at the rack.
Logger A. Salt-checked [date].` When the
logger says you left 50 for 62 all week,
that is a maintenance job, not a mystery
about oak.

## Clients and kitchen discs

The same flattery happens in houses. A gift
hygrometer on a fireplace mantel reads the
fireplace. If you ask a client for a number,
ask where the disc lives and whether it ever
changes. A logger in the hall for a week,
with permission, is a kinder witness than an
argument about how dry the house "feels."

Feel is how flattering instruments survive.
The rack does not feel. It swells. Give it
an instrument that can be wrong in both
directions, and check it as if it might
like you too much. It might.
