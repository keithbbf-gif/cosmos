# A breadboard that doesn't blow

The tenon splits along the shoulder in
February and everyone says breadboards
are trouble. Breadboards are trouble
when they are asked to be clamps. They
are quiet when they are asked to be
caps.

## The job, again

The field changes width. The breadboard
does not change length. The joint
between them must hold the cap on and
let the field travel. That is a tenon
or a long tongue, glued hard in the
center, free toward the edges.

How much center depends on the width
and the species. A narrow cherry hall
top can take more glue than a 42-inch
red oak dining field. I will not print
a universal six inches and walk away.
I will print this: if the outer third
of a wide field is glued, you have
built a splitter. If nothing is glued
and every pin is tight, you have built
a cap that can walk off-center and
then split when a pin refuses.

## Pins that walk

Elongated holes in the tenon, or in
the breadboard, depending on how you
like to work. The pin is tight in one
member and can slide in the other.
Drawbore if you want the shoulder
tight — and then make sure the offset
is not a lock on the outer pins.

Screws from below in slots do the
same work and photograph worse. I
do not care about the photograph on
the underside. I care that the last
fastener can move the product you
wrote on the scrap.

## Shoulders that stay

A breadboard that opens a black line
at the shoulder is a tenon that was
never seated, or a field that cupped
and tore the glue in the center, or
a cap that was flattened after the
joint and no longer matches the
field. Seasonal proud is a step at
the *end* of the field, not a gap
at the shoulder. Learn the
difference before you sand.

## Finish before you close

The field's end grain will show in
winter when the cap stands proud.
If that end is finished, the stripe
is civilized. If it is raw, the
photograph is a tan line. Finish
the underside. A breadboard on a
naked field is a cup machine with
a traditional name.

## When not to breadboard

A plywood top does not need this
treaty. A narrow hall piece may
look better with a simple eased
end and honest end grain. A client
who wants flush in every month
needs a different construction or
a different conversation. Do not
sell a breadboard as a flattening
clamp. It will flatten the tenon
into two pieces.

## Repair without theater

If the tenon already split, stop
gluing the outer third "to tighten
it up." Free the ends. Slot what
was tight. Glue only what should
have been glued. A butterfly across
a split field is a separate
decision and a visible one. A
hidden metal plate that pins the
width is how the next split finds
a new line.

## Shop drawing

`Breadboard: glue center only. Outer
pins in slots sized to [product].
Field ends finished. Proud allowed
in dry season.` That block of text
is the joint. The pretty end grain
on the cap is decoration. Decoration
is allowed. It does not get to
override the slots.
