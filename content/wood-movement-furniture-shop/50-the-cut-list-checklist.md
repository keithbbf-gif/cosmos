# The cut-list checklist

This is the page I want on
the clipboard before a
wide field meets a saw.
It is not a poster of
virtues. It is a set of
blanks. If a blank is
empty, the joint will
fill it in January.

## Identity

- Job and destination
  (house, condo, unknown)
- Species
- Cut: flat / rift /
  quarter (from the end,
  not the sticker)
- Thickness now / after
  mill
- Which dimension is live
  (width of a panel,
  depth of a case side,
  height of a drawer
  side)

## Climate

- Date stock entered shop
  air
- Shop RH and temperature
  this week (rack, not
  the coffee pot)
- Meter: pin / pinless,
  setting, face high/low,
  witness if thick
- Target house RH if
  known
- ΔMC you are designing
  for (write the points)

## Product

- Live finished width
- Coefficient (T or R)
- Width × coeff × ΔMC =
  travel
- Mercy added
- Where the travel lives:
  groove depth, slot
  length, breadboard pin
  walk, drawer light

## Construction tells

- Field floated: yes / no
- Glue on field: none /
  center pin only
- Top travelers: kind and
  placement (tight at
  center, free at ends)
- Both faces finished
  (including underside
  and field ends)
- Back of case: floated /
  sheet / crate-nailed
  (crate-nailed is a
  stop-and-fix)

## Month

- Fit month for doors and
  drawers
- Glue date versus mill
  date (same afternoon
  on 8/4 is a flag)

## Refuse

- Any glue note that
  spans a grain
  disagreement for the
  full meeting
- Any "flush breadboard
  all year"
- Any four tight screws
  at the corners of a
  solid top
- Any "humidity: n/a"

If a refuse is on the
sheet, the sheet goes
back to the drawing.
The saw does not get
to negotiate.

## One line that must exist

`Panel, red oak, flatsawn,
11.25, T 0.00369, ΔMC 4,
travel 0.17, groove allow
0.17 + mercy, no glue on
field.`

That line is a door. A
line that says `oak
panel 11.25` is a
conversation with
August.

## After the job

Keep the clipboard page
in the folder. The
seasonal phone call will
ask what you believed.
The page is the belief.
Memory will say eight
percent and slots. The
page will say what you
actually left.

## Teaching it

An apprentice can run
this page in a week if
you stand there for the
first wide fields. What
they cannot run is your
hope that this oak is
the quiet one. Hope
does not get a blank.
Hope gets the product
or it gets a red X.

## A page that failed and why

I have seen a clipboard
with species and width
and nothing else. The
door was 11.25 oak. The
groove was whatever the
cutter left. August
split a bottom rail.
The redo had the
product on it: T
0.00369, ΔMC 4, travel
0.17, mercy a thirty-
second, no glue on the
field. Same oak. Same
shop. The page was the
difference. The year
does not grade
intentions.

## What you can leave off

Brand of glue, if the
ticket already names
the joint. The client's
favorite football team.
A paragraph about
craft. What you cannot
leave off is the live
dimension and where
the travel lives. A
beautiful cut list
that forgets fasteners
is a beautiful way to
pin a top.

## Five-minute version

If the job is a small
box with no solid
field, you still write
species and that the
openings are a sheet.
If the job has one
wide field, you write
the long line even if
you skip the rest. The
long line is the
series in one
sentence. Skip that
sentence and you have
skipped the syllabus.

If the clipboard is
full of widths and
empty of travel, the
saw is cutting a
wish. Fill the
travel blank. Then
the year has less
to inspect.
