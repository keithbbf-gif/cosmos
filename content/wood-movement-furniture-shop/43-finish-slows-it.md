# Finish slows it

People want a can that "seals
the wood" the way a jar seals
pickles. Furniture finishes
are not jar lids. They are
coats that make water take
the long road. The
destination is still the
EMC of the room. You bought
time. Time is useful. It is
not a dam.

## What a film actually does

A continuous varnish or a
built polyurethane slows
vapor. An oil that lives
mostly in the pores slows
it less. A wax on a dry
board is a polite shrug.
None of them set the
Handbook coefficient to
zero. A dining top in a
dry house will still lose
width. It will lose it
over weeks instead of
days if the film is even
and intact.

That delay is why a
finished piece can survive
a weekend in a truck
better than a raw one. It
is also why a crack in
January still shows up —
just after everyone
thought the glue had
"cured for good."

## Uneven is worse than thin

A heavy face and a naked
back is a cup machine.
The back exchanges faster.
The face holds. The board
bends. This is older than
any brand. Both faces. The
underside can be a simpler
schedule. It cannot be
air.

Edges and end grain are
fast roads. A finished
field with raw ends will
show its mood at the ends
first — proud breadboard,
checked end, a stripe.
Finish the ends.

## Cuts and wear

A film that is worn to
bare at a table edge is a
vent you did not design.
A scratch through to wood
on one face only is a
small climate. Repair is
moisture work as much as
it is color work.

## "Just oil"

Oil is honest and slow to
build a delay. A client
who wants the feel of oil
needs the care card and
the travelers underneath.
They do not get to skip
the underside. They do
not get a warranty that
reads like varnish.

A hard film on a piece
that will live next to a
stove will still lose
width. The film may
check if the wood moves
under it faster than the
film can follow. Brittle
and thick is how you
get a cracked looking
glass on a moving field.
Thin and even is kinder
to a live width.

## Shop sequence

Finish after the piece
knows your air — or
accept that a booth that
is drier than the
assembly floor will pull
a skin you then deliver
into a third climate.
Do not finish one face
"to see the color" and
leave the board on the
bench for a week. You
have started a cup.

## The sentence on the card

"The finish slows water.
It does not stop the
wood from changing width
with the house." That is
enough. If you promise
"sealed," January will
use that word against
the hairline.

## A weekend in a van

A finished table in a
closed van in July will
still move, just slower
than a raw one. The film
buys you the drive. It
does not buy you a week
in the sun at a show.
Crack the van. Blanket,
do not plastic for days.
A raw top in that van
will cup before you
unload. That is the
delay earning its keep.
It is also why a shop
that delivers raw "for
the client to oil" is
delivering a traveler
with no delay at all.

## Cuts through the film

A worn dining edge is a
vent. So is a scratch
to bare wood on a cup
that then gets worse
because one face is
now faster. Repair is
closing the vent as
much as matching color.
A table that is "just
oiled" when people
wanted a film will
need oil on a schedule
or the delay disappears
and the year speeds up.

## Thick and brittle

A heavy conversion film
— or a consumer poly
piled until it looks
like glass — can check
when the field moves
under it. The check
looks like a finish
failure. The field
moved. Thin even coats
follow a live width
better than a helmet.
Helmets belong on
plywood.

Slows is a shop word.
Stops is a catalog word.
Build the travelers as
if the can were empty.
Then the can can do its
real job: time.
