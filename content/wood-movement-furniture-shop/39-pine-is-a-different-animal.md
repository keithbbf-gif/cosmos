# Pine is a different animal

A shop that learned oak will look
at a pine coefficient and get
sloppy. Eastern white pine sits
near 0.00212 tangential in the
usual table — quieter than
walnut. Then the tabletop cups
in a week on a wet slab, or a
painted door shows every rib of
earlywood, and someone says pine
is cheap wood. Pine is fast
wood. Fast is not the same as
loud, and it is not the same as
still.

## The number and the sponge

The Handbook number is real.
A 12-inch flatsawn white pine
field, 4 points of MC, is on
the order of 0.10 inch. You
can leave a smaller groove
than oak. You still leave a
groove. What the number does
not say: pine will get to
that 4 points faster in a
changing room, and the
earlywood will take water
like a wick while the
latewood sulks. The face
goes corrugated. Waterborne
grain-raise is a pine
specialty.

Yellow pines and firs are
other animals again — louder
or denser or both. Read the
species. "Pine" on an invoice
is a family reunion, not a
coefficient.

## Furniture uses

Painted frame-and-panel with
a pine frame and a stable
panel is a decent kitchen
if you tell the truth. A
wide pine slab dining top
is a cupping class unless
you fasten it like oak and
finish both faces and accept
the dents. Knots move
differently from the clear.
A knot is a grain argument
in a circle. Do not put a
tight knot in a latch stile
and then blame February.

## Old pine and new pine

Heart pine from a salvage
beam is not a big-box 2x.
Density, ring count, and
drying history differ. Meter
the salvage. It can be drier
than your shop or wet in
the core of a timber you
thought was furniture. Rip
a witness. Do not romanticize
a wet beam into a top.

## Softwood in a hardwood shop

Aprons in pine under a
hardwood top: the aprons are
still quiet length if the
grain runs long. The top is
still the traveler. A pine
case with hardwood doors:
the case may shrug faster
than the doors. Fit the
month. Do not assume the
softer species is the
stable one.

## What I will not do

I will not treat pine as
"doesn't move" because a
table said 0.002. I will
not skip underside finish.
I will not store pine
furniture stock on a slab
and then act betrayed. I
will not mix construction
pine and furniture pine on
the same ticket without
writing which animal I
meant.

## A painted pine door in August

A slab pine door, 14
inches, flatsawn, fitted
proud in a dry January
shop, painted one face
because the back "doesn't
show" in a pantry. August
swelled the back faster
than the paint would
allow. The door cupped
into the latch. People
said pine is cheap. The
pine was fast, the film
was one-sided, and the
fit had no summer in it.
A frame-and-panel with a
sheet field would have
been the pantry. The slab
was a look. Looks need
travelers and both faces
even when the coefficient
is kind.

## Yellow pine is not white pine

Southern yellow pine in
this region is denser,
harder on tools, and a
different row in the
table. `[VERIFY]` the
species on the ticket
before you borrow white
pine's 0.00212. A
construction 2x ripped
into a "rustic" top is
not furniture KD. Meter
it. Expect pitch, knots
that move, and a cup
if you finish one face.
Call it what it is on
the care card.

## Speed on the rack

Pine will pick up a
weekend of open bay
door faster than oak.
That is useful: you
will see the shop's
week on the pine
before you see it on
the walnut. Keep a
pine witness board on
sticks in the aisle.
When it cups, look at
the logger. The witness
is cheaper than a
finished lid.

Pine is a different sponge
with a kinder coefficient.
Design for the sponge. Use
the coefficient as a
minimum, not as a nap. The
animal is fast. Fast needs
travelers and both faces
and a logger, same as oak,
only sooner.
