# The crack that wasn't your glue

The hairline shows up on a January morning, pale as a new scratch,
right down a glue line you were proud of in August. You run a
thumbnail along it. You think about the bottle. You think about
clamp time. You think about the day you rushed a panel because
the truck was coming.

The bottle is rarely the problem.

## What the crack is saying

Two boards in a glue-up are not a marriage. They are two widths
sharing a film. Across the grain, each board is still talking to
the air. If the pair left your shop around 10 or 11 percent
moisture — a real number in a South Arkansas August if the metal
building was doing what metal buildings do — and the house they
went into sits at 6 or 7 by New Year, those widths got smaller.
The glue did what glue does. It held. The wood did what wood
does. It left.

A glue line is a convenient place for that argument to appear.
People call it a glue failure because the line is where the eye
goes. Sometimes the film did starve. Sometimes the joint was
burnished and the adhesive never bit. Those are real sins and
they leave their own evidence: a clean, shiny cheek, a starved
shoulder, a clamp mark that never quite closed. A seasonal
hairline is different. The wood on both sides of the line is
still attached in places. The gap opens and closes with the
year. In June it may almost disappear and you will get a call
that it "fixed itself," which is how you know you are not
looking at a bottle that forgot its job.

## The number that belongs on the cut list

Take a 12-inch flatsawn red oak width. Tangential coefficient
from the Wood Handbook is about 0.00369 per inch of width per
1 percent moisture. `[VERIFY]` against the edition on the shelf.
Four points of moisture — 11 down to 7, or
8 up to 12 — is:

`12 × 0.00369 × 4 ≈ 0.177 in`

A strong three-sixteenths. That is not a hair. That is a gap
you can see across a dining top if the fasteners, the breadboard,
or the apron refused to go with it. A glued-up panel that was
asked to stay 24 inches wide while each half tried to shed
almost that much is a panel that will pick a glue line and
open it like a seam.

Quartersawn stock moves less across the face. Walnut and cherry
move quieter than oak. None of them move zero. The crack does
not care that the finish was expensive.

## Why August lied

August in this county is wet in the air and wet in the slab if
you let it be. A shop that feels "fine" at 70 percent relative
humidity is a shop whose furniture-dry pile is not furniture-dry.
The boards are at equilibrium with *that* air. You mill them.
You glue them. They look honest. You deliver into a house that
has been closed up with heat since Thanksgiving. The house is
not 70 percent. The house may be 30. The piece has to walk that
gap without a vote.

People blame the client for "running the heat too hard." The
client lives in a house. You built for a shop. Those are two
climates. If you only built for one, the crack is your
handwriting.

## What glue actually does in this story

Hide glue, PVA, polyurethane, epoxy — they fail in different
moods, and that is another draft. Here the point is narrower.
A good glue line is stronger than the fiber next to it in
shear, on a good day, on a test block. Seasonal movement is
not a shear test. It is a slow change of width that the joint
either accommodated or stored as stress. Stored stress looks
for the weakest story. Sometimes that is a glue line that was
only all right. Sometimes it is a check that was already in
the board. Sometimes it is the first fastener that pinned a
top to a rail as if the top were plywood.

If you want to know whether the bottle failed, look at the
cheeks when you have the right to open the joint. Fibers torn
out of the face mean the glue held and the wood left. A glossy
film with no fiber means you never had a joint. Do not guess
from the hallway.

## The call you make

You do not start the call with the word humidity. You start
with the piece. You ask whether the gap was there in October.
You ask whether the house runs a humidifier, or a wood stove,
or a vent that dumps dry air on the sideboard. You do not
accuse. You also do not pretend a dab of filler is a repair.
Filler in a seasonal crack is a wedge you will be asked to
explain in May.

If the top is still fastened tight to an apron, the first
kindness is to free it — slotted screws, figure-eights, buttons
— so the next winter is not a replay. If the panel is a door
and the crack is a glued-in field, you are in a different
conversation. That field was never supposed to be glued.

## What you write down next time

Moisture content of the stack, date, species, cut. Relative
humidity of the shop that week, not the week you bought the
hygrometer. Destination if you know it: a condo with sealed
windows is not a farmhouse hall. Width of the solid field.
The gap you left, in fractions you can measure with a rule,
not a vibe.

The crack in January is a report. It is late, and it is
honest. The bottle will take the blame if you let it. The
air already did the work.
