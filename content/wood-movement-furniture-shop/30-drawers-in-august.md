# Drawers in August

The drawer that ran like glass on
delivery day stops halfway in a wet
week and someone learns to lift or
to hip-check. They will not call it
humidity. They will call it a cheap
drawer. Sometimes they are right
about the cheap. Sometimes they are
right about a January fit.

## Sides are widths

A solid drawer side is a board
standing on edge. Its *height* is
the live dimension. Across 5 or 6
inches of oak, a few points of
moisture is not half an inch, but
it is enough to eat a proud fit.
The opening — a web frame, a case
side, a pair of runners — is
either quiet (plywood, a frame)
or it is moving on a different
schedule.

If you fit a solid side to a
plywood case in a dry week with
no daylight, August will take the
daylight from the grain.

## Where the swell shows

Runners and kicker. The bottom
edge. The top edge kissing a
divider. A wide false front that
binds on a neighbor. A solid
bottom in grooves that was
glued like a panel — that is a
different failure, a split
bottom or a bowed one.

Plywood sides in a plywood case
are a humidity shrug. Solid
sides in a solid case are two
widths that may agree or may
not, depending on grain
direction. A chest with solid
sides and drawers that run on
those sides is a conversation
you have on the drawing, not
at the vise in August.

## Fit by the month

January fit: leave more gap than
your pride wants. August fit:
you may plane a whisper after a
wet week, and you should have
left the whisper in January
instead. Write the month on the
ticket. An apprentice who only
fits in the dry season will
fill a house with hip-checks.

I like to see a little light at
the top of a solid-side drawer
in a dry shop. Not a rattle that
says the runner missed. A light
that says August is invited.

## Bottoms

A solid bottom in grooves needs
the same treaty as a door panel
if the bottom is wide. A plywood
bottom is why most shops sleep.
If you sell a solid bottom, size
the grooves and do not glue the
field. A bottom glued on four
edges is a shallow box lid
waiting to split.

## Slides

Mechanical slides want a stable
box. If the box changes height
and width, the slide takes the
argument and then the screw
holes do. Use slides with a
solid-wood box only if you
understand which member is
allowed to move. Many shops
build a plywood box and a solid
front. That is not a moral
compromise. That is how the
August hip-check stays off the
warranty card.

## The call

Do not start with "humidity."
Start with "the sides grew."
Then the month you fitted.
Then whether you will plane a
runner, wait for October, or
rebuild a box that was never
going to pass. If the house is
a greenhouse, say that after
you have owned the fit.

A drawer is a small climate
report. August reads it out
loud. Write August into the
gap while the shop is still
quiet.
