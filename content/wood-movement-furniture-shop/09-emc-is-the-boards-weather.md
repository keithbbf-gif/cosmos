# EMC is the board's weather

A board on sticks is not waiting for your schedule. It is
waiting for the air. Given enough days, it will settle at
a moisture content the air is willing to live with. That
settling point is equilibrium moisture content. EMC. It
is not a brand. It is a forecast.

## The air names the number

Relative humidity and temperature together set EMC. In
shop-temperature air, a Handbook table looks something
like this — `[VERIFY]` the cell against the edition you
hold, because temperature slides the rows:

At about 70°F, 30 percent RH sits near 6.2 percent EMC.
40 percent RH near 7.7. 50 percent near 9.2. 60 percent
near 11. 70 percent near 13.1. 80 percent near 16.

A furniture shop that holds 40 to 50 percent RH is asking
the rack to live around 8 to 9 percent moisture. A house
that sits at 30 percent in January is asking the same
piece to go toward 6. A porch in August is asking for a
number you do not want in a dining top.

The board does not jump. Thick stock lags. A finish slows
the trip. The destination does not change because you are
in a hurry.

## Why EMC is kinder than "the wood is dry"

Dry is an argument. Eight percent is a sentence. A meter
on a board in a 70 percent shop that reads 8 is a board
that is not done, or a meter that is wrong, or a core
that has not heard about the surface yet. A meter that
reads 11 in that shop is a board that may already be
home. Home for that shop. Not home for a sealed condo.

EMC lets you stop using dry as a moral word. The board
is either near the air or it is still traveling.

## Time is part of the weather

A 4/4 board on sticks with air around it will approach
EMC in days to a couple of weeks, depending on the
species and the starting point. A 8/4 slab will take
longer. A sealed crate will take a season and then
surprise you. A finish on all six sides will stretch the
trip so far that seasonal change becomes a slow breathe
instead of a slam — still a breathe.

If you move stock from a wet shed into a held shop, the
EMC changed when the door closed. The meter will not
agree with the wall for a while. That disagreement is
the work of acclimation, not a defect in the oak.

## Shop wall, house wall

Two EMCs matter. The one your rack is heading for, and
the one the piece will head for after delivery. If those
are close, the ΔMC you design for is small. If you build
in a wet metal building and deliver into January heat,
the ΔMC is the job. Frame-and-panel, slotted tops,
drawer gaps — all of that is just EMC math wearing
joinery.

A client hygrometer in the hall is not a gadget sale. It
is how you both stop guessing which weather the board is
being asked to become.

## What EMC will not do

It will not flatten a board that was cupped in the log.
It will not turn flatsawn into quartersawn. It will not
save a pinned top. It will not make a wet core into a
dry face because the wall said 45 percent. EMC is the
destination. The trip still needs sticks, time, and a
meter that is allowed to disagree with your hope.

## Write the forecast

On the rack tag: date in, species, thickness, shop RH,
target EMC, last meter reading. On the job ticket: shop
EMC the week you glued, house target if you know it. You
are not running a lab. You are refusing to pretend April
and August are the same air.

When someone says the shop "feels fine," look at the
wall and then at the table. Feel is a comfort. EMC is
the weather the board believes.
