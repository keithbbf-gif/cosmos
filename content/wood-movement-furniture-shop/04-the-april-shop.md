# The April shop

There is a week, some years two, when the shop feels like a
house and the house feels like a shop. The metal roof is not
popping. The dehumidifier is not roaring. The heater is off.
You walk in and the hygrometer is sitting in the forties as
if it had always been there. Boards on the rack read like
the boards you meant to buy. Drawers you fitted in a panic
in February suddenly run.

That week is a liar if you let it be the climate you design
for.

## Why April feels like competence

In this county, April is a truce. The Gulf is not yet sitting
on the building. The heat is not yet stripping the air out
of a closed room. Lumber that has been on sticks since winter
is closer to the middle of its year. You glue up and the
panel stays where you left it. You think you have gotten
better.

You have gotten luckier with the air.

A piece built entirely in that truce — milled, glued, fitted,
finished, delivered — is a piece whose first winter and first
August will be news. The shop did not teach it anything. The
house will.

## The other shops inside the same walls

January shop: heater on, air dry, finishes flash, solid stock
reads low if it has been inside, and any board you drag from
a damp shed is a stranger. You can fit a drawer like a piano
key and watch it lock in June.

August shop: the same walls, a different country. Doors swell
in the rack. Waterborne grain-raises like it remembered it
was a plant. A top you flattened on Tuesday has a whisper of
cup on Friday if one face took the afternoon and the other
took the floor.

November shop: people want delivery before a holiday. The
finishing room is warm. The truck is cold. The house is
already closed. You are moving a piece across three climates
in an afternoon and calling it a drop-off.

If your habits only work in April, you do not have habits.
You have a season.

## What to steal from April

The number on the wall. If April sits near 45 percent and
the house you deliver to is supposed to live near that, you
have a target. Write it down. Then spend money and attention
to keep January and August from wandering so far that the
wood has to do all the traveling.

April is also when you catch up the rack. Sticker what you
will cut in the wet months. Reject the board that already
moved in the pile — the one with the dirty sticker mark and
a new gap — before it becomes a door.

## What not to steal

Do not treat April flush as a standard you owe in August.
Do not take the dehumidifier offline because the week was
pretty. Do not mill a dining top from stock that "felt dry"
because the air felt kind. Feel is how April gets into the
cut list.

A lot of shops do their best work in the truce and their
warranty work in the extremes. That pattern is not a mystery.
It is a calendar.

## Building as if the other months exist

Hold a shop range you can defend in July and in January,
even if you cannot hold April's manners all year. Fit
drawers with a little summer in mind if you are fitting them
in a dry week. Leave panel gaps you can measure, not gaps
you remember from a mild Tuesday. Fasten tops for travel.
Write the destination climate on the ticket when you know
it — a lake house, a second floor with afternoon sun, a
condo that never opens a window.

If you only work in April, say so on the card and refuse
August delivery. Most of us cannot say that. So we stop
pretending the truce is the climate.

## A walk through the rack

On an April afternoon the oak looks settled. The walnut has
the color it will have in the house. The pine is still pine.
Walk the ends. Look for the board that cupped toward the
aisle. That board did not read the truce. It read whatever
it was doing last August. It is the honest one. Put it on
the bench and ask what the others will do when the truce
ends.

The April shop is a gift. Spend it on flattening, on
finishing, on the jobs that need still air. Do not spend it
on the belief that wood has calmed down for good. Wood does
not have a good. It has a year.
