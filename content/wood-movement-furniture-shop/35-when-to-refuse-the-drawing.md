# When to refuse the drawing

A drawing can be beautiful and
still be a clamp. Flush
breadboards all year. A 16-inch
flatsawn oak slab door on a
piano hinge. A solid lid with
cleats glued across the back.
A "just oil" top screwed down
through an apron like a
workbench. You can build all
of that. You will also meet
it again.

Refusal is part of humidity
work. It is not temperament.

## The tells

Grain arrows that disagree
with a glue note along the
full meeting. A panel with
no groove depth. A top with
fasteners at the four corners
and no slots. A solid case
with a back nailed like a
shipping crate. A warranty
line that says "no wood
movement" as if that were a
finish option.

If you cannot write a
scrap-paper product next to
a wide field, the drawing is
not finished. Finish it or
hand it back.

## What you offer instead

Not a lecture. A treaty.
Frame-and-panel instead of
the slab. A breadboard that
is allowed to be proud.
Plywood where the opening
must stay put. Quartered
stock if they insist on a
wide solid and will pay.
A care card that names the
winter stripe.

If they want the look of
the clamp, they can have
the look on a material
that does not move that
way. Painted MDF in a
solid frame is an honest
kitchen. It is not a
quartered oak door. Say
the sentence.

## Money is not the test

A deposit does not make the
physics kinder. The jobs
that hurt are the pretty
ones with a rush date. Rush
is how drawings skip the
groove. If the date cannot
hold acclimation, the date
is part of what you refuse.
Offer a later truck or a
different construction.

## How to say it

"This top will split if we
fasten it this way. Here is
the underside I will build.
Here is what January may
show." If they still want
the lock, you can decline.
You can also build the lock
and write that you advised
against it — and you will
still own the photograph in
the town you both live in.
I would rather lose the
job.

## What you do not refuse

A client who runs a dry
house. You can build for
that. A client who wants
solid wood. You can build
for that. A client who
wants a cathedral grain
field. You can build for
that if the fasteners
slide. Refusal is for the
drawing that will not
allow the year in. The
year is not optional.

## Shop culture

If the loudest maker in
the room calls refusal
cowardice, take them to a
January repair and a
ticket with no RH on it.
Cowardice is taking a
clamp because you liked
the deposit. Courage is a
red X on a pretty sheet
and a second sheet that
can survive August.

The drawing is a wish.
The wood is a climate.
When they fight, keep
the climate. The wish
can come back as a
treaty. Treaties last.
Clamps do not.
