# Walnut and cherry still move

A shop that has been humbled by
oak will relax when the ticket
says walnut. The cathedral is
softer, the coefficient is
kinder, the client already
thinks they bought "the stable
one." Then a breadboard glued
in the outer third splits in
February and everyone is
confused because the wood was
expensive.

Expensive is not EMC.

## The quieter numbers

Black walnut tangential about
0.00274. Black cherry about
0.00248. Against red oak's
0.00369, that is a real
discount on travel. A 12-inch
flatsawn walnut field, 4
points of MC: about 0.13
inch. Cherry a little less.
You can leave a smaller
groove. You cannot leave
zero.

A 36-inch walnut dining top,
same 4 points: about 0.39
inch. That is still a
hardware problem. Figure-8s
and slots do not get the
year off because the species
is polite.

## Cherry's other calendar

Cherry changes color in light.
That is not movement, and
clients mix the two. A
winter stripe under a
proud breadboard on cherry
can be a moisture stripe
and a sun stripe stacked.
Finish the field ends.
Tell them the color will
even up if the light is
even, and the proud will
not even up until August.

Cherry also likes to
surprise you in a kiln
story — a little more
motion after a rip than
you wanted. Sticker. Wait.
Do not let the pretty red
rush the glue.

## Walnut's other calendar

Walnut can be calmer in
the clamp and still cup
if you finish one face.
The coefficient does not
exempt the underside. Oily
spots and purple streaks
are finish problems. They
are not permission to skip
acclimation. A wet walnut
slab is a wet slab.

## Mixed with oak

Walnut top, oak base: the
top is quieter, the aprons
are still quiet length.
Fasten for the top's
travel, not for oak's, but
do not drop below a slot
you can actually see. Oak
doors on a walnut case:
the loud member is the
door field. Size those
grooves like oak.

## The trap

"It's walnut, we can glue
the breadboard." "It's
cherry, we can skip the
clips and use four screws."
I have heard both. I have
repaired both. The scrap
paper still gets a
product. If the product is
smaller, the slot can be
smaller. The word *skip*
does not appear.

## What they teach after oak

Mercy in the numbers.
Honesty in the fastening.
A client who paid for
walnut paid for color and
feel, not for a repeal of
January. Put the quieter
coefficient on the ticket
so you do not size an oak
gap and get a rattle you
did not need. Then put
travelers under the top
anyway.

## Worked widths

12-inch flatsawn walnut,
4 points: `12 × 0.00274
× 4 ≈ 0.13`. 12-inch
cherry: `12 × 0.00248 ×
4 ≈ 0.12`. 42-inch
walnut dining field:
`42 × 0.00274 × 4 ≈
0.46`. Half an inch is
still hardware. I have
watched a shop size
walnut grooves like
oak "to be safe" and
then chase a rattle
with glue. Glue in the
groove is how a quiet
species becomes a loud
failure. Size the
product. Add a little
mercy. Do not add a
second species' fear.

## Cherry in this light

South windows will
redden a cherry field
unevenly if a runner
or a vase parks in one
place. Clients mix that
blush with movement.
A winter proud
breadboard on cherry
can show a pale stripe
that is unfinished end
grain *and* a sun line
where the cap shaded
the field. Finish the
ends. Move the vase.
Do not sand the proud
to chase the blush.

## Walnut that arrived wet

A dark, pretty slab
from a yard that
stores under a roof
with no walls can read
10 or 11 while the
shop sits at 8. Walnut
does not get a shorter
acclimation because it
is expensive. Sticker
it. If it smiles when
ripped, it has a
history the stamp
omitted. Quiet
coefficient, same
sticks.

Quiet is not
still. Still is plywood,
and even plywood has a
veneer story.
