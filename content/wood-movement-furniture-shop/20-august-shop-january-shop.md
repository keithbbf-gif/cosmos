# August shop, January shop

You do not get a new building in June. You get
a new air. The same racks, the same concrete,
the same door that sticks when it wants to.
Treat August and January as two shops that
share an address. Your hands already know.
The cut list should.

## August

The air is full. Doors in the rack swell. A
lid you flattened last week has a little
weather in it. Waterborne raises grain like
it is proud of the science. Glue open-time
shortens in the heat and stretches in the
wet, depending on the bottle, and you will
swear the bottle changed. It did not. The
month did.

If you fit drawers in August, you are fitting
them fat. A snug August drawer is a locked
January drawer that then swells again next
August and teaches someone to hip-check it.
Leave a summer-fit a little easier than your
pride wants, or wait for a drier week and
remember you waited.

If you glue a wide panel in August, you are
gluing wetter stock unless you fought the
building. Size gaps for the dry house that
is coming, not for the wet shop you are
standing in.

## January

Heat is on. Air is thin. Finishes flash.
Solid stock that has been inside reads low.
Stock that just came from a shed reads like
a stranger. You can fit a drawer like
jewelry. June will close it.

A January glue-up of dry stock into a frame
is a glue-up that will grow if it ever sees
a wet house or a wet week in the truck.
Panel gaps still need the product on the
scrap. Dry-to-wet is the same formula as
wet-to-dry. The sign flips. The stile does
not care about the sign. It cares about
bottoming out.

## Habits that travel

Meter the week, not the memory of last
August. Fit doors with the other month in
the hinge gap and the latch. Fasten tops
the same way all year; the fasteners do not
get a vacation. Do not take the dehumidifier
offline because January "is handling it."
January is handling dryness. August will
not honor that contract.

Write the month on the ticket next to the
RH. Future-you on a service call needs to
know whether the drawer was born fat or
born thin.

## Delivery months are a third shop

Thanksgiving and the week before a wedding
are not climates. They are calendars. You
will move a piece from a warm finishing
room into a cold truck into a closed house.
That is three EMCs in an afternoon. Blanket
the piece, do not plastic-wrap it for a
week, and do not schedule a glue-up the
morning of the truck. The third shop is
where a lot of first cracks start. They
get blamed on Christmas heat. Some of them
started in the truck.

## Teaching the year

An apprentice hired in April thinks the
shop is the April shop. Keep them through
August and January before you let them
fit a kitchen of drawers unsupervised.
The year is the course. This draft is
only the syllabus page.

Same walls. Two climates. A third if you
deliver. Build as if all three will meet
the piece. They will.
