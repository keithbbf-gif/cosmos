# The split you can save

A crack is not one job.
Some are seasonal mouths
that will close when the
house wets up. Some are
a board that checked from
the pith. Some are a
glue line that never bit.
If you treat all three
with the same syringe of
filler, you will make a
wedge and a repeat
customer.

## First look: the year

Does the gap change with
the month? Ask. Look at
photographs if they have
them. A line that was
gone in August and back
in January wants travel
restored, not a plug. Free
the fasteners. Slot the
breadboard. Take glue out
of a groove if you can
do it without wrecking
the frame. Then let the
line be a line, or a
small finished stripe.

If you fill that line in
January, May will spit
the fill or stretch the
next fiber.

## First look: the back

Pinned top? Glued field?
Naked underside? Those
are saves you do with a
screwdriver and a
schedule, not with color.
Do the mechanical mercy
before you talk about
Dutchmen.

## A split you can glue

Fresh wood, a check that
opened along the grain
from a knock or a pith,
cheeks that will close
without a clamp that
bends the whole top —
that can take a glue
that fits the shop, a
careful close, and then
still needs travelers
so the year does not
open a parallel line.

A butterfly across a
stable field is a
visible choice. I use
it when the client
wants to see the save
and the board will not
keep opening beside the
patch. I do not use it
to hold a locked top
together. The butterfly
then becomes a witness
that we refused to
free the screws.

## A split you cannot honestly glue

A glue line with glossy
cheeks: you never had a
joint. Open it, scrape,
glue, or remake. A field
glued into a frame that
has already split a
stile: the stile is the
patient. The field needs
to be freed. A breadboard
tenon blown along its
length: remake the treaty,
do not epoxy the outer
third "tighter."

## Color and the stripe

If the save leaves a
line, finish the meeting
so the next winter is
not raw wood. Color
match is courtesy. A
perfect invisible fill
on a live width is a
promise the year will
not keep.

## When to say no

Outdoor use, a stove
vent, a piece that lived
in plastic, a client
who wants the lock left
in and the crack gone.
You can still help. Call
it help. A save that
leaves the clamp in
place is a delay.

## The order

Back first. Year second.
Glue only what is a
joint. Travel where the
drawing omitted it.
Color last. That order
is how a split becomes
a piece again instead
of a filled photograph
that fails twice.

## A split that closed in June

A dining field, red oak,
hairline along a glue
line in January, gone
enough in June that a
thumbnail barely caught
it. The underside was a
row of tight screws at
the ends. We slotted the
ends, left the line, and
did not fill. Next
January the line was a
shadow, not a mouth.
The save was the
screwdriver. Color would
have been a wedge.

## A split that needed a bench

A breadboard tenon
blown along its length,
outer third glued, pins
round and tight. No
amount of climate talk
closes a tenon that is
two pieces. We remade
the cap, glued the
center, slotted the
ends, finished the
field ends. The client
saw a new breadboard.
They also saw why the
old one failed. That
is a save. A syringe
in the split would have
been a delay with
their name on it.

If you cannot flip the
piece, you cannot sort
the split. The back is
the diagnosis. Color
is the last courtesy,
not the first tool.
