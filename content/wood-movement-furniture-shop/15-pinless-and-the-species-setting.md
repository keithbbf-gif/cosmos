# Pinless and the species setting

You set the pad on the face. You get a number. You do
not leave holes. That speed is why pinless meters live
in shops that also sell pretty. The speed is also why
whole racks get a number that belongs to last week's
pine.

## What the pad is doing

The meter throws a field into the first fraction of
the thickness — often on the order of 3/4 inch,
sometimes less, sometimes a shallow and a deep mode.
`[VERIFY]` the depth on the tool you hold. It is
reading dielectric behavior, which tracks moisture
and also tracks density. That is why the species
setting, or a specific-gravity number, is the whole
game.

A dense oak and a light poplar at the same true MC
will not look the same to a pad left on one setting.
The meter will happily give you a tenth of a percent
anyway. Precision is not accuracy. The tenth is
costume jewelry if the density is wrong.

## Settings are a shop chore

Change the setting when the species changes. If you
mill walnut in the morning and maple after lunch,
the meter changes at lunch. Write the setting on
the ticket next to the reading. "8.2 pinless" is
incomplete. "8.2 pinless, SG set walnut" is a
sentence.

Exotic and oily woods confuse pads. So do wet
finishes, metal fasteners under a veneer, and a
hand that is also in the field. Lift your off hand.
Keep the pad fully on the face. A pad hanging off
an edge reads air and then you believe the edge is
drier than the field.

## Depth is a limit, not a flaw

On 4/4, pinless is often enough. On 8/4, you are
reading a shell unless you have a deep mode and
you trust it. After a rain on a stickered pile, the
shell can be a point or two from the core. Pinless
will tell you the shell is fine. The rip will tell
you the rest. This is when you poke.

A veneered panel is a special liar. The pad sees
the face veneer and whatever glue and core live in
the field. A number on a plywood sheet is a number
on a plywood sheet. Do not treat it like a solid
oak MC.

## Fast mapping

Pinless earns its keep on a rack: every board, two
or three places, a chalk mark on the wet ones.
You will find the board that came from a different
bundle. You will find the end that sat on concrete.
You will not do that with pins on a hundred faces
anyone intends to show.

Map first. Poke the suspects. That order keeps
holes off the cathedral and keeps the pad from
being the only witness.

## Calibration theater

Some meters have a reference plate or a Cal check.
Use it when the number feels like a wish. Replace
batteries before you replace the species. A dying
battery and a wrong setting in the same afternoon
is how a shop decides all the oak is "ready."

Do not average a rack into one number for the
job ticket. The wettest board in the glue-up is
the board that will move after you think you are
done. If you change species at lunch, change the
setting at lunch and say it out loud so the next
board is not read as fiction.

## Polite is not the same as true

Clients like pinless because it does not scar.
You can demonstrate on their table without
leaving evidence. Fine. Say what you are
reading: a shell, a setting, a moment. If you
need to know whether the underside of a 5/4
top agrees, you still owe the piece a pin in
the waste or a honest shrug.

The pad is a good shop citizen. The species
button is the citizen's spine. Leave it set
wrong and you have a fast machine for
publishing fiction.
