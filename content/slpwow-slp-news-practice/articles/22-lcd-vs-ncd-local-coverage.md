Medicare coverage is not one binder. It is a national layer that is thinner than people think, and a local layer that is thicker than a traveling CF expects.

A **National Coverage Determination** (NCD) is CMS speaking for the country: this service, in these circumstances, is or is not a benefit. A **Local Coverage Determination** (LCD) is a Medicare Administrative Contractor speaking for its neighborhood, plus the billing-and-coding articles that hang off it. The Medicare Coverage Database is the public attic. You can search it. You should, before you tell a colleague “Medicare never pays for that.”

## Why SLP lives in the local attic

There is no single, famous NCD that writes the whole speech-language benefit. The Benefit Policy Manual and the therapy pages do a lot of the national talking. Then contractors write LCDs with names like “Outpatient Speech Language Pathology” and a number (L34429 is one you will see; it is *an* LCD, not *the* law of the land). Another contractor may have a different number, a different ICD list, a different mood about cognitive treatment or a second eval.

If you moved from Noridian country to Palmetto country and brought your old LCD in your head, you brought a souvenir. Souvenirs do not pay claims.

## What an LCD is allowed to be

A list of indications they consider reasonable. A list of ICD-10 codes they expect to see. A paragraph about frequency that is a *coverage* paragraph, not a clinical best-practice. A reminder that skilled documentation exists. Sometimes a tone that feels like a protocol. It is still not your protocol. It is their payment rule.

When an LCD and a society guideline disagree, you have a professional problem, not a news-brief problem. You can follow the guideline and risk the claim, or follow the LCD and write down the tension. This URL will not pick. It will say the tension is real and should be named in the department, not in a shrug.

## What “I read it on a Facebook LCD” gets wrong

Expired LCDs. Retired articles. Someone’s 2018 highlight. A hospital policy that quoted an LCD and then drifted. Always check the database for *retired* and *effective* dates. Always check whether you are looking at the LCD or the billing article — they are a pair, and people cite the wrong twin.

Home-health LCDs and outpatient LCDs are not interchangeable. SNF Part A is a different religion again (essay 19). If the URL in your download folder does not match the benefit the person is on, you are reading a neighbor’s mail.

## A way to use the database without becoming a coder

Search the contractor that actually processes *your* claims. Read the indications paragraph. Read the ICD lists as a map of what they *expect*, not as a diagnosis you must force onto a person. If the thing you want to do is absent, that is information. If it is present with a frequency sentence, that is information. Then talk to the person who owns billing. Do not invent a code pair in the stairwell.

## A hallway version

“National Medicare is a thin book. Our contractor’s LCD is the thick one. If you learned coverage in another state, look it up again.” If they want L34429 as an example of the *genre*, they can have the Coverage Database link. If they want to treat L34429 as Arkansas law, they need to check whether it is even their contractor.
