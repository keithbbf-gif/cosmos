**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

On October 1, 2019, skilled nursing facilities stopped getting paid, under Medicare Part A, as if therapy minutes were the plot. The Patient-Driven Payment Model pays five case-mix stories at once: PT, OT, SLP, nursing, and non-therapy ancillary, plus a non-case-mix sliver. Speech is not a leftover. It is one of the five.

CMS’s MLN slides and the classification walkthrough are still the public map. ASHA’s SNF page is the translation for our codes. The plot is characteristics, not a minute total.

## What actually moves the SLP group

CMS built the SLP component from traits that had predicted SLP cost:

- whether the stay’s clinical category is acute neurologic
- whether certain SLP-related comorbidities are present (ASHA lists a dozen, including aphasia, stroke, TBI, trach/vent while a resident, laryngeal or oral cancers, dysphagia, ALS, apraxia, speech-language deficits)
- cognitive impairment
- a swallowing disorder on the MDS
- a mechanically altered diet

Those combine into twelve groups, SA through SL, with case-mix indexes CMS published in the original rollout (from a low SA to an SL that was more than four). Do not tattoo a CMI on your badge. Indexes get updated. The *shape* is the news: swallow and diet are not footnotes. They are payment furniture. That is why a casually coded “chopped meat” or an unasked swallow question is not a small clerical miss. It is a mis-told story about the person, and the money follows the story.

This page will not tell you how to code the MDS. It will tell you why the SLP who never looks at Section K is reading only half the chart.

## Minutes still exist. They just do not price the day.

Section O still tracks therapy. There is still a public limit on how much of a discipline’s time may be group or concurrent (CMS has kept a 25 percent combined idea in the materials ASHA cites). Minutes can still be audited as a question about whether anyone showed up. They are not, under PDPM, the engine that sets the per diem the way RUG-IV therapy RUGs did.

That was the point. Volume incentives had a reputation. CMS said it wanted clinical characteristics. Whether buildings then under-staffed therapy is a labor story you already know. The payment model does not require that story. It also does not forbid it. News can hold both sentences.

## The SLP component does not fade on the calendar

PT, OT, and NTA get a variable per-diem slide as the stay lengthens. SLP and nursing, in the original design, do not. ASHA is proud of having fought for comorbidities and for that flat SLP line. Pride is allowed. It is not a reason to invent minutes that are not skilled.

A flat component can still be a building that asks you to “justify the group” every Tuesday. Justification is a clinical sentence. It is not a RUG minute chase in a new shirt.

## What this is not

It is not a protocol for treating dysphagia so the diet code looks a certain way. Gaming diet texture to move a CMI is the kind of idea that should die in the hallway. It is not Part B outpatient math. A resident who exhausts Part A and continues under Part B has stepped back into essays 11–18. People mash the parts because the walls did not change. The payer story did.

## A hallway version

“We’re not paid for SLP minutes under Part A anymore. We’re paid a speech component that cares whether this person is neurologic, cognitively impaired, on a changed diet, or has a swallow problem — and whether the MDS tells that truth.” If someone wants the twelve-group table, CMS already printed it. If someone wants a treatment plan, they can have a licensed clinician and a chart, not this URL.
