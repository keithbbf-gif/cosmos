Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

KX is two letters that mean: we are over a published dollar, and we are saying the record still supports medical necessity. That is CMS’s idea, not a clinic slogan.

For calendar year 2026 the dollar is $2,480 for physical therapy and speech-language pathology *combined*, and $2,480 for occupational therapy alone. CMS printed it on the Therapy Services page, in CR language (r13437cp), and in MM14315. ASHA printed it again. If you are reading this after a later CR, do not trust the integer. Trust the live page.

Claims over the threshold *without* the modifier, CMS says, are denied. The denial is for a missing attestation, not for a clinical decision that the person is “done.”

## Combined is the word that bites SLPs

PT and SLP share a bucket. OT has its own. A long PT episode can put a new SLP eval on the wrong side of the line before you have met the person. That is not a conspiracy. It is how the old cap was structured, and the threshold inherited the structure.

If your software shows “SLP cap remaining” as if it were a private pot, the software is telling a 1997 story. Ask whoever owns the software which claims are actually in the combined total. This page will not run your report.

## What the letter is attesting

Not “we like this patient.” Not “the family asked.” Necessity as Medicare means it: skilled, reasonable, documented. The record has to be able to take a question. If the record cannot, the modifier is not a charm. It is a flag on a thin file.

ASHA’s wording is the right temperature: the modifier demonstrates continued medical need. CMS’s wording is colder: confirmation that services are medically necessary as justified in the record. Neither of them is a protocol for how you write the note. Both of them are a reason not to treat KX as a clerical afterthought.

## What you must not hear

You must not hear “stop at $2,480.” That is the ghost from essay 11.

You must not hear “always slap KX on everything in December.” That is how people get conversations with auditors.

You must not hear, from this URL, a step-by-step on which line to mark. MACs and your compliance people own that. We own the distinction.

## The other dollar is not this dollar

Targeted medical review sits at $3,000 for PT+SLP combined (and $3,000 for OT) through CY 2027 as CMS has been printing it, then MEI after that. Essay 16. People mash the two numbers because both showed up when the cap died. They are different jobs. KX is an attestation on the claim. The $3,000 line is a *maybe they will look* line. You can be over KX and never be reviewed. You can be reviewed for other reasons with a perfect modifier.

## A family-facing sentence that is still true

“Medicare doesn’t cut you off at a set dollar the way it used to. After a certain amount we have to attest that the visits are still necessary, and a higher amount can mean a chart gets pulled. We’ll keep you posted if anything about your plan is different.” That is courtesy. It is not a promise about an Advantage product.

If you need a number for a meeting in 2026, the public number is $2,480. If you need a process, close this tab and open your MAC. The letters K and X will still be there. They still will not mean “cap.”

One more habit that keeps the ghost alive: calling the combined PT+SLP bucket “the speech cap remaining.” Speech never owned that bucket. A long PT episode can spend it. An honest weekly huddle names both disciplines when the number gets close. An honest family conversation does not blame the SLP for a PT month, or the PT for an SLP month. The statute combined them. We can at least combine the English.
