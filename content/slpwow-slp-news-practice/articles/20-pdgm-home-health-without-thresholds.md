Home health used to have a reputation: hit the therapy-visit threshold, change the payment. On January 1, 2020, that reputation met a statute. The Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018 told CMS to stop using therapy thresholds for case-mix and to pay in 30-day periods instead of 60-day episodes. The Patient-Driven Groupings Model is the machine CMS built.

CMS’s PDGM page is still the clean public map: admission source (community vs institutional), timing (early vs late), one of twelve clinical groups, a functional level, a comorbidity adjustment. Multiply those drawers and you get 432 payment groups. Therapy visit counts are not one of the drawers.

## What SE20005 said to therapists, and still says

CMS published a special-edition MLN article because the field assumed “no thresholds” meant “no therapy.” The article is blunter than hallway rumor. Two of the twelve clinical groups *are* therapy-shaped (musculoskeletal rehabilitation, neuro/stroke rehabilitation). Therapy can also belong in any other group if it is on the plan of care. The principal diagnosis explains the primary reason for the period. It does not write the plan. There is no improvement standard as a coverage wall: skilled maintenance can still be a Medicare home-health idea when the rules for skilled care are met.

That paragraph is why a stroke patient in a “neuro/stroke” group and a heart-failure patient who cannot follow a three-step med routine can both, in theory, have an SLP on the plan. Theory is not staffing. Staffing is a local story. The payment model does not require an SLP-shaped hole in the week. It also does not excuse one.

## What the 30-day unit changed in a brain

Clinicians who learned “episode” as a two-month arc had to learn a shorter breath. Recertification rhythms moved. LUPA logic (the outlier for too-few visits) still exists as a cousin worry, and people still talk about it as if it were the old threshold in a mask. It is not the same object. If your agency treats LUPA as a reason to invent a visit, that is an agency problem, not a CMS permission.

G0153 — 15-minute home-health SLP time — is how many agencies still talk to the claim. That is a reporting unit, not a PDGM drawer. Do not let a 15-minute increment talk you back into RUG thinking.

## What this is not

It is not a protocol for what to do in the living room. It is not a promise that every person with a communication or swallow need will be referred. Referral is a physician-and-agency behavior. PDGM removed a perverse reason to *over*-send therapy. It did not create a virtue reason to *under*-send speech. Both distortions have been reported in the professional press since 2020. This pack will not pretend one of them is imaginary.

It is not outpatient Part B. A person who “graduates” from home health and walks into a clinic has changed manuals. Keep the manuals in different drawers.

## A hallway version

“Home health Medicare doesn’t pay us extra for hitting a visit count anymore. It pays a 30-day group based on why they’re home, how they function, and what else is wrong. Speech can still belong on the plan in any group. The diagnosis is not the plan.” If someone wants the 432-cell diagram, CMS already drew it.
