For a long time, “open science” in communication disorders sounded like a personality: the lab that posts its scripts, the professor who rants about p-hacking, the student who knows what OSF means. In 2021 and 2022 it became something quieter. It became house policy at the journals most of us actually read.

That is news. It is not a miracle.

## What they put on the public page

*JSLHR* announced registered reports in 2021. The point of the format is rude and useful: reviewers see the introduction and the methods *before* the stars arrive, and the journal commits to publishing the result if the authors do what they said. Null findings get a door. Fishing trips lose a pond. McCreery and Liss wrote the invitation in the journal itself. In 2026 the journals academy was still inviting them, including for work that tries to validate clinical tools built with AI — a topic that otherwise arrives as a demo and a press release.

The broader ASHA journals program said in February 2022 that it was joining the Transparency and Openness Promotion (TOP) effort: data-availability statements, encouragement (not a firing squad) to share data, and badges for people who actually do. The badges — open data, open materials, preregistration — come from the Center for Open Science. Authors self-disclose. The journal does a cursory check that the link works. The authors remain accountable to the rest of us if the link is a sad README.

They also said, clearly, that sharing is not always possible. Child language samples, swallow videos, clinic audio — consent and safety are not a vibe. A paper without a badge is not a guilty paper. A paper with a badge is not a sacred one.

## What this changes for someone who will never submit

You get a few more handles on the PDF.

If there is a data statement, read it. “Available on request” is a polite maybe. A repository link is a better object. If you are not going to download the file, you can still notice whether the authors were willing to be found.

If there is a preregistration link, click it once. See whether the primary outcome in the paper is the primary outcome in the plan. Drift happens. Drift that is not mentioned is the old problem in a new jacket.

If the article is a registered report, you are looking at a rare animal: a methods section that already survived a fight. Reward that with your time. Then still ask the five questions from essay 01. A pre-accepted design can be a pre-accepted thin design.

If none of those objects are there, you are reading the literature the way you always did. Most of it will stay that way for years. Policy is not a time machine.

## What this does not change

It does not make a small n large. It does not turn a university clinic into your rural caseload. It does not replace a guideline, a payer rule, or your license. It does not mean ASHA journals are now the only honest venue. Excellent work still lives in *Dysphagia*, in ENT journals, in education journals that have their own open-science weather.

It does not mean you should scold a colleague for citing a 2010 paper that has no badge. The 2010 paper did not have the option. Read it with the habits in essays 01–05.

## Why the 2026 AI invitation matters as news

Clinical tools that use machine learning arrive with a special kind of swagger. They also arrive with a special kind of opacity: a model that cannot be inspected, a training set you will never see, a performance number from a sample that looks like the developers. Asking those tools to enter as registered reports is a way of saying: show the plan before you show the ROC curve. Whether authors take the invitation is a later story. The invitation itself is the headline.

## A closer for the research-literacy wave

Ten pieces ago we started with a 6:41 a.m. PDF. The habits are small on purpose. Methods first. Abstract last. Sizes over stars. The right ruler for single-case work. Reviews that can show a search. Inboxes that flatter. Drafts that are drafts. Guidelines that still have grades. Conflict lines that get read aloud. And now, a house that asked for more light.

None of that is a protocol. All of it is how you keep your integrity when the next abstract wants your Monday.

The next wave leaves the journal. CMS does not offer badges. It offers transmittals, conversion factors, and a modifier people still call a cap. We will not file a claim. We will say what the public page said, in English.
