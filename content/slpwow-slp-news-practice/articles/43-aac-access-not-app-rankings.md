**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

Every year a listicle ranks AAC apps. Every year a family loses six months waiting for a device evaluation while a child points at a fridge. Those are not the same news cycle. Only one of them should make you angry.

ASHA’s public AAC pages and the National Joint Committee work (NJC) have been boring on purpose: AAC does not stop speech from coming. People have a right to communicate. Light tech and no tech are not a moral failure. The headline that still needs reprinting is *access* — funding, wait lists, school policy, a device that is allowed on the bus, a language on the device that matches the home.

## The funding headline

Medicaid, private plans, and school purchase orders are three wallets (essay 34) with three definitions of “medical necessity” and “educational need” (essay 28). A SGD that a plan will buy for home may be locked in a closet at school because of a liability rumor. A school iPad that is “for everyone” may have no voice, no language system, and a teacher’s email on it. SGD funding rules move; this pack will not freeze a coverage LCD. The news shape is stable: the wait is the intervention you did not mean to prescribe.

## The “wait for speech” headline

It should be dead. It is not. Families are still told that a device will make a child lazy. ASHA’s public sentence is the other way. School teams still delay AAC until a CAS debate ends (essay 42) or a feeding debate ends (essay 41). Communication is allowed during those debates. A core board on a laminated sheet is not a betrayal of a future device. It is Tuesday.

## The listicle headline

App rankings hide language systems, motor access, bilingual support, and the fact that the “best” app is the one the child can use after the battery dies on a field trip. Rankings also hide conflicts (essay 09): the list author is sometimes the vendor. You are allowed to notice.

This page will not name a best app. Naming a best app is an ad.

## School vs medical, again

A hospital may introduce high-tech after a stroke or an HNC surgery (essay 46) in a week. A school may take a semester to write the device into an IEP and then discover the bus driver was never told. A clinic may do the feature match and then send the child back to a classroom that still wants oral answers only. The news is the seam, not the button mapping.

## What this page will not do

Core-vocabulary lists to photocopy. Prompting hierarchies. “Monday through Friday” implementation calendars. Those are protocols and, often, copyrighted curricula. If your district bought a curriculum, date it and train it. Do not ask a news brief to become one.

## A hallway version

“AAC is an access story. I’m not ranking apps, and I’m not telling a family that a device will steal speech. If the wait list is the real intervention, that’s the headline I want in the meeting.” If an administrator wants ASHA’s public page, send it. If they want a shopping list, they want an AT eval, not a blog.

Bilingual access is its own headline and still gets dropped from the listicles. A device that only speaks the school language is a half door. ASHA’s public bilingual pages and the NJC’s work both point at the obvious: the home language belongs on the system if the home is going to use it. That is not a feature-match cookbook. It is a reason to reject a ranking that never asked what the grandmother speaks. If your vendor demo is English-only and your caseload is not, the demo is the news.
