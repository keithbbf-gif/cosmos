The last page of the PDF has a sentence people skip because it feels like gossip. *Dr. A has received consulting fees from Company B. This study was funded by…* That sentence is not gossip. It is part of the methods. ICMJE built a whole form so the sentence would show up.

A conflict is a situation, not a verdict. Money, a patent, a spouse’s job, a speaking circuit, a desire to see one’s own program survive the next budget — all of those can pull a pen. So can the opposite fantasy: that a publicly funded academic has no interests except truth. Everyone has interests. The line tells you which ones the journal was willing to print.

## What the line can hold

**Industry funding of the study.** A thickener, a device, an app, a publisher of a packaged curriculum. The data can still be clean. The abstract is where you walk slowly (essay 02). Ask whether the company saw the manuscript. Ask whether negative endpoints are missing. You may not get answers. Asking is still the job.

**Personal fees.** Consulting, lectures, travel. A $1,500 talk does not buy a field. A decade of talks might buy a tone. Tone is allowed to make you cautious. It is not allowed to make you invent fraud.

**Intellectual property.** The author invented the measure. Of course they hope it works. Of course they should say so. A new language sample tool with an unnamed owner is a worse object than a named one.

**“None declared.”** This can mean none. It can mean the form was empty because nobody asked well. It can mean the author believes a nonprofit relationship does not count. Believe it a little. Verify it if the paper wants you to buy a thing.

## What the line cannot do

It cannot prove the results are false. Treating every industry-funded swallow paper as a lie is how you throw away half a literature and keep your prejudices. It cannot prove the results are true. Treating a federal grant as holy water is how you miss a sloppy design with a patriotic logo.

It cannot replace Methods. A pristine disclosure over a missing control group is a polite cover sheet. A messy disclosure over a registered, blinded trial is a paper you should still read.

## How this shows up in our mail

Device companies fund webinars that quote papers they also funded. Curriculum vendors fund “implementation studies” in districts that already bought the box. App companies fund single-case series with beautiful graphs and no competing treatment. None of that is secret. It is usually in 8-point type.

Professional associations have conflicts too. ASHA is a member organization, a publisher, a CEU vendor, and an advocacy shop. That does not make an ASHA page false. It makes “ASHA says” an incomplete citation. Say which document, which year, which kind of document (portal, position, fee-schedule analysis, public consumer page). Essay 08 started that habit. Keep it.

Guideline panels are where COI gets expensive. The trustworthy-guideline book asked panels to be boring about money. When a hoarseness or reflux document is written by people who sell the drug or the scope, you are not a cynic for reading the roster. You are doing the reading.

## A way to talk about it without becoming a prosecutor

In journal club: “Before we argue the table, let’s read the funding sentence aloud.” That takes twelve seconds. It changes the temperature of the abstract.

In a vendor meeting: “Can I see the study you just cited, including the disclosure.” If they send only a slide, you have your answer.

In your own writing — a note, a blog, a talk — print yours. If you have none, say none. If you have a side gig, say the side gig. Hypocrisy is more damaging than a consulting fee.
