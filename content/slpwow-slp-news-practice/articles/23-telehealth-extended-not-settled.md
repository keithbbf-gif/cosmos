**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

Two clocks have been running since 2020, and they do not chime together.

**Clock one: the code list.** CMS, through the Physician Fee Schedule, can put speech-language pathology services on the Medicare Telehealth Services List. ASHA’s November 2025 fee-schedule PDF said CMS had moved to *permanent* listing for the SLP codes that had been on since the public-health emergency. That is a regulatory win. A listed code is a code Medicare knows how to recognize at a distance.

**Clock two: the practitioner.** Statute has to say that an SLP (and an audiologist, a PT, an OT) is a person who may furnish a Medicare telehealth service. That clock has been a series of short congressional extensions. ASHA’s same November PDF still said authority ran only through January 30, 2026. Then Congress passed the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (H.R. 7148, signed February 3, 2026). ASHA’s news post and CMS’s telehealth FAQ (updated February 26, 2026) now say the extended practitioner list — including SLPs — runs through **December 31, 2027**. After that, unless another law arrives, those professions fall off the “who may furnish” list even if the codes stay listed.

If you only read the November PDF, you are a month behind on clock two. If you only read a tweet that says “telehealth is permanent,” you have mashed the clocks.

## What the FAQ actually said

Through December 31, 2027, beneficiaries can receive Medicare telehealth in the United States and territories without the old rural-facility originating-site story (behavioral health has its own, more permanent path). Through the same date, the extended practitioner list holds. On January 1, 2028, the FAQ’s current public answer is: SLPs, PTs, OTs, and audiologists can no longer furnish Medicare telehealth, and the geographic/site rules return for most non-behavioral services.

That 2028 sentence is a *current* CMS answer, not a prophecy you must treat as fate. Congress can write again. ASHA said it will ask them to. News can hold a date and a lobby at the same time.

CMS also said it would treat the brief lapses (a shutdown stretch, the January 30–February 3 gap) as if the flexibility had not dropped — pay the claims as before. That is a contractor-implementation sentence. Keep the EOBs.

## What this is not

It is not permission from your *state* board. Licensure, internships, and cross-state practice are a different manual. It is not Medicaid. It is not a school IEP. It is not an Advantage plan. It is not a clinical protocol for when telehealth is a good idea for a swallow or a voice. Those judgments stay with you and the person in the room that is not a room.

It is not a billing modifier cookbook. ASHA and CMS have pages for modifiers and place-of-service. They move. This pack will not freeze them.

## Why the two-clock story matters in a staff meeting

A director who heard “CMS made SLP telehealth permanent” and a director who heard “it dies in 2027” can both be quoting a real document. They are quoting different clocks. Put both clocks on the whiteboard. Put the state board under them. Then decide what your clinic promises a family in 2026. Promises that assume 2029 are a kind of fiction.

## A hallway version

“The codes are on CMS’s telehealth list for keeps, as of the last fee schedule. Our *authority to be the person on the call* is a law that currently ends December 31, 2027. I’m not going to collapse those sentences.” If someone wants the FAQ, it is a public PDF. If someone wants a how-to for a first tele-session, they can have a supervisor, not a news brief.
