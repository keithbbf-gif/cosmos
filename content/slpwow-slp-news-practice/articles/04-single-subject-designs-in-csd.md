A physician who wanders into our literature often looks confused. Where are the 400-person trials. Why are there graphs with little dots for one child across thirty sessions. Is this even science.

Yes. It is a different instrument. Pretending it is a broken RCT is how people miss half of communication sciences.

## Why the design exists here

We treat individuals. Many of the behaviors we care about — a specific sound, a specific request on a device, a specific swallow strategy in a specific mealtime — do not wait around for a grant cycle. Single-case experimental designs (multiple baseline, withdrawal, alternating treatments, and their cousins) were built to ask: did *this* person’s behavior change when *this* condition changed, and can I see it on a graph.

That is a causal question. It is not a prevalence question. It is not a “will this work for the next forty people” question. Papers that use the design well are honest about the difference. Papers that use it badly write a Discussion as if three first-graders were a national sample.

Horner and colleagues, and Kazdin’s textbook, spent years telling education and psychology what “well” looks like: repeated measurement, a planned baseline, a clear change in condition, someone checking that the condition actually happened. You do not need their checklists taped to the fridge. You need to notice when a paper skips them. A squiggly line that goes up after a student started coming more often is not a design. It is a calendar.

## How to read the graph like a grown-up

Look at the baseline until you are bored. If the dots are already climbing before the treatment line, the treatment line is late to its own party.

Look at how many times they replicated the effect — across behaviors, across people, across settings. One nice staircase in one child is a story. Three staircases that wait their turn is a stronger story. Still a story about those children.

Look at whether the outcome was collected by someone who did not know the phase. Blinded observation is hard in a school. That does not make unblinded observation a virtue. It makes it a limitation you should see printed.

Look at overlap. If the “treatment” dots sit in the same cloud as the baseline dots, a dramatic caption will not save it. Visual analysis is not mysticism. It is looking.

Do not demand a p-value as a moral certificate. Some papers add randomization tests or effect-size indexes for single-case data. Fine. The graph is still the thing. If you cannot see it, the index is doing public relations.

## What this design cannot do, and should not be asked to do

It cannot, by itself, tell a payer that a procedure is “proven” for a population. Payers who pretend otherwise are confused. Researchers who promise otherwise are selling.

It cannot replace a trial when the question is population-level harm or a small average effect you would miss in six people. Antibiotics and vaccines needed large numbers. A cueing hierarchy for one child’s /k/ might not.

It cannot be turned into a protocol on this website. The graph is news about what happened in a study. The moment we write “do these steps,” we have left news and entered a manual we do not own.

## How the journals are treating the design now

ASHA journals still publish single-case work because the field still needs it. They also, since 2021–2022, have been asking for more transparency from *all* designs: data statements, badges, registered reports for hypothesis-driven work. A registered multiple-baseline is an interesting object. You precommit to the phases before the dots flatter you. That is a good kind of news. It does not make the old graphs in your file cabinet dishonest. It makes the new ones a little harder to fudge.

CONSORT has a sibling conversation for n-of-1 and single-case reporting (CENT / SCRIBE in adjacent fields). When a CSD paper cites a reporting standard and then actually follows it, reward that with your attention. When it cites one and then hides the raw dots in a tiny figure, notice that too.

## A hallway translation

If someone says “it’s only n = 1,” you can say: “It’s n = 1 on purpose. Did they control the phase.” If someone says “this proves the program,” you can say: “It shows a change in these participants under these conditions.” If someone wants the steps, you can say: “Read the paper. This page will not photocopy them.”
