**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

For two years, everyone was an expert on cough. Then the news cycle moved, and the people who still could not talk through a workday, or still coughed on water, were left with clinic wait lists and a noun that kept changing: long COVID, post-COVID conditions, PASC. CDC’s public long-COVID pages still exist. NIDCD still owns voice and swallow as health topics. ASHA still has consumer voice and swallow language. The headline in 2026 is not a new virus. It is that the pages did not vanish when the comments section got bored.

## What the public pages have been willing to say

That COVID can leave people with fatigue, cognitive fog, breathlessness, and — in the subsets we see — voice change, chronic cough, swallow trouble, smell and taste change that wrecks eating. That “you look fine” is not a test. That rehabilitation, when it exists, is a team sport. CDC’s wording moves; check the live URL before you quote a 2022 screenshot. This brief is about the *shape*: communication and swallow are on the list, not a footnote you have to smuggle in.

## What SLPs actually met

Intubated ICU voices. Teachers who taught in a mask for a year and never got the room back (essay 39). Elderly people whose swallow was fragile already and then spent ten days in bed. Children whose first school years were a screen (the pediatric pack can own the language side). People whose ENT visit was a PPI (essay 38) and whose swallow visit was a thickener (essay 36) because those were the habits on the shelf.

You do not need a unique “COVID protocol.” You need the same questions you already had, plus a respect for fatigue that some of our old hour-long sessions pretended not to see. Respect is not a worksheet.

## Payment and telehealth rode along

A lot of post-COVID care happened on a screen because the person could not park. Essay 23 is the Medicare clock: codes listed, practitioner authority extended through 2027, not a personality. Medicaid and commercial plans wrote their own weather. Cognitive communication after COVID sat in the same LCD attic as every other cognitive claim (essay 22). None of that is a new benefit named “long COVID speech.” It is old benefits with a new referring diagnosis. If a plan invented a special bucket, that is local news. Quote the plan, not a reel.

## What this page will not do

Breathing-swallow hierarchies. Vocal function exercise lists. Smell-training photocopies. “Six-week long-COVID program” calendars. Those objects exist in papers and in hospital brands. Dumping them here would be a protocol and, often, a copyright. If a 2025 NIDCD or CDC page added a specific referral sentence, prefer that page to this paragraph, and `[VERIFY]` the URL at publish time.

## A hallway version

“Public agencies still list voice, swallow, and thinking problems among post-COVID leftovers. I’m going to treat the person in front of me, not a brand of pandemic, and I’m not going to sell a COVID protocol from a news site.” If a reporter wants a quote, give them the CDC and ASHA public URLs. If a vendor wants to brand your clinic as a Long COVID Center of Something, ask who owns the outcome data (essay 09).

School SLPs met a quieter version: children whose first classroom years were a screen, teachers whose rooms never got quieter, a nurse’s office full of lingering cough. Not every cough is a swallow problem. Not every fog is a language disorder. The public pages give you permission to ask. They do not give you a screen you can run from a blog. Ask, refer, and refuse the branded six-week package until someone shows you the methods. That refusal is how this whole pack started.
