If you only watch the fluoro, thicker often looks kinder. If you only watch the pneumonia trials, the kindness gets an argument. Both viewings are in the public literature. The news is that they have not been the same movie, and people keep recutting the trailer.

## What the instrumental literature keeps showing

Steele and colleagues, in a 2024 Bayesian look at two large datasets (open in *Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences*), found penetration-aspiration scores were less ugly on thicker liquids than on thin, with the cleaner drop at mildly thick and above. They also said the thing honest instrumental papers now say: we still do not know that long-term thickening prevents the downstream disasters. A 2026 update of the older IDDSI-era systematic review on consistency and swallowing behavior is in the same family — mechanism first, outcome later.

That is a real finding about *bolus flow in a study*. It is not a cafeteria order.

## What the outcome literature has not handed us

O’Keeffe’s 2018 *BMC Geriatrics* essay asked, in public, whether modified diets as practiced were justified by pneumonia prevention. The best-known randomized work (Logemann / Robbins, Protocol 201, people with dementia or Parkinson who aspirated on thin) is the paper everyone cites and few reread. In the trial’s short version: honey-thick looked better on the study for some people; honey-thick was not a pneumonia hero in the follow-along, and dehydration was part of the cost. Kaneoka and colleagues’ 2017 meta-analysis of thin-plus-strategies versus thickened liquids in people who already aspirated on thin did not find a pneumonia difference — and they warned the result only travels to people who were already low-risk enough to be in those studies.

Beck and colleagues’ 2022 guideline update (GRADE, PRISMA) looked again and still could not upgrade thickening to a confident yes for survival, pneumonia, or quality of life. They said the evidence remained thin. A weak recommendation *against* routine thickening, in that tradition, is not a dare to give everyone thin water. It is a grade (essay 08).

## How the trailer gets recut

Vendor decks show the fluoro. Academic threads show the pneumonia forest plot. Hospital policy shows last decade’s in-service. School cafeterias show a laminated “nectar” that may not be anyone’s current IDDSI (essay 35). Families show a grandchild who coughs on water and a grandfather who stopped drinking.

You are allowed to hold all of those objects. You are not allowed, from this URL, to pick the cup.

## What “individualized” means as news, not as a slogan

It means a person, a date, a risk of pneumonia that is not generic, a risk of dehydration that is not generic, a medication that may not dissolve in goo, a preference, an instrumental if you can get one, a re-ask when the body changes. It means the 2024 Steele paper’s other honest line: people are not a single PAS score; they vary across swallows, so one sip is a rumor.

It does not mean a protocol I will now list. It does not mean “water protocol” steps. Those live in papers and in department policies. Dumping them here would be the thing this pack refuses.

## A hallway version

“Thicker often looks safer on a study. Pneumonia trials have not given us a clean win, and dehydration is not imaginary. I’m not going to pretend one paper ended the argument.” If a medical director wants the citations, they are in the bibliography. If they want a house algorithm, they want a committee and a date, not a blog.
