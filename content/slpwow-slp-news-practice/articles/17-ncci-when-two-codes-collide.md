**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

Some code pairs are not allowed to share a claim the way you think they should. CMS’s National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI) publishes procedure-to-procedure (PTP) edits for that reason. The public program has a home page, quarterly files, and a manual. It is not a secret. It is also not a personality test for your documentation.

The idea is older than your EMR: do not pay twice for work that is already inside another code, and do not pay for combinations that CMS has decided are mutually exclusive. The files change. A pair that billed last year can bounce this quarter. That is why “we always bill these together” is not a policy. It is a habit that needs a calendar.

## What an edit is

A row that says: code A and code B, in this order, on this date, in this setting, do not both get paid unless a modifier story exists — and sometimes not even then. Some edits are “never.” Some are “only if you can show a separate, distinct service.” The modifier people reach for in those stories is a compliance object, not a news-brief object. This page will not name a recipe. Naming a recipe is how news becomes a coding product.

If a remittance mentions NCCI, PTP, or “mutually exclusive,” you are in this neighborhood. If it mentions MPPR, you are in essay 14. If it mentions KX, essay 15. The neighborhoods share a zip code. They do not share a rule.

## Why SLPs feel it on eval days

Evaluation and treatment on the same date. Two evaluation codes that overlap in the book. A swallow instrument and a clinical swallow on the same morning. Those are the hallway arguments. Sometimes the book says they are distinct. Sometimes the edit says the second is inside the first. Sometimes your MAC has an article that is stricter than the national file.

The news is not a particular pair — pairs move. The news is that the file is public, quarterly, and more authoritative than a conference slide from 2019.

ASHA tracks SLP-relevant edits and writes member updates when a pair that matters moves. Use those updates as alerts. Then open the CMS file or the MAC article. Do not stop at a screenshot in a group chat.

## What not to do with a bounce

Do not add a modifier because a forum said so. Do not drop a medically necessary service from the plan because an edit exists — the plan is clinical; the claim is a later translation. Do not write a progress note that exists only to “support a 59.” Notes that exist for modifiers read like they exist for modifiers.

Do ask: is this still an edit this quarter. Is there a MAC article. Is the second service actually separate in time, intent, and documentation, or are we double-naming one hour. Those questions belong to you and your coder. The answers do not belong in a blog paragraph.

## Medicaid and other payers

Many state Medicaid programs and commercial plans borrow NCCI logic, or borrow a cousin. Many do not. “Medicare said no” is not a complete sentence in a school billing office. Essay 26 lives there. If you only remember one thing: the CMS NCCI page is about Medicare claims until someone else has adopted it in writing.

## A closer

Two codes can be clinically elegant together and administratively allergic. That allergy is published. It is updated. It is not a cap, not a review threshold, and not a reason to skip the look at the larynx. When in doubt, read the current row. When not in doubt, still glance at the quarter. Pride is how old edits become new denials.
