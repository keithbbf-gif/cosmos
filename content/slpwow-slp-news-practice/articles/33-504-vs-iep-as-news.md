An IEP is a special-education document under IDEA. A 504 plan is a civil-rights document under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act. Both can mention speech. They are not two sizes of the same shirt.

If you only remember one distinction: IDEA is about *specially designed instruction* and related services for a child who meets the Act’s disability categories and needs them for FAPE. 504 is about *non-discrimination* and reasonable access for a person with a disability as 504 defines it — a broader door, a different toolkit, usually fewer service minutes, often more accommodations.

## Why SLPs sit in both rooms

A student with a chronic voice problem, a stutter, a hearing loss, or a mild language issue may get a 504 when the team does not see a need for specialized instruction. A student who used to have an IEP may “dismiss” from IDEA and land on a 504 the next week. A student may have both in sequence, or a 504 in general education while a medical clinic runs the therapy. The SLP is sometimes the only person who can explain why a classroom mic is not the same object as language intervention.

OCR — the Department of Education’s Office for Civil Rights — is the 504 cop. State complaint systems and due process are the IDEA cops. Families mash the cops. So do principals. You do not have to become the lawyer. You do have to stop calling every plan an IEP.

## What the news cycle keeps getting wrong

“504s are the weak IEP.” Sometimes a 504 is the right, lighter tool. Sometimes it is a way to avoid providing service. Both happen. A news brief cannot tell you which one you are looking at in room 12.

“If they have a 504, Medicaid cannot pay.” Not as a federal sentence. The 2014 free-care letter and the 2023 guide (essays 26–27) are about covered services to enrolled children, not about IEP-only forever. Your SPA may still be IEP-only. That is a state fact, not a 504 fact.

“A 504 guarantees clinic coverage.” It does not. It is a school access document. A health plan has not signed it (essay 28).

## Accommodations are not a secret protocol

Extended time, a quiet room, a teacher who does not demand oral reading in front of the class, a bottle of water, an AAC device that is allowed on the field trip — those are access tools. Writing them down is not “doing therapy.” Doing therapy and calling it a 504 accommodation is how teams get confused about who is responsible when the device breaks.

This page will not list the accommodations you should use. Lists become protocols. Protocols are out of scope.

## A hallway version

“IEP means special education under IDEA. 504 means civil-rights access under a different statute. I can serve in both rooms. I will not use the words as synonyms, and I will not promise a clinic because a 504 exists.” If a parent wants the federal pages, they are public. If they want a fight, they want an advocate, not a news URL.

Traveling CFs mash the rooms because graduate school taught one “paperwork” unit. Ask which statute the meeting is under before you speak. An IEP team can discuss specialized instruction. A 504 meeting can discuss access. If you start listing therapy goals in a 504 room, someone will write them down as if they were FAPE minutes, and then no one will own them when the device breaks. If you start listing only accommodations in an IEP room that already found a speech disability, you may have shrunk a service without saying so. The statute you are in should change your verbs. That is not lawyer work. That is literacy.
