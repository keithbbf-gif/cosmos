**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

The parent hands you a printout with barium in the verbs. Or a USB stick. Or a portal screenshot that cuts off the recommendations. The child is in your cafeteria in forty minutes.

This is a common object. It is not a common skill in graduate school.

## What the document is

A medical instrumental study: videofluoroscopy or endoscopy, done on a date, with a bolus set, in a body that may have changed. It answers questions a hospital SLP and a physician asked *that day*. It does not automatically answer whether the child can manage the school pizza, the water fountain, or the IEP goal about “oral motor.”

IDDSI language may or may not be on the page. The hospital may still be speaking National Dysphagia Diet, or a house dialect. ASHA’s diet page is careful: ASHA does not define diet levels, and neither ASHA nor a U.S. payer nationally *mandates* IDDSI, even as ASHA supports the framework. If the report says “nectar” and your cafeteria speaks “mildly thick,” you have a translation job, not a betrayal.

## What you can do without becoming the hospital

You can read the date. A study from last March is a historical document. Kids grow. Neuro changes. Tubes come out.

You can read who was in the room and what they actually tried. If they never tried a regular cup, the sentence “must have honey thick” is a sentence about what was *tested*, or about what someone feared, or both. You cannot finish their test in a cafeteria. You can notice the hole.

You can take the recommendations as a *medical opinion to the team*, not as an IEP you write by yourself. Lunch is a school operation: nurse, dietitian if you have one, principal, family, sometimes a physician order the nurse will not ignore. The SLP is often the only person who can read the study. That does not make the SLP the person who can rewrite a diet order if your state and facility say otherwise. ASHA’s diet page says to follow state and facility rules about who enters orders. School policy is a facility.

You can ask for the missing page. Recommendations without impressions, or impressions without the bolus list, are how rumors start.

You can request, through the family, a conversation with the hospital SLP. Two clocks (essay 30). One child. A ten-minute call prevents a year of chopped-meat folklore.

## What you must not do

You must not repeat the study with a spoon and a rumor. You must not photocopy a protocol from the report into a classroom program. You must not tell a teacher that “ASHA says IDDSI level 5” as if that were a national law. You must not ignore a current NPO order because the child looks fine in homeroom. Looking fine is not a swallow study.

You also must not treat the study as none of your business. Cafeteria risk is a FAPE and a safety issue. “I’m only language” is a sentence that has not aged well in buildings that have one SLP.

## When the religions disagree

Hospital says thickened liquids. Child drinks thin at grandma’s. School nurse wants a doctor order. Parent wants “normal.” This is not a thread you settle with a guideline screenshot (essay 08, essay 36). It is a team, a physician, and sometimes a new study. News can name the conflict. It cannot pick the cup.

## A hallway version

“I’ll read this as a dated medical document, translate it into our cafeteria language, and take it to the team. I will not rerun the fluoro at lunch, and I will not pretend the IEP can replace an order.” If the principal wants a protocol, they want a clinician and a policy, not a blog.
