Two 2026 policies have names that sound like they were designed by a committee that does not eat lunch. They still land on a remittance.

## The efficiency adjustment

CMS finalized a 2.5 percent reduction to work RVUs and intra-service time for a set of codes it believes have gotten faster over a five-year look-back (2021–2025). ASHA’s November 2025 analysis is the public SLP translation: CMS thinks non-time-based procedures, radiology, and diagnostic tests grow more efficient, and the AMA’s valuation process does not harvest that efficiency often enough. The cut may come back every three years with the same logic.

What is *not* in the bucket, per that analysis: services where the clinician’s time *is* the resource, and services on the approved telehealth list. ASHA’s example is the one people needed: 92507 is exempt. New codes in their birth year are exempt. Some SLP diagnostic codes are not. CMS posted a ZIP of the affected list. This pack will not reprint the ZIP.

So the hallway version is not “they cut speech.” It is “they cut work on some of the codes they think got faster, and they left the time-based treatment code alone.” If your clinic lives on an instrumental or a test code that is on the list, your weather is different from a clinic that lives on 92507. Mix matters. Essay 13 said the same about the conversion factor. Same moral, second knife.

ASHA and others argued against it. They lost, for 2026. Losing a comment letter is also news.

## The GPCI floor

Geographic Practice Cost Indices nudge RVUs for local rent and wages. For years a “floor” kept some rural or low-cost localities from falling as far as the raw index wanted. ASHA’s 2026 write-up says that floor goes away, with locality pain beginning January 31, 2026, in the places that had been standing on it. CMS’s fact sheet is drier: GPCIs are being updated as the statute requires.

If you do not know whether your locality was on the floor, you are allowed to not know. Your biller’s locality file knows. A national blog that says “rural gets cut” is a mood. A locality number is a fact.

## How these sit on top of the conversion-factor smile

Remember the two conversion factors (essay 13). They went *up*. Practice-expense policy for 2026, ASHA said, actually helps SLP because we are paid at nonfacility PE. Efficiency and GPCI can take the smile back in particular codes and particular towns. Sequestration can take more. ASHA floated a “could reach about 4 percent” weather report if the knives all land. Treat that as a dated forecast, not a paycheck.

The adult way to read a remittance in March 2026 is: which code, which locality, which date, which other therapy on the day (MPPR). The child way is: “Medicare cut us again” as a single noun.

## What this page will not do

It will not give you a locality fee. It will not list every SLP code on the efficiency ZIP. It will not tell you to change your mix to dodge a work RVU. Mix is clinical. Dodge is a consulting product.

## A closer

2026 is a year of stacked small machines: two factors, an efficiency harvest, a floor that vanished, an MPPR file that did not, a telehealth authority that got two more years. None of the machines is a cap. All of them are why “what did Medicare do to speech” is a bad question. Ask what they did to *this code, in this town, on this day*. Then open the CMS file, not a reel.

Essay 25 is the quality-program door most SLPs never walk through, and why that absence is itself a headline.
