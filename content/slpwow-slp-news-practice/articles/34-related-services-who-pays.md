**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

Related services are how IDEA names the extra professionals who help a child benefit from special education. Speech-language pathology is on the list. The school’s duty to provide FAPE does not wait for an insurance card. That duty is the first money sentence. People bury it because it is expensive.

Medicaid, since a 1988 amendment to the Social Security Act, can be a *payer* for some health-related school services when the child is enrolled and the state plan agrees. CMS’s 2023 guide and MACPAC’s 2024 brief are the current public maps. Private plans sometimes pay for after-school clinic care that looks like the same work and sometimes do not (essay 28). None of those payers replaces the school’s duty. They rearrange who writes the check.

## Three wallets, one child

**The district wallet.** Staff time, contracts, ESY, transportation to a service. This wallet opens because of FAPE, not because a claim paid.

**The Medicaid wallet.** Federal and state match, school-based claiming, sometimes a clinic claim on the same child in the same month if the services are actually different and the rules allow. Consent, documentation, qualified providers (essay 26). This wallet opens because of a benefit, not because an IEP exists — though many SPAs still require the IEP as a practical matter.

**The family / private-plan wallet.** Copays, deductibles, denials, a clinic that is not the school. This wallet opens because a parent chose a second door, or because the school offered only a sliver.

When a superintendent says “we can’t afford the SLP unless Medicaid pays,” they have inverted the duty. When a parent says “the school should stop, insurance will do it,” they have inverted the duty the other way. When a clinic says “we’re the real therapy; school is just homework,” they have started a religious war. The child is still in the cafeteria.

## What the news cycle keeps selling

That Medicaid claiming is a new pot of gold after 2023. It is a possible reimbursement for work you may already be doing, if the state built the door. Gold that requires a time study and a SPA is not gold. It is infrastructure.

That billing Medicaid turns FAPE into a medical model and “hurts” the child. Poorly done claiming can eat clinician time and confuse families. That is a quality problem. It is not a federal prohibition (essay 27).

That a paid clinic hour means the IEP minute can shrink. Sometimes teams reduce school service because a clinic is in place. Sometimes that is coordinated. Sometimes it is a quiet eligibility cut. *Endrew* is in the background. This page will not run your IEP. It will say the shrink should be a team decision with eyes open, not a finance decision with the SLP out of the room.

## What this page will not do

It will not tell you how to stack a school claim and a clinic claim. It will not tell you to refuse Medicaid billing on principle. It will not tell you to chase claiming so hard the sessions disappear. Those are ethics and labor. They deserve a department meeting with an attorney, not a blog recipe.

## A hallway version

“The school owes the related service if the IEP says so, even if every payer says no. Medicaid might chip in if our state built that door. A clinic is a third wallet, not a translation of the IEP.” If someone wants the 2023 guide, send the PDF. If they want you to become the revenue cycle, they want a different job description.
