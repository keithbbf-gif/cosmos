The school bell is a legal object. The hospital minute is a payment object. They both use the word *session* and they do not mean the same thing.

A lot of the friction in our profession is two clocks pretending to be one watch.

## The school clock

The day is built around passing times, testing calendars, bus schedules, and a service-delivery statement on an IEP that may say “120 minutes monthly” without saying *which* Tuesdays. Missed sessions become make-up politics (the 2024 survey in essay 29). Documentation is an education record with a Medicaid cousin. The “week” includes IEP meetings that swallow the only prep hour. Summer is a different statute (ESY), not a lighter version of May.

Progress is a team’s judgment against IEP goals, not a FIM score. Discharge is a team decision, a reevaluation, a parent, a move to another district. It is rarely a KX threshold.

## The hospital clock

The day is built around physician rounds, NPO status, a swallow slot that moved, and a length-of-stay that finance already predicted. Minutes may or may not price the stay (PDPM, essay 19; they do not). Outpatient follow-up lives on the Physician Fee Schedule (essays 11–18). Documentation is a medical record that an auditor may collect (essay 32). The “week” includes weekends if the building is that kind of building. Summer does not exist. Night exists.

Progress is a functional change or a skilled maintenance story. Discharge is a medical and payer event. Families hear “we’re done here” when the building means “we’re done *in this bed*.”

## People who wear both watches

PRN hospital plus a 1.0 FTE school job. A medical CF who takes a school contract in August. A school SLP who covers summer clinic. The body does not change. The verbs do. “Minutes” in the IEP are a promise to a team. “Minutes” on a timed Medicare code are a pot that may not include your 92507. “Discharge” in May can be a celebration. “Discharge” on 3-south can be a Tuesday.

If you supervise someone who just switched buildings, the orientation that matters is the clock. Not the anatomy. They already have the anatomy.

## Records that cross the parking lot

A hospital swallow study in a backpack (essay 31). A school language report at a stroke clinic. A 504 plan at a voice center. Each document is true in its religion. Trouble starts when a reader applies the wrong clock: a clinic that treats IEP minutes as a dosing recipe, a school that treats an NPO order as a forever identity, a parent who hears both and concludes that one of you is lying.

You can prevent some of that with one spoken sentence: “I’m going to read this as a hospital document” or “I’m going to read this as an IEP.” Then ask the question the document can actually answer.

## What this is not

It is not a ranking. School is not lesser medicine. Medicine is not lesser education. ASHA’s membership numbers have said for years that schools still employ a huge share of us. The 2024 caseload photo is a school photo. The CMS fee schedule is a medical photo. This pack prints both on purpose.

It is not a scheduling protocol. If your district wants a 3:1 model and your hospital wants weekend coverage, those are local labor stories.

## A hallway version

“I can do both jobs. I can’t use one clock for both jobs.” If a director wants you to, they want a cheaper universe. If a family is confused, they are not slow. They have been handed two watches and one word.
