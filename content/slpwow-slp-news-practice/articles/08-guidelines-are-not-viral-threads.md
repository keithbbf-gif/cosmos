**Disclaimer.** Educational news briefing for SLPWOW. Not billing advice, not legal advice, not a treatment protocol, and not medical advice. Rules change by contractor, state, and calendar year. Verify the current CMS, MAC, state, or ASHA page before you act. This series does not evaluate patients and does not publish worksheets.

A guideline is a committee that had to show its work. A thread is a person who did not. Both can be right on a Tuesday. Only one has a methods section you can argue with.

The Institute of Medicine (now the National Academy of Medicine) spent a book on what “trustworthy” means: a question, a systematic look at the evidence, a grading of how sure we are, a panel that is not a vendor picnic, a public draft, a plan to update. Most documents that get waved in our hallways fail at least two of those. They still get called guidelines. The word has been promoted beyond its training.

## What a real one looks like when you open it

Key action statements. Each one tagged: strong recommendation, recommendation, option, recommendation *against*. A paragraph that says who the statement is for, and who it is not for. A paragraph that admits the evidence is thin. An algorithm that is a map, not a law.

The 2018 AAO-HNS hoarseness update is a clean public example, and essay 38 will stay with the PPI sentence. For this page, notice the shape. They recommended *against* empiric antireflux medicine for isolated dysphonia without looking at the larynx. They recommended *against* routine antibiotics for hoarseness. They did not write “never.” They wrote a graded no, with reasons (harm, cost, delay) you can read without a login.

ASHA’s Practice Portal is a different object. It is a living professional page, often careful, often useful, and not the same legal species as an AAO-HNS CPG. ASHA position statements (caseload/workload, 2002) are another species still. If you cite them, cite them as what they are. Do not promote a portal page to a statute, or a statute to a portal page.

## What a thread does to that shape

It drops the grade. “They said never give PPI” is easier to post than “recommendation against, isolated dysphonia, no exam, weigh harm.” It drops the exceptions. It drops the year. It adds a dare: if you still do the old thing, you are the problem.

Dares spread faster than grades. They also create a second, opposite dare: if the guideline is from ENT, ignore it, they do not understand therapy. That dare is also a thread. It is not a reading.

You are allowed to disagree with a guideline. You are required, if you are going to disagree in a department, to disagree with the *document* — the table, the exceptions, the date — not with a screenshot.

## How newsrooms and vendors flatten the same way

A press release from a specialty society is already a compression. A health-news write-up is a compression of the compression. A vendor email that begins “according to new guidelines you must…” is a sales letter. Essay 02 was about abstracts. This is the same habit one layer out.

When a headline says a society “now requires” a device, open the PDF. Requirements live in payment policy and in law. Guidelines live in “should” and “may” and “we suggest.” CMS can turn a guideline into a coverage rule. Until it does, the guideline is not a denial letter.

## A way to brief a team without becoming the guideline

Print the action statement, not the tweet. Read the “exceptions” paragraph aloud. Say the year. Say what would make you reopen it (a new trial, a new harm signal, a new CMS transmittal). Then stop. If someone wants the protocol that sits under the statement, they can go to the society page. This series will not photocopy it.

If your team’s actual rule is a hospital policy, say that too. Local policy can be stricter than a guideline and still be legal. Local policy can also be a fossil. Fossils deserve dates.

## What this pack will do with guidelines

We will name them when they are the news: hoarseness and PPI, IDDSI as a framework ASHA supports but does not mandate, ASHA’s refusal to set a national caseload number. We will not turn them into a how-to. If a sentence would let you skip an exam or start an exercise list, it does not belong here.

The next essay is the line many guidelines print in 8-point type: who paid, who consulted, who owns the device. That line is not gossip. It is part of the methods.
