# Claims guardrails — SLP News, practice wave

Internal. Not a blog post. These drafts are clinician-facing **news** for **SLPWOW**. They are not a billing manual, not a treatment protocol, and not a CEU.

If a sentence would let a reader leave thinking *I can bill this tomorrow* or *I should run this protocol on Monday*, cut it. Do not soften it into a how-to.

Counsel / a licensed SLP still owns the last read before any URL goes live. A coder or MAC still owns the last read on a dollar figure.

## What this pack is allowed to be

A magazine-section explanation of public news that already sits on CMS.gov, Medicaid.gov, ASHA.org, ED.gov, IDDSI.org, and named open papers. The legal and ethical object is **surveillance of the mail**: what a rule, a fee schedule, a journal policy, or a guideline actually printed, and what that does *not* license.

It is not coding instruction. It is not a swallow protocol. It is not “red-flag SEO.”

This is the **second** SLP News pack. It does not republish the first pack’s general profession calendar. It stays on research literacy, reimbursement in plain language, school vs medical, and practice-area *headlines*.

## The documents, not the vibe

- CMS Therapy Services pages and transmittals are **payment policy**. They are not a clinic protocol.
- The Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018 repealed the hard outpatient therapy caps. It kept a **KX modifier threshold** and a **targeted medical review** dollar amount. Those are different objects. Do not call the threshold a cap.
- CY 2026 KX modifier thresholds, conversion factors, and MPPR rates are **calendar-year facts**. If a later transmittal moves them, mark the piece `[VERIFY]` or update the number. Do not invent next year’s figure.
- ASHA fee-schedule analyses paraphrase CMS. Prefer the CMS page when the two disagree, and say they disagree.
- IDEA, Section 504, and Medicare “medical necessity” are **three legal languages**. Do not collapse them.
- IDDSI is a **terminology framework**. ASHA supports it. ASHA does not mandate it. Neither does a U.S. payer as a national rule as of the sources in `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`.
- AAO-HNS hoarseness guidance is **otolaryngology’s** public guideline. It is not an SLP treatment recipe.
- ASHA Journals open-science pages describe **what those journals ask authors to do**. They do not make a paper true.

## Never say (billing / coding as instruction)

- “Bill 92507 this way.” “Always append KX after $X.” “Use this NCCI modifier.”
- Minute-by-minute 8-minute-rule worksheets, unit charts to copy into an EMR, or CPT crosswalks that look like a coding product.
- Guarantees of payment, audit-proof language, or “this modifier will protect you.”
- Invented MAC phone scripts.
- Advice to upcode, unbundle, or waive an ABN.

You may *name* the public rule and the public dollar. You may say “claims without the KX above the threshold are denied — that is CMS’s sentence.” You may not write the claim.

## Never say (treatment / DIY clinic)

- Swallow maneuvers, IDDSI kitchen tests as a recipe, FEES/VFSS protocols, voice exercise lists, Lidcombe session steps, CAS cueing hierarchies, AAC app settings, LSVT homework.
- “Try this thickened-liquid protocol.” “Start PPI.” “Skip the instrumental.”
- Dosing of medication, supplement stacks, “ask the doctor about [drug] for voice.”
- Guarantees of outcome (“IDDSI prevents pneumonia,” “thickened liquids stop aspiration pneumonia,” “early therapy will prevent…”)
- Copyrighted worksheet dumps (IDDSI test cards as images, published therapy data sheets, ASHA Practice Portal protocols pasted in).

## Never say (diagnosis / labeling)

- “Your patient has…” from a news brief.
- Invented case vignettes with ages + unique details that could be read as a real patient (“Maya, 72, on 3-south…”). Composite hallway scenes without names are allowed if they teach a *news* distinction.
- Eligibility promises for IDEA, Medicaid, or Medicare.

## Never say (research misconduct of the mouth)

- Invented PMIDs, fake n, or “most studies show” without a named review.
- Treating a preprint as a settled finding.
- Treating a systematic review’s abstract as the methods.
- Calling a journal predatory without a public, checkable fact (email solicitation + missing editorial board is a *pattern to notice*, not a lawsuit you file from a blog).
- “This paper proves treatment X works.”

## Never say (brand / SEO)

- “Best SLP news,” “#1 speech therapy,” city superlatives.
- Disease keywords paired with a booking CTA on these URLs.
- Schema `MedicalWebPage`, `MedicalCondition`, `Drug`, `treats` / `indication`. `Article` / `NewsArticle` is enough.
- Fake credentials or an invented clinical director.
- Patient testimonials in this folder.
- Affiliate wrappers on CMS.gov, ASHA.org, Medicaid.gov, PubMed, or IDEA.ed.gov.

## Allowed shapes

- Dated, sourced description of what CMS, ASHA, ED, IDDSI, AAO-HNS, and named papers actually printed.
- The difference between a **cap**, a **threshold**, a **review trigger**, and a **denial**.
- The difference between **caseload** and **workload**, **IEP need** and **medical necessity**, **NCD** and **LCD**.
- How to *read* a paper: abstract vs methods, p vs effect size, single-subject vs RCT, review vs shopping list, COI line, preprint.
- Practice *headlines*: IDDSI version news, the thickened-liquid evidence argument, FEES vs VFSS *access*, the PPI-for-hoarseness guideline, public fluency guidance vs old folklore.
- A short “what this is not” closer. Not a recap of H2s.

## Dollars, years, and honesty

CY 2026 figures in this pack (KX $2,480 PT+SLP combined; MR threshold $3,000 through CY 2027 as printed; conversion factors $33.40 / $33.57) are taken from CMS pages and ASHA’s public 2026 fee-schedule PDF, retrieved 2026-09-14. If you are reading this after a later CR, do not trust the integer — follow the live CMS Therapy Services page.

Telehealth: CMS’s CY 2026 PFS talk and ASHA’s November 2025 PDF described a shorter congressional window; the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 and CMS telehealth FAQ (updated 2026-02-26) extend practitioner authority through **December 31, 2027**. Prefer the later public documents. Say the earlier PDF is stale on that one point.

Mark unverified numbers `[VERIFY]`. Do not fabricate a CR number.

## Living people and real clinics

Do not invent a WOW Therapies / SLPWOW patient. Do not put Keith’s family in the copy. Public officials, guideline chairs, and researchers may be named for their papers. No health speculation.

## If asked for a protocol or a billing script

Print the news. Refuse the protocol and the claim in one sentence: this series does not teach treatment steps and does not file claims.

## Review stamp (before live)

A piece is not cleared for slpwow.com until:

1. Dollar figures and years match the live CMS/ASHA/ED page, or are marked `[VERIFY]`.
2. No line from the never-say lists remains.
3. Disclaimer is present.
4. Citations resolve.
5. A human editor has read it aloud once.
