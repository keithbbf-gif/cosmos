I used to treat every fig as the same
assignment. That is how the patio plant
died and the yard plant got a wrap it
did not need. They are two winters
that share a species.

The yard fig, in-ground, 8a, hardy
enough, a year or more in the dirt:
crown mound, wind siting, maybe a wrap
when young or when the model is cruel.
The earth does the rest. I judge
success by live wood or by a living
crown and a main crop, not by whether
it looked like a postcard in February.

The patio fig: roots in the air, my
schedule, my back. Garage or buried
pot or I am donating it to the night.
I judge success by whether I actually
moved it, not by the variety's zone
number.

Keeping both on one property is how
you eat in a greedy year and still
have figs in a vortex year. The yard
plant is the insurance. The patio
plant is the flavor you will not
commit to the dirt, or the plant you
will take if you move.

The mistake is letting them swap
methods. Wrapping a twenty-year
in-ground Celeste for a 26°F drizzle
because the patio fig taught you to
fuss. Leaving the patio fig out
because the yard fig taught you to
relax. Each plant trains you. You
have to remember which teacher you
are listening to.

Zone 7 may only have patio winters,
or yard winters that look like patio
work. Zone 9 may only have yard
winters plus a cloth. 8a is the
split-brain zone. Write the two
plans on the same card. Put the card
in the dolly. When the forecast
drops, you should not be inventing
a philosophy. You should be moving
one plant and leaving the other
alone.

I write the two names on the
calendar in November: YARD —
mulch, maybe wrap young. PATIO
— garage after frost. When I
am tired I only read the
capital letters. That is enough
to keep me from wrapping the
wrong plant and ignoring the
right one.

If you only have energy for one
winter, keep the yard fig and
give the patio fig to someone
with a dolly. One well-kept
method beats two half-kept
ones. I have run the half-kept
experiment. I can show you the
empty pots.

The patio fig also teaches watering. In the ground I can
be sloppy in a wet 8a. In a pot I cannot. If I use yard
habits on a patio plant in July, I cook it. If I use
patio fussing on a yard plant in January, I steam it.
The two plants are how I keep those habits from
contaminating each other.

When I travel in December, the yard fig is the one I
do not ask a neighbor to manage. The patio fig is the
one I either bring inside before I leave or I do not
own. A plant that requires a house-sitter to live is
a plant I have to be honest about.
