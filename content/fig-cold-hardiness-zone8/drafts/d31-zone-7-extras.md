When I talk to Zone 7 growers I borrow three extras I
do not use every year in 8a, and I try not to pretend
I am tougher than they are. Their average extreme
minimum is 0–10°F depending on 7a or 7b. Their cold
stays. Their list is not neurotic. It is arithmetic.

Extra one: they protect wood on purpose, not just the
crown. The cage is taller. The bundle is tighter. They
will tip-and-bury without making a joke of it. They
want last year's canes because their season can be
short, and a plant that has to rebuild from the soil
may not ripen a late fig before October.

Extra two: they prune to a winter shape in late fall —
not a hard fruit prune, a height prune — so the
structure they wrap is a structure they can cover.
Virginia Tech even mentions cutting back to a
manageable length as part of wintering. In 8a I am
stingier with fall cuts because I still want breba
in the good years. In 7 I understand the trade.

Extra three: they do not leave pots outside with a
story. The garage is the method. If there is no
garage, the pot is buried, or the plant is in the
ground with a mound that would embarrass an 8a
person.

What I do not borrow from 7: wrapping every plant
every year as identity. If I did that in a mild 8a,
I would cook bark and waste November. What 7 should
not borrow from 8a: "I never wrap." That sentence
is an 8a luxury about established plants in decent
sites. It is a Zone 7 eulogy.

If you live in 8a and you have a 7-shaped yard —
a hole, a wind alley, a north lot — use their extras
without shame. The zip code is not a dare. If you
live in 7 and your fig is on a south wall in a town
that the 2023 map painted warmer, you still had 7
nights in the last decade. Keep the extras. Enjoy
the easy years as easy years, not as a new
personality.

The extra I still resist, even when I
borrow their seriousness, is making
every fig a four-foot object forever.
I like a plant that can be a small
tree in an easy 8a year. So I keep
the 7 extras in a box — cage, bury
option, garage priority — and I use
them on the plants and the years
that look like 7. I do not use them
as my identity. Identity is how you
wrap a 28°F rain.

If you live in 7 and an 8a person
sounds casual, they are not braver.
They are warmer on average. Steal
their siting. Do not steal their
neglect.

If an 8a person visits a 7 yard in
March, they should look at the cages
without mocking them. Those cages are
why there is fruit in August. Mocking
is how we go home and skip a wrap we
needed on a young plant. Respect
travels both ways. Their extras are
not fear. Our skipped wraps are not
always wisdom.
