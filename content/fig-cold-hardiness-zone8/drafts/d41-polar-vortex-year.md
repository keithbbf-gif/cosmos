Once or twice a decade the map is a rumor.
The air that belongs in another country
sits on 8a like it pays rent. Single
digits. A long stay. Wind. Pipes. The fig
is not the most important thing in the
house that week, and that is the first
true sentence.

What I do anyway, in order:

Pots inside, even the ones I was being
stupid about. If the garage will hit
10°F, they come into a colder room of
the house, dark corner, not a sunny
window that will wake them. I can live
with a messy laundry room. I cannot live
with a collection of bricks.

Young in-ground figs: hat on the cage,
crown mounded, no new inventions at
10 p.m. Old stools: extra mulch if I
have it, and I walk away. They have
survived weather I have forgotten. I
will not build a plastic tent in a
windstorm and call it love.

Water the ground a day ahead if it is
dry. Then stop.

Afterward I do not prune on the first
50°F day. I wait. Vortex damage shows
up late. Wood that looks fine can
collapse in April. Fruit buds can be
gone on live canes. I take notes on
the actual low so I do not mythologize
it into "the year everything died"
or "the year I worried for nothing."

Zone 7 calls this winter. Zone 9 calls
this the story they will tell for ten
years, and they should have a cloth
and a plan for the tropicals, not a
fig religion. 8a should have a vortex
plan that is mostly about pots and
first-year plants. If your whole
method depends on 8a never seeing 5°F,
you do not have a method. You have a
hope the jet stream will stay polite.

I do not buy a new heat gadget after
these years. I buy a better dolly and
I plant the next fig closer to the
wall.

After the vortex I walk the yard
with the same card I used for
the lows. I do not buy seven new
varieties in March because I am
lonely for green. I wait to see
which crowns push. The year I
replanted in a panic I planted
into a wet cold spring and paid
twice.

Tell the truth to anyone who asks.
"I moved the pots and mulched the
stools and I still lost a young
one." That sentence helps a
neighbor more than a speech about
toughness. Vortex years are how
we remember that 8a is a statistic.
Statistics have tails.

I tell people not to judge a variety in
the spring after a vortex. Judge it the
following August. If it rebuilt and
ripened, it is still your plant. If it
rebuilt and missed the season, keep it
only if you like the flavor enough to
accept empty years. Vortex years are
honest. They are not the only years.
If you did nothing and some stools lived, write that down too. Vortex years also teach what was already tough.
