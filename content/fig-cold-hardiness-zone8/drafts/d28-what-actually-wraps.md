A fig you trained as a single tall trunk is a flagpole. A
fig you let become a stool of six canes is a bundle. I
know which one I can wrap in twenty minutes, and which
one I will walk away from and call "too big to protect."

Cold country, including the serious 7s, often keeps figs
as bushes on purpose. Virginia Tech says the same: bush
for harvest, for birds, for cold, because the wood is
near the ground. I let three to seven canes, cut the
oldest when they get stiff, and I have a plant I can
cinch and cage. A ten-year standard with scaffolding at
head height is a beautiful thing in Zone 9. In 8a it is
a dare you wrap with a ladder and a prayer.

Split trunks — the ones that fork low — wrap better than
they look if you treat each leader as a cane in a bundle,
not as architecture you must preserve. Winter is not the
time to be sentimental about a shape you made in June.

If you want a tree form in 8a, you are choosing looks
over convenience. Fine. Then the winter plan is: wall,
variety that holds wood, and an acceptance that you will
not bag the whole head. You wrap the young trunk those
first two years. After that you are in the "hope and
mulch" business, or you are building a structure that is
really a shed. I have seen the shed. It can work. It is
not a wrap. It is a building.

Zone 7: do not start a standard unless you like laying
it down. Zone 9: train whatever you want; winter is not
your sculptor. 8a: start as a bush even if you dream of
a tree. You can always pick a leader later, in a year
the plant has earned. You cannot easily un-tree a plant
on a Wednesday night when the model drops to 10.

The question is not "what looks like a fig tree." The
question is "what can I actually put a coat on." If the
answer is nothing, you do not have a wrap plan. You have
a plant.

If you already have a standard, you can
still make next year easier: let two
low suckers stay this summer. They are
backup if the trunk takes a hit, and
they are something you can cage if you
must keep wood. I know that ruins the
look. The look is a June problem. The
cage is a December problem. I let
December win until the plant is old
enough to argue.

A fig you cannot reach the top of is
a fig you will not wrap well. Keep
the winter plant at a height your
shoulders believe in.

A stool you can put two arms around is
a winter plant. A plant you need a
ladder and a second person to bundle
is a summer plant that you have
allowed to outgrow the coat. I cut
for next year's wrap in June, when
I can see, not in a panic in
November when every cut feels like
a crime.
