I love mulch. I also watch people pile
it like a volcano and think they have
wrapped a fig. They have wrapped the
crown, maybe. They have not wrapped
the canes. Those are different jobs.

Mulch is for the battery. A few inches
of leaves, chips, or straw over the
root zone, out past the drip if you
have it, keeps the top of the soil
from flashing with the air. Virginia
Tech talks 6 to 12 inches from the
center. I do not plaster wet mulch
into the bark like a cast. I leave
the stem able to dry. In 8a that
mound is the default on every
in-ground fig, including the ones I
will never wrap.

A coat is for wood. Burlap, cage,
cloth. If you only have energy for
one thing on an established hardy
fig, take the mulch. If you only
have energy for one thing on a
first-year or a pot, do not take
the mulch and call it done. The
young wood and the pot walls are
the weak parts.

Volcano mulch against a trunk is how
you keep the crown wet and invite
rot and rodents. I have done the
volcano. I have also unpacked a
crown in April and found bark that
looked like it had been in a sock.
Lower the pile. Widen it.

Zone 7 needs both mound and coat
more often. Zone 9 needs a mound
on the young and almost never a
coat. 8a needs a mound as
citizenship and a coat as a tool.
When someone says they mulched so
they do not have to think about
winter, I ask whether they meant
the roots or the plant they look
at from the kitchen. Those are
not the same plant on a 12°F
night.

What I use: shredded leaves,
aged chips, pine straw if that
is what the yard makes. What I
do not use: fresh manure as a
winter blanket, or a mountain
of wet grass. Grass heats and
slimes. Manure keeps the crown
awake and wet. I want the
crown asleep and insulated,
not fed.

In spring I pull the mound back
a little so the new shoots are
not punching through a wet
hat. Then the mulch becomes
summer mulch. Same pile. Two
jobs. Neither job is a wrap
for six feet of cane.

I demonstrate this for people with two hands. One hand on
the mulch in January — often not even frozen. One hand on
a bare cane at five feet — as cold as the air. That is the
whole essay in a gesture. Protect the hand that was warm
if you can only protect one thing on an old hardy stool.
Protect both on a child.

If the mulch blows off, I replace it. Wind eats mounds.
A mound that was six inches in November is a suggestion
by February. I do not call that "the plant didn't need
mulch." I call it weather doing what weather does to
loose leaves.
