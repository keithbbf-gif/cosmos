A good wrap is a coat with cuffs you can loosen. A bad wrap is
a bag. I have built both. The bag makes you feel like you did
something. The bag is also how you smell the plant in March
before you see it.

The sequence I actually use on an in-ground 8a fig I have
decided to wrap:

Strip leftover fruit and most leaves after a hard frost. Tie
the canes inward with something that will not girdle them —
cotton twine, strips of cloth. I am not making a telephone
pole. I am making a bundle I can put a cylinder around.

Crown first. Mulch 6 to 12 inches out, a few inches deep,
keeping wet piles off the exact bark if I can. The crown is
the plant if everything else fails. Virginia Tech's backyard
guide is with me here: protect the base.

Breathable inner layer. Burlap. Old canvas. Frost cloth.
Not a painting tarp. Not a trash bag. I want air that can
leave.

A cylinder if the plant is small enough — hardware cloth,
tomato cage, cardboard that I will hate in February. Loose
dry leaves or dry straw in the cylinder. Loose is the word.
Packed wet leaves are a compost tumbler with a fig in it.

A rain hat, not a rain coffin. A tarp or a plastic sheet
over the top, open at the bottom, so water sheds and air
walks through. If the whole thing is sealed to the soil,
I have built a still. Stills make alcohol. They also make
dead cambium.

I do not wrap tight to the bark with plastic. I do not use
the fig as the only post. Wind will take a sail. A stake
or the cage takes the load.

In 8a this whole construction is for young plants, tender
varieties, and the ugly forecast. It is not a religion I
perform on a twenty-year Celeste by the chimney. In Zone 7
it is closer to religion. In Zone 9 it is usually a mistake
if it stays up more than a few nights.

Unwrapping is part of wrapping. If you cannot imagine taking
it apart on a 55°F Saturday in February, do not build a
structure you need a saw to enter. The plant will have a
warm spell. You need to vent or strip the hat. Cooking is
not a spring problem only. Cooking is any week the wrap
outperforms the weather.

The smell test is real. If you walk by the wrap
on a 55°F day and it smells like a gym bag, vent
it or take the hat off. You are not "disturbing
dormancy." You are interrupting a rot. I would
rather a plant take 18°F for a night unwrapped
than sit in that smell until March.

I also write the wrap date on a strip of tape
on the cage. I have forgotten when I built a
thing and then left it on into a heat wave
because it had become furniture. The tape is
rude. I need rude.
