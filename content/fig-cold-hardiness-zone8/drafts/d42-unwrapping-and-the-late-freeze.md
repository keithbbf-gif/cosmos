Taking the coat off is how you lose the
wood you kept, if you do it like a person
who is sick of looking at burlap. I have
unwrapped on a glorious 68°F Friday and
watched 22°F show up on Tuesday. The plant
had started to move. The wrap had been the
only reason it had not moved sooner. I
helped the freeze find soft tissue.

I unwrap in pieces. Hat first, on a mild
spell, so the bundle can dry. Leaves out
of the cage a few days later. Burlap last,
or I leave a loose cloth for a week I do
not trust. The calendar I use is not a
date. It is nights mostly above the mid-
20s and a forecast that is not lying
about a late plunge. In 8a that is often
March and sometimes a rude April.

If I must unwrap because the wrap is
cooking — 80°F in a wet plastic accident —
I take it off and I keep the cloth nearby.
Cooking is now. A late freeze is later.
Later I can re-cover for a night. I cannot
un-steam bark.

Pots come out the way they came in, in
reverse: hours outside, back in if needed,
then a shaded day, then the sun. A pot
dumped onto a full-sun pad after three
months of garage dark will burn leaves it
does not even have yet, and then a freeze
will finish the joke.

Zone 7 unwraps later and should not feel
behind. Zone 9 "unwraps" by taking a cloth
off at 9 a.m. 8a is the zone that gets
false springs. False springs are why I
still own frost cloth in April.

The cocky loss is the one I remember: a
breba I had already counted, gone in a
night I called impossible because the
peaches had already bloomed. The peaches
were wrong too.

I keep the cage standing for a
week after the leaves come out of
it, empty, like a reminder. If a
late night shows up I can drop
cloth over a structure that still
exists. Taking the whole city
down on the first 70°F day is
how I have had to invent a wrap
at dusk from a bedsheet.

The bedsheet worked, once. I do
not want it to be the method.
The method is a staged teardown
and a cloth that has not been
put away in the attic yet.

A late freeze after a sloppy unwrap looks like
burned tips and a breba that yellows and drops.
The wood may still be fine. I do not recage the
whole plant for a week of revenge. I cloth the
nights that earn it. Revenge wrapping in April
is how you cook new leaves.

If you live where peaches bloom in February, you
already know this dance. The fig is not special.
It is just another plant you counted too early.
Staged teardown is the wrap's last job. A pile of burlap in the shed is not a teardown. It is a retreat.
