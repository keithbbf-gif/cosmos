I have a soft spot for figs that do not belong here. That soft spot has
cost me wood, pots, and one entire spring of pretending a Mission was
"just late." Some cultivars are not immoral in 8a. They are just asking
for a California winter and a dry summer, and I keep offering them a
Gulf shoulder and a 12°F night.

Black Mission wants long mild and then long dry. In a wet 8a the fruit
can sour, the plant can keep growing like it does not believe in
November, and then a real freeze finds wood that never hardened. People
still plant it because the name tastes like a grocery store in January.
If I grow Mission now, it is in a pot I can hide, or it is a hobby, not
a plan.

Kadota and some of the honey-green figs are the same kind of sulk —
doable on a wall in 8b/9, a project in 8a, a heartbreak in 7 if you
needed them to be a tree. Anything that needs the fig wasp is a science
project. We do not have the wasp. Parthenocarpic or go home.

Then there are the ones that are hardy enough and still sulk: poor
drainage, too much nitrogen in August, shade from a maple that was
"small when we moved in." Those plants die of the yard, not the zone.
I include them because variety-blaming is how you avoid looking at the
downspout.

A sulk list is personal. Mine is: unnamed "Italian" figs from big-box
endcaps, Mission in the open yard, any fig I planted in a decorative
pot that could not take a watering can without leaking onto the porch
paint. Your list will have a name I like. That is fine. Grow it as a
pot if you love it. Do not make it the plant you brag about to a
Zone 7 cousin as proof that 8a is easy.

Zone 9 can grow the sulkers and then lose them to a wet freeze they
swore would not happen. Zone 7 should not grow the sulkers in the
ground unless they enjoy digging graves. 8a can grow one sulker as
tuition. Pay it once. Take notes. Plant a Celeste beside it so the
year is not empty.

The sulk I regret most was not a Mission. It was a beautiful
green fig I bought because a man at a swap said it tasted like
honey and rain. It did, twice, in two mild years. The third
year was 11°F and wind. I had it in the ground in a decent
spot. The crown lived. The season was not long enough for
that particular fruit to finish on new wood. I ate nothing
and I learned the expensive version of "short-season matters
when wood dies."

If you need a sulker in your life, keep it in a pot you will
actually move. Pay the winter in sweat, not in a lost year.
The yard is for plants that can lose their tops and still
pay rent in August.
