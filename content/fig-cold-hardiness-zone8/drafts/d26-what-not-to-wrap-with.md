I keep a mental dumpster for fig wraps I will not build
again.

Sealed plastic. Contractor bags. Saran on a stick. Clear
sheeting taped to the soil. Virginia Tech says do not use
plastic because you are making a mold room. I say it
because I have opened one and the smell explained itself.
Plastic can be a hat. It cannot be the coat.

Wet hay that is already hot in the bale. You are installing
a compost pile on bark. Dry straw is a maybe. Wet hay is
a no.

Bubble wrap tight to canes. It sweats. It looks like you
tried. The plant pays.

Foam pipe insulation on every twig like the fig is a
plumbing run. Cute. Useless in wind. A good way to miss
the crown, which was the part that mattered.

Christmas paper, tablecloths, that shiny "emergency
blanket" from the trunk as a season-long wrap. Fine for
a two-hour radiation frost on a 9a citrus. Not a January
in 8a.

A wrap that depends on clothespins and hope. Wind will
vote.

What I will use: burlap, frost cloth, canvas, hardware
cloth, dry leaves, a tarp as a roof, twine, stakes. Ugly
materials. Repeatable. If a material cannot breathe and
cannot be taken off on a warm Tuesday, it does not belong
on a dormant fig for a month.

Zone 7 failures look like Zone 8a failures with more
time to rot. Zone 9 failures look like a plant that would
have been fine if you had left it alone with a mulch
ring. The dumpster is the same. The temptation is
stronger when a forecast graphic is red.

If you already wrapped in plastic, go cut vents at the
bottom today. Do not wait for a nice Saturday. The
nice Saturday is when it cooks.

A material test I use in the store: if I
would not sleep under it in the rain and
also would not want it sealed over my face,
it is not a fig coat. Breathable and
removable. That knocks out half the
impulse bin by the register.

I will allow plastic as a roof on a wet
week if the eaves drip into the cage. I
will not allow plastic as pants. The
difference is a gap at the soil. If you
cannot point to the gap, you do not have
a roof. You have a bag. Bags belong in
the dumpster I mentioned, preferably
before they have a plant in them.

I keep one photo of the plastic bag year on my
phone. The bark is orange and wet and the cane
has a slip like a banana. When I want a tidy
clear wrap because it would look "professional,"
I look at the photo. Professional is a word for
people who vent tunnels. I am a person with
burlap.

If a neighbor offers you a roll of sheeting,
take it for a hat and thank them, and do not
tape it to the dirt.
If the dumpster is full, you are still allowed to use less. Less is often the wrap that lives.
