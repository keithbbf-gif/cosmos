The garage is not a greenhouse. That is why it
works. I want a place that stays mostly 30–45°F,
dark or dim, quiet, and boring. A fig in that
room goes to sleep and stays there. A fig in a
sunroom at 65°F tries to be a houseplant and
then gets spider mites and confusion.

Protocol I actually follow:

Move after a hard frost has taken the leaves,
or after I have stripped them. I do not move a
fully leafy pot into the dark and hope. I do
not wait until the ball is already a brick on
the porch.

I water so the soil is just moist, then I stop
fidgeting. Monthly is a default. The finger is
the law.

I do not put the pot on the freezer or next to
the water heater. One is an icebox. One is a
spring. Middle of the floor, away from the
car's nose if I can, on a board if the slab
weeps cold.

Mice like the garage as much as they like the
leaf cage. I do not store bird seed on the fig.
I do check for girdling if I hear them.

In late winter I do not "wake it up" because I
am tired of stepping around it. I wait until
the outdoor nights are mostly above the low
20s and I can start a hardening-off: a few
hours out, back in if a freeze returns.
Virginia Tech describes that dance. It is
annoying. It is cheaper than losing the
plant to a mid-March 19°F after a week on
the deck.

Zone 7 lives or dies on this protocol. Zone 9
uses the garage maybe two nights a decade, or
never, if they can roll the pot to a wall.
8a uses it every year for the plants they
refused to put in the ground. If the garage
hits 20°F on the worst night, I add a blanket
around the pot walls, not a space heater I
will forget.

If your garage is heated to 60 because you
work out there, it is not this protocol. It
is a living room. Find a shed, a cold corner,
or put the fig in the ground and tell the
truth.

I label pots when they go in, because
in February they all look like sticks
and I will put the tender one back
out first by mistake. A strip of tape
with the name and "garage until nights
>25" has saved a sulker.

If the garage is shared with a car,
I put pots where a bumper will not
argue. A fig that survives January
and dies to a parking job is still
a winter loss. I have told that
story as if the weather did it.
The weather did not.

If you do not have a garage, a
closed shed, a cold pantry, or a
buried pot are the cousins. A
sunroom is not a cousin. A heated
workshop is not a cousin. I keep
repeating the temperature band
because people keep "helping" figs
into spring in January.
