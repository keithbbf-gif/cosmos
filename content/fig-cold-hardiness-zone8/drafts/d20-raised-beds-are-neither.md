I planted a fig in a pretty raised bed because the yard was a
sheet of water in February and I wanted drainage. I got drainage.
I also got a root zone that behaved like a wide, shallow pot
with wooden sides. The bed froze from the walls. The fig lived,
barely, and I spent the next autumn heaping mulch against the
lumber like I was sandbagging a creek.

A raised bed is not in-ground. The earth-battery is thinner. The
sides are radiators, especially raw wood and metal. In 8a that
can still be fine if the bed is broad and you mulch the interior
and the exterior. A narrow 4×4 box with a fig in the corner is a
pot with extra steps. In Zone 7 I would not do it unless I was
ready to wrap the whole box.

The gift is real: drainage, warmer soil in spring, a place to
grow a fig where the lawn wants to be a swamp. Nematodes can be
a little less awful in fresh fill, for a while. The bill comes
in January. Pay it with:

- The widest bed you can live with, not a strip.
- Fill that includes mineral soil, not a two-foot tub of potting
  mix that dries and freezes like cake.
- Mulch over the whole surface, and leaves stacked against the
  outside walls before the first hard stay of cold.
- No metal trough unless you enjoy conducting the night into the
  roots.

If the bed is tall and skinny, treat the plant as a pot: you may
need to wrap the box, or accept dieback, or not put your favorite
fig there. If the bed is a broad mound that fades into the yard,
you are closer to in-ground and I stop nagging.

Zone 9 raised beds are mostly a summer and nematode conversation.
Zone 7 raised beds are a winter conversation that people skip
because the lumber looked so clean in April. 8a is where I see
the hybrid death — "it's in soil, so it's fine" — on a box that
has more air contact than a whiskey barrel.

I still use beds. I just do not lie about what they are. They
are a third category. They need a third winter.

I salvaged my bed fig by widening the box into a
berm the second year — knocked a side out, tied it
into the yard, and suddenly the roots had somewhere
to go that was not a wooden oven. If you cannot
widen, treat the bed like a pot: mulch the outside
walls, wrap the box on the ugly night, or accept
dieback as the rent.

Do not put your only fancy fig in the prettiest
narrow bed by the walk. Put the floor variety
there if you must have a fig in that lumber.
Pretty is a summer word. January will not read it.

Metal troughs look clean and conduct
like they are paid to. I will grow
lettuce in them. I will not
overwinter a fig in them without
treating the trough like a pot:
move, wrap the walls, or accept
the freeze-through. Pretty metal
is still a cliff.
