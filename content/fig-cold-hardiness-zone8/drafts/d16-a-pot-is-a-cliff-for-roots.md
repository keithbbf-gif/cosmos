A pot looks like a small garden. In January it is a cliff. The roots
are standing in the air, on every side, with only a half-inch of
plastic or fabric between them and the night. In-ground roots fail
last. Pot roots fail first. I can say that a hundred times and still
watch someone leave a five-gallon fig on a porch table because the
canes look woody.

The canes are not the conversation. The conversation is the ball.
When that ball goes below the mid-twenties and stays there, you are
in the injury range even if the variety is "hardy to 6." Hardiness
ratings assume soil. Your patio is not soil.

What I do with pots in 8a, in order of how much I like the plant:

1. Unheated garage or closed shed, once nights are regularly in the
   20s and a hard freeze is done with the leaves. Dark is fine.
   Dormant figs are not houseplants.
2. If I cannot move it, I set the pot on bare ground on the south
   side, pack leaves or mulch around the pot walls, and wrap the
   pot, not just the twigs. The twigs are the pretty part. The
   walls are the radiator.
3. If the forecast is a long stay under 20, I do not negotiate. The
   pot comes in, even if I have to move the bicycle.

Water is the other cliff. A bone-dry root ball freezes harder and
thaws like a brick. A soggy root ball rots in a garage. I want
slightly moist, like a cake that is two days old. Virginia Tech says
monthly water in winter until just moist. That matches what I do,
except I poke a finger instead of watching the calendar if the garage
is dry.

Zone 7 pots are not optional housing. They are the method. Zone 9
pots are for decks and nematodes and renters; winter is a cold snap
you can roll the pot away from. 8a pots are a choice you have to
keep making every November. If you will not make it, plant in the
ground or give the fig to someone who will.

Fabric pots drain and freeze faster than thick plastic. Glazed
ceramic looks like a gift and cracks. Black plastic in January is
ugly and useful. I know which one I pick when I care about the
roots.

I used to wrap the twigs of a patio fig like I was putting
a child in a scarf and leave the pot naked. The scarf
looked like care. The roots died anyway. Now if I can only
wrap one thing on a pot I cannot move, I wrap the walls
and I set the pot on dirt. The twigs can take more than
the ball. That reversal annoyed me the first year because
it is ugly. Ugly is the correct aesthetic for January.

If you have ten pots, you do not have a hobby. You have
a chore list. Write the list in November: which three
get the garage first, which can cluster on dirt, which
you will give away because you will not haul them. A
fig you will not move is already dead. The night is
just the paperwork.
