The urge to wrap arrives on the first cool weekend, when the
leaves are still green and the garden centers have put out
burlap. That urge is how you trap a plant that has not gone
to sleep. Figs need a few real chills to harden. They need to
drop leaves. They need you to stop watering them like it is
August. A coat in October is a greenhouse with no off switch.

I wait for three things, and I will skip a wrap entirely if
they never line up with a real threat:

1. Leaves gone, or gone enough that I can strip the rest after
   a frost. Virginia Tech uses a hard frost under 29°F as the
   moment to clear leftover fruit and foliage. That is the
   right kind of clock.
2. Wood that looks finished — gray, not succulent, not still
   pushing tips.
3. A forecast that is actually doing the 8a job: mid-teens, or
   a long stay in the 20s with wind, or a young plant that
   has not earned neglect.

If it is early December and we are living at 38°F and rain, I
do not wrap an established in-ground fig just to feel employed.
I might move pots into the garage because pots do not get to
vote. I might stage the cage and the dry leaves on the porch
so I am not shopping twine at 9 p.m. on a Wednesday when the
model drops.

Too late is also real. If the Arctic shot is twelve hours away
and the plant is still a wet leafy mess, I do a crude version:
strip what I can, crown mound, frost cloth, no sealed plastic.
A bad wrap in a hurry beats a perfect wrap that exists only in
my head. It does not beat moving a pot.

Zone 7 wraps earlier in the season because their cold rents.
They still should not wrap a leafy plant in October. They wrap
a dormant plant for a long stay. Zone 9 should almost never
have a wrapping season. They have an event. 8a has a window
that opens after Thanksgiving and closes when the forsythia
starts lying about spring. I do not live in that window with
a wrapped fig from November to April unless the plant is young
or the winter is a known brute.

If you remember only the mistake: early wrap + wet + plastic
is not caution. It is a project against your own bark.

A worked 8a week: Thanksgiving is 45 and rain. I
do not wrap. I move the pots after the first
hard frost the following week. Mid-December the
model shows 17°F with wind on Thursday. Wednesday
I bundle the first-year in-ground fig, hat on,
crown already mounded. The old Celeste by the
wall gets nothing but a glance. Friday I check
the hat. I do not open the bundle. That is a
season. It is not a lifestyle.

If you missed the window and the cold is tonight,
do the crude version and forgive the aesthetics.
A blanket and a cardboard box with the bottom
open has saved a young plant I had ignored. A
perfect cage that exists next Saturday has saved
no one.
