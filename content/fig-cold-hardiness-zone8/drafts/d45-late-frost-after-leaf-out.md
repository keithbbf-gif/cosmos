The January low is the one we train for.
The April frost is the one that takes
the crop we already counted. Leaves
out, breba swelling, a night that
looks harmless on a phone because it
says 31°F and we have gotten stupid
about 31. Radiation frost in a still
sky will burn the first leaves and
the baby figs while the wood stays
fine. You have a living plant and a
ruined early season. That is not
death. It feels like it.

I keep a frost cloth in April for this.
I throw it over the bush at dusk if
the sky is clear and the wind is dead
and the forecast is flirting with
freezing. I take it off in the morning.
I do not build a cage. I do not plug
in lights. This is a night, not a
climate.

Pots that leafed in a warm garage and
then sat out for a pretty week are
the most vulnerable. They believed
you. Harden them, and be willing to
roll them back for one more night.
The embarrassment of a pot in the
kitchen in April is smaller than the
embarrassment of a bald fig in May.

If the frost hits and the leaves go
black, wait. Figs will push again.
Do not strip every leaf in a rage.
Do not drown it. Do not empty a bag
of fertilizer on the trauma. The
main crop can still happen on new
growth if the summer is long enough,
which in 8a it usually is.

Zone 7 gets this too, and they are
less shocked. Zone 9 gets it on
peaches and figs after a warm
February, and they should own cloth
even if they will never own a
January wrap. 8a gets false springs
as a personality. I have stopped
calling a fig "safe for the year"
until the oak is leafing. The oak
is a better liar than I am, and
still better than the ten-day
forecast.

The fruit you counted is not in the
pantry. It is on a branch in a still
night. Cover it or let it go, but
do not be surprised.

I have a cloth that lives on a
hook by the back door from March
through the oak. Not in the attic.
Not in the bin under the seed
potatoes. The reason I lose late
fruit is almost never that I did
not own cloth. It is that I owned
it in the wrong room.

If the frost is a surprise at
6 a.m. and you did not cover,
do not hose the plant with hope.
Let it thaw in the shade. Then
wait. The black leaves will tell
you by afternoon what you lost.
The plant will tell you by May
whether it cared.

I have used an overturned bucket on
a tiny first-year for a late frost
when I could not find the cloth.
The bucket worked. I took it off
at breakfast so I did not bake the
plant at noon. Late frost tools
can be stupid as long as they are
temporary.
