If I cannot get a pot into a garage, I can still
give it a fake in-ground winter. I dig a hole
the pot can sit in, drop it so the rim is near
grade, and mulch over the soil of the pot and
the seam. The sides are no longer radiators.
The ball is back in the planet's battery. It
is ugly. It is effective. It is how Zone 7
people keep a collection when the shed is
full.

In 8a I do this for pots I was lazy about, or
for a winter I do not trust, or for a fabric
pot that would freeze through on a porch in
one long night. I do not do it for every pot
every year, because digging in December clay
is a decision you feel in your wrists.

Rules that keep this from becoming a drowning:

- The hole must drain. A pot in a sump is a
  bucket in a bucket.
- Plastic pots come back out. Fabric pots can
  stay, but they tear, and they also grow
  roots into the yard, which is how a "pot"
  becomes an in-ground plant without your
  permission. Sometimes that is a gift.
  Sometimes it is a Mission in a place you
  did not choose.
- Mark the rim. I have mulched over a pot so
  well I kicked it in March.
- In spring, lift and check drainage holes.
  Worms and mud will have opinions.

Zone 9 almost never needs this. If they do
it, it is for summer watering theater, not
winter. Zone 7 should learn it before they
need it. 8a uses it as the middle path
between "I will definitely move this" and
"I definitely will not."

A lighter version: cluster the pots against
the south wall, pack leaves around all the
walls, and throw cloth over the tops for
the ugly night. Not as good as a hole. Much
better than a single pot on a table. The
table is how you advertise the root ball to
the sky.

I dig the hole before the ground
locks. That sounds obvious. I have
still waited until the forecast was
red and then tried to chop 8a clay
with a mood. The hole is a November
job. The drop-in is a December job.
Split them.

In spring I do not yank a buried pot
on the first warm day and leave it
on the pad. I lift, drain, and park
it on dirt for a week of
hardening-off, same as a garage
pot. The plant does not know it
was "almost in-ground." It knows
it just got a new climate in an
afternoon.

If the water table is high, burying a
pot is how you make a well. I test a
hole with a rain before I commit a
plant to it. If the test hole holds
water, I skip this method and I cluster
on the surface with leaves. A buried
pot in a well is a drowning with extra
dirt.
