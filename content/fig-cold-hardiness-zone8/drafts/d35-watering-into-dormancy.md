A fig that goes into a freeze drunk on August
water and late nitrogen is soft. A fig that
goes into a freeze as a dry bone, especially
in a pot, is brittle in the roots. I am trying
to thread a needle I cannot see, which is why
I use the same boring rule every year.

In late summer I ease off the fertilizer. No
"one more feeding" in September so I can feel
like a grower. Soft tips are a January invoice.
Water still happens in a hot September because
the plant is a plant, but I am not keeping the
soil like a rice paddy.

After leaf drop, in-ground 8a figs in ordinary
rain get nothing from me unless we have a
drought into a hard freeze. Then I water the
root zone a day or two before the night, so
the earth battery is not a dry dust that
swings temperature like air. Wet-not-soggy.
If the yard is already clay soup, I do not
add to it.

Pots are stricter. They dry in wind even when
the sky looks winter. A dry ball freezes
through and stays frozen. A wet ball in a
garage sits and rots. Slightly moist, monthly,
finger test. That sentence is in more
extension guides than mine.

The dry-root freeze is the death people blame
on variety. The pot was light. The night was
18°F. The canes looked alive for a month and
then never leafed, because the roots had
cooked in ice. I have thrown that plant out
and I have deserved to.

Zone 7 pots need the same moisture habit in a
colder garage. Zone 9 in-ground plants need
the opposite reminder: stop watering on a
schedule when the rain is already doing it,
or you will keep them growing into the one
freeze you get. 8a is the place both mistakes
are available in the same November.

A pot I can lift with two fingers in
December is a pot I failed in November.
I have done that lift, cursed, watered,
and then watched the ball freeze anyway
because I watered the night of the
cold, not the day before. Timing
matters. Water, wait, then freeze if
you must. Do not water onto a freeze
and call it a drink. That is a glaze.

In-ground clay is the opposite lecture:
do not add water to a slick yard
because a video from sand country
said to water before a freeze. Know
your dirt. The rule is moisture, not
a ceremony.

I stick a wooden chopstick in pots I do
not trust my finger on. If it comes out
dry, water. If it comes out dark and
smelling, wait. It is not science-fair
pretty. It is better than a schedule
that ignores the actual ball.

In a drought-into-freeze in the yard, I
water in the morning so the soil can
take it before night. I do not sprinkle
the canes. Canes do not drink that way
in January. Roots do.
