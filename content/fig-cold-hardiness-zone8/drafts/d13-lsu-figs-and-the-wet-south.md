Louisiana State University did not breed figs so a person in a dry Zone 6
could win a Facebook argument. They bred them for heat, humidity, and a
long season where rust and souring will take a crop before a January night
does. LSU Purple, LSU Gold, LSU Tiger, Improved Celeste — that family
makes more sense in a wet 8a and in 9 than another "hardy" fig whose
entire personality is surviving Illinois.

I plant them because my summers look like theirs. Closed or tighter eyes
on some of them. Flavor that can stand up to a July that does not quit.
A habit of making a main crop you can actually ripen. Winter is secondary
on these, which is not the same as winter being irrelevant. In 8a I still
site them out of the wind and I still mulch. I do not expect LSU Purple
to laugh at a 7a week the way Chicago Hardy might. If I lived on the
cold edge of 7, I would not make an LSU fig my only in-ground plant.

This is the part 8a people skip when they shop by hardiness number alone.
Hardiness number is a winter statistic. Your plant has to live through
July. A fig that keeps wood at 10°F and then rusts itself bald every
August is not a better plant than one that dies back once a decade and
feeds you in between. I have both kinds. I eat more from the second.

Zone 9 should start here and with Celeste, then get fancy. Zone 7 should
treat LSU figs as pot plants or wall plants unless they have a proven
microclimate and a sense of humor. 8a inland, especially the parts that
are really "hot wet 8a," is LSU country that keeps borrowing Chicago
advice because Chicago advice is what the search engine likes.

If you take one home, do not wrap it in November "because figs get
wrapped." Watch the plant for two winters. If it holds wood with a
crown mound and a wall, let it. If it dies back and still ripens a
main crop by late August, you already won. You do not owe the internet
a cage.

I lost an LSU Purple the year I treated it like a Chicago plant
— late wrap, wet leaves, a tarp I forgot to vent in a 60°F
week. The cold did not kill it. I did, with a northern ritual
in a southern January. That is the hazard of shopping by
hardiness videos filmed in a different humidity.

If you are in a dry 8a (there are some, west and upland), LSU
figs still work, they just are not the only answer. If you are
in a wet 8a, they should be near the top of the list *after*
you have a floor plant. I would not make LSU Purple my only
fig in a windy 7b lot. I would make it my second fig in 8a
dirt that already grows okra like it is showing off.

Rust will still visit. Winter hardiness does not cancel a
fungus. Prune for air in summer. Do not confuse a rusty
August with a failed January.
