An established fig in 8a and a stick you planted in May are not the
same species as far as January is concerned. The established plant
has a root plate, stored sugar, wood that hardened over a real
autumn. The first-year plant has optimism. I wrap the second. I
mulch both. I do not take advice from a person whose "never wrap in
8a" sentence is about a twenty-year stool by a chimney.

First year, in the ground:

- Plant in spring if you can, so the roots get a summer. Fall
  planting works if the soil is still working and you are honest
  about water, but I do not fall-plant a fig and then leave town.
- Mulch the crown after the ground is cold, not in September while
  the soil is still a radiator you are trying to keep open.
- A simple breathable wrap or a leaf-filled cage is cheap insurance.
  You are not wrapping a tree. You are wrapping a teenager.
- Do not fertilize after midsummer. Soft wood is a winter insult
  you paid for at the garden center.

First year, in a pot: the garage is not optional. A one-year root
ball is small. Small balls freeze through. I do not care how hardy
the tag was.

The second winter is a negotiation. If the plant put on real wood
and the roots have shown they can drink, I may leave an in-ground
8a fig with only a crown mound, especially Chicago Hardy or Celeste
on a wall. If it was a sulker, or a late planting, or a windy
site, it gets year-one treatment again. Plants do not age on the
calendar. They age on roots.

Zone 7 first-years should be potted or caged without a debate. Zone
9 first-years still get a frost cloth on the one ugly night, because
young bark is thin and people in 9 are out of practice. 8a first-
years are where I see the most bravado. Bravado is a great way to
buy the same variety twice.

If you only have energy to protect one plant this winter, protect
the youngest. The old one has already voted. The young one is still
asking whether this yard is real.

The first winter is also when people overwater because
they are nervous, or underwater because they think
dormant means stone. In the ground, after leaf drop,
I am mostly leaving it to rain unless we are dry into
a hard freeze. In a pot, I am the rain. Nervous water
in a garage is how you rot a first-year you wrapped
perfectly.

I flag first-year plants with a stake so I do not
mow the crown or forget which one is allowed to be
ugly. March sticks are easy to "clean up" if you
have forgotten they are children. The stake is a
note to my future self, who is careless.

I do not judge a variety by its
first winter. I judge whether I
protected it. If I did not, and it
dies, I have learned about me. If
I did, and it dies, I have learned
about the plant or the hole. Mixing
those lessons is how we slander
good figs.
