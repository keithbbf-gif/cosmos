The worst thing you can do to a decent wrap is
love it every Saturday. Peeking dumps the warm,
lets the rain in, and turns a coat into a hobby.
The second worst thing is never looking, so the
hat collects a pond and the bottom seals with
ice.

I check without undressing the plant.

From the outside: is the hat still a hat? Has
the wind opened a flue on the north side? Is
the cylinder leaning onto the canes? Is water
pooling at the base? I fix those with rocks,
a tie, a shove, a trench in the mulch. I do
not open the whole thing because I am curious
whether the wood is "okay." The wood is asleep.
My curiosity is not a season.

On a 50°F afternoon in a wet 8a January, I will
vent. Lift the hat an hour. Let the steam leave.
Put the hat back before night if the freeze is
returning. That is a check. That is not a
unwrap.

Pots in the garage get a finger in the soil once
or twice a month. If it is dust, a little water.
If it is mud, I leave it and I swear at myself
for the last watering. I do not drag the pot
into the sun "to cheer it up." Dormant figs do
not need cheer. They need not to break dormancy
on a washer lid in February.

Zone 7 checks are about snow load and mice and
whether the tunnel still has an air gap. Zone 9
checks are "is the cloth still on the one night
I used it." 8a checks are the vent-and-hat
habit, plus the garage finger.

If you need to see green wood to sleep, scratch
one cane at the edge, not the whole bundle. Then
go inside. The plant cannot heal your anxiety.
It can only lose its coat while you try.

I give myself two scheduled looks: one
after the first real stay of cold, one
on the first weird warm week. That is
enough for an 8a wrap. Daily looking
is a different hobby. If you want a
daily hobby, keep a thermometer log.
The fig does not need to be opened
for you to have a ritual.

If the cage has a mouse hole, I fix
the hole and I do not poison the
crown. If the hat has flown, I put
it back with a heavier rock and I
do not rebuild the philosophy. Most
midwinter "failures" are hardware.
Treat them as hardware.

I also check the neighbor's wrap, if they
want. A second set of eyes finds the flue
you stopped seeing. I have had a friend
point at a puddle I had walked around for
three weeks. Bring someone. Or take a
photo and look at it in the kitchen, where
you are not already committed to the
structure being fine.
If you only remember one check: the warm wet week. That is when coats become problems.
