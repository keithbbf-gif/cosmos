The thermometer on the shaded porch said 13°F. The fig in the open was living
a colder life than that. Wind does not always drop the air temperature on a
cheap sensor, but it strips the little envelope of warmer air a plant keeps
around its wood. It dries canes. It finds the one gap in your burlap and
plays it like a flute all night. I have started thinking of an exposed 8a
yard as 7b with extra paperwork.

You can feel this without a fig. Stand on the south side of the house at 8
p.m. in January, then walk to the corner where two streets pour the north
wind into your lot. Same zip code. Different zone.

Siting is wind work more than it is USDA work. A south or southwest wall does
three jobs: it blocks the fetch, it stores a little day-heat in the masonry,
and it keeps you from planting a fig as a wind vane. An evergreen hedge on
the north side is not decoration. A fence with gaps is almost useless; the
wind just organizes itself into jets. I learned that with a "privacy fence"
that turned out to be a wind machine aimed at a first-year Chicago Hardy. The
plant lived. The wood did not. The fence is still there. The fig is not in
front of it.

Pots are worse in wind because they rock, they dry, and the whole root ball
is in the airstream. A fabric pot in a north wind is a radiator. If you
cannot move it into a garage, at least get it on the ground on the lee of
the house and block the pot walls. The plant can lose a winter from the
roots while the canes still look brave.

Wrapping against wind is a different craft than wrapping against still cold.
You need the coat to stay put. Twine that would hold on a calm night will
saw through burlap by February. I use more ties than I want to admit, and I
put a stake or a cage in so the wrap is not hanging on the fig's own
shoulders. A tarp that becomes a sail will take a young plant out of the
ground. That is not a metaphor. I have replanted a fig that left with its
coat.

Zone 7 people already talk this way. Zone 9 people meet wind as a summer
drying problem and then get blindsided by a dry freeze with 20 mph nights.
8a is wind country in a lot of the interior South and Mid-Atlantic. If your
forecast is 15°F and "breezy," I treat it as a wrap-or-move night even if
last year's still 15°F did nothing.

A cheap check: on a cold windy afternoon, put your hand on the fig wood in
the open and on the fig wood by the wall. You will not need a spreadsheet.
Plant the next one where your hand was less unhappy.
