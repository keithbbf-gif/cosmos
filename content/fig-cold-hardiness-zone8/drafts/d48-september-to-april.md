I do not run figs on a pretty calendar
with icons. I run them on a few
habits that sit on the same months
every year in 8a. The dates slide.
The order does not.

September. I stop feeding. I pick
what is ripe. I water if the heat is
still a hammer. I do not plant a
fig in a rush unless the soil is
kind and I will be home.

October. Leaves start to look tired.
I do not wrap. I stage: cloth, twine,
leaves drying, dolly where I will
trip over it. I look at which pots
I have been lying about.

November. Frosts come. I strip fruit
that will not ripen. I move pots
after the leaves are gone or after
I take them. I mound crowns when
the ground is cooling, not when it
is still a summer radiator.

December–January. I watch hours, not
just lows. I wrap the young and the
sulkers if the ribbon lies down in
the teens. I leave the old stools
alone unless the air is coming from
another country. I vent a wet wrap
on a 50°F afternoon. I do not peek
for fun.

February. The dangerous warmth. I
keep the hat handy. I do not prune
because I am bored.

March. Scratch, wait, cloth for a
late night. Pots start their
awkward commute. I do not call the
year safe.

April. Last rude frost. Cloth again.
Then I put the kit away, but not
far. May is for something else.

Zone 7 slides this whole thing later
and heavier. Zone 9 compresses it
into a cloth and a mulch ring. 8a
is this sequence, with permission
to skip the wrap in a winter that
never earns it.

If you want one page on the fridge,
this is the page. Not a variety
chart. A year of not getting
lonely in October and not getting
cocky in March.

I reread this sequence every
September because I will try to
skip October staging and then
shop twine in a panic. The
calendar is a fence for me, not
for the fig. The fig does not
need a planner. I do.

If a month goes weird — a
70°F Christmas, a freeze in
late April — I do not rewrite
the whole year. I do the local
move: vent, cloth, wait. The
sequence is a spine. The
weather is the flesh. I do not
throw out the spine because
one month grew a lump.

I put the sequence on the same card as the
two-plant plan. September stop feed. October
stage. November move pots. December watch
hours. February do not prune. March scratch.
April cloth. That is eight verbs. If I cannot
remember eight verbs I cannot remember a
gadget either.

The year I skipped staging I bought wet leaves
from a landscaper and built a slime cage. The
sequence exists to keep me from shopping in a
feeling.
If you forget the rest, remember October staging and March waiting. Those two hold the year together.
