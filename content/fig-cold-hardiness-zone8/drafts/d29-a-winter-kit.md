I used to buy fig winter gadgets. I now buy materials
that have a summer job too.

The kit that has lasted:

- A roll of burlap or a stack of frost cloths I also use
  for late tomatoes and the one camellia.
- Cotton twine and a few stakes.
- A roll of hardware cloth that became two cages and a
  compost sieve.
- A cheap tarp with grommets, reserved as a hat, not as
  a coat.
- Leaves in paper bags under the porch, labeled like I
  am a person who labels things. I am not, except in
  November.
- A min-max thermometer I actually look at.
- Mulch I already needed for the rest of the yard.
- A furniture dolly, because the pot I swore I could
  lift in May is a different pot in December.

What left the kit: heat cables, decorative wraps, a
"fig jacket" that fit no fig I owned, the emergency
blankets, the dream of a custom wooden house I would
build on a clean Saturday.

Zone 7 adds more cage wire and more leaves. Zone 9
subtracts almost everything except the frost cloth and
the dolly. 8a sits with the list above and a decision,
each year, about whether the cage comes out of the shed.

I stage the kit before I need it. That is the whole
trick. The night you need burlap, everyone else needs
burlap. The night you need dry leaves, the leaves are
wet. I look like a hoarder in October. I look prepared
in January, which is the only month the neighbors do
not joke about the bags.

If you have twenty dollars and one fig, buy frost cloth
and twine and steal leaves. If you have a collection,
buy the dolly first. Your back is part of the plant's
hardiness.

I put the kit in one bin, not scattered
through the shed. The night I need it I
am not in a hunting mood. The bin lives
where I will kick it in October, which
is how I remember to dry leaves. A kit
you cannot find is not a kit. It is
storage.

Replace the twine each year if it went
through a wet winter. Replace the tarp
when it rips. Do not replace the whole
philosophy every time a gadget ad loads.
The dolly is the only upgrade that
changed my winter. Everything else was
already in the yard if I was willing
to look ugly.

I lend the kit out. That is how I know what I
actually use. Nobody borrows the fig jacket. They
borrow cloth, twine, and the dolly. After a year
of lending I stopped owning the rest.

If you are starting from zero this afternoon: go
home with cloth and a ball of twine. Rake leaves
onto a tarp and put the tarp under the porch. That
is a kit. You can buy wire next Saturday. You
cannot buy last week's dry leaves next Saturday.
I check the bin in September the way I check the
flashlight drawer before a storm. Same instinct.
Different plant.
