Zone 8a is where fig people get cocky and then get surprised. The map says the
average annual extreme minimum sits between 10 and 15°F. That is not fig country
the way a courtyard in Zone 10 is fig country. It is also not the Michigan problem,
where you lay the whole bush down and bury it like a secret. It is the in-between,
which is a worse teacher than either extreme, because some winters you do almost
nothing and eat two crops, and the next winter you do almost nothing and look at
a bundle of gray sticks in March.

I keep a cheap min-max thermometer on the north side of the shed for this reason.
The zone map is a thirty-year average of the worst night. Your yard is a night.
If that night is 18°F and still, an established Celeste against a brick wall will
often keep its wood. If that night is 11°F and the wind is emptying the heat out
of every cane, the same plant can die back to a stub you will not trust until
May. Both nights are legal 8a.

Zone 7 people already assume they are in a fight. They mulch the crown like they
mean it. They wrap, or they grow in pots and drag the pots. They plant Chicago
Hardy and Improved Celeste and they are not offended when the top dies. Zone 9
people have a different fight: heat, nematodes, rust, souring in the rain, and
the occasional freak freeze that they are not emotionally prepared for. They
should not wrap as a lifestyle. Wrapping in a winter that barely kisses 25°F
is how you steam a dormant fig and teach it mildew.

8a sits in the doorway and hears both conversations. The useful move is to steal
*selectively*. Steal Zone 7's respect for the crown and for the first two winters.
Steal Zone 9's refusal to wrap on a schedule. Keep your own habit, which is to
watch the forecast like it is a person who has lied to you before.

I do not treat 8a as "hardy enough, skip protection." I treat it as: most winters
the earth and a south wall are enough for the right variety, once the plant has
a real root system. Young plants do not get that deal. Neither do pots. Neither
does a fig you stuck in a wind tunnel between two houses because that was where
the hose reached.

The in-between also messes with advice from the internet. A grower in 8b coastal
air will tell you they never wrap. A grower in 7a will show you a photo of a fig
in a leaf-stuffed cage that looks like a science fair. Both are telling the
truth about their nights. If you copy the 8b person in an inland 8a pocket that
drops to 10°F twice a decade, you will eventually pay. If you copy the 7a cage
every year in a mild 8a, you will spend November building a monument to a cold
that does not arrive, and you may hold moisture against the bark until a warm
spell in January cooks it.

So the first fact is not a variety name. The first fact is that 8a is a range
with a personality problem. Plan for the middle. Budget one extra move for the
ugly night. Do not build your whole winter around a YouTube from another zone.
And write down what the thermometer actually did, because next September you
will remember the figs and forget the low.
