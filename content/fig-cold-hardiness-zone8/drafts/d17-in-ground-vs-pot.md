People pick a side. In-ground people talk about "real plants" and
show you a trunk. Pot people talk about control and show you a
garage. Both are trying not to feel foolish when March comes. I keep
both, on purpose, and I assign them different jobs.

In-ground is for the figs I want in ten years. Celeste. Chicago
Hardy. A Turkey that has already proved itself. They get the earth's
blanket, a mulch ring, and a site I chose for wind and sun, not for
how the patio looks from the kitchen. In a normal 8a winter I do not
wrap the established ones. In an ugly forecast I might wrap the
younger in-ground plant and still leave the old stool alone. The
decision is per plant, per night, not a brand loyalty.

Pots are for flavor I have not committed to, for varieties that sulk
here, for the fig I want to keep small, and for any year I am renting
the dirt. Pots are also for Zone 7 winters I do not want to build a
cage for. The pot is not a lesser fig. It is a fig whose roots I
have agreed to manage like livestock. If I will not haul it, I do
not deserve it.

The bad hybrid is the pot I treat like in-ground: left out, unwrapped
at the walls, sitting on concrete, watered like it is July until
November and then ignored. That plant dies a pot death and I am
tempted to write an essay about hardiness. It was not hardiness. It
was a category error.

A simple 8a split:

- Can I dig here, and do I want this plant in this hole at year ten?
  In the ground.
- Is this a trial, a flavor, a renter's plant, or a Zone 7-style
  winter I refuse to wrap? Pot, and I mean the garage.
- Am I trying to have a tree on a balcony and also never move a pot?
  I am trying to have a story. The plant will write the ending.

Zone 7 tilts to pots unless you love the hardy stool and the annual
reset. Zone 9 tilts to ground unless nematodes or space say no. 8a
is the zone where both answers are correct on the same property.
That is not confusion. That is using the tools.

I do not "graduate" every pot into the yard in April because I feel
guilty. Some figs are better as pots forever. Guilt is how you end
up with a Mission in a windy corner and a broken back from a
thirty-gallon tub you swore you would move "if it got bad."

A decision tree I actually use on a plant in a nursery
can:

Do I know the clone and want it here in a decade? Ground.
Is the name a shrug or a sulker? Pot.
Am I renting? Pot.
Is the only sunny spot a pad I cannot dig? Pot, and I
admit the dolly is now a household tool.
Am I hoping the plant will "just adapt"? That is not a
branch on the tree. That is a wish. Wishes freeze.

I have broken the tree. I have put a maybe-Mission in
the yard because I was tired of moving it. I moved
instead the following March, with a shovel, which is
worse than a dolly. The decision is cheaper before
the roots hit the hardpan.
