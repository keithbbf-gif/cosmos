The best wrap I own is a wall I did not build. South or southwest
masonry collects the day and leaks it back at night. It kills the
north fetch. It makes an 8a fig behave like it moved a half zone
without filing paperwork. UT Gardens and every extension bulletin
repeat this because it is true, not because it is poetic.

I have two plants of the same Celeste line. One is twelve feet off
the house in the open. One is three feet off a brick wall that eats
sun until 3 p.m. After a 12°F night with wind, the open plant
tipped back. The wall plant kept last year's wood and gave me a
small breba I did not deserve. Same water. Same mulch. The wall
was the difference I could have planned.

Stolen degrees are not free. A wall can also bounce a February
sun onto bark and split it — southwest injury, the kind that looks
like winter and is actually glare on a frozen cane. I do not paint
trunks. I do not wrap foil on them. I do give a wall plant a little
shade from the late-winter sun if I have a spare evergreen, and I
do not train a single bare trunk onto hot brick like it is an
espalier contest.

Distance matters. Tight against siding is how you rot the siding
and steam the fig. A few feet off, where you can mulch and walk
and wrap if you have to, is the deal. Watch the drip line from
the roof. A winter of gutter ice crashing into a young fig is
not a microclimate. It is a demolition.

Zone 7 should treat a south wall as the price of admission for
in-ground wood. Zone 9 should remember that a south wall in July
is an oven; some of their "winter site" becomes a leaf-scorch
site. 8a can have both gifts in the same year: a January wall
that saves wood and a July wall that asks for water. I still
pick the wall. I can hold a hose. I cannot hold the Arctic.

If you have no wall, you can steal smaller degrees: a slope above
the frost pocket, a windbreak that is actually solid, a site that
is not the low corner where cold air parks overnight. If you have
no degrees to steal, you wrap, or you pot, or you pick the floor
variety and like dieback. The wall is not mandatory. Pretending
you have one when you do not is.

A wall can also lie. White siding on a north lot is
not a south wall. A board fence that stops at four
feet does not stop a night wind at six. I have
planted to a "wall" that was a story I told myself
so I could put the fig where I wanted the photo.

Stand there at 8 p.m. in January before you dig.
If you will not stand there, you do not get to
claim the microclimate. Stolen degrees have to be
felt. Then you can plant in March, a little off
the masonry, out of the gutter's ice, in soil that
drains. The wall is a partner. It is not a spell.
