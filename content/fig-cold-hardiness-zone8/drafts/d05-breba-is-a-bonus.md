Figs can make two crops. The early one, the breba, sits on last year's wood.
The main crop sits on this year's wood. That sentence is the whole reason
winter protection has a point beyond "keep the plant alive."

If you only care that the plant lives, 8a is easy for the right variety. Roots
in the ground, a little mulch, a cultivar that fruits on new wood, and you will
usually eat figs in late summer. If you care about June fruit on a Desert King
or a decent breba on something that actually makes one here, you are in the
keep-the-wood business. That is a different job. That is wrapping, siting,
and not pruning like you are late for a train in November.

I treat breba as a bonus in 8a. Some years the wood comes through and I get
this small, early crop that tastes like I cheated. Some years I get none, and
I do not rewrite my personality. The main crop is the rent. The breba is the
tip. Growers who move here from California sometimes cannot hear that. They
want the old-wood crop because that is the crop they knew. They wrap like
Zone 6 and then they unwrap too late and steam the buds they were trying to
save. Or they plant a San Pedro type and wait for a wasp that does not live
here. Most of what we can grow in 7–9 is parthenocarpic. You do not need a
wasp. You do need last year's wood if you want last year's crop.

Not every "two-crop" label is honest in this climate. A variety can be a
breba machine in a long mild winter and a one-crop plant after a 12°F night.
Read the label as a capability, not a schedule. In Zone 7, plan on main crop
only unless you have a wall, a wrap, and a variety known to hold wood. In
Zone 9, breba is more ordinary, and the hazard is a late frost after a warm
February, not the January low.

If you are deciding whether to wrap an established in-ground fig in 8a, ask
which crop you are buying with that work:

- Wrap to keep wood: you are buying a chance at breba and a taller plant that
  fruits earlier because it does not have to rebuild a bush from the soil.
- Skip the wrap, mulch the crown, pick a short-season main-crop fig: you are
  buying simplicity, and you are accepting that an ugly winter resets the
  architecture.

Both are adult choices. The childish one is wrapping badly — plastic, too
early, too wet — and losing the wood *and* the health of the bark. I would
rather lose breba than spend April peeling slime off a trunk I "protected."

One more thing. People prune for shape in late fall and then complain they
have no breba. Yes. You cut the wood the breba lives on. If you want an early
crop, fall pruning is a short conversation: take out the broken stuff and go
inside. The real cuts wait until you can see what lived.
