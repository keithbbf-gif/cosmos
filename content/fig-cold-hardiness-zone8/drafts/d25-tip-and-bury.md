Laying a fig down and covering it with soil is the old
northern method. You prune to a flexible framework, bend
the canes, pin them, bury them, mark the grave so you do
not till it in March. It keeps wood in places where
wrapping is a wish. It is also a lot of dirt, a lot of
back, and a lot of wet if you do it in an 8a that rains
from November to March.

I have done it once in 8a, on a variety I liked too much
and a winter the models said would be mean. The plant
kept wood. I also kept a backache and a mound that turned
into a slip of mud every time it thawed. I would do it
again for a plant I could not replace, or for a Zone 7
yard with no garage. I would not do it as my 8a default.
The earth is already a blanket for the roots here. Burying
the whole top is how you steal a Zone 6 solution for a
Zone 6 problem you do not have every year.

If you do it:

- Start while the wood will still bend. Frozen canes snap.
  So do canes you let become a stiff tree because you
  wanted a standard.
- Do not bury a leafy, wet plant. Dormant, stripped, then
  down.
- Soil or dry leaves under a board, not a sheet of plastic
  that turns the mound into a swamp.
- Mark it. I have a friend who found his fig with a shovel.
  The fig lost.

Zone 7 people should learn this even if they never use it.
It is the back pocket when a cage is not enough and a pot
is not possible. Zone 9 should not tip-and-bury. They will
rot a perfectly good plant for a freeze that lasted nine
hours. 8a uses it as a special tool: the one plant, the
one winter, the one variety you refuse to lose.

There is a lighter cousin — pinning canes to the ground
and piling leaves, no full burial. That I will do more
often. It is ugly. It works. The neighbors already think
the leaf cage is a body. They can think this is two.

I practiced the bend in October on a young
plant so I knew it would go down. You cannot
learn flexibility at 8 p.m. in a freeze. If
the plant will not bend, you have already
chosen a cage or a dieback year. That is
fine. What is not fine is snapping a leader
you loved because a video from Michigan made
the bury look gentle.

Mark the mound with a stake taller than the
snow you will not get, because in 8a the
marker is for you in March, not for snow.
I have stepped on a buried fig. The fig
remembered.

I only bury plants I have trained
to a low stool. A stiff old standard
will not become flexible because I
need it to this week. Train next
summer or pick another method
tonight. The bury is not a
personality you can put on a tree
at closing time.
