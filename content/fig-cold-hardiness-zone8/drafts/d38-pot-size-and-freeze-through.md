A three-gallon fig is a popsicle waiting for
hours, not days. A twenty-gallon fig is a
roast that takes all night to go cold in the
middle. I pick pot size for roots in July and
I pay for it in January. People talk about
pots as if volume were only about drought and
fruit. Volume is also time.

Small pots dry faster, freeze faster, thaw
faster, and swing. They are fine if they live
in the garage from the first hard freeze to
the last. They are a gamble on a porch in 8a.
I will not overwinter a cutting in a quart
pot outside no matter what the variety is
called. That is not toughness. That is a
science fair.

Larger pots are kinder and heavier. They are
how you lie to yourself: "too big to move,
so it must be almost in-ground." It is not
almost in-ground. It is a large cliff. I
still wrap the walls or bury the pot or
accept that I own a dolly.

Shape matters. Tall and skinny freezes
faster than wide and squat. Black thin
plastic is a thinner cliff than a thick
nursery can. Fabric is all cliff, all
sides, which is why fabric is wonderful
in summer and rude in January.

Zone 7: if it is small, it is inside. If it
is large, it is inside or buried. Zone 9:
size is a watering conversation. 8a: size
is how many hours you have after the
forecast goes red. I have used those hours
badly, staring at a thirty-gallon tub I
was proud of in June.

If you are buying a pot for a fig you will
keep as a pot, buy the wide one you can
still move with a dolly, not the tall one
that looks like a tree in a magazine. The
magazine does not come over on the night
the ball goes to 18°F in the core.

I keep cuttings in small pots on
purpose and I keep those small pots
indoors or in the garage without a
debate. The debate is for the
fifteen-gallon I keep meaning to
plant. If a pot is too heavy to
move and too small to pretend it
is the earth, I have designed a
trap. Either plant it or commit
to the dolly. The in-between is
how collections shrink.

A saucer under a winter pot is a
skating rink. I pull saucers in
November. Drainage is a January
word too.

I now refuse to buy a fig in a pot I cannot
move with the dolly I own. That rule has
saved me from a whiskey barrel I wanted in
May. The barrel would have been a handsome
corpse.

If you already own the barrel, bury it or
empty it into the ground in spring. Do not
spend another January pretending a decorative
weight is a strategy.
Time is the hidden size. Buy time with volume, or buy time with a door you can close.
