If the place burned and I could carry three figs out of 8a, I
would not take the rarest. I would take the ones that still
make a year feel like a year.

First, Celeste, or Improved Celeste if the summers are truly
hot and wet. I want the closed eye and the standing-up fruit
and a plant that understands this weather. In-ground, wall if
I have one, crown mulched. I wrap it when it is young. I stop
wrapping when it has a stool and a couple of honest winters.

Second, Chicago Hardy. Not because I love the fruit the most.
Because I want a floor. If the Celeste has a bad year, I still
want a main crop on new wood. If a cousin in Zone 7 asks for a
cutting, I want to hand them something that will not make me a
liar. This one can live in a slightly worse spot. It has a job.

Third is the selfish one. In a wet 8a I take LSU Purple or
Violette de Bordeaux. Purple if I want a plant that acts like
it was invited to this climate. Violette if I want the flavor
that makes me check the tree before breakfast. Violette would
likely be the pot, or the wall, because I will actually move a
pot for that fruit. LSU Purple I will put in the ground and
refuse to fuss over.

What I would leave: Mission, the unnamed Turkey from the endcap,
anything I bought because a video said it was the best fig in
the world. Best is a feeling. Three plants that fruit after a
12°F night is a winter.

Zone 7, same list, but Chicago Hardy becomes first, Celeste
second if I have a wall, and the selfish fig stays a pot I can
roll. Zone 9, Celeste and an LSU, and the third can be the
romance fig because the romance fig will live if I do not wrap
it into a fungus farm.

I change my mind about the third plant every August. I do not
change my mind about the first two. That is the point of a
floor and a food fig. The collection can be a hobby. Dinner
should not be.

People ask me for a longer list. I have a longer
list. It has changed every year and it has never
fed me when the three failed to. If you have
room for eight, plant eight. If you have room
for eight and you will not wrap or move any of
them, plant two. The limiting reagent is not
dirt. It is January attention.

I would take a neighbor's unnamed fig that fruits
in their 8a yard over a mail-order fourth that
promises a flavor I cannot verify. The three is
a discipline, not a museum cap. Discipline is
how you still have figs when the collection
gets a weather bill.

If you already have twelve, you do
not have to kill nine. You have to
know which three you will haul
water and cages for when you are
tired. The rest are extras. Extras
are allowed. They are not allowed
to confuse you on a red-forecast
night.
