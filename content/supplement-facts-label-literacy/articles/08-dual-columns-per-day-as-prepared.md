Two packets can become five columns. FDA drew that sample. 21 CFR 101.36(e)(10)(iii) is the aggregate nutrition label for a product that contains separately packaged supplements that differ — a morning packet and an evening packet. Chapter IV, question 4-40, walks it: list all dietary ingredients in the first column; amounts and percents for the morning packet in the second and third; the same pair for the evening packet in the fourth and fifth. You may also give each packet its own panel. You may not give them one unlabeled stack of numbers and a banner that says "complete daily system."

Extra columns are how 101.36 handles more than one serving, more than one population, or a per-unit courtesy next to the required per-serving amount. They are not how you hide a 3-capsule serving behind a prettier 1-capsule column.

## Amount per 1 tablet / amount per 2

Chapter IV, 4-20: you may present information on more than one serving. Example: one tablet in the morning and two at night. You must label the columns. "Amount per 1 Tablet" and "Amount per 2 Tablets" is the language 101.36(b)(2)(i)(A) already permits as a heading variant. 4-18 lets you say "Amount Per 2 Tablets" in place of "Amount Per Serving" when that is the serving. 4-19 lets you add a per-unit column *in addition to* the serving column (101.36(b)(2)(iv)).

So a product whose eating occasion is 2 tablets can print Amount Per 2 Tablets as the required heading, and may print Amount Per 1 Tablet to the right. A product that truly has two different occasions — 1 in the morning, 2 at night — can print both as labeled columns. What it cannot do is print only the 1-tablet column when the directions say take 2, or print both columns and put the %DV only on the small one so the large one looks like a footnote.

Each column that carries (b)(2) amounts owes %DVs for those amounts, using the Daily Value for the population that column is for (piece 06). A 2-tablet column is not "200% of the 1-tablet column" as a design shortcut if rounding would treat them differently. Do the division twice if you print two amount columns. Use the unrounded amount in each.

If morning and night are the same formula and the same count, you need one serving and a directions sentence, not two amount columns. A single "daily" column that erases the occasions is piece 03 again.

## Packets

101.36(e)(8): if the product contains two or more separately packaged dietary supplements that differ from each other — morning packet, afternoon packet — you may use individual nutrition labels or one aggregate label. 101.36(e)(10)(iii) is the picture. Chapter IV, 4-40, counts the columns for two packets: five.

"Differ from each other" is the trigger. Two identical sachets in a carton are not an aggregate-label problem. They are one serving, repeated, and servings per container is the sachet count (or half that, if the serving is two sachets). Two different formulas in one carton are either two panels or one aggregate panel that names both.

The first column of the aggregate is the union of the dietary ingredients. A vitamin that appears only at night still gets a row. The morning amount column shows a dash or an omit only in the way the sample handles a nutrient that is not in that packet — do not invent a 0% vitamin row (piece 01, piece 06). Recheck the (e)(10)(iii) illustration before you draw a dash [VERIFY the current 101.36(e)(10) sample for how an absent nutrient is shown in an unused packet column].

Each packet still has a serving size: the maximum recommended of *that* packet per eating occasion (piece 03). "Take the AM packet with breakfast and the PM packet with dinner" is two occasions, two amount columns. Inner units may skip the panel when the outer carton carries full nutrition information and the units are not intended for separate retail sale (Chapter IV, 4-57; 101.36(i)). A carton that says "see sachets" when the sachets say "see carton" is a loop, not a panel.

## Adult vs child %DV columns

Chapter IV, 4-27: you may show more than one %DV column. Four sets of Daily Values. Identify each column. 101.36(e)(10)(ii) is the sample for a multi aimed at children and adults. 101.36(b)(2)(iii) and (i)(1), and Chapter IV, 4-54, restrict which percents you may print on products for children under 4 years: no %DVs for total fat, saturated fat, cholesterol, total carbohydrate, dietary fiber, vitamin K, selenium, manganese, chromium, molybdenum, chloride, sodium, or potassium. Piece 40 is the kids file. Here, the column rule is: name the population, use that population's denominator from 101.9(c)(8)(iv), and do not invent a third "family" column that averages the two.

Prenatal / lactation columns use the pregnant-and-lactating RDI set. If that set lists folate at 600 mcg DFE and vitamin A at 1,300 mcg RAE [VERIFY current 101.9(c)(8)(iv)], those are the denominators for that column. An adult 400 mcg DFE / 900 mcg RAE column on a product directed only at pregnant women is the wrong table. A dual-column bottle that sells to both populations must say which column is which. Color is not a heading.

Serving size is still one object per column of *amounts*. A "kids %" that is just the adult milligrams over a smaller RDI is a different product story. Write it down or do not print it.

## Powders and "as prepared"

"As prepared" is a food-label habit. Cereal with milk. A mix that is incomplete until you add the thing the directions name. 101.9 has a place for that second column on conventional foods. A dietary-supplement capsule does not get prepared. It gets swallowed. Do not print an "as prepared" column on a softgel because it sounded thorough.

Powders and liquids are the edge. If the serving is 1 scoop (12 g) in water, the column is the scoop. If you also show a column for the scoop stirred into 8 oz of milk, you have added the milk's nutrients to a Supplement Facts panel. That is a category problem as much as a column problem (piece 01). If the SKU is a supplement, declare the supplement serving. If you insist on a prepared column, say exactly what was added — "1 scoop plus 8 fl oz whole milk" — and accept that you are now declaring food nutrients you may not have measured in *your* composite (piece 05). Most operators should not do that. Put preparation in the directions. Keep the panel as the scoop.

A "makes 20 drinks" line is servings-per-container math (piece 04), not an as-prepared column. Liquid concentrates that say "as consumed" after dilution need the dilution stated. If 1 tsp is the labeled serving, the required column is the teaspoon. If you only print the prepared column, you have labeled the water.

## A second column that is really a marketing pane

The abuse is stable across catalogs. Required serving is 3 capsules. The designer prints a bold "Per Capsule" column and a pale "Per Serving" column, or the reverse, or only the per-capsule numbers on the PDP and the 3-capsule math in 6-point type. Sometimes the second column is "vs. leading brand" with a %DV invented for a botanical. Sometimes it is a QR that opens a "full daily stack" adding three SKUs the buyer has not purchased.

101.36 allows extra columns for extra *facts about this product*: another serving that the directions actually use, another population the product is actually labeled for, a per-unit split of the declared serving. It does not allow a comparison chart inside the box. It does not allow a column for a serving you told people not to take. It does not allow a column whose only job is to make 3 look like 1.

If the second column is wider, bolder, or the only one that repeats on the PDP, that is the column a reasonable buyer will take as the serving. FTC's 2022 health-products guidance will read the net impression (piece 27). FDA will still composite against the serving in 101.36(b)(1) (piece 05). You can lose both ways: the pretty column becomes the claim, and the legal column becomes the assay target.

A "per day" pane that assumes the buyer also takes three other SKUs is not a dual column. It is a new product you have not boxed. If you cannot name the object in the heading — 1 tablet, AM packet, children 1–3 years, 1 scoop in water — you do not have a column. You have a pane. Delete it.
