**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not a labeling opinion for a specific SKU.

The letter will paste your sentence back to you. FRS International, LLC, MARCS-CMS 606701, dated 15 June 2020, is the worked example. Dual header: FDA and FTC. The agencies reviewed www.frs.com, www.healthyenergy.com, and www.frs-international.com on 1 April 2020 and 4 June 2020, and Facebook at www.facebook.com/FRSEnergyEndurance, which sent buyers to the purchase sites. They needed a browser, not a warehouse tour.

This is not a product review and does not recommend quercetin — or any other ingredient — for COVID-19. Disease names appear because the letter used them as the intended-use hook. Piece 30 is the immune-language companion. This piece teaches the reading. Do not invent another CMS number. 606701 is enough.

## The header

Start at the top, not at the adjectives.

**Recipient.** Thomas C. Lines, FRS International, LLC, 75 Arlington St., Ste 500, Boston, MA 02116. The joint PDF also lists customerservice@frs.com. A letter is addressed to a person and a firm. The print lock (piece 42) should already know who would be on that line.

**Date and number.** 15 June 2020. MARCS-CMS 606701. The same number later appears on an 11 January 2021 close-out note. That page is not a second letter. It is the after-file for this one.

**RE line.** "Unapproved and Misbranded Products Related to Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19)." The heading is the category. A later letter will use a different RE and the same method: identity, hook, quotes, action.

**Who looked, where, when.** The first working paragraph names the URLs and the review dates. Write them down. Facebook is there because it directed buyers to the carts. A Reel you paid for (piece 41) is the later version of that sentence.

**Product identity.** Quercetin-containing soft chew and liquid concentrate products under the brand names Q-Force and FRS Healthy Energy. The letter says those products are intended to mitigate, prevent, treat, diagnose, or cure COVID-19 in people. 21 U.S.C. § 321(g)(1)(B) does not ask what the formulator meant. It asks what the pages said.

## The quotes

After the hook, the letter prints the sentences. That block is the reason this piece exists. The joint PDF lists examples "on your websites that establish the intended use of your products." Among them, quoted here as the letter quoted them:

- "BIG NEWS! DOCTORS ARE TESTING QUERCETINS AS A POTENTIAL DEFENSE VS. CORONAVIRUS" — homepage, www.frs.com
- "WHY STUDY QUERCETINS VS. COVID-19? . . . ANTI-VIRAL STUDIES There are many studies reporting that Quercetins are effective at inhibiting production and replication of a number of different viruses." — www.frs.com/why-study-quercetins.html
- "Lung inflammation is a significant complication of coronavirus infection. Multiple studies have reported that Quercetin displays strong anti-inflammatory capabilities." — same URL
- "COVID-19 causes respiratory infection. Many studies have reported that flavonoids, including Quercetin, decrease Upper Respiratory Tract Infections (URTI) ..." — same URL
- "CORONAVIRUS NEWS: DOCTORS TESTING QUERCETIN – Click here for more info! $250 ORDER LIMIT / 12 CHEW BAGS PER ORDER" — homepage, www.frs-international.com
- "DOCTORS WORLDWIDE ARE TESTING QUERCETINS AS A POTENTIAL DEFENSE AGAINST COVID-19 ... 'A MADE-IN-CANADA SOLUTION TO THE CORONAVIRUS OUTBREAK?'" — www.frs-international.com/pages/coronavirus-news (the letter notes the inner quote as attributed to *Maclean's*)
- CBC-attributed headlines on the same coronavirus-news page, which the letter also pasted
- "IN THE NEWS: DOCTORS ARE STUDYING QUERCETIN AS A DEFENSE AGAINST CORONAVIRUS" — Facebook, 25 February 2020

Read that block as a method. Homepage bursts, a "why study" essay, a scarcity line, a press-clipping page, a social post. The 101.93 disclaimer, if a bottle had one, is not in the quote block. It does not wash a disease claim (piece 22). A newspaper attribution does not wash it either.

A principal display panel can enter the same block. 606701 was URL-heavy. The reading does not change if the pasted line is a carton burst: SKU, sentence, surface, capture date.

## Drug vs misbranded

The letter uses both hooks. Learn them as two sentences.

**Unapproved new drug.** Section 505(a) of the FD&C Act, 21 U.S.C. § 355(a). Because the products, as labeled by those pages, are intended to mitigate, prevent, treat, diagnose, or cure COVID-19, they are new drugs that lack approval.

**Misbranded drug.** Section 502 of the FD&C Act, 21 U.S.C. § 352. Once the product is a drug by intended use, drug labeling rules apply. A dietary-supplement panel does not pull it back.

**Interstate commerce.** Sections 301(a) and (d), 21 U.S.C. § 331(a) and (d). Introduction or delivery for introduction of those articles is prohibited.

The ingredient is not the violation. Quercetin could have sat on a quiet (b)(3) row with an asterisk. The sentences made the drug. Piece 30 said the same of the broader COVID set. This pack opens 606701 and no other CMS file.

FTC's closing paragraph is a third hook. Under the FTC Act it is unlawful to advertise that a product can prevent, treat, or cure human disease without competent and reliable scientific evidence, including, when appropriate, well-controlled human clinical studies. For COVID-19, the letter says no such study is known for the products named. A 101.93 notice would not have saved that paragraph.

## What "discontinue" means for a URL

The letter asks the firm to cease sale of the products *for* the COVID-19 intended use, and to review websites, labels, and other promotional materials. It gives 48 hours to email COVID-19-Task-Force-CFSAN@fda.hhs.gov with steps taken, how recurrence will be prevented, and copies of related documents. FTC wants a parallel 48-hour note to Richard Cleland, then Assistant Director of the Division of Advertising Practices.

Failure language is ordinary: seizure, injunction, and, on the FTC side, a possible order that includes consumer redress. Foreign firms are told the articles can be detained and refused admission.

FDA says the firm will be added to the public fraudulent COVID-19 products list until corrective actions are confirmed. Taking down one URL and leaving the Facebook post is an incomplete correction. A new slug with the same burst is the same product.

"Discontinue" here means the claim-set and the intended use, not a mystical un-invention of the milligram. A quiet, otherwise lawful supplement is a different file from a COVID drug. The URL is part of the label.

The 11 January 2021 note on the same CMS number records that corrective actions appeared to address the letter, and that later inspections will test whether that lasts. Close-out is not a blessing of the formula. It is a note that the pasted sentences came down.

## How to read one before you write one

Open any warning letter — this one, when you practice — with a five-line sheet:

1. **Who and when.** Firm, person, date, CMS number.
2. **What they captured.** URLs, dates of review, social accounts, labels if any.
3. **What they say the product is.** Brand names, dosage forms, the intended-use sentence.
4. **Which statutes.** 505(a), 502, 301, FTC Act. Write the numbers. Do not summarize as "they were unhappy."
5. **Which sentences pasted.** Copy them into a column next to your own inventory (piece 41). If a sentence of yours would sit in that column without changing more than a noun, delete it before you print.

Then walk your PDP and your live listing with the same sheet. Title of the box (piece 01). Serving. Claims on the front. Bullets. A+. Search terms. The last Reel you paid for. The 9 p.m. macro. You are not looking for tone. You are looking for a pasteable sentence.

Counsel owns the last read. This pack will not draft the response email or invent a science partner to stand in front of the quote block. If a sentence of yours would look at home under that RE line, it does not belong next to a cart. 606701 is the proof that someone will type it back, in capital letters, with the URL and the date.
