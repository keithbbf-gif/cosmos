**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not a labeling opinion for a specific SKU.

The investigator can add-to-cart too. 21 U.S.C. § 321(g)(1)(B) reads a product as a drug if it is intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease. Intended use is assembled from labels, websites, social posts, paid scripts, and the sentences a contractor typed into a helpdesk. Piece 20 is the four-corners walk. This piece is the three surfaces that keep arriving after the bottle is already clean: the marketplace listing, the Reel you paid for, and the macro a night-shift agent hits at 9 p.m.

FRS International, MARCS-CMS 606701 (15 June 2020), is the method file — websites plus Facebook, quoted back in the letter (piece 43). Do not invent another CMS number. The method is the point. Amazon did not need to exist for the rule to reach a URL. It exists now, so the URL is a listing.

## Amazon bullets and A-plus

A marketplace product page is labeling. The title, the bullet array, the A+ / Brand Story HTML, the comparison chart, and the lifestyle module under the fold are all sentences you offered for the sale.

Bullets are where disease words hide because they look like features. "Supports immunity during cold season" is already near SECG criterion 8 (piece 30). "Helps with joint pain" is the arthritis-shaped example FDA has been using since 65 FR 1000. "Replaces your [drug]" is the guardrail list. If you would not put the bullet on the principal display panel next to the 101.93 disclaimer, do not put it in a bullet. The disclaimer on the bottle does not wash the listing (piece 22).

A+ modules are worse when they include before/after bodies, white coats, hospital corridors, or star-rating widgets that quote a disease (pieces 29 and 33). 16 CFR Part 255 (2023) treats endorsements as ads. Typicality still applies. A five-star review you chose to display that names a disease is a disease claim you paid to keep on the page.

Nutrient-content adjectives in bullets — "excellent source," "high potency," "clinical strength" — still attach to the rules in pieces 25 and 35. A (b)(3) blend cannot be an excellent source of itself.

Amazon's own style guides and category rules are not 21 CFR. [VERIFY] the current category style if you need the listing to stay up; then run the same sentences through 101.93 and FTC 2022 anyway. A listing that pleases a category manager and names a disease is still a drug file.

## Search terms

Backend search terms are intended use with the lights off. Investigators and plaintiffs know how to open the seller central fields. "Covid," "flu," "insomnia," "arthritis," "chemo," drug brand names, and "natural vaccine" are not discovery keywords. They are claims you attached to the ASIN.

Hidden HTML, alt text, and image filenames do the same work. `sku-name-covid-defense-hero.jpg` is a caption. FRS 606701 quoted homepage headlines and a Facebook post. A filename is less charming and just as pasteable.

Auto-generated terms from a brand registry tool are still yours. If an agency or a vendor packed fifty disease words into the backend, you shipped them. Inventory the field. Delete what you cannot substantiate as a lawful structure/function statement — and even then, a search term is a poor home for 101.93(f) because the disclaimer and the 30-day notice (pieces 22–23) do not travel with a hidden string.

FTC's December 2022 guidance cares about net impression. A clean bullet list over a search-term cloud of disease names is one impression, not two.

## Reels you paid for

A Reel, Short, or TikTok you paid for — money, product, affiliate code, or "seeded" bottle with a script — is an endorsement under 16 CFR 255 and labeling for intended-use purposes. The script is your copy. The caption is your copy. The pinned comment you asked the creator to leave is your copy.

"Not medical advice, just what worked for my [disease]" is the testimonial problem in piece 29. Typicality, disclosure of the relationship, and the disease word are all live. A "day in my protocol" montage with your SKU on the nightstand is a protocol you sponsored. Piece 30 already told you COVID letters treated hashtags and protocols as the violation, not the milligram of vitamin C.

Unpaid organic posts by strangers are a moderation problem, not a free claim. If you repost them, you adopted them. If your community manager replies "yes it helps with that," you wrote a new sentence. If you buy the whitelisting rights and run the creator's face as a Spark Ad, you are in paid media.

Do not ask a creator to say "clinically proven" unless you have the trial on this SKU (piece 28). Do not send a white coat as a prop (piece 33). Do not send a child-with-gummies shot (piece 40). The brief should be the same inventory you use for the bottle: serving, form, amount, allowed structure/function line, disclaimer.

## Chat macros

Customer-service macros are labeling you wrote in advance. The 9 p.m. agent is not a rogue botanist. They are reading your script.

Audit the macros the way you audit bullets. Search the helpdesk for: diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, covid, flu, cancer, arthritis, insomnia, anxiety, depression, pregnancy, breastfeeding, "instead of your prescription," "works with chemo," "heavy-metal free," "FDA approved," "pharmaceutical grade." Those strings are the never-say list in CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS. If a macro contains one, delete it. If an agent improvised one, treat it as a new SKU claim and pull it.

Marketplace "Ask a question" answers, chatbot trees, SMS flows, and the storefront live-chat widget are the same file. A 3PL that answers "is this safe for my child's sleep?" with a yes has just written pediatric intended use. This pack will not supply a sleep-disease answer. The survivable macro is: we do not give medical advice; the product is a dietary supplement; here is the serving on the label; speak with a licensed clinician.

Returns macros that concede a disease result ("sorry it didn't help your [condition]") adopt the condition as the product's job. Write the apology without the diagnosis.

## One inventory of sentences

Keep a single table for each SKU. Columns: sentence, surface (PDP, insert, site, Amazon title, Amazon bullets, A+, search terms, Reel script, pinned comment, chat macro, email flow), claim type (structure/function, nutrient-content, health claim, quality, origin, none), 30-day notice status, substantiation file, owner. If a sentence is not on the table, it is not approved. If two rows disagree — bottle says 2 gummies, Amazon title says 1 — you have piece 42's dispute.

A clean bottle cannot save a dirty bullet. FDA and FTC do not owe you a reading that ignores the listing. The 101.93 disclaimer on the carton does not follow the customer into a Reel. Registration of the facility does not bless the search terms (piece 34).

Print lock (piece 44) should include a screenshot of the live listing and an export of the macros, dated. If you cannot produce those on the morning you send art to the printer, you do not know what you are selling. The investigator already does. They have a cart.
