An original and two copies. The rule still says that. 21 CFR 101.93(a)(1): no later than 30 days after the first marketing of a dietary supplement that bears one of the statements listed in section 403(r)(6) of the Act, the manufacturer, packer, or distributor shall notify the Office of Dietary Supplement Programs (HFS-810), Center for Food Safety and Applied Nutrition, Food and Drug Administration, 5001 Campus Dr., College Park, MD 20740, that it has included such a statement on the label or in the labeling of its product. "An original and two copies of this notification shall be submitted."

88 FR 17718 (24 March 2023) is the amendment that put that office name and street address in the current text. Older printer's notes still say 5100 Paint Branch Parkway or an Office of Nutritional Products mailstop. Do not mail the 1997 address and call it done.

Notification is a dated letter about a sentence on a label. It is not a Supplement Facts panel, not a COA, and not an approval.

## What goes in the notice

101.93(a)(2) is a short list. The notification shall include:

(i) The name and address of the manufacturer, packer, or distributor of the dietary supplement that bears the statement.

(ii) The text of the statement that is being made.

(iii) The name of the dietary ingredient or supplement that is the subject of the statement, if not provided in the text of the statement.

(iv) The name of the dietary supplement (including brand name), if not provided in response to (iii), on whose label, or in whose labeling, the statement appears.

That is the whole contents list. You are not asked to attach the clinical file. You are not asked to attach the Supplement Facts dieline. You are asked to identify who is talking, what they said, about which ingredient or product, under which brand.

The text in (ii) should be the text a buyer will read. If the bottle says "supports healthy joint function" and the notice says "joint health," you have notified a different sentence. If the site says more than the bottle, the site is labeling (piece 20). Notify the statements you are actually making, or take the extras off.

One SKU with three 403(r)(6) statements needs those three texts in the file. A catalog of twenty SKUs needs a notice practice that can identify each product. 101.93(a) attaches to "a dietary supplement" that bears the statement. Do not send one poem for the whole house brand and assume every SKU is covered.

The 30 days run from first marketing with the statement, not from first manufacture, not from first listing in a wholesale book that never sold, and not from the day counsel "approved the concept." First marketing is when the product bearing that statement is offered for sale. If you added the sentence to a live listing on a Tuesday, the clock started Tuesday.

## Where it goes (dated address)

Current 101.93(a)(1), after 88 FR 17718: Office of Dietary Supplement Programs (HFS-810), CFSAN, FDA, 5001 Campus Dr., College Park, MD 20740.

FDA's own industry page on structure/function notifications repeats that mailstop and writes the ZIP as 20740-3835 [VERIFY the agency page on the day you mail, in case the ZIP+4 line moved]. The regulation's sentence stops at 20740.

The rule's paper ritual is an original and two copies. Keep a fourth copy in your own file, date-stamped, with proof of delivery. 101.93 does not require FDA to send you a gold star. Your proof that you sent the notice is the proof you will have.

FDA also hosts an electronic path for structure/function claim notifications (the agency has pointed industry at a web portal; the portal name has changed as FDA shuffled food-submission systems). Recheck the current FDA structure/function notification page before you choose paper or electronic [VERIFY]. This piece teaches the rule that is still in 101.93(a). If you file electronically, keep the same contents, the same 30-day clock, and the same certification. A portal confirmation is not an approval letter.

Do not send the notice to a district inspection office and consider 101.93(a) closed. Do not send it to the facility-registration mailbox. HFS-810 is a specific door.

## Certification is a person

101.93(a)(3): the notice shall be signed by a responsible individual or the person who can certify the accuracy of the information presented and contained in the notice. That individual shall certify that the information in the notice is complete and accurate, **and** that the notifying firm has substantiation that the statement is truthful and not misleading.

A signature block is not a logo. It is a human being willing to say two things: the form is true, and the substantiation file exists. A marketing coordinator who has never seen the file should not be the signer. An outside designer should not be the signer. A vacant "medical director" seat should not be invented for the purpose (piece 33).

The certification is how 343(r)(6)(B) becomes a named act. The statute required the manufacturer to have substantiation. The 1997 rule required someone to sign that the firm has it. If the file is a folder of ads from a competitor, the signature is a problem, not a cure.

Keep the signed notice with the substantiation memo, the label lock, and the first-marketing date. When the statement changes, the notice is about a new text. When the brand name changes and the statement stays, (iv) still wanted the name of the dietary supplement including brand name. File the delta. Do not assume last year's PDF covers this year's rename.

## Silence is not a blessing

FDA does not "approve" a 403(r)(6) statement by sitting quietly. 101.93(a) is a notification. The heading of the section is "certain types of statements," not "permitted after agency review."

A return receipt proves delivery. It does not prove the sentence is in the lane. It does not prove 101.93(g) will not read the sentence as a disease claim. It does not prove FTC will like the net impression. It does not prove a retailer desk will pass it.

Silence after a bad statement is how a firm tells itself a story. The agency may still send a warning letter that pastes the sentence (piece 43). The agency may do nothing for years and then do something. Neither path is a blessing you can print as "FDA notified" on the principal display panel. "FDA notified" as a quality mark is cousin to "FDA approved" (piece 34). The notice is a statutory duty. It is not a seal.

If FDA does write back and objects, that letter is the opposite of silence. Do not keep marketing the objected sentence while you "wait for a final determination" unless counsel has a reason that can survive 321(g). This pack is not that counsel.

## Substantiation was supposed to exist first

The clock in 101.93(a) is 30 days *after* first marketing. The substantiation duty is *before*. § 343(r)(6)(B) is written in the present perfect: the manufacturer **has** substantiation. 101.93(a)(3) certifies that the firm **has** it. You do not get 30 days to start the literature search. You get 30 days to tell FDA you are saying the thing you already could defend.

That order is the scar. Firms print the sentence, launch the listing, then ask a writer for "a couple of citations." Piece 28 is why an ingredient study at the wrong dose is not this SKU. Piece 27 is why FTC will read the sentence as a buyer reads it. The notification file will not fix a missing study. It will only record that someone signed as if the study were there.

Build the file in this order: the exact sentence; the ingredient and the serving; the evidence that matches that sentence; a memo that says why the evidence is enough for each reasonable interpretation; the disclaimer placement (piece 22); the four-corners sweep so a blog does not invent a second sentence (piece 20); then first marketing; then the notice, original and two copies, or the current electronic equivalent, within 30 days.

If you cannot sign 101.93(a)(3) today, do not market the statement today. The 30-day file is a mailbox duty. The substantiation file is why the mailbox is allowed to exist. 1994 wrote both sentences. 1997 wrote the copies. 2023 updated the street. None of those years turned a notification into a license.
