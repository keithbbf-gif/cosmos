**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not a labeling opinion for a specific SKU.

"Third-party tested" is a caption. A lot number is a fact. The caption can sit on a carton, an Amazon A+ module, or a storefront tile without naming the lot, the method, the laboratory, or the rows that were actually run. When those four fields are missing, the badge is doing the same work as "pharmaceutical grade" (piece 35): it is a quality costume.

This pack stays on the label. The companion folder `content/supplement-coa-manufacturing-literacy/` is where you learn to read a certificate of analysis and a plant. Do not clone that curriculum here. You still have to know enough not to print a sentence the COA cannot support.

## The four fields

A useful test sentence has four parts. If any one is absent, you do not have a claim you can defend. You have a mood.

**Lot.** The finished-product lot that is in the bottle the customer will open. A raw-material COA from 2022 does not speak for a fill from last Tuesday. A "representative" lot from a sister SKU does not speak for this SKU. 21 CFR 111.3 defines a lot as a batch, or a specific identified portion of a batch, that is uniform and intended to meet specifications. The word is already in the manufacturing rule. Put it on the story you tell next to the cart.

**Method.** ICP-MS is not HPLC is not a FTIR identity screen is not a microbial plate count. "Tested" without a method lets a metals panel, a potency assay, and a banned-substance screen collapse into one gold sticker. Name the method the way the lab named it, including the version if the lab did.

**Lab.** A third party is a named organization that is not the brand and not the contract manufacturer wearing a different letterhead. An internal QC printout is first-party work. A broker's PDF that redacts the laboratory is not a third party you can point at. Do not invent a lab director or a city plant to launder the sentence (CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS). Science/QA is an empty seat in this pack.

**Which rows.** Identity, potency, specified metals, residual solvents, pesticides, microbes, and sports banned-substance panels are different questions. A clean micro result does not answer lead. A matching vitamin C assay does not answer whether the proprietary blend is the expensive herb or the maltodextrin-adjacent filler (piece 16). Write the rows. "Heavy-metal free" is not a row (piece 38).

A one-page PDF that lacks those four fields is a flyer. You may still keep it in a vendor folder. You may not translate it into a PDP badge.

## Skip-lot vs every batch (plain language)

Not every assay is run on every finished lot. 21 CFR 111.75 is the manufacturing paragraph that lets a firm, in some cases, use a skip-lot program for finished-product testing when it has a basis to do so. The details belong in the COA pack. The label consequence is simpler.

Some plants assay every finished lot for the rows that matter to that SKU. Some rotate. Some rely on incoming ingredient tests plus a periodic finished-product check. A cart badge that says "third-party tested" does not tell the buyer which of those three you do.

Program marks are not interchangeable on this point. Informed Sport's public description is that it tests every sports-supplement batch for banned substances before release. [VERIFY] that program page on the date you brief a retailer; wording is theirs to change. USP Verified includes continuing off-the-shelf surveillance of products already in the program, which is not the same sentence as "every lot we ship was potency-tested yesterday." NSF Certified for Sport is a certification program with its own audit-and-test cycle and a public directory; it is not a one-time PDF.

Ask the plant, in writing, which rows are every batch and which rows are skip-lot. Put the answer next to the badge draft. If the plant will not answer, you do not have a badge.

## Program vs PDF

A program mark and a supplier PDF are different species.

USP Verified, NSF Certified for Sport, and Informed Sport publish directories. USP's mark is a product-level enrollment. NSF Certified for Sport's directory at nsfsport.com lets you search by product, brand, or lot number. Informed Sport publishes a certified-product listing. [VERIFY] the live directory for *this* SKU and *this* lot before the printer file leaves. Family names lie. "Vitamin D3 125 mcg" in three bottle sizes can be three listings, or one listing and two cousins.

A PDF is a document about a sample. It can be excellent. It can be a screen shot of someone else's lot. It can be a method that does not match the specification you printed. It does not grant permission to place a program logo on the carton. PHOTO_NOTES already bans unredacted third-party COAs you do not have the right to show, and it bans gold-badge theater for piece 36.

Retailers blur the species. A 3PL intake form that says "upload third-party test" will accept almost any PDF. Amazon's badges and A+ modules will accept a sentence you typed. None of that converts a flyer into a program. If you show another company's label, lot, or plant photo as if it were yours, that is the white-label scar in CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS, not a quality story.

## What a cart page can say

Write a smaller sentence.

Allowed shape, if true: "Lot 24A031. Lead, cadmium, arsenic, and mercury by ICP-MS at [named lab]. Results against finished-product spec on file." You still need the file. You still need the lot on the bottle to match the lot in the sentence. You still need to avoid turning a result into "safe," "clean," or "free."

Allowed shape, if the SKU is actually enrolled: "USP Verified" with the artwork the program permits, plus a link or a QR to the directory listing. Same for NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport, each under its own rules. Do not stack three marks you do not have.

Forbidden shapes that keep showing up on drafts: "third-party tested for purity and potency" with no lot; "tested by an independent FDA-registered lab" as if registration were a grade (piece 34); "clinically tested" when you mean a metals panel; a seal you drew in the label file.

FTC's December 2022 health-products guidance still sits on the same URL. A quality claim is an advertising claim. Competent and reliable evidence has to match the words as a reasonable consumer takes them. "Third-party tested" taken as "every bottle was checked for everything that could be wrong" is a larger claim than a single skip-lot potency assay.

## What this pack will not fake

This folder will not name a science partner we do not have. It will not invent a laboratory, a chief scientist, or a Utah facility. It will not put a program mark on a sample label. It will not quote a COA we do not hold.

The Supplement Facts box remains 101.36 work: names, servings, weights, the hairline, the other-ingredient list. A test badge is not a row in that box. It does not move a blend from secret to declared. It does not fix a serving size that is smaller than the suggested-use maximum. It does not wash a disease claim on the principal display panel.

If you cannot name the lot, the method, the lab, and the rows, delete the caption. The bottle can ship with a quiet panel. The cart can ship with a milligram and a serving. Those are still the sentences 101.36 knows how to host. The COA pack is waiting when you have a real file to read.
