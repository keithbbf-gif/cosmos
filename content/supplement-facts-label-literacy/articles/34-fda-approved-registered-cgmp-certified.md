**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not a labeling opinion for a specific SKU.

Approval is a drug word. Registration is a mailbox. A dietary supplement is a food category under 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff). FDA does not approve the SKU, the label, or the structure/function sentence before it ships. Facilities that manufacture, process, pack, or hold the article for U.S. consumption must register as food facilities. 21 CFR Part 111, published as a final rule on 25 June 2007 (72 FR 34752), is the manufacturing duty. None of those three facts is a gold seal.

"FDA registered cGMP facility" on a principal display panel is a costume stitched from the mailbox and the duty. Retailers still ask for it. This piece is how to take the costume apart before a desk or a letter does it for you.

## What approval would mean

For a drug, approval is a premarket decision on a new drug application, an abbreviated application, or a biologic license: the product may be lawfully marketed for the labeled intended use. Dietary supplements do not go through that door. FDA's own consumer Q&A says the agency does not approve dietary supplements or their labeling the way it approves drugs. [VERIFY] the current Q&A page before a public import; the sentence has been stable, the URL sometimes moves.

A 30-day 101.93 notification is not approval (piece 23). Silence from College Park is not a blessing. An authorized health claim or a still-operative qualified-health-claim letter is not "FDA approved" either. It is a specific sentence, at a specific level, for a specific substance-disease pair (piece 26). FTC's 2022 guide, Example 48, is the banner "Meets FDA Qualified Health Claim" on a letter that said the relationship was uncertain because the evidence was thin. The Commission calls that a mischaracterization that likely communicates agency approval and a high level of proof.

Example 49 is "FDA Approved" next to dramatic inch-and-pound promises for a device that had a narrower clearance. Juxtaposition writes a new claim. A supplement PDP that puts "FDA approved" next to a bone silhouette or an immune shield is doing the same work — and is false about the approval besides.

"FDA approved facility" is not a substitute. FDA does not approve dietary-supplement plants as a product license. Inspections happen. Form 483s and warning letters happen. A clean inspection is not an approval stamp you print on the bottle.

If a buyer asks whether the SKU is FDA approved, the true sentence is no. The longer true sentence is that the firm is responsible for lawfulness, that the facility should be registered, and that Part 111 applies. Those are duties. They are not a badge.

## What registration is

The Public Health Security and Bioterrorism Preparedness and Response Act of 2002, and 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart H, require domestic and foreign facilities that manufacture, process, pack, or hold food for U.S. consumption to register with FDA before they start. Dietary supplements and dietary ingredients are on the "food" side of that list. Registration is updated and renewed on the schedule the current rule requires. [VERIFY] the live FDA registration Q&A for the current renewal clock and portal name before you describe the mailbox in a retailer packet.

Registration is a list. You give identity, addresses, emergency contacts, and food-product categories so the agency knows where to call. Foreign facilities designate a U.S. agent. FDA does not, by accepting a registration, inspect the plant, certify the quality system, or endorse the SKU. A registration number on a sell sheet is proof that someone filed a form. It is not "FDA registered quality."

Using the number as a quality mark is the usual costume: "FDA Registered Facility No. …" in a seal. That seal tells a reasonable buyer the agency has blessed the building. It has not. The 2022 FTC chapter on mischaracterizing FDA review is the advertising half of the same problem. Piece 27 is the net-impression file.

Retailers may still ask for the number. Give the number as a number. Do not draw a crest around it.

## What Part 111 is

On 25 June 2007 FDA published the dietary-supplement CGMP rule, 21 CFR Part 111: Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Packaging, Labeling, or Holding Operations for Dietary Supplements. The duty is on persons who manufacture, package, label, or hold a dietary supplement. The point of the rule, in the agency's own SECG language, is to ensure the quality of the supplement and to ensure it is packaged and labeled as specified in the master manufacturing record.

Part 111 is law. It is not a certificate. FDA does not send a diploma when you write procedures. FDA inspects against the rule. Failures show up as 483 observations and, sometimes, warning letters. The companion COA pack is the plant-and-record file. This pack will not rewrite it.

"cGMP certified by FDA" is false. The agency is not a certifier of dietary-supplement plants. "cGMP certified" without a named, checkable third-party program is an empty adjective. "Made in an FDA cGMP facility" stitches registration, inspection authority, and the duty into a grade. The grade does not exist.

Compliance dates after the 2007 rule were staggered by firm size; the rule itself is the 2007 event this piece dates to. A 2026 PDP that still talks as if CGMP were optional is talking 2006.

## Marks that are real (pointer to programs)

Some marks are programs with directories. USP Dietary Supplement Verification, NSF Sport, Informed-Sport, and similar schemes have public lookup pages, lot-level or SKU-level scope, and rules about when the mark may appear. Piece 36 is the "third-party tested" sentence without a lot. The COA pack is how to read a certificate of analysis. This piece will not clone either file.

A real mark, used inside its license, is a smaller and better sentence than "FDA registered cGMP." It still is not FDA approval. It still is not a disease claim. It still fails if the directory does not list the SKU you printed the logo on.

A PDF from a lab you hired for one lot is not a program mark. A supplier's advertising badge is not your mark. A "GMP" icon a printer found in a clip-art folder is not Part 111.

"Pharmaceutical grade" and "clinical strength" are the next piece (35). They are not USP marks for dietary supplements. Do not borrow them here to fill the space where "FDA approved" used to sit.

## Sentences that survive a retailer desk

A desk that asks for "FDA certs" is often asking for three different documents. Hand them the three, named correctly.

**Registration.** "The manufacturing facility is registered with FDA as a food facility under 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart H. Registration is a listing requirement, not an approval." Attach the registration confirmation if the retailer needs the number. Do not put the number in a seal on the PDP.

**Part 111.** "The operation is subject to 21 CFR Part 111." If you have a third-party GMP audit, name the program and the scope, and point at the directory. If you have a recent inspection outcome you are willing to share, share the actual outcome, not a crest.

**The SKU.** Supplement Facts per 101.36. Structure/function sentences, if any, substantiated, disclaimed, notified. No "FDA approved." No "FDA registered quality." No "cGMP certified by FDA."

FTC Example 48 and Example 49 are the test for leftover adjectives. If a reasonable buyer would hear that the agency reviewed the formula for efficacy, the adjective comes off. If a marketplace badge engine only accepts "FDA approved" as a checkbox, the checkbox is wrong; do not let a feed lie on your behalf (piece 41).

The box on the bottle is still not a certificate (piece 01). The 30-day file is still not a blessing (piece 23). The plant's registration is still a mailbox. Print the facts that have objects — serving, milligrams, a real program mark, a lot that matches a COA — and leave the agency's name off the gold sticker. Approval is for a drug file you do not have. The duty is heavier than the sticker, and it is already yours.
