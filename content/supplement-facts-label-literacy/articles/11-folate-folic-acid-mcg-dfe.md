DFE is a unit, not a brand of folate. Dietary folate equivalents exist because food folate and folic acid are not absorbed the same way. The 2016 rule (81 FR 33742) made **mcg DFE** the unit on the Nutrition Facts and Supplement Facts amount line. The adult RDI stayed "400" and stopped meaning 400 mcg of folic acid. 400 mcg of folic acid is 680 mcg DFE. A prenatal that still prints "800 mcg" without DFE is a 2014 label wearing a 2026 carton.

This is not a neural-tube-treatment pitch. Authorized health-claim rules, if you need them, live in piece 26. Do not write product copy from this file.

## Why DFE exists

The National Academy of Medicine introduced DFE so one number could cover two bioavailabilities. FDA's August 2019 conversion guidance repeats the school arithmetic: folic acid is about 85% bioavailable; naturally occurring food folate is about 50%; 85 ÷ 50 = 1.7. So:

mcg DFE = mcg naturally occurring folate + (1.7 × mcg folic acid)

101.9(c)(8)(iv) footnote 7: 1 DFE = 1 mcg naturally occurring folate = 0.6 mcg folic acid. That is the same factor inverted (1 ÷ 1.7 ≈ 0.6). ODS's Folate Health Professional fact sheet narrates the same split. Recheck the live ODS page if you quote its prose in a training deck [VERIFY current ODS Folate HP wording].

The 1993 RDI was 400 mcg, unit unspecified in aisle talk, treated in practice as folic acid on many supplement panels. The 2016 RDI is 400 mcg DFE for adults and children 4 years and older [VERIFY 101.9(c)(8)(iv)]. Pregnant and lactating women have a higher RDI in that table (600 mcg DFE in the current listing [VERIFY]). The denominator moved for some columns. The unit moved for all of them.

On a dietary supplement, when folic acid is added, you must declare folate in mcg DFE, the %DV on that DFE amount, *and* the mcg of folic acid in parentheses (101.36(b)(2) and 101.9(c)(8)(vii); conversion guidance, Example 4). FDA's own supplement example:

Folate 680 mcg DFE (400 mcg folic acid) 170%

That is a 400 mcg folic-acid tablet after 2016. It is not a 170% "stronger" tablet than the 2014 version of the same fill. It is the same fill with the unit the rule now requires.

If the product contains only food-form folate and no folic acid, the DFE amount equals the mcg of that folate (factor 1.0) and you do not owe a folic-acid parenthetical. If it contains a synthetic salt other than folic acid — the conversion guidance names calcium L-5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate as the example — FDA said it does not intend to object to a manufacturer's own conversion factor, and it would not expect that factor to exceed 1.7 (81 FR 33742 at 33908–09). Do not print 2.0× because a brochure said "better absorbed." The ceiling in that preamble is 1.7.

## The 1.7 factor

Write it on the worksheet. mcg folic acid × 1.7 = mcg DFE. 200 mcg folic acid = 340 mcg DFE. 400 = 680. 800 = 1,360. %DV for an adult column is DFE ÷ 400 × 100: 170% for 400 mcg folic acid, 340% for 800 mcg folic acid [VERIFY the 400 mcg DFE adult denominator].

A common error is to print 400 mcg DFE when the fill is 400 mcg folic acid, and then to print 100% DV. That panel understates both the DFE amount and the percent. The buyer who compares your "400 mcg DFE" to a competitor's "680 mcg DFE (400 mcg folic acid)" thinks you are weaker. You may be identical. The unit is the difference.

The inverse error is to take a 2014 "800 mcg" prenatal, multiply by 1.7, print 1,360 mcg DFE, and also leave "800 mcg" in the name line without saying folic acid. 101.9(c)(8)(iv) footnote 6: when folic acid is added or when a claim is made about the nutrient, folic acid must be declared in parentheses, as mcg of folic acid. The DFE number without the parenthetical hides the form. The parenthetical without DFE is a 2014 row.

Mixtures add. Conversion guidance Example 6: 50 mcg naturally occurring folate + 100 mcg folic acid + 150 mcg synthetic folate (as calcium L-5-MTHF, using 1.7 in the example) = 475 mcg DFE, with "(100 mcg folic acid)" in the parenthetical. Only folic acid goes in that parenthetical. The methylfolate is in the DFE sum. If you use a factor other than 1.7 for the methyl salt, write the factor on the formula card and do not exceed 1.7.

Food-folate powders are factor 1.0 for the naturally occurring portion. Do not apply 1.7 to a spinach powder because DFE "sounds stronger." DFE is a unit, not a marketing adjective. Supplement %DV rounding is nearest whole percent (101.36(b)(2)(iii)(C); piece 06), not the food-label rounding in the conversion guidance's cereal examples.

## Synonyms in parentheses

Chapter IV, 4-14, and 101.36(b)(2)(i)(B)(2): you may use synonyms in parentheses after the dietary-ingredient name. For this row, the closed list is **folate (folacin or folic acid)**. Alternatively, you may list "folic acid" or "folacin" without parentheses *in place of* "folate." You may not invent "activated folate," "Quatrefolic" as the (b)(2) name, or "L-methylfolate" as a substitute for the word folate on the amount line. Source and form can appear as the usual source syntax — Folate (as calcium L-5-MTHF) — if that is truthful. The *nutrient name* for the DV row is still folate, in mcg DFE.

After 2016, the synonym permission and the DFE unit have to live together. A row that says only "Folic Acid 400 mcg 100%" is a 2014 shape: wrong unit, wrong % if the denominator is DFE, and it skipped the DFE amount the supplement rule now wants when folic acid is added. A row that says "Folate 680 mcg DFE (400 mcg folic acid) 170%" is the 2019 guidance's own picture.

"Folacin" is the old synonym, still on the closed list, not a third form. Methylfolate brand names belong in the source line, not as a replacement unit. If you add both folic acid and a methyl salt, show DFE, show folic acid mcg in parentheses, and name both forms. Do not let the brand name of the salt become the row.

## What a prenatal panel must show

A prenatal is not a different regulation. It is 101.36 with a population column. If the product is directed at pregnant or lactating women, the %DV denominator is that column in 101.9(c)(8)(iv) — 600 mcg DFE in the current table [VERIFY]. 680 mcg DFE from 400 mcg folic acid is then 113% on that column, not 170%. Identify the column (Chapter IV, 4-27; piece 08). A prenatal that uses the adult 400 mcg DFE denominator so the percent looks larger is using the wrong table.

Serving size is still the maximum per eating occasion (piece 03). "Take 2 capsules daily" is a serving of 2 unless you split occasions and label them. The DFE amount is the amount in that serving. A front that says "800 mcg" when two capsules of 400 mcg folic acid are the serving must decide: 800 mcg *folic acid* (1,360 mcg DFE) or 800 mcg DFE (about 471 mcg folic acid). Those are different fills. The 2014 habit of saying "800 mcg" without DFE is why the aisle still argues. Pick the object. Print DFE. Print folic acid mcg in parentheses if folic acid is what you added.

If the prenatal uses only a methyl salt, you still declare folate in mcg DFE. You do not print a folic-acid parenthetical you did not add. You do not call the methyl salt "folic acid" to reuse a 2014 template. You do not skip DFE because "it's not folic acid so the old mcg is fine." The row is folate. The unit is mcg DFE.

Do not print one % and let the PDP say prenatal. Added folic acid owes 100% of the declared folic-acid mcg in the composite (piece 05). You do not assay "DFE." You assay the form, then apply the factor on the label.

## Health-claim fence (pointer only)

There is an authorized health-claim path that mentions folate and neural-tube birth defects. It is 21 CFR 101.14 plus the specific regulation that names the claim, with qualifying levels and model language. Piece 26 is that drawer. Pearson v. Shalala and qualified health claims are also piece 26. This piece will not write the model language, will not put "prevents neural-tube defects" on a sample PDP, and will not treat a mcg DFE row as that claim.

A Supplement Facts row is a quantity. It does not authorize a disease sentence on the principal display panel. Structure/function language is a different drawer (pieces 21–24). A 2014 "800 mcg" with no DFE is a unit the 2016 rule retired on this row.
