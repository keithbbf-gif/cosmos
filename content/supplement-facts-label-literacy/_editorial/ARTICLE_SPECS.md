# Article specs (writers)

Each file: YAML as in `articles/01-…`. Disclaimer first line after front matter:

`**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice and not a labeling opinion for a specific SKU.`

1,100–1,700 words. Open on a dated rule, letter, or number. Specific H2s. No brand names (ElitElixir, Unilever). No style-ban words. Disease names only as FDA/FTC *examples of forbidden claims* or paper/letter titles. Read `articles/01-supplement-facts-is-not-nutrition-facts.md` for voice.

## 02 walk-the-panel-in-order — 2005

Title: Walk the panel in the order the rule wrote it
Thesis: Title → Serving Size → (b)(2) DV ingredients → hairline → (b)(3) other dietary ingredients → other-ingredient statement. 101.36(e) box, bold title larger than other print, hairlines between rows.
Cite: 21 CFR 101.36(b), 101.36(e); FDA DSLG Ch. IV 4-1, 4-35, 4-38
Open: 101.36(e) requires the panel in a box; title larger than all other print in the panel.
H2s: The box and the title; Serving Size before any milligram; (b)(2) in food order; The hairline; (b)(3) and the asterisk; Under the box; Small-pack exceptions are not a new category

## 03 serving-size-is-a-legal-object — 2005

Title: Serving size is a legal object
Thesis: 101.12(b) Table 2 miscellaneous: one serving = maximum amount recommended per eating occasion, else 1 unit. "Take 1–3 tablets" → serving is 3. Must say "Serving Size" exactly.
Cite: 21 CFR 101.12(b) Table 2; 101.36(b)(1); DSLG 4-4, 4-5
Open: If the directions say 1–3 tablets with breakfast, the serving size is 3 tablets. FDA wrote that example.
H2s: The maximum per eating occasion; You may not rename the heading; Household measure plus metric; "Suggested use" that fights the panel; Per unit is extra, not instead; Kids and prenatal columns (pointer)

## 04 servings-per-container-two-gummy-trick — 2023

Title: Servings per container and the two-gummy trick
Thesis: Servings line can be omitted only when it equals net contents. A 60-gummy bottle with serving = 2 is 30 servings. Cohen JAMA 2023 is the dose-vs-label scar; this piece is the container math, piece 40 is kids/candy.
Cite: 21 CFR 101.36(b); DSLG 4-3; Cohen PA et al. JAMA 2023 (name the paper, do not invent n)
Open: A bottle that looks like a month is a month only if serving size is 1.
H2s: When you may drop the line; When two is the serving; Net contents vs days of use; Gummies inherit the same rule; What Cohen showed about declared vs found (no disease story)

## 05 amount-per-serving-not-per-bottle — 2005

Title: Amount per serving is not amount per bottle
Thesis: Amounts are per serving. You may add per-unit column (101.36(b)(2)(iv)). You may say "Amount Per 2 Tablets." Composite 12-subsample compliance (101.36(f)(1)). Added dietary ingredients: 100% of declared (101.9(g)(3)–(4)); naturally occurring 80%.
Cite: 101.36(b)(2); 101.36(f)(1); 101.9(g)(3)–(4); DSLG 4-18, 4-19, 4-41, 4-43
Open: FDA will composite 12 packages or 10% of the lot, whichever is smaller.
H2s: Per serving is the legal number; Extra columns; The 100% rule for what you added; Rose hips vs isolated C; A bottle-front milligram that ignores serving

## 06 percent-daily-value-what-twenty-means — 2016

Title: "% Daily Value: what 20% is, and what it is not"
Thesis: %DV = unrounded amount ÷ Daily Value × 100. Nearest whole percent; "<1%" when it would round to 0. Not an efficacy grade. 20% is a nutrient-content "excellent source" threshold on foods (101.54) — do not smuggle that onto a botanical. Four DV sets; identify columns.
Cite: 101.36(b)(2)(iii); 101.9(c)(8)–(9); 101.54; DSLG 4-22 to 4-27; 81 FR 33742
Open: 60 mg vitamin C is 100% DV because the adult DV is 60 mg in the old example; confirm current adult DVs before print [VERIFY current 101.9 RDI table].
H2s: The division; Rounding and "<1%"; Why you cannot list a 1% RDI vitamin as 0%; More than one column; %DV is not a clinical result; Nutrient-content claims live next door

## 07 no-dv-asterisk-and-the-footnote — 2005

Title: No DV, an asterisk, and the footnote people skip
Thesis: (b)(3) ingredients have no DV. Symbol + "Daily Value not established." 2,000-calorie footnote only when you declare total fat, sat fat, total carb, fiber, or protein. No 2,000/2,500 comparison footnote required (unlike food).
Cite: 101.36(b)(3); 101.36(b)(2)(iii)(D); DSLG 4-28, 4-30, 4-55
Open: Phosphatidylserine is FDA's own example of an other dietary ingredient.
H2s: What "other dietary ingredient" means; Where they sit; The asterisk is not optional; The calorie footnote is conditional; Skipping the footnote is how blends look official

## 08 dual-columns-per-day-as-prepared — 2005

Cite: 101.36(b)(2)(i)(A); 101.36(e)(8); 101.36(e)(10); DSLG 4-20, 4-27, 4-40

## 15 extract-ratios-and-the-constituent — 2005

Title: Extract ratios and the labeled constituent
Thesis: Liquid extracts: volume/weight of total extract + condition of starting material if fresh; may give y:z and solvent (101.36(b)(3)(ii)(B)). Dried extracts: weight of dried extract (ii)(C). Constituents may be indented (iii). 10:1 is not 10× the herb in the body.
Cite: 101.36(b)(3)(ii)–(iii); DSLG 4-31 to 4-33
Open: "Fresh dandelion root extract, x (y:z) in 70% ethanol" is FDA's own pattern.
H2s: Liquid extracts; Dried extracts; Indented constituents; Ratio theater; Solvent belongs on the label or the ingredient list

## 16 proprietary-blends-101-36-c — 2005
