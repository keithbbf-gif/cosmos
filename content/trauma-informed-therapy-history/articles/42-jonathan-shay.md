**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jonathan Shay was born in 1941 and is living as of last verification (2026-09-14). This essay uses the two books. No health talk. No private life.

He spent years as a VA psychiatrist listening to Vietnam combat veterans and reading Homer with them. *Achilles in Vietnam: Combat Trauma and the Undoing of Character* (1994) argued that some of what wrecked these men was the betrayal of “what’s right” by commanders, by the war’s rules, by a homecoming that wanted a hero or a monster. *Odysseus in America* (2002) took the shoreline: the veteran as a dangerous guest. Litz and colleagues, in 2009, made moral injury a research construct. Shay made it usable in English-language psychiatry first.

## A psychiatrist who would not leave command out

The era essay stays with 1994 and 2009 as naming events. This figure stays with the stubbornness about leadership. Achilles breaks, in Shay’s reading, when honor is stolen in public. Vietnam, in the accounts he trusted, broke men when officers wasted lives and official stories insulted the people who had been there. Trauma-informed *systems* talk that never mentions leadership betrayal has read a pamphlet and skipped the VA office.

He is not DSM-III. Moral injury is not another name for the 1980 criterion set. A person can have both. A person can have the shame and the wrecked trust without the classic cluster. The point of a second name is the second injury.

He is not Freyd. Institutional betrayal is a civilian, dependence-on-a-building construct. The rhyme is real. The rooms differ. A platoon is not a residence hall.

## Homer, and the risk of decoration

Using Homer in a VA clinic is easy to mock and easy to romanticize. This essay will do neither at length. The comparison was a working tool for Shay, not a humanities grant. Later chaplains and journalists looted the hexameters. Looting is the afterlife of any good title. Prefer the veterans’ sentences in the books to the newspaper’s Achilles.

Lifton 1973 is the earlier public picture of Vietnam veterans who were neither victims nor executioners. Shay took the character-injury half further. Do not flatten them into one biography.

## Limits

This page will not assign you the *Iliad*. It will not tell the VA what to rate. If you are in danger, call local emergency services. If you are a veteran, use the VA and accredited veteran-service organizations.
