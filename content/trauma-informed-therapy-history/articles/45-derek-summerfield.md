**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Derek Summerfield’s “The Invention of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder and the Social Usefulness of a Psychiatric Category” appeared in the *BMJ* in 2001. He wrote as a psychiatrist who had worked in humanitarian and cross-cultural settings and was tired of watching a Western module arrive with the aid flight. The headline word *invention* is easy to hear as an accusation. The paper is better read as a question about *use*: who a category serves, what it organizes, what it lets an NGO skip.

This pack required itself to read him before it printed forty-five drafts. The era essay on critiques stays with the pair — Young 1995 and Summerfield 2001. This figure stays with the 2001 object and the habit of mind.

## What the 2001 paper is for

It is good for the airport. It is good for the moment a checklist is translated and a political catastrophe becomes a symptom score. It is good for the reminder that some people want a house, a passport, and a fair asylum interview more than they want an hour. The refugee-program essay already walked that trouble through Mollica’s clinic. The trouble remains if you dislike Summerfield’s tone.

It is less good as a universal solvent. A person who was raped last year can hear “invention” as a sneer. A staged history refuses that hearing without refusing the paper. Social usefulness cuts both ways. A category can pad a budget. It can also name an injury a family preferred to call personality.

Young’s monograph is the thicker cousin: diagnosis as cultural achievement, assembled from available pieces. Summerfield is shorter, sharper, aimed at export. Do not flatten them. Do not pick a president of dissent.

## Why a WOW pack keeps a critic

Without this figure, the series is a brochure for SMA 14-4884. With only this figure, the series is a sneer at every ward this pack has named. Burgess’s emergency room, Brave Heart’s 1998 papers, a child who cannot sleep — they do not vanish because a *BMJ* essay is good.

Universal trauma precautions, as manners, are often right. As analysis, they can become a way not to think. Summerfield is one of the people who makes thinking cheaper to start. He does not get to finish it for a speech-language clinic in Southeast Arkansas. WOW Therapies will not use him to sound worldly about “the trauma industry” on a parent-facing page. A sneer at trauma talk is a different injury.

## Limits

This page will not tell you PTSD is fake. It will not tell you it is a natural kind like a femur. It will not help you deny a claim. If you are in danger, call local emergency services.
