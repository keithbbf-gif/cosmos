**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Henry Krystal was born in 1925 in what obituaries file as Poland, survived the Holocaust, trained as a psychiatrist in the United States, and died in 2015. In 1968 he edited *Massive Psychic Trauma*, a volume that gathered clinicians who were trying to name what camps, survival, and aftermath had done to people the official American chart still handled awkwardly. William G. Niederland’s “survivor syndrome” work belongs in the same room. This pack puts Krystal on the calendar because the trauma field’s memory of itself is still too quick to run from Myers to DSM-III without the survivor clinics of the 1950s and 1960s.

## A survivor who became a doctor, and the rules

Living people get extra care in this series; the recently dead get a version of it. This essay uses the public book and the public career. It will not reconstruct camps. It will not embroider a childhood. The fact of survival is already in every responsible obituary. The fact of the 1968 volume is the artifact we can check.

Krystal wrote about alexithymia — difficulty finding words for inner life — in ways later psychosomatic and trauma clinicians looted, sometimes carefully, sometimes as a slogan. This page will not teach you to spot alexithymia in a waiting room. It will say that a psychiatrist who had reasons to know about unspeakability tried to make unspeakability a clinical object without turning it into romance.

## Why 1968 matters to a systems pack

1968 is late enough that American psychiatry had psychoanalysis and early psychopharmacology, and early enough that it did not yet have PTSD. Survivor clinics had to invent language the manual would not give them for another dozen years. That invention is a reminder, again, that official names lag. It is also a reminder that “trauma” was never only a soldier’s word, even when the soldier’s word won the 1980 chart.

Yael Danieli’s 1998 handbook on multigenerational legacies is a later comparative door. Do not make Krystal into Danieli. Do not make either into Brave Heart. Three literatures, three rooms. The rhyme is the refusal to let a single-incident criterion set exhaust a massive, prolonged, political injury.

## Limits

This page will not diagnose a descendant. It will not offer a Holocaust-education hour as therapy. If you are in danger, call local emergency services. If you want survivor-family care, use the clinics and landsmanshaftn and Jewish family services that actually exist — not a 1968 table of contents.

WOW Therapies has no standing to specialize in this literature. A speech-language practice may meet an older adult whose language is changing for neurological reasons. That is speech. Do not file it under Krystal.
