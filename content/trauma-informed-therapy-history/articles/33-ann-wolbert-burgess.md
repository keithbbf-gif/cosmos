**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Ann Wolbert Burgess was born in 1936 and is living as of last verification (2026-09-14). This essay uses the 1974 paper and the public career. No health talk. No private life.

With the sociologist Lynda Lytle Holmstrom she published “Rape Trauma Syndrome” in the *American Journal of Psychiatry* in 1974. They had sat with women in a Boston emergency ward and then sat with them again. They gave civilian medicine a name that did not require a uniform. The era essay stays with the ward. This figure stays with the nurse who made the ward a journal object.

## A psychiatric nurse, not a mascot

Burgess’s later career in forensic nursing, victimology, and — in the public telling — work that touched FBI behavioral-science conversations is how some profiles now lead. ## Why a systems pack keeps a nurse

SAMHSA’s *resist re-traumatization* is a federal verb with a ward ancestor. A kit, a light, a question asked twice, a police officer in the curtain. Harris and Fallot wrote about community desks. Burgess wrote about a night shift. Trauma-informed *medical* rooms, the rehab essay in this pack argues, inherit her problem more directly than they inherit a VA form.

Holmstrom’s name belongs on every citation. This slug is Burgess because the series needed a figure slot and the later public career is hers. The paper is both of theirs. Do not disappear the sociologist.
