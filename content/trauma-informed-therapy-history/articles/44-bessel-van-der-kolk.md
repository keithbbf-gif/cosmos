**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Bessel van der Kolk was born in 1943 and is living as of last verification (2026-09-14). This essay uses the public books. No health talk. No private life. No gossip about institutes or employers.

*Psychological Trauma* (1987) and the 1996 handbook edited with Alexander C. McFarlane and Lars Weisaeth are the scholarly objects. The Developmental Trauma Disorder proposal, refused by DSM-5, is the chart event. *The Body Keeps the Score* (Viking, 2014) is the publishing event. This pack needs all three, in that order of seriousness. The title leaked into intakes. The leak is not a credential. Kardiner already had a watchful body in 1941.

## A career the trade book flattened

Van der Kolk spent decades in Boston-area trauma clinics and in the ISTSS generation’s arguments. He wrote about children, about the body, about treatments this page will not list as a menu. He became, after 2014, the name a general reader brings to a first appointment. Fame of that kind helps a field get read. It also helps a field get sloppy. Polyvagal popular claims, sensory promises, and “the body keeps the score” as a substitute for a citation are the sloppiness. Some of that sloppiness is the market. Some of it is how a long book gets reduced to a proverb.

This series will not adjudicate every method he has praised. It will not teach yoga as psychiatry. It will not teach EMDR because he has discussed it. It will say that a 2014 trade book is a historical fact, and that a refused child diagnosis is a historical fact, and that neither fact makes a speech clinic a trauma center.

McFarlane and Weisaeth belong on the 1996 handbook. Do not disappear the editors. Handbooks are how a field tells itself it has arrived. 1996 is an arrival. 2014 is a different kind of arrival.

## DTD and the hallway

The era essay on the rejected chart already told the refusal. This figure repeats only the career meaning: he wanted a name equal to the child caseload; the manual said no; the hallway kept talking. Hallway diagnoses are how fields live when the box is too small. They are also how workshops sell past the file.

ICD-11’s adult complex PTSD is a cousin victory, not DTD. Do not collapse them to make the biography tidier.

## Limits

This page will not assign you the 2014 book as a workbook. It will not diagnose a child with developmental trauma. If you are in danger, call local emergency services. If you want care, use a licensed clinician — and be wary of any office whose only citation is a proverb.

WOW Therapies will not use his title in a meta description next to a booking button. That pairing is a claims problem.

No portrait in this wave. Living person: license and reason required later. Do not generate a face. Do not use the trade-cover as a clinical credential.
