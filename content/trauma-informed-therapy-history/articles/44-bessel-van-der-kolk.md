**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Bessel van der Kolk was born in 1943 and is living as of last verification (2026-09-14). This essay uses the public books. No health talk. No private life. No gossip about institutes or employers.

*Psychological Trauma* (1987) and the 1996 handbook edited with Alexander C. McFarlane and Lars Weisaeth are the scholarly objects. The Developmental Trauma Disorder proposal, refused by DSM-5, is the chart event. *The Body Keeps the Score* (Viking, 2014) is the publishing event. This pack needs all three, in that order of seriousness. The title leaked into intakes. The leak is not a credential. Kardiner already had a watchful body in 1941.

## A career the trade book flattened

Van der Kolk spent decades in Boston-area trauma clinics and in the ISTSS generation’s arguments. He wrote about children, about the body, about treatments this page will not list as a menu. He became, after 2014, the name a general reader brings to a first appointment. Fame of that kind helps a field get read. It also helps a field get sloppy. Polyvagal popular claims, sensory promises, and “the body keeps the score” as a substitute for a citation are the sloppiness. Some of that sloppiness is the market. Some of it is how a long book gets reduced to a proverb.

This series will not adjudicate every method he has praised. It will not teach yoga as psychiatry. It will not teach EMDR because he has discussed it. It will say that a 2014 trade book is a historical fact, and that a refused child diagnosis is a historical fact, and that neither fact makes a speech clinic a trauma center.

McFarlane and Weisaeth belong on the 1996 handbook. Do not disappear the editors. Handbooks are how a field tells itself it has arrived. 1996 is an arrival. 2014 is a different kind of arrival.

## Three objects: 1987, 1996, 2014

*Psychological Trauma* (American Psychiatric Press, 1987) is an early single-editor book that told American psychiatry the topic had a shelf. *Traumatic Stress* (Guilford, 1996), edited with Alexander C. McFarlane and Lars Weisaeth, is a handbook that told a field it had arrived. *The Body Keeps the Score* (Viking, 2014) is a trade event that told airports the field had a proverb. Three objects. Three jobs. Collapsing them into one proverb is how Kardiner’s 1941 watchful body disappears from the citation.

The Developmental Trauma Disorder proposal, not adopted by DSM-5, is a fourth object and belongs to the rejected-chart essay. This figure page will not retry the work group. It will say a hallway kept talking after 2013 without a box, and that talking is not the same as a code.

Podcasts, large conferences, and a training economy around the Trauma Center — workshops, certificates, waiting lists — are 2014-and-after conditions. Historians will have to separate the 1980s–1990s journal record from the 2010s stage. Training economies are not sins; they are facts. This page started that separation and will not finish it. Finishing it belongs to a biography, and biographies of living people should wait. WOW borrows the older body-watchful idea with a 1941 citation when possible and keeps 2014 in the category *popularization*. Popularization is how teachers find a vocabulary. It is not a care plan or a speech-clinic menu. No gossip about institutes.

## American Psychiatric Press is not Viking

The 1987 book’s imprint is a professional press. The 2014 book’s imprint
is a trade house. That difference is why teachers met a proverb and
psychiatrists had already met a shelf. Citing the proverb as if it were
the 1987 object is how 1941 disappears. Cite Kardiner when the claim is
a watchful body. Cite 2014 when the claim is that a phrase left the shop
and entered the waiting room.

McFarlane and Weisaeth remain on the 1996 handbook line. A three-editor
volume is how a field announced it had chapters. A refused child code is
how a hallway learned it would keep talking without a box. The 1987 imprint
and the 2014 imprint should stay on separate shelves in a citation, not
merged into one proverb that erases Kardiner. Living author: public books only.
No institute gossip on these URLs. No care plan from a title alone.
