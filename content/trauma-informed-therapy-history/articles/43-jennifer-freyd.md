**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jennifer J. Freyd is living as of last verification (2026-09-14). This essay uses the 1994 paper, the 1996 Harvard book, and the 2013 *Journal of Traumatic Stress* paper with Carly Parnitzke Smith. Public papers only. No family gossip. No health talk. The memory-wars decade attached itself to her name in public ways this pack will not recycle.

Betrayal trauma theory, as she developed it in early-1990s talks and then in print, says there can be a social use in remaining unaware of abuse when the perpetrator is the person you must still depend on. Not-knowing, in that frame, can be an adaptation, not a parlor trick. *Betrayal Trauma: The Logic of Forgetting Childhood Abuse* (1996) is the book. Smith and Freyd 2013 moved the dependence into the building: a church, a campus, a unit that advertises safety and then punishes the reporter.

## Two objects, one career this pack needs

The memory-wars essay already refused a verdict. Loftus and McNally are the skeptical research program. Freyd is not their cartoon opposite. She is a different mechanism claim. A staged history can keep both programs on the table. A website that cannot is a faction.

Institutional betrayal is why she belongs in a systems pack, not only in a memory pack. SAMHSA’s *resist re-traumatization* is a cousin verb. Shay’s leadership betrayal is a cousin from the war side. Do not merge the constructs. Do not pretend a 2014 pamphlet invented the building problem.

Later “institutional courage” language, which some campuses adopted as branding, is an afterlife. Branding is the risk. A building that prints courage and keeps the same counsel script has learned a noun.

Smith’s name belongs on the 2013 paper. This slug is Freyd because the 1996 book is the prior theory. Do not disappear the collaborator.
