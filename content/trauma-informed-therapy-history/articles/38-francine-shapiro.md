**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Francine Shapiro was born in 1948 and died in 2019. In 1989 she published two papers introducing eye-movement desensitization (later EMDR): one in the *Journal of Behavior Therapy and Experimental Psychiatry*, one in the *Journal of Traumatic Stress*. She later described a 1987 walk during which she noticed, or believed she noticed, that eye movements changed the intensity of disturbing thoughts. Origin walks are good copy. The 1989 objects are the artifacts this pack can check.

This series names EMDR as an *arrival* in the decades after DSM-III. It will not teach you to move your eyes. It will not settle the mechanism fight. It will not sell the method as settled superiority. The research file is thicker than a sneer and thinner than a miracle.

## A 1989 object in a new field

PTSD had been official for nine years. Randomized trauma-treatment studies were still few. Shapiro’s papers entered a shop that was hungry for something that looked new and testable. Hunger is not validation. Later trials, fidelity fights, and guideline disagreements are the afterlife. Shapiro, in a 2009 interview, complained about studies she thought had used too little treatment or poor fidelity. That complaint is itself a historical object: method founders often hate the first decade of other people’s experiments.

ISTSS guidelines and VA/DoD guidelines have, in various editions, given EMDR a place among trauma-focused therapies. Places in guidelines move. This draft will not freeze a 2026 guideline sentence as if it were 1989. `[VERIFY]` the current VA/DoD wording if an editor wants a live-site footnote. The staged claim is smaller: by the 2000s EMDR was no longer only a curiosity, and it was still a quarrel.

## What this figure is not

She is not Harris and Fallot. EMDR is a trauma-specific treatment. Trauma-informed care is a building. Confusing them is how a clinic website becomes a claims problem. WOW Therapies does not offer EMDR on the strength of this URL. A speech-language practice that advertised eye movements would be off its license.

She is not van der Kolk, though popular audiences sometimes file every “body” method under one trade title. Different shops. Different claims. Different evidence files.

She is not a saint and not a fraud. Those two cartoons flattened a person who built a training institute and a literature. Institutes are commercial and scientific at once. Hold both.

## Limits

This page will not describe bilateral stimulation. It will not give you a target-memory worksheet. If you are in danger, call local emergency services. If you want trauma treatment, use a licensed clinician and a method you have actually discussed — not a 1989 abstract.
