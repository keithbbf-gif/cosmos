# Claims guardrails — trauma-informed care history

Educational history only. These sentences must never go on wowtherapies.com, in ads, or in schema markup.

This file is a refuse list. If a draft drifts toward any line below, cut the line. Do not "soften" it into a how-to.

## Never say (treatment / DIY)

- Any instruction a reader could follow as a session: exposure hierarchies, EMDR eye-movement how-tos, CPT worksheets, PE homework, "ask yourself these ten questions," grounding scripts sold as treatment.
- "Try this at home," "use this worksheet," "self-treat," "you can EMDR your way out of…"
- Dosing of psychiatric medication, taper advice, or "ask your doctor about [drug]" as content.
- "This method is the gold standard for [diagnosis]."
- "Studies prove [school] works better than [school]" without a named trial, a dated review, and a sentence about limits.
- Crisis instructions that replace emergency services. A single line — "If you are in danger, call local emergency services" — is enough. Do not write a safety-planning protocol in these essays.

## Never say (medical / legal / brand)

- That WOW Therapies diagnoses, treats, or "specializes in" PTSD, complex PTSD, or any DSM/ICD trauma diagnosis **in these history posts**.
- That the clinic is a trauma-informed *treatment* center, a rape-crisis program, or a veterans’ clinic.
- That reading an essay is therapy or a substitute for a licensed clinician.
- PHI, composite patients, or "a client once told me" stories. No invented case vignettes with identifying color.
- Guarantees of outcome.
- Anything a reasonable reader would hear as a diagnosis of themselves or their child.
- OT/PT services implied as currently staffed. Speech is what the live body copy offers.

## Never say (history that is too neat)

- "Pinel struck off the chains." (Different pack, same scar. Do not recycle the allegory.)
- "Freud discovered the unconscious."
- "Herman invented trauma." She named a syndrome and forced a conversation. Janet, Kardiner, Burgess, and a veterans’ movement were already in the room.
- "The ACE score predicts your future." The 1998 paper reported graded *associations* in a Kaiser sample. It is not destiny and not a diagnosis.
- "The body keeps the score" as if a 2014 trade book settled neurobiology.
- Polyvagal popular claims as settled physiology.
- Refrigerator-mother, schizophrenogenic-mother, or Bettelheim as fact.
- Conversion-therapy "success."
- Recovered-memory therapy as proven **or** as a hoax that voids every delayed report. The memory-wars essay must hold both the documented harm of some 1990s methods and the documented reality of sexual violence.
- That DSM-III (1980) "ended ideology."
- That SAMHSA’s 2014 pamphlet is a treatment manual.
- That "trauma-informed" on a website equals trauma-specific care.

## Never say (brand / SEO)

- "Best therapist in Arkansas" or any superlative about the practice.
- Disease keywords paired with a CTA ("overcome PTSD today — book now") on these URLs.
- Schema `MedicalTherapy`, `Drug`, or `treats` / `indication` on history posts. `Article` is enough.
- Fake author credentials.
- Patient testimonials in this folder.

## Allowed

- Dated description of what a school claimed, who paid for it, who dissented, and what later researchers revised.
- Named trials as historical events (Foa 1991; Resick’s CPT papers; Linehan 1991 belongs in the other pack).
- Controversy, including racism, sexism, colonial psychiatry, boarding schools, and eugenics, when it is part of the record.
- A short "why a contemporary clinician still reads this" closing that does **not** become a protocol.
- The educational footer already in each draft.
- One sentence pointing a reader who wants *speech* help to the clinic’s service pages — never from a PTSD keyword.

## Living people

Herman, van der Kolk, Foa, Resick, Freyd, Felitti, Bloom, Figley, Horowitz, Brave Heart, Shay, Harris, Fallot, and others who were alive at last verification (2026-09-14) are not public property. No health speculation. No unsourced private life. Public books, public lectures, and public interviews only.

Shapiro died in 2019. Treat her as a historical figure with a contested method, not as a saint or a fraud.

## If asked for both a history and a how-to

Print the history. Refuse the how-to in one sentence: this series does not publish treatment protocols.
