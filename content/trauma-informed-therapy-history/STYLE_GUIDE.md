# Style guide — WOW Therapies, trauma-informed care history

This pack is educational history for [WOWTherapies.com](https://wowtherapies.com): how a wound became a diagnosis, how a diagnosis became a **service-system** idea, and who dissented. It is **not** the counseling-heritage pack and **not** the SLPWOW speech-pathology series.

`voice_check: human` means the writer draft passed this file. `voice_check: edited` means a human editor read the piece aloud and tightened grammar, voice, and guardrails. Neither value is a byline and must not appear on the public page.

## Who is speaking

A historically literate magazine writer who has sat in enough clinic waiting rooms to know that a poster is not a method. Warm without being pastoral. Precise without being a textbook. The reader may be a curious parent, an adult patient, a student, or a clinician. Do not talk down. Do not talk shop as if the room were empty of patients.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas **speech-language** practice. These essays are **heritage education**, not a menu of trauma services and not a claim that the clinic practices every school named here.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" as a metaphor for a field
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- three-beat synonym piles ("heal, grow, and thrive")
- "the father of…" unless a contemporary actually used that phrase and you are quoting the myth, not endorsing it
- "Freud discovered the unconscious"
- "gold-standard treatment" as marketing
- invented quotes
- AI-generated faces or "period photographs" that never existed
- "the body keeps the score" as a credential or as a substitute for a citation
- "try this at home" / session scripts / skill worksheets

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "in this article we will explore," "journey of healing" as filler.

SAMHSA’s principle **Empowerment, Voice and Choice** may be named as a proper title. Do not otherwise sprinkle "empower."

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a room, a document, or a named quarrel. The first sentence should be checkable.
2. Mix sentence length. A short sentence after a long one.
3. Name the book, paper, hospital, or agency. *Using Trauma Theory to Design Service Systems* (2001). SMA 14-4884 (2014). Myers in *The Lancet*, 13 February 1915.
4. Mark uncertainty. `[CITE NEEDED]` or a plain "historians disagree" beats a clean falsehood.
5. Distinguish **trauma-specific treatment** (a billed method for a diagnosed injury) from **trauma-informed care** (how a whole organization tries not to add a second injury). Do not collapse them.
6. Cross-link the counseling-heritage pack for Herman and the short trauma-lineages overview. Do not reprint those two essays.
7. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target 1,200–2,000 words for era / institution essays, 1,000–1,800 for figures. `check_pack.py` fails an era under 1,200 words or a figure under 1,000.

## Claims posture

See `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`. History may describe a method. History may not teach a reader to self-administer it.

Efficacy sentences, if any, stay historical and sourced ("Foa’s 1991 rape-survivor trial reported…") and never become "prolonged exposure will help your PTSD."

Living people get extra care: no gossip, no unsourced health claims.

## Portraits

Public domain or Creative Commons only. Document the file in `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md`. If there is no clean license, use type or a period object — never a generated face. See `PHOTO_NOTES.md`.

## Series voice, in one paragraph

Write as if *The New Yorker* and *Psychotherapy Networker* had to share a desk, and the desk was in a small-city clinic that still answers its own phone. The past is allowed to be strange. The present is not allowed to be sold. Trauma-informed care is allowed to be a federal pamphlet, a feminist clinic, a VA form, and a disputed slogan — sometimes in the same decade.
