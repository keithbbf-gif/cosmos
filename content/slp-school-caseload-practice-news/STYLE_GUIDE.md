# Style guide — SLPWOW school caseload / practice / news

Staged copy for **SLPWOW.com**. Written for working school SLPs, special educators, and the informed parents who sit next to them at IEP tables. This pack does not publish itself.

`voice_check: human` means the draft was written against this file. `voice_check: edited` means an editor pass on top. Neither flag is a byline.

## Who is speaking

A careful writer who has sat with the 2024 ASHA Schools Survey PDF, the IDEA statute, and a principal who still thinks “caseload” is a headcount. Not a union flyer. Not a vendor. Not a scare site. Not “as a school SLP myself…” unless that person is real and named — they are not.

Keith’s wife’s practice is the eventual host. The series should feel like a well-edited magazine section a clinician can finish between a bus duty and an IEP.

## Who it is for

School-based speech-language pathologists first. Special educators, principals, and parents may overhear. Do not write “whether you’re a parent or a clinician.” One reader at a time. When a piece is for a principal brief, say so in the opening.

## Voice

Human. Specific. A little dry warmth is allowed. Cheerleading is not. Panic is not.

Open on a number a reader can check, a statute, a survey table, or a sentence a headline got wrong. Name the document and the year. If you do not have it, do not fake the furniture.

Prefer:

- “ASHA’s 2024 Schools Survey put the median actual monthly caseload at 50 and the median *manageable* caseload at 40.”
- “Caseload is a headcount. Workload is the week.”
- “Endrew F. rejected ‘merely more than de minimis.’ It did not print a minute count.”

Avoid:

- *delve, leverage, robust, game-changer, cutting-edge, in conclusion, it’s important to note, whether you’re a*
- “Imagine a world where every student…”
- Fake clinic names, fake students, fake districts
- “Best school SLP,” city superlatives, booking CTAs

## Length

Each article is a real explainer, not a news card. Target **750–1,200 words** of body. The pack checker fails under 650.

## Facts

- Survey numbers come from the dated ASHA PDF, not from memory of an older year.
- The 2022 Schools Survey median was 48. The 2024 median is 50. Do not blend them.
- ASHA does **not** recommend a national caseload cap. Say that when you talk ratios.
- State medians with n < 25 were blank in the 2024 table. Do not invent them.
- Mark an unverified figure `[VERIFY]`. Do not fabricate a PMID.

## PHI and people

No real student. No “Maya, 8, on my Tuesday group.” Composite hallway scenes without names are allowed if they teach a *practice* distinction, not a case.

No health speculation about living people. Public researchers and Court opinions may be named for their documents.

## What a piece is allowed to do

Explain a document. Separate a headline from a table. Name a door (IEP, 504, MTSS, Medicaid parental consent) without promising what is on the other side. Tell a clinician how to *brief* a principal. Tell a parent how to *read* a service page.

## What a piece is not allowed to do

Diagnose. Publish a treatment protocol. Give billing codes. Tell a district which union vote to take. Promise eligibility. Invent a caseload cap “ASHA says.”

## Disclaimer

Every draft carries the educational / no-PHI disclaimer in the first screen. Do not restyle it as a tiny gray line on import.
