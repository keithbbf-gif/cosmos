# Claims guardrails — school SLP practice, caseload, and news

Internal. Not a blog post. These drafts are educational explainers for **SLPWOW**. They are not a clinic chart, not a legal memo, not a billing manual, and not a student record.

If a sentence would let a reader leave thinking *we just named a child* or *we just changed our IEP minutes because a blog said so*, cut it.

Counsel / a licensed SLP still owns the last read before any URL goes live.

## What this pack is allowed to be

A magazine-section explanation of school speech-language practice: how caseload differs from workload, what ASHA’s 2024 Schools Survey actually measured, how IDEA related services and *Endrew F.* show up in the news, and how to read the headlines that keep using one word for three problems.

It is not evaluation. It is not treatment. It is not “caseload SEO.” It is not PHI.

## The documents, not the vibe

- ASHA 2024 Schools Survey — *SLP Caseload and Workload Characteristics* (fielded Jan–Mar 2024; PDF rev. 3/10/25). Median actual monthly caseload **50** (range 4–351; n = 2,815 full-time clinical providers). Median manageable **40** (n = 2,776). Citation: ASHA (2024), <https://www.asha.org/siteassets/surveys/2024-schools-survey-slp-caseload.pdf>.
- ASHA 2024 Schools Survey — *SLP Workforce and Work Conditions*. Paperwork ranked first of 13 challenges in every facility type. 34% considering a setting change and 27% considering leaving the profession because of burnout. 79% said more openings than seekers. 12% supervised an SLPA. <https://www.asha.org/siteassets/surveys/2024-schools-survey-slp-workforce.pdf>.
- ASHA Practice Portal — Caseload and Workload. Caseload ≠ workload. ASHA does **not** recommend a specific caseload number. Workload analysis (2002 position statement). <https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/caseload-and-workload>.
- ASHA (2010). Roles and responsibilities of SLPs in schools (position + professional issues). <https://www.asha.org/policy/ps2010-00318/>.
- IDEA 2004 (as amended): FAPE, LRE, related services, IEP contents, evaluation timelines as *federal floors* — states may be tighter.
- *Endrew F. v. Douglas County School Dist. RE–1*, 580 U.S. 386 (2017): IEP “reasonably calculated to enable a child to make progress appropriate in light of the child’s circumstances.” Rejected “merely more than de minimis.”
- CMS / ED (May 2023). *Delivering Services in School-Based Settings* (BSCA §11003). School-based Medicaid guide. Not a coding sheet.
- FERPA (20 U.S.C. §1232g) for education records. HIPAA applies to covered entities; most school records are FERPA records. Do not flatten the two.

## Never say (PHI / labeling)

- Any sentence that identifies a real student, family, school, or IEP meeting.
- “Maya, 8, on my Tuesday /r/ group…” or any age + unique detail that could be a real child.
- “Your child has a language disorder / autism / apraxia / SLI.”
- “This IEP means they have X.”
- Clinic testimonials. WOW Therapies / SLPWOW patient stories. Keith’s family in the copy.

Composite hallway or conference-room scenes **without names** are allowed if they teach a practice distinction (caseload vs workload, 504 vs IEP), not a label.

## Never say (practice / legal overreach)

- “ASHA’s caseload cap is 40.” ASHA explicitly refuses a national number.
- “Your district is out of compliance because the median is 50.” A survey is not a finding.
- “Schedule 30 minutes twice a week” as a default, or any dosing recipe.
- Home or school *treatment protocols*: cycles, minimal pairs to drill, PROMPT cues, PECS phases, fluency-shaping steps.
- Billing codes, unit counts, “bill Medicaid this way,” or a promise that a service is covered.
- Union vote advice. “You should strike.” “Refuse this assignment.”
- “This student is eligible” / “dismiss them.”
- That a headline alone justifies changing minutes, groups, or eligibility.

## Never say (news / brand)

- Vendor marketing as SLPWOW endorsement.
- “Best school SLP,” “#1 caseload solution,” city superlatives.
- Schema `MedicalWebPage`, `MedicalCondition`, `Drug`, or `treats`. `Article` is enough.
- Fake credentials.

## Allowed shapes

- Dated, sourced description of what ASHA, IDEA, the Court, CMS, and ED actually printed.
- The difference between **caseload** (headcount / sessions) and **workload** (the week).
- “Median actual 50 / median manageable 40” — always with the 2024 PDF in the same breath the first time it appears in a piece.
- “ASHA does not recommend a specific caseload number” — say why (student needs vary; a number becomes a floor).
- Naming doors: IEP, IFSP, 504, MTSS/RTI, parental consent for Medicaid, FERPA access.
- How to *read* a state ratio bill vs a vacancy story vs a compensatory-services story.
- How to *brief* a principal with a workload list, not a complaint.
- ASHA’s four workload clusters: direct services; indirect support of the educational program; LRE/curriculum support; compliance.

## Percentiles, ranges, and honesty

A median is not a mandate. Indiana’s 2024 median of 78 and New York’s 32 are **survey** numbers from states with enough respondents (n ≥ 25). Blank cells in Table 1 are not zeros. Regional figures (New England manageable 31; two regions actual 58) are still medians.

The 2022 Schools Survey median was 48. Do not silently “update” an old sentence.

Clinical providers averaged **39.3** weekly hours on the surveyed list (capped at 55, which captured 89% of respondents): 22.6 direct, 6.0 documentation, 4.0 diagnostics, 2.5 consultation, 1.1 supervision, 0.7 tech checks, 2.4 other. That is not “a 40-hour week of therapy.”

## If asked for a diagnosis, a protocol, or a billing step

Print the education. Refuse the diagnosis, the protocol, and the billing step in one sentence: this series does not evaluate students, does not publish treatment steps, and does not bill.

## Review stamp (before live)

A piece is not cleared for slpwow.com until:

1. Survey and statute wording match the cited document, or are marked `[VERIFY]`.
2. No line from the never-say lists remains.
3. Disclaimer is present.
4. Citations resolve.
5. A human editor has read it aloud once.
6. No PHI, no named student, no clinic vignette that could be a real child.
