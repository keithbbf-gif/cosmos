**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not legal, billing, union, or clinical advice. Not a diagnosis and not a treatment protocol. This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

A school speech-language pathologist is not a clinic that happens to have a bell schedule.

That sentence offends two groups at once. It offends the administrator who wanted a closet therapist to “fix sounds” and send the child back. It offends the clinician who trained in a medical model and still writes goals no teacher can see from the classroom door.

ASHA’s 2010 position statement and professional-issues statement on roles and responsibilities in schools are the documents that tried to end the argument. Critical roles: prevention, assessment, intervention, program design, data, compliance, collaboration. The work is **educationally relevant**. The student is a student. The measure of the work is access to the curriculum and to the social and academic life of the building — not a therapy-room percentage that never leaves the folder.

This series will not reprint the 2010 PDF. It will keep using its spine.

## The educational object

IDEA funds special education and related services so a child with a disability receives a free appropriate public education. Speech-language pathology is named as a related service. It can also be the primary disability category (essay 17). In both cases the IEP has to be reasonably calculated for progress appropriate in light of the child’s circumstances (*Endrew F.*, 2017). “Merely more than de minimis” is not the standard. A closet that never talks to the classroom is how you get de minimis with better toys.

Educational relevance is essay 18. The short version: if the only person who cares about the goal is the SLP, the goal is probably in the wrong dialect.

LRE is not a vibe. Services are to be delivered with peers to the maximum extent appropriate. Pull-out remains legal and sometimes necessary (essay 25). It is not the default because the closet is free at 9:15.

## What the role is not

It is not a diagnosis mill for the neighborhood. Eligibility is a team decision under state and federal rules (essay 20). A blog does not evaluate.

It is not a private clinic’s hour transplanted into a school. Dosing that ignores the bell, the curriculum, and the rest of the IEP is a medical habit wearing a school badge.

It is not “the person who does autism,” “the person who does reading,” or “the person who does behavior,” though language walks through all three rooms (essays 37 and 39). Role creep without workload relief is how 50 students become an impossible week.

It is not the building’s unpaid interpreter, unofficial counselor, or default 504 coordinator — unless those duties are written and staffed. The 2010 document already warned about other-duties-as-assigned. The 2024 activity list still has 2.4 mean hours of “other.”

## What the role is, on a week that works

Prevention and MTSS *without* using them to delay an evaluation a parent already requested (essay 19). Assessment that can tell language difference from language disability (essay 36). Intervention in more than one location. Collaboration that has minutes on a calendar, not a sticky note. Documentation that a later reader can follow (essay 06). A present level a teacher can use (essay 22).

The 2024 survey’s intervention table is a census of *what people already serve*: autism, language form and content, speech sounds, pragmatics, AAC, fluency, literacy for a minority of SLPs. The roles document is the *why*. The caseload integer is the *how many*. Workload is the *how*.

If your building still introduces you as “the speech teacher,” you can hand them the 2010 one-page summary ASHA published for that exact moment. You can refuse a protocol while you explain the job. You can refuse a student story.

The closet can stay for the sessions that belong there. The job does not live in it.
