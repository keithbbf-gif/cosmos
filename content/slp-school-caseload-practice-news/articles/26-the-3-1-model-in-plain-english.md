Three weeks that look like the schedule everyone understands. One week that looks like the rest of the job.

That is the **3:1 model** as school SLPs have used it: typical direct services for three weeks, then a week (or the equivalent hours sprinkled through the month) for observation, teacher collaboration, IEP writing, materials, progress notes, and the training of the adults who actually spend the day with the student.

The ASHA Leader’s 2019 school-based piece called it one of many workload solutions, not the solution. The Practice Portal lists 3:1 next to cyclical / block schedules as examples. The FAQ still refuses to mandate a model. If someone tells you “ASHA requires 3:1,” they have left the documents.

## Why people reach for it

IEP meetings cancel the only therapy block. Documentation sits at six hours (essay 06) and still loses to the next group. Consultation sits at 2.5 hours and feels like theft. Make-up rules chase minutes that meetings already ate (essay 07). Present levels have no classroom source (essay 22).

3:1 tries to stop pretending those hours are hobbies. It puts them on a calendar a principal can see.

It does not add an FTE. It does not shrink Indiana’s 78. It redistributes a week that already existed. If the week was already impossible, 3:1 will be an impossible week with a new name.

## The sentence that has to be on the IEP

Karen Crossman-Esparza’s Puyallup example, quoted in the Leader piece, is the drafting lesson: the IEP names the district’s 3:1 model, says one week a month is indirect, lists what indirect can include (observation, collaboration, planning, materials, progress notes, staff training), and states the direct minutes for three of four weeks.

Without that sentence, a parent can reasonably believe they bought four weeks of pull-out and received three. That is how you get a compensatory-services letter about a model you thought was obvious (essay 41).

Indirect week is still *for the student*. It is not a catch-up week for another building’s evaluations unless the IEP said so — and it should not say so.

## Variations that are still 3:1-shaped

Some districts keep a calendar week. Some scatter the equivalent hours. Some run nine-and-nine cyclical blocks. The portal’s “dynamic service delivery” sentence is the umbrella: location, amount, and frequency can change with need *if the program stays individualized and written*.

A 3:1 that secretly becomes 4:0 because the indirect week filled with make-ups is not a model. It is a collapse.

A 3:1 that becomes 0:4 because the SLP is covering a vacancy (essay 08) is a staffing crisis.

## What it will not fix

Paperwork ranking first in every facility type. A Medicaid double log. Four buildings. A goal bank that cloned 40 IEPs (essay 23). A principal who thinks indirect means “off.”

It can fix the lie that collaboration happens in the margins. Limited time for collaboration ranked sixth in 2024. The model is a way to stop ranking it.

## How to talk about it without selling it

*This is optional. If we use it, the IEP will say so. Direct minutes will be specified for the direct weeks. The flexible week is student-specific work, not a vacation.*

You can take the Leader article and the portal list to a meeting. You can refuse to install 3:1 as a religion. You can refuse a protocol for the direct weeks.

Three and one is furniture. Write it down or do not claim it.
