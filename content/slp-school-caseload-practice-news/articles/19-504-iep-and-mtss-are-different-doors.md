This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

Three doors share a hallway. People keep using one word — *services*, *speech*, *caseload* — for all three.

**MTSS / RTI** is general education with layers of support. It can include the SLP as a consultant, a screener, or a short-term interventionist. ASHA’s implementation guide says MTSS may address some mild communication needs and **must not delay** a student’s entry into special education when an evaluation is needed.

**Section 504** is a civil-rights statute. A student with a disability that substantially limits a major life activity may receive accommodations, supports, or services under a 504 plan without being eligible for special education under IDEA. Speech-related accommodations exist. They are not an IEP.

**IDEA** is the special-education statute. An IEP is the program. Related services live here when the team writes them here (essay 16).

ASHA’s caseload definition can include all three groups. A district data system that only prints IEP names will under-count the week. A headline that says “caseload of 70” without saying which doors were counted is the same error at scale (essay 01).

## How the doors get jammed

**MTSS as a waiting room.** A parent requests an evaluation. The building says “let’s try a tier first.” Federal rules do not let a district use RTI to delay an evaluation once a request has been made. The exact clock is state-specific (essay 20). The principle is not.

**504 as a consolation IEP.** A team that does not find IDEA eligibility sometimes offers 504 as a prize. Sometimes 504 is the correct door. Sometimes it is a way to avoid writing present levels. OCR, not this blog, owns 504 complaints.

**IEP as the only adult in the building who will help.** Teachers send every language worry to the SLP because MTSS was never built. That is a program-design failure. It becomes a caseload number by October.

**Double counting.** The same student sits in MTSS minutes and IEP minutes and a 504 meeting. The integer looks like three people. The week feels like one very expensive person.

## What each door asks the SLP to write

MTSS asks for a plan the classroom can run and a rule for when to refer. It should not ask for a secret IEP.

504 asks for accommodations that are specific enough to use: extra time for oral responses, a quiet place for presentations, access to an AAC device the student already has. It should not ask for specialized instruction the team just declined under IDEA unless someone has actually decided 504 services go that far *in this district*. Ask counsel.

IDEA asks for the full IEP object: present levels, goals, services, progress (essays 22–24, 29).

If you are writing the same paragraph in all three folders, you have a paperwork problem (essay 06) and possibly a compliance problem.

## How to talk about doors in a caseload meeting

Three lines on a page, no names: how many IEP students (primary SLI vs related service), how many 504 students the SLP is assigned, how many MTSS students have SLP minutes this cycle. Then the four workload clusters.

If the district refuses to count 504 and MTSS, the 2024 median of 50 is not your comparison set. You are in a different survey.

If a parent is reading this: “Is this support MTSS, 504, or an IEP?” is a complete question. You are allowed to want the door that matches the need. You are not required to accept a hallway.

The doors are the law’s furniture. Caseload is just how many people are standing in them.
