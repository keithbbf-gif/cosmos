Seventy-four and a half percent of ASHA’s 2024 school SLPs said they regularly serve students who use **augmentative and alternative communication**. Among those who do, the mean number of students was **7.2**. Tech checks sat at 0.7 hours on the national week. Anyone who has rebuilt a broken button overnight knows those two numbers do not live in the same building.

ASHA’s caseload portal already lists programming and maintenance of AAC among the factors that inflate workload while the *count* of students stays flat. This essay will not teach you a language system. It will keep AAC from being treated as extra credit.

IDEA’s assistive-technology and supplementary-aids language is how a device (or a book, or a no-tech board) gets onto a program. LRE includes the right to communicate *in* the classroom, not only in the closet (essay 24). A device that stays in a backpack is not a service. A device whose vocabulary is last year’s field trip is not educationally relevant (essay 18).

Present levels should say what the student can already say, on what system, with whom (essay 22). Goals should say what the classroom will hear this year (essay 23). Progress should include classroom use, not only a closet probe (essay 29).

## Workload that the integer hides

Feature matching and trials. Funding paperwork this blog will not fill out. Programming classroom vocabulary before a unit (essay 28). Training the adults who prompt — or over-prompt. Backups. Lost chargers. A bus that does not allow the device. A substitute who locked it in a drawer. Telepractice facilitators who cannot troubleshoot (essay 10).

SLPA scope (essay 31) may or may not include this work. Assuming it does is how you get a broken system and a signed note.

Families need a contact that is not a portal dump about a smashed screen (essay 35). They do not need a home program disguised as “just practice the buttons.”

## How administrators misuse the 7.2

“Only seven kids — that is small.” Seven systems are not seven lisps. Specialized settings (essay 12) concentrate them. Neighborhood elementaries hide them inside the 51.

“Buy one iPad, problem solved.” Access is vocabulary, implementation, and the right to use it in every room, not a purchase order.

“The vendor will train everyone in August.” August is not October’s science unit.

## News without a product

Headlines about “AI AAC” belong next to essay 40: a model that predicts words is not a team, not an IEP, and not a place to paste a student. ASHA’s AAC portal is the clinical object. This pack stays educational.

## Language for a principal

*Three-quarters of school SLPs in 2024 regularly served AAC. The mean among them was about seven students. Programming and adult training are workload clusters. If we add a system, we add the hour.*

You can refuse a protocol. You can refuse a named student and a named device brand as if it were a prescription.

A charged device in the wrong room is still an access failure. Tech checks at 0.7 hours nationally are a smear. A Monday with a dead battery is a morning. Count the morning before you call seven students a small row. If the device is the student’s voice in science and it stays in the closet for science, the location line on the IEP is fiction. Fix the location or fix the backpack. Do not call either extra credit.

Access is the job. The integer is not the system. Charge both.
