Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

If the only person who knows whether the goal moved is the SLP, the IEP is a rumor.

IDEA wants a description of how progress toward annual goals will be measured and when periodic reports will be provided. ED’s *Endrew F.* Q&A put that description third, after present levels and goals. The Court wanted a program, not a hope. A program that cannot be checked is a hope with a graph.

ASHA’s documentation portal is the professional-issues page. The 2024 survey put documentation at six hours and paperwork at the top of every facility’s challenge list (essay 06). Progress monitoring is a chunk of those hours. It is also the chunk that makes dismissal possible (essay 21) and make-up conversations less mystical (essay 07).

This essay will not give you a data sheet. It will name the failures.

## Secret systems

A notebook in a bag. A spreadsheet on a personal drive. A probe the parent has never seen described. A Medicaid log that is the only “data” and cannot be shown as educational progress.

FERPA: the education record is the district’s record (essay 33). A shadow system is how you lose a hearing and a laptop.

## Cloned systems

Every student on the caseload has the same 80-percent line. AI filled the comment (essay 40). The graph rises because the probe got easier. The classroom teacher has never seen the skill.

If the present level was classroom-shaped (essay 22) and the goal was curriculum-shaped (essay 23), some of the data should be classroom-shaped: a lab explanation, a device use during centers, a presentation. Closet probes can still exist. They cannot be the only dialect.

## Punitive systems

Data used to shame a student in a group (essay 27). Data used to threaten dismissal. Data withheld until the meeting so no one can prepare.

Progress reports are a process right, not a favor.

## Empty systems

“Making progress” with no number, no example, no setting. “Not making progress” with no change to the program. *Endrew F.* is allergic to both. If the data say the program is not working, the team reconvenes. If the data say the need is gone, the team talks dismissal. If the data say nothing, you are paying six hours for fog.

## Workload without theater

You do not need daily points on every goal for every student. You need a plan the IEP already promised, collected often enough to tell the truth, in a system the next SLP can open.

The 3:1 indirect week (essay 26) can be a scoring and reporting week — for the students the IEP named, not as a dump of last month’s guilt.

Large caseloads make people collect less or collect junk. The portal already connected caseload size to the ability to address all goals. Name that in a principal brief (essay 43). Do not “solve” it by inventing data.

## Language for the family

*Here is how we measure this goal, here is when you will see a report, here is an example of the classroom version of the skill.* No other student’s data. No diagnosis.

You can refuse a commercial progress-monitoring religion. You can refuse a protocol.

The six documentation hours already include this work. Spending them on a graph nobody else can read is how paperwork ranks first and still fails the family. If a parent can read the report and picture the classroom skill, you are in the right dialect. If they can only picture a clipboard, rewrite the next one before you collect another closet percentage.

The report is part of FAPE. Treat it like the minutes. It is the minutes, remembered.
