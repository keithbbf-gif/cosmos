A test is a structured sample. A classroom is the job.

IDEA already says eligibility may not rest on a single procedure. ASHA’s school eligibility materials and the multilingual portal keep repeating the cousin sentence: a score that was not built for this student’s languages, dialect, or sensory access is not a fact. It is a number with a hole in it.

This essay will not name a preferred battery. Batteries go stale and this pack would go stale with them. It will keep two dialects of evidence from killing each other.

Compare this student’s performance on *this* task to the people in the norming sample, if the student is in the sample’s world. Give a second adult a number they can recognize. Sometimes meet a state operational definition that still talks in standard deviations (essay 17).

It is allowed to be wrong for a bilingual student, a student who uses AAC, a student who has never sat a timed booklet, a student whose hearing was not checked. It is allowed to miss classroom language that the booklet never asked for: the science explanation, the argument with a peer, the joke that did not land.

## What classroom language evidence is allowed to do

Show adverse educational effect — the school half of SLI (essay 17). Feed a present level a teacher can use (essay 22). Show whether a goal is living where the curriculum lives (essay 23). Catch a language difference that a translated test pretended was a disorder (essay 36).

It is allowed to be messy. It is not allowed to be a vibe. “I think they struggle” is not data. A dated observation with a work sample and a teacher note is data. Dynamic assessment — teach a bit, see what moves — is data. This series will not write the protocol.

## How caseload pressure picks a dialect

Testing every three years and ignoring the classroom is fast in September and expensive in a hearing. Collecting only classroom anecdotes and skipping a valid test a state requires is fast in the hallway and expensive in a complaint.

The 2024 diagnostics hour (4.0) is the national smear of this choice. Large caseloads shrink observation time — the portal already said so. That is how you get a file of scores and a teacher who says “that is not the kid I have.”

## Progress is not a re-norming

Repeating the same standardized test every nine weeks to “show progress” is often the wrong dialect. Tests were not all built for that. Classroom-shaped probes and work samples were (essay 29). Save the battery for the questions it can answer: eligibility, a suspected change in the disability, a comparison that the team actually needs.

## Language for the table

*The score tells us how this student did on this test compared with the sample. The classroom sample tells us what happens in the curriculum. We will not treat either as a diagnosis. We will not treat a test that does not fit as a fact.*

You can refuse a single-score religion. You can refuse a “tests are useless” religion. You can refuse to publish a student’s scores.

The 4.0 diagnostic hours in the 2024 list are not a mandate to re-norm every quarter. They are a reminder that assessment is a clump, not a hobby. Bring both dialects to the table or admit which one you skipped. A skipped classroom sample is how a score becomes a rumor. A skipped valid test, when the state still requires one, is how a rumor becomes a complaint.

Two dialects. One student. One team. No blog verdict.
