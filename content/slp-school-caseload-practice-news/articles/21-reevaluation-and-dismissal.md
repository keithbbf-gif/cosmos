Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

Ten percent of ASHA’s 2024 clinical providers named **difficulty with dismissal of students from services** as their single greatest barrier to a manageable caseload. That is not a small complaint. It is also not a permission slip.

Dismissal from special education, dismissal from a related service while another category remains, and a change in minutes are different legal objects. All of them run through the IEP team (or the equivalent 504 process). None of them run through a blog, a hallway mood, or a caseload target of 40.

## Reevaluation is the adult in the room

IDEA expects reevaluation at least every three years unless the parent and district agree it is unnecessary, and sooner if the child’s needs change or if a teacher or parent requests it. The point is to ask whether the student still has a disability, still needs special education and related services, and whether the program should change.

A team that never reevaluates will grow a museum. A team that reevaluates only to “cut caseload” will grow a hearing. ASHA’s school eligibility and dismissal pages ask the educational-need question again: is there still an adverse effect the program must address? Not: is the SLP tired? Tired is essay 09. Need is this page.

## Why dismissal feels impossible

**Goals that cannot be met.** If you wrote “will produce /r/ in all conversational settings at 100 percent,” you wrote a trap (essay 23). Mastery that never arrives is not a clinical mystery. It is a drafting problem.

**Fear of the table.** Parent resistance was the top barrier for 4 percent of respondents. Fear of parent resistance is larger than 4 percent and does not have its own checkbox. Prior written notice exists so a team can propose a change and the parent can disagree through the lawful process. Avoiding the notice is how students stay forever.

**The only adult who helps.** If dismissal means the student returns to a classroom with no language support, the team is not dismissing from a service. It is dismissing from the only differentiated instruction in the building. That is an MTSS failure (essay 19), not an SLP personality failure.

**Data no one trusts.** Progress monitoring that lives in a private notebook (essay 29) cannot carry a dismissal conversation. Neither can a single standardized score from three years ago.

**Category confusion.** Ending SLI as a primary category is not the same as ending speech-language as a related service under another category. Ending minutes is not the same as exiting special education. Use the noun that matches the form.

## What dismissal is not

It is not “they are fine in my room.” The educational setting is the classroom, the cafeteria, the lab, the job site.

It is not a reward for good behavior.

It is not a punishment for missed sessions.

It is not a way to make Indiana’s 78 look like New York’s 32 (essay 13).

It is not a diagnosis that the student “never had it.” Eligibility was a team finding at the time. Dismissal is a new finding.

## Language that stays lawful

Propose. Show the present levels and the progress data. State the recommendation. Offer the notice. Offer the meeting. Offer the next door if there is one — classroom accommodations, a 504 conversation, a check-in date.

Do not promise the parent will agree. Do not promise the caseload will drop by Tuesday. Do not name a child in a public essay.

The 10 percent is a description of a bottleneck. The statute is the way through. The integer is not the reason.
