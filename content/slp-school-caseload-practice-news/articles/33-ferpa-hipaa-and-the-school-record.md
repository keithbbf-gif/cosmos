**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not legal, billing, union, or clinical advice. Not a diagnosis and not a treatment protocol. This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

This series promised not to use student records. That promise is the point of the federal privacy statutes, not a brand flourish.

**FERPA** (20 U.S.C. §1232g; 34 C.F.R. Part 99) governs education records held by schools that receive certain federal funds. Speech-language IEPs, progress reports, evaluation reports, and the emails that became the record usually live here. Parents have access rights. “School officials” with a legitimate educational interest may access. Vendors can sit inside the school-official exception when the district retains direct control — PTAC, not a blog, writes that test.

**HIPAA**’s Privacy Rule governs protected health information held by covered entities and their business associates. A school is not automatically a covered entity. A school-based health clinic that bills electronically like a clinic may be. A contractor SLP who is a covered entity in their private practice may be wearing two hats. Flattening “HIPAA” onto every school note is how people sound careful while being wrong.

IDEA’s confidentiality rules lean on FERPA. Medicaid billing (essay 32) is one of the places the hats multiply: an education record shared for claiming needs the consent path the statute and regulation already wrote.

This essay will not decide your hat. Counsel and the district privacy officer will. It will keep the news and the vendor deck from using the wrong acronym as a mood.

## What school SLPs actually leak

Hallway talk with a student name and a goal. A group note that lists another child’s data (essay 27). A photo of a data sheet on a personal phone. An AI prompt that pasted a present level (essay 40). A progress report emailed to the wrong parent.

None of those required a hacker. All of them are how a “no PHI” series stays strict.

## What a parent can ask for

Under FERPA, the education record. Under IDEA, the evaluation and IEP documents the process already promised. A parent does not need this URL to request them. A district does not need this URL to delay them.

## What a vendor will say

“HIPAA-compliant speech software for schools.” Ask whether they mean a BAA, a FERPA school-official contract, a student-data privacy agreement, or a sticker. Ask where the recordings live. Ask who is the record holder when a parent requests the file.

CMS’s 2023 guide and ED’s PTAC pages are the grown-up documents. A homepage badge is not.

## Caseload, briefly

Documentation hours (6.0) include the work of putting the record in the *right* system so it is requestable and not in a bag. Shadow systems are a privacy incident waiting for a lost laptop (essay 29). Large caseloads make shadow systems tempting. Tempting is not lawful.

## Language that stays accurate

*If it is an education record, start with FERPA and IDEA. If a HIPAA covered entity is in the room, add that analysis. Do not paste student text into a public model. This series will not.*

You can print PTAC’s parent page. You can refuse a vendor’s sticker. You can refuse a named student.

IDEA confidentiality leans on FERPA; Medicaid claiming is one place the hats multiply. Ask which statute you are under before you ask which platform. If you need a hallway test: would you forward this sentence to the parent of the student it describes, with the other students stripped out? If not, it does not belong in a group note, a text, or a model prompt.

The record is the student’s. The acronym is not a vibe. The blog is not a file cabinet.
