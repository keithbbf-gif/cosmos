**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not legal, billing, union, or clinical advice. Not a diagnosis and not a treatment protocol. This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

Ninety-three point eight percent of ASHA’s 2024 school SLPs said they regularly serve students in the **autism spectrum disorder** intervention area. The mean number of students, among those who do, was **14.2**. Pragmatics / social communication sat at 86.7 percent and a mean of 14.7. Those rows overlap. They are not identical.

This essay will not diagnose autism. It will not publish a treatment protocol. It will not use a student story. It will treat the 93.8 percent as a **service pattern**: most school SLPs already spend part of the week on educational programs for students whose teams have used that category or that clinical description.

## Educational object, again

The school’s job is the IEP or 504, the curriculum, the peers, the device, the language of the lab (essays 15–18). A medical or clinical identification, if it exists, is data. It is not the program. *Endrew F.* is about the program.

Social-communication goals that only live in a social-skills closet have the same problem as /r/ goals that only live in a portable: the building never sees them (essay 23). Classroom-based and consult models (essays 25, 28, 34) are often the honest ones. Pull-out can still be right for a slice.

AAC is in this week more often than the autism checkbox admits (essay 38). So is literacy for some students and some SLPs (essay 37). So is the English-learner filter (essay 36). None of those is “the autism protocol.”

## How caseload talk goes wrong

“They have autism, so they count as two.” Weighting can be a local statute (essay 05). It is not a blog rule and not a diagnosis.

“Put all the autism kids in one group.” That is stacking (essay 27) wearing a category.

“The SLP owns autism.” Role creep without FTE (essay 15). Teachers, psychologists, OTs, and families remain in the room.

“93.8 percent means an epidemic.” It means school SLPs were asked which areas they serve and almost all said they serve students in this area. Prevalence news is a different desk. Do not smuggle it in.

## News literacy

Headline about a new autism prevalence number: open the CDC page, not a speech caseload table. Headline about a new therapy brand: this pack will not endorse it. Headline about ABA versus developmental models: a school IEP is still an educational program, not a brand war.

ASHA’s autism practice portal is for clinicians. It is not a parent diagnosis tool. The pediatric milestones pack on this brand is surveillance education. Keep the packs in their lanes.

## Language that stays on the right side

*Most school SLPs in 2024 reported serving students in the autism intervention area, with a mean of about fourteen among those who do. This series will not name a child and will not treat a category as a protocol. The IEP is the educational object.*

You can print the 2024 table. You can refuse a treatment step. You can refuse “your child has.”

Pragmatics at 86.7 percent is the cousin row, not a synonym and not a second diagnosis. Staff the communication need the IEP named. If a headline wants a cause or a cure, this desk will not write it. If a meeting wants a protocol named after a brand, this desk will not write that either. The 93.8 percent is permission to staff the week you already have, not permission to label a child from a URL.

The percentage is a workload fact. The student is a student. The blog is not a clinic.
