ASHA has fielded a Schools Survey in even-numbered years since 2004. The 2024 wave went out on January 25 to a random sample of 15,000 ASHA-certified SLPs employed in U.S. school settings, plus all 809 ASHA-certified educational audiologists in those settings. Half of each group got paper. Half got a screen. Follow-ups went out February 22 and March 28. A “be on the lookout” note went out February 15.

The caseload PDF you can still open was revised March 10, 2025. The suggested citation on the last pages is ASHA (2024), *2024 Schools Survey report: SLP caseload and workload characteristics*.

That is the object. It is not a census of every person who ever billed a school speech session. It is not an audit of your district. It is a **weighted member survey** of certified people ASHA could reach.

## Who answered

The SLP sample was stratified by state. Small states were oversampled. Weighting put the results back in line with ASHA’s membership database so Wyoming does not vanish and California does not swallow the median.

After deaths, retirements, bad addresses, and opt-outs, 14,610 SLPs were eligible. 3,749 responded. Combined response rate: **25.7 percent** (postal 29.9 percent; electronic 21.4 percent). The caseload report’s big n for full-time clinical providers with an actual caseload is **2,815**. Manageable-caseload n is **2,776**. Weekly-activity n is **2,347**. State tables only print a cell when at least **25** people in that state answered that item. Blank is not zero.

Ninety percent of employed respondents were clinical service providers. The rest were diagnosticians, administrators/supervisors/directors, special education teachers, consultants, or “other,” each in the low single digits. When this series says “the 2024 median,” it means the clinical-provider table unless it says otherwise.

## What they were asked that headlines skip

They were asked for an **actual monthly caseload** and for a **manageable** caseload for that same load. Those are different questions. Essay 04.

They were asked by **facility**: special day/residential, preschool, elementary, secondary, administrative office, office for telepractice, combination. A telepractice *office* is a work setting in the survey, not a verdict on telepractice as a model. Essay 10.

They were asked by **state** and by **census region**. Indiana’s median actual caseload (78) and New York’s (32) are the edges of the reportable table, not a morality play. Essay 13.

They were asked for a **single greatest barrier** to a manageable caseload. Twenty-four percent said there was no barrier. Twenty-six percent said a shortage of SLPs. The rest split across administration, dismissal, policy, parents, assistants, and “other.” Essay 14.

They were asked which **areas of intervention** they regularly serve, and the mean number of students in each area among those who do. Autism and language (semantics, morphology, syntax) sit at or above 90 percent. Literacy sits at 28 percent of SLPs — and a mean of 14.8 students among those who take it. Those two facts can live in the same building. Essays 37 and 39.

They were asked for **weekly hours** on a fixed list, capped at 55 hours, which captured 89 percent of respondents. Mean total 39.3. Direct intervention 22.6. Documentation 6.0. Diagnostics 4.0. Consultation 2.5. Supervision 1.1. Tech checks 0.7. Other duties 2.4. Essay 06.

They were asked whether they must **make up missed sessions**. Forty-two percent: only in a few circumstances. Thirty-seven percent: always or almost always. Twenty percent: never or almost never. Essay 07.

The companion workforce report asked about full-time versus part-time (87 percent full time), salaried versus contractor (86 / 12 / 1), a ranked list of 13 challenges, burnout (34 percent considering a setting change; 27 percent considering leaving the profession), retention ideas, SLPA supervision (12 percent), union availability (63 percent), and the job market (79 percent more openings than seekers). Essays 08, 09, 31, 42.

## What a 25.7 percent survey is not

It is not “ASHA measured every school.” Contractors who are not ASHA-certified, SLPAs, and people who left the Association do not sit in the frame the same way.

It is not a finding that your building is illegal. Compliance is an IEP and a state complaint process, not a median.

It is not a time-and-motion study. The activity list is seven buckets. “Other duties as assigned” is a confession, not a map of bus duty.

It is not the 2022 survey. The 2022 full-time clinical median was **48**. The 2024 median is **50**. Do not silently update an old slide. The portal page still quotes 2022 in places; check the date on the PDF in your hand.

It is not a student. No row in these tables is a child. This series will not invent one to make a chart warmer.

## How to quote it without lying

Name the report, the year, and the n. “Median actual monthly caseload, full-time clinical providers, 2024 Schools Survey, n = 2,815, was 50.” That sentence is long on purpose.

If you only have a regional number, say so. New England’s manageable median (31) is not Connecticut’s (30) and is not a national cap.

If a cell is blank, say the state did not meet n = 25. Do not average neighboring states to fill it.

If you are talking burnout or unions, you have switched PDFs. Cite the workforce report.

You can print the methods page and the executive summary and take both to a meeting. You can refuse to treat a member survey as a court order. You can still use it, because it is the best public picture we have of how school SLPs described their weeks in the winter of 2024.

That is enough for a news desk. It is not enough for an IEP.
