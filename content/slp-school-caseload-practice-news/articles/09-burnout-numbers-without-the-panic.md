This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

Thirty-four percent of the school SLPs in ASHA’s 2024 workforce report said they were considering changing their work setting because of burnout. Twenty-seven percent said they were considering leaving the profession for the same stated reason.

Those are **considering** items, tied to a word the survey used. They are not a count of resignations. They are not a clinical diagnosis of the clinician. They are not a reason to put a student photograph next to a headline that says “the field is collapsing.”

Read them as weather, next to the challenge ranking (paperwork first, caseload/workload second) and the job-market item (79 percent saw more openings than seekers). A profession can be short-staffed and exhausted in the same year. That is not a paradox. It is how vacancies and remaining loads feed each other (essay 08).

They can bear a conversation about **retention**. The same report asked for suggestions. Fifty percent included placing SLPs on a separate salary schedule from teachers as one of their top two ideas. That is a survey answer, not this magazine’s endorsement, and not a union instruction. Essay 42.

They can bear a conversation about **workload**, because ASHA’s portal already cites occupational stress and burnout literature next to large caseloads (Marante and colleagues, 2023 — `[VERIFY]` the full cite before a live page). The 2024 hour list — 22.6 direct, 6.0 documentation, 4.0 diagnostics — is a week that does not include the drive between buildings.

They can bear a conversation about **role**. “Limited understanding of my role by others” ranked seventh overall and jumped around by facility. Secondary SLPs ranked it higher than preschool SLPs. A person who is tired of explaining that they are not “the speech teacher who does baby talk” is answering a different item than a person who is tired of a 78-student month.

They cannot bear a wellness poster as the intervention. They cannot bear a diagnosis of the reader.

## What they must not be used for

Do not use 27 percent to tell a parent the SLP will be gone in June. Most people who consider leaving do not leave on the survey’s timetable. Some do. The IEP still needs a provider of record.

Do not use 34 percent to smear a setting. “Considering a setting change” includes hospital-to-school, school-to-clinic, elementary-to-secondary, and “I want one building instead of four.” The item is not a map.

Do not use either number as a reason to skip documentation or skip a meeting. Burnout is not a waiver of IDEA.

Do not invent a named clinician who “almost quit last Tuesday.” This series does not do composite martyr stories. The table is enough.

## A quieter pair from the same winter

Twenty-four percent of clinical providers said they had **no barrier** to a manageable caseload. Twelve percent supervised an SLPA. Sixty-three percent had union representation available. Eighty-seven percent worked full time.

A responsible desk prints the burnout items *and* the “no barrier” item. The job is not a single mood. Buildings differ. Years of experience differ — the workforce report says facility type, region, density, and years in the schools and in the profession all moved the challenge rankings. If you are going to quote 27 percent, you can also say the survey allowed those other facts.

## How to write the sentence

*In ASHA’s 2024 Schools Survey workforce report, 34 percent of school SLPs said they were considering a setting change because of burnout, and 27 percent said they were considering leaving the profession. Paperwork ranked as the top challenge in every facility type. This is a member survey, not a resignation count.*

That paragraph can sit in a staff newsletter. It cannot sit on a billboard over a child’s face.

If you are the person who checked those boxes: you are allowed to be tired and still be precise. Precision is the opposite of panic. It is also how you brief a principal (essay 43) without turning your week into a confession.

The numbers are high enough to respect. They are not a verdict on you.
