Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

If you only count sessions, you will not understand September.

This last piece is a map of the series and a map of a year. It is not your district calendar. It is a way to see why ASHA’s 2024 clinical providers reported **39.3** hours on a seven-item list that still felt like more: 22.6 direct, 6.0 documentation, 4.0 diagnostics, 2.5 consultation, 1.1 supervision, 0.7 tech, 2.4 other. The year is how those piles move.

## Late summer

IEP service pages that do not match the master schedule (essay 24). Device backups (essay 38). A roles one-pager for teachers who think you do baby talk (essays 15, 34). A privacy reminder that student text does not go into a public model (essays 33, 40). The three doors — MTSS, 504, IEP — written on a slide without a child (essay 19).

## September–October

The evaluation clock (essay 20). Kindergarten and new-to-district files. Groups that are already piles (essay 27). The first progress probe that is not a secret (essay 29). A principal brief before the extra building lands (essay 43). Headlines about caseload: which of the three objects (essay 01).

## November–December

Meetings eat the only therapy block. Make-up rules show their teeth (essay 07). A 3:1 indirect week, if you use one, has to be on the IEP or it is a rumor (essay 26). Paperwork feels like the whole job because ASHA already ranked it first (essay 06). Families need something that is not a portal dump before break (essay 35).

## January–February

Reevaluations and the dismissal conversations people deferred (essay 21). Midyear data that should change a program under *Endrew F.*, not a hope (essays 16, 23). The Schools Survey, in even years, is in the field — 2024’s wave opened January 25. If you quote last year’s median, check the date (essay 03).

## March–April

State testing fights LRE (essay 24). Secondary credit classes fight pull-out (essay 11). Transition language for older students. Literacy arguments in curriculum meetings (essay 37). English-learner evaluations that need an interpreter, not an English-only booklet (essay 36).

## May–June

Compensatory conversations that were really vacancies (essays 08, 41). Extended-school-year decisions this blog will not make. A workload page for next year’s FTE, with the 50 / 40 pair labeled as a survey, not a cap (essays 02, 04, 05). Retention reality — burnout items, salary-schedule item, union-available item — as data, not a speech (essays 09, 42).

## What this series was

Forty-four staged explainers for SLPWOW. School practice, caseload, and news. Educational. No PHI. No protocols. No billing codes. Companion to the short news cards in `content/slpwow-slp-news/` and not a merger with pediatric milestones or profession history.

If a reader wanted a verdict, they came to the wrong desk. If they wanted a way to read a table, a statute, and a week without putting a child on the page, the folder is the desk.

Print the 2024 executive summaries. Print the portal’s caseload-versus-workload paragraph. Print your four clusters. Leave the student out of the public sentence.

If you only remember one sentence from forty-four drafts, remember this: caseload counts names, workload counts the week, and a headline that uses one word for a statute, a vacancy, and a missed minute has already failed the read.

The year is the job. The integer is a guest. The blog is staged, and it should stay staged until a human who holds a license says a piece may go live.
