**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not legal, billing, union, or clinical advice. Not a diagnosis and not a treatment protocol. This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

In May 2023, CMS — with the U.S. Department of Education — issued *Delivering Services in School-Based Settings*, a guide required by the Bipartisan Safer Communities Act. It updates and supersedes older school-based Medicaid manuals (1997, 2003). The accompanying bulletin told states to expand school-based services and ease administrative burden.

That is a **payment and access** story. It is not a coding class. This essay will not give you a CPT code, a unit, or a “bill it this way.” If someone asked this URL for a billing step, the answer is no.

## What the guide is doing in the news

Medicaid and CHIP cover more than half of U.S. children, the agencies said. Schools are a place those children already are. The guide talks about paying schools for covered services, simplifying interim billing, random-moment time studies, provider enrollment, and *not* paying school-based health contractors on a contingency-fee basis.

It also points at **parental consent** under FERPA and IDEA when records are shared and when public benefits are accessed for IEP services. 34 C.F.R. §300.154 is the IDEA regulation to open; `[VERIFY]` the current text before a live page. Consent to evaluate is not consent to bill. Do not flatten the signatures.

States that use the new flexibilities are on their own clocks. A vendor who says “CMS now requires every SLP to…” is probably selling something. Open the state Medicaid agency, not the vendor.

## What it must not do to the IEP

It must not create a second set of goals that exist only to bill (essay 18). Educational relevance stays the filter. FAPE stays the reason the minute exists.

It must not turn documentation’s first-place ranking (essay 06) into a third log. If school Medicaid adds a form, someone should retire a form. The 2023 guide’s “reduce administrative burden” sentence is the one to read aloud in that meeting.

It must not put a student’s eligibility for Medicaid in a hallway conversation. That is PHI-adjacent and none of a blog’s business.

## How it shows up in a caseload fight

“We need more students on the caseload to capture more Medicaid.” That sentence has left IDEA. It is a compliance problem.

“We cannot dismiss because we would lose billing.” Same problem (essay 21).

“The contractor’s invoice is the program.” Essay 08, plus CMS’s contingency-fee warning.

“Paperwork is first because of Medicaid.” Sometimes. Sometimes paperwork is first because of three platforms and a meeting note. Sort them.

## Language that stays on this side of the line

*CMS and ED published a 2023 guide to help states pay for covered school-based services and to reduce claiming burden. IDEA parental-consent rules still apply when a district accesses public benefits. This series does not bill. Your district’s Medicaid coordinator and counsel own the next document.*

You can print the fact sheet. You can refuse a code. You can refuse a student story.

FAPE is why the minute exists; claiming is why a second form appears. Paperwork already ranks first without a third log. If school Medicaid is the reason for a new form, name the form you will retire in the same sentence. If a vendor offers to “run Medicaid for you” on a percentage of collections, read CMS’s contingency-fee warning again and take it to counsel — not to this URL. If a staff meeting wants a code, the meeting has the wrong guest.

The guide is a federal object. The IEP is still the school object. Keep the invoices from rewriting the second.
