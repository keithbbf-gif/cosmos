**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not legal, billing, union, or clinical advice. Not a diagnosis and not a treatment protocol. This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

A clock is not a yes.

IDEA requires that, once a district has parental consent for an initial evaluation, it complete the evaluation within a federal timeline — and many states wrote a shorter one. This pack will not print the number of days. Open 34 C.F.R. §300.301 and your state regulation. If they disagree, the tighter lawful rule is the one your counsel will care about.

The clock is a **process** right. Eligibility is a **team** finding after the evaluation. Parents who hear “we have 60 days” as “in 60 days they will qualify” have been sold a story. Clinicians who treat the clock as optional because the caseload is 50 have been sold a different story.

## What sits on the SLP’s portion of the clock

Observation, classroom language sampling, standardized tests when they are valid for this student, dynamic assessment, hearing and vision status as already documented or still needed, teacher input, family input, an interpreter if the assessment is not in the language that will tell the truth (essay 36). The 2024 activity list put diagnostics at a mean of **4.0 hours** a week. That is a national smear of a job that arrives in clumps: September, January, the month before kindergarten round-up.

A caseload integer that ignores the evaluation queue will look fine on October 1 and explode on October 20. Workload analysis (essay 02) is how you put the queue on the page before you accept another building.

## What must not eat the clock

**MTSS as delay.** Once a parent requests an evaluation, a tier is not a pause button (essay 19).

**“We do not have an SLP.”** A vacancy (essay 08) is the district’s problem to solve. It does not amend the regulation. Contractors, telepractice, or a delayed IEP with compensatory conversations (essay 41) are the ugly options. Silence is not an option.

**An incomplete file because the platform ate the protocol.** Documentation hours already rank first as a challenge (essay 06). They do not excuse a late evaluation.

**A blog milestone chart.** The pediatric pack on this brand is surveillance education for families. It is not a school evaluation instrument.

## What the evaluation is allowed to conclude

Eligible under SLI. Eligible under another category with speech-language as a related service. Not eligible, with a description of classroom supports and, if appropriate, a 504 referral. Not eligible, and the concern was a language *difference* (essay 36). More information needed — hearing, a medical question the school does not own — with a written plan for what happens next.

Notice what is missing: a diagnosis from this URL. A promise. A protocol.

## Reevaluation is a different clock

Periodic reevaluation (at least every three years unless the team agrees otherwise, and sooner if needed) is its own object (essay 21). Do not steal initial-evaluation panic to skip a reevaluation, or steal reevaluation calm to ignore an initial request.

## Language for both sides of the table

Parent: “When did you receive consent, what is this state’s timeline, and what is the meeting date?” Complete questions. You may bring an advocate. This series is not your advocate.

SLP: “Here is the queue, here are the hours, here is what will slip if we add a building.” No student names on that page.

Administrator: the clock is not a suggestion because ASHA’s median is 50.

Diagnostics at 4.0 mean hours a week is a smear of a job that arrives in clumps. A building that staffs only to the session list will miss the clump. A family that hears only the day count will miss the finding.

The timeline is a floor. Eligibility is a finding. This page is neither.
