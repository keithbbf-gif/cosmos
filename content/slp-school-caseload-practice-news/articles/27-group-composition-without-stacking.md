This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

A group is a choice. A pile is a storage unit.

ASHA’s caseload portal cites work (Cirrin and colleagues; Brandel and Loeb) showing that large caseloads push SLPs from individual to group and from smaller groups to larger ones — *when those changes are not necessarily ideal for the student*. Schooling (2003) is the cautious citation on smaller caseloads and functional progress. None of that is a ban on groups. It is a warning about **stacking**: putting students in the same 30 minutes because they are free at 9:15, not because they share a goal, a language, a need for the same model, or a reason to learn from each other.

This essay will not give you a maximum group size. It will give you a way to tell a group from a pile.

## What a group can be for

Shared language of a classroom unit. Peer models who actually model. A social-communication goal that requires other people. Efficiency that does not erase the service page.

The 2024 intervention means hint at why stacking is tempting. Language (form/content) averaged 23 students among SLPs who serve it. Speech sounds averaged 20. Those piles want groups. Fluency averaged 2.6. Childhood apraxia of speech averaged 3.0. AAC averaged 7.2. If those last three are inside the first two’s Thursday slot, you do not have a group. You have a waiting room.

## Five stacking tells

**Goals that cannot be run in the same activity.** A device program, a new vowel, and a science-retell goal are three lessons.

**Ages or grades that do not share a curriculum.** LRE is not “they are all children.”

**A student whose IEP says individual.** Then the group is a rewrite you did not hold a meeting for.

**A make-up sitting in a group that already had four.** Essay 07. You have just spent someone else’s minutes.

**No one can say, in a sentence, why these people are together.** If the sentence is “the slot,” you have a pile.

## Confidentiality is a group issue

Students hear each other. FERPA does not vanish because the door says Speech (essay 33). Groups need a rule about what is talked about, and adults need a rule about what is written in a shared note. This series will not script the rule. It will say: do not put another student’s data on a page that goes home with a different family.

## Workload, again

Planning one good group activity is work. Planning four unrelated activities in one half hour is more work and worse service. The leftover calendar (essay 24) creates piles. A 3:1 indirect week (essay 26) can be used to regroup — *if* the IEP allows the model to be dynamic.

Secondary groups fight the bell. Elementary groups fight the assumption that all speech-sound students are interchangeable. Specialized settings (essay 12) may run very small groups that still consume a full FTE.

## Language for a principal

*A group is part of FAPE when it matches the goals. A pile is how a caseload of 51 hides a week that cannot deliver the pages we signed.* Print the portal sentence about caseload driving group size. Do not print a student roster in the public version of that talk.

You can refuse a protocol for group therapy. You can refuse a named example.

If you need a number for a hallway, use a question instead: can each IEP on this list be run in the same activity without lying? If the answer is no, split the pile before you add a make-up.

The slot is not a person. The person is not a slot. Keep them apart and the group can earn its keep.
