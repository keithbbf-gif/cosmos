**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not legal, billing, union, or clinical advice. Not a diagnosis and not a treatment protocol. This series does not use student records or any protected health information (PHI). Local IDEA, FERPA, Medicaid, and district rules govern practice. Talk with your district, a licensed speech-language pathologist, or counsel before changing services.

Paperwork ranked first in every facility type in 2024. Vendors noticed.

The news about “AI for IEPs” is usually one of four stories wearing one headline: a **scribe** that turns talk into a note; a **generator** that drafts present levels and goals; a **translator** that makes a report readable; or a **scheduler** that promises to solve caseload. Only the fourth is even about caseload, and it cannot analyze workload (essay 02) unless a human already listed the week.

This essay will not bless a product. It will not ban a tool your district has already contracted. It will keep student text out of a public model and keep *Endrew F.* from being automated.

## What a tool cannot be

**The provider of record.** IDEA still wants a team and a program reasonably calculated for this student. A generator that has never seen the classroom cannot write a present level a teacher can use (essay 22). If two IEPs come out as twins, both failed (essay 23).

**The record holder.** Pasting a present level into a consumer chatbot is how you create a copy of an education record you do not control (essay 33). PTAC’s school-official test is for vendors the *district* contracted, with direct control and a destruction plan — not for a free tab on a phone.

**The consent.** Families did not sign up for a model trained on their child’s language sample. If your district has an AI procedure, open it. If it does not, do not invent one in a closet.

**A diagnosis.** A model that says “this sounds like autism” has left this brand’s never-say list. Delete it.

## What a tool might be, if the district says so

A way to turn a *human* observation into cleaner prose you then edit. A way to summarize a meeting you attended. A way to check that you used the same student name throughout — clerical, not clinical.

ASHA’s documentation portal and any current ASHA AI statement should be opened before a live page quotes the Association; mark `[VERIFY]` if the public page has moved. The short news pack already ran a card on AI scribes. This is the longer desk: same warning, more IEP.

## How the headline should be read

“District adopts AI to cut IEP time.” Ask: which of the four stories? Where does the record live? Who edits? What is forbidden to paste? Did anyone retire a form, or is this a third log (essay 06)?

“AI will fix the SLP shortage.” It will not (essay 14). It may type faster. It will not sit at the table.

“Teachers fear AI-written IEPs.” They should, if the paragraph does not sound like their room.

## Language for a staff memo

*Do not paste student records into a public model. A draft is not an IEP until a human who knows the student and the curriculum signs the educational judgment. This series will not.*

You can print PTAC. You can refuse a vendor deck as policy. You can refuse a named student.

A scheduler that ignores the four workload clusters is a calendar toy. Paperwork ranked first because the pile is real. A generator that makes the pile faster and faker is not relief. If the district has not named an approved tool, the closet tab is not a gray area. It is a record leaving the house. If the district has named one, read the contract for training use, retention, and parent access before you paste a sentence.

The tool types. The team decides. The record stays in the house.
