The number that travels is 50. The number that sits next to it on the same page, and usually does not travel, is 40.

ASHA asked full-time clinical service providers for their actual monthly caseload. The median was 50. The range was 4 to 351. n = 2,815. Then ASHA asked those same people what would be *manageable* for the caseload they actually had. The median answer was 40. Range 0 to 200. n = 2,776.

A ten-student gap at the middle of the distribution is the whole plot of this piece. It is not a national cap. It is not a finding that every SLP is ten over. It is two questions, answered in the same winter, by people who still had to go back to the building on Monday.

## What a median is allowed to hide

Half the respondents sat at or under 50. Half sat at or over. The range up to 351 is doing real work: someone, somewhere, reported a monthly caseload that looks like a typing error until you remember itinerant coverage, summer evaluations counted in a monthly figure, or a data system that never dismisses. ASHA printed the range. Use it.

Manageable has its own range, down to 0. That is a person saying *nothing about this load is manageable*, or a person whose caseload is not the right object. The survey does not interview them for us.

Facility medians refuse the national story. Special day and residential schools sat at 25 actual and 25 manageable — the survey’s lowest pair. Telepractice offices sat at 52 actual, highest of the facility list, with a manageable 45. Elementary: 51 and 45. Secondary: 50 and 45. Combination of facilities: 50 and 45. Preschool sits between the special-school floor and the elementary ridge; quote Figure 1 from the PDF if you need both preschool numbers, and mark `[VERIFY]` if your copy of the figure is muddy.

City, suburb, and rural did **not** split the national pair. Full-time clinical providers reported median actual 50 and manageable 40 in all three. If your feed says “rural caseloads are the crisis,” ask for a table that is not this one.

## What “manageable” is not

It is not ASHA’s recommended caseload. ASHA still refuses to recommend a number (essay 02).

It is not “the size at which Endrew F. is satisfied.” The Court did not print 40.

It is not a promise that 40 students with complex AAC look like 40 students in two articulation groups. Manageable was asked *for their caseload* — the mix they already had — not for a standardized student.

It is not a dismissal criterion. Ten percent of respondents named difficulty dismissing students as their single greatest barrier to a manageable load (essay 21). That is a different essay and a different legal object.

## The gap by state is not a vibe

Among the 32 states with at least 25 answers in the actual column, the high edge was Indiana at 78 actual (manageable 50 — a 28-student gap). The low edge was New York at 32 actual (manageable 30 — a 2-student gap). Arkansas moved one student, 40 to 39. Louisiana moved 17, 60 to 43. Connecticut’s manageable median was 30, tied with New York for the smallest “this would be enough” number. Seven states put manageable at 50, the largest of those medians.

You can love or hate your state’s education politics. You cannot make Indiana’s 78 and New York’s 32 into the same week. Essay 13 stays with that pair. The point here is narrower: **actual** and **manageable** move together only sometimes. Where they are close, the survey is not saying the job is easy. It is saying the people who answered did not describe a large gap. Where they are far, the survey is not saying the statute failed. It is saying the people who answered want a different week than the one they have.

## How a ten-student story gets misused

A union one-pager that prints only 50 is incomplete. A district one-pager that prints only “ASHA says 40” is false. A vendor that sells a scheduling app as the thing that “closes the 50–40 gap” has left the PDF.

The honest sentence is longer: *In 2024, among full-time ASHA-certified clinical providers who answered, the middle actual caseload was 50 and the middle ‘manageable’ answer was 40. ASHA does not set a cap. Student mix is not in the integer. Workload is the rest of the week.*

You can take that sentence into a meeting. You can put the n next to it. You can refuse to diagnose a child or a district from a median.

The integer is a description. The week is the job.
