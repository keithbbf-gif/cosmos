If a general educator cannot see the student from the present-level paragraph, the paragraph failed.

IDEA asks for a statement of present levels of academic achievement and functional performance. ED’s *Endrew F.* Q&A put that statement first in the list of things teams must take seriously. The Court wanted a program reasonably calculated for appropriate progress. You cannot calculate from a paragraph that only says “standard score 78 on Test X.”

This is not a plea for prettier writing. It is a plea for a document that does educational work (essay 18) in a building where the SLP is not the only adult (essay 34).

**The school scene.** What happens in the classroom, in small group, in the hallway, on a presentation day. Not a novel. Three concrete observations a teacher recognizes. No name in this essay; on the real IEP, the name is the student’s and the record is FERPA-protected (essay 33).

**The academic hook.** Which parts of the grade-level curriculum are blocked by communication: word problems, lab directions, the language of compare-and-contrast, the demand to explain a claim. Literacy (essay 37) lives here when it is true. It does not live here as a slogan.

**The functional hook.** Asking for help, joining a group, using AAC that actually speaks the classroom vocabulary (essay 38), staying intelligible to unfamiliar listeners. Secondary present levels that ignore passing periods are lying (essay 11).

**The data, translated.** A standard score can stay if it is valid for this student. Next to it, a sentence in classroom English. “This score sits well below peers on a test of following complex directions; in science, that looks like starting the lab before the third step is out.”

**What is not the problem.** Dialect, emerging English, lack of instruction — named so they are not smuggled into a disability finding (essay 36). Strengths, because *Endrew F.* is about circumstances, not only deficits.

## What a useless present level contains

Only the test table. Only speech-room percentages. Only medical history. Only last year’s paragraph with the date changed. Only adjectives (“struggles,” “defiant,” “lazy”) that would get a teacher in trouble if they said them aloud.

AI drafts that sound like every other student in the caseload (essay 40). If you can swap two IEPs and the present level still “works,” it does not work.

## Workload, honestly

A present level a teacher can use takes longer than a score dump. That time sits in ASHA’s indirect clusters and in the 6.0 mean documentation hours (essay 06). Large caseloads, the portal says, leave less time to know the curriculum across grades. That is how you get paragraphs that could have been written in any state.

The 3:1 model (essay 26) is one way to put observation and teacher talk on the calendar so the paragraph has a source. Consultation at 2.5 mean hours a week is the national smear of that job. If your consultation hour is zero, your present levels will show it.

## A test before you upload

Hand the paragraph to a teacher in that grade — no extra explanation. Ask: “What would you do differently on Monday morning?” If the answer is “I have no idea,” rewrite. If the answer is a specific move, you have a present level.

Do not put the teacher’s name in a public essay. Do not put the student’s.

The IEP starts here. Goals that ignore this paragraph are decorations (essay 23). Minutes that ignore it are a habit (essay 24). The teacher is the rest of the week. Write like they will be in the room, because they will.
