The headline says *caseload*. It almost always means one of three different objects.

Sometimes it means a **ratio bill** — a state number someone wants written into statute. Sometimes it means a **vacancy** — an unfilled FTE, a traveling contractor, a building that has not had a speech-language pathologist since October. Sometimes it means **minutes** — sessions that did not happen, a compensatory-services letter, a due-process complaint that never uses the word *ratio* at all.

If you forward the story to the team chat before you know which object you are holding, you will argue about the wrong lever.

This series is the longer desk. The short news cards live in `content/slpwow-slp-news/`. Here we stay with the school year: how ASHA’s 2024 Schools Survey actually counted, why workload is not a synonym, and how IDEA related services show up when the camera is off.

## The word that does too much work

ASHA’s Practice Portal is blunt. **Caseload** is the number of students with IEPs, IFSPs, and 504 plans an SLP serves — sometimes counted as sessions in a window — and, in some districts, the students sitting in MTSS who never got a disability label. **Workload** is every hour the job requires: face-to-face time plus the rest of the week that makes those minutes legal and educational.

A headline that prints “caseloads hit 70” has not told you whether 70 is a headcount, a weekly session count, a building average, or a Facebook number. It has not told you how many of those students need an AAC system programmed, how many evaluations are open, or whether the SLP is also the only person covering three elementary schools.

ASHA does not recommend a national caseload cap. The 2002 position statement and the current portal page say so for three reasons that still hold: there is no research that crowns one number; student needs vary; and a published “maximum” becomes a floor the moment a caseload dips under it.

So when a bill says “no more than 55,” you are looking at a **policy choice**, not an ASHA finding. When a survey says the median is 50, you are looking at a **description**, not a verdict. Essay 04 stays with that median. Essay 05 stays with the bills.

## Three questions before you share it

**1. Who counted, and of what?**
ASHA’s 2024 caseload report asked full-time clinical service providers for a *monthly* caseload. The median actual size was 50 (range 4–351; n = 2,815). The median size those same people called *manageable* was 40 (n = 2,776). A local reporter who writes “the average caseload is 80” may be quoting a union flyer, a single district’s October count, or a principal who added every student who ever walked into the speech room. Ask for the table.

**2. Is the story about a cap, a body, or a minute?**
A cap is a rule. A body is a hire. A minute is a service that was or was not delivered. Compensatory services (essay 41) are about FAPE, not about whether Indiana’s survey median was 78. Vacancies (essay 08) are about the job market ASHA described — 79 percent of 2024 respondents said there were more openings than seekers — not about whether a ratio bill passed.

**3. What would change if the headline were true?**
If the true object is a statute, the next document is the bill text and the fiscal note. If the true object is a vacancy, the next document is the posting, the contractor invoice, and the make-up-session rule. If the true object is missed minutes, the next document is the IEP service page and the district’s compensatory-services procedure. None of those documents is a blog.

## What the 2024 survey will not do for you

It will not tell you your district is out of compliance. ASHA fielded a member survey, not an audit.

It will not tell you the “right” group size. Brandel and Loeb (2011) and later follow-up work have been used to show that caseload size, not student need, often drives model and intensity. That is a research finding about *practice*, not a prescription for Tuesday.

It will not tell you that paperwork is a personal failing. In the companion workforce report, “large amount of paperwork” ranked first among 13 challenges in *every* facility type. Essay 06.

It will not give you a student. This series does not.
