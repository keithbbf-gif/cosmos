On the 2024 facility table, elementary and secondary look like cousins. Elementary median actual caseload: **51**. Secondary: **50**. Manageable in both rows: **45**. Full-time share: 89 percent elementary, 87 percent secondary. Paperwork first in both. Workload/caseload second in both.

If you stop at the integers, you will staff a high school like a third-grade wing and then wonder why the week broke.

## What the elementary integer is usually made of

Elementary is where speech-sound work and early language groups still dominate many buildings. The 2024 intervention table is national, not elementary-only, but speech-sound disorders were served by 89.1 percent of respondents (mean 20.1 students among those who serve them) and language (semantics, morphology, syntax) by 90.2 percent (mean 23.0). Those means are the large piles. They are also the piles most likely to be grouped, dismissed, or moved into classroom language (essays 27 and 28).

Elementary IEP calendars are dense: initial evaluations, three-year reevaluations, kindergarten transitions from Part C or preschool, 504 conversations that start when a teacher first worries about sounds. Volume of meetings ranked third overall and sat in the top three for elementary. The SLP is often in more buildings per FTE than the secondary colleague, which the survey does not count as a separate integer.

Literacy shows up here as *prevention and MTSS* more than as a 28-percent specialty — though the national literacy row (28.0 percent of SLPs; mean 14.8 students among them) is the reminder that “speech” and “reading” are not a clean divorce. Essay 37.

## What the secondary integer is usually made of

Secondary caseloads compress toward students who did not dismiss, students with complex communication needs, students whose primary category is not SLI, and students whose goals have moved from sounds to the language of algebra word problems, lab reports, and the social life of a hallway.

The workforce table hints at the identity problem: limited understanding of the SLP role ranked **fourth** in secondary and **seventh** nationally. A high-school counselor who thinks speech is preschool articulation will not send the student who cannot unpack a history prompt. Present levels that a science teacher can use (essay 22) are the job. Pull-out flashcards are a stereotype the 2010 roles document already tried to kill.

Secondary schedules fight you. Block periods, credit-bearing classes, and “do not pull from English” notes shrink the legal LRE conversation into a passing period. Minutes and location have to be honest (essay 24) or the IEP is fiction.

Transition planning — postsecondary, vocational, AAC that has to survive graduation — is workload that does not exist in the same form at age seven. The survey does not give it a row. The IEP does.

## How to staff the cousins without pretending they are twins

Do not move an FTE from elementary to secondary because 51 and 50 “look the same.” Ask for the **mix**: percent primary SLI versus related service, percent AAC (nationally 74.5 percent of SLPs serve AAC; mean 7.2 among those who do), percent evaluations open, number of buildings, and meeting load.

Do not assume secondary is “lighter because they can walk to you.” Walking is not a present level.

Do not assume elementary is “only groups of four.” Childhood apraxia of speech was regularly served by 60.1 percent of respondents (mean 3.0). Fluency by 67.6 percent (mean 2.6). Those means are small and still eat a schedule if you pretend they are the same as a speech-sound group.

Preschool, for comparison, sat lower on the actual-caseload ridge and had its own meeting and family-contact pattern (essay 35). Special day/residential sat at 25 and is a different intensity (essay 12). Use the row that matches the building.

The shape is the week inside the integer. The integer is only how many names the data system printed.
