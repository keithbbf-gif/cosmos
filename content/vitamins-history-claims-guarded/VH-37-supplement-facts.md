Two boxes live in an American grocery, and they are not siblings even if they look like it.

Nutrition Facts is a child of the Nutrition Labeling and Education Act of 1990 — a food-label revolution about serving size, calories, a short list of nutrients, a government aesthetic of black Helvetica on a white rectangle. Supplement Facts is a child of DSHEA's 1994 labeling section and the later 21 CFR 101.36. It lists dietary ingredients, "proprietary blends" that can hide amounts behind a sum, percent Daily Values that may be blank for substances that have no DV.

The resemblance is a political costume. If it looks like a food label, it feels like a food. DSHEA wanted that feeling. Critics call the costume a dodge. Both descriptions have ink behind them.

I read Supplement Facts the way I read a balance sheet. What is named. What is lumped. What percent is being implied. A blend line that says "energy complex 800 mg" and then a list of leaves is a literary form. It is legal if it follows the rule. It is not informative in the way a chemist would want. The rule and the chemist are not the same person.

Daily Value percents are leaked planning numbers. They descend from the RDA / RDI / DRV weather. A bottle that says 2000% DV is doing theater with a planning staple. Theater is not automatically a disease claim. It is often a completeness claim, a "more is more." This folder does not cheer the theater.

The box is also a claims fence. What you put *outside* the box — the headline, the little heart, the runner — is where 101.93 lives. Firms that behave inside the box and misbehave above it are the usual letter-from-FDA story.

I am fond of the rectangle the way I am fond of a shipping label. It is how a lot crosses a border of attention. A lot number, a milligram, a percent. None of those is a reason. Reasons live in the verbs, and the verbs are what we watch.

When someone says "it must be serious, it has a Facts panel," they have been convinced by a graphic design. Design is part of commercial history. So is the 1990 law that made the other box famous first. DSHEA borrowed the look and changed the guts.

No serving. No "check your DV." A genealogy of a rectangle.


Look at a proprietary-blend line until it becomes funny. A sum, a mood name, a list of leaves with no milligrams. Funny is the correct response. Then remember that the rectangle next door — Nutrition Facts — cannot pull that trick. Two boxes, two permissions. The resemblance is the trick that is not funny.
