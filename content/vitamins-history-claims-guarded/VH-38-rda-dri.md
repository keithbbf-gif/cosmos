A Recommended Dietary Allowance is a committee sitting around a table arguing about a population.

1941 is the first printed set. The war wanted numbers. The numbers were never "what I, personally, must swallow at breakfast." They were "what we think covers most people if we are feeding a country." Covering most people means the number is high enough for the upper part of a need curve, not a bullseye on your particular Tuesday.

Later the language multiplied. RDA, RDI, DRV, Daily Value, DRI, EAR, UL. Each acronym is a small political creature. The Estimated Average Requirement sits in the middle of a distribution. The RDA sits toward the top. The Tolerable Upper Intake Level is the "enough, friend" line for some nutrients. Labels do not print this ecology. They print % DV, a single percent that pretends the ecology is a ruler.

I have watched people treat 100% as a moral grade. That is the leak from planning to shame. Shame is a commercial friend. A bottle that offers 2000% is selling relief from a grade the shopper invented.

The Institute of Medicine (now National Academy of Medicine) DRI reports of the late 1990s and after are thick, cautious, full of "insufficient evidence." Insufficient evidence does not photograph well. A carton photographs well.

This series will not pick your number. It will say: the number is a tool that escaped the Board. Tools that escape become totems. Totems show up in ads.

Internationally the numbers disagree. That disagreement is not a conspiracy. It is different committees, different diets, different dates. A traveler who finds a new percent on a foreign carton has not crossed into a new truth. They have crossed into another table.

Present tense, thin: committees publish reference values. Labels borrow them. Borrowing is not discovery.

If you want a history of science, read the DRI chapters on a single letter. The hedges will calm you down. If you want a history of commerce, watch which hedges get deleted on the way to the printer.

The number is a wartime planning object that learned to live on plastic. That is the whole plot.


If you ever sit on a committee that sets a number, you learn how little of the argument is chemistry. It is surveys, depletion studies, a fear of printing a target that will be swallowed as a command. The print shop does not attend those meetings. The print shop wants a percent. This folder sides with the meeting, which is another way of saying we will not print your command.
