Robert R. Williams spent a career walking toward a molecule he had met as a child of missionaries in Asia, in the shadow of the rice problem.

He worked in industry — Bell Telephone Laboratories, of all places — and on his own time, and then with a chemical company's patience. Isolation of thiamine in useful form, then synthesis in 1936, then a process that could feed a mill. The rice bran of Funk's era becomes a structure with a pyrimidine and a thiazole. Once you can make that on purpose, you do not need the bran except as nostalgia.

Williams is the American industrial saint of B1, which means the story gets cleaned. The real story is years of bad yields, a foundation (the Williams-Waterman Fund) that later moved money into food work, and a man who believed enrichment was a civic machine. He wanted the cheap loaf to carry what the cheap mill had knocked off. That belief sits behind the 1941 flour standard as surely as any FDA lawyer.

I will not pretend synthesis is morally superior to food. Synthesis is weatherproof. Food is a farm. A country that mills its staple white and then sprinkles a crystal back in has made a choice about taste, shelf life, and politics. Williams helped make that choice chemically possible. Whether the choice was elegant is a different essay. This series promised discovery and commerce. Enrichment is both.

Merck and the other houses that scaled B1 did not do it as charity. They did it because a government identity standard and a wartime mood would buy kilos. The same kilos could go into a tablet tin for the drugstore. One crystal, two channels: the bakery and the aisle. DSHEA is decades away. The split between "food with a powder" and "powder as a food" is already visible.

Thiamine is a chemical name that sounds like a brand even when it isn't. Resist the sound. The 1936 synthesis is a date. The 1941 standard is a date. Your breakfast is your business.

Williams wrote with a reformer's confidence. Reformers overreach. Copywriters overreach more. The lint in this folder exists because of that second overreach. His papers are safe to read. The ads that borrowed his silhouette are not safe to reprint as if they were papers.

Rice, a telephone lab, a thiazole ring, a loaf. That is the chain. I will not add a "therefore take."


The Williams-Waterman Fund later moved money into food work in places that still ate a lot of white rice. That is a sequel with its own politics — American philanthropy, Asian staples, a crystal as foreign policy. This page stays with the 1936 synthesis and the 1941 sack. The sequel exists so you do not think the story ends at a New Jersey plant. Crystals travel. So do the arguments about who should pay for them.
