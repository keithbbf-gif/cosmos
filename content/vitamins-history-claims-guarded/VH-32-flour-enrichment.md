White flour is a technology. Enrichment is an apology written as a standard of identity.

By 1941 the Food and Drug Administration had a definition for "enriched flour." A pound of that flour had to carry stated milligrams of vitamin B1, riboflavin (on a delayed clock), nicotinic acid or its amide, and iron. Calcium and D were optional add-ins within ranges. Wheat germ could appear in a small percentage. The document reads like a recipe written by lawyers.

The National Research Council had already leaned on millers. Britain had moved thiamine into the loaf in 1940. The American note, recorded with a shrug in *Time*, was that bakers thought the country wanted white bread and that white bread stored better in an industrial system. So the country would have white bread with a powder.

I do not romanticize the whole loaf or the powder. Both are choices. The whole loaf keeps more of the seed. The powder is reproducible and cheap once the crystals exist. Williams, Elvehjem, the riboflavin plants — they made the apology possible. Without kilos, the CFR clause is a wish.

Riboflavin's postponed effective date (into 1942) is my favorite line in the standard. Law waiting on a factory. People who talk about "the government added vitamins to bread" skip the waiting. The government added a requirement. Chemistry had to show up.

This is not a dietary-supplement story, and that is why it belongs in the commercial history. The loaf is food. The powder in the loaf is a food additive in a standard of identity. The tablet in the drugstore is a later, different object. DSHEA will, decades later, define a third object. If you smash loaf, tablet, and "supplement" into one moral, you cannot see the statutes.

War is in the room. A nation that thinks in camps and rations thinks in staples. Enrichment is easier to sell when the staple is patriotic. After the war the habit stayed, as habits do.

No one here is being told to buy white bread, or brown, or a bag of crystals. The sack is a legal object. The mill took, the standard put back a short list, and the rest of the seed stayed wherever the mill sent it.


Optional calcium and optional D in the 1941 standard are a tell. The short list is the apology. The optional lines are the upsell the lawyers already imagined. Mills could be more royal if they wanted. Most wanted to obey the short list and get back to white. The short list is the civic object. The upsell is the cousin of the drugstore tin, hiding in a regulation.
