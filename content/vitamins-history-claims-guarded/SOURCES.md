# Sources (folder-level)

Short working bibliography. Individual drafts cite what they lean on. Dates below were checked against the named sources before staging; a later correction belongs in the draft that used the date, not as a silent rewrite of this page.

## Naming and the first concentrates

- Casimir Funk, "On the chemical nature of the substance which cures polyneuritis in birds induced by a diet of polished rice," *Journal of Physiology* 43 (1911): 395–400. Period title — quoted as history, not adopted as a claim.
- Casimir Funk, "The etiology of the deficiency diseases," *Journal of State Medicine* 20 (1912): 341–368.
- J. C. Drummond, "The nomenclature of the so-called accessory food factors (vitamins)," *Biochemical Journal* 14 (1920): 660.
- F. G. Hopkins, "Feeding experiments illustrating the importance of accessory factors in normal dietaries," *Journal of Physiology* 44 (1912): 425–460.
- Richard D. Semba, "The discovery of the vitamins," *International Journal for Vitamin and Nutrition Research* (and related Semba reviews); *Clin Chem* 43 (1997): 680–685 ("Vitamine—vitamin. The early years of discovery").
- Nobel Prize lectures: Hopkins (1929), Eijkman (1929), Szent-Györgyi (1937), Windaus (1928, chemistry), Dam and Doisy (1943), Hodgkin (1964, chemistry of B12).

## Labs, ships, wards (history of inquiry)

- Kanehiro Takaki and the Japanese Navy ration change, 1884 — see Yamashita / *J R Soc Med* accounts (e.g. PMC3725862).
- Christiaan Eijkman, Gerrit Grijns — Batavia chicken work; Nobel theme essay "The Nobel Prize and the discovery of vitamins."
- James Lind, *A Treatise of the Scurvy* (1753).
- Axel Holst and Theodor Frølich, guinea-pig work, 1907.
- Joseph Goldberger et al., orphanage and prison diet studies, 1914–1915; James Lind Library essay on Goldberger.
- Conrad Elvehjem, nicotinic acid in dogs, 1937.
- Robert R. Williams, thiamine isolation/synthesis, 1930s.
- Herbert Evans and Katherine Bishop, 1922 fat-soluble fertility factor in rats.
- Henrik Dam; Edward Doisy — vitamin K.
- Lucy Wills, Bombay / Marmite / monkey work, early 1930s.
- Karl Folkers (Merck) and E. Lester Smith (Glaxo), B12 isolation 1948; Dorothy Hodgkin, structure 1956.
- Harry Steenbock, U.S. patents 1,680,818 et al.; Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation (WARF), 1925.
- Tadeus Reichstein and Andreas Grüssner, *Helv. Chim. Acta* 1933–1934; Hoffmann-La Roche, Redoxon 1934.

## Commerce and statute

- Miles Laboratories, One-A-Day, first use September 1940 (trademark record).
- Enriched flour identity standard, 21 CFR (1941).
- Food and Nutrition Board / first Recommended Dietary Allowances, 1941.
- Vitamin-Mineral Amendment (Proxmire), 1976, FD&C Act § 411, 21 U.S.C. § 350.
- Nutrition Labeling and Education Act of 1990.
- Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994, Pub. L. 103-417; Congress.gov on S.784.
- 21 CFR 101.93; 65 Fed. Reg. 1000 (Jan. 6, 2000).
- U.S. v. F. Hoffmann-La Roche (vitamin cartel plea, 1999); Roche vitamins business later sold to DSM (2003).
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, created by DSHEA § 13.

## How to use these

Cite the paper, the patent, or the statute. Do not cite a paper's *disease title* as a use. Funk's 1911 title uses "cures." That is his word in his year. It is not ours.
