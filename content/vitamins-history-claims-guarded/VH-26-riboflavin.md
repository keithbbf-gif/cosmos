Before it was B2 it was a color.

Whey throws a yellow-green fluorescence if you ask the light the right way. Chemists in the 1930s — Kuhn, Karrer, the usual Central European crew — chased that color into a crystal and then into a ribose tail. Lactoflavin. Riboflavin. A flavin is a stubborn yellow. You can see why a dairy bench would find it before a tablet firm would.

The flavin nucleotides later explain, in textbooks, a slice of respiration. That is mechanism language, and 21 CFR 101.93 would let a label characterize a documented mechanism if the rest of the page stayed out of disease. We are not a label. We can say the chemistry without selling the breath.

Riboflavin's commercial life is partly a dye life. It is used, frankly, as a color in some foods. The same molecule as a nutrient line and as a pretty stain. Shoppers do not love that overlap when they learn it. I think the overlap is clarifying. A vitamin is a chemical. Chemicals have more than one job in a factory.

The 1941 enriched-flour standard included riboflavin, with a delayed effective date because the supply was tight. That little postponement is a tell. Enrichment is an industrial plan. If the crystal does not exist in tonnage, the law waits. Williams's B1 and Elvehjem's niacin had similar logistics. The New Deal state could write "shall contain" only after a plant could obey.

Kuhn's Nobel (1938, chemistry) sat in the political mess of Germany; he was pressured about the prize. The molecule did not care. Molecules are lucky that way.

Present tense, thin: riboflavin fluoresces. It can be made. It was required, on a schedule, in a defined U.S. flour. No lip claim. No "energy" claim. The yellow in the whey is a historical object.

If your milk looks ordinary, it still has the color if you shine the right lamp. That is a kitchen trick, not a therapy. I have done it with students. They remember the glow longer than they remember the letter. Good. Letters are cheap. Glows are specific.


Students remember the glow. Manufacturers remember the delayed effective date. I remember both because they are the same story at two scales: a molecule that announces itself with light, and a law that cannot announce itself until the plant exists. 1941–42 is the year the glow had to become tonnage. Tonnage is unromantic. It is also the only reason the sack could obey the lawyers. A glow without tonnage is a classroom trick. A law without tonnage is a bluff.
