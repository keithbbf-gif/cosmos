Once a synthesis is old, it looks for cheaper electricity and cheaper solvent rules.

Ascorbic acid is the textbook case. Reichstein's hybrid process, Basel's pride in 1934, became a world process, then a Chinese process. By the 2000s the names on the bulk spec sheets were often CSPC, Northeast Pharmaceutical, and their peers. European houses retrenched, specialized, sold. DSM's purchase of Roche's vitamin business was a late Western consolidation in a market whose center of gravity was already moving.

I am not going to do the cartoon of "cheap foreign C." Cartoons are how tariffs talk. The honest sentence is about comparative cost after the patents died and the know-how leaked, as know-how does. Engineers travel. Papers exist. A process that starts in glucose will live where glucose and capital and permissions line up.

Shoppers still meet a Western brand on the tin. The tin is a last-mile object. Under the tin is a drum, and under the drum is a plant you will not be invited to. Private-label gummies from three "companies" may share a drum. That is not a scandal by itself. It is how commodities work. Coffee is like this. Aspirin is like this. Vitamins became like this when they stopped being marvels.

Quality systems — GMP, USP monographs, a buyer's incoming assay — are how a drum is not a rumor. Those systems are commercial history too. They are also not holiness. A certificate is a document. Documents can be good. They can be theater. Distinguishing the two is a buyer's job, not this essay's product endorsement.

The geopolitical aftertaste is real. Governments notice when a vitamin sits in one country's tanks. "Supply chain" became a phrase that grocery people borrowed from defense people. A letter that was once a Cambridge accessory factor is now a strategic input. That sentence would have sounded insane to Hopkins. It is ordinary to a procurement officer.

No flag on a bottle from me. No "buy domestic." A map of tanks, a sold Basel division, a drum.

The rose hip in the 1939 caption has, by this point, become a sticker. The building won.


Engineers I have talked to about C plants talk about solvent recovery and a river, not about Funk. That is the correct conversation for a mature molecule. This series began in a word. It has to end, in part, in a river next to a plant you will not tour. The letter did not stay a Cambridge accessory. It became an effluent problem and a spot price.
