The red crystal arrives in 1948 as if the 1920s had been a prologue written in liver.

Minot and Murphy's liver-diet work, Castle's intrinsic-factor argument, the whole Boston-and-elsewhere ward tradition — that is hospital history. People were eating cooked liver by the plate because a clinic said the plate changed a blood picture. We can name that history without turning a modern capsule into a plate of liver and without turning either into advice.

What this draft wants is the industrial last mile. Karl Folkers at Merck and E. Lester Smith at Glaxo, independently, isolated a red cobalt-containing compound from liver extracts. They were racing. Firms race when a concentrate is already a product and a crystal might be a better product. 1948 is a year of lots, not of myths.

Dorothy Hodgkin solved the structure in 1956, a cathedral of X-ray work. The molecule is enormous by vitamin standards, a corrin ring with a metal in the middle. She received the 1964 chemistry Nobel. The pictures of the model look like a radio set.

B12 is the letter that most tempts a disease claim because the ward name is famous. This folder's rule is extra quiet here. We do not say what the crystal is "for." We say: isolated from liver, structured by Hodgkin, manufactured later by fermentation because you cannot ask a steer to be a factory at that price.

The fermentation turn matters. Streptomyces and other bugs became the plant. A vitamin that animals store in liver is made, for commerce, by microbes in tanks. Vegetal slogans and animal slogans both get confused by that fact. The tank does not care.

Castle's intrinsic factor — a gastric detail — is why a swallowed milligram and an injected milligram are not the same historical object. Injection is a drug route. A tablet is a later retail object. Smashing them together is how you write a claim without using the verb.

I will leave the crystal on the bench. Red, cobalt, late. The ward prologue stays prologue. Nobody here is being told to chew liver or to ask for a shot.


Merck and Glaxo racing a red powder is the 1940s in miniature: two houses, one liver, a cobalt atom as a trophy. The trophy becomes a fermentation later because liver is a stupid factory. Bugs are a smarter one. When a shopper meets B12 in a vegan aisle, they are meeting the bug, not the steer. The aisle rarely says so. The tank does not mind.

## Claims desk

Isolation, structure, fermentation as manufacture. Ward history named and fenced. No blood claim, no shot-as-supplement.
