In 1920 J. C. Drummond published a note so short you can miss it if you turn the page too fast. *Biochemical Journal*, volume 14, page 660. "The nomenclature of the so-called accessory food factors (vitamins)."

The parentheses are doing work. He is already tired of the word, and he is about to save it.

Funk's *vitamine* had implied a chemical class: vital amines. By 1920 that was an embarrassment. Fat-soluble A did not look like an amine. Water-soluble C did not either. The B fraction was a mess of more than one thing, which is a polite way of saying the letter was a boarding house. If you left the *e* on, you were still telling students a story about nitrogen that the crystals would not support.

Drummond's fix is almost comic in its smallness. Drop the letter. *Vitamin* now sits with the names chemists give to neutral substances when they do not yet know the skeleton. He also wants to kill the other mouthfuls then in use — "fat-soluble A," "water-soluble B," "water-soluble C" — and just say vitamin A, vitamin B, vitamin C, and so on, until someone solves the structures and can retire the alphabet.

That is how a field admits it is young. It keeps the nickname and stops pretending the nickname is a formula.

He had earned the right to nag. Drummond had already been in the rice-bran precipitates with Funk in 1914. He knew the concentrates were crude. He knew the word was doing more work than the chemistry. A lot of scientific style is the courage to make a word smaller.

The alphabet stuck harder than he might have liked. We still say B-complex as if B were one tenant. We still meet people who think "vitamin F" is hiding in a catalog, which it is not — that letter got used, abused, and thrown back. The clean A-B-C plan was a hope. Commerce loved letters. Letters fit on a label. Letters look like a complete set you can finish collecting.

Watch that last sentence. A complete set is a sales feeling. The science was never a set with a lid. New letters appeared. Some collapsed into older ones. Some were not vitamins. The alphabet is a filing system that leaked.

Drummond himself later became, in the British war years, a public man of rations and policy. That is another draft. The 1920 note is enough for this one: a chemist performing housekeeping in public, one letter at a time.

If you write the old spelling in a caption, you are quoting 1912. If you write the new spelling, you are speaking after 1920. Neither spelling is a claim. Both are just the sound a field made while it was still arguing about what it had found.
