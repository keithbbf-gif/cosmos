In 1999 F. Hoffmann-La Roche pleaded guilty to participating in a worldwide conspiracy to fix the prices of bulk vitamins. The U.S. fine was $500 million, a record at the time. BASF and others followed with their own checks. Executives flew and met and allocated tons as if the letters were a private railroad.

This is the unromantic end of the Redoxon story. A firm that had once shown a scale model of a C plant as a marvel of technique had, by the 1990s, a commodity business worth colluding over. Collusion is what you do when the molecule is solved, the process is shared, and the only trick left is the invoice.

I want this draft in the series so the discovery glow does not get the last word. Funk's *e*, Reichstein's tanks, Williams's loaf — and then a hotel room where someone says "your share of A is…" The share is the commercial history without perfume.

The Justice Department's language is dry and therefore perfect. Conspiracy. Allocate. Market. The letters are listed like lumber. C, E, A, the B's, beta-carotene. A shopping list for a crime.

Civil suits followed. Buyers who had paid the fixed price wanted money back. The bulk buyers were not shoppers with a tin. They were feed companies, food companies, tablet houses. The cartel sat *under* the aisle. Most people who swallowed a letter in 1997 were eating a conspiracy they could not see.

Roche later sold the vitamins division to DSM (2003). Divestiture after scandal is a classic. The tanks change flags. The molecule does not.

No one in this paragraph is a hero of "natural." The cartel was as happy to fix synthetic lots as any other lots. "Natural" and "synthetic" are shop words. The meeting minutes were about tons.

Why guardrails here? Because scandal can be flipped into a purity claim ("ours is the honest C"). That flip is still a pitch. We will not make it. We will say: the largest vitamin houses of the late twentieth century were convicted of running the market. That is a fact about commerce. It is not a coupon for a smaller house.

A record fine, a sold division, a letter that had become lumber. Keep that next to the paprika.


Feed-grade lots were in the room. Animals ate the conspiracy too. That detail keeps the story from becoming a boutique-human morality play. A vitamin business that sells to a chicken house and a drugstore is one business with two invoices. The meeting allocated both. The fine covered a market, not a lifestyle.
