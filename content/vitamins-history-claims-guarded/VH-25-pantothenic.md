Roger J. Williams named it for a Greek joke. *Panto* — from everywhere. The factor showed up in so many tissues and foods that the honest name was a shrug.

He was working with yeast and with the kind of growth assays that make a microbiologist feel like a god of tiny hungers. A missing drop and the culture sulks. The drop was pantothenic acid. Later, Fritz Lipmann's coenzyme A would give the shrug a job in biochemistry textbooks: a handle on which acetyl groups hang. The 1953 Nobel to Lipmann is a different party. Williams had the isolation grind.

"From everywhere" is commercially awkward. How do you scare a shopper with ubiquity? The aisle prefers rarity. Pantothenic acid ended up as B5, a number, which is easier to print and easier to imply incompleteness about. "Is your multi missing B5?" Completeness again, the collector's itch.

I like Williams because he treated yeast as a philosophical animal. If a bug needs it, maybe a mammal does too — a heuristic, not a proof. The heuristic worked often enough in that decade to become a method. It also swept up things that were not vitamins. Methods over-harvest.

The later "royal jelly" and "stress B" advertising that tried to adopt B5 is refused here on sight. Those campaigns were mood, not Williams. His papers are about a growth factor and a structure that can be synthesized. Synthesis, once again, is the moment a grocery ubiquity becomes a tank ubiquity.

Present tense, small: pantothenic acid is part of coenzyme A. Coenzyme A is a carrier. Carriers are chemistry. Chemistry is not a mood.

If everything contains a little, the commercial question becomes dose theater. This folder does not do dose theater. We do a name that admitted the opposite of scarcity, and a later aisle that pretended not to hear.

Everywhere, then a number, then a carton. The interesting part is the first word.


Williams later wrote wide books about nutrition as a public philosophy. Those books are another man's megadose weather, milder than Pauling's, still a weather. This page keeps the Greek joke and the yeast assay. Philosophies leak into aisles. Jokes leak into names. The name is the only leak I am willing to keep: from everywhere, then a number, then a carton that pretends everywhere was not enough. Ubiquity is a poor slogan, which is why the slogan department threw it away and rented a number instead. B5 fits on a line. "From everywhere" does not, and that is why the aisle looks poorer than the Greek.
