Adolf Windaus already lived among sterols when the vitamin fight knocked on the door.

He had the 1928 chemistry Nobel for that larger life — cholesterol and the stubborn family of solid alcohols. The D story is a chapter, not the book. Ergosterol, a sterol you can get from yeast, takes ultraviolet light and becomes something with the biological activity the lamp-and-rat people had been selling under a letter. The rearrangement is the kind of thing a Göttingen laboratory could love: a structure that changes its mind in the presence of photons.

Once you have a structure, you can stop irradiating whole lard and start irradiating a defined sterol. That is a quality story and a scale story. Yeast is a tank organism. Tanks make industry happy. Cod-liver oil is a fishery. Fisheries make industry nervous. The shift from "a northern oil" to "a yeast sterol plus a lamp" is how D becomes a chemical, not a weather report.

Windaus's group, and the people who followed the ring migrations more finely, also taught a caution the ads ignored: more than one D. The animal form and the plant-sterol form are cousins, not twins. Letters hide cousins. A label that just says "D" is a filing tab. The filing tab is not a lie. It is a compression.

I am not going to walk you through every double bond. You can get that from a review. What belongs here is the handshake between a German organic chemist and an American agricultural patent. Steenbock had the process claim. Windaus had the molecule. Margarine plants and pharmaceutical houses stood in the middle, writing checks.

Göttingen in the late 1920s is also a political weather system about to go dark. That darkness is not a vitamin story, and I will not hijack one. I mention it only so the laboratory does not float in a cartoon Europe. People drew sterols in a real town.

The present-tense residue is small. Sterols rearrange under UV. Yeast can be a source of the starting sterol. Those are facts about shops and tanks. They are not a reason to swallow a capsule, sit on a tanning bed, or trust a cartoon sun on a bottle.

A chalkboard, a mercury lamp, a yeast cake. The letter D is what leaked out into the grocery.


Ergosterol from yeast is a tank story hiding inside a Göttingen chalkboard. Once the sterol is a tank object, D is no longer a fishery rumor. Cod-liver houses felt that shift in the invoice. They still sold romance — the northern boat, the liver. The lamp-and-yeast people sold a number. Numbers win industrial arguments. Romance wins the label for a while longer.
