The words changed. The music did not.

1940s: pep, vim, vigor, the bright eye, the worker who does not flag. 1970s: natural, complete, insurance in a bottle. 1990s: antioxidant, protection, the little shield. 2010s: wellness, ritual, self-care, a soft photograph of linen. The statute learned new names for the same verbs. The verbs still want to sneak toward a disease or toward a structure/function pitch.

I have a folder of ads I will not write *from*. I will write *about*. A mother in an apron, 1952, shaking a tin over a breakfast plate. A runner, 1998, with a sunrise. A ceramic bowl, 2021, with a gummy that looks like fruit. Three decades of art direction, one commercial job: attach a letter to a feeling.

DSHEA and 101.93 did not kill the job. They moved it. The feeling had to stop pointing at a named syndrome and start pointing at "support," "maintain," "healthy." Those words are the legal S/F dialect. This series does not speak it, even though the aisle does. If I say "supports," I have left the museum.

Watch the pictures. 101.93(g)(2)(iv)(E) says pictures can be a disease claim. A red-cross vibe. A lung. A joint with a lightning bolt. The lightning bolt is a symptom cartoon. Cartoons count.

The honest advertising history is not "they lied." Some firms were careful. Some were not. The honest sentence is: isolation created a cheap letter, and cheap letters need stories. Stories drift. Guardrails are how a writer refuses the drift.

I have tried, in forty-four drafts before this one, to keep the story on people, tanks, statutes, and rooms. This last draft (last in the first staged set) is the reminder that the public did not meet Funk. The public met pep, then wellness. If we write as if the public met Funk, we are writing a seminar. Seminars are fine. They are not the market.

No pep from me. No linen-and-ritual dialect either. A change of wardrobe on the same song.

When a future editor wants to "make these drafts more useful to shoppers," that editor is asking to leave the folder. Useful-to-shoppers is a label. We staged a history.


A last exhibit: the star to the disclaimer, tiny, on a webpage that sings. 101.93 said the type had to be bold and not shy. The star is how a designer obeys a rule while still conducting the orchestra. This folder does not conduct. It files the music and leaves the star on someone else's page.
