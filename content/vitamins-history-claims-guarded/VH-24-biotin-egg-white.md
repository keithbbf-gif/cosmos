The egg-white story is a parlor trick that got upgraded into a letter.

Feed a rat a mess of raw egg white and the animal develops a picture the 1930s called egg-white injury. Cook the white and the picture changes. The raw white carries avidin, a protein that locks onto a small factor and will not let the gut have it. The factor picked up names like vitamin H and, later, biotin. Kögl isolated. György argued. The boarding house gained another tenant who had been hiding in the binding.

This is a beautiful mechanistic miniature, and it is a trap. Beauty wants to become advice. "Don't eat raw eggs." "Take biotin for your hair." The first is kitchen hygiene older than biotin. The second is a structure/function favorite this folder will not touch.

I am interested in avidin as an instrument. A protein that sequesters a vitamin is a tool. Later biochemists will use biotin-avidin the way carpenters use clamps. The clamp is a descendant of a rat on a stupid diet. A lot of molecular biology is a descendant of a stupid diet.

Commercially, biotin became a beauty mineral that is not a mineral. Hair-and-nail cartons borrowed a letter most shoppers cannot define. The 1930s papers do not mention a salon. They mention a rat, a white, a bound factor.

Boas, in the 1920s, had already seen the egg-white injury. As usual, the first seer is not the last namer. Science is a relay with sloppy handoffs and too many nicknames. H, biotin, coenzyme R — the same small molecule wearing hats.

Present tense, thin: biotin is a prosthetic group in certain carboxylases. Avidin binds it with a famous affinity. Those are bench facts. They do not tell you to buy a gummy.

If you cook eggs, cook them because you like them cooked, or because of microbes, not because this essay glared at you. We are not a kitchen pamphlet. We are a history of a binding protein that accidentally taught a century how to clamp a molecule.


Avidin's later life in molecular biology — blots, tags, a clamp on a bench that has never seen a rat — is the longest commercial echo in this draft, and it is not a supplement echo. It is a tools echo. Companies sell kits. Kits are products. They are not letters in a gummy. I mention the kits so the egg-white story does not end in a salon. It ends in a catalog that never says 'hair.'
