Henrik Dam was trying to study cholesterol in chicks when the chicks started bleeding.

That is the kind of interruption you either throw out or follow. He followed. A fat-free mash, a hemorrhagic picture, a factor in green leaves and in certain other feeds that put the clotting back. He named it vitamin K, from *Koagulation*, because he was writing in German. The letter is a language fossil.

Edward Doisy, in St. Louis, took the chemistry further — isolation, quinone structures, the split between the plant form and the bacterial forms. The 1943 Nobel in medicine went to both men. The war was on. The papers still got through.

There is a neighboring story that ads love to smash into this one: spoiled sweet clover, cows bleeding, dicumarol, and then warfarin. That is a coumarin story and a pesticide-turned-drug story. It is not "vitamin K therapy," and it is not our product. I mention the clover so nobody thinks Dam was working in a vacuum of agricultural accident. Farms kept handing chemists broken animals.

K is a hard letter for this folder because the hospital use is famous and the supplement aisle is loud. Newborn protocols, anticoagulant clinics — those are drug and practice questions. They are not essays about a 1929 chick mash. We stay with the mash.

Commercially, K was never the celebrity C was. It did not get a Redoxon-scale consumer myth in the 1930s. It got later, quieter channels: a drop, a tablet, a "bone and blood" whisper that this series will not repeat. The whisper is a structure/function move or a disease move depending on the rest of the page. We have neither.

Dam's chicks are enough. A mash without the right fat-soluble passenger. A leaf that carried the passenger. A letter from a German noun. Doisy's quinones. A prize in a war year.

If you remember one thing, remember that K is a filing tab invented because chicks bled on a purified diet. The tab is not an instruction. The chick is not a testimonial.


1943 is a strange year to share a medicine prize. The war ate travel, ate students, ate the easy idea of a European-American conversation. The papers still moved. Quinones still got drawn. I mention the year so the letter K does not float in a cartoon 1930s. It arrives, named, in a decade that had other uses for green leaves and for men. A letter born in a war year should not be asked to smile on a carton as if it had been born in a spa.
