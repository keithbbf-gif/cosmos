DSHEA did not only free an aisle. It built a room in Bethesda.

Section 13 of the 1994 Act created the Office of Dietary Supplements at the National Institutes of Health. The job, on paper: explore the role of supplements in the diet, promote study, collect results. A research office, not a cheering section. Whether it has always felt that way depends on whom you ask and which year you pick. Offices drift. Statutes sit.

I like the office as a historical object because it admits a split. The FDA is the cop (when it acts). The NIH office is supposed to be the library. Cops and libraries in the same subject are how a country says: we will allow the market and we will also try to know things. Knowing things is slower than printing a carton. That slowness is not a conspiracy. It is method.

Section 12's Commission on Dietary Supplement Labels was the other 1994 room — a time-limited group to think about how the new claims lane should look. Commissions write reports. Reports get cited by everyone and obeyed by no one in particular. Still: the statute wanted a conversation, not only a repeal.

ODS fact sheets later became a quiet internet genre. Shoppers meet them when they search a letter. The sheets are cautious, cited, dull in the way I praised monographs for being dull. Firms hate the dullness when it fails to endorse a verb. Researchers like it when it names a trial. This folder is closer to the dullness than to the verb.

I will not treat an ODS paragraph as a structure/function claim we can borrow. If the office says a nutrient has a role in a normal pathway, that is their sentence under their roof. Our roof is history. We can say the office exists because Hatch and Harkin put it in the bill.

Funding fights, later dietary-supplement research centers, the usual NIH weather — those are sequels. The 1995 standing-up of the office is the date. A market that had just been redefined got a scientific mailbox.

No sheet quoted as a pitch. A room, a section number, a library that is not a shop.

When someone says "NIH recommends this bottle," they are almost always lying or compressing. This page refuses the compression.


Bethesda is not Elkhart and not Basel. Putting a mailbox there was a way of saying the subject had become national science, not only a shop. National science is slow and political and sometimes good. This series will not wait for a fact sheet to finish a history. The history was already in the patents and the pots. The mailbox is a late, useful piece of furniture.
