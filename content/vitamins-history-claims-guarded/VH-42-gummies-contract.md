The gummy is a children's technology that conquered the adults.

Gelatin or pectin, sugar or a sugar story, a letter, a mold. The 1990s and 2000s made this ordinary. Brands you have heard of and brands that exist only on a marketplace page often share a contract manufacturer's room. The "company" on the label may be a marketer, an insurer of a story, a buyer of a lot. The kettle is elsewhere.

21 CFR Part 111 — the dietary-supplement GMP rule FDA built with the authority DSHEA left on the table — is how that kettle is supposed to behave. Batch records. Identity tests. A quality unit. The rule is long. Compliance is uneven. The FDA warning-letter archive is a literary genre of its own: firms that could not prove what was in the gummy, or that let the website talk like a clinic.

I care about the contract room because it finishes the Roche-to-drum story. Isolation, synthesis, commodity tank, drum, kettle, bag, ad. The ad is the only part most people meet. The ad says "we." The kettle says "also them."

Private label is not a sin. Your grocer's milk is private label. The sin, when it happens, is a claim the kettle never signed up for — a disease verb added by a marketer who needed a point of difference in a market with no difference left. When the molecule is a commodity, the verb is the only inventory.

Gummies also scramble dose theater. People eat them like candy. That is a compliance and a risk conversation for someone else's desk. Our desk says: a delivery form is a commercial event. It is not a new discovery. Funk did not die for pectin.

If you like the taste, that is a reason people buy candy. It is not a structure/function claim unless a carton makes it one.

The children's shape is a trust technology. Soft, sweet, cartoon. Trust technologies belong in a history of commerce. They do not belong in a laboratory of letters.

A kettle, a rule, a marketer's "we." Keep those three in the same sentence when you look at a bag.


Part 111 is readable if you are stubborn, like the 2000 claims rule. The stubbornness is the point. A marketer who has not read the kettle rule will still write 'we.' A quality unit who has read it writes batch numbers. This folder is closer to the batch number. The bag on the camera is closer to the 'we.' Believe the batch number.
