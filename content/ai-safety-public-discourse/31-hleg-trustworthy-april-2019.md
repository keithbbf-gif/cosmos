# 8 April 2019: Trustworthy AI, in Brussels English

On 8 April 2019, the High-Level Expert Group on Artificial Intelligence, set up by the European Commission, presented *Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI*. A draft had gone out in December 2018; the Group said more than five hundred comments came back. The final public document is the start of the Act's *vocabulary*, even though it is not the Act.

## Three words, seven requirements

Trustworthy, on this page, means lawful, ethical, and robust. Those three are the umbrella. Under them sit seven requirements the Group wanted systems to meet:

1. human agency and oversight
2. technical robustness and safety
3. privacy and data governance
4. transparency
5. diversity, non-discrimination and fairness
6. societal and environmental well-being
7. accountability

If you have been living in Asilomar, some of this rhymes (safety, privacy, shared benefit) and some of it is a different climate (environmental well-being as a listed requirement; discrimination as a first-class heading). The longer-term Asilomar bin is almost absent. Superintelligence does not get a number.

## What kind of document this is

It is expert-group soft law. It does not bind a company the way a regulation does. It was piloted: an assessment list went out in 2019, and a revised ALTAI list arrived in 2020. Soft law with a checklist is how the Commission often tests a field before it spends political capital on a regulation.

The Group itself, in the usual Brussels way, said members supported the overall framework without agreeing with every sentence. That disclaimer is a feature. It tells you the seven requirements are already a coalition.

## Why the Act's later reader should start here

Because when the 2020 White Paper says "ecosystem of trust," it is pointing at this work. Because when the 2021 proposal talks about high-risk requirements — data, documentation, human oversight, accuracy, cybersecurity — you can hear the seven-note scale. The statute will add teeth, annexes, and market-surveillance machinery. It will not start from Asilomar Principle 23.

If you only read FHI-adjacent texts, 2019 Europe can look like a miss. If you only read 2019 Europe, FHI-adjacent texts can look like a miss. They were writing different exams in the same calendar year.

## A classroom use

Have students map each of the seven requirements to an Asilomar principle, or mark "no partner." The empty cells will cluster around recursive self-improvement and existential language. That map is the cleanest one-page explanation of why "AI safety" in Palo Alto and "AI ethics" in Brussels talked past each other even when they shared the word *safety*.
