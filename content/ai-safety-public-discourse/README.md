# Public AI safety discourse — staged draft set

This folder is a **staged educational draft set**. It is not a book, not a policy brief, and not a claim of original research.

The job is modest and specific: help a general reader walk the **public record** of how people have talked about AI safety in the open. The spine of the set is four named public chapters:

1. **Asilomar** — the 1975 recombinant-DNA meeting as a later citation, then the 2017 Beneficial AI conference and the 23 Asilomar AI Principles.
2. **FHI** — the Future of Humanity Institute at Oxford (2005–16 April 2024) and the public life of *Superintelligence* (2014).
3. **OpenAI charter era** — the December 2015 founding text through the 9 April 2018 Charter and the 2019 capped-profit announcement.
4. **EU AI Act public timeline** — from the High-Level Expert Group (2019) and the February 2020 White Paper through Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and Article 113's phased application.

Around that spine sit neighboring public instruments (OECD 2019, UNESCO 2021, Bletchley 2023) and a few **method** pieces on how to read letters, charters, and timelines without turning them into origin myths.

## What "staged" means

Every file in this folder carries `stage: draft` and `status: staged`. Nothing here is cleared for a homepage, a syllabus as-is, or a press quote. A later editor can cut, merge, fact-check against the primary PDF, or throw a piece out. Staging is the point.

## What "novelty-safe" means

These drafts **recap documents already in public circulation**. They do not:

- propose a new safety method, metric, or architecture
- invent a new legal instrument
- settle a scientific controversy
- claim insider knowledge
- treat a blog post as a theorem

Where two public stories disagree, the draft names the disagreement and stops. Dates are given as they appear on official pages, archived snapshots, or widely reported announcements. Signature counts on open letters move; when a count is mentioned, it is dated.

## What "human voice" means here

The pieces are written as if one careful adult is walking another through a stack of public PDFs. Short sentences sit next to longer ones. A document is allowed to be dull. A famous name is not treated as a plot twist. First person appears only when it helps a reader hold a page ("If you only open one annex…").

## What this set is not

It is not a history of any private software project. It is not a vendor brief. It is not a ranking of labs. It does not mention internal tools, house names, or unpublished work.

## How to read

Start with `00-how-to-read-this-set.md`, then follow the numbered files, or jump by era using `INDEX.md`. Public URLs live in `SOURCES.md`. The file list and word counts are in `MANIFEST.md`. After the 2026-09-14 editor pass, see `EDITOR_REPORT.md` and run `python3 check_pack.py` in this folder.
