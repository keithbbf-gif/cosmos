# March 2019: capped-profit becomes a public fact

On 11 March 2019, OpenAI announced a new structure: OpenAI LP, described as a "capped-profit" company, with the nonprofit still in a governing role and investor returns limited (the public figure was a 100x cap). The blog said the mission needed capital at a scale no ordinary nonprofit could raise, on the order of billions.

This draft treats the announcement as a **discourse event**. It changed how every earlier sentence would be read.

## What the announcement said it was solving

The 2015 launch had bragged, in effect, about being unconstrained by financial return. The 2018 Charter had already mentioned the need to marshal substantial resources while minimizing conflicts of interest. The 2019 post is the organizational diagram for that sentence. It says: we still serve the mission; we now have a vehicle that can take investment; the cap is the moral technology that keeps the vehicle from becoming an ordinary startup.

Whether a 100x cap is "not ordinary" is a judgment these notes will not make. The public fact is that the lab thought a cap would do rhetorical and legal work.

## What critics said in the same week

Tech press immediately ran the obvious headline: the nonprofit is not only a nonprofit anymore. Some pieces were measured. Some treated the cap as a fig leaf. The *existence* of that critical chorus is part of the charter era. The 2015 words had created a constituency that felt entitled to a consistency argument.

That constituency included journalists, former fans, and later, officials. Consistency arguments are not always good history — organizations update — but they are good discourse. The 2019 post is unintelligible without the 2015 post.

## How to keep the corporate-law fog at arm's length

The LP, GP, and later diagrams became intricate. Subsequent years added further public restructurings; by 2025–2026 the organization was again explaining itself in new nouns (foundation, public benefit corporation). Those later nouns are outside the "charter era" window this set is trying to hold.

For 2019 literacy you need only:

- a for-profit vehicle appeared
- a cap was advertised
- the nonprofit claimed ongoing control
- the reason given was compute and talent at scale

Everything else is a later chapter, or a lawyer's exhibit.

## Placement next to Europe's 2019

April 2019 is also when the EU High-Level Expert Group published the Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI. March is the LP. The same spring, two different public languages: *fiduciary to humanity with a cap table*, and *seven requirements for trustworthy systems*. They are not answering the same questionnaire. A timeline that only follows labs will miss Brussels. A timeline that only follows Brussels will miss why "nonprofit" had become a fight word in San Francisco.
