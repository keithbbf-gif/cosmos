# December 2015: a founding post as a public event

OpenAI's December 2015 launch is one of the few lab-founding moments that belongs in a *discourse* history rather than only in a company history. The reason is the text. The nonprofit announcement did not say "we will ship an API." It said, in public, that the goal was to advance digital intelligence in a way most likely to benefit humanity as a whole, unconstrained by a need to generate financial return.

That sentence — later quoted, later thrown back, later contextualized by tax filings and restructurings — is the start of what this set calls the **charter era**. The era is a stretch of published purpose language, not a verdict on outcomes.

## What was public on day one

The founding post and contemporaneous press named a nonprofit, a list of people (Musk and Altman as co-chairs in the early telling), and a large pledged sum, commonly reported as a billion dollars. Later reporting, using tax documents, stressed that money received lagged pledges. Both facts can sit on one page. A pledge is a speech act. A Form 990 is a different speech act.

The launch also sat in a weather system this set has already named: *Superintelligence* in circulation, FLI's Puerto Rico meeting earlier in 2015, a press corps ready for "the good lab." OpenAI did not invent that weather. It published into it.

## Why "nonprofit" did discourse work

In 2015 public English, nonprofit was a trust adjective. It meant: not Google, not Facebook, not a quarterly-earnings animal. Whether that contrast was fair to those firms is a separate argument. The contrast was the message.

It also set a later trap. When the 2019 capped-profit structure arrived, every critic had the 2015 sentence on a notecard. That is how founding language works. It is a gift to the organization's future opponents. Organizations still write it, because they need the first headline.

## How to read a founding post

Read it as **self-presentation on a specific week**, not as a contract with the reader. Note the beneficiary ("humanity as a whole"). Note the constraint that is being refused (financial return as the driver). Note the technical object, which in 2015 was still a broad "digital intelligence," not yet the Charter's tighter AGI definition.

Mark what is absent. There is no EU-style risk pyramid. There is no Asilomar numbered list. There is no evaluation harness. Absence is normal in a launch post. Later documents will fill some of the blanks and leave others.

## Placement

Pencil 11 December 2015 (the widely used public date) after Puerto Rico and before Asilomar. The lab is younger than FHI by a decade and older than the Act by nine years. Those intervals keep a reader from treating "AI safety" as a single generation.
