# FHI, 2005–2024: an institute, not a mood

The Future of Humanity Institute was a multidisciplinary research group at the University of Oxford. Nick Bostrom founded it in 2005, initially on a three-year footing, inside the Faculty of Philosophy. It closed on **16 April 2024**. Those are the bones. Almost everything else in the public conversation is a story people tell with those bones.

## What the institute said it was

FHI's own later account, on the institute's public pages and in the 2005–2024 final report, describes a small group that tried to study "big-picture questions for human civilization" while mixing philosophy, computer science, mathematics, and economics. The self-description emphasizes an organizational culture meant to protect unusual questions from ordinary academic weather.

That self-description is a primary source about *aspiration*. It is not an external audit. A student should read it the way you read a lab's about-page: informative, interested.

The topical public image that escaped Oxford was narrower than the self-description. Journalists, especially in the 2014–2024 window, tagged FHI with existential risk, longtermism, effective altruism, and artificial intelligence. Those tags overlap with work the institute and its affiliates actually published. They also flatten a bibliography that included anthropic reasoning, global catastrophic risk more broadly, and other essays that never mentioned a neural network.

## Why a reader of AI safety discourse still starts here

Because *Superintelligence* (2014) made a university group's concerns portable. Because FHI affiliates showed up in the same conference photographs as FLI and lab researchers. Because when Oxford closed the institute, newspapers treated the closure as a paragraph in the AI-safety story, not only as a faculty-administration story.

The educational task is to keep **letterhead** and **atmosphere** apart. People who once sat at FHI went to other universities, companies, and nonprofits. Ideas moved in PDFs. Citing "FHI" for every later use of the phrase *existential risk* is sloppy. Citing FHI for the book, the working papers, the public seminars, and the closure file is accurate.

## The closure, as the institute and the press told it

FHI's public closing text says administrative headwinds inside the Faculty of Philosophy grew over years; a freeze on fundraising and hiring began in 2020; remaining staff contracts were not renewed in late 2023; the institute ended on 16 April 2024. Bostrom, in remarks quoted in the *Guardian* that week, used the phrase "death by bureaucracy." Oxford's own terse notices stressed ordinary restructuring language.

Those are two public tones. An educational draft does not have to decide which tone is fair. It should not invent a third tone — a conspiracy, a triumph, a martyrdom — that the documents do not carry.

## A clean use of the name

In the rest of this set, **FHI** means the Oxford institute and the documents issued under that name between 2005 and the closing week. Adjacent organizations (FLI in the U.S., CSER at Cambridge, later nonprofit spinouts) get their own names when they are the speaker. The weather may be shared. The letterhead is not.
