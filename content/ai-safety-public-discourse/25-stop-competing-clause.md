# The Charter's "stop competing" clause

Buried in the long-term safety heading is the sentence that still startles people who have not seen it. OpenAI wrote that it was concerned about late-stage AGI development becoming a competitive race without time for adequate safety precautions. Therefore, if a value-aligned, safety-conscious project comes close to building AGI before OpenAI does, the organization commits to stop competing with and start assisting that project.

The page even offers a "typical" trigger: a better-than-even chance of success in the next two years. It says specifics would be worked out case by case.

## Why the sentence is historically loud

Companies do not usually pre-commit, on the open web, to lose a race on purpose. The sentence is trying to solve Principle 5's problem with a first-person mechanism. Not "teams should cooperate," but "we will stand down if the other team is the safety-conscious one."

It is also a theory of history in miniature. It assumes there will be a recognizable late stage. It assumes "value-aligned" and "safety-conscious" will be identifiable from the outside. It assumes AGI is the kind of finish line you can see two years out with better-than-even odds. All of those assumptions were contestable in 2018. They remain contestable. The Charter still published them.

## What the sentence is not

It is not a contract with a named counterparty. There is no annexed term sheet.

It is not a regulator's order. No public authority required this wording.

It is not a claim that such a stand-down has happened. This draft knows of no public event in which OpenAI formally invoked the clause. Absence of a public invocation is not proof of hypocrisy. The trigger may not have been met, on the organization's own story. A critic may say the trigger was designed never to be met. Those are arguments. They need evidence this recap does not invent.

## How to teach it

Give students the paragraph and ask them to list the **undefined predicates**: value-aligned, safety-conscious, close to building, better-than-even, success. Then ask who, on the page, may apply those predicates. The page says "we." That is the whole enforcement story.

Compare the clause to Asilomar 5 and 23. Asilomar asked teams and, later, humanity. The Charter names a single organization's conditional behavior. That is more concrete and still not justiciable.

## Why it belongs in a discourse history

Because it taught a mass audience that at least one lab was willing to talk about racing as a moral failure mode, not only as a business plan. Later 2023–2024 political speech about "avoiding an arms race" had this kind of sentence in the cultural background, whether or not speakers had read the blog. Background is not citation. It is still history.

Read it once without smirking. Then, if you must smirk, smirk at the undefined predicates, not at the idea that a race could be unsafe. The idea is older than the lab. The predicates are where a 2018 blog post shows its seams.
