# 2023: two letters, two public temperatures

Open letters are a genre this set has already met (FLI 2015, Asilomar 2017). In 2023 the genre returned at mass-media volume. Two artifacts are enough to teach the year without pretending to have read every petition.

## The March pause letter

On 22 March 2023, the Future of Life Institute published an open letter calling for a pause of at least six months on training systems more powerful than GPT-4. The text asked laboratories to pause, and if they would not, asked governments to intervene. Signatories included recognizable researchers and public figures; the list grew in public and was argued about in public (who signed, who declined, who was a surprise).

The letter is an attempt to turn race-avoidance into a **calendar**. That is its historical interest. Whether any lab treated the six months as binding is a later empirical story; the public fact is that the ask was made, dated, and widely covered.

Critics said the letter was vague on verification, convenient for whoever was already ahead, or a distraction from present harms. Those criticisms are part of the letter's reception, not a reason to leave it out of a timeline.

## The May CAIS statement

On 30 May 2023, the Center for AI Safety released a one-sentence statement: mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war. The shortness was the point. A long essay can be dismissed as a book report. A sentence can be photographed.

The statement collected a set of famous names from industry and academia. It did not ask for a six-month pause. It asked for a ranking: extinction risk belongs on the top shelf. That is a different speech act from FLI's March letter. Collapsing them into "the 2023 letter" is sloppy.

## What the pair did to the word safety

After spring 2023, a newspaper reader could be forgiven for thinking AI safety *meant* extinction. Fairness researchers had to re-introduce themselves. Brussels kept writing the Act it had been writing. Labs issued more evaluation notes. The letters did not unify the field. They **raised the temperature** of one dialect.

Hinton's public comments in the same season — a builder's refusal, in the Weizenbaum-genre sense — added a face to the temperature. Faces travel faster than articles.

## How to file them

March 2023: pause as a proposed coordination device. May 2023: extinction as a one-line priority claim. November 2023: governments at Bletchley. December 2023: EU political agreement. The year is a stack, not a single mood. If you remember only the mood, you will misquote every document in the stack.
