# What "open" meant on the 2015 page

The name was doing work. **Open** AI, in the founding weather, suggested publication, shared research, a counterweight to in-house corporate secrecy. Early public comments and reporting talked about open-sourcing and about not wanting powerful AI locked in a few firms. Then the research matured, the blog posts grew more cautious about releasing weights, and critics said the name had become irony.

This draft is not a ruling on whether the name was betrayed. It is a note about a **word that could not stay still**.

## Open as distribution

In 2015–2017, "open" in this neighborhood often meant: papers, code, and sometimes models should move out of the lab. That meaning sat comfortably next to academic computer science and uncomfortably next to later arguments about misuse. When GPT-2's 2019 staged release became a public fight, the word *open* was already under stress. Staged release is a safety performance. It is also a retreat from the most naive reading of the name.

## Open as governance

A second meaning, quieter on the launch week and louder in later nonprofit rhetoric: open to humanity's interests, not closed around shareholders. The 2018 Charter's "primary fiduciary duty is to humanity" is that meaning in legal-sounding clothes. You can be closed-weights and still claim this kind of open. You can be open-weights and fail it. The adjective has split.

## Open as invitation

A third meaning was cultural. The name invited outsiders to treat the lab as a public project. Journalists, independent researchers, and later litigants accepted the invitation. If you brand yourself as a mission for all humanity, you will be asked, in public, to explain your cap table. That is not unfairness. That is the brand working.

## How neighboring documents used other adjectives

Asilomar said *beneficial*. Europe said *trustworthy*. OECD said *trustworthy* and *responsible stewardship*. Partnership on AI, announced in 2016 by a set of large firms, said *partnership*. None of those names promised weights on a download page. OpenAI's name did, or was heard to.

The educational point is small and stubborn: **do not assume a 2024 fight about model release is the same fight as the 2015 name.** The token persisted. The policy problem changed. People who quote 2015 "open" as a gotcha and people who pretend 2015 never implied publication are both simplifying for a side.

## A reading practice

When you meet the word in a primary text, subscript it.

- open<sub>publish</sub>
- open<sub>mission</sub>
- open<sub>process</sub> (who gets a vote)
- open<sub>weights</sub>

The Charter era mostly argues in the mission subscript, with leftover sparks from the publish subscript. Later internet arguments live in the weights subscript. If you keep the subscripts, the conversation gets less stupid without anyone inventing a new framework.
