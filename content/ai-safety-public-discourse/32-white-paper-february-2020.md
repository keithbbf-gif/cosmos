# 19 February 2020: the White Paper as a public rehearsal

COM(2020) 65, dated 19 February 2020, is the Commission White Paper *On Artificial Intelligence — A European approach to excellence and trust*. White Papers in this system are not laws. They are public rehearsals: here is how we see the problem, here are options, please comment. The comments later become a political resource when the proposal is attacked as a surprise.

## Two ecosystems

The paper's memorable branding is a pair: an **ecosystem of excellence** (investment, skills, research) and an **ecosystem of trust** (a future regulatory framework, especially for high-risk uses). Trust, here, is the HLEG word going to work. Excellence is the industrial-policy twin, so the file cannot be dismissed as only a brake.

That pairing matters for discourse. American coverage of "AI safety" in 2020 was still often about labs and long-term risk. Brussels was already talking about product rules *and* competitiveness in one PDF. If you skip the White Paper, the 2021 proposal looks like it fell from the sky.

## High-risk as a public sketch

The White Paper sketches a risk-based approach before the proposal's pyramid hardens. Not every use of AI should carry the same burden. High-risk uses — the paper talks about sectors and about rights-affecting uses — should meet requirements. Lower-risk uses should not be smothered.

This is ordinary product-safety thinking. It is also a political peace offering to industry: we are not about to license every spreadsheet macro. Later fights (what counts as high-risk, how general-purpose models fit) are fights *inside* this sketch, not against the idea of a sketch.

## Consultation as part of the timeline

The Commission ran a public consultation on the White Paper in 2020. Thousands of responses are part of the administrative file. This set will not pretend to have coded them. The fact of the consultation is the educational object: the Act's ancestors include a comment box, not only a philosopher's book.

COVID-19 also sits in this year. The White Paper's February date is pre-lockdown for most of Europe. The proposal would arrive in April 2021 into a different political weather. Timelines that skip 2020 miss a year of comments and a year of crisis that changed every institution's calendar.

## How to read a White Paper quickly

Read the section headings and the regulatory-options pages. Skip the investment prose on a first pass unless you are studying industrial policy. Mark every time the document says *mandatory* versus *voluntary*. The later Act is the story of some of those *mandatory* seeds growing teeth.
