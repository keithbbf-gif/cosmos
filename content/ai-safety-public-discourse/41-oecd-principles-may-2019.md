# 22 May 2019: the OECD AI Principles as a public standard

The OECD Council adopted its Recommendation on Artificial Intelligence at ministerial level on **22 May 2019** (OECD/LEGAL/0449). The organization has since called it the first intergovernmental standard on AI. A 2024 update adjusted definitions and added language for general-purpose and generative systems. Adherents include OECD members and some non-members; the G20's 2019 AI principles drew on this text.

## What kind of force this is

A recommendation is not a regulation. States adhere. They are expected to promote the principles. There is monitoring language. There is no Article 113. The public power here is **vocabulary and coordination**. The OECD's definition of an AI system later shows up, in family resemblance, in Union drafting. That is influence by phrasing, which is a real kind.

## Five and five

The portable teaching version: five values-based principles (inclusive growth and well-being; human rights and democratic values, including fairness and privacy; transparency and explainability; robustness, security and safety; accountability) and five recommendations to governments (invest in R&D; foster an inclusive ecosystem; enable agile policy; prepare people and the labor market; cooperate internationally).

If this sounds closer to HLEG than to Asilomar's bin three, that is because it is. 2019 official English converged on trustworthy, rights, and robustness. Existential language did not make the ministerial cut.

## Why a discourse history needs a "boring" standard

Because boring standards win meetings. When a later summit wants a baseline everyone has already initialed, OECD 2019 is sitting there. When a country does not want the Act's machinery, it can still point at OECD and say it is not a rogue. The principles are a diplomatic lowest-common-denominator with better prose than most.

They are also a reminder that **safety** in official English often means robustness and security, not control-of-superintelligence. Same word, third dialect, after product-rights and existential.

## How to read the 2024 update

Read it as evidence that the 2019 text was built to be revised. The update's attention to generative AI is a public admission that the object changed. It is not a confession that 2019 was a failure. Standards that cannot update die. Note the date (3 May 2024, in public trackers) and do not quote the 2019 wording as if it were the only official wording after the revision.
