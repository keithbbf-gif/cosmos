# Wiener's warnings, before the word alignment

Norbert Wiener published *Cybernetics* in 1948 and spent the 1950s and 1960s telling anyone who would listen that purpose-seeking machines would not automatically share our purposes. Later alignment writers cite him the way they cite Good: as proof the worry has a mid-century beard.

The citation is fair if it stays modest. Wiener really did write about learning machines, automation, and the danger of literal or runaway goal-seeking. He wrote about labor and about weapons. He wrote in a prose that a non-specialist could follow, which is why the books still get reprinted.

## What a discourse reader should take

**The sorcerer's-apprentice shape.** Wiener liked stories in which a command is fulfilled too well. That shape reappears in popular alignment parables (the well-known "paperclip" illustration is a later, punchier cousin, usually associated with Bostrom's circle rather than with Wiener's pages). Shape is not plagiarism. It is a durable fable type.

**The refusal of neutrality.** Wiener did not treat technical work as morally vacant. That stance is public and early. It matters when someone says ethics was bolted onto AI in 2019. Bolted onto *which* AI, and in which country? The mid-century record is not empty.

**The breadth.** Wiener's worries include unemployment and war, not only a future superintelligence. If you cite him only for the latter, you have narrowed a writer who did not narrow himself.

## What not to do with him

Do not turn cybernetics into a secret origin of the EU AI Act. Brussels cites fundamental rights, product safety, and a 2018–2021 administrative trail.

Do not turn Wiener into a member of FHI. He died in 1964. Genealogy by citation is not employment.

Do not update his examples into commentary on a 2024 model card. He was writing about servomechanisms, computation, and social choice as he knew them.

## Why this older pair (Wiener, Weizenbaum, Good) closes the FHI-era block

Because FHI-adjacent public talk often arrived with a preface: *this is not new.* The preface pointed at these names. A reader who actually opens the older books discovers they are not interchangeable and not a single research program. The preface was doing legitimation. The books were doing their own, older jobs.

After this draft, the set changes register. We leave ancestor-citation and walk into a 2015 corporate blog post. The weather will feel familiar. The letterhead will not.
