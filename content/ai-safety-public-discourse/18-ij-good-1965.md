# 1965: I.J. Good and the intelligence explosion as a citable ancestor

I.J. Good's 1965 paper "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine" is the ancestor later AI-safety writers like to produce when someone says the worry was invented in Silicon Valley. The paper is real. The ancestor ritual is also real. Both belong in a discourse history.

## What the public citation usually includes

Good, a statistician who had worked with Alan Turing, wrote that an ultraintelligent machine could design better machines, producing an "intelligence explosion." The portable quote — the one that moves through blogs — is the claim that the first such machine is the last invention humanity needs to make, provided the machine is obedient enough.

Provided. The clause matters. The 2010s popularization sometimes dropped the hedges and kept the fireworks. A slow reader puts the hedges back.

## What 1965 is not

It is not a research program with a lab notebook. It is not a regulation. It is not "the same paper" as *Superintelligence*, which is a 2014 book with a different apparatus, bibliography, and audience. Citation is not identity.

It is also not secret. People who talk as if Good were a suppressed prophet are performing. The essay has been in the open for decades, reprinted and referenced in ordinary histories of AI.

## How 2010s discourse used him

Good became a **legitimacy token**. If a 1965 statistician said it, the 2014 book is not a fad. That move is understandable and a little circular. Lots of 1960s speculations did not become institute agendas. The question a discourse reader asks is not "was Good clever?" It is "why did this speculation get institutionally warm in the 2000s and 2010s when others stayed cold?"

Possible public answers, none of them proven here: cheaper compute; a visible deep-learning boom; new philanthropic money; a cluster of philosophers willing to treat speculative essays as research objects; a media system that likes explosions. You can list those as hypotheses already circulating. This set does not pick a winner.

## Placement

On a wall timeline, 1965 sits far left, with a thin string to 2014 and another thin string to Asilomar's recursive-self-improvement principle (22). The string means "later writers pointed here." It does not mean a continuous project ran for fifty years in one building.

If you assign Good, assign a short excerpt and a warning: do not update the paper into a commentary on transformer models. The paper does not know what those are. You are reading an ancestor, not a product spec.
