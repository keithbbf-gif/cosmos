# The risk pyramid, as Europe explained it in public

If Asilomar gave the 2010s a numbered list, the Commission gave the 2020s a **pyramid**. Public slide decks and explainers — not only the legal text — taught four altitudes: unacceptable risk (prohibited), high risk (heavy duties), limited risk (mainly transparency), and minimal risk (mostly free). The pyramid is a teaching picture. The articles are fussier than the picture. Both are public.

## Why a pyramid travels

It can be drawn on a whiteboard. A journalist can describe it in four sentences. An SME can ask "where do I sit?" without reading Annex III. Those are political virtues. They are also simplifications. Real systems sit on more than one face of the pyramid. A chatbot can be a limited-risk transparency case and also a component in a high-risk product. Later GPAI rules add a horizontal slab the original cartoons did not emphasize.

Still, as discourse, the pyramid did something Asilomar did not: it told a mass audience that **not all AI is the emergency**. That is the opposite of Principle 21's commensurate-with-existential-impact energy. It is closer to product-safety law, where a toy and a nuclear valve do not share a form.

## Unacceptable, in public talk

The prohibited layer is the one headlines like. Social scoring of the "Orwell" type, certain manipulative practices, certain untargeted scraping of faces, certain real-time biometric uses in public spaces — the exact list moved during negotiation. What did not move is the idea that some practices are not for the single market at any compliance price.

This layer is where European fundamental-rights talk is loudest. It is also where geopolitical comparison talk is loudest ("we are not that kind of state"). Both talks are on the record.

## High-risk, in public talk

High-risk is the pyramid's working floor. Safety components of regulated products, plus listed use-areas (employment, education, credit, migration, some justice and democratic processes — again, the annex moved). The public explanation: if it can hurt health, safety, or fundamental rights at scale, you document, test, oversee, and accept post-market duties.

A student who only knows "alignment" will not, at first, see why a CV-sorting tool is the paradigm. That is the point of teaching the pyramid. Different emergencies.

## Limited and minimal

Transparency for "you are talking to a machine," labels for synthetic media — these are the limited-risk souvenirs. Minimal risk is the Commission's promise to the rest of the economy. Whether a given consumer chatbot is truly minimal is a later compliance fight. The discourse function of the category is reassurance.

## Keep the picture, then open the articles

Assign the pyramid as a first hour. Assign Article 5, Annex III, and (later) the GPAI chapter as a second hour. If you stop after the first hour, you have taught a cartoon. If you skip the first hour, you have thrown newcomers into annexes without a map.
