# Spring 2024: votes, then a signature

After December's political agreement, the file still had to become an act. The European Parliament voted in plenary on **13 March 2024**. The Council completed its adoption in **May 2024**. The regulation as published carries the date of signature **13 June 2024**. The official name is long; the citation you want is **Regulation (EU) 2024/1689**.

This is the unglamorous education. Headlines like "EU passes AI Act" attached to several of these days. A careful reader keeps a small table.

## Why multiple dates exist

In the Ordinary Legislative Procedure, Parliament and Council are co-legislators. Each must formally adopt the same text. Then someone signs. Then the Publications Office puts it in the Official Journal. Then the entry-into-force rule (usually twenty days after publication, unless the act says otherwise) starts the clock. The AI Act follows that choreography.

If you are coming from U.S. news habits, you may want a single "signed into law" day. The Union does not owe you that simplicity.

## What the March vote was, publicly

The March plenary was the photogenic moment: a large yes, a visible no, speeches about rights and competitiveness. Some members said the file had been watered down on biometrics. Some said it would crush startups. The vote still put Parliament's stamp on the December bargain. That stamp is why March often appears in popular timelines as "the" date. It is *a* date.

## What 13 June 2024 is

It is the date of the act as a signed regulation. EUR-Lex's document date and the title block use it. If you are citing the law as a law, this is the adult date to pair with the OJ publication date (12 July 2024) and the entry-into-force date (1 August 2024).

## A habit worth stealing for other files

Make a four-line card for any major EU instrument:

- political agreement
- Parliament vote
- Council adoption / signature
- OJ + entry into force

Then, if the instrument is phased, add Article-whatever's application dates (draft 38). Once you have done this for the AI Act, the Digital Services Act and GDPR stop looking like mystical events and start looking like paperwork. Paperwork is how public power actually arrives.

FHI closed on 16 April 2024, between Parliament's vote and the June signature. The coincidence is a calendar curiosity. It is not a plot.
