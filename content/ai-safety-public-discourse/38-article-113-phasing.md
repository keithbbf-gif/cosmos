# Article 113: the public phasing calendar

Article 113 is the Act's clock face. It says the regulation enters into force twenty days after OJ publication, then applies from **2 August 2026**, with earlier and later slices:

- **2 February 2025** — Chapters I and II (general provisions and prohibited practices, plus the literacy duties that sit in that early bundle as the official explainers describe them).
- **2 August 2025** — governance machinery, GPAI obligations in Chapter V, certain other listed chapters and articles, with a noted exception.
- **2 August 2026** — general application.
- **2 August 2027** — Article 6(1) and corresponding obligations (the high-risk hook that follows other product-safety legislation on a longer fuse).

If you only remember August 2024, you will think firms already lived under the whole statute. If you only remember 2026, you will miss the prohibited-practices wave and the GPAI wave.

## Why legislators phase

Phasing is how you avoid pretending a new regulator, a new notifying-body market, and a new model-evaluation practice can appear on a Monday. It is also how you make a political deal palatable: some duties now, some later. Every major digital file of the last decade has used a version of this trick.

The educational risk is that phasing becomes an excuse to ignore the text. "It doesn't apply yet" was, in 2024–2025, sometimes true and sometimes a way not to read Chapter II. By February 2025, that dodge had shrunk.

## How this calendar differs from principle lists

Asilomar has no application date. The Charter has no application date. OECD recommendations have adherents and follow-up reports, not a 2 August fuse. Article 113 is what it looks like when safety-adjacent talk enters **time as a legal technology**.

That is why this set spends a whole draft on one article. Not because 113 is philosophically deep. Because it is how a public promise becomes a deadline.

## Keep the official explainer nearby

The Commission's AI Act Service Desk paraphrase of Article 113 is a decent companion to the OJ text. If they ever diverge, the OJ wins. Companions are for navigation.

A novelty-safe close: this draft does not advise a compliance program. It recites a calendar that is already on EUR-Lex. If your lawyer needs more, that is a different profession.
