# Cooperative orientation, on a company blog

The Charter's fourth heading promises a cooperative orientation: actively cooperate with other institutions; work to create a global community; share safety research. It also says OpenAI will not be a "us versus them" project. In a document that just argued for technical leadership, this heading is the counterweight. Leadership without cooperation would look like a race. Cooperation without leadership, on the previous heading's logic, would look like irrelevance.

## What "share safety research" meant as a public norm

In 2018, sharing safety research could mean papers on robustness, on specification, on policy. It fit the academic-adjacent culture the nonprofit still performed. It also fit Asilomar's research-culture principle (4). After productization, sharing became harder to narrate: some evaluations are dual-use; some "safety" methods are product features; some partners are also competitors.

The heading does not resolve those later tensions. It sets a 2018 expectation that later readers used as a yardstick. Yardsticks are one of the things charters are for.

## Cooperation with whom?

The page says other institutions and a global community. It does not say governments first, or civil society first, or rival labs first. The emptiness is typical and important. Partnership on AI already existed (2016) as a firm-to-firm and multi-stakeholder attempt. Academic workshops existed. The Charter does not name them. A reader can take that as breadth or as vagueness.

By 2023–2024, cooperation had new official rooms: Bletchley, Seoul, the EU's GPAI code-of-practice process. Those rooms are not the Charter's children. They are the weather the heading said it wanted.

## The "not us versus them" line

This may be the most perishable sentence on the page. Public life after 2021 was full of them: other labs, other states, other factions inside AI politics (ethics vs existential, open vs closed). A sentence that refuses "us versus them" is doing mood work. Mood work ages fast.

Still, as a historical artifact, the line tells you what the 2018 authors thought a mission lab should sound like. Not a fortress. Not a church at war. A node in a community that did not yet exist in the form they were describing.

## Teaching the tension as the document

If you assign only the safety-stand-down clause, students remember a thriller. If you assign only this heading, they remember a press kit. Assign both on the same sheet. The Charter is the tension. Discourse history is, in part, the story of which heading later speakers quoted and which they forgot.

A novelty-safe close: this draft does not propose a cooperation protocol. It notes that a prominent lab, in 2018, thought it necessary to *say* cooperate in the same document that said lead. That pairing is the charter era in one breath.
