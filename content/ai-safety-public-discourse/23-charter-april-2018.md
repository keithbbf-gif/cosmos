# 9 April 2018: the Charter as a public text

On 9 April 2018, OpenAI published a Charter. The blog post said the document reflected a strategy refined over two years, with feedback from people inside and outside the organization. It said the timeline to AGI remained uncertain. Then it offered a definition and a short list of commitments.

This is the central document of the charter era. Later fights keep returning to it because it is short enough to screenshot and specific enough to argue about.

## The definition that did public work

The Charter defines AGI as highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work. That is not a neuroscience definition. It is an economic-autonomy definition. It tells you what kind of future system the rest of the promises are about. It also tells you why critics could later say: your chatbot is not this, so your AGI sentences are marketing — or, conversely, your chatbot is on the road to this, so your AGI sentences are overdue.

The definition is a public object. Treat it as one. Do not silently replace it with "smarter than Einstein" or "has consciousness." Those are other movies.

## The mission sentence

"Ensure that artificial general intelligence … benefits all of humanity." The verb is *ensure*. The beneficiary is universal. A second sentence says the mission is also fulfilled if OpenAI's work helps others achieve the outcome. That second sentence is easy to skip. It is how the text avoids claiming a monopoly on the build. Asilomar Principle 23 worried about one organization owning superintelligence. The Charter, on this point, performs non-monopoly at the level of mission-success conditions.

## The headings, as published

The commitments travel under ordinary-looking labels that later drafts in this set take one at a time:

- Broadly distributed benefits
- Long-term safety
- Technical leadership
- Cooperative orientation

A charter with headings is already halfway to Asilomar's bins. The difference is first person. Asilomar said *should*. The Charter says *we commit*.

## How to keep the document in 2018

The Charter is pre-ChatGPT, pre-Act, pre-2023 letters. It is also pre-2019 LP in calendar time, though later journalists argued the resource language was already pointing at a fundraising need (draft 30). Read it first as a 2018 page. Then, if you want, watch what 2019 and 2023 did to it. Do not start with the later irony. Irony is cheap and often parasitic on not having read the headings.

A printed copy with four margin marks — one per heading — is enough apparatus for a seminar. The next four drafts are those marks, written out.
