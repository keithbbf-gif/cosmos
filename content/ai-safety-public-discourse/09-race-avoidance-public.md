# Race avoidance leaves the workshop and enters the press

Principle 5 says teams developing AI systems should actively cooperate to avoid corner-cutting on safety standards. Principle 18 says an arms race in lethal autonomous weapons should be avoided. The 2018 OpenAI Charter later worries, in first person, about late-stage AGI development becoming a competitive race without time for adequate safety precautions. By 2023, newspaper readers could meet the race metaphor in headlines about chips, models, and states.

This draft is about the **public life of the metaphor**, not about whether a race is "really" happening. That latter question is an empirical fight these staged notes will not pretend to win.

## Two races, already distinguished in 2017

Asilomar split the metaphor.

**Safety-standards race (Principle 5)** is intra-industry and intra-field. The fear is a team shipping an unsafe system because another team might ship first. The prescribed behavior is cooperation. The missing pieces are the usual ones: who sets the standard, who verifies corner-cutting, what happens to the team that slows down and loses a market.

**Weapons race (Principle 18)** is interstate and military. The object is lethal autonomous weapons. The prescribed behavior is avoidance. This line sits in the ethics bin, next to civic process and shared prosperity, which tells you the list treated weapons as a present political object, not only as a science-fiction annex.

Keeping the two races separate is basic literacy. A story about export controls on GPUs is not automatically a story about a loitering munition. A story about two labs releasing chatbots in the same quarter is not automatically a story about Article 36 weapons reviews. Public talk loves to blend them because "race" is an efficient noun.

## Why journalists liked the word

Race has plot. It has winners, clocks, and shame. A principle about "constructive exchange with policy-makers" does not have plot. So January 2017 coverage leaned toward Musk, Hawking, and arms races even when the document under discussion was a twenty-three-item list with a privacy clause. That lean is part of the discourse history. It taught a mass audience that AI safety was about a sprint, sometimes before it taught them that AI safety was about a dataset.

## What later letters did with the metaphor

The March 2023 FLI open letter asking for a pause on giant training runs is, among other things, an attempt to turn Principle 5 into a calendar. A pause is a race-management device: everyone take a breath, or at least the named class of experiments. Whether firms treated it as binding is a later public story. The genre move is visible without that verdict.

The Charter's "stop competing and start assisting" sentence (see draft 25) is another device: a conditional surrender of the race if a safety-conscious project is closer to AGI. It is a remarkable sentence to put on a company blog. It is also, as published, a promise with a triggering condition the page itself calls typical rather than contractual.

## How to stay educational

When you meet "the AI race" in a speech, split it:

- race for **prestige and benchmarks**
- race for **product market**
- race for **military systems**
- race for **a notional AGI**

Asilomar named two of those. The Charter named a third flavor. The EU Act, interestingly, barely uses race talk in its operative machinery. It uses risk and product placement. That difference is one of the cleanest public clues that Brussels and Pacific Grove were not writing the same genre, even when they shared adjectives.
