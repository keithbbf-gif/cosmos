# 12 July and 1 August 2024: the Act becomes a clock

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 was published in the Official Journal on **12 July 2024** (OJ L, 2024/1689). Article 113 says the regulation enters into force on the twentieth day after publication. The Commission marked **1 August 2024** as the day the AI Act entered into force.

Entry into force is not "everything applies tomorrow." It is the moment the act exists as living law and the application calendar inside Article 113 starts to be real rather than prospective. The Commission's own news note that day still described the Act as aiming to foster responsible development and to address risks to health, safety, and fundamental rights — the 2019–2021 vocabulary, not the 2014 bookstore vocabulary.

## Why the Official Journal date matters

Until a regulation is published, ordinary people and most companies are reading leaks, four-column tables, and law-firm newsletters. Publication is the moment a citation becomes stable. You can say 2024/1689 and be understood in a way you cannot be understood if you say "the December deal."

The Union's Official Journal is not a press release. It is the public instrument of record. Teaching that fact is more useful, long-term, than teaching a hot take about any single article.

## What the 1 August news note emphasized

The Commission's 1 August 2024 page stressed a familiar pair: protect people, give developers and deployers clear duties, reduce burdens where possible. It reminded readers of the April 2021 proposal and the December 2023 agreement. It did not, in that short note, narrate intelligence explosions.

That silence is a discourse fact. The institution that wrote the Act used its first day in force to sound like a product-and-rights regulator. Other institutions, the same summer, were talking about evaluations of frontier models. Both conversations were public. They were not the same press office.

## A small accuracy drill

Ask a room: "When did the EU AI Act become law?" Collect the answers. You will get December 2023, March 2024, June 2024, July 2024, August 2024, and August 2026. Then walk the table from drafts 35–38. The drill is not pedantry. It is how you stop a slogan from replacing a file.

If someone says "2026," they are thinking of general application (2 August 2026). They are not entirely wrong. They are answering a different question: *when do most of the duties bite?* That is the next draft.
