# 23 November 2021: UNESCO's ethics recommendation

In November 2021, UNESCO's General Conference adopted the *Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence*. UNESCO's public pages call it the first global standard on AI ethics, adopted by 193 member states. The date — **23 November 2021** — sits seven months after COM(2021) 206. Same year, different room, different force.

## What "global" means here

UNESCO membership is wider than the OECD and wider than the Union. A recommendation that 193 states can adopt will not read like a high-risk annex. It will read like values: dignity, human rights, gender equality, environment, data, transparency, and a set of policy areas (education, culture, health, economy) that match UNESCO's mandate.

"Global" is true as a membership claim. It is not true as a claim of uniform implementation. UNESCO's later work has been to help states write national methods and to name "readiness." Those follow-on tools are public. They are still not the Act.

## Why it belongs next to the Act, not inside it

Because some speeches treat "the UN did AI ethics" as a substitute for statutory duties, and other speeches treat UNESCO as irrelevant because it cannot fine anyone. Both speeches miss the document's actual job: a **shared official ethics script** for countries that will never be EU member states and may not be OECD adherents.

If you are teaching a non-European student, UNESCO 2021 may be the first primary text that feels addressed to their government. That is enough reason to keep it in the set.

## How it sounds next to Asilomar

You will hear shared adjectives: transparency, safety, human control of a sort, benefits that should not be hoarded. You will not hear a recursive-self-improvement clause. You will hear more about education, culture, and gender than Pacific Grove printed. Mandate shapes list. This is not a mystery.

## What a reader will actually find

UNESCO's public summaries stress values and policy areas more than product annexes. Dignity and human rights sit next to environment, gender, and education. Data governance and transparency appear, as they do in OECD and HLEG English. The implementation story, on UNESCO's own pages, is readiness methods, national commissions, and capacity work — the slow furniture of a specialized agency.

That furniture is easy for a lab-blog reader to mock. It is also how a ministry that will never write an AI Act still gets a document to point at when a reporter asks "what is your policy?" Mock and use, again.

The November 2021 date is easy to lose next to the April 2021 Commission proposal. Keep both. One is a Union statute-in-waiting. One is a 193-state ethics script. A student who can explain the difference in one minute has learned more than a student who can recite a single prohibited practice and nothing else.

## Novelty-safe use

Quote the recommendation as a 2021 General Conference act. Do not claim it secretly governs a lab in California. Do not claim it is empty because it lacks penalties. Empty and unenforceable are different. A script can still be cited in a national white paper. Citation is a kind of life.
