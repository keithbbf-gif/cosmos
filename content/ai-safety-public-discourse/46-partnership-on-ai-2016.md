# 2016: Partnership on AI, a firm-to-firm public ritual

In September 2016, a group of large technology companies announced the Partnership on AI. Early public names included Amazon, Facebook, Google, IBM, and Microsoft; DeepMind was in the Google orbit of the story; OpenAI later joined, in the public telling, as a way to put a nonprofit-flavored lab at the table. The Partnership's published purpose was to study and formulate best practices, and to host a multi-stakeholder conversation.

## Why it is easy to forget

Because it did not produce a 23-principle moment or a regulation. Because multi-stakeholder partnerships are the beige of governance history. Because later years offered louder objects.

It still belongs in 2016, between OpenAI's founding and Asilomar. It shows that **firms already knew they needed a joint public language** before the Charter and before HLEG. The language they chose was "partnership" and "best practices," not "existential" and not "harmonised rules."

## What the ritual does

A partnership lets rivals sit in a room that is not a price-fixing room (in aspiration) and not a courtroom. It produces papers, working groups, and a website. It gives a journalist a place to call besides a single firm's comms shop. It also gives critics a target: captured, slow, toothless.

Those criticisms are standard for the genre (see also other industry safety associations in aviation, advertising, and social media). The educational point is the genre, not a score.

## How it sits next to the Charter's cooperation heading

The 2018 Charter promised a cooperative orientation. A partnership that already existed was one obvious venue. Whether any given lab used the venue sincerely is a later, evidence-hungry question. The public sequence is enough: 2016 partnership announced; 2017 principles signed; 2018 charter published. Cooperation was a *fashionable official noun* before it was a 2023 summit noun.

## What the 2016 announcements actually sounded like

Contemporaneous coverage treated the Partnership as a trust-and-best-practices shop: safety, fairness, transparency, and labor-adjacent questions in the same beige paragraph. That mix is Dialect A in this set's later vocabulary, with a splash of workplace politics. Dialect B was not the press release. If you only read 2016 firm statements, you would not predict Principle 23. If you only read Principle 23, you would not predict a partnership working group on fairness in hiring tools.

The gap is the history. 2016 firms were already under public pressure about bias and platforms. They built a table for that pressure. Long-horizon control talk had a different table, often a university or a nonprofit one. People sometimes sat at both. The tables did not merge.

## Do not upgrade

Do not call the Partnership a regulator. Do not call it a secret government. Do not omit it just because it is beige. Beige is most of how institutions actually talk when they are not having a night of trilogue. A beige 2016 website is still a primary source for what large firms were willing to say together before the Charter, before HLEG, and before anyone had to explain a general-purpose model to a minister.
