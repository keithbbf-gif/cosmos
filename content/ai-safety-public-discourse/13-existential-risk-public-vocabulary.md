# Existential risk: a phrase that escaped the seminar

**Existential risk** is now a phrase a cable-news chyron can carry. That is a historical fact about language, not a proof about the future. In the public AI-safety story, the phrase is one of FHI-adjacent writing's most successful exports. Asilomar Principle 21 uses "catastrophic or existential risks" as if a general reader might already know the second adjective. By 2023, the Center for AI Safety could put extinction in a one-sentence open letter and expect the sentence to travel.

## A working public meaning

In the academic usage that reached the public, an existential risk is a risk that could destroy humanity's long-term potential — through extinction or through a permanent, drastic collapse of the future people might otherwise have had. Different authors draw the boundary in different places. The portable journalistic meaning is simpler and sloppier: *a risk that could end us.*

Both meanings are doing moral work. They rank a scenario above ordinary disasters. Once a speaker has used the phrase, a listener is being asked to treat the topic as cousin to nuclear war or an uncontained pandemic, not cousin to a biased hiring tool. That ranking is exactly why other communities pushed back: they heard present harms being demoted.

## How the phrase behaved in AI talk

Three public behaviors matter.

**It bundled AI with older files.** Nuclear and biological catastrophic risk already had institutions, treaties, and films. Calling AI an existential risk invited that furniture into the room. Sometimes the invitation was explicit (the 2023 CAIS letter places extinction risk "alongside" pandemics and nuclear war). Sometimes it was atmospheric.

**It licensed long time-horizons.** If the worst case is species-level, then work that looks idle on a quarterly product calendar can be described as urgent. Critics heard privilege. Advocates heard prudence. The phrase is the switch.

**It became a tribal badge.** By the early 2020s you could roughly guess a speaker's conference circuit from whether they said *existential risk*, *AI safety*, *AI ethics*, *responsible AI*, or *trustworthy AI*. The badges overlap in real people. In public branding they often do not.

## How to read the phrase without taking a loyalty oath

Ask what **mechanism** the speaker has in mind. Extinction via autonomous weapons is a different story from extinction via a misaligned labor-replacing agent, which is a different story from a slow lock-in of a bad global regime. The phrase hides the mechanism. Mechanisms are where evidence could, in principle, enter.

Ask what **time** the speaker is using. A risk this decade and a risk this century are both sometimes called existential. The word does not confess the clock.

Ask what **is being compared**. Alongside nuclear war is one comparison. Instead of workplace discrimination is another, usually implied rather than argued.

## What this set will not do

It will not estimate p(doom). It will not offer a new taxonomy. It will not say the phrase is illegal in polite company or mandatory in serious company. It will treat "existential risk" as a **public vocabulary item** with a traceable path through books, principles, and letters. Literacy is knowing when the item has been placed on the table, and what it is being used to do to the other items already there.
