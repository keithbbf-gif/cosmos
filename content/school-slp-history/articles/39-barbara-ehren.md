This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

For a long time the American school speech job was a little-kid job in the public mind: kindergarten lisps, first-grade “baby talk,” the occasional stammerer. Adolescents who had language disabilities that showed up as failing science, not as a frontal lisp, could be exited, ignored, or handed to a resource teacher. Barbara J. Ehren’s published work — University of Central Florida faculty pages, ASHA journal papers, chapters on curriculum-relevant intervention and the SLP as a member of a literacy team — is the late-century refusal of that picture.

1997’s IDEA emphasis on the general curriculum and NCLB’s literacy panic made her timing look inevitable. It was not inevitable. Someone had to say, in a professional voice administrators could hear, that a fourteen-year-old’s “speech” file might be a language-and-curriculum file, and that pull-out drill of an isolated skill could miss the class the child was failing.

## What this profile will not do

It will not reprint a therapy hierarchy. It will not turn “curriculum-relevant” into a worksheet. It will not invent a childhood. Birth year: not independently confirmed. Living public scholar. Stick to the books and papers.

## What later people kept

Permission for a high-school SLP to sit in a department meeting and talk about the language of history class. A vocabulary that 2010’s ASHA roles statement could absorb: collaboration, literacy, the general education environment. What they discarded, or should have: the idea that every child must be served in the classroom or the clinician is a failure; Ehren’s public work is more careful than the slogan. What they still argue about: whether a building that wants literacy scores will fund language work that does not look like a reading program.

She is a figure of the *school as a curriculum*, not of the 1910 circuit. Gifford surveyed defects. Ehren surveyed the mismatch between a student’s language and a textbook. Both surveys invent work. Only one of them makes sense after 1997.

The residue is a teenager still on a caseload. That sentence was not obvious in 1960. It is still not obvious in some districts that dismiss at fifth grade as a habit. The habit is the thing her papers were written against.
