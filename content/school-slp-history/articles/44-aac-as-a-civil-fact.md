This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The 1975 related-services parentheses do not say “augmentative and alternative communication.” They say speech pathology. Later practice, later regulations, and later funding streams (including some Medicaid codes, some IDEA assistive-technology language, some ADA/504 access arguments) had to make a board, a device, a symbol system into something a school could owe.

This essay will not teach a device. It will not rank apps. It will say that a child who cannot use spoken language as the building’s default still has a FAPE and an LRE, and that a closet which only serves mouths is serving a 1910 census.

## From hospital cart to backpack

Early AAC in American schools often arrived as a specialist’s kit: a borrowed board, a device that lived in a locked cabinet, a one-day vendor demo. The civil fact is portability and consistency. A system that cannot go to lunch, to the bus, or to July is not a language. It is a prop. ESY (essay 32) and transition (essay 33) are where that sentence becomes a legal problem, not only a clinical disappointment.

Rowley was about an interpreter for a child who already had spoken and residual-hearing access. AAC fights are often about children who were never going to be Amy Rowley. Endrew F.’s “appropriately ambitious” is the later adjective teams reach for when last year’s device goal was “will tolerate the board” for the third time.

## Who paid, and who decided

Assistive technology under IDEA is a consideration the IEP team must make. It is not automatically a particular brand. Districts fear the expensive device and the cheaper neglect. Families fear the device that stays at school. 504 and ADA sometimes enter when the issue is access to the program as a program, not only specially designed instruction. The three-statute bulletin board (essay 34) reappears with a charger.

Speech-language pathologists became, in many buildings, the default AT people without being AT engineers. Occupational therapists, assistive-technology specialists, and teachers of the deaf are the other chairs. A history that makes AAC “the SLP’s gadget” is a staffing convenience, not a complete census.

## Who was left out

Children whose behavior label hid a missing language system. Children whose families were told to wait for speech to “come in.” Children whose home language is not the language on the overlay. Children who received a device and no partner training — which is not a protocol we will write, only a common way a purchase fails.

COVID (essay 22) made the cabinet problem obvious: devices that lived at school could not go home, and home broadband could not carry a dedicated system. Some children lost their language for a year because the language was property of a closet. That is a civil sentence, not a tech-support sentence.

The residue is a civil object that looks like plastic. The 1910 correctionist could not have billed it. The 1975 statute did not name it. The modern IEP that skips it for a child who has no other way into the curriculum is skipping LRE in hardware form. This series will not name the hardware. It will name the skip. Garret F. taught districts that expensive support can still be related. A communication system is cheaper than a nurse and still easy to treat as optional. The option is the failure.
