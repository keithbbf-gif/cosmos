This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The U.S. Reports caption is *Endrew F., a minor, by and through his parents and next friends, Joseph F. and Jennifer F. v. Douglas County School District RE-1*. The Court does not print the family’s surname. This pack follows the caption. Endrew is a child with autism who attended public school in Douglas County, Colorado. His parents saw IEPs that, in their view, repeated themselves. They placed him in a private school, saw progress they had not seen, and asked the district to pay. The Tenth Circuit said the public IEP was enough because it aimed at a benefit “merely more than de minimis.”

On 22 March 2017, Chief Justice Roberts, for a unanimous Court, reversed that standard. To meet IDEA’s substantive FAPE duty, a school must offer an IEP reasonably calculated to enable the child to make progress appropriate in light of the child’s circumstances. The IEP is a prospective plan, not a court’s ideal. It must aim at progress — academic and functional. For a child fully integrated in the regular classroom, *Rowley*’s picture (passing marks, advancement) still informs what “appropriate” often looks like. For a child who is not, grade-level advancement need not be the aim if it is not a reasonable prospect — but the program must still be appropriately ambitious. The Tenth Circuit’s test was “markedly” too low. Instruction that aims that low is “tantamount to sitting idly” until the child is old enough to drop out.

## Why school speech files felt this the next week

Because a speech goal that never changes except the date now has a Supreme Court adjective against it: *ambitious*, in light of the child. Because ASHA’s workload page quietly added the case next to the 2002 position statement. Because “some benefit” as a professional shrug became harder to say in a meeting without someone quoting Roberts.

Because the confirmation hearing of Neil Gorsuch, the same week, dragged in his 2008 Tenth Circuit opinion in *Thompson R2-J School District v. Luke P.*, which had used the “merely more than de minimis” formulation. That is political weather around the caption, not a fact about Endrew. The holding does not depend on it. A history that skips it is cleaner. A history that mentions it is honest about why the decision was on television by lunch.

## What the opinion is not

It is not a formula. Roberts said so. It is not a guarantee of private-school reimbursement on these facts; the Court remanded the standard, not a check. It is not a speech-language protocol. It is not permission to print Endrew’s face or full name. It is not *Rowley* overruled. It is *Rowley* read against a generation of minimalism.

Parents heard “we won a higher standard.” Districts heard “we already write ambitious IEPs.” Both sentences can be empty. The residue is a quote a team can put on a wall and still have to write Tuesday’s service. School speech-language pathologists, who live on repeated sessions, are the people most tempted to confuse repetition with a plan. Endrew F. is the caption that makes that confusion more expensive.
