This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

On 29 November 1975, S. 6 became Public Law 94-142, 89 Stat. 773, the Education for All Handicapped Children Act. The American Presidency Project’s copy of Gerald Ford’s statement is dated 2 December. He had already signed. He wanted the record to show that he thought the bill promised more than the federal government would deliver, that the paperwork would eat the money, and that there would be time — full effect not until fiscal 1978 — to rewrite it. Later special-education lawyers like to say there was no ceremony. The statement is the ceremony: a grudging signature and a warning.

The Act’s four purposes, as the Department of Education still reprints them, were: a free appropriate public education that emphasizes special education and related services; protection of children’s and parents’ rights; help to states and localities; and a way to measure whether the effort worked. The findings named two groups: more than a million children excluded entirely, and a larger group with only limited access.

## What the 1975 text handed the speech office

Definition (17) said related services were transportation and “developmental, corrective, and other supportive services (including speech pathology and audiology, psychological services, physical and occupational therapy, recreation, and medical and counseling services, except that such medical services shall be for diagnostic and evaluation purposes only)” as required to help a handicapped child benefit from special education, plus early identification. The parentheses are the century. Speech pathology is named. It is named as support for special education, not as a hobby of the English department.

Definition (16) said special education was specially designed instruction at no cost to parents. Definition (18) said FAPE was special education and related services, public, without charge, meeting state standards, including preschool through secondary as the state provided, in conformity with an individualized education program. The IEP is already in the 1975 furniture. So is the idea that a related service exists to help the child *benefit from* special education. That “benefit” sentence will go to the Supreme Court in 1982 and 2017.

States could treat speech pathology itself as special education when it was the child’s specially designed instruction. That option is why a child whose only service is speech can still be a “child with a disability” on the child count. Stan Dublinske spent a career explaining that sentence to people who wanted a failing reading score first. Essay 37 is his letterhead.

## What did not happen on signing day

No caseload number. No ASHA card requirement. No promise that every closet would become a room. No overnight staff. The 1976–77 child count, on the Department’s history page, is 3,694,000 students aged 3–21. The speech workforce did not multiply on 29 November. It inherited a legal duty and a form.

The Act is an amendment to the older Education of the Handicapped Act (1966). It is a funding bargain with conditions, not a constitutional holding. States could, in theory, refuse the money. In practice the money and the norm arrived together. By the time the law was fully effective, the itinerant of 1910 had become a person whose schedule could be exhibited in a hearing.

Ford’s complaint about paperwork is the one sentence every later reauthorization still answers and never quite kills. The 2004 Act will advertise reduction. The folder will not get thinner.

## The name on the door

People said “94-142” the way they later said “IDEA.” The number was the culture. Speech teachers learned it in workshops. Parents learned it from other parents. The association learned it as a survival subject. Dublinske’s 1981 USC dissertation is the school-services director going back to school to measure the impact. The statute had already measured the job.
