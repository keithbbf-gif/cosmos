This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

American public schools stop in summer. Children with disabilities do not stop needing language, or a device, or a routine that holds a skill. The 1975 Act did not print “extended school year.” Courts and then regulations had to say that FAPE can include services beyond the regular calendar when the child’s program would otherwise be inappropriate.

The vernacular that stuck is regression and recoupment: will the child lose critical skills over the break, and will the loss take too long to get back? Some circuits and state rules use that pair. Others warn that it is too narrow — that emerging skills, the nature of the disability, or the break’s length can matter even when last August’s scores came back by October. This page will not pick a circuit’s test for a particular child. It will say the civic object is a summer that does not undo the year.

## Why speech files feel July

Spoken language and AAC use are easy to leave at school. A device that stays in a locked closet from June to August is a policy failure with a cable. Articulation skills, in the old correctionist picture, were supposed to be “drilled” until they held. Language, for a child who only practices it with one adult in one room, may not hold. ESY arguments in speech are often arguments about whether the skill lives in the child’s life or only in the closet.

Districts fear a summer program that becomes a de facto extra semester for everyone. The law does not require that. It requires an individual look. Teams that use a single cutoff score to deny every speech ESY request are running a policy, not an IEP. Teams that offer two weeks of random groups to every child as a peace offering are also not reading the individual.

## Who summer leaves out

Working parents who cannot deliver a child to a two-hour window in a distant building. Rural children. Children whose ESY is written and then unstaffed. The itinerant model in July is a thinner itinerant. Related-service ESY is often the first line cut when the nurse and the bus already cost the earth.

Private-school summers and camp brochures are not ESY. Reimbursement fights about those placements are a different caption. This essay stays with the public calendar.

Some states publish ESY eligibility worksheets. This pack will not reprint them. A worksheet can be a memory aid or a forbidden single test, depending on whether a team is allowed to look up from it. Regression data from last August is useful and not a crystal ball. Emerging skills that have no last August yet are why some courts refused to make recoupment the only door.

The residue is a May meeting that feels like a second IEP. School speech-language pathologists learn to bring a year’s data and still be asked for a crystal ball. Rowley and Endrew F. are both in that room: not maximization, not trivial progress, a plan reasonably calculated for this child. Summer is just the plan’s hardest month. The 1910 itinerant did not have to write July. She also did not have to answer a hearing officer about it. The modern itinerant writes July because FAPE does not take a recess even when the building does. A bus and a nurse in July are related-services problems Tatro and Garret F. already explained in the regular year. Speech in July is the quieter sibling of those captions.
