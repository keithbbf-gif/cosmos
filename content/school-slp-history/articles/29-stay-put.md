This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Stay-put is a short statutory command with a long hallway life. During the pendency of proceedings, the child remains in the then-current educational placement unless the parents and the agency agree otherwise. The 1975 Act put it there so a district could not “win” a dispute by moving the child first and litigating afterward. *Honig v. Doe* (1988) said Congress meant it, even when the building was afraid.

## What “placement” includes when you hold a bag

For a speech-language pathologist, stay-put is not only a classroom door. It is the last agreed package: minutes, location, group size, the consult to the teacher. A district that cuts speech from six times a month to zero “until we rewrite the IEP” during a fight is attempting a change of placement. A parent who refuses every offer and also demands a new private school at public expense is testing the other edge: what is the *current* placement if the last agreed IEP is a year stale and the child is already elsewhere?

Later cases and commentaries argue those edges. This essay will not pick a winner for a particular file. It will say the civic design is conservative in the old sense: keep the child where the last agreement left her while the next agreement is made or a hearing officer speaks.

## The ten-day valve and the later code

Honig allowed a short traditional suspension. Later IDEA amendments built a more elaborate valve: manifestation determinations, special rules for weapons and drugs and serious bodily injury, interim alternative educational settings. Administrators learned a new calendar. Speech schedules learned to follow the child into the interim setting — or to document why they did not, which is a legal risk this page will not coach.

Stay-put can feel, to a tired teacher, like a rule that protects a dangerous situation. Brennan’s answer was: go to court, do not freelance. To a tired parent, stay-put can feel like a rule that traps a child in a failing program. The answer there is to seek an agreement or a hearing, not to pretend the statute is a suggestion. Both tired people are why the rule exists.

## Who stay-put leaves in the closet

The child whose last IEP was thin. Stay-put preserves a bad program as faithfully as a good one. That is not a reason to abolish it. It is a reason Endrew F. matters on the substance side. Procedure holds the chair still. Substance is supposed to make the chair worth holding.

It also leaves in place a service the building has quietly stopped staffing. A stay-put right to three sessions a week is a paper right if the only clinician is on medical leave and no contractor has been called. Courts have had to say that implementation is part of FAPE. Stay-put without a body in the room is a caption without a child.

Private unilateral placement muddies the noun. If the parent has already moved the child, “current educational placement” may be the last agreed public IEP, not the new private chair. Courts have spent decades on that sentence. A speech contractor who shows up at the private school “because stay-put” may be in the wrong building. The statute is a pause, not a GPS.

The residue is a pause button. School speech work, which loves motion — fade, dismiss, increase, change the model — has to stop when the legal fight starts. The 1910 itinerant did not have a pause button. She had a principal. The pause button is an improvement and a frustration. Both can be true in the same week. Honig’s ten days and 1997’s later valves are the only legal fidgets. Everything else is an agreement or a gavel.
