This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A 1910 Chicago speech teacher needed whatever the board of education required of teachers. A 1975 related-service provider needed whatever the state education agency put in its 94-142 plan. A 1990s hire in many states needed three pieces of paper: a department-of-education certificate (or a pupil-personnel / related-service credential), a department-of-health or speech-pathology board license, and, if the district or the Medicaid plan wanted it, the ASHA Certificate of Clinical Competence.

They are three tickets to three rooms. Mixing them is how a job posting lies and how a parent gets told “she’s not qualified” about the wrong ticket.

## The education ticket

This is the oldest. It says the person may work in *this state’s public schools*. It may require a master’s, a practicum in schools, a test, a background check. Some states still let a teacher with an add-on endorsement do the job. Some require the same license the clinic requires plus a school orientation. NCLB’s “highly qualified” weather (essay 16) briefly made secretaries treat this ticket as if it were a core-content teaching certificate. It is not.

## The license

State licensure of speech-language pathology is a late-century health-profession project. It is about title protection and private practice and, increasingly, telepractice across lines. A school employee in some states is exempt from the health license if she holds the education credential. In other states she is not. The Audiology & Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact is a 2020s attempt to make the license portable. March 2020 (essay 22) made the portability problem obvious.

## The ASHA card

The CCC-SLP is a private association credential, born of the academy that began in 1925 and of later certification standards. It is not a government license. Many districts require it anyway. Many Medicaid plans treat it as a quality proxy. ASHA’s advocacy for the card in schools is both a standards project and a membership project. Both can be true. A history that pretends the CCC is in the 1975 Act is inventing a statute.

## Who the three tickets leave out

Rural districts that cannot hire a person who holds all three. Clinicians trained abroad. Assistants, whose supervision rules are another ticket pile. People who have the skills and not the exam date. Children in those districts. The compact and the assistant category are attempts to widen the door without erasing a standard. Whether they do is a live policy fight, not a settled history.

Medicaid (essay 21) often wants the license or the CCC as a billing proxy. A district that hires on the education ticket alone may then discover the claim will not pay. That discovery is how a third ticket becomes mandatory without a legislature saying so. Ford’s second pocket has a dress code.

The residue is a personnel file thicker than the 1910 teacher’s. Gifford trained her own staff because no normal school yet taught the job. A modern HR office will not accept that sentence. The tickets are how a related service became a profession inside a school. They are also how a vacancy stays a vacancy from September to May. A child-find duty that cannot staff the finder is a duty on paper. The 1975 Act assumed a person would exist. The three tickets are how the person is allowed to exist. They are not how she is paid enough to stay.
