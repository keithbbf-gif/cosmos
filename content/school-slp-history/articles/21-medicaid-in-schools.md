This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Medicaid is a health-insurance program, jointly federal and state, born in 1965. Schools are education agencies. For a long time those two sentences did not share a ledger. Then Congress and the Health Care Financing Administration (later CMS) made it possible, especially after late-1980s technical changes, for schools to seek Medicaid payment for covered services provided to eligible children — including, in many states, speech-language pathology that is medically necessary and already on an IEP.

The civic idea was simple: if Medicaid would have paid a clinic, it should not refuse the same service because the room is a school. The practical idea, for boards of education, was also simple: IDEA promised services and never fully paid for them. Ford had said as much in 1975. Medicaid looked like a second pocket.

## What the second pocket cost the closet

Notes. Parental consent. A National Provider Identifier. Supervision rules that follow the state Medicaid plan, not only the education code. Random moments when a clinician is told that a session “doesn’t look billable” and therefore, in a panicked building, “doesn’t look necessary.” Those two adjectives are not synonyms. IDEA necessity is educational. Medicaid necessity is medical. A district that lets the billing adjective run the IEP has swapped statutes.

The 2013 IDEA regulations tightened parental consent and notice when a public agency wants to access a parent’s public benefits or insurance. The Department’s history page records that revision. Families had been surprised to find a Medicaid clock running on a school service they thought was simply FAPE. FAPE is still without charge to the parent. Billing Medicaid is a recovery from a third party, with rules. This page will not write those rules as a how-to. They change by state. They are also easy to get wrong in a way that harms a family’s benefits. That is why the 2013 notice exists.

## Who was left out

Uninsured children. Children whose families refused consent. Children whose service was educationally necessary and not “medical” in the state plan’s eyes. Undocumented children, depending on the state’s Medicaid reach. A program that funds the billable hour can quietly defund the unbillable one — consultation, teacher coaching, the AAC trial that did not produce a code. ASHA’s workload argument (essay 18) is, in part, a protest against that quiet defunding.

Random Medicaid audits became part of school-speech folklore in the 1990s and 2000s. Folklore is not a source. The public fact is that education agencies became health-care billers without becoming hospitals. The mismatch produced both legitimate recoveries and ugly incentives.

## What this is not

It is not permission to treat a school SLP as a hospital outpatient department. It is not a reason to put a diagnosis on a child to make a claim. It is not legal or billing advice. A later editor who wants a state-specific explainer should commission one from someone who reads that state’s plan. This pack stays with the historical turn: the closet gained a ledger.

The residue is a session note that has to satisfy a teacher, a parent, an IEP, and an auditor. Van Riper’s clinic note did not have that fourth reader. The 1910 itinerant did not either. The fourth reader is a 1988-and-after creature. Some days it pays for a second clinician. Some days it is why the first clinician stays late.
