This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Letitia Elwood Raubicheck became director of speech improvement for the New York City schools in 1928, succeeding a line of supervisors Duchan names in the 2010 *LSHSS* paper. Before that she had chaired the elocution department at Julia Richman High School. The job title still carried the nineteenth century. The work, by 1928, was a city bureau: specialists, centers, a count of pupils.

Duchan, citing a 1939 *New York Times* report, puts the program under Raubicheck at 38 specialists, 250 centers, and 27,000 pupils. Those are newspaper numbers, not a ledger this pack has re-added. They are enough to say the scale: this was not a principal’s side class at Public School 166. It was a system.

## The books

*Voice and Speech Problems* (Prentice-Hall, 1931), with Estelle Headley Davis and Lydia Adele Carll. “Speech Improvement Program for Elementary Schools,” *Quarterly Journal of Speech*, 1934. *Teaching Speech in Secondary Schools* (Prentice-Hall, 1935). *How to Teach Good Speech in the Elementary Schools* (Noble and Noble, 1937). The titles tell the theory: *good speech* and *improvement* sit next to *problems*. The classroom teacher is an audience. Raubicheck wrote for the building, not only for the specialist.

Library reprint headers give 1892–1981. This pack uses those dates and flags them as catalog facts pending a necrology. A later pass should pin a death notice. Uncertainty is a fact.

## What later people kept

The idea that a huge district needs a supervisor, not only itinerants. The idea that secondary students have a speech life, not only first-graders with a lisp. What they discarded, or should have: *good speech* as a moral weather, the easy slide from disability to elocution, the city-count that can become a factory. What they still argue about: whether a speech-improvement program for the whole elementary school is prevention or a way to avoid identifying children who need an IEP. 1975 made that argument legal. Raubicheck was already running the program as education.

She is not in the Hotel McAlpin story as a founder in the sibling pack’s sense. She is the school-administration twin of that academy: the person who had to staff Tuesday. Duchan’s 2002 *Leader* piece reminds readers that nine of the 1925 charter members already sat in school programs. Raubicheck’s New York is the scale those members were pointing at.
