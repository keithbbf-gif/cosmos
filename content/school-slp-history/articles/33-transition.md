This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The Department of Education’s IDEA history page groups the 1983 amendments (Pub. L. 98-199), the 1990 Act (Pub. L. 101-476), and the 1997 amendments as the years transition became a national project: identify post-school objectives, refer to community agencies, link to job placement and follow-up, and write down who is responsible for each activity. 1990 is the year most school people remember the individual transition plan becoming a required IEP object. Later regulations set the age — 16 in federal law, earlier in some states — when the page must appear.

The high-school speech-language pathologist of 1978 could still write as if the child would be in the closet forever. After 1990, the closet had an exit sign.

## What transition asked of a communication specialist

Not a job-coaching protocol. A look at the language of the next room: a workplace, a community college, a group home, a bus route, a benefits interview. AAC that cannot leave the building is a transition failure. A teen who can pass an articulation probe and cannot request a clarification in a noisy job is a transition failure. Those sentences are observations about the statute’s horizon, not homework for a parent.

1997’s general-curriculum emphasis and 2004’s accountability weather pulled some transition pages toward diploma-or-bust. The Department’s own exit numbers (52 percent regular diploma among known leavers 14–21 in 1994–95; 72.7 percent in 2017–18) are agency facts with definitions that shift. They are not a speech outcome measure. They are why administrators wanted every related service to look like English class.

## Who the page left out

Students who age out without a meeting that included them. Students whose transition plan is a photocopied list of agencies. Students in rural counties where the “community agency” is a two-hour drive. Students whose families fear adult services more than they fear a familiar high school. Students whose communication disability was treated as elementary and never rewritten for sixteen.

Part C to Part B at age three is also a transition, and families often experience it as a cliff. This essay’s center is the later cliff: school to not-school. The earlier cliff is in essay 12.

## What this is not

It is not a career-counseling session. It is not a promise of employment. IDEA transition is a planning duty, not a job guarantee. Adult service systems (vocational rehabilitation, developmental-disability waivers) have their own doors and waiting lists. Mixing those doors into one cheerful paragraph is how a transition page becomes fiction.

The student’s own voice on the page is the part teams still skip. 1997 and later regulations pushed for the child to be invited when transition is discussed. A speech-language pathologist who has spent years talking *about* a student is not automatically good at making space *for* the student. AAC users are the test of that space. A meeting that talks over a device is a meeting that has already failed the horizon.

The residue is a required conversation about after. School speech work, which began as a correction of recitation, ends — if it is paying attention — as a question about whether the person can make themselves understood in a life the district will not run. The 1910 teacher did not have to ask. The 1990 statute said someone must. The someone is a team. The SLP is on it when communication is the thing that will fail first. Diploma percentages will not tell you if that sentence was kept.
