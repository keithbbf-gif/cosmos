This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Amber Tatro was a child with spina bifida in the Irving Independent School District, Texas. She needed clean intermittent catheterization every few hours. Without it she could not stay at school. The district provided some services and refused to provide CIC, calling it a medical service the 1975 Act had left out except for diagnosis and evaluation. The Supreme Court, in *Irving Independent School District v. Tatro*, 468 U.S. 883 (1984), disagreed.

Chief Justice Burger’s opinion set a bright line that school nurses still live inside. If a physician must perform the procedure, it is a medical service (and, except for diagnosis/evaluation, not a related service). If a nurse or a trained layperson can perform it, it may be a related service when it is necessary for the child to benefit from special education. CIC, the Court said, met that test. Amber could not sit in the classroom without it. The service was supportive in the statute’s sense, like the bus in the same definition.

## Why the speech closet keeps this caption

Because speech pathology is on the same list as the service Irving did not want to give. The list is not a hierarchy of prestige. It is a category of supports that make special education possible. A district that later says “we don’t do that, we’re a school, not a hospital” is speaking Irving’s lines. The Court already answered, for CIC.

Because the bright line is also a limit. A later team cannot drag every health need onto the related-services page by calling it “supportive.” Physician-only care stays on the medical side. Garret F. (1999) will test whether *continuous* nursing still fits the nurse side of the line. It will. Essay 26.

Because Tatro is a reminder that the 1975 parentheses were about a child’s day, not about a profession’s identity. Catheterization is not speech work. The definition that includes both is the definition that funds the closet.

## What the opinion is not

It is not a nursing manual. It is not a judgment about Amber’s inner life. It is not a holding that every health procedure is FAPE. It is not a blank check on cost — though Garret F. will say cost is not the test for crossing the line. It is a reading of “medical services” in a list that already feared becoming a hospital statute.

Parents of children with complex health needs still quote Tatro in IEP meetings. School boards still hire nurses because of it. Speech-language pathologists sit down the hall from those nurses and share a legal ancestor. The ancestor is a child who needed to stay in the building through the afternoon.

Irving, Texas, is not Peekskill. The two captions together — Rowley and Tatro — are the early 1980s Court teaching districts that FAPE has a ceiling (not maximization) and related services have a floor (not whatever the board finds convenient to call medical). School speech work lives between that ceiling and that floor.
