This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The 1910 speech teacher was a board employee. The 2020s speech-language pathologist in a public school might be an employee, a staffing-agency contractor, a telepractice vendor in another state, or a private clinician the district pays for a particular child. Parents, meanwhile, may hire someone next door and still expect the IEP to talk to that someone. Private schools have a child-find relationship with the public district and a thinner “equitable services” duty, not FAPE, after the 2006 regulation shift to the LEA where the private school sits.

This is not a business guide. It is the last civic fact in the series: the school job has never been only a school paycheck.

## Why districts buy a person by the hour

Vacancies. Rural maps. Medicaid-eligible hours. A sudden bilingual need. A child whose device exceeds the staff’s training. Cost, sometimes, against a benefits package. The contractor can be excellent and still be a stranger to the faculty meeting 1997 required. Workload (essay 18) gets worse when the building’s memory walks out every June. Three tickets (essay 41) get weirder when the contractor’s license is from a compact state and the education certificate is a waiver.

WOW Therapies, the household brand that will one day host this series, sits on this side of the street as a practice, not as a district. These essays do not recruit for it. They note, once, that the private door is part of the public history. Families have always bought help the board did not give. 1975 made some of that help a district duty. It did not make the private door vanish.

## Parentally placed private school children

Child find still applies. FAPE does not, in the same way, if the parent placed the child privately and declined a public offer. Equitable services are a proportionate share, often a thinner speech schedule, often delivered at a public site or by contract. Families hear “we found your child” and then meet a smaller program. The 2006 shift of responsibility to the private school’s district was an attempt to stop children falling between two residences. It also moved contracts.

Reimbursement cases — a parent who places privately and asks the public district to pay — are a different legal object (Endrew F. began as one). This essay will not coach that filing.

## Who the two-door system leaves out

Children whose parents cannot pay the next-door clinician while they wait for a district hire. Children whose contractor changes three times in a year. Children in private schools whose equitable-services speech is a thirty-minute group once a month and a brochure. Children whose IEP and whose private therapist are writing two different centuries.

Telepractice vendors after 2020 (essay 22) are a new species of next-door: the door is a laptop. Licensure compact rules and FERPA agreements with the vendor are the new furniture. A child can now have a public IEP implemented by a person the teacher has never met. That can be the only available FAPE. It can also be a way to staff a building without joining it. 1997’s general-educator chair assumes a person who can come to the meeting. A vendor in another time zone is a stress test of that chair.

The residue is a street. On one side, a statute, a closet, a child count. On the other, a clinic with a lockbox and a different clock. School-based SLP history is the story of the first side learning it is a civil office. The second side is older than 94-142 and will outlive the next reauthorization. A magazine that pretends they never speak is lying. A magazine that turns this paragraph into a booking button is also lying. The series stops at the curb.
