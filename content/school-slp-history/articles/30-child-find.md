This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

PARC’s 1971 consent decree appointed a special master to identify mentally retarded children who had been denied an education. Mills ordered the District of Columbia to find the children it had excluded. The 1975 Act nationalized that search and called it, in later regulation, child find: a state and district duty to locate, identify, and evaluate children who may have disabilities and need special education, including children who are homeless, wards of the state, or attending private school.

It is an affirmative duty. A parent should not have to invent the school system. In practice, parents still do most of the inventing. The legal fact is that “we didn’t know” is a weak district sentence if the child was in the building failing, or at home after a quiet exclusion, or in a daycare the district never asked.

## The speech office as a finder

School speech-language pathologists have always been finders. The 1910 survey of “defects” is a proto-child-find with a narrower and uglier noun. Kindergarten screenings, teacher referrals, the nurse who hears a voice, the reading specialist who notices a child cannot follow a three-step direction — those are the modern search party. ASHA’s workload papers include these hours because they are hours. A caseload cap that ignores child find treats the search as a hobby.

Private-school child find, after the 2006 regulation shift, sits with the district where the private school is located. Equitable services are a different, thinner duty than FAPE. Mixing them is how a family is told “we found your child” and then “we do not owe the same program.” Essay 45.

## What child find is not

It is not a diagnosis. A screening is not an evaluation. An evaluation is not an IEP. The pediatric milestone sibling pack spends a whole essay on that triangle for parents of babies. The school version adds a fourth point: eligibility. A child can be found, evaluated, and still not be a “child with a disability” under IDEA if the team, following the statute, so decides. 504 may still apply. This page will not run that decision. It will say the search cannot stop at the children who are easy to serve.

It is not a permission to delay. RTI and MTSS (essay 43) are supports. They are not a waiting room that replaces child find. Federal guidance has had to repeat that sentence because buildings like waiting rooms.

## Who the search still misses

Children who move. Children who are home without a parent who can navigate a website. Children whose disability looks like “behavior.” Children whose home language is not the language of the referral form. Adolescents who have learned to be quiet. The 1970 census — one in five educated — is the ghost. Child find is the statute’s apology to that census. An apology that does not staff the search is a press release.

Homelessness and foster care are named in later child-find language because those children move across LEA lines the way the 1910 itinerant moved across buildings. A screening calendar that only touches stable kindergarteners is a calendar for the easy half of the census.

The residue is a calendar of screenings and a legal risk if the calendar is a sham. The speech teacher of 1916 counted defects for a superintendent’s report. The SLP of 2004 counts referrals because a hearing officer will ask when the district first had reason to suspect. Same bag. Different audience for the number. RTI’s graph (essay 43) is not a substitute audience. It is a possible tool inside a search that must still be able to say *we looked, we found, we evaluated, we decided*.
