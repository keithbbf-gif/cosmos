This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

On 3 December 2004, the Individuals with Disabilities Education Improvement Act, Public Law 108-446, became the version of IDEA people still mean when they say “current law,” pending a reauthorization that has not arrived. The Department of Education’s history page says the 2004 Act aligned IDEA with No Child Left Behind, called for early intervening services, greater accountability, and raised standards for special-education instructors. The 2006 regulations then told schools to use research-based interventions when assisting students with learning difficulties or determining eligibility, and added a resolution session when a parent files a due-process complaint.

## Alignment is a political word

NCLB (signed 8 January 2002) had already made the regular school a testing machine. 2004 tried to keep children with disabilities from being a separate, quieter machine. Highly qualified teachers, adequate yearly progress, scientifically based research — those NCLB nouns walked into IEP meetings and into the way some districts wanted speech goals to sound like reading interventions. Sometimes that was a useful demand for evidence. Sometimes it was a demand that a communication goal pretend to be a DIBELS score.

“Improvement” in the short title is a Congress mood. Practitioners remember paperwork reduction language and then the folder that did not shrink. The resolution session is a new waiting room before a hearing. Early intervening services (EIS) are a permission to use a slice of Part B money for children *not* identified as needing special education but who need academic or behavioral support. That slice is the legal ancestor of many RTI/MTSS speech experiments. Essay 43 is the door those experiments built — and the delay they can become.

## Specific learning disability and the missing discrepancy

2004 is famous in psychology offices for allowing states to drop the IQ-achievement discrepancy as the required way to find a specific learning disability, and to use a process based on the child’s response to scientific, research-based intervention. Speech-language pathologists were pulled into that process as language people, because a “learning disability” in school English is often a language disability in clinic English. Nickola Wolf Nelson’s curriculum-based language work had been arguing that for years. Essay 40.

This page will not tell a team how to run that process. It will say that 2004 changed the *permission structure*. Districts that had waited for a discrepancy number could now wait for a response-to-intervention graph instead. Waiting is the moral risk. The statute and later guidance say the process must not delay a full evaluation when one is needed. Practice has not always heard that sentence.

## Private-school equitable services

The 2006 regulations also shifted responsibility for equitable services to parentally placed private-school children to the LEA where the private school sits, not where the child lives. Speech contracts moved. Essay 45 is that neighborhood.

Personnel: the Department’s page counts 695,466 teachers and related-service personnel in 2005–06 and 942,446 in 2017–18. The speech line is inside those totals, not identical with them. Growth is not the same as a manageable workload. The 2002 ASHA workload statement was already two years old when 2004 passed. The Act did not enact it.

The residue of 2004 is a statute still waiting for a successor and a profession still arguing about graphs. Alignment with a testing law that ESSA later softened (essay 17) left a taste in the speech room: prove it, count it, do not be the reason the building misses AYP. Some of that pressure was civic. Some of it was panic with a spreadsheet.
