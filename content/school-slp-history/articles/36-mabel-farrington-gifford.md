This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Judy Felson Duchan’s public biography of Mabel Farrington Gifford opens on a person who stuttered, who spent 1901–1903 at the Natural Instruction Institute (also called the Corrective Speech Institute) in Buffalo, who later trained at the Scripture clinic at Columbia, and who worked in Minnesota schools for the deaf and for people then labeled “feeble-minded.” In 1915–1940 she was also a speech therapist at the University of California Medical School in San Francisco. The school story starts in 1916.

The San Francisco Board of Education, having seen her demonstrations at the Panama-Pacific Exposition, asked her to organize a public-school program. She became Supervisor of Speech Correction. A first-year survey found 1,486 children with “speech defects.” She had one assistant. The 1926 California superintendent’s report, which Duchan quotes at length, describes five centers, a half-day assembly, twenty-minute follow-up drills, two classes (stammerers/stutterers/cluttering; lispers and “infantile” speech), and, in 1917, high-school work that included “the correction of foreign pronunciation.” Cross’s 1961 thesis says she rode east in 1917 to watch Minneapolis, Saint Paul, Detroit, Chicago, Boston, Newark, and New York.

## The bureau

In the late 1920s California created a state bureau for the correction of speech defects. Duchan dates Gifford’s chiefship 1927–1940. Some secondary pages say the bureau began in 1926, with a gift from S. Waldo Coleman that helped subsidize a statewide program. This pack treats 1927 as Duchan’s date for the chief’s title and 1926 as the year some reports use for the bureau’s birth, and refuses to pretend those two sentences have been reconciled in one ledger. By 1930, one California study says, thirty-three districts had classes.

She taught everywhere a later CV can still list: San Francisco State, University of California extension and summer sessions, San Diego State, even San Quentin (1928–1935). She wrote *Speech Defects and Disorders and Their Correction* (1926), *Correcting Nervous Speech Disorders* (1939), *How to Overcome Stammering* (1940), and *Speech Correction in the Elementary School* (California State Department of Education, 1948). She was an ASHA charter member and vice president in 1931.

## What later people kept

A state-level school speech office. A survey-first habit. The honest residue of a person who had been a patient of institutes and then built a public system. What they discarded, or should have: home practice schedules presented as if a magazine should reprint them (this one will not); “foreign pronunciation” as a defect; “nervous speech disorders” as a sack that hid more than it explained. What they still argue about: whether a statewide bureau civilizes the closet or standardizes a thin model.

She is one of the school administrators Duchan counts among the 1925 academy’s charter world. The sibling profession pack may name her in a list. This profile is the California route: Exposition, survey, train, bureau, prison lecture, elementary bulletin. New sentences. Same person.
