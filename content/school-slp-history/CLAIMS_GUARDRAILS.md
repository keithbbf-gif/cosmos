# Claims Guardrails — School-Based SLP History & Policy

This pack is educational history and civic policy. It is not a treatment manual, not an IEP clinic, and not a law office.

## Hard refusals

- No protocols, cue hierarchies, session plans, or “try this if your child…”
- No eligibility decision trees a parent could run at the kitchen table.
- No Medicaid billing steps, NPI advice, or “how to code a school visit.”
- No due-process tactics, sample complaint language, or “what to say at the meeting.”
- No living student’s name except the public captions already in reporters.
- No generated faces. No downloaded portraits in this pass.

## Historical job titles and “who was first”

Judy Felson Duchan, “The Early Years of Language, Speech, and Hearing Services in U.S. Schools,” *Language, Speech, and Hearing Services in Schools* 41 (2010): 152–160 (advance 2009), is the preferred secondary for city hiring dates. She places Chicago’s ten teachers and Detroit’s two speech centers in 1910, New York experiments from 1908/1911, and most major cities’ first clinicians between 1895 and 1921. Local theses (Cross on San Francisco, 1961) sometimes claim an earlier “first.” When they disagree, the article says so. Do not invent a single American origin city.

## Medical and educational performance

The OSERS letter to Stan Dublinske on “adversely affects educational performance” is a public policy interpretation: a child with a speech or language impairment is not automatically ineligible because report-card numbers look fine. Cite the letter. Do not turn it into an eligibility worksheet.

## Case children

Amy Rowley, Amber Tatro, Garret Frey, and Endrew F. are public litigants. Use the facts the opinions recites. Do not invent classrooms, pets, or inner lives. Endrew’s full surname is not used in the U.S. Reports caption; this pack follows the caption.

## Words that smuggle a cure or a sales pitch

Do not write “gold-standard treatment,” “self-treat,” “book with us,” or “try this at home.” Historical *speech correction* is a job title, not a promise.

## Eugenics, oralism, race, and exclusion

Early school speech programs sat beside oralist deaf education, IQ cutoffs, and statutes that kept children out of the building. Do not sanitize. Do not sermonize. Put the primary act on the page, then the later judgment.
