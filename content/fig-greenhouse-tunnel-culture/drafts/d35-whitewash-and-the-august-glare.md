August light in a dirty-clear hoop is a glare you can feel
in your neck. The film that was a gift in February is a
lens in August. Whitewash is how greenhouse people dull a
lens without building a second roof. It is ugly. It is
cheap. It wears off. Those are compliments.

Use a compound meant for film or glass. Not leftover wall
paint. Not a mystery bucket from a barn stall. I have
seen latex flake into a bed and sit there like snow that
does not melt. A real shading compound you can scrub in
September is a tool. A permanent indoor paint is a
mistake you will buy a new roll to hide.

How much: enough that you can look at the peak at noon
without cursing, not enough that the house is a cave.
You can always add a second pass. You cannot un-paint a
mood. I start thin on the south and west. I leave the
north lonelier. Figs still want sun. They do not want a
skillet on the south pots.

Zone 9 may whitewash earlier and longer. Zone 7 may skip
it in a house that opens like a barn. 8a does it in the
rude years and skips it in a mild, open summer. The
skipping year is how you forget the bucket. Keep the
bucket where the crank lives. If the crank is in your
hand at 10 a.m. and the glare is still a dare, the
bucket is next.

Wash it off when the fruit needs the last photons. Dusty
film plus leftover wash plus a 30-percent cloth you
forgot is three hats. Three hats in October is how a
late fig stays late. Scrub. A hose and a broom from the
outside, or a rain week if you timed the compound to be
wimpy. I like wimpy in 8a. I can re-apply. I cannot
re-ripen a fruit that sat in a cave.

Whitewash is not ventilation. People paint a sealed hoop
and call it a climate. They painted a car. Open the car,
then paint if you still need a hat. The order matters.
I will say it until I am tired: crank, then cloth or
wash, then water. Never water, then paint, then forget
the crank.

Glass houses take wash better than cheap film. If your
film is in year four and brittle, do not attack it with
a broom like you are mad at a driveway. You will zipper
it. Replace the roll, then wash the new one in year two
when you know the glare. Year-one film can wait and see.
Year-one plants can wait and see. August will still be
there.

I do not whitewash the winter shop. The shop wants every
honest photon it can get without climbing above 50°F. A
white shop is a cave with pots. If the shop glares, you
have too much sun for a dormancy room — add a shade
*screen* you can take down, or move the cans off the
south wall. Do not paint the only winter light you have
and then wonder why the wood never hardened. Different
rooms. Different hats.

I keep a photo of the peak at noon in June and in
August. The June photo tells me if last year’s leftover
wash is still a cloud. The August photo tells me if I
was too proud to paint. Two photos, one bucket. That is
cheaper than a new roll and ruder than a feeling.

Do not wash over tears. Patch first. Wash is not glue.
