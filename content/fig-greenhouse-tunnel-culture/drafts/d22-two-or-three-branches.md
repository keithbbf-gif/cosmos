Zhi said it until it was almost a joke, which means it was
not a joke. Extra branches, no fruit. Two or three are
enough. At most four. Keep checking the suckers. Tip in
August so the energy goes to ripening what you already
have. I have watched 8a figs do the same math outdoors. A
house just makes the thicket faster, because the plant has
a longer year and nothing to stop it.

A fig is a suckering stool if you let it be. That is useful
when winter takes the top — you get a bush and a crop on
new wood. Under plastic, if the whole point was to *keep*
wood, a stool of twelve is a waste of the house. You paid
for last year’s arms. Then you let this year’s sprouts
shade them into sticks.

I pick two or three, or I pick a cordon. I do not pick
“natural.” Natural in a hoop is a jungle by July. Air in
the middle is how rust stays a leaf problem instead of a
row problem. It is how you see the fruit. It is how a
second cover can sit on wood instead of on a pile.

August tipping is a season tool. Zhi used it. I use it on
outdoor plants that are still pushing when the fruit needs
to finish. In a house it matters more, because the house
will let a fig grow until Thanksgiving if you keep the
water on. A late flush is pretty and then it is tender
going into a night. Cut the argument off while the fruit
can still use the plant.

Zone 7 people sometimes refuse to prune because they are
scared of losing the only wood that lived. Then they lose
the fruit on that wood because the plant spent August on
new sprouts. Zone 9 people forget to prune because the
plant is a weed and “figs don’t need it.” Then the hoop is
a mite house. 8a people forget because we were in the shop
with cups. I have done that. The row does not care that
Jack had a tray.

Suckers at the base are free plants if you want them. They
are weeds if you already chose the arms. The propagation
pack can have the sucker draft. This pack wants them gone
unless you are replacing an arm. A replacement arm is a
decision. A sprout is a habit.

If the plant already is a thicket, do not “fix” it in July
with a hero cut that sunburns every inner leaf. Take extra
wood in the dormant season. In summer, only take what is
in the way of fruit and air. The house will wait one
winter. It will not wait three summers of a jungle you
were proud of in May.

Two arms look lonely in year one. They look like a crop in
year three. Lonely is the correct picture. Fill the space
with air, not with extra trunks you will later hate.

I mark the keepers with tape in May so I do not argue with
myself in July. The plant will offer a better-looking sprout
every week. The tape is the decision I already made. If a
keeper breaks, I promote a taped backup, not a random hero.
Random heroes are how a stool becomes twelve again. Two arms
and a backup bud is a plan. A thicket is the absence of tape.

If I am gone a month in summer, I ask someone to rub
suckers, not to fertilize. A month of sprouts is a
thicket. A month without feed is a fig. I know which
month I can fix.
