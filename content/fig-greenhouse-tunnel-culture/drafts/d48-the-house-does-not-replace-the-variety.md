I have spent this pack talking about steel, film, cranks,
chips, and papers. I will end on the thing the steel
cannot do. It cannot turn a late name into an early one.
It cannot turn a crowd called Brown Turkey into Gao’s
plant. It cannot turn a Smyrna into a common fig. It
cannot turn a bad stick from a bad seller into a season.

The introduction page said variety makes the difference
in flavor, quality, ripening, and whether a plant even
belongs in a region. The house is a region you invent
for a few weeks. It is not a new species. Plant the
name that already wants to finish, then let the roof
steal days. That has been the sentence since d01. I am
saying it last so it is the one you hear when you close
the folder.

Zhi evicted what did not fruit. Boyer watched Pastillier
drop and Teramo split. Gao only wanted to talk about two
names in Ohio. Those are selection stories. A house is a
selector with expensive rent. Every chair has to earn.
Outdoors you can hide a dud. Under plastic the dud is in
your face every time you walk the crank.

Zone 7: the variety *is* the winter method as much as
the chips are. Chicago Hardy as a floor. Tight-eye
Celeste types if you have heat enough to finish them.
Do not fill a gothic with late religions. Zone 9: the
variety is flavor and rust and nematodes. The house is
optional. Do not let steel make you sloppy about names.
8a: the variety is the in-between. A short list in the
ground, a museum in cans, a wall that already works.
The house, if it exists, is for the short list.

I will still grow a name I love that does not finish
most years. I will grow it in a can. I will shuffle it.
I will eat three figs and call it a holiday. I will not
call it a reason to buy 72 feet of pipe. Love is allowed
in a pot. Love is a bad orchard plan.

If you take one action after this pack, do not order a
kit. Order, or trade, or cut, two names you have tasted
or that a person with a phone number has tasted in a
climate like yours. Plant them where the hole drains and
the sun is 6–8 hours. Walk them for a year. Then ask
what failed. If what failed was October, build. If what
failed was July water, read the soil pack. If what
failed was a tag, change the tag, not the sky.

The papers I cited are honest about limits. Gao would
not cost the hoop. Zhi lost wood. Boyer cooked a row and
still told the truth. FigRoots refused indoor heat and
named the shuffle. Field practice still outruns paper.
Your yard will outrun this pack. Good. Write what the
thermometer did and what the fruit did. That pair is
the only culture I trust.

A house is a tool. A variety is the crop. Keep those
jobs in that order and you can grow a fig in Zone 7–9
without lying to yourself. Reverse them and you will
own a beautiful bag of air and a late green fruit. I
have seen the bag. I would rather see the fruit.

If this pack did its job, you can name the failure
before you name the kit. October green. July cook.
January wood. A tag that lied. Only one of those is a
steel problem. The rest are culture you already had to
do in the yard. Do that first. Then, if October is
still the thief, build. I will help you crank. I will
not help you hide a late name under a new roof and
call it a climate.
