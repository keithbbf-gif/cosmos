The cold-hardiness pack already said this, and I will say it
again because a tunnel makes people sloppy. Brown Turkey in
commerce is a crowd. Eastern Brown Turkey, Texas pot plants,
a big-box tag, a neighbor’s “the brown one,” Gao’s trial
plant, a European name that landed on a different clone —
those are not one fruit. A house will not merge them.

Gao’s Brown Turkey in Piketon was a plant they trusted enough
to recommend beside Hardy Chicago. It was larger. It was
later. Under a tunnel it still needed the extra weeks. That
is a useful sentence about *that* plant in *that* trial. It
is not a blessing on the tag in your trunk.

I have eaten Brown Turkeys that were fine and Brown Turkeys
that were a wet sponge. I have seen a “Turkey” that wanted
all of October and a “Turkey” that behaved like an early
main crop on a wall. If I put the late one in a house and
expect Gao’s September, I am lying to the steel. If I put
the early one in a house and brag that plastic made it
early, I am lying to the name.

The house makes identity *more* important, not less. Space
is limited. Two or three branches, 8–10 ft, a 14×32 that
holds a short list. Every wrong tag is a chair at a small
table. Outdoors you can hide a dud on the fence. Under
plastic the dud is in your way every time you crank the
side.

Zone 7 people grab Brown Turkey because the catalog said
hardy. Sometimes they get a plant that dies to the ground
and still fruits late on new wood. Sometimes they get a
plant that keeps wood and still does not finish. The tag
did not tell them which. Zone 9 people grab it because
every store has one. They already have a climate that can
finish a lot of names. They do not need a crowd. They need
a fruit they like. 8a should treat a Turkey as a plant to
be tasted two years in a row before it earns a permanent
hole in the house.

How I handle a crowd: I do not evict a fruit I like because
the name is messy. I evict the name from the argument. The
label on the pot becomes a code I wrote — BT-wall, BT-store,
BT-trade — and the fruit has to keep the chair. If I pass
wood, I pass the code and the caveat, not a cleaned-up
identity.

If you are filling a tunnel from a big-box spring table, you
are filling it with a crowd on purpose. Do that once, taste,
and then replace from a person with a phone number. FigRoots
already said this about cuttings and scams. The house does
not change the scam. It only makes the wasted years more
expensive per square foot.

A tunnel is a selector. It will tell you, faster than a
fence row, which plant finishes. Believe the plant. Do not
believe the word Turkey just because a paper in Ohio had a
good one. Papers have plants. Stores have words.

When I write a caption I refuse the word Turkey unless the pot
already has my code on it. A plate of brown figs is not a type.
A neighbor saying “the good brown one” is not a type. Gao’s
trial is a type *in that row*. If I pass wood, I pass the code
and the year I tasted it. That is slower than a marketplace
listing. It is how a crowd does not become a second crowd with
my name on it.
