Black Mission is a religion. I have watched people plant it
in a hoop in Zone 7 because a California packet said it was
the fig, and then they wait through a beautiful September
of green fruit. A house can move a calendar. It cannot turn
a late name into an early one just because you spent on
steel.

Mission, Kadota, a lot of the drying figs, a lot of the
“commercial California” tags — those plants evolved a year
that is not our year. Even in Zone 9 they can sulk in a
rain week or finish in a trickle. Under plastic in 8a they
are a museum piece unless you already know *your* clone
finishes on a wall. If you do not know that, do not give
the plant a chair in a 14×32.

The test is not the catalog days-to-ripe. The test is: does
this name finish in your county in a normal year on a south
wall? If yes, a house is gravy. If no, a house is a hope.
Hope is a fine pot in the shop. Hope is a bad orchard row.

I will make room for one late name in a can I can shuffle.
I will not make room for a row of them. The row is for
Chicago Hardy, Celeste types, LSU names, Ronde, a Longue
d’Aout, the early purple Zhi would have kept. The late
honey fig I love can visit. It can even ripen a few in a
kind October under film. It does not get to set the
spacing.

Zone 7 should be ruthless. October is the crop. A name that
starts in October is a name that meets frost as a green
ball. Gao watched outdoor fruit die in that meeting. The
tunnel’s job is to get *early* names through a longer
window, not to baptize a Mission. Zone 9 can play with
Mission if the rain is the only thief. Still pick a
tight-eye if August is a sluice. 8a can love Mission in a
pot and refuse it a hole.

There is a forum move where someone says “it would have
ripened if I had a greenhouse.” Sometimes that is true for
a name that was ten days short. Sometimes it is a name that
was six weeks short. A house is two to six weeks if you
operate it. It is not a new climate zone. Coleman’s double
cover as a zone move is about winter cold, not about
turning 8a into 10a for a late fig in October.

If you already planted the religion, taste two years before
you evict. Maybe your clone is an early pretender with a
famous name. Maybe it is the real late thing. The fruit
will say. The steel will not convert it. I would rather
eat a small Chicago in September than photograph a perfect
green Mission in October and write a caption about next
year. Next year the Mission will still be late. The
Chicago will still be food.

If a Mission — or any late religion — finishes for you in a
kind 8a hoop, write it down as *your* clone and *your* year.
Do not write it as a rule for a neighbor in 7A. Ten days of
house is not six weeks of climate. I will celebrate your
three ripe Missions. I will not let them set the spacing in
a house I have to crank. Holidays belong in a can.

I will dry a late fig in a kind house if it actually
finishes. Drying is not a consolation for green fruit.
Green fruit is compost. The religion can wait in a can
until the fruit is jam.
