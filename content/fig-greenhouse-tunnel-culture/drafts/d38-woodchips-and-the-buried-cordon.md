Zhi bent branches down, pinned them, used barrels and
cloth, and still lost most of the wood. The wood that
lived had been under chips. The next winter she buried on
purpose. She also said fig wood did not rot under those
chips, which is the sentence people need because they
imagine a grave. A grave is wet clay on a crown all
summer. A winter chip pile on a pinned arm is a blanket.

I will not tell you chips are sterile or that fresh chips
never heat. She thought fresh chips would kill, and they
did not, in that year, on that wood. That is one farm.
Our 8a chips from a tree company are a gift and a weed
seed and a carbon pile. I use them as a winter blanket
on wood I can pin, and I pull them back off the crown
when the plant needs to breathe in spring. A chip volcano
against a trunk in June is a different sin. The soil pack
already yelled about that.

Boyer’s version did not bury so much as tent: fabric on
hoops, ground as the battery, cordon in the warm layer.
Zhi’s version put the wood *in* the battery. Both beat
barrels-as-furnace. I would rather pin and chip in 7A
and 8a on an ugly forecast than trust a drum of water.

How I would do it on a short list: two arms I can bend
without a crack, a pin or a sandbag, chips or leaves that
are dry-ish, a marker so I do not walk the plant in
February. I do not bury a chandelier. Height is a pruning
problem because winter is a burial problem. If the arm
will not bend, it is not a cordon yet. Wait a year. Or
lose the top and keep the crown.

Zone 7: this is the method if you want wood and you will
not heat the sky. Zone 9: do not bury a fig for fun. You
will steam it in a 60°F week. 8a: bury on the headline
night, not as a November lifestyle. A kind 8a winter
under a pile is a mold story. I have left a wrap on too
long outdoors. I can imagine a chip pile doing the same
in a hoop that hits 80°F on a January Thursday. Pull a
corner. Vent the house. The blanket is for the night, not
for your identity.

Voles like a chip hotel. Boyer ran box traps all winter
and called it enough. I will not ignore a vole in a hoop.
A girdled cordon under chips is a quiet death. Walk. Look
for runs. A cat is not a plan. A trap is a plan. If you
cannot trap, keep the blanket to a short window around
the ugly nights instead of a three-month nest.

I like tree-company chips because they are free and they
are a volume you cannot buy in bags without crying. Zhi
liked that too. Free is not a reason to bury a plant you
cannot find. Mark the arm. Write the date you covered.
Write the date you uncovered. Next September you will
remember the figs and forget the pile. The vole will not
forget.

I do not bury a plant I have not walked for suckers.
A sprout under a pile is a rot or a vole straw. Clean
the stool, then pin the arms, then chip. Order matters.
A dirty bury is a hotel.

In spring I uncover on a cloudy day if I can. A sudden
sun on white wood is a sunburn I invented. The house
already makes February rude. I do not add a blast.
