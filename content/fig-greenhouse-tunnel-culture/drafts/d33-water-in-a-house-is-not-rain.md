A roof ends the argument with the sky. That is the point
for fruit. It is also a new job for you. Outdoor figs in
8a get thunderstorms whether you deserve them. Tunnel figs
get what you give them, plus the leak at the baseboard.
Zhi learned she did not need much water and that an
automatic system was not necessary. I believe the “not
much” more than I believe “never.” A fruiting plant in
July on a berm still drinks. A #3 on woven fabric drinks
like a tourist.

The soil pack already has the hose chapter: finger, not
Tuesday; deep and infrequent once the plant has a county;
do not binge before a storm; water before a hard winter
freeze on established outdoor trees. Under plastic I steal
the finger and I throw away the storm. There is no storm
on the fruit. There may still be a storm on the runoff.
Watch the floor after a three-inch rain even if the roof
held. The clay does not care that you bought film.

Drip is the house tool. Emitters at the root, a pressure
regulator, a filter if your well laughs at you. One zone
for in-ground, another for cans if they share the roof.
I have run one zone and drowned a berm to save a pot. The
pot was happy. The berm grew rust and a smell.

Fertigation belongs to the watering page on FigRoots, not
to a sealed July. If you fertigate a cooker you will grow
a jungle and a salt rim. Feed when the house is a growing
room, not when it is a car. Jack’s cups have their own
religion. The orchard row in year three can take a
soluble sip on a schedule you already published. Do not
invent a second one for the hoop.

Zone 7 under-waters in spring because they are scared of
rot in a cold floor, then over-waters in August because
the fruit looks tired. Zone 9 over-waters because the hose
is a habit and the house feels like a pot. 8a does both
in the same month. The finger is the religion. The timer
is a servant. Zhi’s students built a system and the figs
did not need the system. That is a permission to be
simple, not a permission to vanish.

Winter water is a sip. FigRoots said barely moist for pots
in the shuffle. In-ground in a cold house can sit a long
time if the fall was honest. I do not run drip on a timer
in January. I walk. If the chip pile is a dust and the
wood is wrinkling, a drink. If the floor is a dish, I
have a drainage problem, not a drought.

Split is the next draft. Here I only say: even before a
week of ripening, do not invent a drought and then a
flood under a roof. The roof already removed the sky’s
flood. Do not replace it with a hose you feel guilty
about. Guilt is a binge. The house makes guilt louder
because you cannot blame the weather. Good. Blame the
finger. Then use it.

I shut the timer off when I leave town for more than a
weekend unless someone will walk the finger. A timer
without a finger is how a berm becomes a lake and a
can becomes a bone. Two failures, one servant. I would
rather ask a neighbor to lift a pot than to trust a
dial I set in a mood.

Winter shutoff is a date I write, not a feeling. When
leaves are gone and wood is brown, the drip sleeps. A
January emitter that still clicks is a rot I paid for.
The house already stopped the sky. I can stop the hose.
