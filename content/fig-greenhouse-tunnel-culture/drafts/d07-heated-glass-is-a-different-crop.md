Boyer said it in a Pennsylvania grant report and I will say it in
Arkansas English. Historically, heating a whole high tunnel for
figs has been a waste of energy and space. You are warming air the
film will not hold, to save wood you could have laid down and
covered. I am not against heat as a night of emergency. I am
against heat as a personality.

Figs want a winter. FigRoots already refused indoor heat for that
reason. A dormancy is not a romance. It is how the wood finishes
and how the plant stops trying to be a June flush in a January
window. If you keep a glass house at 60°F because you like green
leaves, you will get green leaves and soft tips and a scale
problem, and then a 28°F night when a breaker trips will write a
uglier story than a plant that was allowed to sleep.

There is a crop that lives in heated glass. Dutch tomatoes. Some
citrus. A winter salad. Figs can be forced. People have done it.
I have seen photos of January fruit under lights. I have not seen
a backyard in 8a where that math beat a Celeste on a wall in
August. Propane is a bill. Electricity is a bill. The fig does not
pay you back in January the way a ripe main-crop fig pays you in
September.

Zone 7 is tempted hardest. The year is short. The catalog says
“greenhouse fig.” The heater is already in the barn for chicks.
Then the wood never lignifies, the buds break in a warm week, and
the late freeze — the one Zhi named after a Maryland spring — kills
the flush you paid to wake. You did not buy season. You bought an
early funeral.

Zone 9 should almost never heat a fig house. If your night is 28°F
twice, a blanket does it. Heat in a Zone 9 hoop is how you grow
fungus and a utility bill.

8a can keep a *shop* above 40°F so roots in cans do not sit in a
hard freeze. That is the shuffle sentence. It is not “run the
greenhouse like a living room.” Forty is not sixty. Forty is a
cold room with a floor. The plants look dead. That is correct.

If you have a glass house you already heat for something else, do
not add figs to the same thermostat without a wall. The tomato
setpoint will ruin the fig. Put the fig on the cold side. Put the
tomato on the warm side. Or put the fig in the garage and let the
glass keep its tomatoes. One thermostat cannot hold two religions.

I will not write a wiring diagram. Keith does money and a fire
plan. A space heater on a cord under dry film is how people make
the news. If a night is ugly enough to want heat, I would rather
bury wood under chips, or roll pots into a room that already has a
wall, than teach a hoop to be a furnace. The crop I want is a ripe
fig in a county that can already grow one. Heat is a different
crop. Name it that, or do not start it.

Boyer’s alternative was not a furnace. It was low cordons and a
floating cover, the Coleman double-cover idea used as a zone move
on the *wood*, not on the whole volume of the house. That is a
different religion: heat the ground you already have, for the
hours you need, with a blanket you can pull. I will write the
blanket later. I am only saying here that “heated greenhouse
figs” and “figs in an unheated house with a second cover” are
not cousins. One is a bill. The other is a chore. I will take
the chore in 8a.

If you already own heated glass and you want a fig in it, treat
the fig as a guest with a curfew. Let it go leafless. Keep the
can on the cold side. Water like a sip. Do not fertigate a
dormant stick because the tomatoes are hungry. In March, move it
out or vent hard before the flush cooks against the glass. A
guest that stays all year becomes a roommate. Roommates want a
lease. Figs want a winter.
