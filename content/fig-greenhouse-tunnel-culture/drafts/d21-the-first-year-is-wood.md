Boyer’s mentor told him the quiet part after a thin 2023:
it will take more than one to two years. I will tell it
before you crank the first side. The first year in a house
is wood. Wood thickness. Wood that will take a night. A
cordon that can hold a vertical. A stool that has two arms
you chose, not twelve the plant invented.

Zhi’s first year looked like a miracle — fivefold, earlier —
on one-year trees that still had last year’s wood. Then
winter took the wood and year two was a late crop on new
growth. That is not a reason to skip a house. It is a
reason not to budget year-one fruit as a personality. The
house can give you a first-year taste. It cannot give you
an established tree.

FigRoots already said a tree takes about three years to get
well-established, and that is when the quality and the
volume show up. The house does not cancel that clock. It
may move the clock. It may also move bud-break into a frost
you have to dance with. Early is not free.

What I do in year one under plastic: pick a trunk or two
arms, tip the extra, pull fruit if the plant is a stick
with ambition, water like a new pot even if it is in the
ground, and do not fertigate it into a jungle. Jack can
take the extra wood as cuttings if the name is worth a
cup. The row needs lignified tissue more than it needs a
photo of a fig the size of a marble.

Zone 7 year one is almost always “dieback insurance.” You
are building a crown that can come back. A house helps the
wood ripen if you vent and then let it harden in fall. A
house hurts the wood if you keep it green into November
with water and nitrogen. Zone 9 year one is establishment
in heat. Do not let the hoop cook the baby while you are
proud of the steel. 8a is both: harden the wood, do not
bake the can.

I will eat a first-year fig if the plant can spare it. I
will not sell a first-year row in my head. Gao’s 2–5 lb
was a three-year window, not a ribbon-cutting. Ernst’s
10 lb is a profit conversation for a plant that exists.
Your invoice for steel will come due before the tree does.
That is not a reason to skip. It is a reason to plant
names you will still like in year three, at a spacing you
will still be able to walk.

If you need fruit this September, buy fruit from someone
who already did year three, or pick the outdoor Celeste
that already lives on your wall. Use the house as a
student. Students do not pay rent in figs the first term.
They pay in wood. Wood is the rent I want.

If a first-year plant sets a ridiculous crop under plastic, I
thin it. A stick with forty figs is a stick that will not
thicken. I will eat six and take the rest off while they are
still hard. That feels wasteful. It is how year three exists.
Boyer pulled fruit in the cordon year on purpose. I steal that
even if I never run his wire. Wood is the assignment. Fruit is
the bonus you do not let steal the assignment.

I write “wood year” on the label tape so I do not lie
to myself at a market. The tape is ugly. The lie is
uglier. Year three can have a different tape.
