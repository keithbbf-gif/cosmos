I am not going to give you a pounds-per-square-foot number I
do not own. I am going to tell you that cheap kits fail in
ice, and that ice is the load people forget because the
catalog photo is a snow globe. Wet ice on film is a slab. A
Quonset holds a slab in the middle. A gothic tries to shed
it. A kit with skinny bows and a long run between purlins
will take a knee, and then your fig row is under a rib.

Zone 7 should spend here. The house is a season tool. A
collapsed house is a season plus a cleanup plus a plant with
a scar. Read the snow and wind rating as a variety claim —
optimistic, useful, not a warranty — and then look at what
failed in your county the last ugly year. If every surviving
hoop has a gothic and a second purlin, believe the field.

8a gets a pass some winters and a bill some winters. That
pass is how people buy the weekend kit. I have looked at
those kits. I have also looked at the photos after a glaze.
If you buy cheap in 8a, buy a plan to drop the film when ice
is in the forecast, or buy a house you can get inside and
push from below. A broom on a film from the outside is how
people tear a four-year roll. Pushing from inside with a
board is a farm trick. It is also how you punch a hole if
you are angry.

Zone 9 can laugh until the one ice night. Then the laugh is
expensive. If the hoop is a rain roof you strip in November,
the ice problem shrinks. If you leave film on because you
might want a January salad, you have accepted a Zone 7 load
in a Zone 9 mind. Name that.

Anchors are part of the load. A house that lifts in wind is
a house that failed even if the snow never came. Ground posts
deep enough for your soil, not for a catalog sand. Our clay
holds a post when it is wet and then opens a gap when it
bakes. I have had to reset. Reset is cheaper than a kite.

Do not confuse “cattle panel hoop” with “high tunnel.” A
cattle-panel house can grow a few pots and a tomato. It can
also fold. I have used small hoops as inner covers. I do not
use them as the only house over a collection I care about.
The inner hoop is a blanket. The outer house is a building.
Buildings have ratings. Blankets have clips.

If money is the constraint — and it is, Keith does money — I
would rather have a shorter house with honest steel than a
long house that is a wish. Forty feet of kit that fails is
not more orchard than twenty feet that stands. Zhi’s 34×72
is a farm. Boyer’s 5,000 square feet is a farm. Your first
house can be a 14×32 you can actually vent, bury wood in, and
repair without a crew. Start there. Earn the long house with
a year of crank work, not with a credit card and a video.
Wind is the load that shows up more often than snow in 8a. A house
that is light in the bows and light in the anchors will walk. I have
seen a hoop walk a foot and take a drip line with it. Ground posts
and a baseboard that is actually fastened are not decorations. They
are how the cheap kit becomes a kit that stays.

If you buy cattle panels for an inner cover, keep them inner. Do not
promote them to the only house over a collection you would cry
about. Tears do not straighten a bow. Honest steel does.
