A Quonset is a hoop. A gothic is a hoop that comes to a point so
snow and ice have a place to leave. That is the whole geometry
lesson. The rest is money and a county.

Zone 7 should hear the gothic sentence before they hear the price
sentence. Ice is heavier than the pretty picture of snow. A cheap
Quonset that looks fine in October will take a knee in a January
ice storm and then you have a fig row under a broken rib. I have
seen photos. I have not seen a fig that enjoyed a rib in its
crown.

8a gets ice some years and shrugs some years. That is the
in-between again. I will not tell you that you must gothic. I
will tell you that if your county has a story about a hoop that
folded, believe the story. Buy the rib count the story implies.
A kit that is priced like a weekend project is priced that way
because the steel is a weekend.

Zone 9 can run a Quonset and sleep, until the one ice night that
was not supposed to happen. Then the same cheap kit becomes a
Zone 7 problem for a week. If the house is a rain roof you can
strip, you can drop the film before the ice if you are home and
paying attention. If you are not home, the geometry is your
insurance.

Height is the other half of gothic vs Quonset. A low Quonset puts
fruiting wood into the film by year two if you let a fig be a
fig. Zhi and Boyer both spent ink on training because a fig in a
house is a height problem. Gothic gives you a little more air at
the peak. It does not give you permission to skip the pruners. I
would still rather have a 14-foot gothic I vent than a 7-foot
Quonset I love for the first June and hate for the third.

Do not mix the snow-load conversation with the heater
conversation. A gothic does not keep a night warm. It keeps a
roof from coming down. Those are different kindnesses. People
buy a tall house and then skip the second winter cover because
the peak “looks protected.” The peak is empty air. The wood is
on the ground where the cold is.

I am not an engineer. I will not give you a psf number I
invented. I will tell you to read the kit’s snow and wind rating
as if it were a variety claim — which is to say, as a starting
point that may be optimistic — and then look at what your county
extension and your neighbors’ hoops did in the last ugly year.
If nobody around you runs film through winter, ask why. Sometimes
the answer is that they drop the plastic in November. That is a
method. It is also a method that leaves your figs in the open
for the night you built the house to survive.

Pick a shape for the ice you actually get, a height for the
training you will actually do, and a door you will actually use.
Pretty is last. Pretty is how a Quonset ends up in a catalog
and then in a ditch.
I will add one county sentence. If your extension office or your
insurance talks about ice storms as a yearly person, gothic is not a
style. It is a shed. If they talk about ice as a story from 2021, you
can run a Quonset and keep a plan to drop film or push from inside.
The plan has to live on a nail, not in a memory. Ice does not wait
for you to find the ladder.

A used gothic from a vegetable grower is often a better buy than a
new skinny Quonset. Look at the bows, not the Facebook title. If the
film is shot and the steel is honest, you bought the right half. Film
is a roll. Steel is the argument.
