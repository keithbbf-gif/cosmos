Zhi tried woodchips, black plastic, digging, and then
sweet potato vines. The vines where they covered were the
weeds that lost. She harvested potatoes after the fig
leaves were gone. That is a farm sentence I like because
it is a second crop that does a first job. I will not
pretend we ran it. I will pretend a floor is a decision.

A hoop floor wants to be a lawn, a mud, or a plastic
sea. Lawn is a competitor. Mud is a rust splash and a
pan. A plastic sea is a bathtub. Woven landscape fabric
with holes at the trunks — Boyer’s 3.2 oz polypropylene —
is a walk I understand. Chips on top of that is a walk
plus a winter blanket you can scrape. Solid film as a
floor is a smell.

Sweet potatoes in 8a will run. They will also feed you.
If I used them, I would keep them off the trunk and off
the drip emitter, and I would treat them as a summer
floor, not as a vine in the canopy. A potato vine in the
fig canopy is a new thicket you installed. Zhi used them
where the chips failed as a weed stop. That is a gap
filler, not a religion.

Black plastic heated her weeds into a new problem and
did not end them. I have seen the same outdoors. Black
film is a skillet and a slit where a nutgrass invents a
life. I would rather pull a weed on a cool morning than
manage a slit.

Do not cultivate a shallow fig mat to “keep it tidy.”
NC State has been saying mulch instead of the hoe for a
long time. In a house the hoe is also how you nick a
drip line. Mulch. Pull. Walk. The aisle can be fabric.
The trunk ring can be chips you keep off the bark.

Zone 7 floors stay wet longer. A weed in a wet floor is
a rust partner. Zone 9 floors grow weeds like a dare.
8a floors glaze if you walk them wet and then they bake
into pottery. Walk boards if you have to be in there
after a rain. The clay remembers your shoe.

A winter floor under a chip blanket is a vole story. A
summer floor under a potato vine is a harvest story if
you planned it, and a mess if you did not. Write the
floor on the same card as the job of the house. Shop
floors should be dry enough for a wagon. Orchard floors
should drain and not compete. If you only have one
floor, you only have one job. We have been trying to
keep those jobs from stacking since the first draft.

I will take a slightly weedy hoop I can walk over a
clean hoop I cannot breathe in. Clean is not sterile.
Clean is an aisle, a trunk you can see, and a weed you
pull before it flowers. The potato can have the rest
if it earns the space. If it does not, it is a weed
with a good reputation.

I do not plant a floor crop I will not harvest. An
abandoned potato vine is a thicket I installed. If I
am not going to dig in November, I do not plant in
May. Weeds I will pull are more honest than a crop I
will ignore.

Edges at the baseboard are where the weeds win. Walk
the edge. The middle lies. The edge tells the truth.

I pull before seed. One walk in June saves three in August. The hoop makes weeds think they have a season too. They do. End it early.
