I am going to put the borrowed numbers on one table so the rest of
the pack can point at them instead of inventing a second trial. We
did not run Piketon. We did not run a 34×72 in Maryland 7A. We did
not train 120 cordons in a Pennsylvania 6b tunnel. If I say “five
times” or “230 pounds” later, I am quoting. If I say what *we* saw
in 8a, I will say it is a yard.

**OSU South Centers, Piketon, Gao and Slaughter, 2017–2020,
Ohioline HYG-1439.** Hardy Chicago and a Brown Turkey in field and
high tunnel. In 2019 the tunnel fruit began ripening around early
September and ran into November. First-three-year ripe yield was
about **2–5 lb per plant** inside and about **1 lb** outside. Hardy
Chicago fruit averaged about **18 g** and ripened roughly three
weeks earlier outdoors than Brown Turkey. Brown Turkey was larger
and later. They recommended a high tunnel for an earlier and longer
harvest. They did **not** run a cost-benefit. They said so.

**University of Kentucky, Ernst 2018, cited in that same fact
sheet.** About **10 lb per plant** sold at least **$3/lb** as a
profit conversation. That is a paper floor for people who want a
farm. It is not a promise that your first year in a hoop will pay
the steel.

**SARE FNE24-104, Miaochan Zhi, Thousand Springs Orchard, Maryland
Zone 7A.** Forty one-year trees in a 34×72, ten outside, local
germplasm plus named wood. First year: tunnel trees yielded about
**five times** the outside trees and ripened months earlier. Winter:
water barrels on the north side, woodchips, branches bent down.
Night in the tunnel still fell to the outside night. Most wood died
except wood that ended up under chips. Second year the crop was
late. Spacing they would keep: about **8–10 ft**. Branches they
would keep: **two or three**. Sweet potatoes as a floor crop that
also suppressed weeds. A late frost after an early spring flush
killed growth they had already paid for.

**SARE FNE22-003, Craig Boyer, Pennsylvania Zone 6b.** Japanese
stepover cordon, trees **7 ft** in-row and **6 ft** between rows,
about 120 trees in 5,000 square feet. Low tunnels of DeWitt AG19 on
64-inch hoops inside the high tunnel. February–March 2024: ground
under the row cover **20–23°F** warmer than outside ground. Buds
opening late February, about two months ahead of open-field wood.
First fruit 21 July 2024 (Dr. Gowaty). Total 2024 fruit **230.98
lb**. Longue d’Aout led, then Dr. Gowaty, Ronde de Bordeaux,
Atreano. Mid-April 2024: row cover left on Longue d’Aout cooked the
row to the ground. The plants came back. They still picked 73 lb
off the re-grown wood. That is a lesson, not a method I want to
repeat.

I am not going to blend those four stories into one “high tunnels
give 5× and July fruit.” Piketon is southern Ohio. Zhi is 7A
Maryland with winter-kill in year two. Boyer is 6b with a second
cover and a training system that is a job. Kentucky is a price.
Our 8a yard is a pile of pots and a smaller in-ground set. The
useful steal is the mechanism: a roof buys days; a second cover
buys a night; heat of the sun will also kill you; spacing and two
branches beat a thicket; a name that finishes beats a name you
loved on a forum.

Zone 7 should read Zhi and Boyer before they read a kit page. Zone
9 should read Gao’s caution about cost and then ask whether they
need a house at all. 8a should read all four and then walk their
own year with a thermometer. I will not restage these counts as
ours. I will not put them on a hero image. I will use them as a
floor for honesty.
