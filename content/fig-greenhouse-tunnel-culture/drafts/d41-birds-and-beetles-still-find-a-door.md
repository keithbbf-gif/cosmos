A roof is not a jar. Birds learn a door. Beetles learn a
gap. Raccoons learn a latch if you have the kind of
county that teaches them. I have walked into a hoop at
dawn and seen the festival. The plastic did not owe me a
guard.

Screen is a trade. You keep some animals out and you keep
some air out. In a fruit week I will close a screen door
if I have one. In a 100°F week I will take the screen
off before I cook the crop to save two figs. Zone 9
should almost always pick the air. Zone 7 can screen a
September. 8a will do both in ten days if the year is
rude.

Netting over a row inside the house is a second trade. It
catches birds and it catches your hair and it holds
humidity on a fruit you should have picked. I use it on a
short list I cannot sit with. I do not net a jungle. If
the plant is a jungle, the net is a web for rust.

Beetles — the green June kind, the fig kind people argue
about on forums — want ripe fruit. The house does not
hide ripe fruit. It concentrates it. Pick now. A sour fig
left on the arm is a beetle bell. Zhi’s ant sentence
belongs here too: a thin-skinned honey with a big eye is
a hotel. Tight-eye names in a humid house are less of a
hotel. They are not a lock.

I am not writing a spray card. I am writing a door habit
and a pick habit. If your county has a black fig fly
conversation, that is quarantine and a nursery rule, not
a hoop trick. Do not move soil like a tourist. FigRoots
already side-eyed big-box trees for that reason. A house
does not wash a pest off a can you rolled in.

Mice and voles are the winter door. Boyer boxed his
traps. I will not leave a chip pile as a hotel and then
act surprised. A hoop is a barn. Barns have rodents if
you feed them cover and fruit mash. Clean the mash. Trap
the runs. Do not poison a story next to a crop you intend
to eat with your hands.

The honest sale of a house is weather, not wildlife. If
wildlife was your only problem, a net on an outdoor tree
is cheaper than steel. If weather *and* wildlife are the
problem, you still need the crank and the pick. A sealed
house to keep birds out is a cooker that happens to be
empty of birds and full of sour.

I close the door at night in fruit season because I am
not a romantic about raccoons. I open it in the morning
because I am not a romantic about rust. That is the whole
IPM program I will stand behind without a license. The
rest is your county and your eyes. Walk at dawn once.
The festival will teach you which gap to close.

I pick damaged fruit first, not last. A beetle fig left
for later is a bell. The pretty ones can wait an hour.
The wounded ones are the inoculum. Zhi’s immediate-pick
sentence is a pest sentence. I steal it for birds too.
A pecked fig is not a gift to the row.

If a raccoon beats a latch, I change the latch. I do
not seal the house for the season. A sealed house is
still a cooker. A better latch is a latch.
