A roof is a liar in February. The day is 70°F under film
and 48°F in the yard, and a fig that was sleeping decides
it lives in another county. Boyer watched buds open at the
end of February in 6b under a low cover — two months ahead
of open-field wood. Zhi watched tunnel trees start early
and then meet a late frost. Both stories are the same
animal: you bought days at the front of the year, and the
year can still collect.

I want those days. July fruit in a cold county is how you
get paid. I also want a hand on the cover, because a wake
is not a harvest. A wake is a bet. Green tissue at 22°F is
a lost bet. FigRoots already said unlignified wood dies in
a freeze. A February flush is unlignified by definition.

The shop and the orchard disagree here. A winter room at
40°F should *not* wake a pot. If the shop is waking pots
in January, the shop is too kind — too much sun, too much
heat leak from a water heater, too many cups on a south
sill. Jack wants roots, not a June flush on a stick. The
orchard row, if you left a second cover on to save wood,
may wake because you did the job too well. That is the
row-cover dance in the next draft. Here I only want the
fact: early is a feature and a fuse.

Zone 7 should expect the fuse. The whole point of the
house is a longer year. Budget the March nights. Budget a
cover you can throw on at 6 p.m. Budget a vent you will
open at 10 a.m. when the same week hits 80°F. Zone 9
should not have a February fuse unless the hoop is sealed.
If Zone 9 wood is waking under plastic in January, open
the house or take the film off. You are forcing a plant
that already had a long year. 8a will get a kind February
every few years and a rude March after it. Write both on
the calendar in pencil.

Breba shows up in this conversation. Boyer saw breba form
in March and was not running a breba program. Early wake
plus last year’s wood is how breba happens. If winter took
the wood, you do not have breba, you have a late main crop
on new sprouts. A house that keeps wood is a breba machine
whether you asked or not. A house that loses wood is a
main-crop machine with a head start. Know which one you
built this year before you brag about two crops.

I do not try to keep a fig asleep until a polite date. I
try to keep the flush from being tender on a night I can
see coming. That is a smaller job. It needs a thermometer
and a habit, not a heater. If you cannot be the habit, do
not buy the early year. Plant outdoors and take the later,
simpler crop. Early is not free. The house will invoice
you in March.

I do not try to put a fig back to sleep once it has woken.
I try to keep the night from collecting the flush. People
run a hoop wide open in a cold March day to “harden” a
plant that already leafed. That can work. It can also
desiccate a tender leaf and then freeze it. Harden with
air that is not a wind tunnel, then cover the night. Early
is a fuse. You do not stomp a fuse. You walk it.
