Fig rust is a humid-South tax. A house does not forgive it.
A house can make it worse. Still, wet air, leaf on leaf,
overhead water, a floor that never dries — that is a rust
room with a roof. I have walked an outdoor tree after a
week of storms and a hoop after the same week. The hoop
lost if the sides had been a gesture.

The soil pack already said rust leaves are not a gift you
should mulch back on. I will say it in the house. Do not
leave a yellow carpet under a cordon and call it organic.
Rake the tax out. Burn or trash if your county talks that
way. Do not make next year’s inoculum this year’s floor.

Air is the fungicide I will actually use. Two or three
branches. 8–10 ft. Sides up. Ends that are not closets.
Boyer pulled lower leaves as fruit ripened, tomato-style,
so air could move. I steal that in a hoop. I do not strip
a plant naked in July and then wonder why the sun cooked
the arms. Lower leaves first, the ones that touch the
wet floor.

Overhead water is a rust hose. Drip or a wand at the
mix, not a shower at 6 p.m. The soil pack said it. The
house makes it louder because the leaf does not get a
wind to dry it. If you must sprinkle, do it at a morning
you will follow with an open side, not at dusk in a
closed bag.

Zone 9 rust can eat a canopy and still leave you a crop
if the year is long. Zone 7 rust on a short year is a
crop plus a winter going into thin wood. 8a is the tax
every wet summer, house or not. The house should be the
place you *can* keep foliage dry. If it is not, you built
a barn for the disease.

Other wet-air problems wear different names — anthracnose,
souring, a gray fuzz on a fruit you should have picked
yesterday. The move is the same: pick, air, dry floor,
fewer arms. I am not writing a spray program in this pack.
County extension can name a product. I will name the
habit. A sealed hoop plus a weekly cocktail is a hobby I
do not want.

Scale and mites like a house that is kind and dusty.
Winter shop at 40°F is less of a mite farm than a 60°F
glass room with a June flush in January. If you see dust
and speckle in July, water the air is not the first move
— air the air, hose the leaf underside in the morning,
and stop flexing the thermometer. A cooker with mites is
a cooker.

Still air is a design. You can see it. The spider web
across the aisle, the smell when you first open the door,
the leaf that never dries. Design it out with the crank
and the spacing. Do not perfume it with a spray and a
hope. The house is allowed to smell like dirt and fruit.
It is not allowed to smell like a closed gym.

I do not compost rust leaves next to the hoop. I do not
leave a “tea” barrel of bad leaves as a science fair.
The soil pack already refused rust litter as a gift. The
house pack refuses it as a floor. Next year’s tax starts
on this year’s wet yellow. Rake it. Then open the side
so the next leaf can dry. That is the program.
