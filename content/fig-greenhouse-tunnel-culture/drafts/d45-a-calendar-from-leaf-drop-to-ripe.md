I will write an 8a fruit-house year as a calendar, not
as a law. Zone 7 slides it later on the back end and
meaner in January. Zone 9 slides it earlier on the
strip and lazier on the blanket. Your yard will lie to
this page. That is why the notebook exists.

**November.** Leaves go. Feed is over. Water is a sip.
Wood you want to keep is low enough to cover. Film is
on if ice is a story in your county. Shop cans roll to
the 40°F room. Orchard wood gets chips or a cover on
the headline nights, not as a lifestyle if the week is
60°F.

**December–January.** Sticks are correct. Traps out if
you have a chip hotel. Patch film. Read the min-max at
plant height. Vent a 70°F Thursday so you do not wake
a bud. Do not fertigate a stick. Jack’s cups can have
a mat. The row cannot.

**February.** The liar month. A cover that worked at
night is a cooker at noon. Start the dance if wood is
swelling. Boyer saw late-February buds under a double
cover in 6b. We may see a swell in a kind 8a hoop.
Have the blanket within reach. Have the crank within
reach. They are the same week.

**March–April.** Fuse. Late frost after a warm week.
Zhi paid this invoice. Sides down at night, open by
late morning. Inner cover off if 80°F is on the
sensor. Do not leave a fabric sauna because you went
to town. This is the row-cover dance. Put it on the
calendar like trash day.

**May.** Cover gone if the night is kind. Sides are the
new dance. Do not feed a thicket. Rub extra buds. Pot
block that wintered in the shop can come out to the
open wall. Pot-up before June if a cup became a plant
— that sentence is already on FigRoots.

**June–July.** Skeleton days. Shade or wash if the
glare is a dare. Drip as a finger, not a mood. Rust
watch. Two arms, not twelve. Height off the film.
This is not the month you flex a thermometer.

**August.** Tip if fruit needs the plant. Taper feed.
Pick what is ready. Tight-eye earns its keep. Storms
hit the runoff even if they miss the fruit. Watch the
floor. Whitewash comes off if the last photons matter.

**September–October.** The reason. Walk every day you
can. Pick before the first hard night. Gao and Zhi
both used this window as the sale. A kind year runs
into November under film. A rude year ends on a Sunday
you were supposed to be elsewhere. Be here.

**The week after the last pick.** Clean mash. Pull
covers back to the ready pile. Let the plant sleep.
Write the dates. Next year the dates are the crop’s
memory. You will remember the figs. The notebook will
remember the low.

If this calendar feels like a job, it is. If it feels
like too much job, do not build the house. The outdoor
wall has a shorter card. I like a short card. I also
like a ripe fig in a week the yard cannot give. That
is the only reason the long card exists.

I photocopy this calendar and then I cross it out. The
cross-outs are the year. A clean calendar is a lie. 8a
will hand you a February wake and a quiet February in
consecutive years. The notebook is the real calendar.
This page is a starter. Throw it away when your dates
are better.
