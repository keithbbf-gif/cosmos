The long sides get the poetry. The end walls get the still air.
I have walked into a hoop with the sides up and felt a dead
pocket at the east door where a fig was sulking in its own
breath. Ends are where we park the wagon, hang the hose, stack
the unused cover, and then forget that a plant is living in a
corner we invented.

A door that actually opens wide is a tool. A cute door that
fits a person and not a wagon is a hobby. I want to roll a
#5 out without turning it on its side. I want to get a second
cover in without tearing it on a latch. I want to leave in a
hurry when the sky turns green. That is an end-wall
conversation. It is not a catalog conversation about “double
doors for a grand entrance.”

Screen is a fight. Insects and birds will find a door. A
screen that you can close on a fruit week is a kindness. A
screen you cannot take off on a 100°F afternoon is a lung
restriction. Zone 9 should be able to run without screen and
accept some beetles rather than cook the crop. Zone 7 can
screen a September and still need the sides up. 8a will do
both in the same month if the year is rude.

Solid end walls make a house stronger in wind. They also make
a house more of a bag. I like a solid bottom and an opening
top, or a wall I can roll. I do not like a plywood cathedral
with a pet door. The wind has to have a way through or it
will take a way through. A failed end wall in a storm is how
film becomes a sail.

The still-air pocket is also where rust starts and where a
heater, if you were foolish enough to run one, would sit and
dry one plant while the rest of the row slept in damp. Put
the thermometer at plant height near an end, not only in the
pretty middle. The middle is a lie you tell visitors. The end
is where you learn.

I train myself to walk the ends first. If the end plants look
better than the middle, the sides are doing the work and the
ends are the vent. If the end plants look worse, I have built
a closet and planted it. Move the closet — the stacked trays,
the extra bags of chips, the mower you should not have parked
there — or move the plant. Do not fertilize a closet.

Zone 7 ends take winter wind. Face the ugliest wind with the
stronger wall and put the working door on the kinder end if
you can. Zone 9 ends take afternoon glare. A white wall can
be a reflector that cooks the first pot. 8a gets both, because
of course it does. Stand in the doorway in January and in
July before you call the house finished. The doorway will tell
you whether you built a room or a still.
I will park the wagon *outside* the end if the end plant is sulking.
The wagon is not a resident. It is a guest that overstays. Same for
the extra bags of chips, the unused shade cloth, the mower. If the
end wall is a closet, I built a closet. Closets do not ripen figs.

A removable end panel is worth more than a pretty window. When you
need a breeze down the spine, the panel comes out. When January
wants a wall, the panel goes back. That is a personality switch you
can do without a motor. I like switches I can do with a wrench.
