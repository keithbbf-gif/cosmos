If I built one fruit house in 8a and I was not playing
farm, I would build a gothic I could stand in, 14×32
or the nearest honest kit, four-year film, roll-up on
both long sides, a door a wagon fits, posts on a pad
that drains. I would not heat it. I would stage an
inner cover and a pile of chips. I would buy the crank
like it was the crop.

Inside: one row, maybe two if I can still walk with my
arms out. Six to eight plants at 8–10 ft, not fourteen.
Two or three arms, or one low cordon I can pin. South
side of the aisle for the names I trust. A strip along
the north wall for cans I still intend to shuffle —
tender honey figs, a one-of-one, the museum. Two hose
zones if the cans and the ground share a roof.

Names I would give a hole: Chicago Hardy, an Improved
Celeste or a tight-eye Celeste I have tasted to type,
LSU Purple, Ronde de Bordeaux, a Longue d’Aout if I
wanted Boyer’s workhorse, one early purple that behaves
like Zhi’s keeper. Names I would give a can: the late
religion, the unknown trade, anything I have not eaten
for two years. Names I would not let in: a Smyrna that
needs a wasp, a big-box Turkey I cannot call, a
Mission I am growing as a personality.

Floor: woven fabric in the aisle, chips at the trunks
kept off the bark, no solid plastic sea. A thermometer
at plant height at an end, not only in the pretty
middle. A notebook on a nail. A trap box if the chips
are a hotel.

I would not share this roof with a tomato program that
wants 70°F at night. I would not share it with fifty
cups on a mat unless a bench on the north end can be
a *shop strip* with its own rule: 40–50°F, no feed, no
June flush. If Jack needs more than a strip, he gets
the real shop. The 14×32 is a fruit tool.

Zone 7 version of this house: same steel, more inner
cover, more bury, fewer cans, earlier names only. Zone
9 version: maybe no gothic at all — a skeleton I can
strip, tight-eye names, rain as the enemy. 8a version
is the one I wrote. In-between. Two personalities. A
short list.

Year one I would plant and train and refuse to count
pounds. Year two I would still refuse to pay the steel
with a story. Year three I would walk the dates and
decide if the missed outdoor weeks were worth the
crank. If they were not, the kit can become a winter
shop or a tomato barn or a pile of pipe. The figs can
go back to a wall. I would rather have a wall that
feeds me than a 14×32 I resent.

This is not a shopping list Keith has to buy. It is a
picture of restraint. Restraint is the only way a small
house beats a big wish. I have more names than 32 feet.
That is why I have cans. The house is for the names I
will pick in the rain. The rest of the collection can
wait in the shop. It has been waiting. It knows how.

I would leave the east 8 ft as wagon and cover storage
if the plants would otherwise eat the door. An empty
end is not wasted. It is how I get a blanket on in two
minutes. A stuffed end is how I skip the dance. The
14×32 is allowed to have a blank. Blanks are air.
