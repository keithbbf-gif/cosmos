The soil pack already said this about a planting hole. I will
say it about a building. If you set a hoop on a pad that holds
a lake, you did not build a fig house. You built a bathtub
with a roof. The film will keep rain off the fruit and then
the same rain will arrive as runoff and sit on the crown. I
have watched a post-hole fill and stay full. The fig in the
next hole did not care that the roof was new.

Our 8a clay is a pantry until it is a bowl. A house makes the
bowl more honest because you will water when the sky cannot,
and you will walk the same aisle until it is a pan. Floor
traffic is a soil type you invent. If the aisle glazes, the
roots at the edge of the aisle live in a plate.

Site the house on a slight grade, or make one. I would rather
scrape a shallow crown and plant on the high side than import
a truck of “greenhouse mix” and set it in a dish. The soil
pack already refused the pretty hole. The house pack refuses
the pretty pad. A French drain is overkill for a backyard hoop
until you have watched water after a three-inch rain. Then it
might not be. Watch first.

Posts sit in the same clay. A post in a puddle is a rot and a
wiggle. Set posts so the splash does not live against the
steel, and so the film base is not a gutter pointed at a
trunk. I have seen a baseboard shed water straight into the
first row. That is a design. It is a bad one.

Zone 7 clay plus a winter house is how you get a wet crown
going into a freeze. That is a worse night than a dry crown
at the same temperature. Zone 9 sand plus a house is how you
get a sieve that needs a hose every day and also a nematode
conversation if you brought old garden dirt in. 8a gets the
bowl. Raise the plant if the hole holds a lake. The house
does not cancel the puddle test. Dig, fill, come back in the
morning. If the water is still there, the house can wait.

Do not floor a hoop with a solid plastic sheet unless you like
hydroponics and a smell. Woven landscape fabric, the kind
Boyer used under a cordon, is a different sentence: weed
suppression, a dry-ish walk, soil still able to breathe at
the trunk hole. Solid plastic is a bathtub liner. I have
stepped on both.

If the only flat spot on the lot is the wet spot, you do not
have a house site. You have a place to put a winter shop on
gravel and pots on a bench. The orchard row can live on the
ugly slope that drains. Figs will take a slope. They will not
take a sealed clay bowl with a new roof and a hopeful
invoice. Site the drain first. The steel will still be for
sale next month. The lake will still be there too.
Gravel as a pad is a maybe. Gravel on clay can be a bowl with rocks
in it. I have made that bowl. Water sat in the rocks and the post
wiggled. A slight crown of *native* dirt that sheds, with gravel
only in the wagon aisle, has treated me better. The plants want the
dirt. The wagon wants the gravel. Do not give the plants the
wagon's floor.

If you already built on a lake, raise the plants now. Berms, not a
deeper hole. The soil pack already refused the pretty hole. A house
does not change that refusal. It only makes the lake more expensive
per square foot.
