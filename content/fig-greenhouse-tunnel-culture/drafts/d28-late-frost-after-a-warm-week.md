Zhi wrote the sentence I keep in my pocket. In spring the
fig started to grow, then a late frost killed them all.
They still grew sooner than the outside trees. That is the
whole spring risk in one farm year. The house did its job.
The calendar did its job. They were not the same job.

8a knows this dance outdoors. A 75°F Thursday, a 28°F
Sunday, a pecan crop that vanishes, a fig tip that blackens.
A house makes the Thursday ruder — hotter, earlier — and
does not always cancel the Sunday. Single-layer film on a
radiative night can be a greenhouse until dusk and a cold
frame after. If the sky is clear and the wind dies, the
leaves you woke are a bet.

What I do when the radio says frost after a flush: sides
down before dark, a second cover on the wood or on the
pots, barrels if they are already there (courtesy, not
faith), and I do not water at dusk like I am putting a
child to bed. Wet crowns and a freeze are a worse pair
than dryish crowns and a freeze. The soil pack said water
before a *hard winter* freeze on established trees. A
April frost on a flush is a different sip. I would rather
the plant be slightly thirsty than sitting in a dish.

If the frost wins, do not fertilize the black tips as a
funeral. Wait. See what pushes from lower buds. See what
pushes from the crown. Zhi’s plants still outpaced the
outdoors. A lost flush is not a lost year unless you rip
the plant out in a mood. I have ripped in a mood. I do
not recommend it.

Zone 7 should treat late frost as a scheduled holiday.
Have the cover staged in March, not in the barn under a
pile of pots. Zone 9 gets fewer Sundays and then gets
emotionally ruined by one. A 9A hoop that was stripped
for summer should still have a blanket in a closet. 8a
should have the blanket within reach from the week the
buds swell until the week pecans are safe. That is a long
reach. That is the in-between.

Pots are easier to save than a cordon. You can shuffle a
#3 into the shop in one evening if you did not plant
fifty of them in the orchard row. That is a reason to
keep tender names in cans even under film. The house is
a season. The shop is a Sunday.

I do not use a heater for this night if I can avoid it.
A heater that fails at 3 a.m. is a story. A blanket that
is already on the plant at 8 p.m. is a chore. I will take
the chore. If the night is a polar headline, I will take
the shop for every can I can roll and the blanket for
everything I married to the ground. The headline is not
a reason to have skipped the habit all month.

The warm week is the trap. You open everything because
the house is a car. You leave it open because you are
tired of the dance. Then the Sunday arrives. Close on
Saturday afternoon. You can open again at 10 a.m. The
fig will not sue you for a missed hour of Sunday sun.
It will sue you for a tender leaf at 28°F.

After a black-tip morning I wait ten days before I cut.
The black is obvious. The live bud below it is not obvious
until it moves. A hero prune on Monday can take the backup
you needed on Friday. Zhi’s plants still outpaced the yard
after a kill. Let yours try. Then cut the dead, not the
mood.
