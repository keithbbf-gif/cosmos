PapaFig writes the row. I write the cans. The collection
is a pile of 3- and 5-gallon pots and a paint pen, and
in 8a it does not spend January on the gravel. We
shuffle. The room stays above **40°F** and below the
kindness that wakes a stick. That sentence is already on
[Winter Protection](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/).
I am not moving it.

A fruit tunnel is a bad collection room if you ask it to
be 40°F and also a solar orchard. The orchard wants
February sun. The cans want a dim, even cool. If they
share a roof, the cans go to the north bench, off the
stripe, and they do not get the drip zone you built for
a cordon. I have watched a #3 on an orchard emitter
drink like a tourist all winter. That can was not
dormant. It was a project.

Labels leave before roots if you let them. In a shop they
leave before spring if the tape freezes and peels. I
write two sides of the pot with a paint pen. I do not
trust a tag in a tangle. The greenhouse-photos folder is
full of this boredom on purpose. Boredom is how a
collection stays a collection instead of a box of
mysteries.

Water is a sip. Barely moist. A saucer under a winter
can is a drowning kit. The soil pack already yelled
about saucers. In a cold house a saucer is also a
little ice rink. Lift the pot. If it is a brick, wait.
If it is a feather and the wood is wrinkling, a drink.
Tuesday is not a religion.

Cups are not cans. A cutting on a mat at 75–78°F is a
rooting job. A #3 in the same room as a mat will wake
if the mat is the climate. Give the cups a corner. Give
the cans the rest. I have ruined a pot block by being
proud of a tray. The tray rooted. The pots leafed in
January and then a 25°F night cleaned the bench.

Zone 7 shops work harder. The night is meaner. The pile
is often all cans. Do not use the fruit hoop as the
only shop unless you can keep a north room honestly
cold and the orchard honestly covered. Zone 9 shops may
be a grouping of pots against a wall with a blanket on
the cans. 8a is a door and a wagon and a room we
already named.

Fall airlayers that need a few more weeks of root can
come into a shop that is warm enough for roots. That is
on the airlayer page. It is not permission to keep the
whole collection at rooting temperature. One bench.
One rule. The rest sleep.

If you build steel for the collection, build a shop:
wagon door, dry floor, thermometer at pot height, no
heater pointed at a can, space to walk without knocking
a paint pen name onto the floor. If you build steel for
fruit, do not fill it with the museum. The museum will
win the aisle and lose the crop. I will take the museum
in a room I can close. PapaFig can have the 14×32. We
already tried to mash them. The mash is how labels get
lost and figs get cooked. Keep the pens on different
benches even when the roof is the same.

I will not stack cans so tight a mite walks the block
like a road. Air in a shop is not a July crank, but it
is not zero. A little space, a weekly look, a sip that
is a sip. The collection is a lot of names. It is still
a lot of plants. Plants in a pile are a pile.
