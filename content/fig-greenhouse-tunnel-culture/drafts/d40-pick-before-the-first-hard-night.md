Figs do not ripen off the tree like a pear you bullied.
The introduction page already said the only good fig is a
ripe one, and that most people have never had one. A house
gives you more days to get there. It does not let you
harvest a green ball on Friday and serve jam on Saturday.

What the house *does* let you do is pick in a rain and
pick later into a fall that would have killed the outdoor
row. Gao’s tunnel ran into November. Zhi’s ran later than
the yard. Boyer’s October warmed up on purpose — sides
managed, doors used as a solar trick — and fruit that had
stalled started again. That is an operator, not a kit.

The first hard night still wins any fruit that is not yet
jam. I walk the row the afternoon before. Soft ones in a
flat. Almost-soft ones if I know that name will finish on
a plate in a day — some will, many will not. Green ones
stay or come off if I am tipping to push the last energy
into the ones that can. Zhi tipped in August for that
reason. In October I tip because the night is the pruner
if I am not.

A house can hold 35°F fruit a little longer than a yard.
It cannot hold 25°F fruit because you liked the look of a
full tree. Pull the ripe. Leave the fantasy. I have left
the fantasy and inventoried mush. Mush is not a caption I
want.

Zone 7: this walk is the crop. Miss it and the steel was
a decoration. Zone 9: the first hard night may be a rumor
until it is a headline. Still walk before the headline.
8a: you will have a year where November 10 is fine and a
year where October 20 is the end. The house moves the
date. It does not mail you the date. The thermometer
does.

After the night, do not leave spoiled fruit on the arm.
Sour is a yeast you are farming. Pick the dead into a
bucket. Chickens if you have them. Trash if you do not.
The winter wood does not need a ferment necklace.

I like a morning pick for quality and an evening pick
when the radio is ugly. The evening pick is not romantic.
It is a headlamp and sticky fingers and a flat on the
wagon. That is the job you bought with the steel. If you
will not do that job, do not build the house for market,
and maybe do not build it for yourself. An outdoor
Celeste you walk at 6 p.m. will feed you. A hoop you
ignore on a frost night will feed the compost.

Write the last pick date next to the low temperature.
Next year that pair is worth more than a catalog. The
house is a machine for last picks. Operate it like one.
The first pick in July is bragging. The last pick in
October is the reason.

I take a headlamp even when I think I will be done at
dusk. The last ten figs are the ones you miss, and they
are the ones the night takes. A lamp is a harvest tool.
Pride about finishing in daylight is how you leave a
flat on the tree.

After the walk I set the flats somewhere cool and single
layer. The house was for the tree. The kitchen is for
the fruit. Do not leave a flat in the hoop overnight to
“keep picking.” The night does not care that you were
efficient.
