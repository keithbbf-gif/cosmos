Gao said there is a difference between growing a hardy
fig in Ohio and making money. He did not run the
cost-benefit on the Piketon house. He pointed at Ernst
in Kentucky: about **10 lb** a plant, sold at least
**$3/lb**, as a conversation about profit. I will not
improve those numbers. I will not put them on a hero
image. I will say they are a paper floor from a paper
that is not our orchard.

Zhi’s neighbor wanted figs for a market. Demand was not
her problem. Winter wood was her problem. First-year
fivefold was her first-year. Second-year late crop was
her second-year. A market plan that used year-one as
the personality would have lied. Boyer’s 230.98 lb in
2024 was year-something on a system with labor he
named. His 2023 was not enough to sell. The mentor said
wait. The mentor was right.

I grow to eat and to keep a collection alive. If I sold,
I would sell a ripe fig that cannot ride a truck, which
is the only fig worth selling locally. That fig needs a
name that finishes, a pick that happens today, and a
customer who is not expecting a California rock. The
house helps the first two. It does not find the
customer.

Zone 7 market is a season-extension bet. The steel is
the bet. The winter method is the interest. If you
cannot do the dance, you are betting with a hole in
your pocket. Zone 9 market may not need steel — it may
need a dry week and a tight eye and a fridge. 8a market
is a maybe that I will not dress up as a plan. A 14×32
of six names at 8–10 ft is a farmstand experiment, not
a living.

Test a small planting, Gao said. I will repeat it. Do
not finance a gothic on a forum thread. Finance a year
of fruit you already know how to pick, then ask whether
the missed weeks were worth steel. If the missed weeks
were October green fruit, maybe. If the missed weeks
were you on vacation, no.

Labor is the line nobody puts on the kit page. Crank,
cover, pick, thin, trap, patch. Boyer said the stepover
is labor up front. Zhi said fruit needs immediate pick
and constant pruning. I say a house is a job. If the
Kentucky number requires 10 lb at $3, do the hours
before you do the spreadsheet. Hours are the true unit.
Pounds are what the hours sometimes turn into.

I will not tell Keith to build a market. Keith does
money. I will tell a reader: the house can make a fig
you cannot buy. That is already a kind of profit. If
you need the other kind, read Ernst and Gao as a
warning label, not as a business plan. Then grow a
short list, keep records, and let year three talk. Year
one is wood. Year one is not a stall.

I will not price a fig I have not picked at 6 a.m. and
sold by noon. A ripe fig is a clock. The Kentucky
number assumes a fruit that exists and a buyer who
understands jam. If your buyer wants a rock that ships,
you are in a different crop. The house will not make
that crop. It will make the opposite.

Write hours for a month before you write dollars. If
the hours already hurt, the dollars will not heal them.

If I sold, I would sell a name I can say out loud and a pick date I wrote down. A crowd called Turkey at $3 is a way to teach a customer to distrust figs. I would rather sell fewer and stay welcome.
