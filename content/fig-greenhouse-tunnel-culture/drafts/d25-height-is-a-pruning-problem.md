Figs do not read the peak height on the kit page. They grow
until they find the film, and then they grow along it, and
then they grow through it. I have seen a cane print itself
on plastic like a fossil. That fossil is a hole in the next
wind.

A 7-foot Quonset is a first-year house and a third-year
argument. A 12- or 14-foot gothic is a few more seasons
before the argument. Neither is a reason to skip the
pruners. Zhi’s trees were “very vigorous.” Boyer’s
verticals were trained to strings on purpose so they would
not invent a ceiling. The ceiling is always there. You
either assign the wood a job below it or you let the wood
choose the film.

I tip before the cane leans on the plastic. That is not
art. That is a walk with a pair of loppers on a Saturday
you would rather skip. If I wait until the cane is lying
on the film, I tear the film taking it down, or I leave it
and call it shade cloth. Shade cloth should be a cloth,
not a diseased leaf stuck to the sky.

Fruit at eight feet in a hoop is fruit you will not pick
clean. You will leave the high ones for the beetles and
the sour. Train for a shoulder-height crop if you are a
person and not a ladder company. The house already wants
two or three arms. Those arms can be long and still be
low. Long and low is a cordon. Long and high is a waste
of a roof.

Zone 7 height is also a winter problem. You cannot bury an
eight-foot chandelier. You can bury a two-foot arm. If the
point of the house was wood, keep the wood where a chip
pile or a blanket can find it. Zone 9 height is a picking
problem and a rust problem. High shade in a hoop is still
air. 8a is both. I would rather pick at my chest than
admire a peak.

Strings, like Boyer’s tomahooks, keep verticals in a
column so the aisle stays an aisle. I have used a cheaper
version: one wire, a bit of twine, a clip. The plant will
still try to be a bush. Your job is to be more stubborn
than a bud. Every unused bud on a cordon is a future
thicket. Rub it when it is a bump, not when it is a
branch.

If the film already has a cane in it, cut the cane first,
then patch the film. Do not pull. Pulling is how a small
hole becomes a zipper. I have zippered a hoop. I did not
like the sound.

Height is not vigor you should be proud of. Height is a
missed walk. Walk more. Cut sooner. Leave the peak for
hot air to leave. The fig can live in the first seven
feet. The last three feet are for the crank and the
weather, not for a name you wanted to photograph from the
door.

I keep a stick cut to the height I will tolerate and I walk
it down the row like a fool. If a cane is taller than the
stick, it is a cane I missed. The stick is not art. It is how
I stop arguing with a peak. The peak is for hot air. The stick
is for fruit I can pick without a ladder I will not fetch on
a frost night.

A cane through the film is a leak I patch the same day.
A leak I admire is a zipper next week. Cut, then patch,
then walk the stick again. The peak is not a trophy.
