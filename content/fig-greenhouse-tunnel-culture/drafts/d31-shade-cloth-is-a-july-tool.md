Shade cloth is a hat. It is not a climate. I put it on when
the house is open and still too bright, and I take it off
when the fruit needs every photon to finish. People leave
it on from April to November because it looks official.
Then October fruit sits under a 40-percent cloud you
installed, and they blame the variety.

Percent is a guess until you live with it. I like a light
cloth — 30 percent, maybe 40 — over an 8a hoop in the rude
weeks, on the south and west if I cannot do the whole
roof. A 70-percent cloth turns a fig house into a patio
shade and a rust room. Figs want sun. FigRoots already
said 6–8 hours. A house that cuts that in half is not a
favor.

Zone 9 may need the hat more days than 8a. The year is a
glare. Zone 7 may need it fewer days, and then only on a
hoop that cannot open enough. If your Zone 7 house needs
heavy shade all summer, your house cannot breathe. Fix the
side. Do not buy a darker cloth.

Whitewash is the cousin you paint. It wears. It looks
ugly. It works on glass and on film if you use a product
meant for it, not leftover interior latex. I have seen
latex flake into a bed. I do not want that in a crown.
A real greenhouse shading compound is boring and correct.
A cheap thin wash you can scrub in September is better
than a permanent cloud.

How I decide: if the leaf is cupping at 10 a.m. with the
sides up, the plant is asking for a hat or a drink or
both. Feel the mix. If the mix is honest and the leaf is
still a cup, the light plus the bag is the problem. Hat.
If the mix is a bone, water first. A hat on a dry pot is
how you hide a wilt until it is a crisp.

Pots on the south floor get the first hat. In-ground on a
berm can wait. The can is the skillet. A board, a lighter
pot, a cloth draped over a wire — those are small hats.
The whole-house cloth is for the week the forecast is a
dare.

Take it off when nights cool and the fruit is softening.
The last three weeks of ripening are not the week I want
a cloud. A house already steals some light through dusty
film. Do not steal more out of habit. Dust is a shade
cloth you did not choose; wash the film if the year is
long and the plastic is a yellow cave.

I will not run cloth in winter to “moderate.” Winter wants
photons and a second cover at night, not a hat all day.
A winter cloth is how you keep a fig half-awake in a dim
room. Dim and cool is a shop. Dim and sealed is a fungus.
Know which room you are in. July is a hat. January is a
blanket. They are not the same textile.

Clips and a ridge rope beat a cloth you weighted with
bricks on the film. Bricks walk. Bricks tear. I have
torn a roll to save a cloth. That is a bad trade. Hang
the hat like it is a hat, not like it is a tarp over a
boat. Take it down like it is a hat too — before the
first fall front you wanted every photon for.

I number the clips so I can find them in October when
I want them gone. Lost clips become mower food and film
tears. A hat should come off as clean as it went on.
