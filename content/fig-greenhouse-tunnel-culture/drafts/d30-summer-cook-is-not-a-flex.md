I have opened a hoop at 2 p.m. in July and felt the air
come out like a dryer. People post that number like it is
proof the house is “working.” A working house in July is
a house that is not a car. 110°F at the peak is a warning.
120°F at the leaf is an injury. Boyer’s cooked row was in
April. Our July will do it without a second cover if you
leave the sides down because you went to town.

Figs can take heat. Mediterranean hills are not shy. They
take heat *with air and a root that can dry a little*. A
sealed hoop is heat with still wet breath. That is a
different climate. Rust likes it. Mites like it. Soft
fruit likes to sour in it even without rain. The leaf
cups, then the edge browns, then you fertilize.

The fix is not a stronger fan in a closed bag. The fix is
the long wall open, the ends open, shade if the year is
rude, and a walk at 10 a.m. not 4 p.m. By 4 p.m. you are
documenting a failure. I would rather be sweaty at 10 and
bored at 4.

Pots cook faster than ground. Black plastic in a sun
stripe is a skillet. I have moved a #3 two feet and saved
it. I have also not moved it and written a smaller plant
the next spring. If the orchard is cans, give them a
lighter can or a board under the south side, or accept
that July in a hoop is a shuffle of a different kind:
shuffle into shade, not into a garage.

Zone 7 can get smug about summer because they built for
winter. Their July is still a cooker if the kit is sealed.
Zone 9 should treat a closed hoop in July as malpractice.
Strip it. 8a should be able to take the house to a
skeleton on the ugly weeks and still have a roof for the
afternoon storm. That is the whole summer sale: rain off
the fruit, air on the leaf. If you cannot have both, take
the air and pick before the rain. A dry fruit on a cooked
plant is not a win.

Whitewash and shade cloth are the next draft. This draft
is the attitude. Do not flex the thermometer. Flex the
crank. If a visitor wants to see the house in July, show
them the open side and a fig you can stand next to. If
they want a sauna they can go sit in a car. I will not
grow fruit in a car and call it culture.

Water is not a substitute for air. A wet pot in a cooker
is a steamed root. Open first, then sip. The soil pack
already said afternoon wilt is not always a cry. In a
hoop that sentence gets louder. Look at the new leaf. If
it is cooked, air. If it is limp and the mix is light,
water. If you cannot tell, feel the mix, do not feel your
guilt.

Summer is why I said in d02 that a fruit house and a
winter shop are different jobs. The shop can be closed.
The fruit house cannot. If they share a roof, July is
when the shop pots move to the open wall and the orchard
row gets the breeze. Do not make Jack’s cups share a
cooker with a fruiting cordon. One of them will lose.
Usually both.

If I have to leave town in July, I leave the sides *up*
and a note on the door, not the sides down and a prayer.
A rain on the fruit is a sour risk. A sealed cooker is a
dead canopy. I will take the rain on a tight-eye name
before I take a 120°F funeral. That is the July order.
Pride about a dry roof can wait until I am home to open
it.
