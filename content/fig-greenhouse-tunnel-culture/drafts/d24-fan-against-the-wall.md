A house does not cancel a wall. Sometimes the wall *is* the
house: a south brick, a lean-to film, two arms tied to
wires, fruit where you can see it. Old fig countries did
this before anybody said “high tunnel.” I still like it for
8a because a wall is thermal mass you do not have to fill
with a hose, and a fan is a plant you can wrap without
building a gothic.

A fan is two to five ribs off a short trunk, tied to a
plane. A palmette is a tidier cousin. I do not need the
French word to make a fig flat. I need the fig flat so the
sun hits the wood, the air hits the leaf, and a blanket
hits the plant instead of a ball of twigs. Zhi wanted
espalier for the same reasons: two to four branches near
the ground, light on the wood, a cover that fits.

Inside a hoop, a fan along the long side can use the roll-up
as weather and the film as a second sky. Do not jam the
ribs into the film. Height is a later draft. The wall or
the wire has to hold the rib *below* the place ice will
sit. A fig that has grown through the plastic is a leak
and a tear waiting on a wind.

Outdoors against brick, the fan is often enough that I do
not build steel. Celeste on a wall in 8a has finished for
people who never owned a crank. The house is for the names
that need more days, or for a row you want to pick in the
rain. Do not put a fan on a wall *and* a hoop over the
same plant unless you like cooking a brick oven. I have
felt that air. The brick stores the day and gives it back
at 9 p.m. when you wanted a cool-down.

Zone 7: a fan you can pin to the ground or wrap as a
package is a winter method. A tall palmette you cannot
reach with a blanket is a pride project. Zone 9: a fan is
how you keep a fig from eating a patio. The house, if any,
is a rain roof you can strip. 8a: steal the flatness. Steal
the wall. Steal a hoop only for the fruit that still dies
green.

Ties should be ugly and kind. A wire that bites will girdle
a rib that got thick while you were in the shop. I use
something that stretches or I retie. Tomato clips, the ones
Boyer used, are honest. They are also easy to forget until
they are tight. Walk the fan when you walk the crank.

I will take a messy two-arm fan over a pretty eight-arm
drawing. Two arms, 8–10 ft of house, a wall if I have one.
The rest is a museum. Museums belong in pots. The wall is
for a fruit you intend to eat while standing there, juice
on the wrist, no story about steel required.

A lean-to film on a wall is allowed to be the whole house for
one or two plants. I have wanted that more than I have wanted
a 34×72. One wall, one fan, one blanket, one crank if the film
is a skirt. That is Zone 7–8a culture without a farm invoice.
If the wall already finishes Celeste, the skirt can wait. If
the wall almost finishes, the skirt is the cheapest steel I
know.

Retie after a growth spurt or the wire will write a
ring. I have grown a ring. The ring does not forgive
the pretty palmette drawing. Ugly ties that get redone
beat pretty ties that get forgotten.
