Yellow leaves in a hoop make people into pharmacists. The
jug comes out. The soluble comes out. The plant was asking
for air, or a drain, or less kindness, and it gets salt
and a jungle. I have done the pharmacist walk. The plant
did not thank me.

FigRoots already has a fertigation page. Established pots,
a water-soluble sip, heavy feeders, slow-release as a
cousin. I am not rewriting that page. I am putting a gate
in front of it: if the house is a car, do not feed the
passenger. Open the door. If the floor is a dish, do not
feed the drowning. If the plant is a thicket, do not feed
the thicket. If the wood is heading into a freeze, do not
feed a late flush.

Year one under plastic wants wood, not a magazine canopy.
A light hand. Compost as a topping, the way the soil pack
said, not a burial. Year three can take a schedule you
already trust on outdoor pots. The hoop is not a reason
to double it. The hoop already doubled the year.

Yellow can be wet, dry, hungry, rusty, mite-bitten, or a
pot that is a brick. The soil pack walked that. In a
house I add: cooked. A cooked leaf yellows and cups and
then someone fertilizes the skillet. Feel the mix. Feel
the air. Look at the side. If the side is down at 2 p.m.,
you found the problem in the crank, not in the jug.

Zone 7 late nitrogen is how you send tender wood into a
night. NC State has been saying that for outdoor figs
longer than this pack has existed. A house makes the late
flush easier, so the refusal has to be firmer. Stop
feeding when you want the plant to finish fruit and
harden. Zone 9 can feed longer and still should not feed
a rusted cooker. 8a should treat August like a taper, not
like a contest.

Jack’s cups are a different gate. A cutting on a mat is
not an orchard row. Do not fertigate a stick because the
row got a sip. Do not starve a fruiting cordon because
the cups are on water-only. Two religions. Two cans.

If you need a rule you can tape to the crank: vents
first, finger second, jug third, and never the reverse
on a hot day. I have reversed it. I got a beautiful
thicket and a rust week and a fruit that stayed green
while the plant wrote new leaves. The fruit was the
assignment. The leaves were the distraction. Fertilizer
is good at distractions.

A house already wants to make a fig look rich. Rich is
not the crop. Ripe is the crop. Feed for ripe. Vent for
alive. If you only have time for one, vent. A hungry fig
with air will still try. A fed fig in a bag will try to
die with good color.

I hide the jug behind the crank so I have to touch the
crank to feed. That is a joke that became a habit. The
habit is the gate. If I cannot open the side, I cannot
reach the soluble. A yellow leaf in a closed hoop can
wait one more hour. The crank cannot.

Salt rim on a pot is a note I write: too kind, too
often. I do not answer a salt rim with more kindness.
I flush once, vent, and wait. The plant will tell me
if it was hungry or cooked. Hungry waits better than
cooked.

If the leaf is rust, the jug is not a fungicide. Open, rake, wait. Feed a clean canopy or do not feed.
