Zhi put a row of large water barrels on the north side of a
34×72 in Maryland 7A. The idea is old and not foolish: water
eats the day’s extra heat and gives some of it back at night.
She also put about a foot of woodchips down in November so the
ground would not freeze. Then she read the sensors. The tunnel
night still fell to the outside night. Most of the branches
died. The branches that lived were the ones that ended up under
the chips.

I am not sneering at barrels. I am refusing the sales sentence
that says barrels are a heater. They are a buffer on a sunny
swing. They are almost nothing on a windy 8°F night under
single-layer film. If your Zone 7 plan is “barrels, therefore
wood lives,” you have Zhi’s first winter in front of you. She
already paid for that lesson. You do not have to.

What I steal is the thing that worked by accident. Wood on the
ground, under chips, did not rot in her report, and it lived.
That is a bury. It is the same family as the tip-and-bury
people do outdoors, just with a roof overhead. Boyer’s version
was a floating cover over a cordon at ground level, and his
sensors said the ground under the cover ran 20–23°F warmer
than outside ground in the late winter of 2024. That is a
number with a method attached. Barrels did not give Zhi that
number.

8a can use a barrel as a courtesy. A black barrel on the north
wall will take the edge off a sunny February day that would
otherwise spike the hoop and wake a bud, then leak a little
warmth after dark. That is a courtesy. It is not a plan for
12°F. The plan for 12°F is wood near the ground, a second
cover, chips or a blanket, and a thermometer you believe.

Zone 9 almost does not need the barrel conversation. If you
are running barrels in 9A you are decorating. Spend the money
on a crank. Zone 7 can run barrels *and* a bury. Do not run
barrels *instead* of a bury.

Hay bales show up in the same paragraph as barrels in a lot of
grant language. Hay is a mold factory if it stays wet against
bark. I will use a bale as a windbreak away from the wood. I
will not hug a trunk with a wet bale and call it passive
solar. Chips, if they are the accidental hero, still have to
stay off the crown in summer or you will have a vole hotel and
a rot. Zhi’s winter chip was a blanket. Summer chips are a
mulch. Different jobs.

If you want thermal mass that does not become a science fair,
the ground is the mass. That is why cordons at soil level keep
showing up in the cold-house papers. You are clipping the
plant to the battery that already exists. A barrel is a
battery you have to fill, dump, and not let breed mosquitoes
in May. I will take the ground.

Write the barrel down as a maybe. Write the second cover down
as a must in Zone 7 and as a bad-night tool in 8a. If a seller
talks barrels and does not talk covers, the seller is talking
to a different crop.
Mosquitoes are the May invoice for a barrel you loved in January.
Dump it or screen it or accept a new crop you did not want. I dump.
The courtesy is seasonal. The larvae are not a thermal mass I need.

If you paint a barrel black, you also made a summer skillet next to
a plant. Move it off the trunk line before June or the plant on that
end lives in a different county. Mass is a winter tool. In July it
is furniture. Furniture can leave.
