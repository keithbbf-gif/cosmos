Coleman’s double-cover sentence got figs into a Pennsylvania
grant because Boyer needed a zone move that did not burn
propane. Cover the *crop*, not the sky. A high tunnel is
the first filter. A low tunnel is the second. The ground
is the battery. When outdoor air hit 4°F one December
night, he recorded 24°F on the soil under the inner cover
and 17°F on the hoop floor beside it. That is not a
furnace. That is a sandwich.

I steal the sandwich for 8a ugly nights and for Zone 7 as
a lifestyle. I do not steal it as a reason to skip the
outer house or to skip dormancy. Two layers can also be
two cookers. The dance in spring is how you pay for the
sandwich. If you will not dance, do not build the inner
hoop, or pull it before the 70°F weeks.

Hardware is humble. Wire hoops, a light fabric, a way to
weight the edge so the wind does not make a kite. Boyer
used 64-inch pre-arched wire and AG19. I have used cheaper
hoops and a frost cloth. The cheap version still needs a
gap so the fabric is not sitting wet on a bud. A cover
that is a wet rag on a tip is a freeze plus a rot.

Plastic as a third layer on an ugly night is in his
toolkit. It is also a steamer the next noon. If you add
plastic, you added a chore at 9 a.m. I would rather have
fabric I can leave a crack in than plastic I will forget.

Zone 7: inner hoop is the crop. Outer hoop is the wind.
Together they are why you might keep wood. Alone, the
outer is Zhi’s first winter. Alone, the inner in an open
field is a vegetable trick that may not be enough at
−10°F. He was in a high tunnel. Do not skip that part
when you copy a photo of a little hoop.

Zone 9: an inner hoop on a fig is usually theater. Use a
blanket on the one freak night. Do not build a second
house inside a house you should have stripped.

8a: stage the inner hoop like a fire extinguisher. Put it
on when the radio is honest. Take it off when the house
itself is enough. A January Thursday at 80°F under two
covers is a wake you did not want. Crack the outer, pull
the inner, go drink coffee. The sandwich is not your
identity.

Boyer also said low tunnels are cheap season in the *open
field*, not only inside a high tunnel. That is a vegetable
truth. For figs in 7–8a, an inner hoop without an outer
house is a maybe. I would use it on a short cordon I can
watch, not on a 8-foot bush I cannot wrap. The method
fits the shape. Shape the plant to the method, or pick a
different method. A chandelier under a 64-inch hoop is a
joke the ice will get.

I write “inner on / inner off” on the same page as the
lows. If I cannot remember last March, I will cook this
March. The sandwich is only as smart as the notebook.
Boyer had minute logs. I have a page. The page is the
minimum.

A low hoop that sags onto buds is a wet rag. Reset the
wire. A sag is not a style.

I will not inner-cover a chandelier. Shape first, sandwich second. A 64-inch hoop is a measurement, not a suggestion. If the wood is above it, I cut the wood or I skip the night and take the dieback. Those are the choices. A sagging tent over an 8-foot bush is a joke the ice will finish.
