A house does not make a late fig early. It gives a fig more
days. If the name wanted November in a California valley, a
tunnel in 8a may still hand you green fruit and a story. Plant
names that already know how to finish, then let the roof
steal a few more weeks. That is the variety chapter. It is
not a catalog.

Gao only wanted to talk about two names in southern Ohio:
Hardy Chicago and a Brown Turkey they trusted. Chicago was
smaller — about 18 g — and earlier by roughly three weeks
outdoors. Brown Turkey was larger and later. In a tunnel both
got a longer harvest. That is a cold-limit list, not a flavor
list. I will not pretend it is our menu.

Boyer’s 2024 ranking, in a 6b house with a second cover, is a
different menu: Longue d’Aout first and most consistent, then
Dr. Gowaty, Ronde de Bordeaux, Atreano. Pastillier dropped
and did not finish. Teramo, which is early outdoors for some
people, was not early for him under plastic and liked to
split. I steal the humility. A name’s reputation is a climate.
Move the climate, and the reputation has to re-earn itself.

Zhi kept the ones that fruited and removed the ones that did
not. She described, without catalog names, a purple-skinned
early fig with red flesh and a long pick, a green-firm fig
with red flesh she thought was commercial, a thin-skinned
honey-green that bruised and invited ants, and a small purple
that was late and not worth the wait. That is a selection
process. It is also permission to evict a romantic name from
expensive space.

What I will plant under plastic in 8a, if the house is for
fruit and not for a museum: Chicago Hardy / Bensonhurst
(small, early, a floor), Improved Celeste and the tight-eye
Celeste types (humid South, closed eye, a rain week), LSU
Purple and the LSU set that was bred for the wet South,
Ronde de Bordeaux (small, flavor, can be early), a Longue
d’Aout if I want Boyer’s workhorse, and one or two honey
figs I already know will finish on a wall. I will not fill a
14×32 with Black Mission, Kadota, and a late Smyrna that
needs a wasp. Common figs. Persistent. No caprification
theater.

Zone 7 should bias earlier and hardier. The house is buying
October. Do not spend the October you bought on a name that
needs it just to start. Zone 9 can play more, because the
year is long, but a house in 9 is a rain roof — tight-eye
still earns its keep. 8a is a short list plus a few
experiments in pots you can evict.

“Brown Turkey” is a later draft. I will only say here that a
tag is not a genotype. Plant a name you can taste to type,
from a person you would call on the phone. The steel is
expensive. The wrong stick in the steel is more expensive.
Finish is a habit. The house is a helper. It is not a
translator.

I will taste a house fig next to an outdoor fig of the same name
before I declare the house a flavor machine. Sometimes the house
fig is sweeter because it stayed on longer. Sometimes it is
watery because I watered a mood. The name did not change. The
operator did. If the outdoor tree already finishes and tastes
like itself, the house has to beat that fruit, not just beat a
frost date. Flavor is a variety plus a week. Steel is only the
week.
