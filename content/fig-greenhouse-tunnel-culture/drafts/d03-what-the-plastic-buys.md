Plastic is not magic. It is a filter. It lets light in, it knocks
weather back, and it lies about night. If you know which of those
three you are buying, you will like the house. If you think you are
buying a climate, you will hate July.

The first thing film buys in a humid South is a dry fruit. Open-eye
figs sour when rain sits in the eye. Split is a crack after a swing.
Both get worse when the sky pays and then pays again. A roof does
not stop a hose binge. It does stop the August thunderstorm from
using your fig as a cup. That alone has talked more people into a
hoop than any yield chart.

The second thing it buys is time. In Maryland 7A, Zhi watched tunnel
figs move toward September instead of a mid-October race with the
first average frost around the 15th, and she kept picking into early
November when the plastic was still a blanket. At Piketon, Gao’s
2019 tunnel fruit started around early September and ran into
November. Outside, the same idea died when the air sat on 32°F and
the fruit was still green. Those are not our trees. They are the
reason I believe a house can be a harvest tool in a short year.

The third thing it buys is wind. A fig in a chute between two
buildings loses heat and water faster than a fig in a still corner.
Film is a windbreak that also happens to be a roof. I like that in
January. I fight it in July.

Here is what plastic does not buy. It does not buy a furnace. Zhi
put water barrels on the north side and watched the tunnel night
fall to the outside night. The film is a solar collector until the
sun leaves. Then it is a bag. Coleman’s double-cover trick — the one
Boyer used as a low tunnel inside the high tunnel — is a second
filter, not a heater. I will write that later. I will not let a
sales page tell you single-layer film is a Zone 7 furnace.

It does not buy summer air conditioning. A closed hoop at noon in
8a is a car. Boyer recorded 120°F under row cover in spring and
cooked a whole Longue d’Aout row when he left the cover on into
mid-April. That was Pennsylvania. Our July is not kinder.

It does not buy you a variety. A late Mission under plastic is still
a late Mission. You may get two extra weeks. You may not get
October-to-ripe if the name wanted November in California.

Zone 7 is buying time and a dry November week. Zone 9 is buying a
dry fruit in a rainy month, and it had better be able to take the
roof or the sides off when the month is also a cooker. 8a is buying
a little of both and paying for it in the vent schedule.

I walk a house like I walk a pot. I ask what it is doing today, not
what the brochure said in January. If the fruit is dry and the wood
is brown, the plastic earned its keep. If the leaves are rusted and
the tips are cooked and the night was the same as the yard, the
plastic was a costume. Take it seriously or take it down. Do not
leave a costume over a crop and call it culture.

One more buy that does not show up on a kit page: you can pick in
a rain. That sounds small until you have a flat of soft fruit and
a Gulf afternoon and no roof. Outdoor figs wait. Tunnel figs can
be walked. Zhi said fruit needs to be picked now, not later. I
believe her. A house that lets you harvest on a wet day is doing a
job even if the yield chart is boring.

A last thing plastic buys, if you let it: a place to hang a
thermometer and tell the truth. Outside air is a rumor you get
from a phone. Inside air is a number you wrote down at pot height.
I would rather have a $12 sensor and a notebook than a second
layer of marketing film. The plastic is the room. The notebook is
how you learn the room. Without the notebook you will repeat the
same July and call it bad luck.
