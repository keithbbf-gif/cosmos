# Fig greenhouse, hoophouse, and tunnel culture — Zone 7–9

Staged draft set. Not published copy. Not a kit catalog. Not a promise that
plastic turns Arkansas into California.

These pieces are for a person who already has a fig, or is about to spend money
on a house, and wants the plastic told in a human voice. The center of gravity
is **USDA Zone 8a** (average annual extreme minimum **10–15°F**), rural South
Arkansas heat, clay, thunderstorms, and a collection that lives mostly in
**3- and 5-gallon** pots with a smaller in-ground set. Zone 7 and Zone 9 sit
in the room the whole time, because 8a people borrow a Zone 7 winter house
and a Zone 9 summer vent schedule and get hurt when they borrow the wrong one.

This pack **unpacks** the greenhouse sentence on
[Winter Protection](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) — pots go to a
garage, shed, or greenhouse that stays above **40°F**, and indoor heat is
refused because figs still need a dormant period — and the fall-airlayer
line on
[Setting Fall Airlayers in Zones 6–9](https://figroots.com/2025/09/18/setting-fall-airlayers-in-zones-6-9/).
It does not rewrite those pages. It does not invent a second A/B trial. The
**fig shuffle** is a winter shop. A tunnel with figs in the ground is a
different crop.

## Voice

First person, a yard in 8a, neighbors in 7 and 9. **PapaFig (Keith Chambers)**
writes the house, the row, the vents, and the winter. **Jack Chambers** writes
the shop bench when the house is being used as a winter room for cups and
pots — not as an orchard. Do not mash the pens. Catalog language and
“year-round fig greenhouse” recipes get pushed off the table. If a number
is already on FigRoots (shuffle above 40°F, no indoor heat, pot-up before
June) or in a named paper (OSU Piketon, SARE FNE24-104 Maryland, SARE
FNE22-003 Pennsylvania, University of Kentucky market floor), the draft
cites that page. It does not invent a second trial.

## Do not treat this as

- A substitute for your county extension or a snow-load engineer
- A heated-glass business plan
- Advice to leave row cover on into a 120°F April afternoon
- A promise that water barrels keep a Zone 7 night above kill
- Variety identity. “Brown Turkey” in commerce is a crowd.

## Sources that informed the set (not copied)

- FigRoots: [Winter Protection](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/),
  [Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/),
  [An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/),
  [Setting Fall Airlayers in Zones 6–9](https://figroots.com/2025/09/18/setting-fall-airlayers-in-zones-6-9/)
- Gao and Slaughter, OSU South Centers / Ohioline HYG-1439, *Growing Hardy
  Figs in Ohio* (Piketon 2017–2020): high-tunnel harvest from early
  September into November; first-three-year ripe yield about **2–5 lb**
  per plant inside vs about **1 lb** outside; Hardy Chicago ~18 g and
  earlier; Brown Turkey larger and later
- Zhi, SARE FNE24-104, Thousand Springs Orchard, Maryland Zone 7A: first
  year ~fivefold yield and earlier ripening; second year wood died unless
  buried under woodchips; water barrels did not hold the night; 8–10 ft
  spacing; keep two or three branches
- Boyer, SARE FNE22-003, Pennsylvania Zone 6b: Japanese stepover cordon
  plus low tunnel inside a high tunnel; ground under row cover **20–23°F**
  warmer than outside ground in Feb–Mar 2024; first harvest 21 July 2024;
  230.98 lb in 2024; row-cover cook of Longue d’Aout in mid-April
- Ernst, University of Kentucky (cited in HYG-1439): roughly **10 lb**
  per plant sold at least **$3/lb** as a profit floor — a paper floor,
  not our orchard
- USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map: 8a = 10–15°F average annual extreme
  minimum
- Coleman, *The Winter Harvest Handbook* (double cover as a zone move) —
  cited the way Boyer cited it, as a method, not as fig gospel

Field practice still outruns paper. The drafts say so.
