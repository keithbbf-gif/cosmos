# Paula Leslie

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

A fluoro loop can show residue. It cannot tell you what a life is for. Paula Leslie’s essays and talks — a long University of Pittsburgh stretch, later a UK address on public pages — are the literature this profile uses when the cup becomes a moral object. Risk feeding. End-of-life swallowing. The difference between a hazard and a harm. She was living when these drafts were staged (September 2026). Published papers and lectures only. This profile will not decide a stranger’s last meal.

## Hazard is not harm

Leslie’s neighborhood is the sentence hospitals keep skipping: not every documented risk is a reason to empty the cup. Thirst is a harm. Dryness is a harm. A pneumonia that might happen is a different kind of sentence from a pneumonia that did. Families hear “NPO” as abandonment or as rescue. Both hearings have been wrong. Both have been recorded.

Pittsburgh as a workplace sits next to Coyle’s instrumentation-and-ethics line in this series. Two consciences, one city, different tools. The later UK address is a continuation, not a conversion narrative. This profile will not invent one.

“Risk feeding” as a British-influenced phrase is easy to hear as a dare. In Leslie’s neighborhood it is closer to a documented choice: a person, or someone with authority to speak for them, accepts a hazard because dryness or a tube would be the larger harm. The paperwork around that choice is a later institutional object. This magazine is not the paperwork.

American NPO culture and UK risk-feeding culture are not mirrors. Leslie’s career crossing the ocean is a fact, not a conversion story. Read the papers dated on each side of the water as themselves.
