# Walter B. Cannon

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

Prairie du Chien, Wisconsin, 19 October 1871. Cambridge and Boston for the long career. New Hampshire, 1 October 1945. The obituaries will talk about homeostasis and the sympathetic nervous system. This profile talks about a student in Bowditch’s laboratory in 1896 who put bismuth subnitrate in a goose’s meal so a new kind of light would draw a stomach.

## A student, then a chair

Cannon entered Harvard’s medical world as a young man in a hurry to use the Röntgen tube for motion, not for broken bones. The 1898 *American Journal of Physiology* papers — stomach; esophagus with A. Moser — are the swallow shelf. *The Mechanical Factors of Digestion* (Arnold, 1911) is the book-length version. Then the career widens: shock, emotion, the wartime nervous system, *The Wisdom of the Body*. The swallow does not vanish. It gets out-shouted.

He became the kind of Harvard physiologist other societies name medals after. The Society of Gastrointestinal Radiologists’ Walter B. Cannon Medal is a later memory habit. Donner won it in 1983. The medal does not make Cannon a radiologist in the hospital sense. He was a physiologist who lent radiology a method.

Prairie du Chien is an easy boyhood slide. Skip it unless you are writing a different magazine. The usable scene is Bowditch’s laboratory and a goose with a long neck, 1896–97, a student betting that Röntgen’s toy could draw a viscus in motion. The 1898 esophagus paper with Moser is the swallow shelf. Homeostasis is the fame that ate the shelf. This profile puts the shelf back.

He wrote as a man who liked a clear sentence. *The Mechanical Factors of Digestion* is still readable if you can tolerate 1911 manners. The later fame — *Bodily Changes in Pain, Hunger, Fear and Rage*; the emergency theory of the adrenals — made him a public physiologist. GI radiology kept the student. Speech-language pathology, when it is honest, keeps the student too: the meal as a thing that can be stained.

## What later people kept

The opaque meal. The idea that motility is a public fact. The humility, when he is at his best, that a plate is a shadow of a movement and not the movement.

What they discarded, or should have: the casual leap from “Cannon” to “barium swallow” to “cookie swallow.” Bismuth first. Barium later. Modified textures much later. Three objects. One ancestor.

## Residue

A Wisconsin birth and a 1945 death, a month shy of seventy-four. Geese in a Boston laboratory. A later dark room that still believes a meal can be stained into honesty. The residue is the stain.
