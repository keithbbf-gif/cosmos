# Bronwyn Jones

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.


Atlases are arguments. *Normal and Abnormal Swallowing: Imaging in Diagnosis and Therapy* (Springer, 1991), edited with Martin W. Donner, argued that the swallow had a radiology you could teach from plates, not only from a hallway adjective. Bronwyn Jones is the Hopkins radiologist whose name sits on that spine and on the papers that followed it. She was living when this pack was staged. This profile uses published work and institutional pages only.

## After the founder

Donner opened the center and the journal. Jones is what a field looks like when the founder dies in 1992 and the pictures still have to be read on a Monday. The later edition of the atlas, and the teaching that went with it, are the continuation. Swallowing radiology at Hopkins did not become an SLP service because an SLP wrote a book in Evanston. It remained a radiology with collaborators. That sequence matters. Students who meet VFSS only as an SLP competency sometimes forget the people who owned the tower.

Her papers treat the pharynx and the esophagus as one crossing that happens to be inconveniently fast. The adult stroke swallow and the structural swallow both appear. This pack will not inventory them. The point is a career spent making the image a shared note.

Springer atlases have a look: dense plates, short legends, a reader who is expected to already know a swallow. Jones’s book with Donner is of that family. Later fellows used it the way ENT residents used Jackson — as a thing you could open when the hallway adjective failed. The second edition and the teaching files that followed are why she is not a footnote to 13 April 1992. A field that only remembers founders is a field that cannot staff a Monday.

Faculty pages will list committees and courses. This pack will not scrape them into a paragraph. The atlas and the Hopkins address are the public facts that earn the slot.

## What later people kept

The atlas habit. The refusal to let “barium swallow” mean only a column in the chest. A Hopkins address that still means something on a CV.

What they had to argue with: American SLP training that sometimes treated the radiologist as a button-pusher. A good atlas is a rebuke to that.
