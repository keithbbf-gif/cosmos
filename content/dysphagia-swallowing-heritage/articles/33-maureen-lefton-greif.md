# Maureen Lefton-Greif

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

If Wisconsin is Arvedson’s children’s hospital, Johns Hopkins is Maureen A. Lefton-Greif’s academic address. Pediatric dysphagia reviews, airway-and-swallow papers, later editions of the pediatric textbook that already had Arvedson’s name on it — a career spent making the infant and child meal a Hopkins subject after Bosma’s atlas and Donner’s adult center had already made the building famous for other ages. She was living when this pack was staged. Published work only.

## A building that already had a swallow

Hopkins did not need Lefton-Greif to discover deglutition. It needed someone to keep the pediatric question from being an adult study with the milliampere turned down. Radiation in a small body, FEES in a small nose, the child who never gets either picture — those are her neighborhood in the reviews. The parent as historian is in the neighborhood too. A magazine will not tell that parent how to feed tonight.

The later shared textbook spine is a truce between two American pediatric addresses. Truces are historical objects. So are single-author myths. This pack will not pick a founder.

Hopkins pediatrics already had airway and GI in the building. Lefton-Greif’s reviews are what it looks like when an SLP writes those services into one swallow paragraph without pretending they are one protocol. Congenital airway, chronic lung, the child who aspirates and still needs calories — the papers keep putting growth next to the picture. Adult dysphagia training often forgets growth. Pediatric papers cannot.

The later shared textbook spine with Arvedson is a printed truce between Baltimore and Milwaukee. Students who treat it as a single brand have not looked at the title pages.

## What later people kept

A Hopkins pediatric swallow literature. A name students meet after they meet Arvedson, or before, depending on the syllabus.

What they had to argue with: the adult cookie as a default, and the NICU as the only pediatric story.

## Residue

A Baltimore building and a review article a later team still assigns. The residue is the child who is not a small Logemann patient.
