# James F. Bosma

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

1916. Death on Friday, 22 June 2001, of heart failure, at the University of Maryland Medical Center. The Baltimore *Sun* printed the obituary on the 28th. He was eighty-five. Birth day is not forced in this pack. James F. Bosma is here because pediatric dysphagia has a cine-and-atlas ancestor, and because the ancestor sat, later, in Martin Donner’s adult swallowing center as if the crossing of air and food did not care about your age.

## Stockholm, then Bethesda

From 1959 to 1961 he was at Karolinska Institutet, looking at infant cry as a motion with John Lind and H. M. Truby. The 1965 *Acta Paediatrica* supplement is still a shock if you expect a speech paper: cine, acoustic traces, neonatal transition, swallow distortions when the upper pharynx is the wrong shape. In 1961 he joined the National Institute of Dental Research. Oral sensation and perception. Anatomical plates. He retired from NIH in 1982 and did not retire from the subject.

Johns Hopkins University Press published *Anatomy of the Infant Head* in 1986. Four hundred-odd pages. *New Born Infant Cry* had already appeared in 1965. The atlas is the object a later feeding-team student can still lift. It is not a protocol. It is a refusal to treat the infant head as a cute small adult.

He continued clinically with the Johns Hopkins Swallowing Center and as a research member in neonatology at Maryland. Donner’s memorial in *Dysphagia* would later come from Bosma’s hand — a pediatric physiologist writing the radiologist down. That crossing is a fact, not a merger.

The Baltimore *Sun* obituary is careful about the late appointments: Hopkins Swallowing Center, Maryland neonatology, a man who had retired from NIH and not from the infant. That is the career shape. A later feeding-team syllabus that starts in 1993 has skipped the 1965 supplement and the 1986 atlas. Skipping is how a profession stays young. It is not how a heritage pack should behave.

## What later people kept

Suck–swallow–breathe as a coordination you can film. The atlas habit. The idea that a congenital shape problem is a swallow problem.

What they discarded: some of the older positional folklore that attached itself to his name in hallway talk. What this pack will not do is reprint a prone-feeding recipe from a 1960 *Acta* paper as if it were advice. History of a publication is allowed. A magazine instruction is not.

## Residue

An unnamed 1916, a June 2001 death in Baltimore, a 1986 book. The residue is a flickering infant hyoid on a reel a later team will quote without watching.
