## A laryngologist who went lower

Killian’s training is the German university ladder of the late nineteenth century — laryngology as a young specialty still arguing with general surgery. Direct bronchoscopy was not a wellness visit. It was a bet that you could enter a tube people had been approaching from the neck or not at all. The bet killed people when it went wrong. It saved people when it went right. Both sentences belong on the page.

Killian’s triangle — the hypopharyngeal weak spot later attached to Zenker’s diverticulum — is the quieter residue for a swallow audience. You can spend a career talking about pouches without saying his name. The name is still on the anatomy.

Berlin and Freiburg sit on different versions of the CV depending on which memorial you open. This pack will not invent a tidy ladder. What is stable is the 1897 removal and the later teaching that made direct endoscopy a German export. American visitors came. Jackson is the American who made the export a clinic. ## What later people kept

The permission to look *down*. The idea that a foreign body is a case, not a rumor. A triangle on a board.

What they discarded: the rigidity, the theater, the casualness with which early endoscopy treated a demonstration as a public hour. Flexible scopes and outpatient rooms are descendants, not copies. Jackson will Americanize the clinic. Killian is the European door.

If you only remember the 1897 afternoon, you miss the quieter anatomy. Killian’s triangle is how a later swallow clinician names a weak wall without saying Freiburg. Names that detach from dates are still history. They are just history that has forgotten its ticket.

## Residue

A Mainz birth, a 1921 death, a Freiburg afternoon that later slides still show. The residue is a triangle and a tube.
