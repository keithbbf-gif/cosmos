# Nectar, Honey, and the Long Road to IDDSI

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

For a generation of American hospitals the swallow meal came in a folk dialect: thin, nectar, honey, pudding. The National Dysphagia Diet Task Force published *National Dysphagia Diet: Standardization for Optimal Care* with the American Dietetic Association in 2002 and tried to make the dialect official. Anyone who has stood in two kitchens knows how official a word like “nectar” is. One hospital’s nectar is another’s rumor. A third hospital uses a brand thickener and a laminated card. The patient drinks whatever arrives.

In 2013 an international group convened as the International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative. The framework papers — Cichero and colleagues, *Dysphagia* and allied journals, 2016–17 — tried to replace the dialect with levels and with tests you can do to a liquid or a solid without asking it to taste like a flower. Catriona Steele’s Toronto group is one of the laboratories that treated thin liquid as a measurable object rather than a vibe. Both get lives in this pack.

This essay is about the vocabulary. It is not a chart for a refrigerator.

## Why texture became a specialty

If you believe a thin liquid is the dangerous one, you will thicken. That belief is older than IDDSI. It sits in Logemann-era teaching, in nursing practice, in the thickener industry that grew up to meet a fear. Some of the belief is physiology — flow, timing, a airway that is slow to close. Some of it is culture. A thickened cup looks like care. An empty cup looks like neglect. Families can taste the difference even when they cannot name a level.

The NDD was an American attempt to stop the worst of the dialect problem. It also froze a set of food-names — “dysphagia pureed,” “mechanical soft” — that kitchens already knew how to argue about. IDDSI was an attempt to stop the *national* problem: Japan, the UK, Australia, Canada, and the United States were not speaking the same meal. A research paper that said “honey-thick” was already untranslatable. A traveler with a printed diet card was a person carrying a local faith. Cichero’s public work is, among other things, a translator’s work.

Industry is in this story whether a magazine likes it or not. Powder thickeners and pre-thickened drinks made a fear billable. Some of that product paid for research. Some of it paid for a laminated card that outran the paper. Say both.

## What the decade thought it was solving

Choking. Aspiration. The lawsuit. The kitchen that could not reproduce yesterday’s tray. The paper that could not be compared with the paper from another country.

Two or three institutions, not twelve: the 2002 ADA/NDD task force; the 2013 IDDSI convening; the labs (Queensland, Toronto, and others) that agreed to treat texture as an instrumented fact.

## Who was left out

Pleasure. Taste. The person who stops drinking because the cup is glue. Some of the later ethics literature — Leslie’s neighborhood — is a rebuke to texture-as-virtue. Put that rebuke in a sentence. Also left out: households that never saw a laminated card and thickened with whatever was in the pantry. A heritage series that only cites task forces has forgotten the pantry.

Infants, again, live on a different shelf. Pediatric textures have their own fights. Do not paste IDDSI onto a 1965 Bosma film and call it continuity.

## Residue

The residue is a number on a tray ticket and a set of syringes and forks in a training video this pack will not reprint as instructions. Readers who want to know what their parent should drink will not be answered here. They will be told that the words on the ticket have a history, that the history includes a 2002 American diet and a 2013 international committee, and that a licensed clinician still has to attach those words to a person.
