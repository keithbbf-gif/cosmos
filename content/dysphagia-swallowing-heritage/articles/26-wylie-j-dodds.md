# Wylie J. Dodds

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

> **Portrait placeholder.** No public-domain or clearly licensed portrait located as of the pack date. See `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` for hunt status. Do not invent or generate a substitute likeness.


Wylie J. Dodds — Jerry in the Milwaukee hallway — died in 1992 of a brain tumor. He was fifty-eight, say the Society of Abdominal Radiology and the motility-society memorials. This pack therefore prints 1934–1992 and flags the birth year as arithmetic from those sentences, not as a birth certificate. He had kept meeting trainees weekly through three years of disability. That sentence is in the memorials. It is enough house detail.

## San Francisco, then Wisconsin

Training under Alexander Margulis (the memorials’ spelling varies: Magulis/Margulis) in the San Francisco GI-radiology world, after service in the U.S. Air Force. Then the Medical College of Wisconsin, recruited into a young department that wanted motility as a laboratory, not only as a complaint. His group’s bet: record contractility and *watch* the bolus in the same act. Cat and human. Esophagus first. The pharynx, later, in papers that put his name next to Jeri Logemann’s and Peter Kahrilas’s.

The paper the SAR page calls most famous is not a swallow-therapy paper. It is “Mechanisms of Gastroesophageal Reflux in Patients with Reflux Esophagitis,” *New England Journal of Medicine*. More than 350 manuscripts, says the same page. The Walter Cannon Award sat on his shelf too. Milwaukee is also Reza Shaker’s later address. The city kept a motility habit after 1992.

The feline esophagus paper from the fellowship years is the origin story the motility memorials like: a cat, a tube that records pressure, a barium column you can watch at the same time. It is a small paper with a large permission. Milwaukee then scaled the permission into a human lab and a pile of trainees. Logemann’s physiology citations in the 1980s are, in part, a record of that scaling. An SLP book and a GI radiologist’s traces can share a swallow without sharing a credential.

## What later people kept

The combined picture: pressure plus barium. The idea that an SLP’s swallow question could be a GI physiologist’s question. A research award with his name on it, and a DRS lecture named Dodds–Donner — two men, one year.

What they discarded: the hope that one laboratory style would settle every argument about the UES. What this pack will not do is turn a manometry tracing into a home program.

## Residue

An inferred 1934, a 1992 death, a Milwaukee lab. The residue is a tracing and a picture stapled together, and a lecture that still says both names.
