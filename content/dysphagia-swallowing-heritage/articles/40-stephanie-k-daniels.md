# Stephanie K. Daniels

*Educational history for SLPWOW. Not a treatment plan and not a substitute for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

A clean voice after a sip is not a clearance. Stephanie K. Daniels’s papers from VA and academic stroke services in the 1990s and after — clinical indicators, clusters of signs, the chance a later instrumental exam would show trouble — are the American literature this pack uses for that rebuke. She was living when this pack was staged. University of Houston appears on later public pages. DRS Gold Medal, 2026, on the society’s awards list, is a footnote. Published papers only. No item list.

## Clusters, not a stamp

The indicator papers are careful in ways the pocket card is not. A cluster raises a probability. It does not name a disease. It does not cancel a picture. Daniels’s career is easy to flatten into “the bedside lady.” Flattening is the misuse. The papers are about how often the bedside lies, and in which direction.

VA stroke wards as a workplace matter. So does the later textbook-and-teaching life that carried those papers into programs that had never seen a Houston or New Orleans ward. This pack will not write a campus novel.

VA stroke services in the 1990s were a particular census: men, often, with vascular histories, a rehab bed, a fluoro slot you had to fight for. The indicator papers come out of that census. They travel badly when a later hospital treats them as universal folklore. Daniels’s later teaching life is, in part, the work of saying how far a cluster can travel. This pack will not do that calibration for a reader. It will say the calibration exists.

A pocket card with her name on it, if you have seen one, is a descendant. Descendants are not the papers. The papers still have methods sections. The cards, usually, do not. If you cannot point to a year and a sample, you are holding folklore that borrowed her name.

## What later people kept

Distrust of a single sip. A research dialect for “signs.”

What they had to argue with: the laminated descendant, and the opposite sin — ignoring the bedside because a paper once said it lies.
