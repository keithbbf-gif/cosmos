# Claims Guardrails — Aphasia History Pack

Staged. Educational history only.

## Hard stops

- No treatment protocols, home programs, or “try this if…” language.
- No living-patient stories, even anonymized composites.
- No invented quotations.
- No generated faces; no stock “stroke survivor smiling with therapist” on a nineteenth-century essay.
- No claim that SLPWOW or WOW Therapies invented, owns, or uniquely practices a historical method.
- No “gold-standard treatment” language. Tests are historical objects, not endorsements.

## Historical patients

Leborgne (“Tan”), Lelong, and Lordat’s 1825 self-report are already in the public scientific literature. Name them as history. Do not invent inner lives. Do not write their wards as entertainment.

## Living figures

Martha Taylor Sarno, Audrey Holland, Andrew Kertesz, and M. Marsel Mesulam (and any other living subject) are treated as public scientific figures. Stick to published books, tests, and papers. No private biography. Portraits: hunt only; almost certainly not public domain.

## Uncertainty

If a death year, first-edition year, or “who coined it” is contested, the article says so. A missing date in `INDEX.md` is better than a guessed one.

## Relationship to the profession pack

This series may retell 1861 and 1874 because they are aphasia’s public events. It must not copy the sibling pack’s sentences. New scene, new residue, deeper argument.
