This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jean-Baptiste Bouillaud was born in 1796 and died in 1881. He was a Paris physician of the generation that took Gall’s cortex seriously and wanted hospital proof. In the 1820s he argued that the frontal lobes were necessary for articulated speech. He collected cases of loss of speech with frontal disease and cases of frontal disease with loss of speech, which is not the same collection, though it is easy to print them as if they were.

He is remembered for a wager: money against anyone who could show a severe speech loss without a frontal lesion. Whether the purse was cash or theater, the posture was scientific and a little bullying. Claims you can lose are better than bumps you can only admire. Claims you can lose are also how you stay wrong with confidence.

## The son-in-law and the cue

Ernest Auburtin married into the argument. In 1861, at the Société d’Anthropologie, he told the room that a living man with a frontal lesion and lost speech was the fact they needed. Broca had such a man at Bicêtre. The autopsy arrived in a week that already had a side. Bouillaud’s frontal loyalty is therefore not a footnote to 1861. It is one of the reasons 1861 could happen as a society event rather than as a private curiosity.

The loyalty was too clean. Speech fails from temporal lesions, from subcortical breaks, from a hundred places Bouillaud did not want. Pierre Marie would spend 1906 saying the third frontal convolution was not a throne. Both men can be in one history. The throne was always the problem.

## Other furniture

Bouillaud also wrote about the heart and rheumatism; nineteenth-century Paris did not require a man to be only one disease. Aphasologists who meet him only as “the bet” miss the ordinary consultant. The ordinary consultant is why students existed to hear Auburtin.

He was not a therapist. He did not sit an hour with a naming card. He sat on committees and in wards where the prize was the lesion.

## The consultant’s other organs

Bouillaud wrote about the heart and rheumatism. Paris did not require a man to be only a speech theorist. Aphasiology is greedy: it keeps the bet and mislays the rest of the vita. The bet mattered because it made a claim you could lose. The rest of the vita mattered because students existed to hear Auburtin in 1861.

The frontal loyalty was too clean. Temporal lesions, subcortical breaks, Marie’s 1906 rudeness — all of them are later answers to a man who wanted the lobes to be enough. He was not a fool. He was a man with a favorite place.


Auburtin’s 1861 cue only works if the room already has a side. Bouillaud had been building the side for decades. That is his aphasia vita: not the purse, the preparation. Broca brought the brain. Bouillaud had already told the society what kind of brain to want. Medicine rewards the meeting. It should also reward the years of argument that made the meeting possible.
