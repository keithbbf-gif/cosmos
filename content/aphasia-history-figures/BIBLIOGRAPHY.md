# Bibliography — Aphasia History Pack

Sources consulted for staged copy, 14 September 2026. Prefer the primary paper over a textbook paraphrase.

## Primary and near-primary

- Gesner, J. A. P. *Samlung von Beobachtungen aus der Arzneygelahrtheit und Naturkunde.* Vol. 2. Nördlingen: Beck, 1770. Chapter: “Die Sprachamnesie.”
- Lordat, J. “Analyse de la parole pour servir à la théorie de divers cas d’alalie et de paralalie…” *Journal de la Société de Médecine Pratique de Montpellier* 7 (1843): 333–353, 417–433; 8: 1–17.
- Broca, P. “Remarques sur le siège de la faculté du langage articulé…” *Bulletin de la Société Anatomique de Paris* 6 (1861): 330–357.
- Wernicke, C. *Der aphasische Symptomencomplex.* Breslau: Cohn & Weigert, 1874.
- Lichtheim, L. “On Aphasia.” *Brain* 7 (1885): 433–484.
- Marie, P. “Révision de la question de l’aphasie: la troisième circonvolution frontale gauche ne joue aucun rôle spécial dans la fonction du langage.” *Semaine médicale* 26 (23 May 1906).
- Head, H. *Aphasia and Kindred Disorders of Speech.* 2 vols. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1926.
- Weisenburg, T., and K. E. McBride. *Aphasia: A Clinical and Psychological Study.* New York: Commonwealth Fund, 1935.
- Jakobson, R. *Kindersprache, Aphasie und allgemeine Lautgesetze.* Uppsala, 1941.
- Luria, A. R. *Traumatic Aphasia.* Russian 1947; English, The Hague: Mouton, 1970.
- Goldstein, K. *Language and Language Disturbances.* New York: Grune & Stratton, 1948.
- Penfield, W., and L. Roberts. *Speech and Brain Mechanisms.* Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1959.
- Schuell, H., J. J. Jenkins, and E. Jiménez-Pabón. *Aphasia in Adults.* New York: Harper & Row, 1964.
- Geschwind, N. “Disconnexion Syndromes in Animals and Man.” *Brain* 88 (1965): 237–294, 585–644.
- Critchley, M. *Aphasiology and Other Aspects of Language.* London: Edward Arnold, 1970.
- Goodglass, H., and E. Kaplan. *The Assessment of Aphasia and Related Disorders.* Philadelphia: Lea & Febiger, 1972.
- Mesulam, M.-M. “Slowly Progressive Aphasia without Generalized Dementia.” *Annals of Neurology* 11 (1982): 592–598.
- Kertesz, A. *The Western Aphasia Battery.* New York: Grune & Stratton, 1982.

## Secondary (used, argued with)

- Benton, A. L. “Johann A. P. Gesner on Aphasia.” *Medical History* 9 (1965): 54–60.
- Benton, A. L., and R. J. Joynt. “Three Pioneers in the Study of Aphasia.” *Journal of the History of Medicine* 18 (1963): 381–384.
- Luzzatti, C. “Johann August Philipp Gesner (1738–1801).” *Journal of the History of Neurosciences* 11 (2002): 29–34.
- Eling, P., and H. Whitaker. Histories of aphasiology (various chapters, 2009–).
- Brais, B. “The Third Left Frontal Convolution Plays No Role in Language.” *Neurology* 42 (1992): 690.
- Lecours, A. R., F. Lhermitte, and others on Alajouanine and phonetic disintegration.
- Duchan, J. History of Speech-Language Pathology pages (Buffalo). Used for Sarno and clinic timelines; not as the sole warrant for a birth year.
- LaPointe, L. L., on Katharine McBride (Oxford handbook chapter, 2015).
- Finger, S. *Origins of Neuroscience.* Oxford, 1994. For Gall/Bouillaud context.
- Tesak, J., and C. Code. *Milestones in the History of Aphasia.* Hove: Psychology Press, 2008.
- ASHA Archives (omeka) — association dates only.
