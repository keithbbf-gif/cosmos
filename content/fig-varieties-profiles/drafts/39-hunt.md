E. W. Hunt of Eatonton, Georgia, bred this in the 1920s. [Alabama ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/): small brown, amber pulp tinged strawberry, pyriform with a short distinct neck, **long slender stems to 3/4 inch** that help it shed rain and prevent souring, superb flavor, sweet and rich, not a heavy bearer, well adapted in the rainier South. No synonyms.

A fig with no synonyms is a small miracle. Do not give it one.

Common type. Persistent. No wasp. Jack already defined that on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). LSU’s program used Hunt. You can taste that intention in O’Rourke’s long peduncle note. Different plants. Same idea: hang the fruit off a stalk and let water miss the eye.

## Not a heavy bearer

That sentence is why Hunt disappears from marketplace lists. People want Red Sicilian volume. Hunt wants a wet week and a good bite. I will plant a fig that is scarce and excellent before I plant a sixth Turkey.

I did not name Hunt on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). Alabama’s “superb” is their word. A small rich brown fig with a stem like a wire is something I want on a bench next to Celeste. Celeste I am not a fan of so far. Hunt might be the brown fig that is a conversation. [VERIFY] when the plate repeats. I will not invent a berry score or a brix number I did not record.

The introduction page already said it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. A first-year pot can lie about volume. “Not a heavy bearer” is Alabama’s adult sentence. Do not call the tree shy in year one and throw it away. Do not call it shy in year three if you hat-racked it in January.

Eye: the stem is the trick, not a claim of invisibility. Still pick. Still a storm can win. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said the ostiole is a humidity judge. Hunt’s judge is a hanging door. Photograph the peduncle after rain. That still is the cultivar.

Birds: small brown. They will still find it if it is the only ripe thing. Net or pick. A green fig lasts longer. This is not a green fig.

## Rain is the job it was hired for

UAEX’s souring sentence is water in the eye plus heat after rain. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit sours and many split in an unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year. Hunt was bred so the rain would slide off a long stalk. That is a Southern invention, not a California shipping trait.

TAMU’s [disease handbook](https://plantdiseasehandbook.tamu.edu/food-crops/fruit-crops/fig/) puts Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma in the closed-eye resistant column and Magnolia and Kadota in the open-eye souring column. Hunt is not on that Texas table. Alabama’s rain-shed claim is the closest extension sentence we have. [VERIFY] on *our* week. A long stem does not cancel a balloon. Split is still water and skin. Even water. A drought then a flood will unzip a small fig as surely as a large one.

Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. A day-early Hunt in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A sour brown fig on the ground is a waste of a scarce bearer.

Do not plant Hunt as the only fig if you wanted a bowl every day. Plant it as the wet-week brown. Pair it with a productive berry — Red Sicilian, Syrian Dark #2, Negra — for the weeks Hunt is being itself.

## Wood, rust, winter

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf, defoliation while fruit is trying to finish. Hunt does not have an LSU rust sentence. Rake. Do not leave a wet skirt of last year’s leaves. I will not advertise rust-proof.

Mosaic mottles leaves on a lot of old Southern browns. Mottled is not a reason to discard a tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood off a fruiting Hunt and start a new pot.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. Hunt is not Alma and it is not the LSU Purple rumor.

Cold: Alabama groups Celeste, English Brown Turkey, Alma, and Hardy Chicago in the 15–20°F talk. Hunt is “well adapted in the rainier South,” not a Chicago resprout advertisement. Treat it as a Southern brown that wants last year’s wood if you want this year’s crop until the plant proves it is a Turkey. UAEX already told you a hard late-winter prune on Celeste deletes the year. I will not hat-rack a scarce bearer to prove I can make a standard.

If it dies to the crown, five or six trunks at two feet, TAMU’s freeze chapter, and wait. A scarce bearer on new wood in a short year may give you leaves. Pair it.

## Sister slugs — not synonyms

Hunt has no synonyms on Alabama’s sheet. I will not invent a pile to make this page look busy.

**O’Rourke** is the official LSU Improved Celeste: tan-brown, closed eye, fruit sets upright on a long peduncle and hangs when ripe. Same rain idea. Different plant. Different century. Do not call Hunt an O’Rourke because both hang.

**Celeste / Malta** is the small tight sugar fig. Tight eye, green ostiole until almost ripe, hates a January saw. I am not a fan so far. If your Hunt is Celeste, the stem will say so — Celeste does not grow a 3/4-inch wire to show off.

**Alma** is the 1975 Texas closed-eye tool fig. Golden brown, rot-resistant talk, not a conversation. Different job.

**Osborn Prolific** is the plant Alabama gave long slender stalks to **1 inch**. Bronze to brown, very productive, a synonym pile of its own (Archipel, Hardy Prolific, Neveralla). If your “Hunt” is productive and the stalk is an inch, you may have Osborn. Relabel. Do not fold Osborn into Hunt to tidy a bench.

**Southern Brown Turkey** is the rebound workhorse. Copper, useful, not rich. If your Hunt is that plate and that volume, it is not Hunt.

## How I would run it

One in-ground candidate in the rainiest hole, or a pot you do not forget. Wood from a fruiting Hunt with a visible long peduncle. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Pot up to a **#3** before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick. Do not hat-rack a scarce bearer.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July. Hunt was built for wet weeks, not for a west fry on black plastic.

In-ground in 8a: east wall if you have one, clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots. Do not plant it in last year’s tomato hole. Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how a rain-shed fig writes a tender will.

If a plant labeled Hunt has a short fat neck and a wide eye, it is not Hunt. Relabel. If it never hangs on a long stem, take the name off. The stem is the field mark. Eatonton did not breed a Turkey and forget to tell Auburn.

If the plate is ordinary and the stem is real, keep it as a wet-week tool and graft a berry onto a sister pot. If the plate is superb and the stem is real, keep the mother tree and take wood for people who live in steam. That is the share list Hunt belongs on — not the beginner box.

Write Hunt on the pot that hangs a small rich brown fig on a long stem after rain. If the stem is ordinary, the name is ordinary too. Take it off.
