Champagne is a word that makes a catalog sound expensive. The plant is a Gulf yellow fig that LSU tested as **Golden Celeste** and released in the 2007 group with [Tiger](lsu-tiger) and [O’Rourke](lsu-orourke). Medium fruit. Yellow. Good cold tolerance in their notes. Performs where Celeste thrives. Stagg’s 2025 caption: moderate-sized yellow fruit on upright trees.

It is not [LSU Gold](lsu-gold). Gold is the large one, average cold, later clock. Champagne is the Celeste-shaped idea in a yellow skin. If you merge them because both are yellow, you will prune wrong, pick wrong, and blame Louisiana.

## Golden Celeste was a test name

Test names leak. People still sell Golden Celeste. People still sell Champagne. People sell both as if they were sisters. Official release: Champagne. If your fruit is medium, yellow, Celeste-timed, and the tree is upright, you may have it. If your fruit is huge and late, you may have Gold. If your fruit is small, brown, and sugar, you have [Celeste](celeste) with a hopeful label.

The official six are O’Rourke, Champagne, Tiger, LSU Gold, [LSU Purple](lsu-purple), [Scott’s Black](lsu-scotts-black). [Hollier](lsu-hollier) is the unofficial yellow-with-pink-red-pulp leak people park next to this name. If your “Champagne” cuts pink-red and the seller said Hollier last year, you have a driveway argument, not a release. [Jack Lily](lsu-jack-lily) is unofficial and green. Different job.

I will not invent a pedigree chart. O’Rourke’s program used Celeste and Hunt and whatever else they had. The 1990s evaluation orchard chose what to name. Champagne is what they named.

Common type. No wasp.

## Do not steal Celeste’s field marks

Celeste’s eye stays green until late. Alabama wrote that down. Champagne is not Celeste. Do not steal the field mark. Look at your ostiole. [VERIFY]. They released it for the sticky air. That is not the same sentence as “sealed vault.”

[Kadota](kadota) is the other pale fig people will hang this next to. Kadota is Dottato, canning, open-enough eye for TAMU’s souring table. Champagne is a 2007 Gulf release. [Peter’s Honey](peters-honey) / Lattarula is the Northwest honey pile. [Calimyrna](calimyrna) is a Smyrna that drops. Jack already sorted the types on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). Champagne is common. Persistent. No pollen brush.

O’Rourke is the official Improved Celeste — tan-brown, long peduncle, hangs when ripe. Champagne is the yellow test-name cousin in the same 2007 group. Same climate class talk. Different plate color.

## What the sheets actually wrote

Polozola and Stagg, 2025: tested as Golden Celeste. Good cold tolerance. Medium-sized yellow fruit. The tree performs well in areas where Celeste thrives. Stagg’s photo line: moderate-sized yellow fruit on upright trees that can get large (the 2021 clip said the same size story).

Older AgCenter news (2015): Champagne in the 2007 trio with Tiger and O’Rourke. Purple 1991, Gold 1995. That dating is the news-clip version. The 2025 history is the six-name version I will keep using.

Alabama’s 2023 guide does not give Champagne its own cultivar paragraph the way it gives Gold and Purple. I will not invent an ANR-1145 flavor wheel to fill that hole. Florida HS27 lists Champagne among cultivars UF has not tested. Gulf timing is not Ozark timing. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is a count.

UAEX: common figs for Arkansas. This one qualifies. “Where Celeste thrives” is LSU saying yard fig, not California shipping fig. That is why it belongs in an 8a conversation more honestly than Gold does. It still is not a Chicago Hardy resprout story.

## The 8a week

Pick: morning. Soft. Neck wilt. Treat it as earlier than Gold, in the Celeste neighborhood. [VERIFY] your first soften. A yellow fig that finishes before the worst August storms is worth more than a larger fig that sours. I will not invent a Saline County date.

Rain: a wet week will still split a ripe yellow fig. Souring is water in the eye plus heat. Pick. Do not hang it for a prettier gold because you wanted Gold and got medium.

Birds: yellow is less of a billboard than purple. Less is not none. Net or pick.

Rust: Gulf leaves. Rake litter. Yellow then reddish spots, blisters under the leaf. Do not promote Champagne as rust-proof because Gold got the “moderate” sentence.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. Celeste-class fruit still sulks in a west fry — Alabama already said Celeste turns tough and falls in hot, dry weather. I will not pretend a yellow skin cancels that week.

Pots versus ground: we run hundreds of pots and about 25–30 in-ground. I said that on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). One **#3**. Pot up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. Do not push nitrogen into September. In-ground if it earns the hole — east wall, mulch, water even. A drought-then-flood week splits pale figs the same way it splits Kadota.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree that is Champagne, not a marketplace Golden Celeste with no pictures. The [introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already closed the review window before you taste type.

Stagg said the trees can get large. A **#3** is the prove-out pot. If it earns a hole, give it room the way you would a happy Celeste, not the way you would a juvenile [Tiger](lsu-tiger) that wants to lie down. Upright is the habit they wrote. Do not plant it under a pecan and then call the calendar a liar.

Prune: people hat-rack it like a Turkey because “LSU figs are tough.” O’Rourke hangs on a long peduncle and still wants crop wood. Champagne sits in the Celeste climate class. Leave last year’s wood if you want this year’s fruit. Dead wood only. UAEX already wrote the Celeste prune warning. I will not repeat a January saw and then call Louisiana a liar.

## Culinary, honest

Fresh: honey-leaning yellow, not berry. Lazy Dog had young trees and no plate yet in their writeup. I did not put Champagne on the Fig Jam list. I will eat a yellow fig in a dry week and tell you if it is a conversation when I have repeated fruit. Until then it is a Gulf tool with a party name.

Jam: medium fruit, Celeste-class volume if the tree is happy. Pale jar. If you wanted a kettle of giant gold figs, you wanted Gold. Different draft. Different clock. If the cut is pink-red and the seller said Champagne, look at [Hollier](lsu-hollier) before you write a release name on a leak.

Kid bowl: a ripe yellow fig a child can see. Easier sell than a small brown Celeste if the kid judges with their eyes. Still not a soda. Still not Gold.

If the fruit is yellow and you like it better than Celeste — easy, in my mouth so far — keep it. I am not a fan of Celeste so far. A yellow Celeste-class fig has a chance to change a week without changing my mind about the brown one.

## How I would run it

If you like Celeste’s calendar and want a yellow plate, Champagne is the official name to trial. People expect Gold and get medium. They call it a dud. It is not a dud. It is the other yellow release.

Write Champagne on the pot when the fruit is medium, yellow, earlier than Gold, and repeats. Write Golden Celeste only as a synonym, in parentheses, after the fruit agrees. The test name is history. The plate is the name.
