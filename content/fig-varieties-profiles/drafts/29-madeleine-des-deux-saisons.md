Deux saisons means two seasons. The French named the fig for breba plus main. Collectors buy the name. Then they prune in January, take a late freeze in March, and write a review that Madeleine “doesn’t do two crops here.”

UAEX already told you breba rarely overwinters in Arkansas. Madeleine did not repeal the county.

Common type in the accounts I trust. No wasp. That is better than a San Pedro. The breba is still last year’s wood.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) page is the fork you have to get right before you spend money. Common figs persist without pollen — breba and main, if the wood lives. San Pedro figs persist on the breba and often drop the main crop unless a wasp or a brush brings pollen. Smyrna figs drop both. Desert King / King is the San Pedro Americans actually plant. Alabama filed King as a large breba from late June to August that *sometimes* sets main-crop figs without pollination. Sometimes. Madeleine is sold as a common fig that fruits twice because the wood can. If your Madeleine drops a main crop like a Smyrna, [VERIFY] type. You may have a San Pedro with a French sticker, or a thirsty young plant, or a year.

Royal Vineyard is the San Pedro ANR-1145 already wrote off for the South: brebas only, late frosts destroy the fruit in most years. Do not merge Madeleine into that pile because both promise spring fruit. Different machines.

Condit’s 1955 monograph is full of French names and two-crop talk. I will not pretend I matched our stick to a 1955 plate. The name on the tag is a claim. The wood is the evidence.

## What grower talk says

Early, productive, small-to-medium, a fig people in cooler Europe like because the first crop can beat a short summer. Flavor: mild berry-honey, not a Negra. I did not plate it on The Fig Jam. Consensus, not a signed score. [VERIFY] on our tree.

Keith’s 25 July 2025 list was other fruit. Looking-forward names were Yellow Long Neck, White Madeira #1, CDD Mutante, I-258, and the rest of that sentence. Madeleine was not on it. Jack’s early darks were Syrian Dark #2 and the Unk Greek. Same job class as an early bowl. Different names.

If your Madeleine is huge and late, you have a different French name. Col de Dame is late and necked. Dalmatie is large and green and late. Gold is large and late. Madeleine’s reputation is the opposite direction: get in, get out, maybe twice. If it never brebas even on protected wood, you may have a mislabel or a year. Two years. The introduction page already said 1–3 years to prove type.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) asks, will you try to get breba? That is a real question, not a catalog flourish. Breba means you will protect last year’s wood. Pots you shuffle. No hat-rack. A cover on a late freeze after budbreak. If you will not do that work, buy a main-crop common fig and stop paying extra for a French sentence.

GDD still matters. Early is the hope. Early is not a date I will invent for Saline County. Write the first breba swell and the first main-crop soften on the tag. Empty notebook is allowed. Empty notebook plus a two-season tag is a lie.

## Rain, split, sour, birds

Small-to-medium helps. Tight-eye talk on Madeleine is mixed in the forums. I will not stamp tight without a photograph of *our* ostiole after a storm. [VERIFY]. UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. ANR-1145: the riper the figs, the more they split and sour. Pick on the soft. Neck wilt. Morning.

A two-crop name does not sew the eye shut. Spring breba in a wet May is still a balloon. Main crop in a wet August is still a balloon. Even water. Drought then flood throws crop — the how-to pack already said figs drop for water, heat, type, a Smyrna without pollen, a young plant. Do not blame Madeleine for a hose you forgot.

Birds: early fruit is the first bowl they find. Dark-enough or not, they will sample. Green-yellow clones hide a little. [VERIFY] skin color on the plant you have — listings disagree, and I will not paint a fruit I have not plated. Net or pick.

Sister slugs: Ronde de Bordeaux is the small early dark people plant for a short year. Pastilière is the early dark with a drop reputation. Celeste is the Southern early sugar fig that hates a January saw. Madeleine is the French two-season word. Do not merge four early figs because they all beat Madeira to the bowl.

Olympian is the other “keep the wood” sales pitch — large purple, Northwest. Different fruit. Same winter lecture. Desert King is the San Pedro lecture. Read that page before you plant a second two-crop name.

## How I would run it

If you want two-crop theater, protect old wood: pot, shuffle, no hat-rack, cover a late freeze. Then count. If breba shows, the name earned a little of itself. If only main crop shows, you still have a common fig. Relabel your expectations, not necessarily the pot.

Do not plant this as the only fig for a child. Plant a main-crop common fig. Plant Madeleine as a bonus if you like French names and you will do the winter work.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a tree that actually fruited. Pot up before June. One **#3** until it proves the name. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

**6–8 hours** of sun. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. South shade plus water is already on Pick The Right Fig. Nematodes: pots.

Hardiness: I will not call Madeleine Chicago-hardy. ANR-1145’s 15–20°F conversation is the old favorites. A French two-season fig in 8a is a pot-honest trial. In-ground after a mild string of winters if you like what you ate. A late freeze after budbreak is cruel to any early mover. Pots you can roll. In-ground you cover or you take the hit.

Do not feed for leaves in September. Succulent late wood is how a two-season fig writes a tender will and then misses the first season next year.

If the main crop is early, productive, and good enough in a dry week, keep it even if breba never shows. That is still a useful common fig. If the main crop is late and ordinary, you paid for a name. Graft. We graft. We do not perform funerals for live roots.

Write Madeleine des Deux Saisons on the pot when an early common fig repeats and, if you protected the wood, sometimes brebas. Write “two seasons” in the notebook only on years you counted two. The notebook is allowed to be emptier than the tag.
