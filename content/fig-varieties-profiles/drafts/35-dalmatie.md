Dalmatie — Dalmatian in some listings, not the Brunswick synonym Alabama hung on Magnolia — is a large green fig with a strawberry cut in the stories. Collectors like the size. Size is a late tax.

Common type. No wasp. Green ripe fruit. Birds miss a few. You will miss a few if you wait for it to look “ripe-colored.” Feel.

I did not plate Dalmatie on The Fig Jam. Consensus flavor: rich strawberry, a fig people compare to a good Adriatic on a bigger body. [VERIFY].

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer: common figs persist without pollen. Dalmatie sits in that bucket in the accounts I use. You do not buy a wasp. You do not brush an ostiole. San Pedro is King — main crop often wants pollen. Smyrna is Calimyrna — both crops want pollen. If your Dalmatie drops every large green fig at marble size, look at water and age first. Then type. [VERIFY]. A late common fig that never finishes is usually growing degree days, not a missing wasp. A Smyrna without a wasp is a leaf bush. This plant should not be that machine.

## Name pile

Brunswick / Magnolia / Madonna / Dalmatian is a bronze-yellow open-eye preserve fig. Different plant. Alabama listed Brunswick as bronzy yellow, open eyes, ruined by rain, synonyms Dalmatian, Madonna, Magnolia. Condit folded Brunswick into a twelve-name pile in 1955. TAMU’s disease handbook put Magnolia with Kadota in the open-eye trouble column. That is the kettle fig. That is not this page.

If your “Dalmatie” is bronze and open and early-mid, you may have Magnolia. If it is grass-green and huge and late, you may have this. If a seller writes “Dalmatian Magnolia Dalmatie,” they are making a smoothie out of two continents.

Verte is another green strawberry fig, smaller, and Keith already said Verte splits on us. Different draft. Alabama has two entries — Green Ischia and Verte — in one pile. Collectors argue. I will not settle it here. I will say: our Verte splits. Dalmatie is the large late green people want a bowl from. Do not merge them because both are green inside a strawberry cut.

Adriatic / Verdone: California commercial green, light strawberry, disappointing in the South on Alabama’s sheet. I still named Adriatic JH on The Fig Jam. JH is a collector suffix. Dalmatie is not JH. Conadria is the Adriatic hybrid with rebound. Different plant. Col de Dame Blanc is a necked late green. Photograph the neck. Dalmatie is size. Blanc is the lady’s neck. Panache stripes. LSU Gold is yellow-blushed and late. None of those are Dalmatie because the photo was green.

Condit’s 1955 monograph is why Dalmatian means Brunswick in the Southern sheets. The collector word Dalmatie is a later French/collector use. The tag is a claim. The fruit is the evidence.

## Size is a late tax

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD first, and humid climates want tight eyes and smaller fruit. Dalmatie is the opposite sentence: large, green, late. 8a can finish it in a long year. 8a can also stare at baseballs in October.

I will not invent a Saline County ripen date. Write the first morning a large green fig gives. Write the last hard one after the first cool night. Both numbers are data. Head start in the hoop. Black pot. East wall. Do not feed for leaves in September. None of that turns a large late fig into Ronde de Bordeaux. It might finish a large late fig.

Scott’s Black, Gold, Madeira, Col de Dame, Dalmatie. That is a museum of unfinished fruit if you plant them as your first five. Pair this with an early fig. The early one is dinner. This one is the September maybe.

Breba: UAEX — rarely overwinters in Arkansas. A large green fig may throw a spring fig after a civil winter on protected wood. Bonus. Not the plan. Do not hat-rack it in January. You will not get an early large fig. You will get a later large fig on shorter wood, or nothing.

Hardiness: not Chicago Hardy. ANR-1145’s 15–20°F conversation is Celeste, English Brown Turkey, Alma, Hardy Chicago. A large late green in 8a is a pot you shuffle. A #3 freezes at the rim.

## Rain, split, sour, birds

Large fruit is a balloon. UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. ANR-1145: the riper they are, the more they split and sour; pick immature for preserves if that is the week you got. [VERIFY] the ostiole on *our* fruit. I will not stamp tight. I will not stamp open. Photograph after a storm.

Even water. Drought then flood is how large figs unzip. Pick what softened. Do not wait for the skin to turn grocery-yellow. Green ripe fruit is a feel. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. The introduction page already said most people have never had a good fig because they never had a ripe one. A large green is the trap.

A day-early large fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A split large fig on the ground is a beetle farm.

Birds: green helps. Size does not. They will still take a bite out of a baseball as if they were sampling. Net the week it softens.

Nematodes: pots. Mosaic mottles a lot of green-fig leaves. Mottled is not a discard. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood.

Flavor: rich strawberry on a bigger body is the consensus sentence. I did not plate Dalmatie on 25 July 2025. Jack did not either. Looking-forward names that day were other fruit — Yellow Long Neck, White Madeira #1, CDD Mutante, I-258. Dalmatie was not on the favorite pile and it was not on that looking-forward list. I will not write a signed tasting paragraph. Dry-week berry is the hope. Wet-week mealy or split is the risk. Both are weather. [VERIFY].

## How I would run it

One pot with a head start if you want large green fruit. Wood from a fruiting large green, source written. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Pot up before June. One **#3** or a **#5** if the plant is already a hog. Fertigate once it is a plant.

**6–8 hours** of sun. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. South shade plus water is already on Pick The Right Fig.

Do not plant it as the only fig. Plant a small tight-eye eater. Plant Dalmatie as the September maybe.

If it finishes, you have a bowl that looks like a postcard and eats like a berry. If it does not, you have a story about GDD you already knew. Graft. Keep the hole. We like a long trial. We still do not owe a listing a third autumn of hard baseballs.

Do not mail cuttings as Dalmatie until a large late green strawberry fig repeats. Do not mail them as Magnolia. Do not mail them as Dalmatian. Those words have already wrecked enough tags.

Write Dalmatie on the pot when a large late green strawberry fig repeats. Write unknown-large-green-late if the clock is real and the passport is not. The clock still decides dinner.
