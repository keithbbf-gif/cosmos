Scott’s Black is the LSU dark fig people mention when they have already planted [Purple](lsu-purple) and want a larger, later, red-pulp plate. The 2025 history: large, round, plump, dark skin, red pulp, intensely delicious, disease-resistant, productive, a top choice for humid, mild-winter areas. Complements the other releases by adding a fruit type.

Mild winter. Humid. That is south Louisiana talk. We can grow it in 8a. We cannot bully a short autumn into a Gulf October.

FigPotter said Scott’s Black is too late for Michigan without a head start. We are not Michigan. We are also not Port Sulphur. Head starts in the hoop help. A pot you can move helps. An in-ground tree in a year that turns cool in September will hold hard fruit while you stare at it.

## Official dark, not the leak yellow

Scott’s **Black** is a release. Scott’s **Yellow** (sometimes Brandy in the threads) is a leak. Do not order the second because you liked the first name. Do not assume a yellow fig with Scott on the tag is a sport you can write a paper about. Unofficial numbers left the stations. Polozola and Stagg said so.

The official six are [O’Rourke](lsu-orourke), [Champagne](lsu-champagne), [Tiger](lsu-tiger), [LSU Gold](lsu-gold), LSU Purple, Scott’s Black. [Hollier](lsu-hollier) and [Jack Lily](lsu-jack-lily) are unofficial. A nursery rename as Fantasia shows up in threads. [VERIFY] before you treat that sticker as the release.

Common type. No wasp.

## Do not confuse the dark LSU pile

Purple is the young-bearing recognizable dark — glossy, oblong, fruits in one or two years, quality on older wood. Scott’s Black is the later, plumper, “quality” dark in the official six. If your plant fruits in year one like Purple and stays small, [VERIFY]. If it never colors before frost, believe the clock.

It is not [Black Mission](black-mission). Mission is Franciscana, California shipping, two-crop romance, Alabama’s “disappointing in the South” hardiness line. Scott’s Black is a Gulf humidity release.

It is not Negra d’Agde. I named Negra on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). I did not name Scott’s Black. Different job. Do not merge them to win a sentence.

Jack Lily is unofficial, small, green, tight-eye, already on [Fig Reviews](https://figroots.com/fig_reviews_archive/). If your “Scott’s Black” is small and green, you have the wrong stick.

## What the sheets actually wrote

Polozola and Stagg, 2025: large, round, plump, good-quality fruit, dark skin, red pulp. Intensely delicious. Disease-resistant and productive. Top choice for humid, mild-winter areas. A great option for home gardeners in hot, humid Southern regions. Complements current recommended varieties by increasing the diversity of fruit types.

Older AgCenter clips (the “continues fig research” piece) already listed Scott’s Black with the other five. A later video talk listed five and left it off. The 2025 *Louisiana Agriculture* history puts it back in the official six. I will use that list. I will not settle a forum fight about who Scott was.

Alabama ANR-1145 does not give Scott’s Black a cultivar paragraph. Florida HS27 does not test it in the Monticello list I have. UAEX wants common figs for Arkansas. This one qualifies on type. Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer: common, no wasp. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) is the GDD page. Late fruit is a GDD argument. I will not invent a Saline County finish date.

“Intensely delicious” is LSU’s word. I will not invent a ranking next to Negra d’Agde to sound complete. When we have repeated ripe fruit I will say what it is. Until then: dark, red pulp, Gulf humidity breeding. That is already a reason to trial one.

## The 8a week

Pick: morning. Soft. Neck wilt. Late fruit and late storms arrive together. Large figs split. A hard dark fig in October is not “almost.” It is the year editing you. Pick what softened. Let the rest go.

Rain: humid-South release. Still pick after a storm week. Souring is water in the eye plus heat. Tight-enough is not a vault. Do not hang a plump black fig for a prettier photograph if the forecast is rain.

Birds: black. A green fig lasts longer. This one is not green when you want it. Net. This is a picking job, not a weekend hobby.

Rust and disease: “resistant” in the 2025 paragraph. Resistance is not absence. Rake litter. Look at both sides of the leaf. Yellow then reddish spots, blisters underneath. Do not caption one clean August as a cultivar trait.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July so the leaves hold while a late crop tries to finish. A late fig in shade is a later fig.

Pots versus ground: we run hundreds of pots, about 25–30 in-ground. I said that on The Fig Jam. One **#3**, hoop in spring if you have it, pot up before June, fertigate once it is a plant. A pot you can roll is how you steal days — roll it into heat in April, roll it out of a 15°F night, roll it under cloth in a July bake. That is not a greenhouse industry. That is a wagon. Do not push nitrogen into September on a late variety — tender wood plus a late crop is how you lose both. In-ground on an east wall if the year is long enough to justify the hole. Two short years in a row is data.

Compare it to [Purple](lsu-purple) on the same bench if you have both. Purple will try to feed you while Scott’s Black is still a hard dark marble. That is not a failure of Black. That is why the six exist as a set and not as one glossy photo.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree that is actually Black, not Yellow. The [introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers and 1–3 years to prove type.

Cold: mild-winter recommendation. Not Celeste. Not Chicago Hardy. Shuffle the pot. In-ground is a Gulf sentence living in 8a.

## Culinary, honest

Fresh: the late bowl if the year allows. Red pulp is the cut people want. LSU’s “intensely delicious” has to finish first. A hard October fig is not a tasting note. It is livestock feed or the compost.

Jam: if you get a pile of ripe dark fruit, kettle it. Dark jar, red pulp. A short year gives you a tasting, not a pantry. Do not plant this as your only preserve tree. [Kadota](kadota) and a loading [Champagne](lsu-champagne) will put more jars on a shelf in a year that turns cool early.

Kid bowl: do not plant it as the only fig for a child. Plant Purple or a Celeste-class tree for the early bowl. Plant Scott’s Black for the late bowl if the year allows. A kid who waits until frost for the famous dark fig will decide figs are a hassle.

## How I would run it

If your season is long and you want an official LSU dark that is not Purple, this is the stick.

If two short years in a row leave you with hard fruit, graft something earlier onto the hole — Negra, Ronde de Bordeaux, Jack Lily, a Turkey type if you only wanted food. Keep the roots if they are healthy. Late is a trait. It does not become early because you are optimistic.

Write Scott’s Black on the pot when the fruit is large, dark, round-plump, late, and repeats. Write Scott’s Yellow on a different pot, in pencil, until someone who held the station wood says otherwise.
