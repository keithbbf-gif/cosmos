Two tigers walk into a fig collection. One is a Louisiana release with a stripe on the *unripe* fruit and a tree that sprawls while it is young. The other is a French chimera with green-and-yellow stripes on the ripe skin and a leaf that stays ordinary. People call both Tiger. Only one is LSU Tiger.

If you came here for the candy-stripe fig, read the [Panache](panache) draft. This page is the Gulf animal.

## What LSU released

2007 group with [Champagne](lsu-champagne) and [O’Rourke](lsu-orourke). Good cold tolerance in their notes. Large fruit. Stripe on immature fruit. Trees small to moderate. Juvenile wood tends to grow horizontally. Common type. No wasp.

Stagg’s photo caption: Tiger is a large-sized fig that develops a distinctive dark stripe before ripening. Shoot that stage. A ripe Tiger without a green photo is just a large fig on the internet.

The official six are O’Rourke, Champagne, Tiger, [LSU Gold](lsu-gold), [LSU Purple](lsu-purple), [Scott’s Black](lsu-scotts-black). [Hollier](lsu-hollier) and [Jack Lily](lsu-jack-lily) are unofficial. “LSU Tiger Panache” is a seller making a smoothie out of two plants.

## Do not confuse the stripe pile

[Panache](panache) / Panachée / Variegato / “Tiger Fig” on a marketplace: green-and-yellow stripes on the *ripe* fruit, strawberry pulp, leaf not variegated, chimera. Alabama: excellent talk in California, mediocre and mealy in the South. If the ripe fruit is striped green and yellow, you do not have the LSU release.

If the *unripe* fruit is striped and the ripe fruit is a normal large fig, you might have LSU Tiger.

“Improved Tiger” is a wish. A variegated leaf is a different plant. A yellow ripe fig with no green-stage stripe may be Gold or Champagne or a nobody. Wait two crops. Photograph the green stripe. Photograph the ripe plate. Keep both pictures on the pot tag if you have to. Future-you will thank present-you when the cuttings go out.

Gold is the upright hog — largest official fruit, later clock. Tiger is large and in the 2007 group with better cold talk than Gold’s “average.” Champagne is the medium yellow. Purple is the glossy early dark. Do not sort them by the word “LSU” on a bag.

## What the sheets actually wrote

Polozola and Stagg, 2025: good cold tolerance, large fruit with a stripe on immature fruit, trees small to moderate, tendency to grow horizontally during the juvenile phase. Stagg’s 2021/2025 photo line: large-sized fig, distinctive dark stripe before ripening.

Older AgCenter clips put Tiger in the 2007 trio with Champagne and O’Rourke. The 2025 history is the six-name list I will keep using.

Alabama ANR-1145 does not give LSU Tiger its own paragraph. It does give Panachée a paragraph and lists Tiger as a Panache synonym. That is the name-pile scar in print. Read the fruit, not the synonym list, when someone says Tiger.

Florida HS27 lists Tiger among cultivars UF has not tested. UAEX wants common figs for Arkansas. Tiger qualifies on type. Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) page is the primer: common, no wasp. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) is the climate page. I will not invent a Saline County soften date. [VERIFY] yours.

Flavor: Gulf growers like it. Release language is large, good-quality fruit, more diversity of types. I did not put Tiger on the Fig Jam short list. I will not invent a berry-versus-honey speech to fill the hole. [VERIFY] on our plate when we have repeated fruit and write it then.

## The sideways tree

People prune a juvenile Tiger like they are making a standard. The plant is trying to be a bush that leans. LSU told you. Let it be a low, wide thing or you will fight wood that wanted to lie down. In a pot the sprawl is manageable. In the ground it wants room at the skirts.

Small-to-moderate mature size is a gift in a collection. Gold is the upright hog. Tiger is the one that stays in the conversation without eating the fence.

Cold talk is better than Gold’s “average.” Still not a [Chicago Hardy](chicago-hardy) story. Still not a [Celeste](celeste) “please do not chop me” story, but do not assume a January hat-rack is free. Leave crop wood. Dead wood only. Shuffle pots. In-ground is a Gulf recommendation living in 8a.

## The 8a week

Pick: morning. Soft. Large fruit. A stripe on a hard green fig is a field mark, not a ripe cue. People pick Panache pretty and eat it early. People pick Tiger striped and eat it raw. Wait for give. Shoot the green fruit in the same week you shoot the ripe plate. One picture is a listing. Two pictures are a type.

Rain: large figs split. Souring is water in the eye plus heat. Bred for humidity, not a vault. Pick after a storm week in the morning, or pick before the storm if the fruit is already soft.

Birds: a large ripening fig is lunch. Stripe or no stripe. Net or pick.

Rust: rake litter. Yellow then reddish spots, blisters under the leaf. Tiger did not get Gold’s “moderate resistance” sentence. Do not write one on it.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. A low, wide juvenile in a west fry cooks on the south skirt.

Pots versus ground: we run hundreds of pots, about 25–30 in-ground. I said that on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). One **#3** you pot up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. Do not push nitrogen into September. The sprawl in a pot is a pruning job, not a surprise. In-ground wants skirt room. Do not plant it as the only path-side tree and then complain it leaned into the mower.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree that striped green. The [introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted wood and a multi-year prove-out. If Tiger never stripes for you, [VERIFY] type. Some years the mark is faint. Some plants are the wrong plant. Believe a second year before you mail cuttings with the official name.

Nematodes: I have not seen LSU hang a nematode sentence on Tiger the way Alabama hung one on Purple. Pots if the ground is dirty. A sprawling juvenile in nematode clay is how you lose a year and still have no stripe photo.

## Culinary, honest

Fresh: large ripe fruit is the job if the week cooperates. I will not invent a flavor family to sound complete. When we have repeated ripe fruit I will say what it is. Until then: official large LSU fig, not Gold’s clock, not Panache’s candy cane.

Jam: large fruit fills a kettle if you get a pile. A short year gives you a few and a photograph of a green stripe.

Kid bowl: a big fig a child can see. The green stripe is the show-and-tell. The ripe fig is the snack. Do not let them eat the striped hard ones. That is how “LSU is overrated” starts in a kitchen.

If you wanted the conversation-piece stripes on the ripe skin, buy Panache and accept Alabama’s “mediocre in the South” warning. Different job. Different profile.

## How I would run it

One plant if you want an official large LSU fig that is not Gold’s clock. Wood from a fruiting tree.

Write LSU Tiger on the pot when the green fruit stripes and the ripe fruit is large and repeats. Write Panache on a different pot. The word tiger is not a cultivar.
