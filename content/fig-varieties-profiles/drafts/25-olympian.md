Olympian is a chance seedling story out of the Pacific Northwest — large, purple, strawberry pulp, a breba people talk about, wood that takes more cold than a California Mission. Grower consensus. I did not plate it on The Fig Jam. I will not write a signed flavor paragraph I would not sign.

Common type. No wasp. That already makes it more honest in 8a than Desert King.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) page is the one-minute primer: common figs persist without pollen; San Pedro figs need pollen for the main crop but not the breba; Smyrna figs need pollen for both. Olympian sits in the first bucket. You do not buy a wasp. You do not brush an ostiole. You still have to keep last year’s wood if you wanted the second-crop story the listing sold you.

Desert King is the San Pedro people confuse with “two crops because the tag said so.” Olympian is not that machine. Main crop on this year’s wood can set without a meeting. Breba is still last year’s wood. UAEX already said breba rarely overwinters in Arkansas. A pot you shuffle can keep that wood. In-ground you take the spring you get. A late freeze after budbreak deletes the romance before the main crop has a vote.

Condit’s 1955 monograph never saw this plant. He counted hundreds of common figs and still left room for a later chance seedling. A city on a tag is not a pedigree and it is not a zone map. [VERIFY] the source of any stick that cost “Olympian money.”

## What the name is selling

Size. Color. A second crop. Hardiness. Those are four sales words. Only one of them is free.

Size: large figs split and take more growing degree days to finish. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is the first filter — a wonderful fig that does not finish is a leaf bush with a receipt. A Northwest summer is long daylight and dry hang. Ours is heat, humidity, clay, and an August that can turn a balloon into vinegar. I will not invent a Saline County soften date. Write the first morning the neck wilts and the fruit gives. That date is yours. [VERIFY] finish dates here.

Color: purple-black when it is true. Birds can read that billboard from the road. A green fig lasts longer on the tree. This is not a green fig. Net or pick. Do not write a post about “the year the birds won” as if that were weather.

Breba: last year’s wood. I said it twice because the catalog will say it once and skip the winter. Protect old wood if you want the breba story. Do not prune it like a Turkey in January and then write a post that Olympian “doesn’t breba in Arkansas.” You ate the wood. Main crop on current wood is the crop you can plan. That is true of most common figs in 8a. Olympian does not get a special exemption because a Gig Harbor orchard posted a June photo.

Hardiness: better than Mission is a low bar in the South. Florida’s sheet and Alabama both call Mission disappointing here because the wood is not very hardy. Better than Celeste is a claim I will not stamp without a winter we measured. Alabama still groups Celeste, English Brown Turkey, Alma, and Hardy Chicago when they talk 15–20°F wood. That is a conversation number, not a warranty. Treat Olympian as a pot-honest fig and an in-ground trial. A #3 still freezes at the rim. Hardy talk is dormant wood, not a pot left in a wind tunnel.

If your “Olympian” is small and early, you may have Ronde de Bordeaux or a Chicago-type. If it is yellow, you have a hopeful label. If it is copper and rebounds like a Turkey, you bought the pile. Wait for fruit. The introduction page already said it takes 1–3 years to prove type. A first-year cup can lie.

## Sister slugs, not sisters

Mission is the California dark commercial fig Condit filed with Franciscana. Same color family in a photo. Different climate sentence. Mission wants a dry finish and wood that does not love a sharp night. Olympian’s reputation is the opposite direction — cooler summer, more cold talk, a breba people chase in the Northwest. Do not merge them because both are purple.

Chicago Hardy is the resprout story. Small dark, dies back, fruits on new wood if the year is long enough. Olympian’s sales pitch is *keeping* wood, not losing it. If your plant dies to the crown every winter and still tries to feed you on suckers, you may have a useful dark fig and you may not have Olympian.

Black Madeira is late, intense, and on my Fig Jam favorite list. Olympian is not Madeira. Negra d’Agde is the dark berry Jack scored. Different pot. Different job.

## Rain, split, sour

UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. Large fruit is a balloon. Split and sour can arrive in the same bowl. I will not call Olympian tight-eyed without a photograph of *our* ostiole after a storm. [VERIFY] the eye. Tight is a screen, not a vault. Physics can still unzip a ripe fig.

Pick in the morning. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Do not wait for jet-black if the skin is already thin and the forecast is a storm. A day-early large fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A day-late large fig on the ground is a beetle farm.

Even water. Drought then flood is how large figs split. FigRoots how-to posts already unpacked that. This page will not restage it. Afternoon cloth in July is South Arkansas, not a personality. **6–8 hours** of sun. A purple fig in deep shade is still shade.

Nematodes: pots. Pick The Right Fig already said if you have nematodes you grow in pots. Olympian is not a nematode-resistant fairy tale. LSU Purple gets that rumor from release notes. Different plant.

## Flavor I will not fake

Grower consensus: strawberry pulp, large, a fig people in cooler summers like enough to keep talking about. That is their plate. I did not name Olympian on The Fig Jam on 25 July 2025. Jack did not either. Looking-forward names in that interview were other fruit — Yellow Long Neck, White Madeira #1, CDD Mutante, I-258, and the rest of that list. Olympian was not on it.

I will eat a finished large purple fig in a dry week and write what it was. Until the plate repeats I will not stamp a homestead score. Dry-week berry-leaning is the hope in the stories. Wet-week mealy or sour is the risk in our steam. Both are weather. [VERIFY] on our tree.

## How I would run it

One **#3**. Wood from a fruiting tree with large purple fruit, not a listing with a mountain. **6–8 inches**, three nodes, lignified if you can. Pot up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

Protect old wood if you want the breba story: shuffle the pot, no hat-rack in a civil January, cover a late freeze if the buds already moved. Then count. If breba shows, the name earned a little of itself. If only main crop shows, you still have a common fig. Relabel your expectations, not necessarily the pot.

Main crop on current wood is the crop you can plan. If that crop is large, sweet, and berry-leaning in a dry week, keep the tree. If it is late and sour, keep a tighter fig for August — Celeste-class, Jack Lily, Syrian Dark #2 — and let Olympian be a September maybe.

Do not plant it as the only fig. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said humid climates want tight eyes and smaller fruit. A large purple from Washington is the opposite of that sentence. Pair it with a small tight-eye eater.

Do not row it. Tell a neighbor Negra, Jack Lily, a tool tree, then Olympian if they still have a pot and they will do the winter work.

If two years end in hard fruit, believe it. Keep the plant as a pot you start early or graft something earlier on the hole. We graft. We do not throw a live root system in a pile to prove we have taste.

Write Olympian on the pot when a large purple fig repeats and the source was not a blender. Write unknown-large-purple if size is all you have. Size is not a cultivar.
