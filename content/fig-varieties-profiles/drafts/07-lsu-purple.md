LSU Purple is the one you have already seen. Glossy. Dark. Oblong. The 2025 history says it is probably the most recognizable of the LSU-bred varieties. It was the early release — **1991** on the older AgCenter clips — and it did the thing breeders want a new fig to do in public: it fruited on a young plant.

Most figs want three or four juvenile years. Purple will throw a small crop in one or two. That sentence has sold more sticks than flavor ever did. I do not blame the plant. I blame the person who reviewed a cup and called the cultivar finished.

## Official six, not the whole internet

Polozola and Stagg named six: [O’Rourke](lsu-orourke), [Champagne](lsu-champagne), [Tiger](lsu-tiger), [LSU Gold](lsu-gold), LSU Purple, [Scott’s Black](lsu-scotts-black). Purple is the 1991 dark. Scott’s Black is the later, plumper official dark. [Hollier](lsu-hollier) and [Jack Lily](lsu-jack-lily) are unofficial. A marketplace “LSU Purple” is easy to photograph. Glossy sells. True-to-type is a purple stem, a young crop, and a better plate in year three.

## Do not confuse the dark pile

It is not Scott’s Black. Scott’s Black is large, round, plump, red pulp, late, humid-mild-winter talk. Purple is the recognizable oblong glossy that fruits while you are still waiting on everybody else.

It is not [Black Mission](black-mission). Mission is Franciscana, a California commercial common fig, two-crop romance, disappointing-in-the-South hardiness on Alabama’s sheet. Purple is a Gulf release.

It is not Violette de Bordeaux / Negronne. Different argument, different draft. It is not a Turkey type with a paint job. If your “Purple” is brown, early, and sugar, you may have the [Brown Turkey](brown-turkey) pile. If it is black and late and huge, you may have a wish. Year two.

Jack did not put LSU Purple on his short plate list. He put [Jack Lily](lsu-jack-lily), Red Sicilian, Negra d’Agde. Different jobs. Purple is the Gulf producer that gets you eating while the collection is still a parking lot of cups.

## What the sheets actually wrote

Polozola and Stagg, 2025: heavy crop of bright purple fruit. Can ripen on very young plants. Most varieties need three or four juvenile years; Purple can make small crops in one to two. Fruit quality good to excellent **on older trees**. Recommended for the Gulf Coast. Stagg’s photo line: most recognizable because of the dark purple, glossy hue and oblong shape.

Alabama ANR-1145: small- to medium-size purple fig with strawberry pulp. Variable shape and flavor. Bred by E.N. O’Rourke, released 1991. Stems are always purple — that is the field mark they want you to use. “LSU says the plant is nematode resistant.” Well adapted to the Deep South. Hardiness not established. Seems tender. No synonyms.

Treat the nematode sentence as a breeding claim, not a license to plant in last year’s okra hole. [VERIFY] if you are buying it as a nematode solution. I still put rare wood in pots if the ground is dirty. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already sent nematodes to the pot bench.

Common type. No wasp. Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer is the type page. Purple does not need pollen. UAEX already limited Arkansas recommendations to common figs. This one qualifies.

Alabama’s “seems tender” matches how I treat it: not [Celeste](celeste), not [Chicago Hardy](chicago-hardy), not a Turkey-type resprout hero. In a pot it is honest. In the ground in 8a it is a Gulf fig living north of the story.

## Young fruit versus a tree

The first Purple you eat off a **#3** can be fine and still not be the fig. Thin skin. Smaller. A little grassy. People then say Purple is overrated and plant something with a Portuguese name. Wait. LSU told you the quality lives on older wood. I will wait with them.

Flavor family in the sheets: berry-leaning purple, strawberry pulp, not a honey Gold, not a sugar Celeste. Variable shape and flavor — Alabama again. That variability is why a mislabel hides easily. I will not invent a homestead ranking next to Negra d’Agde. I did not name Purple on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). Speed is the gift I will stand on.

## The 8a week

Pick: morning. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Wet August still sours a late hang. Eye was bred for humidity, not a vault. Do not wait for jet-black if the skin is already thin and the forecast is rain.

Rain: souring is water in the eye plus heat. Split is a balloon. A loaded young shoot after a storm week is how people decide Purple “doesn’t keep.” It kept long enough. You were late.

Birds: dark fruit. A green fig lasts longer. Purple is not green when you want it. Net or pick. Do not write a forum post about the year the birds won as if that were weather.

Rust: Purple is not the rust story LSU hangs on Gold. Rake litter. Yellow then reddish spots, blisters under the leaf. Do not read one clean year as resistance.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. A glossy dark fig in a west fry cooks the leaves while the fruit is still a cup.

Pots versus ground: we run hundreds of pots, about 25–30 in-ground. Purple in a **#3** is how most people meet it. Pot up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. A young plant that fruits too hard will stall. Pick what you want, water, and do not treat a one-year crop as a dare. Late nitrogen makes tender wood. Purple already wants to grow. Do not help it write a soft will in September.

In-ground: Gulf recommendation. 8a trial. Mulch over shallow roots. Shuffle the pot if you like the fruit and fear the winter.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree with purple stems. It roots like any common fig. The [introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers and a multi-year prove-out. Glossy photos are not a pedigree.

I will not invent a Saline County first-soften date. [VERIFY] yours and write it on the tag.

## Culinary, honest

Fresh: the year-one cups are a tease. Eat them. Do not review the cultivar on them. Older-wood Purple is the plate LSU called good to excellent. Berry-leaning in the Gulf talk. Variable, so your tree has to say it twice.

Jam: a heavy crop on an older tree is how you fill a kettle. Dark fruit makes a dark jar. If you only have young cups, you have a tasting, not a pantry.

Kid bowl: this is the honest kid fig in the official six. Fruit this year. Dark. Sweet enough. A child will eat a ripe Purple while [Gold](lsu-gold) is still hard and Scott’s Black is still a rumor. That is why people plant five before they have tasted two. Plant one. Eat two summers. Then decide.

## How I would run it

One plant for the kid who wants figs this year. Common fig. Wood from a fruiting tree.

Do not plant twelve because the first cup set fruit. We have three hundred names. We do not need a Purple orchard to prove we like LSU.

If the older tree is only “fine” and you wanted a conversation, graft. Keep the early-bearing roots if they are healthy. Purple’s gift is speed. Speed is not the same as a favorite.

If the stem is green and the fruit is brown, change the label.

Write LSU Purple on the pot when the stems are purple, the young plant fruits, the older plate improves, and it repeats.
