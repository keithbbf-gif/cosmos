Common type. Persistent. No wasp. Jack already defined that on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). Late. Dark. Intense berry when it is true and finished. A fig that made a thousand marketplace listings and a thousand mislabels.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. Madeira is one of the reasons. Once you have this plate in a year that finishes, the tool figs taste like tools. I will still keep a tool fig for the years Madeira is a bead.

## What fame costs

Price. Lies. A three-year wait to learn you bought a dark fig that is not this dark fig. [An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers matter and it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. eBay reviews close before you taste type. Madeira is the exhibit.

If your “Madeira” is early, small, and sugar, you have a Turkey or a Chicago-type. If it is productive and medium and berry-sweet in August, you may have something better for this climate — and it is still not Madeira. If it is late, sparse, and still hard in October, you may have the clock without the plate.

Wait. Photograph. Do not mail cuttings as Black Madeira until the plate repeats the reason you paid. Caption the clone we have. A marketplace hero shot is not our tree.

I will not invent a Saline County soften date. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is a count, not a vibe. Late plus a short year is a leaf year. Write the first soften on the tag when it happens. [VERIFY] every new stick, including wood we already trust, because fame moves cuttings that should have stayed home.

White Madeira #1 was a looking-forward name in 2025. Not this page. Not plated in that interview. Colonel Littman’s Black Cross was the other dark I wanted to taste. Different draft when we earn it.

## Climate is the hard mode

Late plus humidity is the exam. Open-enough eye in a lot of talk. [VERIFY] ours. I will not print tight unless the still shows it. TAMU’s [disease handbook](https://plantdiseasehandbook.tamu.edu/food-crops/fruit-crops/fig/) put Magnolia and Kadota in the open-eye souring column and Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma on the closed side. Madeira is not on that table. Look at the ostiole after the first storm.

UAEX’s souring sentence is water in the eye plus heat after rain. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit sours and many split in an unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year. A late dark fig hanging into September storms is a balloon and a door. Split and sour can share a bowl.

Even water. A drought then a flood is how famous figs unzip the week you finally got color. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Do not wait for a Portuguese postcard color in a Gulf steam bath. A day-early Madeira in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A split Madeira on the ground is a beetle farm that cost too much.

Hoop. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July so the leaves do not cook while the fruit tries to finish. A west fry on black plastic is how a late fig writes a short year.

I like this fig. I also like figs we actually eat. Negra and Red Sicilian pay rent. Syrian Dark #2 pays rent. Madeira pays rent in the right year. Stack your collection so a bad year is not a Madeira year only. [Fig Reviews](https://figroots.com/fig_reviews_archive/) already scored Red Sicilian and Negra. Madeira does not get a fake card on this page. The interview list is the card we have.

Birds: dark and late. They will wait with you. Net the week it finally colors, or pick what softened and leave the hard ones. A green fig lasts longer. This is not green when you want it.

## Rust, wood, winter

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf, defoliation while late fruit is still hard. A famous fig that drops its leaves in August will not finish a Portuguese postcard. Rake. Do not leave a wet skirt of last year’s leaves. I will not advertise rust-proof. I have not measured that.

Mosaic mottles leaves on a lot of dark collector figs. Alabama hung the heavy sentence on Mission and Adriatic. Mottled is not a reason to discard a tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood off the plant we trust.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. Madeira is not the LSU Purple rumor.

Cold: late figs are often tenderer than Celeste. Alabama’s 15–20°F talk names Celeste, English Brown Turkey, Alma, and Hardy Chicago — not Madeira. Treat it as a pot you shuffle. Our [winter protection](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) page already said barely moist, not a heated den. In-ground in 8a is a trial, not a grandpa recommendation. Tips burn. A late freeze after budbreak deletes wood you wanted for next year’s structure. If it dies to the crown, five or six trunks at two feet, TAMU’s chapter, and know that a resprout year may be a leaf year. Late plus new wood is how fame becomes a bush.

Do not hat-rack a late berry fig in January. Crop wood is the plan. Fame is not a reason to make a standard.

Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how a late fig writes a tender will and then misses October.

## Sister slugs

**Negra d’Agde** is berry first, tight eye, productive, on Jack’s list and on our review card. Different clock. Different intensity. Keep both if you can.

**Nuestra Señora del Carmen** is large, very sweet, large eye, splits in rain. Jack said it. Different door. Different size.

**Red Sicilian** is Jack’s first: productive, sweet, nice berry, medium. A better first dark purchase than Madeira if you live in steam.

**Mission / Franciscana** is the California commercial dark. Alabama called it disappointing in the South, not very hardy, often mosaic-mottled. If your Madeira is that plate and that winter, relabel.

**Chicago Hardy** is the small dark resprout story. Early enough to finish on new wood some years. Not this intensity. Not this fame.

## How I would run it

We run it as a named favorite and as a verification project. Wood off the plant we trust, **6–8 inches**, three nodes, labeled like it might still be wrong. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Extra pots. No beginner sales. Pot up to a **#3** before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth. Late fruit still needs days. Shade that cuts you under six hours is how a famous fig stays green.

If a plant never finishes two years running, believe your GDD. Graft. Keep the hole. Fame is not a reason to stare at green beads. We graft. We do not perform funerals for live roots.

Write Black Madeira on the pot that matches an intense late dark berry fig we would put on that interview list again. Write unknown-late-dark if you are still in the three-year wait. The wait is the price. Pay it before you print the name.
