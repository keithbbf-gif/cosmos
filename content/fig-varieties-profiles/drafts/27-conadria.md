Condit wanted a fig that could work in more than one climate. Conadria — Adriatic hybrid, released **1956** or **1957** depending on the sheet — is medium to large, yellow-green, light strawberry pulp, rich flavor, pyriform. Alabama: well adapted in California and the Southeast, hardy with good rebound from freezes.

That rebound sentence is why this page exists next to the pretty Condit figs that do not have it.

Common type. No wasp. Not a Smyrna. People see yellow-green and think Calimyrna. Different machine. This one stays on the tree without a wasp.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) page: common figs persist; Smyrna figs drop unless caprified; San Pedro figs need pollen for the main crop. Calimyrna is Sari Lop / Lob Injir — caducous, large open eye, the California commercial Smyrna. ANR-1145 says not practical for Southern growers. Conadria was bred so a person without a wasp could still have an Adriatic-family fig. Persistent. You do not buy pollen. You do not brush an ostiole. If your “Conadria” drops a main crop at marble size every time, look at type and water first. [VERIFY]. You may have a Smyrna with a stolen name.

Condit’s 1955 monograph is the type-and-synonym book. Conadria is the year after — first of the Condit hybrids a lot of collectors still plant. Alabama lists the synonym pair: Adriatic Hybrid, Verdone Hybrid. Verdone is Condit’s Adriatic. Same blood-talk as Flanders. Flanders is the 1975 pretty one. Conadria is the 1950s worker. Excel is the 1975 Kadota hybrid. Tena is the hot-and-dry sibling. Read the right page.

## What it is for

A yellow fig that can take a Southern winter better than Mission and better than Flanders. Fruit in the Adriatic / Verdone idea: green skin, strawberry pulp, rich if it finishes. A preserve fig and a fresh fig. Not Jack’s berry-first list.

I did not plate Conadria on The Fig Jam. I will treat Alabama’s “rich” as their word and our plate as unwritten until it repeats. Keith’s yellow talk in that interview was other names, and even those looking-forward yellows — Yellow Long Neck, I-258 — were untried on 25 July 2025. Conadria was not on the favorite pile and it was not on the looking-forward list. Consensus among Southern sheets is the rebound plus a usable yellow-green plate. Consensus is not a homestead score. [VERIFY].

Eye: not the closed-eye famous. [VERIFY] after rain. Hybrid does not mean sealed. UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. Texas Plant Disease Handbook files Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma on the closed side; Magnolia and Kadota take more open-eye trouble. Conadria did not get a closed-eye paragraph. Photograph the ostiole. The closed ones belong in 8a. The open ones belong in a dry week or a preserve pot.

Season: mid, not Ronde-de-Bordeaux-early, not Scott’s-late. [VERIFY] dates. I will not invent a Saline County soften. Write the first morning the neck wilts. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is the filter. A mid yellow that finishes is useful. A mid yellow that sulks into October is another late tax you did not need.

Birds: green-yellow helps. Size does not. They will still take a bite out of a large one as if they were sampling. Net or pick.

## Freeze

Good rebound means you rebuild the bush — five or six trunks at two feet, TAMU’s freeze chapter — and you may still get fruit on new wood, Turkey-class, if the year is long enough. NC State said Brown Turkey and Brunswick produce fair-to-good crops on suckers the season after freeze injury. Alabama put Conadria in that conversation with the word rebound. Pair with an early fig if food matters in a kill year. Late figs on new wood are leaves.

Do not hat-rack a healthy Conadria in a civil January just because it can rebound. Rebound is insurance. Crop wood is the plan. Celeste hates a hard late-winter prune — UAEX already wrote that. Conadria is not Celeste. It is also not a Turkey you chop for sport. Dead wood. Crossing wood. Leave the rest.

Hardiness talk on ANR-1145 is 15–20°F for dormant wood on the old favorites. Conadria’s sentence is “hardy with good rebound,” not “hardier than Celeste.” A pot still freezes at the rim. Shuffle it. In-ground in 8a is the reason this hybrid still gets planted: a yellow fig that can take a bite and try again.

Mission is the dark California commercial fig that Alabama called disappointing in the South because it is not very hardy. Do not use Mission as your yellow-fig winter test. Use the winter you actually had.

## Sister slugs

Calimyrna: Smyrna, wasp, open eye, nutty seed crunch when caprified. Jack already said caprified fruit runs sweeter with more seed crunch. That is not an Arkansas backyard project. Do not plant Calimyrna because the skin looked like Conadria in a catalog.

Adriatic / Verdone: the parent idea. Alabama called commercial Adriatic disappointing in the South, mosaic-prone, better in the Northwest. I still named Adriatic JH on the Fig Jam list. JH is a collector suffix. Conadria is the hybrid with rebound. Different plants.

Flanders: violet stripes, long neck, not particularly hardy, good on the West Coast. Pretty. Tender. Not this page.

Excel: Kadota hybrid, 1975, amber, hardy-and-promising on Alabama’s sheet. Plant it next to Conadria if you have both. Same storm. Two ostioles.

LSU Gold: official 1995 release, large, late, strawberry pulp. Gulf clock. Different breeding program. Do not call Conadria “the old Gold.”

White Marseilles / Blanche / Lemon: old Southern yellow yard fig, white pulp, many seeds, turbinate without a neck. Different shape. Different pile.

## How I would run it

One in-ground candidate if you want a yellow fig that is not LSU and not a Smyrna. Start it in a **#3**, pot up before June, and shuffle a **#5** until you trust it. Wood from a fruiting tree. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Fertigate once it is a plant.

**6–8 hours** of sun. East wall if you have one. Afternoon cloth in July. Even water — drought then flood splits a medium-to-large fig. Clay that drains. Mulch over shallow roots. Nematodes: pots. Pick The Right Fig already said that.

If you wanted Gold’s size, this may feel smaller. If you wanted Celeste’s eye, look at the ostiole before you commit a hole. If you wanted Jack’s berry-first bowl, this is the wrong family. Honey-strawberry on a green skin is the hope. A kid will eat a finished one. I will put it in a bowl if the berry figs are still cups. I will not pretend it is Negra.

Preserve: a yellow fig that rebounds is a homestead tool. Janet Carson’s old line on picking a few days early for the kettle still applies to any open-enough yellow. A fruit that would sour on Sunday can still be Monday jam if you walk the tree after the storm.

Do not plant it as the only fig. Plant a small tight-eye eater. Plant Conadria as the yellow that might still be there after a bad January.

Write Conadria on the pot when a yellow-green strawberry-pulp fig repeats and the plant comes back from a bite. Write Adriatic hybrid in the notebook. Write Calimyrna never.
