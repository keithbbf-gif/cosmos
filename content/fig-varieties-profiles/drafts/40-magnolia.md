Texas grew Magnolia for the kettle. Large-enough bronze to yellow fig, open eye, a fig that makes jam and does not pretend to be Negra. Condit folded Brunswick into a twelve-name pile. [Alabama ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) listed **Brunswick** as medium to large, bronzy yellow, rich flavor, oblique-turbinate, well adapted in the Southwest and drier areas of the South, fruit ruined by excessive rain since it has open eyes, fairly hardy — synonyms Dalmatian, Madonna, Magnolia.

Dalmatian here is not Dalmatie the large green collector fig. Madonna is not a prayer. Magnolia is the Texas sales name. If your grandpa in East Texas has a preserve tree, this is often it.

Common type. Persistent. No wasp. Jack already defined that on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). TAMU [disease handbook](https://plantdiseasehandbook.tamu.edu/food-crops/fruit-crops/fig/): Magnolia and Kadota take more open-eye trouble. Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma sit on the closed side. Their souring table is blunt. Magnolia: susceptible. Kadota — they spelled it Kodata: susceptible. Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma: resistant. The prose says figs with open eyes should not be planted if you wanted a fresh fig that survives a wet week.

## What it is for

Jars. Early pick — Janet Carson said you can pick a few days early for preserves. A fruit that would sour on Sunday can still be Monday jam if you walk the tree after the storm. Alabama’s trouble table says the same thing in fewer words: fruit sours and many split — replace with a more suitable variety, or pick immature fruit for preserves. Magnolia is the variety that sentence was written around.

Fresh dessert in a dry week: fine, rich, open-eye bronze. Fresh dessert in a wet week: vinegar. Do not write a bad review of a kettle fig because you ate it as a clamshell.

I did not name Magnolia on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). I am not a fan of tool brown figs for eating so far. I will still tell a neighbor who puts up jam to take wood off a fruiting Magnolia instead of buying a French late fig.

NC State: Brown Turkey and Brunswick produce on suckers after freeze injury. Rebound class. TAMU’s freeze chapter — five or six trunks when the shoots are about two feet — is how you turn that thicket back into a bush you can pick. A preserve tree that comes back is a homestead tool.

The introduction page already said it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. A first-year bronze cup can lie about size. A first-year dry June can lie about the eye. Wait for an August storm. The storm is the type test.

## Rain is not a surprise

UAEX’s souring sentence is water in the eye plus heat after rain. Dried-fruit beetles walk in through an open ostiole and carry the organisms TAMU filed under souring. African fig fly, in Alabama’s newer pest paragraph, uses the same door. Tight-eye varieties make a small one. Magnolia hired a large one so a processor could see a fig.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said humidity is the judge. Magnolia fails that test on purpose. It was selected for a dry finish and a kettle, not for a humid Sunday in Saline County.

Split is a balloon. Large-enough bronze fruit plus a storm week is physics. Even water. A drought then a flood is how preserve figs unzip before you get them in the pot. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft — or, for jam, a few days early. A day-early Magnolia in a kettle is the honest use. A day-late Magnolia on the ground is a beetle farm.

Do not plant it as the only fresh fig. Plant a tight-eye eater. Plant Magnolia next to the sugar pot. I will eat a dry-week bronze fig. I will not pretend it is Negra, Maryland Berry, or Black Madeira.

Birds: bronze-yellow is quieter than Mission black. Not invisible. A ripe Magnolia still smells like lunch. Net if it is the only tree, or pick for the pot before they sample every fruit.

## Rust, wood, winter

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf, defoliation while you are trying to fill jars. Magnolia does not have an LSU rust sentence. Rake. Do not leave a wet skirt of last year’s leaves.

Mosaic mottles leaves on a lot of old preserve trees. Alabama hung the heavy mosaic sentence on Adriatic and Mission. Mottled is not a reason to discard a kettle tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. Magnolia is not Alma. Texas released Alma in 1975 as the closed-eye answer to this exact souring story. If you wanted a fresh fig for humid weeks, Alma is the Texas name, not Magnolia.

Cold: fairly hardy on Alabama’s sheet. Rebound on NC State’s. Treat it as Turkey-class after a kill, not as Celeste-class with the saw. You can prune a Turkey type and still get fruit on new wood. You cannot do that to every brown fig and then blame Georgia. Magnolia will often forgive a hard cut. That is one reason homesteaders kept it.

Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how a fairly hardy preserve tree writes a tender will. Alabama’s 15–20°F talk is for dormant wood, not for a pot rim.

## Sister slugs

**Dalmatie** is a large late grass-green strawberry fig. Collectors say Dalmatian and mean that plant. Alabama hung **Dalmatian** on Brunswick as a synonym. Those are not the same fig. If your “Dalmatian” is bronze, open-eyed, and early-mid, you have this page. If it is grass-green, huge, and still hard in October, you have the other page. Write the color. Write the clock. Do not merge them to win a sentence.

**Kadota / Dottato** is the other open-eye kettle fig on TAMU’s trouble column. Pale yellow, honey, California canning. Different parent. Same August warning.

**Southern Brown Turkey / Texas Everbearing** is the rebound eater UAEX actually recommends. Closed-enough eye in TAMU’s table. Useful. Not rich. I am not a fan so far. If your Magnolia is that plate and that eye, relabel.

**Excel** is Condit’s Kadota hybrid. Yellow, promising in early Southeast trials. Not a bronze preserve fig.

**Verte** is a grass-green splitter I already named. Different color. Different job.

## How I would run it

If you can, take cuttings of the local preserve tree. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. If you buy Magnolia, expect the eye. Pick for the pot, not for the photograph. Pot up to a **#3** before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July. An open-eye fig in a west fry still sours when the storm comes. Shade does not sew the ostiole shut.

In-ground in 8a: east wall if you have one, clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots. Do not plant it in last year’s tomato hole. Plant it where you will walk with a bucket after rain, not where you will forget it until Sunday.

If you wanted only fresh fruit, this is the wrong first tree. Graft a tight-eye eater onto the hole after you have enough jars, or keep Magnolia as the kettle and plant Negra or Syrian Dark #2 as the plate. We graft. We do not throw a live root system in a pile because the eye was honest.

Write Magnolia / Brunswick on the pot that matches an open-eye bronze preserve fig. Write Dalmatie never. Write unknown-preserve-bronze if the kettle is the only name you trust. The kettle is an honest name.
