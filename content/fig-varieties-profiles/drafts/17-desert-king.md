Jack’s type page: San Pedro figs need pollen to ripen the main crop but not the breba. Desert King is the San Pedro Americans actually plant. Alabama filed it under King — medium greenish yellow, strawberry pulp, pyriform to oblique, a large breba from late June to August, sometimes sets main-crop figs without pollination, sweet and rich, well adapted in the Northwest and cooler South.

Sometimes. That word is doing more work than the word King.

## Two crops, two machines

Breba is last year’s wood. Persistent on a San Pedro. In the Northwest, King’s breba is the crop people eat. In Arkansas, UAEX already said breba rarely overwinters. A late freeze after budbreak deletes the job this cultivar was hired for.

Main crop is this year’s wood. Caducous-leaning. No wasp, no pollen, and you watch figs swell and drop. A few climates and a few years it sets anyway. Do not build a plan on “sometimes.”

[ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) put King in the intermediate group with Lampeira and San Pedro: no pollen needed for breba on old wood; pollen needed for the main crop in some environments. [Jack already defined the four types](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) on FigRoots. I will not restage that primer. I will say this: a Smyrna without a wasp is a leaf bush. A San Pedro without a civil winter is a leaf bush that advertised two crops.

People buy Desert King because a catalog said two crops. They receive a plant that needs a civil winter *and* a wasp they do not have. That is not a bargain.

Common-fig neighbors will fruit the main crop without a meeting. King may not. If your King is dropping marble-sized main-crop figs and the Celeste next to it is hanging, you do not have a bad King. You have a San Pedro. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit that drops at one-third to one-half size is often the wrong type or a type that wants pollination. Believe the drop.

I will not write a how-to for *Blastophaga* in Bradley County. Explain the type. Do not culture a wasp yard. Calimyrna is the Smyrna version of this mistake. King is the San Pedro version. Different drop. Same brochure.

## The name pile

King, Desert King, White King. Alabama bolded King and listed the other two as synonyms. Condit’s monograph is the reason the sheet prefers King. Nurseries prefer Desert King because it sounds like a shipping fig from a dry place. It is not a common yellow you can ignore. It is not Kadota. It is not Peter’s Honey / Lattarula. It is not LSU Gold.

If a seller writes “common fig, two crops, Desert King,” they skipped a type. If a neighbor’s “King” hangs a main crop every year with no wasp, [VERIFY] type. You may have a common yellow with a famous name. Eat it. Relabel. A persistent King is a gift or a mislabel. Either way the plate is the name.

Do not merge it with California Brown Turkey / San Piero. That pile already wrecked the Turkey draft. San Pedro the type and San Piero the Turkey synonym are different words doing different damage.

The [introduction page](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said 1–3 years to prove type. A first-year drop is a clue. A second-year drop is a type.

## Climate it was written for

Northwest. Cooler South. Not a Gulf late-fig. Not a Calimyrna. Not a common yellow you can ignore.

Alabama’s late June to August breba window is *their* sheet talking about a plant that kept last year’s wood. It is not a ripening date I will hang on Saline County. UAEX already voted: breba rarely persists here. [VERIFY] whether your pot even carried a swell through spring before you call the cultivar a failure.

Flavor of a finished breba: sweet, rich, strawberry pulp, the reason the name stayed. I did not plate it on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). Northern friends will fight you for it. Our winter has a vote. No homestead score. I will not invent one next to Negra or Red Sicilian to look complete.

Eye: not Celeste. Pick what actually ripens. Do not wait for a second crop that is a type problem. UAEX’s souring sentence still applies to the fruit that *does* finish: water in the eye plus heat after rain.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. That does not make King the correction. King is a type lesson. The berry figs on our lists are common figs that set without a meeting.

## How I run it in 8a

Sun: **6–8 hours**. UAEX’s minimum. Afternoon cloth in July. East wall if you have one — not only for heat. Last year’s wood needs a civil spring. A west fry that pushes early budbreak then a late freeze is how you delete the breba on purpose. The [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) page already unpacked South shade plus water and the breba problem. I will not restage it.

Pots: this is a pot crop if you want the breba. **#3** / **#5**, potted up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. Shuffle it. [Our winter page](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) already said barely moist, not a heated den. You are not storing a Turkey type you can hat-rack. You are storing last year’s wood. A pot you can roll is how King earns a hole in this climate. In-ground King in 8a is a bet on a mild spring.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a tree that actually breba’d. Lignified if you can. Do not take wood off a plant that has never shown a persistent breba and call it King because the tag did.

Prune: this is not a Turkey. Leave last year’s wood if you wanted this year’s breba. Dead wood. Crossing wood. A January hat-rack on a San Pedro is how you fire the employee and keep paying rent. UAEX’s Celeste prune warning is the closest Southern sentence. King is stricter. The crop *is* the old wood.

Water: even. Drought drops fruit. Flood splits what ripened. Alabama said the riper the fig, the more it splits and sours. Morning pick on the breba that made it. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Not a calendar I will invent.

Birds: green-yellow hides a little. They still find a soft fig. Net or pick. One neglected bowl teaches the address.

Late nitrogen: stop so wood can lignify. Green shoots die first. Tender September wood is a winter bill you pay in missing breba.

Nematodes: dirty ground stays in a pot. King is not a nematode solution.

Rain: pick what is giving before the storm. A day-early breba in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. Do not leave finished fruit to advertise for beetles while you wait for a main crop the type did not promise.

## How I would run it

If you want breba and you can protect last year’s wood — pot, shuffle, hoop, east wall, no January hat-rack — trial one. Common sense, not a wasp yard. If the breba eats, keep it as a breba tree. Do not wait three years for a main crop that the type did not promise you.

If you cannot protect old wood, do not plant King as a beginner fig. Plant a common fig that fruits on this year’s wood. Turkey type, Purple, Celeste-class, Jack Lily. Ronde de Bordeaux if you wanted an early dark bowl. Alma if you wanted a closed-eye brown tool. Peter’s Honey if you wanted a yellow honey fig that does not ask for a wasp.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a tree that actually breba’d. Pot up before June.

If the main crop sets and ripens without pollen, [VERIFY] type. You may have a common yellow with a famous name. Eat it. Relabel.

Write Desert King / King on the pot when the breba is the show and the main crop behaves like a San Pedro. Write San Pedro on the same tag so future-you does not prune it like a Turkey.
