If you grew up on canned figs in heavy syrup, you grew up on **Kadota**, or on something close enough that the industry did not care. Condit called Dottato probably the most important single fig variety in the world. Italy had been eating it and drying it for centuries under a pile of names — Ottato, Uttato, Gentile, Binello — before California printed **Kadota** on a crate.

It is a **common** fig. Persistent. No wasp. That is the good news. The rest is climate.

## What the plant is

Greenish white to lemon-yellow. Small to medium in the old sheets, larger when a California orchard waters it for fresh market. Pyriform. Amber pulp. Honey, not berry. A rich, sweet, all-purpose fig if you are in the Southwest or a dry pocket of the South. The most common canned fig because the skin is pale, the flavor is clean, and the fruit looks like a fig in a jar.

Condit’s key called the eye small but the type is not Celeste. Texas A&M’s disease handbook puts Kadota with Magnolia in the open-eye trouble column. Florida says the ostiole is open and partly sealed with a honey-like drop, and quality declines in extremely wet weather. That drop is not a cork. It is sugar water at the door. Beetles can read.

Janice Seedless Kadota is a later sport people chase. Different listing. Do not assume the seedless stick is the canning clone. [VERIFY] fruit.

## Do not confuse the yellow pile

Nurseries will sell “Honey Fig” as if that were a cultivar. Alabama ANR-1145 lists Honey Fig as a Kadota synonym. The marketplace does not. [Peter’s Honey](peters-honey) / Lattarula is a different argument — Northwest backyard legend, Blanche-group talk, Alabama reserved judgment. [White Marseilles](white-marseilles) sits in that same pale-yellow traffic jam. Kadota is Dottato. If the fruit is yellow-green and the eye is a problem in rain, you may have this. If it is a cool-summer honey fig people swear by west of the Cascades, you may have Lattarula. Taste both.

[Excel](excel) is Condit’s 1975 Kadota hybrid. Alabama: superb flavor, promising early Southeast trials. A parent is not a clone. White Ischia is another small yellow that disappoints in the South on Alabama’s sheet. Name pile. Plate.

[Calimyrna](calimyrna) is a large yellow California name and a Smyrna. It drops unless it is caprified. Kadota sets without a wasp. If your “Kadota” never holds fruit, [VERIFY] type before you blame August.

[LSU Gold](lsu-gold) is the official Gulf yellow that is large and late. [LSU Champagne](lsu-champagne) is the official medium yellow tested as Golden Celeste. Neither is Dottato. Write the one you have.

## What the sheets actually wrote

Condit, 1955: Kadota = Dottato. One of the five California commercial names he listed with Mission, Adriatic, Calimyrna, and their Brown Turkey. That monograph is a California description. It is not an 8a trial.

Alabama ANR-1145 (reviewed 2023): greenish white, small- to medium-size, pyriform, vigorous, delicious fresh or dried, rich sweet all-purpose fig, the most common canned fig. Well adapted in the Southwest and drier areas of the South. Fairly hardy. Synonyms: Dottato, Honey Fig. That “drier areas of the South” clause is the polite warning. They did not write “plant this as your only fig in a county where August is a wet blanket.”

Texas Plant Disease Handbook — Fig: plant closed-eye figs. Examples of closed: Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma. Magnolia and Kadota (they typed “Kodata” in the souring table) sit in the susceptible column. Souring is fungi and bacteria carried in by the dried-fruit beetle through an open ostiole. No spray is a vault. Variety choice is the control they still print.

UF/IFAS HS27: medium-large yellow fruit, open ostiole partly sealed with a honey-like substance, quality declines in extremely wet weather. Better suited for canning and preserves than for a fresh plate in a wet year. Their July start is Florida. I will not steal it for Saline County.

UAEX already told Arkansas growers to plant **common** figs. Kadota qualifies on type. It does not qualify as the county-agent first pick. That job still belongs to [Celeste](celeste) and a Turkey type. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already unpacked tight eye, souring, and why a California shipping photo is a bad 8a purchase.

## The 8a week

We have humidity. Kadota was hired for dry heat and a processing line.

Souring is water in the eye plus heat. Split is a balloon. Kadota will give you both in a storm week if you let it hang for a prettier yellow. Pick on the soft. Morning. Neck wilt. Give. Do not wait for the grocery color. A day-early pale fig in the fridge is still a fig. A day-late Kadota on the ground is vinegar and wings.

Birds: a green fig lasts longer than a [Black Mission](black-mission). Kadota is green-yellow when ripe. That helps. It does not make you invisible. A ripe Kadota still smells like lunch. Net or pick.

Rust will still find the leaves. Kadota is not an LSU rust talk. LSU Gold gets “moderate resistance” in the 2025 *Louisiana Agriculture* history. Kadota gets whatever the week gives. Rake litter. Yellow then reddish spots, blisters under the leaf. Do not read one clean July as a cultivar trait.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. A canning fig in deep shade is still shade. Afternoon cloth in July is South Arkansas, not a personality.

Pots versus ground: we run hundreds of pots and about **25–30** in-ground after the 2024–25 reset I said on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). Kadota earns a **#3** you watch in storm week more honestly than it earns the only in-ground hole. Pot up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. In-ground: east wall, water even. A drought-then-flood week is how pale figs split.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree if you can. The [introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said 1–3 years to prove type.

I will not invent a Saline County soften date. [VERIFY] your first soft fig and write it on the tag.

## Culinary, without a romance novel

Fresh: a good fig in a dry week and a sad fig in a wet one. Honey. Nutty if it dried in the right air. We do not sun-dry in this humidity — NC State already told you that. A dehydrator is a machine. A windowsill is a mold farm. I will eat a tree-ripe Kadota in a dry stretch. I will not pretend it is Negra d’Agde.

Jam and the jar: this is the honest job. Pale skin, clean honey, a fig that still looks like a fig under glass. If you put up preserves and you want the jar your aunt made, Kadota is the name. [Magnolia](magnolia) is the other preserve conversation — bronze, open eye, Texas kettle. Different color in the pot. Same rain argument.

Kid bowl: a child who likes sweet and does not want berry acid will eat a ripe Kadota. A child who was promised “the fig from the grocery clamshell” will be confused, because that clamshell was picked for the truck. Tree-ripe or the jar. Those are the two honest plates.

I did not put Kadota on the Fig Jam favorite list. Jack did not either. That is not a curse. That is a plate that likes berry more than syrup.

## How I would run it

If you put up preserves and you want a pale fig that tastes like the jar, Kadota is the honest name. Plant it where August is drier than ours, or keep it in a **#3** you watch in storm week. Common type. No wasp. Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer already settled that sentence.

In-ground in 8a: I would not make this the only tree. I would make it a preserve tree next to a tight-eye eater — Celeste, [Alma](alma), [O’Rourke](lsu-orourke) if the hanging peduncle proves out. East wall. Water even.

If the eye sours three years running, believe it. Graft the hole to Alma or Celeste or an LSU with a smaller door. Keep the roots. Kadota taught you the canned-fig lesson: the industry picked a fruit that looks like a fig under glass. Your weather is not glass.

Write Kadota / Dottato on the pot when the fruit is pale, honey, and honest about rain. Until then it is a yellow fig with a crate name.
