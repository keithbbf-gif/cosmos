Ira Condit released Flanders in **1975** with a handful of other hybrids. Alabama: greenish yellow, medium, violet stripes, amber pulp, pyriform with a long slender neck, fine flavor, vigorous, not particularly hardy, good on the West Coast. Synonym: Verdone Hybrid.

Common type. No wasp. A pretty fig. Pretty is a reason to trial one pot. It is not a reason to skip Celeste-class wood in the ground.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer: common figs persist without pollen. Flanders is in that bucket. You do not need a wasp. You do not need a caprifig. You still need a winter that does not eat a plant Alabama already called not particularly hardy, and a summer with enough growing degree days to finish a medium fig that was written for the West Coast.

Condit’s 1955 monograph is the synonym bible Alabama still bolds names from. Flanders is later work — Storey, Condit, and the UC line that also gave us Conadria, Excel, and Tena. The 1955 book will not settle your tag. The 1975 release note and ANR-1145 will. A breeding year is not a zone.

Verdone is Condit’s name for Adriatic. Verdone Hybrid on a Flanders tag means Adriatic blood, not “this is Adriatic JH.” I named Adriatic JH on The Fig Jam. I did not name Flanders. Different pot. Different job.

## Stripes, again

Three photographs. Learn them or you will buy the wrong stick twice.

**Panache** (Panachée, Tiger, Variegato): green and yellow stripes on the *ripe* skin. Chimera. Leaf stays ordinary. Alabama: aficionados in California say excellent; in the South, flavor is mediocre, mealy texture. That is a hard sentence and I will not sand it. If you came here for the candy-cane fig, read the Panache draft.

**LSU Tiger** (2007, official): dark stripe on *immature* fruit. Ripe fruit is a large ordinary Gulf fig. Juvenile wood wants to grow sideways. Stagg’s caption is the field mark. A seller who writes “LSU Tiger Panache Flanders” is making a smoothie.

**Flanders**: violet stripes on a yellow-green fig with a long slender neck. Amber pulp. Medium. Pyriform. If your plant does not match that still, you do not have Flanders. If the leaf is variegated, you have a different argument. If the ripe fruit is a candy cane, you have Panache. If the stripe vanishes when the fruit colors up and the body is large and Gulf, you may have Tiger.

Yellow Long Neck is a looking-forward name from the 25 July 2025 interview — untried on that plate. A long yellow neck without violet stripes is not Flanders. Hunt has a long *stem* (peduncle) and a small brown body. Do not merge four long things into one cultivar because they are long.

The long neck is a pick cue and a rain shed, maybe. Hunt of Eatonton, Georgia, was bred for a long stem so water would miss the eye. Flanders came from a California hybrid block. Do not steal Hunt’s Southern rot story for a Condit pretty fig. [VERIFY] after rain. A neck is not a closed ostiole.

## Climate

“Good on the West Coast” and “not particularly hardy” is Alabama telling you this is not Chicago Hardy and not Alma. ANR-1145’s hardy conversation is 15–20°F on dormant wood for the old favorites. Flanders did not get that sentence. In 8a I would keep it in a pot I can shuffle. In-ground it is a dare after a mild string of winters.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD, humidity, and eye size decide whether a famous fig is *your* fig. Flanders is medium, pretty, and West Coast. Our steam is not a Mediterranean hang. I will not invent a Saline County ripen date. Write the first soften on the tag. [VERIFY].

Breba: UAEX said breba rarely overwinters in Arkansas. Flanders is not sold as a two-crop machine the way Madeleine and King are. If you get a spring fig, last year’s wood lived. If you do not, you still have a common fig. Do not hat-rack it in January to “make it bushy” and then complain it never brebas.

Sister slugs in the Condit box: Conadria got Alabama’s hardy-with-good-rebound sentence. Excel got hardy-and-promising. Tena got hot-and-dry. Flanders got pretty-and-tender. Read the right page. I like a hybrid that was aimed at more than Fresno. I will not pretend the pretty one inherited Conadria’s rebound.

## Flavor I will not fake

Alabama’s word is fine. Amber pulp. A Verdone / Adriatic family idea with makeup. I did not plate Flanders on The Fig Jam. Jack did not either. Consensus among collectors is “pretty and worth a pot if it eats.” That is consensus, not a homestead score.

Dry-week honey-berry is the hope. Wet-week mealy is the risk — the same humidity tax Alabama put on Panache. One plant. [VERIFY] on our tree before anyone writes a FigRoots score.

Eye: not the reason people plant it. Humidity will still vote. UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. Pick on the soft. Neck wilt. Morning. A day-early striped fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A day-late striped fig on the ground is a photo for the birds.

Birds: a striped fig is a sign. Green-yellow helps a little until the violet shows. Then they can see it. Net or pick.

Split: medium is not immune. Drought then flood. Overripe hang. Even water. Alabama’s trouble table is blunt — fruit sours and many split in an unusually wet year or on an unsuitable variety. Flanders was not written as a wet-August tool.

## How I would run it

Collector pot. One **#3**, pot up before June. Wood from a fruiting striped-neck tree, not a listing with a French filter and a mountain. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Fertigate once it is a plant.

East wall or afternoon cloth in July. **6–8 hours** of sun. South shade plus water is already on the Pick The Right Fig page. Do not sun-cook a West Coast pretty fig on black plastic and then call Condit a liar.

Do not row it. Do not tell a neighbor this is the fig that will make them love figs. Tell them Negra, Jack Lily, a tool Celeste or Turkey, then Flanders if they still have a pot and they like a photograph.

If it eats, keep it. If it is only pretty, keep one and graft the extras. We do not owe Condit a shrine. We owe him a citation and an honest winter.

If two winters burn it to the rim, believe Alabama. Keep it as a shuffle pot or move the hole to Conadria if you wanted a yellow-green hybrid that comes back.

Nematodes: pots. Mosaic mottles a lot of Adriatic-family leaves. Mottled is not a reason to discard a tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood.

Write Flanders on the pot when the long-neck violet-stripe fruit repeats. Write Verdone hybrid only as a notebook synonym. Write unknown-striped-neck if you are still guessing. Guessing is allowed. Printing is not.
