People buy Calimyrna the way they buy a memory. The grocery dried fig. Nutty. Seedy. The one that made “fig” mean something in an American pantry. Then they plant a stick in 8a and wait for that pantry to grow on a branch.

Jack already wrote the type primer. **Smyrna** figs need pollen to ripen fruit — breba or main. No wasp, no crop. You can brush ostioles by hand if you have caprifig pollen and a lot of patience. That is a hobby. It is not a yard tree for a kid who asked for figs.

Calimyrna is the California name for **Sari Lop** — Turkish *sarı lop*, yellow and delicious — also Lob Injir, Erbeyli, Aidin. George Roeding paid **$25** for the word Calimyrna so a Turkish fig could wear California. The first commercial planting at Fresno in 1886 did not crop until 1900, after the blastophaga was actually working. That is the origin story. It is also the warning label.

## Caducous means it drops

Alabama’s words: caducous (Smyrna) figs need pollination to set crops. Without pollination the fruit drops before it matures. Examples: Marabout, Calimyrna, Zidi. Jack said the same thing in fewer words on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/): Smyrna figs need to be pollinated to ripen any fruit, breba or main. Common figs are self-pollinating. San Pedro needs pollen for the main crop. Caprifigs are male trees; a few taste fine and most do not.

A neighbor will tell you the tree is “unproductive.” The tree is productive at dropping. That is the type working.

Fruit when it is caprified: large, yellow, oblate-spherical, large open eye, amber pulp, many seeds, rich nutty flavor. The seeds are the point. Jack said caprified figs are generally sweeter, with more seed crunch, better flavor, texture, and looks. He is right about the class. He did not tell you to start a wasp yard in Bradley County.

The eye is large and open. Even in California that is a beetle door. In our August it would be a joke if the fruit ever stayed on to meet the joke.

## Do not confuse the famous yellow names

[Kadota](kadota) is also a California commercial yellow. It is a **common** fig. Persistent. No wasp. Honey. Canning. Open-enough eye for TAMU to put it in the souring column with Magnolia. Different machine. If your yellow tree sets and ripens without any pollen you know about, you do not have a miracle Calimyrna. You have a common yellow fig with a famous sticker.

[LSU Gold](lsu-gold) is the official Gulf large yellow. [LSU Champagne](lsu-champagne) is the official medium yellow. [Peter’s Honey](peters-honey) / Lattarula is the Northwest honey argument. None of those need a wasp. [Desert King](desert-king) is the San Pedro people confuse with “two-crop magic” — breba without pollen, main crop often a different story. Calimyrna is not San Pedro. It is not common. It is caducous all the way down.

Sierra and Sequoia are later UC common figs bred to *replace* some of the Smyrna headache for growers who still wanted a large yellow fig without a wasp. Different drafts if we write them. They are not Calimyrna. They are the industry admitting the wasp is a job.

A catalog “starter pack” with Calimyrna next to Celeste is selling names. The [introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already told you to buy from people who have fruit, and to wait 1–3 years to prove type. A Smyrna wastes those years as a leaf bush.

## What the sheets actually wrote

Condit, 1955: Calimyrna = Sari Lop / Lob Injir. One of the five California commercial names — Mission, Kadota, Adriatic, Calimyrna, Brown Turkey. Four of those can be common-fig conversations (their Turkey is San Piero, not your neighbor’s Everbearing). Calimyrna is the one that is a different machine.

Alabama ANR-1145: “The California commercial fig. A large yellow fig with amber pulp and a large open eye. Oblate-spherical. Sweet, very rich nutty flavor. Numerous seeds. Smyrna type. Needs pollination. Not practical for southern growers.” Synonyms: Erbeyli, Lop Injir, Sari Lop. That last sentence is the county-agent version of this whole page.

UAEX: common figs only for Arkansas. Alabama: only common types if you want Alabama fruit. TAMU’s Texas fruit notes say the same thing another way — Smyrna, San Pedro, and caprifig are not the Gulf backyard recommendation, and open-eye shipping figs are not the souring recommendation.

I will not invent a Saline County ripen date for a fig that, without pollen, does not ripen here at all.

## What we do not do here

We do not write a how-to for *Blastophaga psenes* in South Arkansas. The wasp is a regulated, climate-bound, industry-shaped animal. Caprifigs are male trees. You would need the right capri crop (profichi) timed to the Smyrna crop, in a place the wasp can live. That is not a #3 on a wagon.

Jack said you can buy pollen and brush ostioles. People do that. I will not pretend we run that as a homestead system. If you want to experiment, do it as an experiment. Label it. Do not tell a first-year grower that Calimyrna is “just another yellow fig.”

This page exists so you do not donate three years to a leaf bush.

## The 8a week on a tree that may never finish

Sun still matters. **6–8 hours.** A Smyrna in shade is a prettier leaf bush. Afternoon cloth in July still saves leaves.

Pots versus ground: we keep most of the collection in **#3** and **#5** pots — I said hundreds of pots, about 25–30 in-ground, on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). A Calimyrna does not earn the in-ground hole unless you already knew the deal and wanted the shade. Pot up before June if you are rooting wood. Fertigate once it is a plant. The plant will grow. Growth is not a crop.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, like any fig stick. It will root. Rooting is not a reason to keep the name.

Birds and rust and rain are theoretical until fruit stays on. If fruit is setting, swelling, and dropping at marble size, believe Condit. Believe Jack. Believe the ground under the tree. If fruit is setting and ripening with no pollen you know about, [VERIFY] the type. A persistent “Calimyrna” is not a miracle. It is a mislabel. Eat the fruit. Write the honest name.

Rust will still find the leaves. Rake. A leaf bush gets rust like a fruiting tree.

Pick: you will not have a pick on a true Smyrna here. If you do, you are either brushing pollen or you have the wrong plant. Do not write a ripen date either way until the plate repeats.

## Culinary, honest

The pantry fig is a caprified Sari Lop from a dry valley with an industry around a wasp. Nutty. Seedy. That is a real food. It is not a food your un-caprified 8a tree will put in a bowl.

Fresh: none, if the type is true and you have no pollen. Do not invent a “green Calimyrna salad.” Dropped buttons are not a crop.

Jam: none, same reason. You cannot kettle what hit the dirt at marble size.

Kid bowl: buy the dried ones if that is the flavor they asked for. Or plant a common honey fig you can actually ripen — [Kadota](kadota) in a dry week, [LSU Gold](lsu-gold) if it finishes, [Champagne](lsu-champagne), Peter’s Honey if that is the tree you have — or plant a berry fig and admit the craving changed. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) is the climate page. This is the type page’s expensive example.

I did not put Calimyrna on the Fig Jam favorite list. Jack did not either. We were talking about fruit we eat.

## If you already have the tree

Keep it if you like the shade and you understand the deal. Graft it if you wanted fruit. A live Smyrna root system is a perfectly good hole for a common scion. We graft. We do not shame a tree for being the type on the tag.

Write Calimyrna / Sari Lop on a pot only if you mean a Smyrna, and then write the rest of the sentence: **needs caprification**. If you will not write the second half, do not write the first.
