Pastilière — you will see it without the accent — is an early dark fig people either love for the calendar or curse for the mess. Grower talk: small-to-medium, dark, berry-leaning, early, a dropper. Figs swell, then a wind or a dry spell or a mood puts them on the ground.

The how-to pack already said figs drop for reasons that are not a curse: water, heat, type, a Smyrna without pollen, a young plant. Pastilière has a reputation on top of those reasons. [VERIFY] on our tree before we call it a chronic dropper. I did not plate it on The Fig Jam.

Common type. No wasp. If it drops at marble size every time, look at type and water first.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer: common figs persist without pollen. Pastilière is in that bucket in the accounts I use. You do not buy a wasp. You do not brush an ostiole. San Pedro figs drop a main crop without pollen — Desert King is the American example. Smyrna figs drop both crops. If your Pastilière sheds every fig at marble size two years running with even water, [VERIFY] type. You may have a caducous plant with a French sticker. You may have a hose problem. You may have a young pot that is still deciding to be a tree.

Condit’s 1955 monograph has Violette and early dark piles. Marketplace tags say Pastiliere, Pastilière, Violette. Violette de Bordeaux is a different small black — Negronne, deep red pulp, Alabama’s Bordeaux line. Do not merge every French violet word. Ronde de Bordeaux is the other small early dark. Different shape talk. Different pot.

Alabama did not give Pastilière its own ANR-1145 line. Collector consensus is the source. Mark it.

## Early is the job

Ronde de Bordeaux, Pastilière, a Celeste-class brown — those are the figs that feed you while Madeira is a bead. If Pastilière also drops ripe fruit, you pick the ground. Chickens help. Pride does not.

A dropper that drops ripe is still food. A dropper that drops hard is a management problem. Know which one you have.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is the filter and early is how a short year still feeds you. I will not invent a Saline County soften date. Write the first dark fig that gives — on the tree or on the mulch. Both dates are data. Early is the hope. Early is not a warranty.

In a resprout year after a freeze, early figs on new wood are food. Late figs on new wood are leaves. If you wanted a winter-kill story, Chicago Hardy is the resprout name. Pastilière is the summer partner if it actually fruits early on the wood you have. [VERIFY].

Breba: UAEX — rarely overwinters in Arkansas. An early dark may throw a spring fig after a civil winter. Last year’s wood. Do not hat-rack it in January and then call it a one-crop fig. Main crop on current wood is the crop you can plan.

Birds and squirrels finish what the drop starts. Net. Morning walk. Dark and early is the first billboard in the yard. Green figs hide. This one does not.

Eye: not the reason people plant it. [VERIFY] after rain. UAEX souring story still applies. ANR-1145: the riper they are, the more they split and sour. A fig that drops ripe can beat a storm. A fig that hangs through a storm can sour. Pick what softened. Pick what hit the ground before the beetles do.

Split: small-to-medium helps. Drought then flood still unzips. Even water. The drop reputation and the split reputation can be the same week if you let a pot cook and then drown it.

Flavor: berry-leaning in the talk. Not a Negra speech. Not a Madeira contest. A handful in July. Consensus. I did not plate Pastilière on 25 July 2025. Jack’s early darks were Syrian Dark #2 and Navid’s Unk Dark Greek. Same job class. Different names. I will not stamp a homestead score. [VERIFY] on our tree.

## Sister slugs

Ronde de Bordeaux: small, round, early, tight-eye talk. If your Pastilière is tiny, round, and never drops, you may have RdB. If your RdB dumps half the crop on the mulch, you may have this.

Violette de Bordeaux: small black, deep red, medium eye. Different plate.

Noire de Caromb: productive dark, working bowl, not famous for drop.

Col de Dame Noire: late, neck, collector. Opposite clock.

Malta Black: our clone folder. Caption that clone as that clone.

Brown Turkey: copper, rebound, sugar. If your “Pastilière” is a Turkey, the drop may be water and the plate will say so.

## How I would run it

One pot, or in-ground if you want a tree you walk daily. Wood from a fruiting early dark. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Even water — drought then flood makes any fig throw crop. Pot up before June. One **#3** until the calendar and the drop both repeat. Fertigate once it is a plant.

**6–8 hours** of sun. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. South shade plus water is already on Pick The Right Fig. Nematodes: pots. A #3 still freezes at the rim. Shuffle it.

Hardiness: not a Chicago Hardy stamp. ANR-1145’s 15–20°F list is the old favorites. An early French dark in 8a is a pot-honest trial. In-ground if you will walk it every morning in the pick window.

If the drop is the plant and the plate is good, keep it and pick. If the drop is the plant and the plate is ordinary, graft. We do not need a messy ordinary fig. We have Turkeys for ordinary, and they rebound.

Do not plant it as the only fig if you travel in July. Plant a tight-eye eater that hangs. Plant Pastilière as the tree you visit with a bowl.

A morning walk is the cultivar. Bowl in one hand. Soft figs off the skirt. Hard figs left if they are still swelling. Split figs in the trash, not in a pile under the pot. ANR-1145 already said do not leave fruit piles for beetles. The drop reputation is how those piles start if you sleep in.

If you pot it, set the **#3** where you can see the rim from the kitchen door. A dropper in the back row is a squirrel buffet. In-ground, keep the skirt clear so you can see what hit. Mulch, not tall grass.

Do not mail cuttings as Pastilière until an early dark fig repeats and the drop, if it happens, is in the notes. Behavior without a pedigree is still useful. A pedigree without behavior is a sticker.

Write Pastilière on the pot when an early dark fig repeats and the drop, if it happens, is part of the notes. Write unknown-early-dark-drop if you are describing behavior without a pedigree. Behavior is useful. Pedigree is optional.
