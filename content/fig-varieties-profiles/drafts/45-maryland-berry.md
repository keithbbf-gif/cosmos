I named Maryland Berry on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) in the same breath as Navid’s Unk, BGM Unk, Labrijta, Melanzana Merdoscola. Some of those names are places. Some are Unk. The point of the list was not a pedigree. It was a plate.

Maryland Berry is a berry fig we liked enough to say into a microphone. That is the official note. The rest is how not to ruin it with a story.

Common type unless the tree teaches us otherwise. I expect common. Persistent. No wasp. Jack already defined that on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). If a plant labeled Maryland Berry drops at marble size every year, look at water and type before you write a curse. We do not keep a wasp in Bradley County.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. Maryland Berry is one of the reasons I can say that without being cruel to a grandpa tree. Different mouths. Different jobs.

## What I will not write

I will not invent a ripening date, an ostiole grade, or a brix number I did not record. I will not say it tastes like Negra or like Red Sicilian to tidy the set. I will say: berry, a favorite, wait for *our* stills.

[An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. A first-year cup can lie. A regional name can lie for a decade. Photograph the cut once a year so the memory does not drift. If the folder is thin, shoot the cut before you write poetry.

If a seller lists Maryland Berry, ask for fruit photos and a source. Regional names wander. A “Maryland” seedling and this clone may not be friends. The introduction page already said trusted sellers matter and the review window closes before you taste type.

If our plant stops matching the memory, we write unknown-berry and keep eating. The interview is not a tattoo.

[Fig Reviews](https://figroots.com/fig_reviews_archive/) already scored Red Sicilian, Negra d’Agde, and LSU Jack Lily. Maryland Berry does not get a fake card on this page. The microphone is the card we have.

Jack did not put Maryland Berry on his short list. He ranked Red Sicilian, Navid’s Unk Dark Greek, Syrian Dark #2, Jack Lilly, NSDC, Negra d’Agde. I put Maryland Berry on mine. Two lists. One house. Do not move his ranking onto my name.

## Unk is a feature

Navid’s Unk and BGM Unk sat on the same list. Jack ranked Navid’s Unk Dark Greek second. Unknown is how honest collections work. The unknown-names how-to in the other pack is the sister essay. This page is one named berry that could have been an Unk if the person who passed it on had been more tired.

Do not “solve” Maryland Berry into a European synonym to feel smart. If a match appears in year four, add a hypothesis. Keep the inventory name until the match is boringly obvious.

Labrijta, Melanzana Merdoscola, Red Lebanese Bekaa Valley — those sat in the same breath. Places and Unks and a Maryland. The honesty is the list, not a pedigree chart.

## Rain, eye, birds — without a fake grade

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said the ostiole is a humidity judge and GDD is a count. I will not print tight or open until the still shows it. Photograph size and eye in the same frame. Future-us will need both.

TAMU’s [disease handbook](https://plantdiseasehandbook.tamu.edu/food-crops/fruit-crops/fig/) puts Magnolia and Kadota in the open-eye souring column and Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma on the closed side. Maryland Berry is not on that table. UAEX’s souring sentence is still water in the eye plus heat after rain. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit sours and many split in an unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year. Those rules apply to a favorite. A favorite is not a vault.

NSDC is the large-eyed splitter Jack already named. If Maryland Berry starts splitting like that lady, write it down. Do not protect the interview from the weather.

Split is a balloon. Even water. A drought then a flood is how berry figs unzip the week you wanted a photograph. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. A day-early berry fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A split favorite on the ground is a bad way to defend a list.

Birds: if it colors dark, they will find it. If it stays quieter, they will still find it when it is the only ripe thing. Net or pick. I will not invent the skin color to finish this paragraph.

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf, defoliation while fruit is trying to finish. [Alabama ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) already wrote the rust chapter. Rake. Do not leave a wet skirt of last year’s leaves. I will not advertise rust-proof on a plant I did not measure for rust.

Mosaic mottles leaves on a lot of berry figs we actually like. Mottled is not a reason to discard a tree that plates. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood off the favorite tree.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. Maryland Berry is not Alma and it is not the LSU Purple rumor.

## Sister slugs

**Negra d’Agde** is berry first, tight eye, productive, on both of our lists and on the review card. Different name. Do not merge them because both say berry.

**Red Sicilian** is Jack’s first: productive, sweet, nice berry, medium. A bowl fig. Different sugar talk.

**Syrian Dark #2** is Jack’s third: extremely tight, small, productive, strong berry, moderately sweet. Humidity syllabus. Different number.

**Nuestra Señora del Carmen** is large, very sweet, large eye, splits in rain. Different door.

**Black Madeira** is late, famous, intense when it finishes. Different clock.

**Navid’s Unk Dark Greek** is Jack’s second. Unk is allowed. Do not upgrade an Unk into Maryland Berry because the cut looks close. Do not upgrade Maryland Berry into a named European fig because a thread got excited.

## How I would run it

Keeper. Wood off the favorite tree, **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Label twice. Paint pen on the pot. Pot up to a **#3** before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July. A berry fig in deep shade is still shade. A berry fig in a west fry on black plastic will cook and then split when you water it.

In-ground when it earns it. East wall if you have one, clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots. Do not plant it in last year’s tomato hole. Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how a favorite writes a tender will.

Same water and shade rules as every berry fig we actually like. Pick in the morning. Photograph the cut once a year so the memory does not drift. Write the first soften on the tag. [VERIFY]. I will not invent a Saline County date.

Our [winter protection](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) page named Smith and Chicago Hardy, not Maryland Berry. Treat a pot as a pot you shuffle. Barely moist in the shop. Not a heated den. Alabama’s 15–20°F talk is for dormant wood they named. This name is not on that list. If it dies to the crown, five or six trunks at two feet, TAMU’s chapter, and keep the hole. We graft. We do not perform funerals for live roots.

Write Maryland Berry on the pot that matches the fig I was willing to name. Write Unk-berry-20XX on the ones that eat like it and came without papers. Both labels can be true. Only one of them is bragging.
