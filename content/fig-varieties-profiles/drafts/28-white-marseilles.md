Jefferson grew a Marseilles fig and America never let the name rest. Alabama bolded **Blanche** and then listed Bianci, Lemon, Marseilles, Mayes Yellow, White Marseilles, White Russian. Medium to large greenish yellow, white pulp, many seeds, turbinate without a neck, sweet and delicate, nutty from soft seeds. Some eyes open, some relatively closed. Well adapted in the South. Fairly hardy.

Lemon is what a lot of Southern yards say. If your grandpa’s yellow fig is this shape, you may already have the profile.

Common type. No wasp.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer: common figs persist without pollen. Blanche is in that bucket. You do not need a wasp. You do not need a caprifig. A yellow fig that drops at marble size every time is either thirsty, young, or the wrong type. Look at water first. Then look at the tag. [VERIFY].

Condit’s 1955 monograph is why Alabama bolded Blanche. That is the Condit-aligned name. The yard still says Lemon. The marketplace still says White Marseilles because French sells. I will not fight a grandpa who calls his tree Lemon. I will fight a seller who ships Kadota as Marseilles because both are yellow.

## Same pile as Peter’s Honey — not the same pot

Lattarula / Peter’s Honey may be Blanche. Alabama would not stamp it. ANR-1145, last reviewed June 2023, is blunt: Condit does not identify Lattarula as a distinct variety and considers it a synonym for Blanche. Alabama Extension horticulturists reserve judgment pending further study. Synonym on that line: Italian Honey Fig.

I gave Peter’s Honey its own draft so we could be honest twice. This page is the old Southern Lemon / Marseilles tree, the one that predates forum names. That page is the Northwest honey fig people pay a nursery word for. They may be one plant. They may not. The plate does not care what 1955 decided until the ostiole and the cut match.

If your Lemon is Kadota, the rain will say so. Kadota is Dottato — greenish white, pyriform, open-enough eye, the canned fig of the Southwest. TAMU put Kadota with Magnolia in the open-eye trouble column. If your Lemon is Champagne, the calendar and the official LSU tag will say so. Champagne is a 2007 release, not a Jefferson tree. If it is a seedling from a yard, it is a yard fig. Call it Lemon-ours until it matches.

I-258 is a different Italian honey people chase. Keith had not plated it as of July 2025. Its own draft. Do not merge.

Excel is Condit’s 1975 Kadota hybrid. Conadria is the Adriatic hybrid with rebound. Gold is late and large. None of those are Blanche just because they are yellow-green in a listing photo.

## Eye is the fork

Alabama said it out loud: some specimens open, some closed. That is not a cop-out. That is a pile. Photograph the ostiole. The closed ones belong in 8a. The open ones belong in a dry week or a preserve pot.

UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. Dried-fruit beetles want a door. ANR-1145’s trouble table: fruit sours and many split — unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year. Pick immature for preserves if that is the week you got. Janet Carson’s old kettle line still works on a yellow fig that will not wait.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said humid climates want tight eyes and smaller fruit. Blanche is medium to large. Some eyes close. Some do not. Do not buy the name and assume you bought Celeste’s door. Celeste’s eye stays green until the fig is almost ripe. A Lemon eye that colors early and sits open is a different tree.

Flavor: delicate, nutty, old-fashioned. Not Negra. A Sunday fig. I did not name it on The Fig Jam. Jack did not either. I would still eat a grandpa Lemon before I bought a marketplace “White Marseilles” with a French filter. Tree-ripe in a dry week is the only fair plate. Grocery yellow figs are picked for the truck.

I will not invent a homestead tasting wheel. Soft seeds that read as nutty is Alabama’s sentence. White pulp is Alabama’s sentence. Sweet and delicate is Alabama’s sentence. Our plate is unwritten until it repeats. [VERIFY].

Season: I will not invent a Saline County date. Write the first soften. Mid-to-old-yard timing in a lot of talk. Earlier than Gold. Later than a Celeste-class brown if your clone is the large one. [VERIFY].

Birds: yellow-green ripe fruit hides a little. Not a cloak. Net or pick if it is the only ripe thing.

Breba: UAEX said breba rarely overwinters in Arkansas. An old yard Lemon may throw a spring fig after a civil winter. That is last year’s wood. Do not hat-rack a bearing Lemon in January and then call the variety a one-crop fig. You ate the wood. Main crop on current wood is the crop you can plan.

## Hardiness and the hole

Fairly hardy on Alabama’s sheet. That is kinder than Mission and kinder than Flanders. It is not a Chicago Hardy brand. ANR-1145’s 15–20°F conversation is still the old favorites — Celeste, English Brown Turkey, Alma, Hardy Chicago. Blanche sits in “fairly hardy.” A pot still freezes at the rim. Shuffle it.

Mosaic mottles leaves on a lot of old yellow figs. Mottled is not a reason to discard a tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cuttings off a cleaner neighbor tree and start a new hole.

Nematodes: pots. Pick The Right Fig already said it. Alma and some LSU talk get the resistance rumor. Blanche does not.

## How I would run it

If you have a yard Lemon that already proved the county, take **6–8 inch** wood off *that*. Three nodes. Do not replace it with a mail-order Marseilles until the mail-order fruits. The introduction page already said the review window closes before you taste type. Trusted sellers. 1–3 years.

If you do not have one, one pot is enough. A **#3**, pot up before June. Fairly hardy. Still shuffle. Still do not hat-rack if you wanted fruit. Fertigate once it is a plant.

**6–8 hours** of sun. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. Even water. Do not sun-dry a delicate yellow on a roof in our humidity. A dehydrator is a machine. A windowsill is a science fair.

In-ground: clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots, not last year’s tomato hole if nematodes live there. A grandpa Lemon that already lives in the county is worth more than a French PDF.

Do not plant it as the only fig if the eye is open. Plant a tight-eye eater. Plant the Lemon as the Sunday yellow.

If the eye sours three years, graft a tighter yellow — Jack Lily is Adriatic family on our review card, small and tight; Champagne is the official LSU honey if you want a release name. If the plate is honey-nutty and you like it better than Gold’s late clock, keep it and stop hunting synonyms.

Write White Marseilles / Blanche on the pot that matches the old yellow yard fig. Write Lemon-ours if that is the honest name. Write unknown-yellow-yard if you are still in year one. Year one lies.
