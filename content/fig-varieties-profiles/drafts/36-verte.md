On [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) I said I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far, and that some (Verte) have had a problem with splitting, but we have not cut any because we like a long trial and will probably go in-ground before we decide. In those cases we graft. The tree lives as rootstock.

That is the profile. I can decorate it with Condit and Alabama. I will not walk it back.

## What the sheets say

[Alabama ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) lists **Green Ischia** as a small grass-green fig, dark strawberry pulp, oblate to spherical, small fairly closed eye, good quality, not as good as Brown Turkey, matures late, fairly hardy — synonyms Coeur, Verdale, Verte. Then they list **Verte** again as medium to large, grass-green, dark strawberry, rare breba, pyriform with or without a neck, small fairly closed eye, excellent flavor, under trial in the South — synonyms Ischia Green, Verdae, Coeur, Figue d’Espagne.

Two entries. One pile. Collectors argue whether Verte and Green Ischia are the same plate. I will not settle it in a paragraph. I will say: our plant labeled Verte splits. We are still trialing. Jack said Verte and Syiah Orak might have to go but it was still early.

Common type. Persistent. No wasp. Jack already drew that line on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). A green strawberry fig that drops at marble size is a water problem or a mislabeled caducous, not a reason to start a wasp project in Bradley County.

Eye “fairly closed” on paper. Split anyway. Tight is not a split charm. Split is water and skin and a ripe balloon. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said the ostiole is a humidity judge, not a magic cork. Verte is the exhibit I was willing to name out loud.

## Flavor versus weather

Excellent flavor on a dry week is why Verte stayed famous. People compare it to a serious green fig, a strawberry jam with a thin skin. Thin skin is the split. I will eat the dry-week fruit and still call the wet-week fruit a problem, because I already did.

Alabama’s Green Ischia line is cooler than their Verte line: good, not as good as Brown Turkey, late. Their Verte line is excellent, under trial. That gap is why I will not merge the two names to tidy a spreadsheet. Taste the tree we have. Caption the clone we have. The introduction page already said it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. A first-year cup can lie. A first-year split can also lie — young wood and irregular water make any fig unzip. Year two and year three are the argument.

I will not invent a Saline County soften date. Late-ish is Alabama’s Green Ischia clock. Late plus a split habit is a double tax. Pair this plant with an early tight-eye fig that pays rent in July — Jack Lily, Syrian Dark #2, a Celeste-class brown if you like sugar. I am not a fan of Celeste so far. I still want something on the plate while Verte is deciding whether to finish or to crack.

Breba: Alabama said rare on Verte. In Arkansas, UAEX already told you breba rarely overwinters. Do not buy this tree for a June crop. Main crop on current wood is the crop.

## Rain, split, sour

UAEX’s souring sentence is water in the eye plus heat after rain. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit sours and many split in an unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year, and the remedy is a more suitable variety or pick immature fruit for preserves. Verte is not a kettle fig in my mouth. If I pick it early I am admitting the week beat the skin.

Split and sour can share a bowl. A side crack is a balloon. An ostiole-end split is a door. Dried-fruit beetles read sugar at that door. TAMU’s disease handbook put Magnolia and Kadota — they spelled Kadota “Kodata” — in the open-eye souring column, and Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma on the closed side. Verte is not on that table. “Fairly closed” is not a Texas resistant rating. [VERIFY] the ostiole on *our* fruit after the first storm. Photograph it wet.

Even water. A drought then a flood is how thin-skinned green figs unzip. I will not write a grid. I will walk it in the morning. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. A day-early Verte in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A split Verte on the ground is wings and vinegar.

I will not tell a neighbor to plant Verte as the only green fig. I will tell them to plant it as a trial next to a tighter green — Jack Lily is Adriatic family on our review card, small, unofficial LSU — and to keep the hole if the split does not improve on a better site.

## Birds, rust, mosaic

Birds: green helps. A grass-green ripe fig hides longer than a Mission billboard. Split fruit is a bird bell. Once the skin opens, every jay in the county can read lunch. Net the trial if it is the only ripe thing that week, or pick what softened and leave the hard ones.

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf, defoliation while late fruit is trying to finish. Rake. Do not leave a wet skirt of last year’s leaves. I will not advertise Verte as rust-proof.

Mosaic mottles leaves on a lot of old green figs. Alabama hung “very subject to mosaic” on Adriatic, not on Verte. Mottled is still not a reason to discard a tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. Verte is not Alma and it is not the LSU Purple rumor.

## Sister slugs

**Dalmatie** is a large late green strawberry fig. Different profile. Different clock. If your “Verte” is baseball-sized and still hard in October, you may have the size tax without our split sentence.

**Adriatic / Verdone** is Condit’s commercial green. Alabama called it disappointing in the South. I still named **Adriatic JH** as a favorite. JH is a suffix, not a license to call every green strawberry fig Adriatic, and not a license to call ours Verte.

**White Ischia** is Alabama’s small yellow Ischia — poor in the South. Different color. Different job.

**Conadria** is Condit’s Adriatic hybrid with a rebound sentence. Yellow-green, light strawberry, freeze talk. Different plant.

Do not merge four green strawberry ideas because the cut looks pink. The cut is a family. The split note is the cultivar we have.

## How I would run it

We already are. Long trial. In-ground before a death sentence. Graft if the split does not improve on a better site. Do not mail cuttings as a beginner fig. Mail them with the split sentence attached.

Wood off the trial tree only if someone wants the experiment. **6–8 inches**, three nodes, lignified if you can. Alabama’s chapter likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Pot up to a **#3** before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight and freedom from competing trees so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July so the leaves do not cook while the fruit tries to finish. A Verte in a west fry on black plastic is a sulk. A Verte in deep shade is still shade.

In-ground in 8a: east wall if you have one, clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots. Do not plant it in last year’s tomato hole if nematodes live there. Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how a “fairly hardy” variety writes a tender will. Alabama’s 15–20°F conversation is for dormant wood, not for a pot rim and not for a warm spell in March.

Pick in the morning. Walk it after a storm. Write the first soften date on the tag. [VERIFY]. I will not invent one for Saline County.

If the split stays the story after a better site and even water, graft. Keep the hole. The tree lives as rootstock. That is the sentence I already said. A live root system is not a funeral.

Write Verte on the pot we have been honest about. Write Green Ischia only if a second plant matches that sheet and not our splitter — or if they match and the argument is over. Until then, our inventory name plus the word split in the notes. The notes are the cultivar.
