Collectors call I-258 a honey fig, Italian, often in the same breath as Lattarula / Peter’s Honey. That breath is how name piles start. This page will not finish the pile for them.

## What a number is

Italian growers and later collectors numbered seedlings and selections. I-258 is one that escaped into English-language trades. The number is an inventory. It is not a guarantee of honey, a closed eye, or a kind winter.

[An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers matter and it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. A number on a tag is a claim. A marketplace “Italian honey rare” is a blender. The review window closes before you taste type.

Peter’s Honey, Blanche, Kadota, Champagne, and Excel are all yellow-honey conversations. I-258 may join that bench when we eat it. Until then it is a stick with a number.

Jack did not rank it. He ranked Jack Lily, which is a small green berry fig, not a honey number. [Fig Reviews](https://figroots.com/fig_reviews_archive/) already scored that plant. Do not move his ranking onto a number he did not name.

I will not invent a ripening date for Saline County. I will not invent an ostiole grade. I will not invent a brix number. I will not write “rich honey with notes of…” in a caption. I will not caption a Commons yellow fig as 258. I will not merge it with Peter’s Honey to tidy INDEX.

## Sister slugs — the yellow bench we will not collapse

**Peter’s Honey / Lattarula / Italian Honey** is the Northwest honey argument. Condit pointed at Blanche. [Alabama ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) reserved judgment on Lattarula. That page already exists. This number does not get to finish that fight.

**White Marseilles / Blanche / Lemon** is the old Southern yellow yard fig. Some eyes open, some closed. Alabama bolded Blanche. If a numbered Italian eats like a grandpa Lemon, we say so after we eat it. We do not erase the number on a hunch.

**Kadota / Dottato** is the canned fig. Open-enough eye. TAMU’s [disease handbook](https://plantdiseasehandbook.tamu.edu/food-crops/fruit-crops/fig/) put it with Magnolia in the souring column. If I-258 sours like Kadota, the rain said so. That is a result, not a synonym.

**Excel** is Condit’s 1975 Kadota hybrid: medium yellow, amber pulp, oblate to spherical, early Southeast trials promising, seems very hardy. Synonym: Kadota Hybrid. Different paper. Different year.

**LSU Champagne** and **LSU Gold** are official yellows. Gold is large and late. Champagne is the other Gulf honey people reach for. Different stickers. Different drafts.

**Yellow Long Neck** was the first looking-forward name in the same sentence. Holding page. A neck is not a number. Do not merge them because both were wishes in July.

**White Ischia** is Alabama’s small yellow Ischia — poor in the South. Different listing.

Do not merge seven yellow ideas because the cut looks amber. We have not earned a cut.

## Climate without a plate

A numbered Italian still has an eye, a clock, and a split risk. I will not guess tight or open. I will not guess Gold-late or Champagne-early. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is a count and the ostiole is a humidity judge. Those rules apply to a fig I have not met.

TAMU’s closed-eye set is Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma. Their open-eye trouble set is Magnolia and Kadota. I-258 is not on that table. Photograph the ostiole after the first storm. [VERIFY]. If it sours like Kadota, we have a yellow fig with a door. If it holds through a wet week, we have a number worth keeping.

UAEX’s souring sentence is water in the eye plus heat after rain. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit sours and many split in an unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year. Split is a balloon. Even water. A drought then a flood is how any yellow fig unzips. I will not write that this cultivar splits. I will write that we will watch.

Birds: yellow hides less than green and more than black. A ripe yellow fig still smells like lunch. Net the week it softens, if it softens.

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf. A late yellow that drops leaves in August will not finish a honey story. Rake. I will not advertise rust-proof on a plant we have not fruited.

Cold: Alabama’s 15–20°F talk names Celeste, English Brown Turkey, Alma, and Hardy Chicago. A numbered Italian is not on that list. Treat it as a pot we shuffle. Our [winter protection](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) page named Smith and Chicago, not I-258. Barely moist in the shop. Not a heated den.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. A new number is not Alma.

Breba: I will not guess. In Arkansas, UAEX already told you breba rarely overwinters. Main crop on current wood is the crop we can plan for.

## What we refuse to fake

Looking forward is a kind of honesty. Pretending you already ate it is not.

I will not sell unfruited I-258 as a flavor. I will not put Jack’s ranking on it. I will not write a homestead score. I will not use a grocery Kadota photo as 258. I will not call it Lattarula because the breath in a thread was the same breath.

If it tastes like Blanche, we say so and we do not erase the number until we are sure. If it tastes like nothing because it never finishes, we have another calendar lesson. If it is excellent and distinct, the next editor writes the cut and this holding paragraph comes out.

## How I would run it

One **#3**. Wood from a source that uses the number and has fruit photos, not “Italian honey rare.” **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Label like it might be wrong. Pot up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick. Write dates.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July. A yellow fig in a west fry on black plastic will cook the pot and then split when you panic-water it — if it fruits.

In-ground in 8a only after we have eaten it two years running. East wall if we get that far. Clay that drains. Mulch over shallow roots. Do not plant a looking-forward number in last year’s tomato hole. Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how an unknown Italian writes a tender will.

Pick in the morning if anything softens. Write the first soften on the tag. [VERIFY]. Walk it after a storm. Photograph the number on the pot, the ostiole, and the cut on the same day. Future-us will need all three.

If it never finishes, graft a yellow we already eat — Champagne, Excel if that tree already proved a rain week, a grandpa Lemon if that is the honest hole — and keep the roots. We graft. We do not perform funerals for live sticks.
