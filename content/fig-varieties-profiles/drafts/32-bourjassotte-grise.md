Grise means gray. The fruit wears bloom — that dusty violet that looks like you should not touch it and then you do. Collectors talk about Bourjassotte Grise the way they talk about a good Madeira year: rich, honey and berry in the same bite, a fig that makes a grocery clamshell feel like a prank.

Consensus. I did not name it on The Fig Jam. I will not stamp a homestead score. I will tell you it is worth one honest pot.

Common type in the accounts I use. No wasp. If your Grise drops a main crop like a Smyrna, [VERIFY] type.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) page: common figs persist; San Pedro figs need pollen for the main crop; Smyrna figs need pollen for both. Bourjassotte Grise is sold as common. You do not buy a wasp. You do not brush an ostiole. A plant that drops every main-crop fig at marble size is thirsty, young, or the wrong type. Look at water first. Then look at the tag.

Desert King is the San Pedro people plant for breba. Calimyrna is the Smyrna. Do not give a bloomed gray fig a wasp yard because the listing said Provençal. If it sets and ripens a main crop without pollen, you have a common fig or you have a year. Eat it. Keep notes.

Condit’s 1955 monograph has Bourjassotte / Barnissotte piles — Nero, Brogiotto, old Provençal family words. Alabama bolded **Nero** and listed Barnisotte and Brogiotto Nero as a large purplish black with an open medium eye. That is not automatically this pot. Grise is the gray-bloomed sister collectors mean when they say Bourjassotte Grise. Noire and Blanche arguments exist inside that family. I will not flatten them to tidy a paragraph. Photograph the bloom. Photograph the cut.

## What to look at

Bloom. Neck or rounded body depending on the crop. Pulp that should not be pale if the reputation is real. A clock that is not Ronde-de-Bordeaux-early. [VERIFY] dates. I will not invent a Saline County soften.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is the first filter. A rich French fig that finishes in October in Provence can finish in a long 8a year and can also sit hard through the first cool night. Head start in the hoop. East wall. Do not feed for leaves in September. None of that turns Grise into an early bowl fig. It might finish a mid-to-late one.

Eye: not the reason people plant it. Humidity will still vote. UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. ANR-1145: the riper they are, the more they split and sour. Pick on the soft. Bloom rubs off in a bowl. That is cosmetic. Sour is not. Photograph the ostiole after a storm. [VERIFY] ours. Tight-eye talk on this name is forum talk. I will not stamp tight.

Birds: dusty-dark. They can see it. Green figs hide. This one does not. Net or pick.

Split: even water. Drought then flood. A bloomed fig you waited for is the one you leave one more day. That is how you lose it. Morning pick. A day-early fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig.

Breba: UAEX — rarely overwinters in Arkansas. If you get a spring fig, last year’s wood lived. Do not hat-rack a collector fig in January and then write that Grise never brebas. Main crop on current wood is the crop you can plan.

Name pile: Bourjassotte is an old Provençal family. Noire, Grise, Blanche arguments exist. Violette de Bordeaux is a different small black — Negronne, deep red pulp, Alabama’s Bordeaux line. Do not merge gray bloom into VdB because both sound French. Col de Dame Grise is a different lady’s-neck argument. Do not merge gray words. Black Madeira is the late intensity I named on The Fig Jam. Grise is not Madeira. Noire de Caromb is a working dark. Different job.

Flanders has violet stripes on yellow-green. Panache has candy-cane stripes. LSU Tiger stripes when it is green. None of those are bloom. Bloom is dust you can thumb off. Stripe is pigment in the skin.

## Flavor I will not fake

Rich honey-berry is the consensus sentence. I did not plate Bourjassotte Grise on 25 July 2025. Jack did not either. Looking-forward names that day were other fruit — Yellow Long Neck, White Madeira #1, CDD Mutante, I-258. Grise was not on that list and it was not on the favorite pile.

I will eat a finished bloomed fig in a dry week and write what it was. Until the plate repeats I will not stamp a homestead score. Dry-week rich is the hope. Wet-week ordinary is the risk. Both are weather. [VERIFY] on our tree.

Alabama did not give Grise its own ANR-1145 line the way they gave Flanders, Conadria, and Blanche. That is a limit. Collector consensus is not extension. Mark it.

## How I would run it

Collector pot. Wood from a fruiting bloomed fig, not a gray filter on a phone. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Pot up before June. East wall. Even water. One **#3** until it proves the plate. Fertigate once it is a plant.

**6–8 hours** of sun. Afternoon cloth in July. South shade plus water is already on Pick The Right Fig. Nematodes: pots. A #3 still freezes at the rim. Shuffle it. ANR-1145’s 15–20°F conversation is the old favorites, not this name.

Do not row it. Do not tell a neighbor this is the fig that will make them love figs. Tell them Negra, Jack Lily, a tool tree, then Grise if they still have a pot and they will wait on a clock.

If it finishes and the plate is rich, keep it and take wood. If it finishes and the plate is only pretty, keep one photo plant. If it does not finish, it is another late French fig teaching GDD. Graft. Keep the hole.

Sister slugs keep walking in. LSU Purple is an official dark Gulf fig with purple stems. Scott’s Black is late and official. Neither wears this bloom. A dusty thumbprint on a gray-violet skin is the field mark. If your fruit is shiny black with no dust, you have a different dark. If your fruit is small, wine-red inside, and almost black, you may have VdB. Relabel.

Rust and a late canopy still matter. A bloomed fig that loses its leaves in September will not finish the last hanging fruit. Rake. Even water. The how-to pack already unpacked rust.

Do not mail cuttings as Bourjassotte Grise until the bloomed gray-violet fruit repeats and the cut is worth the name. Madeira money and Grise money attract the same wrong wood. Trusted sellers. 1–3 years. The introduction page already said it.

Write Bourjassotte Grise on the pot when the bloomed gray-violet fruit repeats and the cut is worth the name. Write unknown-gray-bloom if you only have the dust. Dust is a clue. It is not a cultivar.
