LSU Gold is the fig people point at when they want to prove Louisiana bred something that looks like a prize. Jason Stagg’s photo caption in the 2025 *Louisiana Agriculture* piece is blunt: Gold is the largest fruit of the LSU fig varieties. The tree is vigorous and upright. The skin goes yellow to gold. The release notes say average cold resistance, moderate rust resistance, recommended for the Gulf Coast.

That last clause is doing work. Gulf Coast is not a short Ozark autumn. We are 8a. We can ripen Gold. We can also watch a large yellow fig sit hard while the nights drop. GDD is a count, not a vibe. I will not invent a Saline County finish date. [VERIFY] your first soften and write it on the tag.

## Official, not a rumor

Older sheets — Alabama’s 2023 review among them — still carry the rumor that Gold was never officially released. Defer to the people who hold the germplasm. Polozola and Stagg, 2025: Gold is one of the six. The six are [O’Rourke](lsu-orourke), [Champagne](lsu-champagne), [Tiger](lsu-tiger), LSU Gold, [LSU Purple](lsu-purple), [Scott’s Black](lsu-scotts-black). Purple 1991, Gold 1995, the 2007 trio of Tiger / Champagne / O’Rourke on the older news clips. Scott’s Black completed the set people actually mean when they say “the releases.”

[Hollier](lsu-hollier) and [Jack Lily](lsu-jack-lily) are not in that sentence. Unofficial wood left the stations. The 2025 history also says many varieties circulate as LSU releases with no connection to the breeding. Marketplace “LSU Gold” is still a claim. Wait for fruit. A large yellow fig that finishes early and tastes like Celeste might be something else. A large yellow fig that never finishes might be Gold in a short year, or it might be a late nobody. Year two. The [introduction](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already gave you 1–3 years to prove type.

## Do not confuse the yellow LSU pile

It is not [Champagne](lsu-champagne). Champagne is medium, yellow, tested as Golden Celeste, better cold talk, Celeste-class calendar. Gold is the large one, average cold, later clock. If you merge them because both are yellow, you will prune wrong, pick wrong, and blame Louisiana.

It is not [Kadota](kadota). Kadota is Dottato, the canned fig, open-enough eye for TAMU to file it with Magnolia. Gold is a Gulf release bred for sticky air. Alabama’s older Gold note still said a small eye that leaks honeydew. A leak is sugar at the door. [VERIFY] on your fruit. I will not advertise a sealed vault.

It is not [Calimyrna](calimyrna). Calimyrna is a Smyrna. Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) page already told you that class drops without pollen. Gold is a **common** fig. Persistent. No wasp.

It is not [Excel](excel), Condit’s 1975 Kadota hybrid. It is not [Peter’s Honey](peters-honey). It is not a “Golden Celeste” listing that forgot Champagne’s release name. If the fruit is huge, yellow-gold, and late, you may have Gold. If it is medium and earlier, look at Champagne. If it is small, brown, and sugar, you have [Celeste](celeste) with a hopeful label.

## What the sheets actually wrote

Polozola and Stagg, spring 2025: large yellow-to-gold fruit on a vigorous, upright tree. Average resistance to cold. Moderate resistance to fig leaf rust. Recommended for the Gulf Coast. Stagg’s photo line: largest fruit of the LSU fig varieties. The 2021 AgCenter jam-history clip said the same size headline another way — Gold couldn’t be more different from Purple.

Alabama ANR-1145: large yellow fig blushed with red, strawberry pulp, outstanding flavor, small eye that leaks honeydew, “deserves wider trials,” plus the stale “never officially released” rumor. Florida HS27: large yellow fig with pink-to-red pulp, open eye, pick as soon as mature because spoilage may occur, good fresh and for preserving; their July–August window is Florida. The sheets do not agree on pulp color. LSU’s 2025 paragraph does not name pulp. [VERIFY] the cut on your tree. I will not pick a color to win a sentence.

UAEX still wants common figs for Arkansas. Gold qualifies on type. It does not get a Celeste-first recommendation. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) is where GDD and a short year already got unpacked. This page is the size story that needs those days.

## The 8a week

Common type. No wasp.

Pick: a Gold that is large and still firm is not ripe. A Gold that is large, colored, and soft is a meal. Morning. Neck wilt. Give. Size is why people overpick late. Birds can see yellow. So can you.

Rain: large fruit, late storms. Tight-enough eye helps souring. It does not stop a balloon. Water, then sun. Physics does not care about the LSU logo. Pick before the storm if the fruit is already soft. A pretty gold color after a wet night is how you get split skins and a sour week.

Birds: yellow is less of a billboard than Purple or Scott’s Black. Less is not none. Net or pick.

Rust: moderate resistance is not “no rust.” LSU’s own rust story is spores on leaf litter, yellow then reddish spots, blisters under the leaf. Gold is better than a tender imported leaf in a lot of Gulf talk. It is not a force field. Rake. Do not read one clean July as a cultivar trait.

Cold: average. Not Celeste, not [Chicago Hardy](chicago-hardy). In a pot you shuffle it. In the ground you accept dieback years. The tree is upright and vigorous when it is happy — the opposite of [Tiger](lsu-tiger)’s juvenile horizontal sprawl. Give it room or keep it in a **#5** you prune on purpose.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July so the leaves do not cook while the fruit tries to finish.

Pots versus ground: we run hundreds of pots and about 25–30 in-ground. I said that on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). Gold in a **#3** is honest if you pot up before June and fertigate once it is a plant. A late large fig in a small pot will stall. Do not push nitrogen into September if you still want the wood in March. In-ground is a Gulf recommendation living in 8a. Nematodes: I have not seen LSU hang a nematode claim on Gold the way some writeups hang it on Purple. Pots if the ground is dirty.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, from a fruiting Gulf tree if you can. It roots like any common fig.

I will not invent a Saline County finish date. If the year is short, pick what softened and let the hard ones go to the chickens. Do not wait for a California gold color in October fog.

## Culinary, without a prize ribbon

Fresh: that is the headline if the calendar closes. A large ripe yellow fig is a bowl by itself. Honey-leaning in the release language — “good-to-outstanding” on a big fruit. Lazy Dog and a hundred Gulf growers will tell you it is a honey fig worth the wait. I will not put a homestead score on this page that I did not say on The Fig Jam. Gold was not on that short list. That does not make it a bad fig. It makes it a Gulf tool until the plate is on the record.

Jam: if you get a pile, kettle it. Pale-gold fruit makes a pretty jar. Volume is the question in 8a, not a recipe. A short year gives you a tasting, not a pantry.

Kid bowl: kids like a big yellow fig they can see. Give them a soft one. A hard Gold is a dare, not a snack. If you need a bowl before school starts, plant [Purple](lsu-purple) or a Celeste-class tree for the early fruit and let Gold be the late bowl.

## How I would run it

If you have a long, hot season and you want a yellow fig that can fill a bowl, Gold is the official name. Plant it as a main-crop tree, not as a breba fantasy.

If it never finishes two years running, believe your GDD. Graft a tighter, earlier yellow — Champagne, a true Celeste if you like sugar, Excel if that is the tree you have — and keep the vigor in the hole. Gold is a size story. Size needs days.

Write LSU Gold on the pot when the fruit is large, yellow-gold, late, and repeats. If the tag said Gold and the fruit is small and early, you have a different yellow fig. Keep the better one. Change the label.
