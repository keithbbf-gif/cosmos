I named Nuestra Señora del Carmen first in my pile. Jack put it fifth: very sweet, nice berry, large eye, splits if there is much rain, large fig.

That is a complete cultivar profile. The rest is how to live with it in 8a.

Common type. No wasp. A Spanish name that collectors shorten to NSDC because life is short and the fruit is not.

## The name pile

Nuestra Señora del Carmen. NSDC. Carmen. A lady’s name on a large fig. If a plant labeled NSDC is small-eyed and small-fruited, [VERIFY]. You may have a better rainy-week fig with a stolen name. Eat it. Relabel.

Do not merge it with Negra d’Agde. Negra is moderate, tight-eyed, berry over sugar — Jack’s sixth, sweetness 3.5 on the [Fig Reviews](https://figroots.com/fig_reviews_archive/) card. Do not merge it with Red Sicilian. Red Sicilian is medium, sweet plus berry, productive, Jack’s first. Do not merge it with Violette de Bordeaux or Ronde de Bordeaux because someone said “dark berry.” Do not merge it with Malta Black. That folder is a clone caption, not a promotion to a Fig Jam favorite.

Alabama [ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) does not give NSDC a paragraph. I will not invent Auburn language. The [Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) sentences are the on-record profile. I will not add a homestead score on top. Jack did not put a 1–5 card on this one the way the site did for Red Sicilian, Negra, and Jack Lily. I will not invent one so the page matches.

The [introduction page](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers and 1–3 years to prove type. A marketplace “NSDC” without an eye photo is a size word.

[Jack’s type page](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) already said common figs set without a wasp. NSDC is that type. Do not buy pollen. Do not buy a San Pedro brochure and hope a large Spanish fig will hang a second crop. Desert King is a different machine.

## Two weeks

Dry week: a large, very sweet berry fig. This is why it is on both lists. This is why I said I was looking forward to *more* NSDC in the same interview. We wanted more of the good week.

Wet week: the large eye is a door. Jack said splits. UAEX said souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. A large fig is a balloon. You can have split and sour in the same bowl. Alabama said the riper the fig, the more it splits and sours. Morning pick is not optional on this plant. It is the crop.

People then call NSDC overrated. They ate the wet week. Pick before the storm if the fruit is already giving. A day-early large fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A split fig on the ground is a beetle farm.

I will not tell you to plant a row of these as your only berry. I will tell you to plant one, pick like it is a job, and keep a tight-eye fig for the weeks NSDC fails. Negra. Jack Lily. Syrian Dark #2. Ronde de Bordeaux if you wanted the early clock. Alma if you wanted a closed-eye brown tool TAMU put on the resistant side of the souring table.

I will not invent a ripening date for Saline County. The interview did not hang a calendar on this fig. [VERIFY] your first soften and write it on the tag.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. NSDC is the other end of that sentence — a large sweet berry I named first. The tool figs still have a job. This one has a weather job.

## Size is a trap

Large sells cuttings. Large also means fewer fruit per bowl, more split, more bird damage per unit, more disappointment when a short year does not finish the last ones. Pair with Syrian Dark #2 or Negra or Jack Lily. Small figs pay rent in bad weather.

Birds: large and dark-enough. They will take a bite out of each one as if they were sampling. Net. UAEX said birds, squirrels, and raccoons like figs as much as people do, and daily picking may be needed. A large fig with one bird hole is a wasted week. Net before the first one colors, not after the third one is sampled.

Black Madeira is in my pile too — late, famous, a long-year hang. NSDC is not Madeira. Do not wait on a dry hang that our humidity will not give you. Pick when it gives.

Verte already splits on us. I said that on the same interview. A split fig is not automatically NSDC. Verte is a green fig with its own draft. Photograph the eye. Photograph the split. The stills tell different stories.

## How I run it in 8a

Sun: **6–8 hours**. UAEX’s minimum. Afternoon cloth in July. East wall if you have one — less west fry, a little less balloon stress. The [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) page already unpacked South shade plus water and tight eye as a humidity judge. NSDC fails the tight-eye hire. That is not a surprise. Jack already told you.

Pots: we already run it. **#3** / **#5**, potted up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. In a pot you can move it out of a west fry. Shuffle it. [Our winter page](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) already said barely moist, not a heated den. Container roots still freeze at the rim around the 15°F talk. Chicago Hardy and Smith are the colder names on that page. This lady still has to come inside.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off the tree we like. Lignified if you can. Do not mail it to a beginner as a “set and forget.” Mail it with Jack’s sentence attached. Extra pots if we share. Do not mail a small-eyed look-alike as NSDC.

In-ground you accept storm weeks. East wall. Even water — a drought then a flood is how large figs unzip. TAMU’s five-or-six-trunk chapter if a freeze takes the top. Leave crop wood if the top lived. Dead wood. Crossing wood. Do not hat-rack a large berry fig in January unless the plant proved it is a Turkey. UAEX’s Celeste prune warning stays in the pocket.

Water: even. This is the cultivar that punishes a neglected hose. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Walk it before the forecast, not after.

Rain: the profile. Photograph the split after rain if you want an honest still. PHOTO_NOTES already asked for that shot. Both halves are true — the dry-week plate and the storm week.

Late nitrogen: stop so September wood can lignify. Green shoots die first. A large fig on tender wood is a winter bill.

Nematodes: dirty ground stays in a pot. A Fig Jam favorite is not a nematode solution.

## How I would run it

We already run it. Keep it. Wood off the tree we like, **6–8 inches**, three nodes.

Plant one. Pick like it is a job. Keep a tight-eye fig for the weeks this one fails. If you wanted only tight eyes, plant Negra and Jack Lily and leave the lady in somebody else’s yard. If you wanted the dry-week plate I named first, plant her and watch the sky.

If a plant labeled NSDC is small-eyed and small-fruited, [VERIFY]. You may have a better rainy-week fig with a stolen name. Eat it. Relabel.

Write Nuestra Señora del Carmen on the pot that splits in rain and sings in a dry week. If it never splits and never gets large, it is a different lady. Let her have her own name.
