The Northwest grows a yellow-green fig they call Lattarula and swears it is the honey fig. Nurseries sell Peter’s Honey. People say Italian Honey. Condit did not treat Lattarula as its own variety and pointed at **Blanche**. Alabama reserved judgment. White Marseilles / Lemon / Mayes Yellow sit in the same yellow pile on the other page.

I will not solve 1955 in a blog draft. I will tell you how to live with the tree.

## The name pile

[ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) is the cleanest Southern paragraph we have. Lattarula: medium to large yellowish green, widely grown in the Northwest, said to be very sweet. Condit does not identify it as a distinct variety and considers it a synonym for Blanche. Alabama Extension horticulturists reserve judgment pending further study. Synonym on that sheet: Italian Honey Fig.

Blanche on the same sheet: usually called Lemon in the South. Medium to large greenish yellow, white pulp, many seeds, turbinate without a neck, sweet and delicate, nutty texture from the soft seeds. Some trees open-eyed, some relatively closed. Well adapted in the South. Fairly hardy. Synonyms: Bianci, Lemon, Marseilles, Mayes Yellow, White Marseilles, White Russian.

That is two catalog entries and one argument. Peter’s Honey is the nursery sticker that walks into the argument without asking Condit. Kadota / Dottato is a different yellow — Alabama’s canned-fig, open-eye, Southwest sentence. LSU Gold is a different yellow — official release, late clock, Gulf breeding. I-258 is a different Italian honey people chase. Keith had not plated I-258 as of July 2025. Its own draft. Do not merge.

A seller lists Peter’s Honey. A forum says that *is* Lattarula. A book says Lattarula *is* Blanche. A neighbor says Blanche *is* Lemon. You now have one pot and four histories.

Do this. Eat it two years. Photograph the eye, the cut, the shape. If it matches a known Blanche / White Marseilles on the place, call it that. If it is the Northwest honey fig you paid for and it stays true, call it Peter’s Honey / Lattarula and leave the Condit fight in the notebook. If it is Kadota, the eye and the rain will say so.

The [introduction page](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers and 1–3 years to prove type. A first-year yellow cup can lie.

## What you are probably holding

A common fig. Persistent. No wasp. [Jack’s type page](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) already drew that line. Medium to large yellowish green. Sweet. Honey. Soft seeds that read as nutty when the fruit is finished. A backyard fig of the Northwest because it likes cooler summers than Kadota’s canning valley.

In the South it can be excellent in a dry week and ordinary in a wet one. Yellow-green ripe fruit hides from birds a little. The eye is the argument. Some Blanche-group trees are closed enough. Some are not. [VERIFY] yours after the first storm. Alabama said the Blanche pile splits that way on purpose. TAMU’s disease handbook puts closed-eye hope on Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma — not on this yellow stack. Magnolia and Kadota sit on the open-eye trouble side. If your “Peter’s Honey” sours like a Kadota, believe the eye, not the sticker.

Flavor: honey, not berry. If you wanted Negra, this will not convert you. If you wanted the grocery dried-fig memory without a wasp, this is closer than Calimyrna. Calimyrna is a Smyrna. It drops here. This plant should not.

I did not name Peter’s Honey on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). Jack did not either. It is a tool honey fig. We have yellow LSU fruit for the Gulf version of that craving. No homestead score. I will not invent one next to Champagne or Gold to fill the page.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. A honey yellow is a different mouth than those two and a different mouth than Jack’s berry list. That is all I will say until the plate repeats.

## How I run it in 8a

Sun: **6–8 hours**. UAEX’s minimum if you hope to harvest. Afternoon cloth in July. East wall if you have one. The [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) page already unpacked South shade plus water. A yellow fig in a west fry cooks and then people call honey figs bland.

Pots: one **#3** / **#5**, potted up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. Shuffle it. [Our winter page](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) already said barely moist, not a heated den. Fairly hardy on Alabama’s Blanche line is not a reason to leave a pot in a wind tunnel. Container roots still freeze at the rim around the 15°F talk.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree. Lignified if you can. Do not mail a June leaf as Lattarula. Do not mail it as White Marseilles unless the plate matched.

Prune: dead wood, crossing wood. Leave crop wood. These are not Turkey types unless your particular clone says otherwise. A January hat-rack on a Blanche-class tree can delete a year the way UAEX said a hard late-winter prune deletes Celeste. Wait until the plant proves it fruits on a hat-rack before you swing.

Water: even. Drought drops. Flood splits. Alabama said the riper the fig, the more it splits and sours. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Not a calendar date I will invent for Saline County. [VERIFY] your first soften and write it on the tag.

Rain: photograph the ostiole. If it is open and August is wet, you will sour. Pick before the storm if fruit is already giving. A day-early fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. Do not sun-dry in our humidity. That is a California sentence. Use a dehydrator if you want leather.

Birds: yellow-green hides a little. They still find a soft fig. Net or pick. UAEX said daily picking may be needed when a tree is in production. One neglected bowl teaches the address.

Late nitrogen: stop so September wood can lignify. Green shoots die first.

Nematodes: dirty ground stays in a pot. This yellow is not a nematode solution. Alma gets that rumor in some talk. Pots if the ground is dirty.

## How I would run it

One yellow honey fig in the collection is enough until you have a favorite. Wood from a fruiting tree. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Pot up before June.

In 8a: east wall, even water, pick on the soft. Do not sun-dry. Do not hat-rack if you wanted fruit.

If the eye sours three years, graft a tighter yellow. Gold is late. Champagne is the official LSU yellow people actually eat. White Marseilles is the other draft in this pile. If the plate is honey and you like it better than Gold’s late clock, keep it and stop hunting synonyms.

If you wanted berry, plant Negra d’Agde or Red Sicilian. If you wanted a closed-eye brown tool, plant Alma or keep the grandpa Celeste. If you wanted a San Pedro brochure, that is Desert King — different type, different disappointment.

Write Peter’s Honey (Lattarula group) on the pot when a yellow-green honey fig repeats. Write Blanche only if you are sure you mean Condit’s plant. Write unknown-honey-yellow if you are an honest person having a good week.
