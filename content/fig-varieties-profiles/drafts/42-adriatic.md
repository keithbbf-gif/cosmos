Condit put Adriatic in the California commercial five. [Alabama ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/): medium, green to greenish yellow, light strawberry pulp, good flavor, turbinate, small neck or none, very subject to mosaic, well adapted in the Northwest, disappointing in the South, fairly hardy, good for drying. Synonyms: Chico, Grosse Verte, Nebian, Strawberry, Verdone.

Disappointing in the South is a climate sentence. I still named **Adriatic JH** on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) in the favorite pile with NSDC, Negra, Maryland Berry, and Black Madeira. JH is a collector suffix — a particular cut of the Adriatic idea that we liked enough to say out loud. It is not a license to call every green strawberry fig Adriatic.

Common type. Persistent. No wasp. Jack already defined that on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). Green strawberry is not a Smyrna. Calimyrna is the wasp fig. This one stays on the tree without a pollen brush.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. Adriatic JH is one of the reasons I can say that without being a snob about a grandpa tree. Different mouths. Different jobs.

## Green, strawberry, mosaic

The eating is the cut: pale skin, strawberry pulp, a fig that looks like it is not ripe until you have ruined two of them waiting. Feel. Soft. [An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said the flavor surprise is the point, and that it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. A first-year green cup can lie. A crate plant can lie for three years.

Mosaic mottles leaves. Alabama said very subject. Condit and the Florida sheet hang the same talk on Mission. Mottled is not a reason to discard a tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood. TAMU’s mosaic paragraph says the virus moves on cuttings and on an eriophyid mite. Start from a tree that fruits and does not look like it is dissolving.

Drying: California and the Northwest. Alabama said good for drying. NC State already told you we do not sun-dry in this humidity. A dehydrator is a machine. A windowsill is a mold farm. Do not buy Adriatic in 8a because a California crate dried it.

Eye: small in the sheet. [VERIFY] after rain. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said the ostiole is a humidity judge. TAMU’s [disease handbook](https://plantdiseasehandbook.tamu.edu/food-crops/fruit-crops/fig/) put Magnolia and Kadota in the open-eye souring column and Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma on the closed side. Adriatic is not on that table. Small is not Celeste-tight. Photograph the ostiole wet.

Green figs hide from birds a little. Sour does not hide. UAEX’s souring sentence is water in the eye plus heat after rain. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit sours and many split in an unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year. That is the “disappointing in the South” sentence with weather attached.

Split is a balloon. Even water. A drought then a flood is how green strawberry figs unzip the week they finally soften. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Do not wait for yellow if the fruit already gave. A day-early green fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A sour Adriatic on the ground is how a crate name earns Alabama’s adjective.

## JH versus the crate name

If your plant is a generic “White Adriatic” from a box store, believe Alabama until the plate argues. If your plant is Adriatic JH from a source we trust, believe the interview and still [VERIFY] every new stick.

[Fig Reviews](https://figroots.com/fig_reviews_archive/) already scored Jack Lily — Adriatic family on our card, small, tight, unofficial LSU. Different plant. Do not replace JH with Jack Lily because both say Adriatic in a notebook. Do not replace Jack Lily with a crate Adriatic because the word matched.

Verte is a green strawberry splitter I already named on The Fig Jam. Different profile. Different honesty. If your Adriatic splits like our Verte, write the split. Do not merge the names to tidy a bench.

Conadria is Condit’s Adriatic hybrid, **1957**, yellow-green, light strawberry, rebound from freezes. Different plant. Different winter sentence.

Grosse Verte and Verdone sit on Alabama’s synonym line. Those are crate-and-book words. They are not a license to call Dalmatie an Adriatic because both are green and large-ish. Dalmatie is the late baseball. This is the commercial medium.

I will not invent a Saline County soften date for JH or for the crate. Write the first soften on the tag. [VERIFY]. Midseason in a lot of California talk is not a Gulf calendar.

## Rust, wood, winter

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf, defoliation while fruit is trying to finish. Adriatic does not have an LSU rust sentence. Rake. Do not leave a wet skirt of last year’s leaves. Mosaic plus rust on the same tree is a ugly August. Ugly August is not a reason to dump a JH that still plates. It is a reason to rake.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. Adriatic is not Alma and it is not the LSU Purple rumor.

Cold: fairly hardy on Alabama’s sheet. Not Celeste. Not Chicago Hardy. Our [winter protection](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) page named Smith and Chicago, not Adriatic. Treat a crate Adriatic as a pot you shuffle until it proves a winter. Treat JH as a keeper you still do not leave in a wind tunnel. Alabama’s 15–20°F talk is for dormant wood, not for a pot rim.

Do not hat-rack a green strawberry fig in January unless the plant proved it is a Turkey. Crop wood is the plan. If it dies to the crown, five or six trunks at two feet, TAMU’s chapter, and pair it with an early fig in the kill year.

Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how “fairly hardy” writes a tender will.

Birds: green helps. Split fruit is a bird bell. Net the week it softens if it is the only ripe thing, or pick.

## How I would run it

Keep the JH we named. Wood off that tree, **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Do not replace it with a cheaper “Adriatic” because the word matched. Pot up to a **#3** before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain and so you are not growing a leaf bush. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July. A green fig in deep shade stays a green bead.

If you only have the crate name, one pot. If it disappoints in the steam, you were warned. Graft a tighter green — Jack Lily — or a dark berry that already works. Keep the hole. We graft. We do not throw a live root system in a pile because Auburn said disappointing.

In-ground in 8a only after the plate repeats the reason I said JH out loud. East wall if you have one, clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots. Do not plant it in last year’s tomato hole.

Write Adriatic JH on the pot that matches the fig I was willing to name next to Negra. Write Adriatic / Verdone on a crate plant that matches the commercial green. Write unknown-green-strawberry if you are eating a good fig with a mottled leaf and no papers. Good is allowed without papers.
