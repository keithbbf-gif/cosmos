Panache is the fig you plant because you want to show someone a fig. Green skin with yellow stripes. Strawberry pulp. A neck. The leaf is not variegated. People who only saw the fruit expect a carnival tree and then look confused at a normal hand of five lobes.

It is a chimera — two tissues living in one skin. That is why the stripes exist and why a poorly taken cutting can revert to a plain green fig. Take wood that striped. If a shoot goes plain, do not mail that shoot as Panache.

Common type. No wasp.

## Not LSU Tiger

LSU Tiger stripes when the fruit is *green* and then ripens into a large ordinary fig. Panache stripes when you are bragging. A seller who writes “LSU Tiger Panache” is making a smoothie. I already wrote that on the Tiger page. I will write it here until the listings dry up.

[ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) filed the chimera under **Panachée**: green fruit with yellow stripes, strawberry pulp, pyriform with a prominent neck, mealy texture, leaf not variegated. Synonyms: Panache, Tiger, Variegato. That last Tiger is the problem. The official LSU release is a different plant — 2007 group with Champagne and O’Rourke, stripe on *immature* fruit, large ripe fig, juvenile wood that wants to grow sideways. Polozola and Stagg’s 2025 history keeps Tiger in the official six. Panache is not on that list.

Variegato, Tiger, Panachée. Same chimera if the fruit is the candy cane. Different plant if it is not. Flanders is another Condit hybrid with violet stripes on yellow-green — long neck, its own draft. Do not merge a striped Flanders into Panache because both photograph as “the striped one.”

## Flavor is the fight

Alabama: aficionados in California say it can produce excellent fresh fruit. In the South, flavor is mediocre. Mealy texture. That is a hard sentence and I will not sand it. Dry heat and a long hang make a different plate than our steam.

I did not put Panache on the [Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) list. Keith’s looking-forward names were other late berries and long-necks. This is a conversation-piece fig. If it eats above mediocre for us, I will say so when the plate repeats. Until then I will treat Alabama as the Southern default and California as a different climate. No homestead score. No invented “better than Gold” sentence.

Eye: not the reason you plant it. Pick on the soft. Stripes fade and muddy when the fruit is truly ripe — another reason people pick it pretty and eat it early. Underripe Panache is why “mediocre” gets worse.

Birds: a striped fig is a sign. Net or pick. They do not need a berry education. They need a color.

## How it fails

Reversion. One plain green shoot takes over a pot if you let it. Cut it. Wood off a reverted shoot is a plain green fig with a liar’s tag. The chimera is not a paint job. It is a tissue argument. Lose the striped layer and you have lost the cultivar.

People eat it underripe because the stripes are prettier then. Photograph the ripe muddy stripes if you want an honest still. The pretty one is the sales photo. The eating one is softer and less of a candy cane.

A short year leaves you with striped baseballs. Pretty. Pointless. Panache is not an early clock like Ronde de Bordeaux. It is not a resprout insurance fig like Chicago Hardy. If the year spends June growing wood, this plant may not finish a show fruit.

Humidity and a mealy texture are not a crime. They are a reason this is not your only fig. UAEX’s souring sentence still applies if the eye lets water in. [VERIFY] the ostiole on your clone after rain. Alabama did not brag a tight eye here. Morning pick. A day-early fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A split striped fig on the ground is a beetle farm with a costume.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. Panache is not the correction. Negra d’Agde and Red Sicilian are the correction. This is the plant you keep if you wanted to hand a kid a fig that looks like a story.

## How I run it in 8a

Sun: **6–8 hours**. UAEX’s minimum. Afternoon cloth in July. East wall if you have one. The [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) page already unpacked South shade plus water. A chimera in deep shade is a green leaf with no show.

Pots: one **#3**, potted up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. Shuffle it. [Our winter page](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) already said barely moist, not a heated den. Do not leave a conversation-piece pot in a wind tunnel because you liked the photo.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off striped wood only. Lignified if you can. If a shoot goes plain, cut it and do not share that stick as Panache. Alabama’s cutting chapter is shorter sticks. We like enough wood to see last year’s color and to see that the fruit on that wood striped.

Water: even. Drought then a flood splits fruit. Alabama said the riper the fig, the more it splits and sours. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Not a calendar date I will invent for Bradley County. [VERIFY] your first soften and write it on the tag.

Birds: net. Stripes are a billboard. Squirrels can read.

Prune: dead wood, crossing wood, reverted wood. Leave crop wood. Do not hat-rack a chimera in January unless you wanted a leaf bush and a year of no show. Common type. Persistent. No wasp. [Jack’s type page](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) already said that. Do not buy pollen for stripes.

Late nitrogen: stop so September wood can lignify. Green shoots die first.

Nematodes: dirty ground stays in a pot. Panache is not a nematode solution.

## How I would run it

One plant, in a pot, if you want the photo. Wood from a striped fruiting tree only. Pot up before June.

Do not plant a row. Do not tell a beginner this is the fig that will make them love figs. Tell them it is a chimera that looks like a story. The eating figs are Negra, Jack Lily, a tool Celeste, an LSU that finishes. Peter’s Honey is the yellow honey argument. Desert King is a San Pedro with a brochure. None of those are this plant.

If it reverts, relabel. If it eats well in a dry week, keep it and shut up about California. If it eats like wet bread, graft a berry fig on the hole and keep one striped pot for the kids. We graft. We do not throw a live hole away to prove we have taste.

The [introduction page](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers and a wait to prove type. A marketplace “Tiger Fig” photo is often this chimera and sometimes the LSU release. Ask for a green-fruit photo and a ripe-fruit photo. Keep both.

Write Panache on the pot when the ripe fruit stripes and the leaf stays green. Write unknown-striped if it stripes and the shape does not match. Write LSU Tiger on a different pot when the *green* fruit stripes and the ripe fruit is a large ordinary fig. Write nothing if you only have a marketplace photo.
