The grocery Mission is why a lot of people think they do not like figs. It rode a truck. It was picked for skin, not for a plate. Then a catalog sold you the romance: Franciscan missions, black fruit, two crops, the fig that built California.

Condit put Mission among the five commercial names California actually agreed on — Mission, Kadota, Adriatic, Calimyrna, and their Brown Turkey. He wanted the botanical name **Franciscana**. The yard says Black Mission. The plant is a **common** fig. Persistent. No wasp. That part is easy. The climate part is not.

## What it is when it is true

Large dark fig. Light strawberry to amber-red pulp. Brebas pyriform with thick necks; main-crop figs smaller and more variable. In California it is an everbearing shipping and drying fig. In a long, dry summer it will hang two crops and make you feel like you cheated.

Florida’s sheet is colder: not sufficiently hardy for the southeastern United States. Alabama: disappointing in the South since it is not very hardy. Often mosaic-mottled; the mottle does not seem to wreck the crop. Those are extension sentences, not forum vibes.

I will not invent a Saline County ripening date. I will tell you what the type asks for. Heat. A long season. Wood that does not love a 15°F night the way Celeste does. An eye that is not Celeste-tight. Dark skin that every bird in the county can read.

If your “Mission” is small, early, and sugar-brown, you bought a Celeste with a story. If it is copper and rebounds like a Turkey, you bought the pile. Wait for fruit. The tag is a claim.

## Two crops, one winter

Mission is famous for breba. Breba is last year’s wood. In Arkansas, UAEX already told you breba rarely overwinters. A late freeze after budbreak deletes the romance before the main crop has a vote. Pots you can roll into the shop. In-ground you take what the winter leaves.

Main crop on current wood is the crop you can plan for here. That is true of most common figs in 8a. Mission does not get a special exemption because a California orchard posted a June photo.

Desert King is the San Pedro that people confuse with “two-crop magic.” Mission is not San Pedro. It does not need a wasp for the main crop. It needs a winter that does not eat the wood you wanted for breba, and a summer long enough to finish dark fruit before the first hard night. Those are different machines.

## Humidity edits the plate

A dark fig in a wet August is a beetle advertisement and a bird feeder. Open-enough eye plus rain plus heat is the UAEX souring story. Mission was selected for California’s dry finish, not for our steam. I have watched people call it “poor quality in the South” when what they had was a sour week and a late pick.

Pick in the morning. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Do not wait for jet-black if the skin is already thin and the forecast is a storm. A day-early Mission in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A day-late Mission on the ground is vinegar and wings.

Split happens. Side cracks and ostiole-end splits shorten life — Kong’s California trial said so on Brown Turkey and Sierra, not on our Mission, but the physics is not a mystery. Water in, skin out. Tight-eye varieties forgive more weeks. Mission is not the variety I plant to win August.

Flavor when it is right: berry-leaning, jammy, the reason the name stayed famous. Flavor when it rode a truck: cardboard with a seed. Do not judge the cultivar by the clamshell. Do not judge an 8a tree by a Fresno orchard either.

My favorite dark figs on the record are other names — Negra d’Agde, Black Madeira, Red Sicilian, NSDC. I did not put Mission on that list. That is not a vendetta. That is a plate and a climate.

## Mosaic, wood, and the hole

Mottled leaves are common. Florida says they do not seem to affect the crop. I still would not start a collection from the sickest-looking Mission on a parking lot. Take wood from a tree that fruits and does not look like it is dissolving.

Hardiness: treat it as tenderer than Celeste and less forgiving than a Southern Turkey type after a kill. In a pot it is honest. You can shuffle it. In the ground in 8a it is a trial, not a grandpa recommendation. If it dies to the crown and comes back, keep five or six trunks and wait. If it sulks for two summers and the fruit sours, graft the hole. We graft. We do not perform funerals for live roots.

Birds: net or pick. A green fig lasts longer. Mission is not green when you want it.

Nematodes: pots. Mission is not a nematode-resistant fairy tale. LSU Purple gets that rumor from the release notes. Different plant.

## How I would run it

If you have California heat and dry Augusts, grow it. You are having the argument the name was written for.

If you have South Arkansas, put it in a **#3** you can afford to lose, pot up before June, east wall or afternoon cloth, water like a shallow-rooted pot, and wait two fruiting years before you buy five more. Common type. No wasp. No pollen brush.

Do not buy a “Black Mission” off a marketplace because the photos are black. Black is easy to photograph. True-to-type is a plate in year two.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July. A dark fig in a west fry on black plastic is a bird feeder and a souring lab.

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf, defoliation while late dark fruit is still trying to finish. Mission does not have an LSU rust sentence. Rake. Do not leave a wet skirt of last year’s leaves. I will not advertise rust-proof.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree, not a parking-lot tag. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how a California name writes a tender will in 8a.

If it fruits and you like it, good. Write Mission / Franciscana on the pot when the fruit repeats: dark, pyriform, berry-leaning, two-crop only if the wood actually overwintered. If it fruits and you do not like it, keep the hole for a Negra or a Madeira or whatever Jack is ranking that year. The Mission taught you the dark-fig lesson: humidity is the editor. California wrote the first draft.
