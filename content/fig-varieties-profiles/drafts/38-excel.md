Condit released Excel in **1975**. [Alabama ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/): medium yellow, amber pulp, oblate to spherical, well adapted in California, early Southeast trials very promising, seems very hardy, superb flavor. Synonym: Kadota Hybrid.

Superb is their word. Kadota hybrid is the warning. Kadota’s job in our climate is an open-enough eye and a wet week. Excel exists because someone wanted Kadota’s honey on a plant that might live wider. Whether the eye came along is a plate-and-rain question. [VERIFY].

Common type. Persistent. No wasp. Jack already drew that line on [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/). Yellow is not a Smyrna. Calimyrna is the wasp fig. This one stays on the tree without a pollen brush. Do not buy a wasp for it.

I did not plate Excel on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith). I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. A dry-week yellow fig that finishes earlier than Gold would earn a pot here. A sour-week Kadota remix would earn a graft.

## Hybrid is not a vault

I like a Condit hybrid that was aimed at more than Fresno. Conadria — Adriatic hybrid, **1957** on Alabama’s sheet — got the rebound sentence: hardy with good rebound from freezes, well adapted in California and the Southeast. Excel got the hardy-and-promising sentence. Flanders — also 1975, Verdone hybrid, long slender neck, violet stripes — got the pretty-and-tender sentence: vigorous but not particularly hardy, good on the West Coast. Tena, same year, likes hot dry weather. Read the right page. Do not hang Conadria’s rebound on Excel because both say Condit.

Flavor: honey, amber, “superb” on a West Coast sheet and on Alabama’s trial note. I will treat superb as their word and our plate as unwritten until it repeats. The introduction page already said it takes **1–3 years** to prove type. A first-year yellow cup can lie about honey. A first-year storm can lie about the eye.

Season: treat as earlier than Gold until dates say otherwise. Earlier yellow is useful. LSU Gold is the official large late yellow — average cold, moderate rust talk, Gulf Coast recommendation. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said GDD is a count. A yellow fig that finishes while Gold is still hard is a clock tool. [VERIFY] the first soften and write it on the tag. I will not invent a Saline County date.

Champagne is the other official LSU yellow people reach for when they want honey without Gold’s calendar. Different release. Different profile. Do not “upgrade” an Excel by calling it Champagne because both are yellow.

## The parent is allowed to win

TAMU’s [disease handbook](https://plantdiseasehandbook.tamu.edu/food-crops/fruit-crops/fig/) puts Kadota — they spelled it Kodata — with Magnolia in the open-eye souring column. Celeste, Texas Everbearing, and Alma sit on the closed side. The prose is blunt: figs with open eyes should not be planted. Varieties with closed eyes only.

Excel is not on that table. Hybrid does not mean sealed. Plant it next to Kadota if you have both. Same storm. Two ostioles. That is the trial. If Excel sours like Kadota, the parent name won. Write Kadota in the notes and decide whether the hole is a preserve pot or a graft.

UAEX’s souring sentence is water in the eye plus heat after rain. Alabama’s trouble table says fruit sours and many split in an unsuitable variety or an unusually wet year. The remedy they offer is a more suitable variety or pick immature fruit for preserves. Kadota was hired for a kettle. Excel was hired for a wider map. If I pick Excel early I am admitting the week beat the eye.

Split is a balloon. Even water. A drought then a flood is how yellow figs unzip. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Do not wait for a grocery lemon color if the forecast is a storm. A day-early yellow fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A sour Kadota remix on the ground is vinegar and wings.

Birds: yellow. Less than black. Not none. A ripe Excel still smells like lunch.

Rust is the Gulf leaf tax. Yellow-green flecks, then yellowish-brown, blisters under the leaf. Gold gets “moderate resistance” in LSU’s 2025 history. Excel gets whatever the week gives. Rake. Do not advertise rust-proof.

Mosaic mottles leaves on a lot of old yellow and green figs. Alabama hung “very subject” on Adriatic. Mottled is not a reason to discard a tree that fruits. Dwarfed fruit is a reason to take cleaner wood.

Nematodes: pots if the ground is dirty. Excel is not the LSU Purple rumor and it is not Alma.

## Sister slugs

**Kadota / Dottato** is the canned parent. Greenish white to lemon, open-enough eye, honey. Florida said quality declines in extremely wet weather. If your Excel is that plate, the hybrid did not arrive.

**Peter’s Honey / Lattarula / Blanche** is the Northwest honey argument. Alabama reserved judgment on Lattarula. Different pile. Taste both. Do not merge them to tidy a tag list.

**White Marseilles / Lemon** is the old Southern yellow yard fig. Some eyes open, some closed. Different history.

**I-258** is a numbered Italian I had not plated as of 25 July 2025. Holding page. Do not finish that pile here.

**LSU Gold** is large and late. **Champagne** is the other official yellow. **Conadria** is yellow-green strawberry pulp with rebound. **Flanders** is striped and tender. Four Condit or LSU yellows. Four jobs.

## How I would run it

One pot. Wood from a fruiting Excel, not a “Kadota improved” marketplace smoothie. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Alabama likes 3–6 inch pencil wood. We run a little longer. Pot up to a **#3** before June. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick.

Sun: **6–8 hours**. Alabama wants a full eight so fruit dries after rain. We give morning sun and afternoon cloth in July. A yellow fig in a west fry on black plastic will cook the pot and then split when you panic-water it.

In-ground in 8a only after the rain week is kinder than Kadota two years running. East wall if you have one, clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots. Do not plant it in last year’s tomato hole. Do not fertilize late. Succulent September wood is how “seems very hardy” writes a tender will. Alabama’s 15–20°F talk is for dormant wood, not for a pot rim.

Do not hat-rack a Condit hybrid in January because it “seems hardy.” Hardy is winter. Crop wood is the plan. If it dies to the crown, five or six trunks at two feet, TAMU’s chapter, and pair it with an early fig in the kill year.

If the eye sours three years running, believe it. Graft a tighter yellow or a tight-eye eater and keep the vigor in the hole. We graft. We do not throw a live root system in a pile to prove we have taste.

Write Excel on the pot when a yellow amber fig repeats and the rain week is kinder than Kadota. Write Kadota if it is not. The parent name is allowed to win.
