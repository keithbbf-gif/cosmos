Caromb is a town. Noire is dark. The fig is a productive dark common fig people grow when they want a Bordeaux-class bowl without Madeira’s drama. Consensus: berry, productive, not the latest of the late, not the tightest of the tight. [VERIFY] every word on our tree.

I did not name it on The Fig Jam. Jack’s productive dark is Red Sicilian. If Caromb is in that job class, it has to prove it next to that card, not on a forum average. One season is a rumor. Two seasons is a note.

Common type. No wasp.

Jack’s [Types of Figs](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) primer: common figs persist without pollen. Noire de Caromb sits in that bucket in the accounts I use. You do not buy a wasp. You do not brush an ostiole. San Pedro is King — main crop often wants pollen. Smyrna is Calimyrna — both crops want pollen. If your Caromb drops a main crop at marble size every time, look at water and age first. Then type. [VERIFY]. A productive common fig that never sets is usually a young pot or a hose, not a missing *Blastophaga*.

Condit’s 1955 monograph will not settle a marketplace tag that says Caromb. The town is real. The plant is a claim. Alabama did not give Noire de Caromb its own ANR-1145 line the way they gave Bordeaux, Mission, and Brown Turkey. Collector consensus is the source. Mark it. Extension is not a fig-fanatics thread.

## Useful versus famous

Famous is Black Madeira. Useful is a dark fig you pick for two weeks without writing a speech. Red Sicilian already has the job in this house — Jack ranked it first on 25 July 2025. Caromb is a candidate, not a replacement, until the plates sit side by side.

I named Negra d’Agde, Red Sicilian, NSDC, Black Madeira, Maryland Berry. I did not name Caromb. Looking-forward that day included CDD Mutante, Yellow Long Neck, I-258 — not this town. Consensus among growers is berry and volume. Consensus is not a homestead score. [VERIFY].

Eye after rain is the interview. Productive plus open is a souring row. Productive plus decent is a keeper. UAEX: souring when water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. ANR-1145: dried-fruit beetles enter through the eye; open eyes are particularly susceptible; fruit sours and many split in a wet year. Photograph the ostiole. I will not stamp tight. I will not stamp open. The still decides.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said humid climates want tight eyes and smaller fruit. Volume figs are often the opposite — more fruit, more doors, more bird damage per week. If Caromb is the productive dark in the stories, you walk the tree. A loaded week you skip is a sour week.

Birds: dark, lots. Net. They will sample each one as if they were shopping. A green fig lasts longer. This is not a green fig.

Split: volume figs get left on the tree. Do not. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. A day-early dark fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A day-late dark fig on the ground is vinegar and wings.

Even water. Drought then flood throws crop and unzips what stays. The how-to pack already unpacked drop and split. This page will not restage it.

Season: not the latest of the late, in the talk. That still is not Ronde-de-Bordeaux-early. [VERIFY] dates. I will not invent a Saline County soften. Write the first ripe morning. Write the last hard one. GDD still matters. A “not the latest” French dark can still lose a cool September.

Breba: UAEX — rarely overwinters in Arkansas. If you get a spring fig, the wood lived. Do not hat-rack a productive dark in January for sport. Main crop on current wood is the crop you can plan. Leave crop wood. Dead wood only.

Hardiness: not a Chicago Hardy brand. ANR-1145’s 15–20°F list is the old favorites. A Provençal dark in 8a is a pot you shuffle until you trust it. In-ground after it proves volume and a plate you will actually eat.

## How I would run it

One pot. Wood from a fruiting productive dark, source written down. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Pot up before June. One **#3** until volume repeats. Fertigate once it is a plant.

**6–8 hours** of sun. East wall. Afternoon cloth in July. South shade plus water is already on Pick The Right Fig. Nematodes: pots.

If it produces like Jack’s number one and eats like a berry, keep it. If it produces like a Turkey and eats like sugar, relabel. If it is late and sparse, it is not the Caromb in the stories.

Do not plant it as the only fig until the eye passes a storm week. Plant a tight-eye eater next to it. The volume fig is the bowl in a dry stretch. The tight fig is dinner after rain.

If the first year is sparse, wait. A young common fig will drop cups and lie. The introduction page already said 1–3 years to prove type. Volume is a second-year sentence, not a June listing photo.

A productive dark also means a picking job. Mississippi State gave Southern Brown Turkey about a 60-day pick. That is a harvest job on a tool tree. If Caromb is the volume dark in the stories, you walk it the same way. If you will not walk the tree, you will wear it — sour, split, birds, and a forum post about “the year it all went at once.”

Rust still yellows a loaded canopy. ANR-1145: heavy defoliation cuts fruit size and quality. Rake. Do not leave a souring pile under a productive tree. Dried-fruit beetles breed in that pile. The how-to pack already said sanitation. This page will not restage it.

Do not mail cuttings as Noire de Caromb until a productive dark berry fig repeats from a careful source. Town names sell. Fruit does not care about the town.

Write Noire de Caromb on the pot that matches a productive dark berry fig from a careful source. Write unknown-dark-productive if the volume is real and the name is not. Volume is already a trait worth keeping.
