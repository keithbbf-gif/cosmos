Alabama: small-to-medium, light brown to violet skin, strawberry-pink pulp, small eye, pyriform with a long slender neck, excellent flavor, very hardy. Resembles Brown Turkey. Hardiness group with Celeste, English Brown Turkey, Alma.

Resembles Brown Turkey is doing a lot of work. A lot of “Hardy Chicago” in the North is a dark Mt. Etna / Sicilian-type that dies to the ground and fruits on new wood. A lot of Southern “Chicago” is a Turkey-shaped plant with a city on the tag. Wait for fruit. The city is not a pedigree.

## The name pile

Chicago Hardy and Hardy Chicago are the same sales argument. The city is a climate story, not a Condit chapter. Alabama [ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) files it under Hardy Chicago and stops there. No synonym stack like Celeste’s Malta / Sugar Fig pile. That does not mean the stick in the mail is clean.

Mt. Etna, Sal’s, Italian Black, and a dozen “dies to the ground, fruits anyway” names ride the same forum. They may be kin. They may be a pile. Do not merge them on a label to feel organized. A dark long-neck fig that resprouts is a *class*. A cultivar is a plate that repeats.

If the fruit is copper-brown, late, and Turkey-shaped, write a Turkey type and read that draft. If it is small, early, and sugar, you may have wandered into Celeste. If the only thing you measured is that the crown came back, write unknown-dark-hardy. Winter is a trait. It is not a cultivar.

I will not invent a Chicago pedigree from a marketplace photo. The [introduction page](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers and a 1–3 year wait to prove type. A first-year cup on a “Chicago” tag can lie.

## What hardy means

Dormant wood that takes more cold than Mission. A crown that resprouts when the top is gone. Fruit on new wood after a kill, like a Turkey type, if the season is long enough to finish.

It does not mean you skip the fig shuffle. Pots still freeze at the rim. Roots in a **#3** are not a Chicago trunk. [Our winter page](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) already said bring pots in, barely moist, not a heated den. That page named this cultivar and Smith for a reason. It also said container roots can cook when the pot sits in a wind tunnel at 15°F talk. Hardy wood above ground does not warm the mix.

It does not mean 8a in-ground is automatic. We are on the kind side of hardy. Alabama’s conversation number for unprotected dormant wood is **15–20°F**. The old UAEX clip talks **~15°F**. TAMU’s freeze chapter talks **~17°F**. Those are conversation numbers, not a warranty for a Saline County night after a warm spell. Late freeze after budbreak is cruel to early movers. Cover or roll.

It does not mean the fruit is Celeste-early. A resprout year is a short year. Short years want early figs. If Chicago fruits late for you, a kill-and-come-back season may give you leaves. Pair it with something that finishes — Ronde de Bordeaux, a Celeste-class tree, Jack Lily — if food is the point. Late celebrities like Black Madeira and Scott’s Black are the wrong partners for a year that already spent June growing wood.

Common type. Persistent. No wasp. UAEX is blunt: in Arkansas we grow common figs. [Jack’s type page](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) already drew the line. Do not buy pollen for a Chicago.

## Flavor talk

Alabama says excellent. Northern growers treat it like a berry fig worth the winter theater. I did not put Chicago Hardy on the [Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) list. I will eat it when we have a clean plate and say so. Until then I will not write a tasting note I would not sign. No homestead score. No invented “better than Negra” sentence to fill a hole.

Small dark fruit. Birds can see it. Tight-enough eye in the sheets. [VERIFY] yours. If the eye is open and the plant is a Turkey, you may have the pile. UAEX’s souring sentence still applies: water in the eye plus heat after rain. Tight is a door, not a vault. Split is a balloon that ran out of skin. Hardy does not sew either shut.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. That is on the record. Chicago sitting in the same hardiness group as those two does not make it a dessert fig and does not make it a tool I will pretend to love. It makes it a plant that can take a bite and try again.

## How I run it in 8a

Sun: **6–8 hours**. UAEX said the same minimum if you hope to harvest fruit. A hardy fig in deep shade is still shade. Afternoon cloth in July is South Arkansas, not a personality. East wall if you have one. The [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) page already unpacked South shade plus water. I will not restage that essay.

Pots: most of our collection lives in **#3** / **#5**. Pot up before June so the root can work the mix before the steam. Fertigate once it is a plant, not while it is a stick. Shuffle it. Do not leave a “hardy” **#3** on a wagon in January because the tag had a city on it.

Wood: take **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a tree that already fruit-and-returned. Lignified if you can. Alabama’s cutting chapter is shorter sticks; we like enough wood to see last year’s color. Root it like any other common fig. Do not mail a June leaf from a box-store “Chicago.”

In-ground: a bush you rebuild. TAMU’s freeze chapter — five or six trunks when the shoots are about two feet — is how you turn a resprout thicket back into something you can pick. Alabama wants a bush in the South, not a standard you will lose to the first hard night. Leave crop wood if the top lived. Dead wood only. Do not hat-rack a plant you have not typed. If it is actually a Celeste-class tree wearing a Chicago tag, a January saw deletes the year. UAEX already wrote that sentence on Celeste.

Water: even. Hardy is winter. July is still July. Drought then a flood is how fruit splits. Alabama said the riper the fig, the more it splits and sours. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Not a calendar date I will invent for Bradley County. [VERIFY] your first soften and write it on the tag.

Rain week: walk the tree before the storm if fruit is already giving. A day-early fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. A split fig on the ground is a beetle farm. Dried-fruit beetles want a door. Photograph the ostiole after rain. The still is the argument.

Birds: dark fruit is a billboard. Net or pick. UAEX said daily picking may be needed when a tree is in production. One neglected bowl teaches the birds the address. If the ground is dirty with nematodes, keep Chicago in a pot. It is not a nematode solution. Stop late nitrogen so September wood can lignify. Green shoots die first.

## How I would plant it for a neighbor

If you are north of us and you want a fig that returns, this is the name extension agents will tolerate. Common type. No wasp. Wood from a fruiting hardy tree, not a box-store “Chicago” in June leaf.

If you are us, plant it as insurance, not as the only bowl. Pair it with an early dark like Ronde if you want food the year the top dies. Pair it with Alma if you wanted a closed-eye brown tool in the same hardy group. Do not pair it with Desert King and expect a San Pedro second crop to save a short year. King wants last year’s wood. Chicago’s job is this year’s wood after a bite.

Do not skip water because the name said hardy. Do not skip the shuffle because 8a is “south.”

Write Chicago Hardy on the pot when a small dark long-neck fig returns after a bite and the fruit repeats. Write unknown-dark-hardy if the winter is the only thing you have measured. The city on the tag is a climate wish. The plate is the name.
