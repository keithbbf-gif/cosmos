Alma does not have a fan club. That is a compliment.

Texas A&M released it in **1975**. Alabama: small-to-medium golden brown, pyriform, amber pulp, sweet and delicate, well adapted in the Southeast, highly resistant to fruit rots, wood very hardy. TAMU’s disease handbook puts it with Celeste and Texas Everbearing in the closed-eye set. Magnolia and Kadota sit on the other side of that sentence.

Common type. No wasp. A tool fig. I am not a fan of Celeste so far. Alma is in that usefulness class with a different skin.

## The name pile

Alma is a short name and that helps. [ANR-1145](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/fig-production-guide/) does not hang a synonym stack on it. No Malta. No Sugar Fig. No Everbearing. The year and the station are the pedigree: Texas A&M, 1975, humid-South job.

That does not mean every “Alma” sticker is clean. 1975 is a long time for a name to wander. If the fruit is open-eyed and sours like Magnolia, [VERIFY] type. If it is small, early, and sugar with an eye that stays green until ripe, you may have wandered into Celeste. If it is copper, late, and rebounds from a hat-rack, you may have a Turkey type. Write what the plate repeats.

Condit is not the father of this one the way he is of Conadria and Excel. Those are California hybrids with their own drafts. Alma is a Texas answer to rot. Keep the sentences apart.

The [introduction page](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said trusted sellers and 1–3 years to prove type. A first-year brown cup can lie.

## What “rot-resistant” is allowed to mean

It means the eye and the skin were selected so humid weeks sour less than an open-eye preserve fig. It does not mean you can leave ripe fruit in a storm for a photograph. It does not mean dried-fruit beetles retired. Tight is a door. Pick is still a morning job.

TAMU’s souring table files Alma as resistant, same column as Celeste and Texas Everbearing. Magnolia and Kadota (they typed Kodata on that page) sit in susceptible. UAEX’s souring sentence is the climate version: water enters the eye, worse after rain plus heat. Closed-eye is a screen. Physics can still split a balloon.

If your Alma sours like a Kadota, [VERIFY] type.

Flavor: sweet, delicate, amber. Not a berry conversation. A kid fig. A preserve fig. A “we ate figs this year” fig. I did not put it on [The Fig Jam](https://www.thefigjam.co/p/interview-with-a-fig-grower-keith) list. Jack did not either. I would still plant it for a neighbor who wanted one tree that does not need a forum account. No homestead score. I will not invent one next to Negra so Alma looks fashionable.

Hardy wood: Alabama groups it with Celeste, English Brown Turkey, and Hardy Chicago in the 15–20°F talk. Conversation number. UAEX’s older clip talks ~15°F. TAMU’s freeze chapter talks ~17°F. Pots still freeze. In-ground in 8a is a reasonable trial. Chicago Hardy is the resprout brand. Alma is the “wood that takes a bite and still wants to be a yard fig” sentence without a city on the tag.

[Jack’s type page](https://figroots.com/2025/08/17/types-of-figs/) already said common figs set without a wasp. Alma is that type. Do not buy pollen. Do not buy a San Pedro brochure and hope Alma’s closed eye will fix Desert King’s main-crop drop. Different machines.

## How it fails

People want Black Madeira and get Alma and feel cheated. Alma did not cheat. The catalog did, if the catalog used the word “superb” without the word “useful.”

A hard prune on a Celeste-class tree deletes fruit. I treat Alma that way until the plant proves it is a Turkey. Dead wood only in January. UAEX’s Celeste warning is the Southern sentence I will not ignore just because the tag says Texas.

Late nitrogen. Tender September wood. Same as everybody. Green shoots die first.

Open-eye heartbreak on a mislabeled tree. Photograph the ostiole after rain. The still is the argument.

I am not a fan of Brown Turkey or Celeste so far. Alma lives in that usefulness class. I can respect a tool and still want a berry fig on the plate. Those are different compliments.

## How I run it in 8a

Sun: **6–8 hours**. UAEX’s minimum. Afternoon cloth in July. East wall if you have one. The [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) page already unpacked South shade plus water. A closed-eye fig in deep shade is still shade.

Pots: **#3** / **#5**, potted up before June. Fertigate once it is a plant. Shuffle it. [Our winter page](https://figroots.com/winter-protection/) already said barely moist, not a heated den. Hardy wood above ground does not warm the mix. Smith and Chicago Hardy are the names that page used for colder talk. Alma belongs in the same conversation without stealing their job.

Wood: **6–8 inches**, three nodes, off a fruiting tree. Lignified if you can. Do not mail a June leaf as 1975 Texas.

In-ground: east wall, clay that drains, mulch over shallow roots. TAMU’s freeze chapter — five or six trunks at two feet — if a bad night takes the top. Alabama wants a bush in the South, not a standard. Leave crop wood if the top lived.

Water: even. Drought drops. Flood splits. Alabama said the riper the fig, the more it splits and sours. Morning pick. Neck wilt. Give. Soft. Not a calendar date I will invent for Bradley County. [VERIFY] your first soften and write it on the tag.

Rain: this is the week Alma was hired for. Walk the tree anyway. A day-early fig in the fridge at about **40°F** is still a fig. Closed-eye is not a vault. Split still happens if you drought then flood a ripe balloon.

Birds: golden-brown hides better than Negra. They still find a soft fig. Net or pick. UAEX said daily picking may be needed when a tree is in production. One neglected bowl teaches the address.

Nematodes: dirty ground stays in a pot. Some old talk treats Alma as tougher in bad soil. I will not upgrade a rumor. The pick-the-right-fig page already sent nematode ground to containers.

## How I would run it

If you already have Celeste and a Turkey type, you may not need Alma. If you have open-eye heartbreak and you want a closed-eye brown fig that is not Celeste, this is the Texas name.

Wood from a fruiting tree. **6–8 inches**, three nodes. Pot up before June. East wall in-ground.

If the plate is dull and the tree is healthy, graft a berry fig and keep the hardy closed-eye roots. Negra, Red Sicilian, Jack Lily — those are the mouths on our lists. That is a compliment Alma understands. We graft. We do not throw a live hole away.

Hunt is the other Southern rain fig in this pack — long peduncle, Alabama’s shed-the-water sentence. Magnolia is the open-eye preserve fig TAMU put on the wrong side of the souring table. Do not plant Magnolia to fix Alma’s lack of fame.

Write Alma on the pot when a golden-brown closed-eye fig repeats and the rain week does not turn the bowl to vinegar. Write unknown-brown-tight if you only know the second half.
