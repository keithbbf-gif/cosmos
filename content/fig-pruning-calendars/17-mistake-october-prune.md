October is when the rest of the yard is being put away. The tomatoes come out. The perennials get cut. The fig is still holding a few leaves and a few late figs and it looks unfinished. So people finish it.

That is the mistake.

## What an October cut does in 8a

It tells a plant that still has soil heat to grow. The buds behind your heading break. You get four inches of soft green that will not lignify before the first real cold. Those four inches die, and they often die a little farther back than they would have if you had left the hardened tip alone. You did not “get ahead of February.” You bought extra dieback.

You also opened wounds in a month when the plant is not about to push a callus at spring speed. In the humid South, fig wood already decays like it is in a hurry. Fall stubs are invitations.

## “But UF said after harvest”

UF/IFAS, writing Florida, says if you prune, do it after harvest in late summer so next year’s buds can initiate, and they warn that heavy winter pruning can wipe a crop. That is a real sentence in a real bulletin. It is also a sentence from a place where “winter” is not 8a’s 12 °F.

If you are in Gainesville and the plant is done in August, a careful post-harvest thin is a local argument. If you are in an 8a Piedmont October, with fruit still on Alma and a frost in the next three weeks, you are not in that argument. You are in the one where we put the pruners down.

## What you can do in October that is not a prune

- Pick what is ripe.
- Prop a limb.
- Tag a leader you already know is coming out in late winter. Ribbon, not a cut.
- Stop watering into lushness.
- After leaf drop and after the soil cools, mulch.

If a branch is broken and peeling, take the hanging piece. That is storm work. It does not license a shape-up.

## The feeling you are trying to soothe

Unfinished-ness. I understand it. The fig will look like a wreck from November to March. That is what a deciduous fruiting shrub looks like. Tidy is not a harvest.

If you need a Saturday job, sharpen the bypass pruners and oil the wooden handles. February will use them. October will not forgive them.
