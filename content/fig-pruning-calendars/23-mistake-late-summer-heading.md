June pinch: a thumbnail, a blind pole, a request for laterals. That is a conversation.

Late July or August heading: a foot or two off a shoot that already has figs along it. That is a cancellation.

The main crop is on that shoot. The figs nearest the tip are often the last to finish. You take the tip, the plant throws laterals, the remaining fruit stalls, and in 8a you have moved ripeness toward the first cool night.

## Why people do it

The bush is in the walkway. The figs are high. Something looks “too vigorous,” which is a phrase we use when we are uncomfortable with a plant that is doing its job.

If a shoot is in the walkway in August, tie it or take the *whole* shoot at a crotch if you must, and accept the fruit on that shoot is spent. Do not shorten all the tall shoots to a nice line. A nice line is a hedge. A hedge of fig laterals in September is rust and hard fruit.

## Short-season math

7a and the colder 8a pockets have a limited number of warm nights left after August 1. A heading now is not “invigorating.” It is a delay you cannot buy back. This is the same reason draft 39 exists: do not spend time you do not have.

If the walkway is the real problem, put a stake and a tie on the offending shoot today and write “thin at the base in February” on the shed card. That keeps the fruit on the plant and keeps you from inventing a summer architecture season.

9b can shrug. Even 9b should not heading-cut a Gold or a Magnolia in a rainy August for fun. Those fruits are already in a spoilage race.

## What is still legal

- A broken wand.
- A water sprout with no fruit that is shading a cluster you care about. Take it at the base.
- Harvest.

If the plant is a monster, put a reminder on January 20, not a saw in the August heat. Structure is a dormant job in 8a. Late summer is a bowl job.
