Most figs in a Southern yard are common type. They set fruit without a wasp. You can argue about breba versus main and still eat something.

San Pedro types are a different contract. The breba — on last year’s wood — sets without pollination. The main crop — on this year’s wood — wants caprification, which means a caprifig and *Blastophaga psenes*. That wasp is not part of ordinary life in Georgia, Alabama, Oklahoma, or the Carolinas. California has a fig industry. You have a county.

Desert King is the name that gets people in trouble because it is famous, cold-tolerant for a West-coast fig, and sold by nurseries that do not print the flower type on the pot.

## The prune, then

If you are growing a true San Pedro in zone 8a–9b without a wasp, **the breba is the crop.** Last year’s extensions are not a bonus. They are the pantry. A late-winter heading that would be a reasonable main-crop reset on Brown Turkey is a full-year eviction on Desert King.

Your February job is almost priestly:

- Take dead wood.
- Take a crossing arm if the middle is a wreck.
- Do not shorten the fruitful laterals.
- If you must shorten for a clothesline, shorten to a lateral that still carries last season’s nodes, and accept that you just spent some fruit.

## Should you even have one in 8a?

Only if you can keep that old wood alive. 8a’s 10–15 °F nights can take the tips, and the tips are the point. A south wall, a wrap, or a pot that goes into a cold garage is the real prune — the one that happens in December. Shears are secondary.

In 9a/9b, an in-ground Desert King with light hands can be honest. In 7b it is a project. In 7a it is a pot or a story.

## Lookalikes and lazy labels

Nurseries mix names. “King” this, “Adriatic” that. If the plant makes a decent main crop in your wasp-free yard, you may not have a San Pedro, or you may have a common fig that someone liked the sound of. Eat what you have. Prune what you have. Do not apply a Desert King religion to a Celeste, and do not apply a Celeste heading to a plant that never sets a main crop.

## The mistake, written as a scene

January, 8b, sunny. You watch a video of a man in a short-sleeve shirt reducing a fig to scaffolding. You do that to the Desert King. June arrives. Empty. August arrives. A few hard green things that never sweeten, or nothing. You decide the variety is weak. The variety is waiting for a wasp you do not have, on wood you threw in the street.

Common figs forgive a wrong vote. San Pedro types keep the receipt.
