A south brick wall in 7b can behave like 8b. In 8a it can keep tips through a night that strips the same variety in the open yard. That is why people fan figs. The prune has to serve the wall, or you just have a thicket leaning on masonry.

## The shape

A fan: arms left and right, some up, nothing shooting straight at your face, nothing burying the brick in a wet mat. Wires or vine eyes. You will retie every year. If you will not retie, do not start a fan.

## Late winter

Take what grows *out* from the wall — the front-facing shoots that turn the fan into a hedge. Take what grows *behind* the wires if you can reach it; that is the rot side.

Leave the laterals that lie on the wire and still have last year’s length if you want breba. The wall is how those beads survive. Heading them is how you waste the wall.

In 7a, the wall plus a wrap is the only reason you have old wood. Treat it as rare.

## Summer

Pinch the front-facing shoots at a few leaves. Tie in a replacement where an old arm went bare. Do not let a vertical take over the gutter.

Stop pinching when the main crop is sizing. A wall holds heat. That is good for ripeness and bad if you keep making soft tips into October.

## Moisture against brick

Air still matters. A fan that is a solid green door will rust and will keep the mortar wet. Thin so you can see brick in places. That looks incomplete. It is correct.

## 8a open-yard people

If you do not have a wall, do not fake a fan with stakes in the lawn and then copy wall-pruning dates. A fan in the wind is just a flat bush that catches every degree of cold. Site first. Shears second.
