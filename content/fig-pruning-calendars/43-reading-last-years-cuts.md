A fig will tell you what you did. You have to look at the knuckles.

## What a heading looks like a year later

A stub or a fork with a thick ring, and two or three vigorous shoots from just behind it. If those shoots are long and the fruit was late, your heading was hard and the season collected the tax. If those shoots are moderate and the fruit was on time, the heading was a size you can repeat.

If there is a dead horn beyond a live fork, you left a stub. Cut the horn to the live collar this year. Do not leave it because “that’s where I always cut.”

## What a thinning cut looks like a year later

A clean crotch, daylight, a neighbor arm that got thicker. If the hole filled with five suckers, you thinned and then you did not come back in May. The May visit is part of the thinning cut. People forget that and decide thinning “doesn’t work.”

## What no cut looks like a year later

Long, gray-bottomed, smooth-topped extensions. Beads in a kind winter. Fruit at a height you need a ladder for. That is the unpruned story. It is not a moral failure. It is a height problem or a breba success. Decide which.

## Dieback vs your cut

Dieback dies to a random line, often mid-internode, and the pith is brown. Your cut is a plane. If you cannot tell them apart, you are looking too fast. Dieback means winter wrote in the book. Your job is catch-up (draft 38), not a matching heading on the arms that lived.

## 8a habit

Take a picture of the bush from the same step on the path on the day you prune and on the first of August. Two pictures. Next February you will not remember which plant was late. The pictures will. Phones are a better log than memory and a better log than a tidy notebook you will not open.

If last year’s pictures show late fruit after a hard heading, the book is already recommending a lighter sentence. You do not need a new theory. You need to obey the plant you already have.
