In 7 and in the honest 8a winters there are two jobs that look like they belong on the same Saturday: take the wrap off, and prune. They do not belong in the same hour, and they do not belong in the reverse order.

## Fall: do not prune so it will wrap easier

Shortening in October so the blanket fits is draft 17 wearing a hat. Build a wrap that fits the plant you have, or take *one* offending leader in late winter next year. Do not cut the plant to the size of the tarp.

## Winter: do not open the wrap to prune

If you must check, check. If you start “just taking a few” inside the wrap in January, you have combined drafts 18 and 17. Close it. Go inside.

## Spring: unwrap, wait, then cut

Unwrap when the nights you believe in are over, or when the wrap is cooking the plant on a 70 °F week. Then wait. A week is not piety. The wood changes color and the buds move once they see sun. You will prune more accurately. You will also not cut a leader that was only pale from being wrapped.

If a cold night is coming after you unwrap, a sheet for one night is cheaper than a heading you do in a panic.

## 8a people who never wrap

You can skip this draft until the winter you need it. The year you need it, the order still matters. The neighbor who cuts in January and wraps the stubs is running the movie backward.

## What the wrap is for

Old wood. Tips. Breba. A crown that does not sit in a wind lane. It is not a license to keep a twenty-foot tree. If the plant is too big to wrap, the prune (over years) is how you re-enter this draft. Draft 30 is the form. This is the sequence.

If you only wrap every third winter, still keep the form. The year you skip the wrap and get 11 °F is the year the bush-shaped plant loses tips and the tree-shaped plant loses a trunk. Sequence cannot save a form you refused in February.
