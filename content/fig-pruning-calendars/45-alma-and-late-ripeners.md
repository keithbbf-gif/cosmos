UGA suggests Alma if you want to extend the season. UF describes a late-July-through-August fruit, moderately hardy, sweet, a medium-open eye. In a Gulf year that extension is a gift. In an 8a year with an October 10 frost, that extension is a dare.

A dare you do not make worse with a hard February heading.

## The rule for late varieties

Leave more last-year architecture than you think. You may not care about a breba on Alma. You care about a plant that does not spend May rebuilding before it even starts the crop you bought it for.

Celeste can take a moderate heading and still finish in July in 8a. Alma, Pasquale (which UF does not even recommend for the Southeast, and which ripens toward November), and other late names cannot donate those weeks.

## 8a

Site Alma in the hottest honest pocket — south, not a frost bowl. Prune for air because the eye is not tight. Do not prune for a short dome. If the plant is too big, remove a leader, do not tip the whole canopy.

If your fall frost is early, Alma may be the wrong plant. Pruning will not make it Celeste.

## 9a/9b

Alma is more at home. You can take more wood. You still should not shove a late fig later for a walkway when a thinning cut would have saved the sidewalk and the calendar.

## Pasquale and the true late freaks

If you are growing a fig that wants November, you are in a 9b conversation or a pot-in-a-greenhouse conversation. Do not apply this folder’s 8a February heading to that plant and then write that late figs “don’t ripen here.” They didn’t ripen because you added a delay to a delay.

## Pairing

A yard with Celeste and Alma is a good yard if you prune them as two calendars. Celeste can be a little shorter, a little more headed. Alma stays longer-armed and earlier-to-work in the spring. Same Saturday, different motion (draft 40).
