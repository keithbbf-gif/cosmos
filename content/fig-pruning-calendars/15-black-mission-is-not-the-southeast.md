UF/IFAS says Mission is not sufficiently cold hardy for the southeastern United States. That sentence should have ended the Facebook thread. It did not.

People plant Black Mission in 8a because the fruit in the picture is dark and the name sounds like a place they would like to be. Then they ask how to prune it. The honest first answer is: you prune a plant that is trying not to die.

## What “pruning Mission” usually means here

You are cutting dead wood after winter. Again. You are cutting a leader that split because the plant pushed late and a 18 °F night cooked the sap. You are not managing a two-crop California orchard.

If the plant lives in 9b, unwrapped, most winters, you may get the famous long season — fruit from summer toward winter, “everbearing” in the loose catalog sense. The prune then is size and air, with respect for whatever breba wood you actually have. Mission is a common type; it does not need a wasp. It needs heat and a winter that does not behave like Georgia.

## 8a reality

A south wall, a wrap, or a pot. If you will not do those, plant Celeste. If you will do those, your “prune” is mostly: do not heading-cut in January the one wand that lived, and do not fall-prune the plant into a soft October flush.

A hard dormant reset on a marginally hardy Mission is how you spend a year growing replacement wood that will also be tender in November.

## The mistake underneath the mistake

People use pruning to solve a climate problem. They think if they keep the plant small it will be hardier. Small is easier to wrap. That part is true. Small because you cut it on March 1 every year is just a plant that never has old, hardened wood. For Mission in 8a, old hardened wood is the rare resource. Keep it when you get it.

## If you already have one

Do not rip it out in a sulk. Watch it for two winters. If it dies to the ground and returns, you have a Mission-shaped experiment that may still fruit late on new wood in a long 8a year. Prune that like Chicago Hardy: stool, stake, pinch, no sculpture. If it never hardens and never fruits, stop buying the California books for it.
