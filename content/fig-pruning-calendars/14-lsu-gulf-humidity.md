LSU Purple, LSU Gold, Champagne, O’Rourke, Tiger — these are Louisiana answers to Louisiana problems: heat, rain, a need for a closed or partly closed eye, and a main crop that can stand the Gulf. They are not Mission. They do not want to be a twenty-foot standard with a painted trunk.

If you prune them for a postcard, you will get rust, souring on the ones with a looser eye (Gold), and a late crop that sits in September rain.

## What to take in late winter

The middle. Not because a book said “open vase,” because last August the interior was a wet closet.

Take two or three whole arms that point in or cross. That is a thinning cut. The perimeter laterals can stay if you want fruit. LSU Purple in particular will keep ripening into fall in 9a. If you head every wand, you shove that habit even later, and “into fall” becomes “into the first cool night with wet fruit.”

In 8a, be gentler still. Purple will try to finish. Your job is not to dare it.

## Gold is not Purple

LSU Gold is a large yellow fig with an open eye. UF says pick it as soon as it is mature because spoilage is waiting. Pruning will not close the ostiole. Pruning can keep the fruit out of a leafy cup where rain sits. If you only have room for one LSU plant in a wet 8b/9a yard, Purple is the easier prune and the easier fruit.

## Champagne and the Celeste cousins

Champagne (Golden Celeste) and O’Rourke (Improved Celeste) carry Celeste timing — early July in the literature. Do not give them a 9b hard heading in 8a. You bought them to be early. Early is a prune you *don’t* do.

## Gulf summers and the June pinch

These plants grow. A June pinch on a five-foot blind shoot is useful. An August heading is how you get a flush of soft leaves and fig rust on the new growth while the fruit is trying to finish. Put the nitrogen away by midsummer too. A prune-and-feed special in July is a rust subscription.

## 8a, not Baton Rouge

An 8a Piedmont summer is not Houma. You still have humidity. You do not always have the same length of fall. Steal the air-cuts from the Gulf drafts. Leave the late-season confidence at the coastal plain.
