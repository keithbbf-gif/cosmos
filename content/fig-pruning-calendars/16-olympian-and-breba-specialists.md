Some figs earn their keep in June. Olympian is the one that gets passed around as a cold-hardy breba plant — purple, early, worth wrapping for. There are others in the hobby lists (a good Violette de Bordeaux in a kind climate, some Mt. Etna types that hold tips). The names shift. The pruning rule does not: **if you bought it for the early fruit, last year’s extensions are the crop.**

## 8a, which is the honest home for this idea

Olympian can keep wood through a 10–15 °F night more often than Mission. It will not keep wood through a sloppy fall and a sudden 12 °F. So the year has two tools:

1. **December:** wrap or site. South wall. Out of the wind. This is the prune that happens with blankets.
2. **Late February:** dead wood, a little air, no heading of the long laterals that still have beads.

If you also want a smaller plant, take a whole leader out at the base and leave the others long. Height control by subtraction, not by tipping.

## 7b

Some years you will get the breba. Some years you will get a nice story about the year you did. Do not do a January heading “just in case.” If the tips die, you will cut them when they show dead. If they live, a January heading is how you donate them to the yard waste tote.

## 9a

You can get greedy in a useful way: leave almost everything, then thin after you see which laterals are loaded. A June bowl is the point. The main crop is extra.

## Specialists are not a whole orchard

If every fig in the yard is a breba specialist and you live in 8a, one ugly winter takes your June and leaves you waiting on a late main crop you did not select for. Pair an Olympian with a Brown Turkey or an LSU Purple. Prune them differently, on the same Saturday if you want, but not with the same motion. Draft 40 is the yard version of this sentence.

## How to tell you are doing it wrong

You have never seen an overwintering fig on the plant in March because there is never any last-year length left. You still like the leaf. That is a shrub. It is allowed. It is not a breba specialist anymore.
