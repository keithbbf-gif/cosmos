Summer pruning on a fig, in this climate, should look like you forgot your shears and used your fingers.

## What you are pinching

A current-season shoot that is a pole: long internodes, no laterals, heading for the wire. Take the tip. The plant will break buds below and give you side wood. Side wood is fruit wood next year and sometimes more main-crop sites this year if you do it early enough.

## When

In 8a, late May through June is the honest window. Early July if the plant is still throwing blind canes after a kill-down. After the main-crop figs are sizing along the shoot, you are late. See draft 23.

How to tell a pinch from a heading in the hand: a pinch is the last inch or two of a still-soft tip, before it has lignified, before it has a row of pea figs. A heading is a woody cut through last month’s growth. If you need bypass pruners, you are probably late or you are doing winter work in summer clothes.

## What you are not doing

- Shaping a dome.
- Matching heights.
- “Tipping everything back by a third.” That is a dormant heading, done at the worst time.

## Zone 7 after a restart

The new canes are the whole plant. A June pinch is how you keep them from being twelve-foot ribbons. One pinch. Stake. Do not pinch weekly like a basil plant. Weekly pinching is how you stay in leaf forever.

## 9a/9b

You can pinch a little later because you have season left. You still should not turn June into a second winter prune. Heat plus wounds plus humidity is rust weather.

## Gloves

Yes. Soft tips still bleed. The latex on a green pinch is plenty. Do it in the evening if you are the person who burns. Draft 34.

## How you know it worked

In August you have a few forks instead of a single spear, and you can pick without a ladder. If you have a dense mop of laterals and no fruit, you pinched too much and maybe fed too much. Next year, pinch fewer spears and leave the nitrogen in the bag after June.
