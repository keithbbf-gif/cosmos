Celeste (Celestial, Blue Celeste, Little Brown Sugar — the small brown fig your aunt called “sugar fig”) is the second plant in the Southern yard after Brown Turkey, and it is the one people over-prune because the fruit is small and they think the bush should be small to match.

The fruit is small because it is Celeste. The closed eye is why it does not sour in a June rain the way Magnolia does. The plant wants a moderate life: sun, not too much nitrogen, and a prune that does not turn it into a stump.

## What Celeste is doing

Fairly cold hardy for a fig. Main crop early — July is a reasonable 8a expectation when the wood lived. Breba exists but is not the headline. UGA puts it in the mountains with protection, and in the Piedmont as a serious candidate. UF lists it as a standard. None of those bulletins ask you to carve it into a vase for Instagram.

Celeste drops fruit when water and heat get stupid. A hard prune plus a drought plus a thunderstorm is three insults. You will get a carpet of hard little figs and a lecture from the plant.

## The 8a prune I would actually do

Late February, after the worst cold, before a full leaf.

- Dead and rubbing wood.
- Extra suckers. Celeste suckers. Keep a replacement if an old leader is hollow.
- A light heading only on the wands that are in the wires or the neighbor’s lot. Not a uniform one-third on every arm just because UGA said one-third is how you get branching. You may already have branching.
- A hole in the middle if last year the interior leaves rusted off in August.

Leave the rest. Celeste at eight feet, pickable, slightly messy, is a better plant than Celeste at four feet with a late crop and sunburned bark.

## Zone 7b

Wait for green. Celeste will die back some winters. Clean to live. Do not also “train.” If you want a shorter plant in 7b, choose the leaders you wrap, do not choose a January haircut.

## Zone 9a

You can take more. The season will pay you. I still would not gut it. Early main crop is Celeste’s gift. Delay it for no reason and you have a Brown Turkey with smaller fruit.

## The closed-eye note that belongs in a pruning folder

You cannot prune a closed eye onto a Magnolia. You can prune air around a Celeste so the leaves stay on and the fruit ripens on the plant instead of in a wet wad. That is the humidity prune: a few thinning cuts, not a flattening.

If your Celeste is a thicket, the mistake was four years of kindness, not one February of restraint. Take out a whole leader at the base this year. Take another next year. Renovation by subtraction, not by heading every tip.
