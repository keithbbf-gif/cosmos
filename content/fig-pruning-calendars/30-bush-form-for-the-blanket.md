People apologize for a multi-stem fig. They say they never got around to making it a tree. In zones 7 and 8a the apology is backwards. A short, several-stem bush is the form you can put a blanket on.

## Why the blanket cares about the prune

You cannot wrap a twenty-foot standard. You can wrap a six-to-eight-foot stool: tomato cages, cardboard, leaves, burlap, maybe a breathable outer skin so it does not become a mold tent for three months.

If your late-winter prune is aiming at a taller and taller tree, you are pruning out of the wrap. If your late-winter prune keeps three to eight leaders and refuses the skyward extra, you are pruning *into* a winter you can manage.

## How to cut for a wrap, without ruining June

Do this in late February in 8a, after the wrap comes off and after you have looked at the ten-day:

- Take height off one or two leaders that have left the blanket’s future. Prefer a thinning cut to a lower lateral, not a hedge line.
- Do not heading-cut every tip “so it will fit next December.” That is a main-crop-only vote. If you want breba, choose which leaders will stay short enough to wrap and leave *those* tips. The ones you cannot wrap are the ones you shorten or remove.

The plant will not be a sphere. Spheres are what hedge trimmers make. A wrappable bush is a cluster with a height you can reach on a ladder you do not hate.

## 7a

The wrap is the season. The prune is after green, and the form is already a stool. Keep it a stool. Every year you let four canes become twelve feet of unstakeable rubber, you have made a plant you will not wrap well the following December.

## Mold

A wrap that holds wet leaves against green bark in a 50 °F January thaw is a different mistake. Breathable outer layer. Do not wrap on a soaking day. Unwrap for a weird warm week if you are attentive; most people are not, so err toward a wrap that can dry a little.

Pruning will not fix a plastic-tarp sauna. I mention it here because people prune in March, see mildew, and blame the cut. Blame the tarp.

## 8a center

Some 8a winters you will not wrap and you will be fine. The year you need the wrap, you will need the form. Prune for the year you need it, not for the year you brag about.
