There is a week when the buds are fat, the tips are showing a seam of green, and you can still see the whole skeleton. After that week, you are cutting leaves.

In 8a that week is often the first or second week of March. In a warm 8b it is February. In a stubborn inland 8a it can be later. The week is a stage, not a date.

## What is still allowed

- A leader you already tagged, taken at the base.
- Dead wood you are now sure of.
- A heading on a still-shut wand if you missed February and the ten-day is kind.

## What is no longer allowed

A tour of the plant “while I’m here” that tips every shoot. The plant is moving. Latex is moving. Insects and rust will use fresh wounds in a way they do not use a January cut. And you will knock swelling brebas off with your sleeve.

## If you miss the week

You missed it. Summer pinch only. Structure waits until next late winter. People cannot stand this. They invent a “late prune is fine.” A late structural prune is how you delay the main crop (draft 39) and open wood in humid weather.

Missing a year of structure is not a crisis. A thicket is a three-year object (draft 22). One missed March is a note on the calendar, not a license to saw in April.

## 7a

Bud swell may happen on the live wood while the dead wood is still a mystery. That is why 7a does not use this week the way 8a does. 7a uses green leaves. Different signal. Do not force this draft onto a plant that is still a stick on March 20.

## How to catch the week

Walk every Saturday from mid-February. When you see the seam of green on two varieties, that is the alarm. Not a notification from an app. The plant.
