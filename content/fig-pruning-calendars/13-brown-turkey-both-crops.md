The Brown Turkey in the Southeast is not the California Brown Turkey, and it is not one clone. Brunswick, Eastern Brown Turkey, Texas Everbearing, Harrison, Ramsey — the names are a junk drawer. What you probably have is a bronze fig, amber pulp, small-to-medium eye, a July breba if the wood lived, and a main crop that starts about a month later and keeps trying.

UGA’s useful sentence: Brown Turkey will make figs on current growth after being killed to the ground. That is why it is in 7a. UF’s useful sentence: a small spring crop, then the main crop. That is why it is in 8a and 9a.

You prune a plant that can do both, which means you can ruin either.

## The 8a default I like

Split the vote (draft 09).

Leave two or three last-year extensions long on the sunny side. Those are your July figs — not huge, not many, earlier than the rest of the yard.

Head the rest of last year’s growth by about a third in late February. That is your August weight.

Thin the suckers so you still have a bush, not a hedge. Brown Turkey will replace itself whether you ask or not.

## If you only want one job from it

**Jam plant.** Take the tips. You will lose July. You will gain vigor and a thicker late set, and you will not be climbing.

**Breakfast plant.** Leave the tips. Accept a bigger bush. Pick the early ones before the June beetles find them.

Do not try to have a four-foot jam plant that also makes a heavy breba. That is two books in one box.

## After a kill-down

You are in Chicago Hardy’s country. Wait for green. Keep a few canes. Stake. Pinch in June. The main crop will be later than the year the old wood lived. In 7a it may be a race. In 8a you still have August and September if you did not also fertilize it into a forest.

## The name problem, practically

If your “Brown Turkey” never makes a breba even in a 9a year with unpruned tips, you may have a different bronze fig, or you may be heading it every winter and not noticing. If it makes huge, open-eye, souring fruit, you may have drifted into Magnolia/Brunswick territory. Prune that one for air and pick it early for preserves. Do not treat it like a closed-eye Celeste.

## A small kindness

Brown Turkey wood is weak and the fruit is heavier than Celeste. If you leave long breba wands, give them a tie in May. A split crotch in June is a prune you did not schedule, and it always happens the week you have company.
