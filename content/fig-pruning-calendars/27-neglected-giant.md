Someone stopped pruning in 2019. The fig did not stop. It is now a room. The fruit is in the eaves. The interior is two dead arms and a graveyard of leaves. You have one Saturday and a cheap saw. This is how people make the renovation worse.

## Do not do the haircut

A uniform reduction to eight feet gives you a thousand watersprouts and no fruit you like. It also sunburns bark that has not seen full south light since the last administration.

## Do this instead, over two or three Februaries

**Year A.** Take out the dead. Then take one or two of the worst live leaders at the base — the ones that lean on the house, the ones that are hollow, the ones that cross the middle like a bar. Stop. You will think you did not do enough. You did.

**Summer A.** Let it respond. Thin only the sprouts that are in a walkway.

**Year B.** Take another major leader if you still need height off the roof. Start a counted set of younger stems on the outer stool if the old ones are all bark and moss.

**Year C.** Now you may head some of last year’s extensions on the keepers, if you want a main-crop reset. If the giant is a breba tree you actually eat in June, do not.

## 8a timing

Late February, after the cold you believe in, before bud swell. Not October. Not August. A giant cut in August is a late flush and a winter kill at a scale you will feel in the shoulders.

## Zone 7 giants

They are rarer, because winter renovates for you. If you have one, it is a microclimate story — a courtyard, a city, a wall. Still do not take it to the ground in January for neatness. Take dead, then one live problem stem after green, then wait.

## The house

If a leader is on the shingles, that is the one you take first, even if it is the “best” stem. A fig against asphalt shingles is a moisture problem that is not in the horticulture bulletins and is still your problem.

## Afterward

Whitewash newly exposed big wood if the south side is sudden. Or hang a shade cloth for a month if you are that sort of person. Or, better, never open a north-facing cave to full south in one day.
