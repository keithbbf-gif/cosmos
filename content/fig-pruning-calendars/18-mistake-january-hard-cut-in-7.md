Someone in a short-sleeve climate posted a video on January 10. The fig is a skeleton. They reduce it to knuckles. The comments say “so clean.” You are in 7a or 7b. You do that.

Then you get a 4 °F night in early February, or a 10 °F night after a thaw, and the knuckles you left are the living tissue you just exposed and the buds you just asked to wake up behind a cut.

## Two injuries, one Saturday

**Injury one:** you removed wood that might have lived, including any breba and any early main-crop laterals.

**Injury two:** you made the remaining wood more vulnerable. A cut is not a hat. It is a wound. In a zone where the coldest nights are still on the calendar, a wound is a place winter can enter.

The 8a version of this mistake is the same motion on a year that is about to deliver a 12 °F surprise. 8a people think they are 8b in January. Some years they are not.

## What you meant to do

You meant to be decisive. Zone 7 figs look hopeless in January. They are sticks. Waiting feels like neglect. It is not. Waiting is the technique.

The decisive day is the day green tells you where the live line is. That day is often April. You will not get a video with good lighting and bare-ground satisfaction. You will get a muddy pair of boots and a pile of truly dead canes. That pile is the correct pile.

## If you already did it

Do not “fix” it with another cut. Mulch. Wrap what is left if the cold is still coming. In spring, take whatever died back *to the new live line*, not to a deeper, neater idea. Then grow a main crop if the season allows. You are in a recovery year. Recovery years hate additional opinions.

## The sentence to tape to the shed

*In zone 7, January is for blankets, not for architecture.*
