The folder is centered on 8a because that is where the arguments meet. It is not one argument.

## Piedmont (a lot of Georgia, the Carolinas, Alabama upland)

UGA is speaking to you. Bush form. Three to eight leaders. Late winter after frost danger, before growth. Celeste, Chicago Hardy, Brown Turkey. Dieback is not a scandal. Rust is summer. Your prune is late February into March, and some years you wait for green like a 7b.

Do not take Gulf harvest dates and apply them to your Celeste. Your July is not their June.

## Gulf edge (the 8a that is trying to be 8b/9a)

Longer fall. Earlier spring. Humidity with a different weight. LSU varieties make sense. You can prune earlier. You must open the canopy or the fruit sours and the leaves drop in August. UF’s caution about heavy winter pruning erasing a crop should ring louder here, because you actually have a crop to erase — breba is real more years than not.

A hard heading is less dangerous for *time* and more expensive for *June*.

## Ozarks and the western 8a

Drier air some years, brutal wind, sudden drops. A fig can take cold better when it is dry and truly dormant, and worse when a January thaw comes off the plains. Site is everything: south wall, out of the north. Your prune wait is inland (draft 36). Your disease pressure may be lighter than the Piedmont. Do not open the bush as hard as a Gulf grower just because you liked their photographs. You need leaf, and you need wood that did not get a windburn heading on a 60 °F afternoon before a 10 °F night.

## Why a single “zone 8a calendar” still exists

Because the *decision rule* is shared: worst cold likely done, buds not committed, vote on the tips. The month you land on, the varieties you trust, and how hard you head — that is the neighborhood.

If you move from one of these to another, do not bring the Saturday. Bring the rule.
