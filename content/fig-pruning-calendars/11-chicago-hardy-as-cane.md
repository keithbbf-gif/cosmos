Chicago Hardy (Bensonhurst Purple, and a few other street names) is in zone 7 yards because it will come back from the crown and fruit on new wood. That is a raspberry sentence. Treat it like an apple and you will be sad in two directions: you will protect a trunk that winter is going to take, and you will refuse to thin the thicket that is the actual plant.

## In 7a and the mean 7b years

You are growing a stool. The “tree” is a courtesy title.

After green shows, cut the dead canes to the live line. If that line is the mulch, you now have a pile of new shoots. Keep a handful that have room to thicken — three to eight is still the right scale, even when they are this year’s. Stake them before they are seven feet of rubber with figs on the end.

Do not save a two-inch “trunk” of dead pipe because it looks official. It is a stub. Stubs rot into the crown.

A heading to force laterals can wait until you see whether the new canes are blind poles. June pinch, not April sculpture.

## In 8a, most years

Chicago Hardy will hold old wood through a 10–15 °F night more often than Celeste’s reputation suggests, but it is not magic. If the tips live, you can have a small breba. It is not why you bought it. I would still leave a couple of last-year extensions if they are alive, because early fruit is early fruit, and this plant is not a San Pedro — you are not betting the year.

The main prune is still: open the middle, drop extra suckers, keep the bush at a height you will actually pick. Chicago Hardy will go vertical like it is late for a train.

## The cane-fruit habits that help

- Replace a tired leader with a sucker on purpose. That is not sloppiness. That is how you keep young wood without a renovation year.
- Tie canes that will fruit. Fig wood is weak. The catalogs forget to say that.
- After a kill-down winter, do not fertilize like you are growing corn. You want those new canes to stop and fruit, not to be ten feet of leaf.

## The cane-fruit habit that does not help

Cutting everything to two inches every April “because that’s what you do with hardy figs.” If old wood lived, you just threw away an earlier main crop and any breba. Dieback is a measurement. It is not a style.

## 8a center, said again

If you only have room for one fig in 8a and you want jam in August, Brown Turkey or Celeste may feed you better in a kind year. If you want a plant that still has a job after a bad January, Chicago Hardy is the insurance. Prune the insurance like insurance: keep it able to restart, not pretty in February.
