9a is where the breba stops being a story you tell after a mild winter and starts being a crop you can put on a plate in May or June. Twenty to twenty-five on the card. A dormant fig that hardened in the fall is not thinking about death. It is thinking about those pea-sized overwintering figs at the ends of last year’s wands.

If you prune 9a like 7a, you will have a very polite bush and a late summer. If that is what you want, say it. Do not wander into January with a folding saw and then wonder where June went.

## The window is early, and that is the trap

Gulf and low-South 9a figs move in late winter. Wait until March and you are cutting into sap and swelling buds. The clean structural week is often mid-January to mid-February. In a warm pocket it is the first half of January.

That early window is exactly when people overdo it, because the bush is naked and every long shoot looks like a mistake.

Those long shoots are the pantry.

## A 9a prune that keeps the early crop

Keep three to eight leaders if you are in the UGA bush-form religion, which still works in 9a even though you could train a short tree and get away with it. Then:

- Take dead, diseased, and rubbing wood.
- Take the sucker you do not have a hole for.
- Take one older, barky, unproductive wand if the middle is a cave.
- Leave last year’s laterals long enough that the distal nodes stay on the plant.

You can shorten a leader that is heading for the phone line. Shorten it to a lateral that already exists, not to a bald stub. The lateral you keep should still have some of last season’s length on it if you want figs in June.

## When a harder cut is still honest

You have a Magnolia that sours in the rain and you only want the later, firmer main crop. You have a fig against a walkway and the city will cite you. You have a first-year plant that needs a fork. Fine. Cut. Just do not also plant Desert King and treat it like a caneberry.

In 9a a hard winter heading still has calendar left to make a main crop. The cost is the breba and, on some varieties, a few weeks of delay. You can afford the delay more than 8a can. You cannot afford to pretend there was no cost.

## Humidity is the other prune

9a in the Southeast is not 9a in a dry valley. Rust, souring, split fruit on open-eye types — those are canopy problems. January is when you take two crossing arms out of the middle so June air has a hallway. That thinning protects fruit quality without sacrificing every breba tip on the perimeter.

If you only thin the easy stuff at the outside, you keep the disease room and lose the fruit. People do this constantly because the outside is where they can stand.

## Looking north from 9a

8a readers: do not steal this January confidence. Steal the *tip* idea. When your winter leaves you those overwintering figs, you are briefly in a 9a problem, and the answer is the same: the ends of last year’s wood are not extra.
