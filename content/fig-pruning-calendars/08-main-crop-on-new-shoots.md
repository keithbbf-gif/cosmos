The main crop is the fig most 7–9 yards actually eat. It starts after the leaves, on the current season’s shoots, in the leaf axils of wood that was not there in March. It ripens in the second half of summer, sometimes into fall. It is why a fig killed to the ground can still feed you in a long year.

If you only remember one mechanical fact, remember this: **you cannot prune main-crop wood in February because it has not been grown.** You can only prune the *conditions* for it — how many shoots, how much sun, how late they are allowed to start.

## What you are setting up

A dormant heading on last year’s extension does two jobs at once. It removes breba room, and it tells the plant to throw vigorous new shoots from lower buds. Those new shoots are main-crop factories. They are also late. The harder you cut, the more the plant spends April and May making canes instead of settling into fruit.

In 9b that delay is a shrug. In 8a it is a negotiation. In 7a it can be a lost September.

So the main-crop prune is not “cut as hard as you can.” It is “cut as hard as your frost dates will pay for.”

## What main-crop figs look like in June

New green shoots, two to four feet of spring ambition, and little figs forming along them, often starting after a few nodes. They are not the leftovers of last year. They are this year’s sentence. If you pinch the tip of that shoot in June, you may get laterals and a more compact set. If you cut that shoot in half in late July because it looks tall, you throw away the figs that were about to size, and the regrowth will try to fruit in a month you do not have.

## Varieties that forgive a main-crop prune

Eastern Brown Turkey (the one in a thousand Southern yards, also sold under a pile of names) will fruit on new wood after a freeze. UGA says this out loud. Chicago Hardy is in the catalogs for the same reason. LSU Purple will keep trying into the fall in a long year. These are the plants you prune for a stool of productive canes when winter has already taken the top.

Celeste will also main-crop, but it does not love being turned into a stump, and it drops fruit when it is mad about water and heat. A moderate heading, not a renovation, is plenty.

## Varieties that do not forgive it

Anything you planted *for* the breba. Desert King, if you are even growing it honestly. Some Olympian trees that earn their keep in June. A Black Mission you insisted on in 8b — the early fruit was the interesting part, and the late fruit may not beat your weather anyway.

## The 8a main-crop calendar, short

- Late February / early March: take dead wood. If you are choosing main crop, head last year’s extensions by about a third. Leave enough leaf area in the plant’s memory that it does not panic into a forest of suckers.
- May: thin to the canes you can support.
- June: pinch the blind poles.
- After the fruit is on: hands off the length.

If you want both crops on the same plant, you do not “split the difference” by heading every shoot halfway. You leave some last-year extensions long, and you head others. The bush will look uneven. Uneven is the point. Even is a haircut. Figs do not pay you for even.
