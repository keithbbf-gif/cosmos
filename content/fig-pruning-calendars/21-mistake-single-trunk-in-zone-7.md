A fig can be a tree. In a climate that does not kill trunks, a short standard with three or four scaffolds is handsome and pickable. UGA’s pruning bulletin says the tree form is not practical in the Georgia Piedmont because plants freeze back to the ground. That is a Piedmont sentence that 7a and a lot of 7b should tattoo on a glove.

## Why people try anyway

They want a “real tree.” They want to mow under it. They saw a Mission in a courtyard. They were told suckers are sloppy.

Suckers in zone 7 are the plant. The crown is the plant. The trunk is a candidate, not a identity.

When you rub every sucker and pour the plant’s future into one stem, a single 5 °F night can take the orchard. A bush with six leaders can lose three and still have a year.

## 8a is not innocent

8a can keep a trunk more years than 7a. It can also lose one in the winter that dips to 11 °F after a thaw. If you want a small tree in 8a, keep two low backups for a couple of years until you believe your microclimate. Then you can rub the extras. Belief should be expensive and slow.

## How to undo it without a funeral

If you already have a single trunk and winter cooked it, do not keep the dead column “for structure.” Cut it to the live crown. Let the suckers come. Choose three to eight. You have not failed. You have arrived at the form the climate was asking for.

If the trunk lived, you can still lay down a second and third leader from low shoots this year. It will look like a mistake until it looks like insurance.

## Mowing

Mulch a wide circle. Do not mow the crown. The single-trunk dream is often a mowing dream. A bush with mulch is less work than a tree you wrap with a blanket and a prayer every January.
