Every fig prune in zones 7–9 is a vote. People pretend they are voting for “health” or “airflow.” Those are real. They are also how we avoid saying the blunt thing: **this cut either keeps last year’s tips or it spends them.**

You can do both on one plant. You cannot do both on one wand.

## The ballot

**Leave the tips.** You are betting that winter is done, that a late freeze will not cook the embryos, and that you would rather eat in June than have a shorter bush. This is the 9a default and the 8a mild-year default.

**Take the tips.** You are betting that the breba would have been small, frost-killed, or in the way, and that you want strong new wood and a heavier late crop. This is the 7a default and the 8a year after a 12 °F night has already made the bet for you.

**Split the plant.** Leave two leaders long. Head two. Thin the middle. This is the grown-up 8a move. The bush looks a little drunk until July. Then it looks like a person who planned.

## What you are not allowed to do is be surprised

If you head every long shoot in February and then complain that there was no breba, that is not data about the variety. That is data about you.

If you refuse to cut anything for four years, keep every sucker, and then complain that the figs are at twenty feet in the squirrel county, that is also you. The tradeoff has a second face: unpruned breba wood becomes unpickable breba wood.

## How zone 8a should vote, most years

Look at the wood in the third week of February.

- Tips live, beads present, ten-day forecast without a teen-degree night: vote *leave* on the varieties you like early (Brown Turkey breba, Olympian). Vote *take* on the giant that is eating the fence, or on a late ripener you need to keep compact so it finishes.
- Tips dead, pith in the last foot: winter already voted *take*. Clean to live wood. Do not also impose a second, fashionable heading below that.

## How people hide from the vote

They take “just the tall ones.” The tall ones are often last year’s best extensions.

They take “just a little off each.” That is a heading of everything. It is the most efficient way to erase a breba.

They prune in October so they “don’t have to decide in the cold.” That is a different mistake, and it writes soft wood for the winter to kill. See draft 17.

## A sentence to say out loud

Before the first cut that is not dead wood, say: *I am keeping June, or I am buying August.* If you cannot finish the sentence, put the pruners down and walk around the bush once more. The tree will wait a day. It will not wait through a confused haircut.
