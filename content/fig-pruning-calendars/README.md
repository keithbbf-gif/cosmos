# Fig pruning calendars — draft set

Staged drafts for backyard fig pruning in **USDA zones 7–9**, written from **zone 8a as the center**. These are not a published book and not a substitute for a county Extension bulletin. They are working copy: human voice, one subject per file, meant to be cut, kept, or rewritten.

## What this set covers

1. **Season and climate** — when the shears come out, and why a January cut that is fine in 9b is a bad idea in 7a.
2. **Breba vs main crop** — fruit on last year’s wood vs fruit on this year’s shoots, and how a single winter heading decides which crop you keep.
3. **Mistakes** — the cuts that feel tidy and then cost a summer.

## How to read the numbers

| Zone | Typical annual extreme min | Fig winter, in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7a | 0 to 5 °F | Dieback is normal. Main crop is the crop. Wait for green before you trust a cut. |
| 7b | 5 to 10 °F | Some years you keep old wood. Some years you do not. |
| **8a (center)** | **10 to 15 °F** | **The dishonest middle. Fully dormant wood can live. Soft wood and a two-night 12 °F will not.** |
| 8b | 15 to 20 °F | Dormant prune is usually safe by late January. Breba is worth planning for. |
| 9a | 20 to 25 °F | January window. Light hands if you want the early figs. |
| 9b | 25 to 30 °F | Long season. A hard cut still has time to make a main crop. |

A zone card is not a microclimate. A south brick wall in 7b can behave like 8b. An open hill in 8a can behave like 7b on a windy January night.

## Sources these drafts lean on

- UGA Extension C 945 / C 1087 — bush form, 3–8 leaders, late winter after frost danger, head one-third to one-half of last year’s growth if you want branching, cut to a bud or a branch, no stubs.
- UF/IFAS HS27 (Sarkhosh & Andersen) — common types for the Southeast, breba vs main, freeze injury, the warning that heavy winter pruning can erase next year’s crop, late-summer prune note for Florida.
- Common horticulture of *Ficus carica*: breba on previous-season wood; main crop on current-season shoots; latex (ficin) irritates skin, worse in sun.

Where those sources disagree — Florida late-summer prune vs Georgia late-winter heading — the drafts say so instead of picking a winner and hiding the fight.

## Voice and staging

Each file is a **draft**. Front matter marks `draft: true`, `status: staged`, and `voice_check: edited` after the editor pass. Staged PD/CC/USDA figures and rights live in `RIGHTS.md`; see `EDITOR_REPORT.md` for the 2026-09-14 editor + image SEO pass. Do not treat a confident sentence as a county recommendation. If a cut is irreversible, the draft should say that before it says how.
