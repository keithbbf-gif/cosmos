Fig latex comes out white and looks like an emergency. People reach for pruning sealer, latex house paint, wood glue, even aluminum foil. I have seen all of these.

The plant was already sealing. That white is the plant. It is messy, it will stain your shirt, and it will irritate your skin. It is not a hole you are required to plug.

## Why sealers are a bad match here

Fig wood is weak and already inclined to decay. A coating that traps moisture against pith is not a kindness. You want a clean cut to a bud or a collar, sun and air, and a plant that is about to grow. You do not want a painted stub. UGA’s warning about stubs is the real wound care: don’t leave one. The cut should have a reason to callus — a bud or a branch just below it.

White latex paint on *bark* to reduce sun scald on an exposed trunk is a different practice, and UF mentions it for trunks and major limbs that suddenly see sun. That is a thin paint on intact bark, not a cap on a saw cut. If you just opened a bush that had a dark interior, the remaining leaders can burn. A dilute white paint on those stems is a shade, not a scab.

## What to do instead

- Sharp bypass pruners for anything you can eat with one hand. A folding saw for the rest. Anvil pruners mash fig wood.
- Cut to a bud or a crotch. No horns.
- Gloves. Long sleeves. Latex in sun is a dermatitis story (draft 34).
- If you opened the canopy hard, consider that shade paint on newly exposed bark, or just don’t open it that hard in one year.

## The 8a version of this mistake

Someone takes three big leaders out of a mature bush in March, paints the cookies, and then the afternoon sun cooks the remaining arms because the interior had never seen March sun. The paint on the cookies did nothing. The missing shade did everything.

If you need to reduce a giant, take one major leader this year and another next year. That is wound care you can see from the road.
