8b’s card is 15 to 20 °F. A dormant fig that had a real autumn — no August fertilizer, no September flood of nitrogen, leaves down, wood brown — will usually take that. So the internet says prune in January. The internet is not wrong. It is incomplete.

January is legal. It is not mandatory, and it is not a dare.

## Why January works here

Buds are still shut. You can see every crotch. Sap is slower than it will be in April, which matters because fig cuts weep latex whenever they feel like it, but they weep less when the bush is asleep. You have the whole spring for wounds to callus before the humid season really sits down.

If you want a main-crop machine — fewer, thicker leaders, last year’s wands shortened one-third to one-half — January in 8b is a clean time to do it. The season is long enough that the delay in the main crop still leaves you August fruit on Celeste and Brown Turkey.

## Why January fails here

Because 8b still has 8a nights. Coastal 8b is not inland 8b. A river-bottom 8b can take a 14 °F morning after a 60 °F week, and the wood you shortened on January 8 is the wood that was just thinking about moving.

Also because people hear “January” and they bring a pole saw. Heavy winter pruning, UF/IFAS says plainly, can eliminate the following year’s crop. They are talking in part about next year’s *breba*, which lives on the wood you just dropped in a pile. In 8b the breba is not a myth. Brown Turkey will give you a July handful if you leave the old laterals alone. Celeste is more of a main-crop personality, but it still wears last year’s tips.

So: January is a permission, not a personality.

## A sane 8b year

**Early January.** Walk. Tag. Do not cut during a hard freeze week just to have a project.

**Mid to late January, or the first quiet week of February.** Dead wood. Crossing wood. Suckers. Then the decision:

- *Breba year:* stop. Maybe take one crowded leader out at the base.
- *Main-crop year:* head last year’s growth on the kept leaders. Cut to a bud, not to a blank stub. UGA is boring and right about stubs. Fig wood rots.

**March.** You should already be done with structure. If you are not, you are now in 8a’s late window and you need to hurry or wait until next year.

**Summer.** Pinch. Thin a little air. Stop by the time the main crop is sizing.

## The sloppiness

8b gardeners get proud. The bush lives. The January cut becomes a habit. Then they buy an Olympian because someone said the breba is dessert, and they keep doing the January heading, and they decide Olympian is a fraud.

The variety is not the fraud. The calendar was written for a different intention.

If you live on the 8a/8b line — and a lot of “8b” yards are 8a on the nights that matter — keep draft 01 in the other hand. The first year you see 12 °F on your own thermometer, you are not in the January club anymore.
