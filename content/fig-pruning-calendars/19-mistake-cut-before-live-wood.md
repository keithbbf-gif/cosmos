Fig bark does not color-code. Live and dead both look like last year’s weather. If you prune on color, you will take a live wand that was late to wake and you will leave a dead wand that still has a polite tan.

## The tests that work

**Scratch.** A thumbnail or a knife, a small patch, not a ring. Green-white and damp under the outer bark is life. Brown and dry is not. If you ring the wand, you may kill what you were testing. One small window.

**Bud.** A bud that swells and shows green is a better test than a scratch. That is why 7a waits. In 8a after a mean winter, wait too.

**The bead.** A shriveled overwintering fig that falls off in your hand is dead fruit. It is not always a dead wand. Do not take the wand for the bead.

## The test that does not work

Bending. Live fig wood is pithy and will snap when it is cold. Dead fig wood also snaps. You will stand there with two broken ends and no information.

## How this becomes a bad prune

You go out on March 2 in 8a because the calendar said late winter. Half the buds are still shut. You decide the shut half is dead and you cut to the “live” half. April 10 the shut half would have moved. You just headed a living plant on a rumor.

Or the reverse: you leave a thick dead pipe because it looks structural. In June it is a wasp hotel and a rot straw into the crotch. Dead wood can come out once you are sure. Sure is a later date than tidy wants.

## A method that keeps you honest

Mark, don’t cut, on the first walk. Ribbon on anything you *think* is dead. Walk again in ten days. If nothing has moved and the scratch is brown, then cut to the live crotch. Two walks is not indecision. It is how you avoid buying a year.
