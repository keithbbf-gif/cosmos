# Conservation vs Commercial Reupholstery

## Learning objective

A brand that offers recover services can tell a customer when the job is **use-again upholstery** and when the object should go to a **conservator** — and will not call a full strip “museum restoration.”

## Thirty-second floor version

“If you sit on it every night, we can reupholster it: new webbing, springs, foam or hair, new cover. If it is a documented historic object, a conservator may keep the old stuffing as evidence and only stabilize. Those are different jobs. We do not call a full strip a conservation.”

## History

The V&A’s work on settee W.15-1945 is the teaching case. Conservators lifted covers, read **tack holes vs tacks**, thread types, and later repairs, and concluded the under-upholstery was original enough to display in the British Galleries. Cleaning was tested so dyes and stamped wool would survive. The goal was **knowledge and display**, not a firmer sit for a TV room.

Commercial reupholstery, as taught by AMUSF and trade schools, is usually a **strip to the frame**: pull tacks or staples, evaluate rails, re-web, re-spring, re-stuff, re-cover. It is honorable. It erases stratigraphy. A 1920s sofa that has been recovered four times has four lost covers in a landfill and one live cover. Brands that sell “restore your heirloom” should say which path they mean.

Decision tree for the sales floor (not a legal test):

1. Is there a museum, auction, or family archive identity? → pause; photograph; recommend a conservator.
2. Is the frame broken or wormed? → furniture conservator / frame shop first.
3. Is the request “make it sit like a new sofa”? → commercial reupholstery; budget hair vs foam honestly.
4. Is the request “keep grandmother’s needlepoint”? → save the show cover; rebuild under it if possible.

**First-up and documentation.** Even commercial shops can photograph layers as they come off. That costs an hour and creates the only “provenance” a family sofa will ever have. History of the object starts at the strip if nobody wrote it down.

Brands that sell new sofas should not mock recoverers. The recover shop is the **aftermarket** that makes “buy once” true (UH-44). Partner or refer.

Never offer to “antique” a new frame with fake worm holes and call it conservation.

## What a brand may honestly say

- “We reupholster for use. We document layers if you want a file.”
- “This piece should be seen by a conservator before anyone strips it.”
- “Museum conservation keeps evidence. That can mean a seat you must not sit on.”

## What a brand must not say

- “Museum restoration” on a staple-gun recover.
- “We’ll keep it original” then dump the hair.
- Chemical strippers on a gilt frame as DIY advice.
