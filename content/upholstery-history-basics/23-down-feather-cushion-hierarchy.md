# Down, Feather, and the Cushion Hierarchy

## Learning objective

Sales can explain **down, feather, and blend** as a hierarchy of loft and labor (fluff me), not as a moral hierarchy, and can describe the ticking that makes the fill usable.

## Thirty-second floor version

“Down is the soft undercoating; feathers have quills. Furniture usually uses a blend, in down-proof ticking, often wrapped around a foam core so you get a crown without a pancake. Ancient people already stuffed linen with waterfowl feathers. The sewn box construction is modern shop practice.”

## History

Feather cushions are ancient (UH-03). What changes is **containment**. A loose bag of feathers on a klinē is not a boxed T-cushion with a zipper and a foam slab.

Trade vocabulary for training:

- **Down** — clusters, loft, no stiff quill. Expensive. Migrates. Needs baffles or tight ticking.
- **Feather** — quill, support, noise, can poke. Cheap bulk.
- **Down/feather blend** — the ordinary “down cushion” on a sofa. Ratios (e.g. 80/20, 50/50) must be on the law label where required.
- **Down-proof ticking** — tight cotton (or later coated cloth) that keeps plumes inside.
- **Baffle box / channel** — interior walls that stop all the fill from falling to one end.
- **Foam-down (or spring-down)** — a foam (or innerspring) core in a down envelope. The dominant late-twentieth-century “luxury” sit. Honest name: it is a **hybrid**, not a pure down pillow the size of a seat.

Labor: down seats require **daily dressing**. Victorian servants and modern homeowners who hate chopping cushions will hate honest down. A brand that sells down without teaching the fluff is setting up returns.

Ethics and regulation: live-pluck scandals, responsible-down standards, and allergy claims belong to the spec team. History does not exempt a brand from current animal-welfare questions.

Early Eames lounge cushions (collector literature) used feather and down in glove leather; later production uses foam. That single product history is a parable: **even icons change fill**. A brand that restores a 1950s piece should decide whether it is conserving a sit or updating it (UH-39).

Waterfowl vs chicken feather: chicken is cheaper and worse for loft. If the label says “feather,” ask which bird.

## What a brand may honestly say

- “This cushion is [ratio] down/feather in down-proof ticking, [with / without] a foam core.”
- “Down seating is historic. Our boxed construction is specified and labeled.”
- “You will need to fluff these. If you do not want that, choose the foam seat.”

## What a brand must not say

- “100% down” on a foam-down core.
- “Hypoallergenic down” as a magic word without a standard.
- “No maintenance, like a pillow.”
