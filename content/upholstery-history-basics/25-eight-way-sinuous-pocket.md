# Eight-Way Hand-Tied, Sinuous, and Pocket Springs

## Learning objective

Sales can compare three systems without a religious war: **hand-tied coils**, **sinuous wire**, **pocket (Marshall) coils** — and can spot “fake eight-way” talk.

## Thirty-second floor version

“Eight-way hand-tied means each coil is tied by hand in four directions — eight knots — so the deck moves as a net. It’s labor. Sinuous springs are zigzag wires clipped to the rails, common since the 1930s, and they’re what Florence Knoll’s lounge used as No-Sag. Pocket coils, Marshall’s idea around 1899, put each spring in a cloth bag. None of these is ‘fake’ if we name it and build it well.”

## History

**Hand-tied coils.** Crofton already requires tying so springs stay upright and help one another. Twentieth-century American high-end language crystallizes this as **eight-way hand-tied**: twine in front-back, side-side, and two diagonals; a knot as the twine enters and leaves each coil. The result is a net. Soft twine is quieter than metal clips. Labor is high. Repair means a skilled upholsterer.

**Fake eight-way.** Drop-in units, clips, or a few ties sold as “eight-way” are a known retail abuse. The floor test used by honest shops: lift the cushion, press the deck, feel individual coils, look under the cambric if the customer is allowed. Training should teach the test and forbid the lie.

**Sinuous (zigzag, No-Sag).** From the 1930s, a continuous S-shaped wire runs from rail to rail, clipped, often with helical cross-wires. Fast, cheap, thin, good enough when gauge and clip count are right (trade talk: ~8-gauge, multiple “silent” ties). Knoll’s 1954 lounge specifies No-Sag. A well-made sinuous seat can outlast a sloppy tied deck. A brand should price the labor difference, not invent a moral difference.

**Pocket / Marshall coils.** James Marshall, Canadian machinist, invents the individually cloth-wrapped coil in **1899**, patents in Canada **1900**, then the US and UK in the early 1900s (Marshall Mattress company history; Wikipedia summary of the patents). The system becomes a mattress standard (Vi-Spring in the UK licensed lineage). In seating, pocket units give **point-elastic** comfort — neighbors don’t all dip together. They are not “new this decade” unless the *application* is new.

**Which to specify.** Tight modern sofas: sinuous or webbing. Deep traditional: tied coils or a quality pocket unit. Motion furniture: engineered metal. Hospitality abuse: the cheapest zigzag. History does not pick a winner; **specification** does.

## What a brand may honestly say

- “This deck is eight-way hand-tied. Here is a photo of the twine.”
- “This deck is 8-gauge sinuous with [n] cross-wires. It’s a 1930s factory method done properly.”
- “These cushions use Marshall-type pocket coils, a design patented at the turn of the twentieth century.”

## What a brand must not say

- “Eight-way” without eight ways.
- “Sinuous never lasts.”
- “We invented the pocket spring.”
