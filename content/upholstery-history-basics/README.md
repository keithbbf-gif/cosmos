# Upholstery History Basics

**Status:** staged drafts (not published catalog copy).  
**Audience:** furniture brands — sales training, merchandising, product development, and educational content.  
**Series rule:** quality over speed. A claim is not evidence. Lore is labeled lore.

This folder is a **staged curriculum**, not a dump of blog posts. Read in stage order. Each file is an independent teaching draft that a brand can assign to a role (sales floor, catalog desk, product engineer, conservation partner) without rewriting history to fit a collection name.

## Why this exists

Furniture brands routinely inherit period names, construction slogans, and origin stories that sound older and cleaner than the record. “Chippendale sofa,” “Chesterfield invented in 1750,” “eight-way hand-tied since the 1700s,” and “foam-free like the Victorians” are common. This series teaches the **documented** history of padded seating so a brand can:

- name a collection after a *form* or a *construction* without inventing a founder myth
- explain why a seat costs what it costs (labor, materials, regulation)
- train a sales associate to speak for 30 seconds without fabricating
- distinguish museum objects, factory SKUs, and marketing adjectives

It is educational. It is not a legal opinion, a flammability certificate, or a conservation report.

## File contract

Every draft uses the same front matter and the same teaching spine:

1. **Learning objective** — one sentence a trainer can put on a slide.
2. **Thirty-second floor version** — what a salesperson may say if the customer asked only one question.
3. **History** — dated, sourced, with uncertainty marked.
4. **What a brand may honestly say** — copy that survives a fact-check.
5. **What a brand must not say** — the fabricated-compliance class (a green claim with no artifact).
6. **Sources** — museums, statutes, pattern books, company archives. Trade blogs are cited only when they point at a primary.
7. **See also** — the next draft in the stage.

`status: draft` means the text is staged for review. It is not live web copy until a brand historian or counsel signs it.

## How to review

1. Open `MANIFEST.md` and confirm 48 `status: draft` rows.
2. Spot-check any three drafts from different stages for the seven-part spine.
3. Reject any sentence that names a living brand as the inventor of a centuries-old form unless a patent or archive is cited in that same draft.

## Out of scope

- Arkansas factory biographies (separate series).
- SKU pricing, vendor negotiation, or “best sofa 2026” listicles.
- Instructions for defeating fire tests or relabeling fill.
- Verbatim reproduction of copyrighted pattern-book plates (cite; do not paste).
