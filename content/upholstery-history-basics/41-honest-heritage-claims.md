# Honest Heritage Claims (Anti-Placation)

## Learning objective

Anyone who writes catalog copy can apply a **fail-closed heritage test**: if the live factory cannot emit the proof, the sentence is not printed.

## Thirty-second floor version

“We may say what we can show: a date on a charter we do not own, a construction photo, a test certificate, a pattern-book plate we cite. We may not say ‘since 1750,’ ‘invented by the Earl,’ or ‘museum quality’ to soothe a buyer. A green sentence with no artifact is a lie.”

## History

Furniture marketing has a long record of **useful fiction**: Chippendale as a personality cult, Chesterfield as a deathbed gift, “hand-tied since the 1700s,” “chemical-free Victorian foam,” “Gobelins atelier,” “fireproof linen.” Those sentences exist because they close a sale. They are the **placation class** — a plausible story offered in place of the object.

This series was written so a brand can do the other thing:

| Tempting line | Honest line |
| --- | --- |
| Invented 1750 by the Earl | Form with equal-height arms; 19th-c. popularity; lore labeled lore |
| Chippendale original | *Director* taste; splat/leg type; 1754 book |
| Eight-way since Georgian times | Tied coils after 1830s shop practice; eight-way is later language |
| Museum restoration | Commercial recover or conservator — pick one |
| Passes all fire laws | Named test, named market, named cert |
| Ancient Egyptian upholstery | Inlaid wood that *imitates* hide; separate cushions |
| Guild-certified | The Upholders exist; we are not them |

**The artifact rule.** For construction: a photo of the deck. For origin: a factory date, a bill of materials, a patent number. For style: a plate citation. For law: a file. If the artifact is missing, the claim is unpublished. That is how you keep a brand from fabricating compliance with history.

**Collection naming.** Name after a **form** (camelback), a **construction** (hand-tied), a **place you actually make** (Hickory), or a **documented designer license**. Do not name after a living museum object you do not own.

**Comparatives.** “The only authentic,” “the original Chesterfield,” “the real eight-way” are fight words. History is a commons.

This is the same ethic as a ledger that refuses a corrupt line: **visible refusal is correct**. A catalog that says “we don’t know” on the Earl story is more premium than one that knows too much.

## What a brand may honestly say

- Any sentence in this series’ “may honestly say” blocks, matched to the SKU.
- “We looked; we don’t have a primary for that story.”
- “Inspired by [plate / object / region], built by [factory] in [year].”

## What a brand must not say

- Founder myths without paper.
- Test results without files.
- “Hand-made” on a staple line with one hand-sewn pillow.
