# Fire Law: UK 1988 and California TB 117-2013

## Learning objective

Anyone who writes a hangtag can state that **UK and California tests are not the same**, that **TB 117-2013 is not a UK passport**, and that **historic fill is not an exemption** unless the statute says so.

## Thirty-second floor version

“The UK’s 1988 furniture fire regulations ask for cigarette resistance, filling tests, and usually a match test on covers, with labels. California’s TB 117-2013 is a smolder test for materials in upholstered furniture sold there. Passing one is not passing the other. Our compliance team owns the certificates. Sales does not improvise.”

## History

Upholstery fire law is a **late-twentieth-century** chapter. It exists because foam and synthetic covers changed how domestic fires grow. History of horsehair does not repeal it.

### United Kingdom — 1988

The Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 (SI 1988/1324), amended 1989 and 1993, set resistance levels for domestic upholstered furniture. Core ideas from the statutory text and the official guide:

- upholstery must pass a **cigarette test** (with listed exceptions)
- **fillings** must pass ignitability tests; crumb foam must come from combustion-modified foam
- **covers** generally need a **match test**, or a specified alternative route (e.g. interliner)
- **display and permanent labels** are part of the duty
- some rules reach **second-hand** furniture
- **export** and certain antiques (goods made before 1 January 1950, and materials for re-upholstering those goods) are treated differently in the regulations — **read the current text**, do not guess at the sales desk

This is why UK sofas feel and smell the way they do, and why some COM cloths cannot go on a UK domestic sofa without a barrier. A US mill shipping to Britain needs a **UK file**, not a California hangtag.

### California — TB 117 then 117-2013

California’s TB 117 (1975) tested resilient filling, including an **open-flame** component that the industry often met with flame-retardant chemicals in foam. **TB 117-2013** revises the bulletin toward **smolder resistance** of the cover/barrier/fill mockup (aligned with ASTM E1353 / UFAC-type thinking) and was intended to **reduce reliance on those chemicals**. Furniture manufactured after **1 January 2015** for California must carry the TB 117-2013 flammability notice. A law label for fill remains a separate requirement. TB 116 is a different, voluntary cigarette-on-furniture standard; do not confuse the numbers.

CPSC staff have published skepticism that small-scale smolder tests predict full-scale furniture fires. That debate is for policy people. Sales people need the **label that matches the market**.

### Training rules

- Do not say “fireproof.”
- Do not say “passes all US and UK tests” without both files.
- Do not tell a customer to remove a barrier cloth to make the sit softer.
- Historic reproductions for **props** vs **domestic sale** are different legal objects.

This draft is education, not counsel. The statute and the notified body win.

## What a brand may honestly say

- “UK domestic sofas in this line meet the 1988 Regulations as amended. The permanent label is here.”
- “California-sold pieces meet TB 117-2013. That is a smolder standard, not the old open-flame foam test.”
- “COM must be approved against the destination country’s tests.”

## What a brand must not say

- “TB 117-2013 = UK match test.”
- “Organic hair, so we skipped the test.”
- Coaching customers to defeat a label.
