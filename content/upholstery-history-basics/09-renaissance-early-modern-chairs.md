# Renaissance and Early Modern Padded Chairs

## Learning objective

Merchandisers can date the shift from **portable cushion on hard seat** to **padding built onto the chair**, and can name a few forms without calling everything “Renaissance sofa.”

## Thirty-second floor version

“In the 1500s and 1600s, European chairs start to carry their own padding — leather, velvet, or embroidery tacked to the frame. The sit is still shallow by later standards. A surviving late-seventeenth-century settee at the V&A still has its original stuffed layers and iron tacks. That’s the beginning of the carcass we sell, not a Victorian cloud.”

## History

Renaissance rooms still use chests, benches, and X-chairs. The **Dante** and **Savonarola** chairs are folding or fixed X-frames with leather or fabric seats — a memory of the curule, not a club chair. The French **caquetoire** (conversation chair) widens the seat and lifts the arms for skirts. Italian *sgabelli* remain mostly wood. Padding, when it appears, is often a thin stuffed panel or a leather seat nailed with large decorative nails.

The important change for this curriculum is **attachment**. Instead of a cushion you take away, the cover is tacked through to the frame. The V&A’s late-seventeenth-century settee (W.15-1945), studied for the British Galleries, keeps original under-upholstery, canvas-work embroidery top covers, silk and linen trimmings, and stamped yellow wool outside covers. Conservators read **tack holes and thread** to prove the layers were original. Hand-made iron upholstery tacks attach cover and fringe. Blue linen thread, doubled, makes up cushions and stitches trim. That object is a teaching kit: show wood, stuffed back and seat, show cover, fringe, and a cheaper outside wool.

What the sit feels like: firmer, thinner, closer to the rail than a 1920s easy chair. No coil springs. Horsehair and textile layers are shaped by stitching and by the frame’s profile. Brands that sell “Renaissance” with six-inch foam and a waterfall skirt are selling a costume silhouette.

State rooms of the sixteenth century also use **cloth of gold, velvet, and leather** as political surfaces. The chair of estate is a textile object. Do not reduce it to a SKU with a lion finial.

X-frame folding stools and chairs remain in court use (the bishop’s faldstool comparison Carter made for Tutankhamun is a later echo). When a modern brand sells a folding leather camp stool, the honest European cousin is this family — plus Pratt’s later camp-equipage patents (UH-24), not a Chesterfield.

## What a brand may honestly say

- “From the 1500s, some European chairs carry tacked leather or textile padding instead of a loose cushion only.”
- “Museum settees of the late 1600s show layered stuffing, embroidery, and iron tacks — still without coil springs.”
- “Our nailhead trim is in the decorative-nail tradition of early modern leather chairs.”

## What a brand must not say

- “Renaissance cloud comfort.”
- “Original 16th-century springs.”
- Using a Medici name on a foam recliner.
