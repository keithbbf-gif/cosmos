# High Point, Hickory, and American Factory Upholstery

## Learning objective

American brand staff can locate **High Point as the market** and **the Catawba Valley / Hickory as a custom upholstery factory belt**, and can explain why upholstery stayed onshore longer than wood case goods.

## Thirty-second floor version

“High Point’s wholesale market opened 1 March 1909 so Southern factories could compete with Chicago and New York. Hickory and the Catawba Valley still make a lot of custom upholstery — foam and fabric are expensive to ship as finished sofas. ‘Made in North Carolina’ is a logistics story as much as a romance.”

## History

Late-nineteenth-century Piedmont North Carolina had hardwood, rail, and labor. High Point’s first furniture factory is commonly dated to **1888** (High Point Furniture Manufacturing Company) in local histories. On **1 March 1909** the first **Southern Furniture Market** opened in High Point; a North Carolina General Assembly centennial resolution (2009) restates that purpose: a southern market against existing northern ones. That market — now High Point Market — is still the largest home-furnishings trade show of its kind. Brands that “go to Market” are standing in that 1909 decision.

Hickory’s story is more **factory-and-retail**. Early local plants (late nineteenth century) and a mid-twentieth-century wholesale exhibition culture (Leroy Lail and others; later Hickory Furniture Mart as a year-round retail destination) made the town a consumer-facing “furniture capital” in its own marketing. Do not fight High Point and Hickory for a crown in copy. They are **different jobs**: market vs a dense upholstery-making valley.

**Why upholstery stayed.** Trade commentary (AHFA and regional press) is consistent: case goods went offshore because wood can move as parts and flat packs; **upholstered pieces are bulky, custom, and costly per container**. Custom one-offs cannot be cheaply batch-made in a distant mill. That is why Catawba Valley shops still hire sewers, framers, and upholsterers.

**The Furniture Academy.** In 2012 regional manufacturers and CVCC paused an outdated program; in **2014** the Catawba Valley Furniture Academy opened with founding industry partners **Century, Lee Industries, Lexington Home Brands, Sherrill, and Vanguard**. A 2016 Alexander County academy followed (Craftmaster, Kincaid, Mitchell Gold + Bob Williams among founding partners). Programs cover upholstery, sewing, frame building, finishing. Placement claims in press should be re-checked before a brand quotes them, but the **existence of an industry-built school** is the educational fact: the craft is still a job, not a costume.

A brand that manufactures there can say so with a **plant address**. A brand that only shows in High Point should not say “handmade in our Hickory workshop.”

## What a brand may honestly say

- “We show at High Point Market, which began as the Southern Furniture Market on 1 March 1909.”
- “This line is upholstered in the Catawba Valley. Custom upholstery stayed domestic in part because it ships badly.”
- “We hire from [named academy]” — if true.

## What a brand must not say

- “We founded the 1909 market.”
- “Furniture capital of the world” as an unearned slogan.
- A High Point showroom photo captioned as the factory.
