# Latex (1929) and the Polyurethane Takeover

## Learning objective

Product teams can date **latex foam** to Murphy/Dunlop 1929 and **polyurethane furniture foam** to the late 1950s–60s, and can explain why factories switched (cost, process control) without a purity sermon.

## Thirty-second floor version

“In 1929 a Dunlop chemist in Birmingham, E. A. Murphy, whipped latex, soap, and gelling agents in a mixer and invented foam rubber — Dunlopillo. It went into buses, cinemas, and mattresses. After the war, polyurethane foam was cheaper and easier to run. Most sofas since the 1960s sit on polyfoam. ‘Foam-free’ is a specification, not a return to 1750.”

## History

**29 January-era 1929, Fort Dunlop.** Murphy measures latex, soap, and gelling agents into a cake mixer, whisks, steam-cures. Dunlopillo becomes a product line for automotive seats, cinema seats, mattresses, pillows, ships. A 1931 latex mattress; London bus seats by 1932 (automotive-cushioning histories). War cuts plantation latex; postwar supply returns. Headquarters later at Pannal; Talalay equipment in the 1960s. Murphy later takes the Charles Goodyear Medal (1966) and writes “Some Early Adventures with Latex.”

Two latex processes matter in spec sheets:

- **Dunlopillo / Dunlop** — intense mixing (Oakes), chemical gel.
- **Talalay** (Goodrich lineage) — vacuum, CO₂, pin-freezing for more uniform blocks.

**Polyurethane.** Isocyanate foam chemistry exists from the late 1930s; flexible PU foam becomes a furniture commodity in the **late 1950s** and dominates in the **1960s** because it is cheaper, easier to pour or cut, and more dimensionally consistent for trim. Automotive histories are explicit: latex was the comfort yardstick; urethane won on cost and process. Trade commentary from upholsterers (e.g. repair shops noting failed foam) is real: **bad foam** is a top repair. Density and ILD (indentation load deflection) are the adult words. “Cloud” is not.

**Soy and “bio” foams** are twenty-first-century marketing layers on PU chemistry. Teach them as **partial polyol substitution**, not as a vegetable cushion your grandmother would recognize.

**Memory foam** (viscoelastic, NASA-adjacent later consumer story) is a furniture chapter of the 1990s–2000s. Do not back-date it to Dunlopillo.

Fire chemistry (UH-40) is why foam became a political object: open-flame tests pushed halogenated flame retardants; California’s TB 117-2013 stepped back from that particular path. A brand that says “natural latex, no chemicals” still has to pass the tests of the market it sells in.

Italian and contract designers who **mold foam as form** (UH-20) are using the takeover as an aesthetic. That is historically literate. Pretending foam never happened is not.

## What a brand may honestly say

- “Latex foam dates to Dunlop in 1929. We use [Dunlop / Talalay / blend] at [density].”
- “This seat is polyurethane foam, specified at [density / ILD]. That’s the post-1960s normal.”
- “Foam-free in this line means [hair / down / springs]. It is not a legal fire exemption.”

## What a brand must not say

- “Chemical-free foam.”
- “Victorian latex.”
- “Soy foam = a soybean cushion.”
