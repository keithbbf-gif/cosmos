This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

C. Woodruff “Woody” Starkweather gave American clinicians a way to talk about a child’s fluency as a relation between what the child could do and what the situation asked. Demands-capacities — elaborated with colleagues including Susan Gottwald — is a model, not a test battery and not a parenting verdict. It can be used to slow a room down. It can be abused to tell a family they are the demand. The abuse is not the model’s only life, and this article will not pretend it is innocent.

This profile treats a living (or at least not publicly necrologized) scholar. A 2017 public note describes a Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia intensive program named for him after a former client’s gift, directed by Joseph Donaher. A Stuttering Foundation 2010 notice records a Malcolm Fraser Award. He taught at Hunter College and then at Temple University (the public biographical sketches say Temple from the mid-1970s into 2000). He served the International Fluency Association; a 1995 *JFD* interview calls him president-elect, and a 1997 note addresses him as past president. No death notice was found in a university necrology at pack date. Dates of birth are not printed on the pages cited here. Incomplete is the honest field.

## The books

*Fluency and Stuttering* and later clinical writing on childhood fluency are the objects students meet. Curlee’s edited volumes gave him chapters. This page will not extract a demands list as a home chart. The historical claim is smaller: after Johnson’s adjective and before ICF’s participation grammar, Starkweather offered a middle vocabulary — capacity, demand, a child who is not only a rate.

## What later people kept

A way to think about pressure without a porch experiment. An IFA institutional life. What they discarded, or should watch: a model that becomes a mother’s report card. What they still use: the CHOP program’s name as a late public fact, and the Fraser award as a peer fact.

Temple’s fluency culture in the last quarter of the century sat between New York (Hunter) and a later Vermont coda that public sketches mention without a necrology. This article will not invent a retirement party. It will say that a model about demands and capacities became, for better and worse, the language a student used when a parent asked “did I cause this?” The honest use of the model is: rooms have pressure; children have limits; neither fact is a verdict on a mother’s character. The dishonest use is a chart that grades the dinner table. Donaher’s later CHOP program, named after a gift, is a public institutional fact. It is not a warrant to download a demands worksheet from a random blog.
