This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Richard Frederick Curlee made treatment design a subject. Before “evidence-based practice” was an imported slogan, he and a small group of fluency researchers insisted that a claim about therapy needed a design, a follow-up, and a willingness to publish a dull result. The University of Arizona faculty memorial page (1935–2008; Arizona 1975–2002) quotes Roger Ingham and Edward Conture on that point. The quote is a colleague’s sentence. It is also why he is in this profile.

Wake Forest B.A., 1961. USC M.A. 1965 and Ph.D. 1967 — Perkins’s campus. Associate Secretary for Research and Scientific Affairs at ASHA, 1971–1975. Then Tucson: interim department head, associate dean of the graduate college, editor of the *Journal of Fluency Disorders*, Malcolm Fraser Award, ASHA Fellow, Honors of the Association 2004. *Stuttering and Related Disorders of Fluency* (Thieme, 1993) and the Curlee & Perkins / Curlee & Siegel *Nature and Treatment of Stuttering* volumes are the books students still find used.

## The job that mattered

Not a named therapy. An editorial standard. Early-intervention debates in the 1990s — including public arguments with Yairi about when to treat a preschooler — are part of the record (*AJSLP* 1997 and the surrounding papers). This profile will not pick a 1997 winner. It will say that Curlee was willing to be the person who asked whether the data could carry the claim.

He died in 2008. The Arizona page does not print a day in the short memorial; library catalogs use 1935–2008. This article will not invent a Tuesday.

## What later people kept

A suspicion of testimonials. A journal that could reject a miracle. What they discarded: the idea that design alone makes a clinic kind. What they still use: the edited volumes as a map of a late-century argument.

Call him Dr. Curlee. Do not turn an edited handbook into a home program. Ingham and Conture appear here as memorialists, not as second sitters.

Tucson is not a second Iowa. It is a research campus that hired a man who had already been ASHA’s research secretary and then let him edit a fluency journal. The combination — association office, desert department, *JFD* — is a late-century career shape: the field had become large enough to need an editor who would send a miracle back. Students who only meet him as “Curlee & Siegel” owe him that refusal.
