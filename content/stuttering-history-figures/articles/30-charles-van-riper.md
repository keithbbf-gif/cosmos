This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Charles Gage Van Riper opened a speech clinic at Western State Teachers College in 1936 and then wrote the book American undergraduates wore out. *Speech Correction: Principles and Methods* (Prentice-Hall, 1939) went through ten editions. The stuttering chapters taught a craft — identification, desensitization, modification, stabilization — that later students reduced to a mnemonic and some supervisors reduced to a photocopied hierarchy. The mnemonic is not a protocol on this page. The book is a historical object.

He was born 1 December 1905 in Champion, Michigan, and died in September 1994. Contemporaneous memorials use 25 September; some secondary lists print the 26th. This article follows the memorials and flags the split, as the sibling profession pack already did. He stuttered. He had been a patient of other people’s certainty, including a surgical generation’s leftovers and the masters’ promises. Kalamazoo was his answer: a room where the stammer could be worked with instead of abolished on paper.

## The work

The 1939 textbook, then later *The Nature of Stuttering* and *The Treatment of Stuttering* in the early 1970s. Pull-outs, cancellations, preparatory sets — furniture that still starts arguments. Malcolm Fraser walked into this orbit in 1947 and left with a foundation. Van Riper’s name is on the Foundation’s origin story. That is influence without a royalty.

He was willing to live with a messy cause. That was a fight with Iowa. Johnson wanted the adjective. Travis wanted the hemisphere. Van Riper wanted a person who could stay in the sentence. Hugo Gregory later tried to marry this craft to the fluency-shaping machines. Van Riper did not have to live long into that peace conference to know the war had been real.

## What later people kept

A permission to work with the stammer as it is. A teacherly prose that does not sound like a grant. What they discarded, or should have: the hierarchy as weekend homework; the idea that ten editions settle a science. What they still argue about: whether a person should stay in a block or slide toward a shaped fluency. The argument is the residue.

The sibling pack’s Van Riper profile is a profession-textbook vita. This one is the craft and the Fraser conversation. New sentences. Same clinic door. Western Michigan students still walk past his name. The name is not a protocol. It is an address that refused, for a long time, to promise a cured tongue.
