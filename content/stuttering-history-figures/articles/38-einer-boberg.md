This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Einer Boberg (born Ejnar Bidstrup Bovbjerg, 1935, Dalum, near Drumheller, Alberta) stuttered badly enough to leave school after grade eight, try the farm, try the violin, and eventually decide that the only way to live with the mouth was to enter the field that claimed to know it. Minnesota summer clinics — Bryngelson’s campus in the background — then relapse, then again. An Iowa M.A. in 1963, in Johnson’s weather. A Minnesota Ph.D. in 1968. In 1971 he went home to head a new speech-pathology program at the University of Alberta. He died in Edmonton on 1 October 1995 after a short illness (cancer), a few months before ISTAR moved into a larger room.

## The institute and the book

In 1986, with Deborah Kully, and with help from the Alberta Elks and the Purple Cross Fund, he founded the Institute for Stuttering Treatment and Research. Year-round treatment instead of a summer-only hospital program. Clients from far away. Students. Research. *Comprehensive Stuttering Program* (Boberg & Kully, 1985) is the published engine: prolonged speech in a contingency frame, plus the maintenance question he had already been asking at Banff conferences. This page will not list the targets. It will say that a Canadian university address tried to keep the machine honest after month fourteen.

He organized international conferences, took honors from the Canadian association in 1983, and in 1991 became the first president of the International Fluency Association. Roger Ingham’s 1998 *JSLPA* memorial introduction is a colleague’s map of the maintenance work. Name Kully. Name Marilyn Langevin in the later ISTAR story. A founder who dies in year nine of an institute is otherwise turned into a sole genius.

## What later people kept

Maintenance as a moral. Self-help as part of a university program, not a rival church. What they discarded: any claim that 1985 boards settle 2026. What they still use: ISTAR as a place, and the idea that relapse is a research topic.

He is Dr. Boberg. He is not an American ASHA mascot. Alberta is the residue. So is a violin he did not make a career of.

The Banff maintenance conferences are easy to reduce to scenery. They were a research argument with a mountain outside the window: what happens after the intensive month, and who is responsible for the fourteenth month? Self-help groups were invited into that argument instead of being treated as a rival church. A university that can sit with a self-help chair is a different university from 1939 Iowa. Boberg’s IFA presidency in 1991 put that difference on an international letterhead.
