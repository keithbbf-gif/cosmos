This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Dean Elmer Williams taught Iowa students to ask a child what the child was doing in the moment of struggle. The question sounded simple. It was a refusal of both the diagnosogenic shrug and the machine’s first-week graph. If the speaker could describe the push, the hold, the backup, the speaker might change the next attempt. Later students called the direction “forward-moving speech.” This page will not teach it. It will say that a teacher at Iowa, after Johnson, tried to keep the campus and lose the porch.

He died 31 July 1994 in Iowa City. Edward G. Conture’s remembrance, as extracted on the Kuster pages and in a 2003 ISAD note by Robert Quesal, is the warrant for that date. Quesal, Williams’s penultimate doctoral student, is also the warrant for the career shape: bachelor’s at Iowa, master’s at Florida State, doctorate at Iowa (the 1952 dissertation is *An evaluation of masseter muscle action potentials in stuttered and nonstuttered speech*), Indiana University, then back to Iowa until retirement in 1987. ASHA Fellow; Honors of the Association, 1992.

Birth year 1924 appears in FamilySearch (“Dr. Dean Elmer Williams, 1924–1994”). It does not appear in the Conture remembrance used for the death. This pack prints 1924 with that flag. A missing newspaper birth notice is better than a guessed farmhouse.

## The work

Papers with Franklin Silverman and others on children who stutter. Teacher notes that the Stuttering Foundation later circulated (including school-facing pamphlets). A clinical style that made students remember the hour more than the theory. Quesal and J. Scott Yaruss’s 2000 *CICSD* historical piece is a student’s map, not a hagiography.

He worked in Johnson’s building without becoming Johnson’s experiment. That is a harder job than it looks.

## What later people kept

The question. A child-facing tone that did not condescend. What they discarded: any attempt to freeze a 1950s Iowa hour as a 2026 protocol. What they still argue about: how much description is enough before you change the speech.

Do not photocopy Foundation pamphlets from a random scan. Name Quesal as a source, not as a second subject.

Iowa after Johnson needed teachers who could keep the building without keeping the porch. Williams was one. The 1952 masseter dissertation is a young man’s laboratory object; the later school pamphlets are an old teacher’s civic object. The vita holds both. A magazine that prints only the question is being kind. The question is still the part a student remembers.
