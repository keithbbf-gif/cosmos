This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In the 1980s and 1990s, at a health-service address in Lidcombe, New South Wales, Mark Onslow and colleagues developed a preschool stuttering treatment that made the parent the daily clinician. Verbal contingencies on stutter-free speech and on stuttering; a weekly visit to a therapist who coached the parent; severity ratings; a maintenance phase. Early outcome papers begin around 1990 (Onslow, Costa, Rue). Randomized trials came later, in the 2000s, and the arguments came with them: how strong is the effect, for whom, at what age, and what happens to the child who was going to recover anyway?

This page will not teach Lidcombe. ASHA’s Practice Portal and the program’s own training exist for people who intend to use it. A magazine that extracts the steps is a pirate manual.

## What the decades thought they were solving

Access, timing, and the Iowa shadow. If many children recover and some do not, can you treat early without becoming Mary Tudor? Lidcombe’s answer was: treat the speech, not the label’s magic; use a parent who already lives in the house; measure. Critics’ answer was: you are still putting consequences on a toddler’s mouth, and your control groups do not settle natural recovery. Both sentences can be in the same history.

Ann Packman is part of the Australian authorship and should be named in any honest paragraph. So should the health-service setting. This was not, at first, a private-pay boutique.

## Another parent, another city

Lena Rustin’s London work, from the 1970s into the 1993 opening of the Michael Palin Centre for Stammering Children, put parents in the room for a different reason. The British tradition she shaped was interaction, family communication, the child’s confidence, a specialist NHS address with a charity partner (Action for Stammering Children). Palin, the public name, is a later gift. Rustin’s wish for a centre of excellence is the earlier fact.

Do not collapse Lidcombe and Palin into “parent programs.” One is a verbal-contingency treatment with a fidelity culture. The other is a family-and-child specialist service with a broader social brief. A rural American clinic that borrows a little of each without training in either is a third thing, and not a named method.

## Who was left out

Parents who cannot do daily structured talking. Families whose language is not the program’s language. Fathers, in the early films, who appear as weekends. Children in foster care — the orphanage problem in a new key. Australian Aboriginal families and British families outside the Centre’s reach.

Yairi and Ambrose’s Illinois natural-recovery data sit beside both programs as an uncomfortable cousin. If many preschoolers recover, early treatment has to prove it is not taking credit for time. The later trials tried. The argument is not finished and this article will not finish it.

## Residue

The parent in the chair is now ordinary. That is new. Van Riper’s parent was a homework helper. Johnson’s parent was a possible cause. Rustin’s parent is a conversational partner. Onslow’s parent is a trained contingency manager. A 2026 evaluation that asks what the family already does is living in this history.

Do not download a Lidcombe worksheet from a random blog and call it evidence. Do not use this essay as a substitute for supervision. A method with a training culture is a historical fact. A PDF without that culture is just a sheet of paper.
