This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Lee Edward Travis treated stuttering as if a psychology department could own it. At Iowa in the 1920s and after, that ownership became a campus fact: graduate students, recording rooms, a textbook, a pipeline into the American Academy of Speech Correction (the association that would become ASHA). Wendell Johnson was one of the students. So, in a looser sense, was the idea that Midwestern measurement could replace a London master.

He was born in 1896 and died in 1987. He stuttered. The childhood is the story, as it is for Johnson and Van Riper, but this profile will not write a novel about it. The job that mattered was Iowa, then later California (USC among the stations), and a long old age in which the dominance theory had already been politely retired.

## The 1931 book

*Speech Pathology* (New York: D. Appleton, 1931) is an early American hard cover that does not apologize for the laboratory. Handedness, the nervous child, the device. Students copied diagrams. Clinicians told left-handed children to pick a side. The advice felt like medicine because it came from a campus that looked like medicine.

Orton’s reading neurology is in the air. Travis made it speak. Bryngelson carried laterality north. The later tables were modest. The book had already done its work.

He was also an organizational founder. The sibling profession pack has him in the Hotel McAlpin generation. This pack will only say that the man who wanted a hemisphere also wanted a profession. Those wants are not the same, and they shared a vita.

## What later people kept

The laboratory as a fluency address. The student who stutters as a legitimate scientist of his own mouth. What they discarded: the simple dominance arrow, the switched pen as treatment, some of the certainty. Johnson kept the campus and changed the cause. Van Riper kept the stammer and walked to Kalamazoo.

Do not assign laterality homework. Do not call 1931 clinicians SLPs. Travis helped make the credential possible. He did not hold it.

USC, later, received him as a senior name. California is not a second Iowa. It is a long coda in which the 1931 diagrams had already become historical. Students who met him old were meeting a founder, not a current laterality protocol. That distinction is what a magazine owes a long life. The book stays. The fork does not.

The sibling pack’s Travis profile is a profession ancestor. This one is the hemisphere and the hallway. New scene. Same man.
