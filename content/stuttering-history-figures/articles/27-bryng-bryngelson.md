This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Bryng Bryngelson took Iowa’s laterality story to Minneapolis and built clinics while he argued it. The University of Minnesota hired him in 1927 to start a speech-department clinic; a pediatrics clinic followed in 1931; a VA aphasia clinic in 1945. Stuttering was the specialty. The dominance theory was the research. Voluntary stuttering — bounce, an “objective attitude” — was the clinical cousin that outlived the theory.

He was born 22 April 1892 in Otisco, Minnesota, and died in 1979 (ASHA obituary, 1980). Carleton College 1916; a year at Yale; an Iowa master’s in 1926; a Wisconsin research year; Minnesota; an Iowa Ph.D. in 1931. Judy Duchan’s pages note work under Wendell Johnson on the doctorate. The sequence is a Midwestern tour of the new field.

## The papers and the rooms

“Sidedness as an Etiological Factor in Stuttering” (1935). The 1937 note with Bernice Rutherford comparing stutterers and nonstutterers. The 1939 *Journal of Speech Disorders* laterality study. Small groups, a theory leaning forward. Rutherford’s name is easy to drop if you only cite Bryngelson. Do not drop it.

He also founded the Minnesota Speech and Hearing Association and served the national association (he was among the early presidents of what became ASHA). The organizational work is why Duchan calls him a key historical figure even if a 2026 student only meets “voluntary stuttering” as a Van Riper or Sheehan noun. Bryngelson was already asking people to stutter on purpose in Minnesota.

## What later people kept

The clinics. The idea that a person can approach a feared word instead of starving it. What they discarded: sidedness as etiology. Einer Boberg, who later stuttered through a Minnesota summer clinic as a young man, is a downstream patient of this campus. That is not a lineage claim. It is a geography.

Do not turn bounce into a weekend assignment from a brand site. Name Rutherford. Name the VA clinic so the vita is not only children.

Minnesota’s speech clinics were a civic fact, not only a laterality fact. A university that starts a pediatrics clinic in 1931 is making a claim about who stammering belongs to: not only the paying adult, not only the psychology graduate student. The claim is incomplete — farm children still had to reach Minneapolis — but it is a different claim from Hunt’s drawing-room. Bryngelson’s rooms are why a Midwestern map of fluency has two dots, Iowa City and the Twin Cities, instead of one.
