This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Emil Fröschels (also Froeschels in American print) coined and organized more than he is now credited for in undergraduate slides. *Logopädie*. The 1924 international association (IALP). A Viennese clinic that treated stuttering as a struggle a person could unlearn, not a tongue a surgeon should shorten. Then 1938, because he was Jewish, and a second career in the United States.

He was born in 1884 and died in 1972. Some web pages mis-place his medical degree in Jena. Vienna 1907 is the better-sourced degree, as the sibling profession pack already flagged. This pack will not “correct” a university website in the body of a magazine piece beyond that sentence. Uncertainty is a fact.

## The work

Before the suitcase: papers and books on voice and speech, a chewing method (*Kaumethode*) meant to make speaking too ordinary to guard, a European reputation. The chew became a cartoon in later American textbooks — people miming mastication. The cartoon is lazy. The historical object is a physician trying to break a feared word by changing the act.

1924 is a congress and a noun. The sibling pack tells the profession’s birth. This profile tells a fluency address. A stammerer could be sent to a named specialty.

After the suitcase: American speech correction received a European founder as a refugee. He taught, wrote in English, and watched a Midwestern field use his noun without always using his city. New York and the émigré medical world are part of the afterlife. So is the fact that IALP had to live with Vienna’s becoming unsafe for the man who had helped to build it.

## What later people kept

The name of the European therapy trade. A permission to treat fear as part of the stammer. The chew, mostly as a museum piece. What they had to keep and sometimes dropped: 1938. A method without an exit visa is a demonstration tape.

Do not assign chewing homework from this page. Do not call him an SLP. He was a physician who founded a therapy field.

American print often spelled him Froeschels because typesetters did not want the ö. Use Fröschels on first mention, as the style guide says, and keep the American spelling when you are citing a U.S. title page. The two spellings are not two men. The 1938 suitcase is not two careers either. It is one vita with a border in the middle.
