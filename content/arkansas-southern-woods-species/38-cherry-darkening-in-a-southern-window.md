Cherry is a clock.

Fresh heart is pale, almost a
blond-pink. Give it ultraviolet
and oxygen and months, and it
goes toward that deep reddish
brown people think they bought.
A Southern window accelerates
the clock. We have more sun,
more hours of it, and houses
that put tables in the light
because the light is the point
of the room. A runner down the
middle of a cherry table will
print a pale ghost in one
summer. A wall-facing side of
a chest will stay young while
the window side ages. Clients
call that a defect. It is the
species.

There is no finish that stops
this completely without also
stopping the look you wanted.
UV inhibitors help. They do
not freeze time. The honest
move is to tell the story
before delivery: this piece
will change, it will change
faster on the sun side, if
you move a lamp you will see
a map. Some people then pick
walnut. Some people like the
map.

Arkansas humidity is not the
main darkening driver. Light
is. Humidity is the movement
driver, and cherry is kind
there if it was dried. Do not
confuse a color change with a
cup. They can happen in the
same season and they are not
the same complaint.

I have left a scrap of the
delivery cherry on the
customer’s sill and a scrap
in a drawer. At the one-year
visit the two scraps are the
conversation. It works better
than a paragraph. The sill
scrap is the future of the
tabletop. The drawer scrap is
the past they thought they
were buying.

Factory cherry from the
catalog era was often stained
to the future color on day
one so the set would match
in the showroom. That is a
valid production choice. It
is also how you get a dead,
even red that never had a
life. I would rather deliver
pale and let Arkansas do the
work. If the set must match
a stained piece already in
the house, then stain, and
say that the new wood will
still go its own way
underneath.

I have tried the old shop
trick of putting the finished
piece in the sun for a week
before delivery to “get ahead
of it.” You get ahead of
nothing. You get a week of
color and then the house
does the next fifty weeks
unevenly. The trick is a
way to make yourself feel
busy. Acclimation for
moisture is real. Tanning
for color is theater.

What does work is rotating
the piece in the first year
if it is a table you can
turn, or accepting a runner
and living with the ghost,
or using the ghost as a
reason to keep the top
bare. Some families like
the pale stripe. It is a
calendar.

Sapwood does not darken on
the same schedule. A sap
streak that is cute in
month one can be a
lightning bolt in year
three. Plan the sap on
purpose or cut it off.
Cherry will not hide your
indecision. A Southern
window will publish it.
