# Arkansas / Southern furniture woods — staged drafts

Series folder for **properties and history** of the five woods that actually show up
in Southern and Arkansas furniture: oak, pine, cypress, walnut, cherry.

These are **drafts**. They are staged for review, not published copy. Each file
stands alone. Read one, or read the lot. They do not share a template beyond
the frontmatter, and they are not meant to.

## Images + figure SEO

Lead **`<figure>`** blocks (species plates + SVG diagrams), `meta_description` on
figure-bearing drafts, and per-file **`RIGHTS.md`** under `plates/<id>/`.

schematics only — no AI-generated wood or tree imagery.

## What “staged” means here

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `stage` | `draft` |
| `status` | `staged` |
| Voice | Human. Shop and place, not catalog SEO. |
| Gate | A number or a place only the real wood / real town can support. |

Do not flatten these into one “species guide.” The point of forty-plus pieces is
that oak in the Ouachitas is not oak on a Crossett invoice, and cherry in the
Boston Mountains is not the Pennsylvania cherry the 1912 bulletin said was
“the country’s commercial supply.”

## Numbers used across the series

These are **handbook-order of magnitude** figures (USDA Forest Products
Laboratory *Wood Handbook*, FPL-GTR-190 and the usual Janka compilations
drawn from it). A single board will lie. A species average is a starting
point, not a promise.

| Wood | Janka (lbf, approx.) | SG @ 12% MC (approx.) | Radial / tangential shrink, green→OD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White oak | 1,360 | 0.68 | 5.6% / 10.5% |
| Northern red oak | 1,290 | 0.63 | 4.0% / 8.6% |
| Southern red oak | ~1,060 | ~0.59 | ~4.7% / 11.3% |
| Cherrybark oak | ~1,480 | higher than most reds | — |
| Shortleaf / loblolly SYP | 690 | 0.51 | 4.6–4.8% / 7.4–7.7% |
| Longleaf pine | 870 | 0.59 | 5.1% / 7.5% |
| Baldcypress | 510–570 | 0.46 | 3.8% / 6.2% |
| Black walnut | 1,010 | 0.55 | 5.5% / 7.8% |
| Black cherry | 950 | 0.50 | 3.7% / 7.1% |

## Sources the drafts lean on

- USDA Forest Products Laboratory, *Wood Handbook* (FPL-GTR-190), Ch. 1 species
  notes and Ch. 4 shrinkage tables.
- J. T. Harris, Hu Maxwell, and Francis Kiefer, *Wood-using Industries and
  National Forests of Arkansas* (1912). Shortleaf as three-quarters of
  manufacturing wood; furniture in the state then mostly red gum and white oak;
  cypress mills already overcutting demand.
- University of Arkansas System Division of Agriculture, FSA5035, *The White
  Oak Resource in Arkansas and the Gulf Region* (white-oak group ~21% of
  hardwood volume to Arkansas mills; tyloses and cooperage).
- Encyclopedia of Arkansas, “Timber Industry”; Crossett Lumber Company;
  Fort Smith furniture (Ott, Meier & Co., 1878; Edward Ballman and later
  companies).
- Purdue Extension FNR-278, black walnut (steaming, sapwood, 1970s price).
- Don C. Bragg, “Cypress Lumbering in Antebellum Arkansas,” *Arkansas Review*
  42 (2011).

If a draft states a number, it should be reconcilable with one of those. If it
states a town, the town should have had a mill, a factory, or a forest that
justifies the sentence.
