# IKEA and the flat-pack table

The dining table that comes home in a flat box — particleboard or veneered composite, cam locks in the seat, a hex key in a bag, legs you screw on — is the Hitchcock chair of the late century: a volume product, a brand, a first table for people who will later buy wood or never will. The INGATORP, the BJURSTA, later the EKEDALEN — names change. The idea does not: a table that ships cheap because you are the factory’s last worker.

This is not a sneer. Flat-pack is an engineering and a class fact. American dining-room history after 1985 that omits it is a collector history.

## Plymouth Meeting and the first American box

The Plymouth Meeting store is the pin — IKEA opened in the United States in 1985 (Plymouth Meeting, Pennsylvania). This page will not invent a first-week sales figure or a ribbon-cutting anecdote. The furniture fact is a warehouse that sold a dining table you put in a car, or in two cars, and finished at home.

Ingvar Kamprad’s Swedish company is older than the American store (founded 1943 in the company’s own telling). The American dining room does not need the founder’s biography. It needs the catalog that taught a generation what a table cost and what it weighed. The catalog is a pattern book. The warehouse path — past the room sets, past the meatballs, to the self-serve racks — is a retail architecture. You pick a flat box the way a 1910 household picked a crate from a depot, except you are also the assembler.

Names of tables change across catalogs. Cite a year when you name a model. INGATORP is an extendable with a gate or a leaf habit that photographs as traditional-modern. BJURSTA is an extendable that a lot of apartments owned. NORDEN is a gateleg, a hall-table idea in birch, the old folding problem solved for a studio. EKEDALEN and MÖRBYLÅNGA are later names in the same job: everyday four, holiday more, a top that is a veneer or a leaf of oak over a constructed core. This page will not invent a price. Prices moved. The box stayed.

## You are the shop

The instruction sheet is a pattern book. The cam lock is a joint. A steel cam rotates in a particleboard or MDF seat and grabs a dowel’s head. Done well, it is a knockdown the Shakers would have understood as a keyed tenon in a different material. Done poorly, or done a second time after a move, it is a stripped socket. The failure mode is a stripped insert in particleboard, which is a wood-movement problem without the wood. You cannot plane it. You can glue it, once. The second move to a new apartment is often the table’s death. That planned shortness of life is a dining-room history. Federal tables moved with the family for a century. This table moves twice.

Extensions: many IKEA tables still grow, with leaves stored in the table or in a closet. The Federal problem, a cheap slide. Alignment is luck. A leaf that is a different batch of paper-grain will show. A leaf stored in a damp basement will swell when the main top has not. Composite does not cup the way flatsawn oak cups; it swells at the edge banding and it sags across a long span if the underframe is a thin rail.

The hex key is a tool mark. Keep it. The drawer or the bag in the underside is where it lives. A table that has lost its key and gained a random hardware-store hex is still the same table. A table that has gained wood screws beside the cams, because someone did not trust the cams, is a document of doubt. That doubt is reasonable.

## What the top is

Particleboard, MDF, honeycomb, a veneer of birch or oak, a paper foil that is a photograph of oak, a plastic laminate. The USDA *Wood Handbook* will tell you what solid oak and walnut do. It will also, in the composite chapters of later editions and in the laboratory’s other papers, tell you what particleboard is: wood particles, resin, a panel that has no grain to quarter. This table is not a species. It is a panel product. Saying “oak” because the foil is oak-colored is the Baltimore painted-rosewood trick in a new century. The ethics are different (no island labor in the veneer photograph, but there is a composite-and-freight labor, and there is a forestry behind the particles). Do not wash the box with a joke about Allen wrenches and stop. Someone assembled it after a shift.

Real veneer over a constructed core is a better IKEA object than a foil. You can see a veneer at the edge, or you can see that the edge is a band of a different material. A solid-wood edge around a composite field is a common compromise: the hand hits wood; the field is stable and cheap. Look at the corner. The band is where dinner chips it.

Birch, in the 1990s tables, is already a period color: pale, a little yellow, a finish that is a thin lacquer. Oak, later, is the farmhouse answer from a Swedish warehouse. The MÖRBYLÅNGA-type oak top — a stained oak veneer, a thick look — is a 2010s object that wants the same photograph as the industrial trestle essay. It is still a box table. The underside will say so.

## What it replaced

The dinette, the garage-sale suite, the unpainted furniture store. It also replaced, for a generation, the idea that a dining table is an heirloom. Some buyers later want the heirloom and find a hardwood shop or an antique. Some never do. Both are American.

It replaced, in apartments, the gateleg that a grandmother would have lent. NORDEN is that grandmother in birch: fold, push against the wall, eat two or four. The hall-table essay is the ancestor. The difference is the expected life and the joint. A 1720 gateleg was a capital good. A flat-pack gateleg is a consumable that remembers the capital good’s job.

It did not replace the formal dining room in houses that still had one. It sat in the kitchen, or in the first apartment, or in the dining room of a house that had stopped believing in mahogany. The formal-fifties suite and the Early American maple lost their children to the warehouse.

## Design, chairs, and the mixed room

IKEA’s better tables are real design at a price: a leg that is a blade, a top that is honest about being thin. The worse tables are a 1990s oval with a gold fleck. The company’s Swedish modern inheritance is closer to Danish oil than to Memphis, cheaper than both. A birch-veneer table from the 1990s is already a period object. Keep one if the cams still hold.

Chairs: the IKEA dining chair is a separate forest (POÄNG is not dining). ADDE, INGOLF, NORDMYRA, and names that will have changed by the time you read this — a wood chair, a plastic chair, a foam pad. The matched set in the warehouse photo is a suite in the Grand Rapids sense. People mix. An IKEA table with leftover captain’s chairs is a document. An IKEA table with Eames shells is a document. An IKEA table with folding chairs is the hall’s honesty.

Labor in the plants is factory labor, often far from Plymouth Meeting. This page will not invent a mill town. The American fact is the store, the box, the evening on the floor with a hex key, the meal the next night.

## How to look

Turn the table over before you buy it used. Count the cams. See whether the sockets are stripped. See whether someone has added blocks or metal plates. See whether the legs are original (the same finish, the same screw pattern) or a later four-by-four. Look at the edge banding. Look at the paper grain: does it wrap in a way no tree would, a repeating cathedral every eighteen inches? That repeat is a foil. A veneer has a more stubborn mismatch.

If the table extends, pull the leaves. Do they match? Is the slide bent? Does the table rack when the leaves are in? A little rack is ordinary. A lot of rack is a core that has loosened from a rail.

If you eat at a flat-pack table, you are in the majority. This series’ mahogany hours are the minority that got photographed. The hex key in the drawer belongs next to the pintle and the Briggs crank: mechanisms that let a table exist in a house that could not otherwise have one. The difference is the expected life. American dining furniture had not been a consumable before, not at this scale. That is the historical claim. Whether a shop table of oak should exist is a later, local question. The box already exists. It held dinner.
