# Bibliography

Working list for the series. Object records beat general surveys. If a draft cites a fact not on this list, the draft should name the source in its own Sources section.

## Rooms and the dining room as a type

- Leslie Susan Berman, *The Evolution of the Nineteenth-Century American Dining Room: From Sitting Room to Separate Room* (M.S. thesis, University of Pennsylvania, 1997). Inventories, builder’s guides, etiquette. Biddle 1805 Plate 37 as first American-authored guide with a labeled dining room and sideboard recess.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, American Wing, Baltimore Room (Gallery 724), Craig townhouse parlor furnished as a dining room since 1924. <https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/collection-areas/the-american-wing/period-rooms/baltimore-room>
- Dumbarton House, “Fran, Have you Supplied the Table?” Federal-era service and the “new, promiscuous way of dining.”
- Commonplace, “The Sideboard Takes Center Stage” — Federal sideboard as a new American form; Eliza Leslie, *The House Book* (1844).
- Owen Biddle, *The Young Carpenter’s Assistant* (Philadelphia, 1805).
- Asher Benjamin, *The Country Builder’s Assistant* (Greenfield, Mass., 1797).
- William Pain, *The Practical House Carpenter* (American issues 1790s).
- Andrew Jackson Downing, *The Architecture of Country Houses* (1850).
- John Claudius Loudon, *Encyclopaedia of Cottage, Farm, and Villa Architecture* (1833) — English influence named in Berman.

## Pattern books and price books

- Thomas Chippendale, *The Gentleman and Cabinet-Maker’s Director* (London, 1754; later eds.).
- George Hepplewhite, *The Cabinet-Maker and Upholsterer’s Guide* (London, 1788).
- Thomas Sheraton, *The Cabinet-Maker and Upholsterer’s Drawing-Book* (London, 1793).
- Thomas Shearer and the *Cabinet-Makers’ London Book of Prices* (1788) — sideboard as a priced form.
- *The London Chair-Makers’ and Carvers’ Book of Prices* (1802; 1808 supplement) — curule and Grecian seating.

## American furniture monographs

- Charles F. Montgomery, *American Furniture: The Federal Period* (Winterthur / Viking, 1966).
- Peter M. Kenny and Michael K. Brown, *Duncan Phyfe: Master Cabinetmaker in New York* (Metropolitan Museum of Art, 2011).
- Patricia Phillips Marshall and Jo Ramsay Leimenstoll, *Thomas Day: Master Craftsman and Free Man of Color* (University of North Carolina Press, 2010).
- Smithsonian American Art Museum / Renwick, *Thomas Day* exhibition record.
- Jonathan L. Fairbanks and Elizabeth Bidwell Bates, *American Furniture, 1620 to the Present*.
- Morrison H. Heckscher, *American Furniture in The Metropolitan Museum of Art, I. Late Colonial Period: The Queen Anne and Chippendale Styles*.
- Ronald L. Hurst and Jonathan Prown, *Southern Furniture 1680–1830: The Colonial Williamsburg Collection*.
- John Bivins and Forsyth Alexander, MESDA publications on Southern furniture.
- Jennifer L. Anderson, *Mahogany: The Costs of Luxury in Early America* (Harvard, 2012).
- Adam Bowett, *The English Mahogany Trade 1700–1793* / *Georgian Group Journal* (1997).
- Chipstone, *American Furniture* annual, including “The Missing Chapter in the Life of Thomas Day” (2013).
- Winterthur Museum object records and *Winterthur Portfolio*.

## Objects named in drafts

- Met 31.44.15, Pembroke table, attributed to the workshop of Duncan Phyfe, New York, 1810–20. Mahogany, brass, tulip poplar, white pine.
- Met 22.98, drop-leaf Pembroke, Phyfe workshop, 1810–20. Mahogany, white pine, cherry.
- Met 1971.160, sideboard table, attributed to Duncan Phyfe, ca. 1815. Mahogany, marble.
- Brooklyn Museum 1997.150.15a–c, dining table attributed to Duncan Phyfe, ca. 1805. Mahogany; beech.
- MESDA 3163, sideboard table, Charleston, 1750–1765. Mahogany, cypress, marble.
- North Carolina Museum of History, Thomas Day sideboard for Caleb H. Richmond, mahogany and veneer, yellow pine, tulip poplar, walnut.
- Gustav Stickley, Bungalow Trestle Table, model no. 445, ca. 1901; Dining Table no. 631 (idea 1901; numbered ca. 1904). David Cathers; Stickley Project.

## Factory, patents, modern

- Derin Bray and Dennis Carr, “The Ingenious Patent Extension Tables of Cornelius Briggs,” *Antiques and Fine Arts* (2010). Briggs patent 1843; MFA Boston related table.
- Robert Jupe, British patent 6788 (March 1835), expanding circular table; Johnstone, Jupe & Co. / Johnstone & Jeanes.
- Henry Ford, “The Hitchcock Chair: An American Innovation”; Connecticut mills record for the 1825 Hitchcocksville factory.
- Knoll / KnollStudio, Saarinen Pedestal Collection, designed 1956–57, continuous production from 1956. “Slum of legs.”
- University of Delaware, Material Matters, “Hunt Boards: Colonial Myth and Colonial Revival Reality” (2017) — the name is twentieth-century.
