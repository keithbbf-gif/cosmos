# Wood slides versus steel

Wood slides are a conversation with
the house. Steel slides are a
conversation with a factory.

A finger-jointed hardwood slide,
cambered, waxed, sized to the
opening, will tell you when the
air is dry. It will drag in
January and run like a gossip in
June. That drag is information. It
is also, if you live in a tight
house with forced air, a reason
people start to hate the table.
Wax is the truce. So is a little
extra clearance you cut on
purpose, knowing February is
coming.

Steel — ball-bearing, geared, the
long zinc telescopes — does not
care about February. It cares
about parallel. If you mount it
true, it is a gift to one-person
operation. If you mount it out,
it is a drawer that never forgets
your mistake. It also sounds like
an office. I have sat at walnut
that whispered and steel that
announced every guest like a
filing cabinet. You can damp it.
You cannot make it become oak.

Weight capacity is the steel
argument I accept. Long openings,
heavy tops, pedestal splits: wood
has a span limit and a creep. The
specialty houses will sell you
longer wood, cambered harder. At
some length I would rather a
geared metal pair and a wooden
world around it. Hybrid is not a
vice.

What I will not accept is steel
screwed into particle web with
no way to retighten. That is a
disposable conversation. Wood
screwed into wood can be
serviced with a screwdriver and
a curse. Design the access. If
I have to remove the top to
reach a slide screw, you have
designed a service out of the
life of the object.

Sound, climate, service, span.
Pick the slide for those four,
not for the decade the catalog
is trying to inhabit. A 1960s
oak flip-leaf wants wood. A
10-foot walnut banquet that
opens another 4 wants steel and
no apology. A little Pembroke
wants neither. It wants a
knuckle hinge and a bracket.
Do not buy a slide for a
problem you do not have.
