# Hardware you are supposed to forget

Good iron disappears into the meal.

I do not mean hidden at all costs. I mean
it does not become the conversation. Pins
that start without a hunt. A lock you can
find with a hand while talking. Slides
that do not announce each roller. A
folding leaf that does not require a
lecture.

Bad hardware is charismatic. It clicks in
a cheap way. It has a badge. It needs an
Allen key that lived in a drawer that
was later used for napkins. It is a
different color from the rest of the
underside, a zinc island in a wood sea,
and every time you drop a fork you see
it.

There is a fashion for exposing
mechanisms as jewelry — brass racks,
visible gears, a capstan you are
supposed to admire. Jupe's brass boss
was a claim of patent, not a lifestyle.
If you expose the machine, it has to
be as well-made as the top. A pretty
gear next to a drywall screw is a
joke the table tells on its maker.

I specify finishes on hardware the way
I specify finishes on wood. Steel that
will see hands gets wax or a real
plating, not a shop smear. Wood slides
get the same oil family as the
undercarriage, not a random varnish
that will squeak. Screws are driven
to a depth and then ignored; if they
are a design element you have already
lost.

The test is Thanksgiving with new
people. If anyone has to go under the
table, the hardware has failed the
social job even if it has not failed
the plane. Children will find every
catch. Design catches that can survive
being found.

A hand should find the catch in the
dark. If I have to look, I have
already left the conversation. Put
the lock on the same side of every
table you build, and put a small
notch where the fingers go. Repeating
yourself is how hardware becomes
habit instead of a puzzle.

Forgettable is not sloppy. Forgettable
is the highest compliment I give a
slide. You remember the people. The
table did not interrupt. That is
design.
