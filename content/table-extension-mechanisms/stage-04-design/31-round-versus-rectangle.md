# Circles do not add seats the way catalogs say

A rectangle adds length. You can point at the
new inches and know where the new plates go:
two on the sides per leaf, if the leaf is
wide enough, plus the old heads.

A circle adds circumference. The plates are
wedges. The conversation is better. The
arithmetic is worse.

Six around a 150–160 cm table is a human
dinner. Twelve around 230 cm is a meeting.
The catalog will write 6–12 as if the
experience scaled. It does not. At twelve
the person across the roast is a rumor.
You have gained seats and lost the reason
people wanted a round table, which was to
be in one conversation.

Seams multiply. A rectangle with one leaf
has two joints, or one if you are a
draw-leaf and the drop hides the story.
A Jupe with eight segments and eight
wedges is a clock face of joints. Some
will land under plates. That is not a
defect. It is the shape.

Serving is different. A rectangular leaf
gives you a runway for platters down the
middle. A grown circle gives you a larger
middle and a longer reach. Lazy Susans
appear, which are another mechanism, and
they fight with filling pieces if anyone
was not paying attention.

I pick a circle when the room is square
and the household hates hierarchy. No
heads. No foot. The Jupe idea belongs to
that ethic — the host not exiled to an
end. I pick a rectangle when the room is
a galley or the count has to swing by
four without becoming a conference.

Place settings on a grown circle also
fight serving dishes. The middle is
farther from every hand. Platters
orbit or they stack. A rectangular
leaf at least gives you a street
down the center. I have watched a
round holiday become a pass-the-
bowl religion because nobody could
reach.

Do not use a circle to solve a seating
number you are ashamed of. The shame will
still be there, just closer to your
neighbors' elbows. And do not use a
rectangle with twelve leaves to imitate
fellowship. You will have a refectory
and two conversations that cannot hear
each other. Shape is not a moral. It is
a count and a room. Start there, then
buy the crank.
