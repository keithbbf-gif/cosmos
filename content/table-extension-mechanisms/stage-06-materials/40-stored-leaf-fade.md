# The closet keeps a different sun

Ultraviolet is a finish. The dining
room applies it every day. The
closet does not.

A stored leaf comes out younger.
Cherry is the loud example: the
top goes toward that deep red-
brown, the leaf is still the
blond it was when you paid for
it. Walnut goes cooler and
quieter in the light; the leaf
stays chocolate and obvious.
Oak yellows. Mahogany does
whatever the last century's
finish allowed. People call this
a defect of the leaf. It is a
defect of time and of a room
with a window.

Self-storing does not save you.
The undercarriage is also a
closet, just a warmer one. The
leaf sleeps on its side or
folded and never sees the same
sky. Five years of Tuesday sun
on the top is a different
career.

I have two honest responses and
one dishonest one. Honest: let
the mismatch be, and stop
bookmatching as if you could
fool the future. Honest: store
the leaf in the room, standing,
face to the same window, and
accept the clutter. Dishonest:
stain the leaf darker on the
day of the party. It will be
wrong in a new way, and next
year you will stain it again,
and at some point you will have
a leaf that is a painting of a
table.

Some old houses stood extra
leaves behind a door in the
dining room itself. Ugly. Kind.
The boards aged in public. If
you cannot stand the sight of a
leaf at rest, you may not get
to stand the sight of a leaf at
work.

There is a small mercy in oil
and wax. They do not plasticize
the difference. Film finishes
lock a color and then the sun
works on the film. When you
finally unfold, you are
comparing two different
varnishes as well as two
different woods. I would rather
oil a table I am willing to
re-oil than pretend a catalyzed
coat is a time machine.
