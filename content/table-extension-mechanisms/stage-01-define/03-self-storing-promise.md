# What self-storing actually promises

Self-storing is a storage claim. That is all.

It says: when you do not need the extra board, the table still
knows where the board lives. You will not find it in six months
behind the guest-room ironing board. You will not leave it in the
old house. You will not commission a replacement that almost
matches.

Those are real goods. Fine Woodworking has published more than one
maker who took a job building the lost leaves. I have watched a
family eat for a decade at 72 inches because the 18-inch board
lived at the other parent's apartment. Self-storing is an answer
to a social problem, not a joinery problem.

It does not promise a plane.

A folding center leaf can store beautifully and still arrive a
hair proud, or a hair starved, or with its hinge line prouder than
either half. The keys that should register it can miss because
the top halves did not open square. The pivot pins can wear. The
shelf that holds the folded leaf can sag until the next opening
is a fight. All of that can be true while the brochure still
says self-storing, which it still is.

It does not promise one-person operation.

Some butterflies do. Some want two hands and a hip. Draw-leaves
are self-storing in the oldest sense — the extra tops live under
the main one — and they still want a clear run at both ends and a
person who understands the drop. Jupe's filling pieces were
stored in a *fitted case*. That is self-storing if you keep the
case in the room. It is a second object if you don't. I have seen
the case used as a sideboard. That is luck, not design.

It does not promise the stored face will age with the working
face.

A board that lives in the dark keeps its color. The table that
lives under the window does not. Five years later you unfold a
younger leaf into an older top. Self-storing made the mismatch
inevitable. The closet leaf at least had a chance of living in
similar light if someone stood it against a wall. I am not
praising closets. I am saying the storage claim and the finish
claim point opposite ways, and almost no catalog mentions it.

So when I write self-storing I mean: the extra surface has a
designed home in the undercarriage or in a case that belongs to
the table. I do not mean good. I do not mean easy. I do not mean
matched. Those are other sentences, and they need other proofs —
a plane, a hand count, a winter.

If a maker wants credit for more than storage, they can show me
the keys, the equalizer, the camber, and a photograph of the same
table closed, because that is where the extra board spends its
life, folded in the dark, waiting to embarrass Thanksgiving.
