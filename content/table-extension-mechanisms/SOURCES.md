# Sources and what I will not pretend

Drafts. Facts I leaned on, and where I
stopped. Not a bibliography for show.

## Objects and dates I will stand on

- Rijksmuseum BK-NM-5850, draw-leaf
  (*trektafel*), Netherlands, c. 1600–1625.
  Closed about 170 cm; open about 320 cm.
  The top drops between the withdrawn
  leaves. Museum object page.
- Robert Jupe, British patent 6788, sealed
  11 March 1835. Expanding table whose
  sections diverge from a common centre;
  gaps filled by leaves or filling pieces.
  *Nature* "Calendar of Patent Records"
  (1929) restates the grant.
- Jupe at 47 Welbeck Street; work with
  John Johnstone; later Johnstone & Jeanes;
  brass bosses. Sotheby's, Thomas Williams,
  "The Marvellous Mechanics of Jupe,
  Johnstone & Jeanes" (2019). One cited
  table: about 160–229 cm, six to twelve,
  sixteen filling pieces in two sizes, fitted
  case.
- Jupe v. Pratt, 6 December 1836; drawings
  at Kew, MFC 1/42. Furniture History
  Society newsletter 209 (February 2018).
  I do not invent the verdict.
- Gateleg: English form by the later
  sixteenth / seventeenth century; spider-leg
  later eighteenth. Britannica, "gateleg
  table" / "drop-leaf table."
- Pembroke: word in circulation by 1778;
  Sheraton, *Cabinet Dictionary*, 1803,
  names a lady who ordered one. Rival
  attribution to Henry Herbert, 9th Earl.
  I do not referee.
- Sofa table: end flaps, c. 1790s–Regency.
  Britannica; trade guides.
- New York oval falling-leaf tables: Peter
  M. Kenny / Chipstone, "Flat Gates,
  Draw-Bars, Twists, and Urns" (1994),
  including the 1724 Van Cortlandt
  inventory note.
- Butterfly as New England wing bracket:
  late seventeenth century; American, not
  an English import, in the old university
  extension furniture bulletins.
- Butterfly as folding center leaf: later
  shop practice; Fine Woodworking project
  essays on pivot, keys, shelf.
- Concertina card tables: early eighteenth
  century England; apron expands so a leg
  stays at each corner.
- Berlage trektafel, Amsterdam, 1902,
  made by J. B. Hillen. Rijksmuseum.

## Mechanisms I have used with my hands

Equalizer slides, drop-in pins, flip-leaves
in the Danish/IKEA family, gatelegs,
ordinary split-pedestal store tables. Shop
sizes for Rockler equalizers (28 / 38 inch)
are trade sizes, not endorsements.

## What I will not pretend

- That "wake table" is a documented
  coffin specification. Use is real.
  Origin-as-coffin is dealer weather
  until a primary source shows up.
- That mid-century never extended. It
  often refused. Exceptions exist.
- That I have handled the Johnson or
  Van Cortlandt tables. I have read.
- Exact seating numbers on any one Jupe
  without the plates on the table.
- Legal novelty of any current radial
  copy. This folder is not counsel and
  not a filing.
