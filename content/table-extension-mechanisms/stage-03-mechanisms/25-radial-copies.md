# Lotus tables after Jupe

Call them lotus, radial, expanding round, Jupe-
style. The motion is the same pie.

Segments ride on slides that point at the
center. A capstan, a cam, a set of arms, or
now a motor, drives them out together. The
circle gets gaps. You insert wedges. Sometimes
there are two sets of wedges for two diameters.
Sometimes a ring of extra wood is hinged and
flops out, which is a different, usually worse,
idea: the rim becomes a set of flaps with
seams that want to telegraph.

The copies that work still obey Jupe's second
clause. The spaces are filled by pieces you
can hold. Those pieces have a home. The home
is a case, a drawer, a closet the designer
admitted. I do not accept "the leaves store
underneath" on a round table unless I have
seen the underneath. Round undercarriages are
full of geometry already. Cramming sixteen
wedges in there is how you get a rattle and
a missing small set.

Handedness matters more than on rectangles.
A wedge has a narrow end and a finish that
was meant to continue a particular grain
path. Number them. I like discreet stamps on
the underside, not romance. If the shop
bookmatches around the circle, the numbers
are the only way a tired host will get the
sunburst back.

The mechanism's job is equal travel. If one
segment lags, the gaps are unequal and the
wedges fight. That is the radial version of
the grin. You feel it as a wedge that needs
a shove in one slot and falls through another.
Do not plane the wedge in the dining room.
Fix the lag.

I am cool to motorized lotuses that still
make you handle the wedges. You have
automated the easy half — the turning — and
left the host with an armful of awkward
boards. If you motorize, motorize a design
that does not need a second object, or own
the ritual and make the case beautiful
enough to live in the room. Johnstone &
Jeanes at least stamped the box.

A circle that grows is still a circle that
must be leveled, seated, and forgiven for
its many seams. Do not sell it as magic.
Sell it as a pie with a crank and a case.
That is already enough.
