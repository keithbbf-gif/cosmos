# The skirt has to grow

Open a split-top table and look from a chair,
not from above.

The top is now longer. The apron — the skirt
that made the closed table look like a
finished box — is still the old length, or
it has broken into two short aprons with a
dark gap between them. Through that gap you
see slides, screws, maybe a folding leaf
edgewise like a book. The table looks gutted.
Light from the opposite window makes the gap
a lantern. I have sat through otherwise good
meals staring at that hole, thinking about
the underside of furniture like it was a
wound.

A telescoping apron is the dining version of
the concertina. Extra skirt travels out of
the main skirt, or a center apron piece
rides up with the leaf, or hinged blades
fold into place. The job is optical and
social: hide the machine, keep the box.
The job is also structural. An apron is a
beam. When you cut it to let slides through,
you owe the top some of that beam back.

Cheap tables skip it. The photograph is
taken from high enough that the gap is a
shadow. In a real room the chairs are low
and the guests have eyes. If you can see
the equalizer, the equalizer should have
been designed as a visible object, not as
a secret that failed.

I like a center leaf that carries its own
short apron, pinned so the line of the
skirt continues. The line will not be
perfect. A small reveal is honest. A
two-inch void is not. On round tables the
problem becomes a ring: the rim must grow
or you get a pie with a thin crust and a
visible tin.

Closed, the telescoping bits have to
disappear without making the skirt thick
as a trunk. That is a drawing problem.
Some shops double the apron and accept the
weight. Some cut the inner runners so fine
they rack. I would rather a slightly heavy
closed table than a light one that flashes
its underwear when company comes.

If you cannot grow the skirt, change the
design language. A trestle with open sides
does not promise a box. A mid-century slab
on a pedestal does not either — until you
add a leaf and suddenly everyone can see
you were pretending. Pick a language that
can survive the open state. The skirt is
not trim. It is the table's face when
people are sitting, which is the only time
the face is actually used.
