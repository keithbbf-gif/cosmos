# Where the legs go when the top grows

An extension is also a story about the floor.

Four legs, closed, sit under four corners. Open the
top along its length and the corners travel. The
legs either travel with their half of the top, or
they stay and the top slides over them. Those are
different dinners.

If the legs travel, the long sides in the middle
have no post. Sitters are happy. The leaves are a
bridge. You now owe stiffness or a fifth leg or a
sag bar. If the legs stay, the overhangs grow at
the ends and someone at the head is sitting in a
cantilever. The middle sitters may find a leg
where the extra leaf created a new place setting.
You have moved the conflict, not deleted it.

Pedestals rewrite the story. Two stems part. The
long sides stay clean. The middle span is still a
bridge, often a worse one, because a stem is a
smaller patch of support than a four-leg frame.
Trestles are the cousin: two walls of wood that
travel or stay, with the same span math.

A hybrid I respect, when it is honest, is the
stationary center leg on a five-leg table, with
slides that let the ends run out. The center
keeps the leaves from becoming a trampoline. The
ends take the new sitters. You have admitted
that the machine has a middle. Victorian shops
knew this. Some modern catalogs hide the fifth
leg until it drops, as if shame were a finish.

Round pedestals are a special case. One stem
under a Jupe or a lotus has to stay while the
top grows around it. The stem is the world
axis. If that axis is not stiff, the whole
circle rocks. If the feet do not spread enough
for the large diameter, the open table is a
wine glass. I have sat at one that felt brave
closed and drunk open. The carving was
excellent. The footprint was a closed-table
footprint.

Choose the floor story before you choose the
leaf. If the room needs clean long sides —
narrow chairs, kids who kick — pedestals or
traveling four-legs. If the room needs a
stiff middle for a heavy roast on a wide
leaf, keep something under the roast. Do not
ask walnut figure to hold a pot.
