# Swing the support, steal a sitter

A gate is a successful support and a failed chair.

The leaf wants a vertical near its free edge. The
sitter wants that same volume for thighs. You cannot
give the volume to both. Every gateleg is a
negotiation, and most of them were negotiated in favor
of the leaf, because a sagging flap is visible from
the door and a bruised knee is only reported by the
guest who already feels like an extra.

Place the pivot as far toward the corner as the
stretchers allow, and the gate supports the leaf near
the end — structurally kind, socially cruel to the
head-of-table seat. Place it toward the middle, and
the long flap becomes a diving board with a post under
its ribs. Two gates per flap fix the dive and double
the theft.

I swing a gate with the leaf still down, first, to
see where it wants to live. Then I lift the leaf and
watch the last ten degrees. If the gate has to be
kicked into a notch, it will be kicked forever, and
the notch will wear into a suggestion. If a spring
catch or a hook takes it, someone has to know the
hook exists. Undocumented hooks are how leaves fall
during the toast.

Knee maps are not academic. Sit in every position
with the table open before you sell the thing. The
center seats along the fixed board are the good ones.
The seats over the gates are the punishment seats.
If your holiday count requires those seats, you chose
the wrong mechanism. Concertina and pedestal exist
because this map is ugly.

There is a smaller mercy: a swinging bracket instead
of a full gate. The wing butterfly, the Pembroke
knuckle, the iron stay. They steal less floor and
less shin. They also carry less leaf. Use them on
tea-width flaps. Do not use them on a harvest leaf
unless you like the sound of a hinge asking for help.

I will not design a dining gateleg for a room that
already has a proper dining table. I will design one
for a room that *is* the dining table on some nights
and a passage on others. In that room the stolen
sitter is the price of having the room back on
Tuesday. Price it honestly. Do not call it seats
eight.
