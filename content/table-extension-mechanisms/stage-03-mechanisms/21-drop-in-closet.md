# Loose leaves are a second piece of furniture

A drop-in leaf looks like the simplest mechanism in
the room. Open the slides. Set the board in the gap.
Close on the pins. Eat.

The simplicity is a lie about storage.

That board is another object. It has width, a finish,
pins or holes, maybe a skirt of its own. It wants a
climate that resembles the table's. It wants not to
be used as a shelf in the closet. It wants not to
live in a car trunk from November to January. It
wants a bag if you insist on stacking two, because
finished faces against each other will print.

I treat a set of loose leaves as a small cabinet
project. A press, a rack, a fitted case in the Jupe
sense. If the house will not give them a home, do
not specify loose leaves. Specify a motion that
keeps the extra surface in the table, and accept
the mechanical cost.

The drop-in ritual is also a two-hand, often
two-person job once the leaf is 18 by 42 and solid
oak. People drop the far edge onto a pin and chew
the hole. They put the leaf in upside down because
one face was stored against the wall and looks
safer. They leave a pin standing and close the
table on it. I have seen a pin-shaped bruise in
an otherwise kind mahogany, a little oval of
compressed grain, the fossil of a Thanksgiving.

Alignment is not free just because the leaf is
loose. Pins should be short enough to start after
the faces are close, long enough to actually
register. Three pins across a wide table beat two.
A single center pin is how you get a leaf that
winds. If the slides are not equalized, the gap
is a parallelogram and the leaf will not go in
without a fight. People then plane a corner in
the hallway. That is a scar you can date to a
specific holiday if the family still argues about
it.

I am not against drop-ins. They let you add length
in steps. They let you take the table back to a
closed seam that can be very quiet. They let a
shop build a beautiful closed object and a set of
boards from the same flitch. I am against treating
them as "no mechanism." The mechanism is the
slides, the pins, the case, and the culture that
remembers where the case is.

If that culture is weak — renters, moves, two
households, a leaf that already vanished once —
do not repeat the same optimism. Build the storage
into the table or admit you are designing a pair.
