# Long boards and a story that sells

America likes a long table. Harvest, farm, wake — the
words are doing social work.

A harvest table, in shop talk, is often a narrow six- or
eight-footer with long drop leaves on the sides, so it
can live against a wall and come out for the crew. That
part I believe. Rural rooms were passages. A permanent
wide table would have been an insult to walking. The
leaves are the harvest. The closed object is the hallway.

"Wake table" is muddier. Dealers will tell you the Irish
or Irish-American drop-leaf was built so a coffin could
rest on it, leaves up, body in the parlor. Sometimes the
story adds that the table is the length of a man. I have
not found a shop record I trust that says *make it for
the corpse*. I have found a lot of surfaces that could
hold one, because a dining table that seats a family will,
as a matter of grim geometry, hold a box.

I will not repeat the coffin story as origin. I will
repeat it as sales copy that attached itself to a real
form: a rectangular drop-leaf, often with swinging
supports, often a little severe, the kind of object a
house already owned when someone died. Using the dining
table for a wake is a use. It is not a patent.

The long American board without leaves belongs here only
as a refusal. A fixed harvest slab does not extend. It
occupies. That is a different contract with the room.
Some households chose it and moved chairs forever. Some
chose the flaps. The flaps are the mechanism. The word
"harvest" is the mood.

What I take from this family is scale and bluntness.
These are not Pembrokes. The leaves are long. Sag is not
theoretical. A twelve-inch bracket that will hold a
tea-flap will laugh and fail under a six-foot leaf with
two relatives leaning. You need swing arms with a decent
bearing, or a gate, or a draw-bar, and you need the leaf
itself to be stiff — breadboard, battens, a decent
thickness. Gravity is the critic.

If a dealer says wake, I look at the hinges and the
supports, not the story. If the leaf is original and the
rule joint still meets, that is history. If the story is
the only intact part, that is a different trade.
