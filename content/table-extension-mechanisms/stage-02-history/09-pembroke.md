# A breakfast table that learned manners

Pembroke is a drop-leaf that went to town.

Small rectangular top, sometimes oval if you are lucky and
rich. A flap on each long side, held by hinged brackets, not
usually by a full gate. A drawer in the frieze. Legs that
want to be square-tapered or a pillar-and-claw if the date
has moved. You can eat breakfast on it, write a letter, deal
cards, set a tea service. Jane Austen, in the unfinished
*The Watsons*, sits a woman at "the best Pembroke table,
with the best tea-things." That is the job: the best small
surface in a room that has several.

The name is a fight I will not referee. Sheraton, *Cabinet
Dictionary*, 1803: a breakfast table named for the lady who
first ordered one. Other mouths give it to Henry Herbert,
9th Earl of Pembroke. BADA will tell you both stories.
Neither story is the mechanism. The mechanism is a pair of
flaps and a pair of brackets on a table light enough to live
against a wall and come out for company.

What matters historically is the scale shift. A gateleg is a
dining answer. A Pembroke is a room answer. It extends
across its width, not enough to seat a harvest crew, enough
to turn a lamp table into a two-person meal. The flaps are
short. The brackets are short. Knee conflict shrinks. You
can sit without marrying a gate.

By 1778 the word is in the dictionary. The 1760s are when
dealers like to start the form. I treat those as trade
dates, not gospel. What I trust is the social slot: late
Georgian houses had too many specialized tables, and this
one survived because it could do more than one specialty.

Oval Pembrokes cost more now because they always did. An oval
with flaps is a drawing problem. The flap edge has to know
the curve. The closed silhouette has to remain an oval, not
a rectangle with bitten sides. When it works, the open table
is a little pond. When it fails, you see the joints as
chords across a circle, which is a sad thing to look at
while you butter toast.

I include Pembroke in a dining-extension series because it
teaches a limit. Not every extension is trying to get to
twelve. Some are trying to get to two without owning a
second table. That is a more common modern problem than we
admit. A city apartment that wants guests twice a month does
not need a Jupe. It needs a polite flap and a bracket that
does not rattle.
