# The Ice Man's Two Mushrooms

In September 1991 two walkers on the Tisenjoch, a saddle between the Ötztal and the Schnalstal, saw a brown back sticking out of ice. The body they had found would be named Ötzi in the papers. He had died in the late fourth millennium BCE with an arrowhead in his left shoulder, a fresh cut on his hand, and — this is the part the pharmacy histories like — two walnut-sized pieces of birch polypore strung on leather thongs among his kit.

The fungus is *Fomitopsis betulina* (the older labels say *Piptoporus betulinus*). It grows on birch. It takes a spark. It also contains compounds later chemists would isolate and argue over. None of that tells you what the man thought he was carrying.

## A body with an inventory

Ötzi is famous because the ice kept more than bone. There was a copper axe, a yew bow, a quiver with unfinished arrows, a retoucher, tinder fungus of another species (*Fomes fomentarius*) in a belt pouch, grains, ibex, sloe. The stomach and intestines later gave pollen and whipworm (*Trichuris trichiura*). The last meal was a heavy, fatty one. The arrow was not a metaphor.

The birch-polypore pieces hung separately, like charms or like tools you did not want mixed with food. Early reports reached for medicine because the man had parasites and because European folk use of the same fungus includes purgatives and wound dressing. That leap is understandable. It is still a leap. A lump that helps you start a fire on a wet pass is already worth the weight.

## What the fungus can do, and what we cannot know

Birch polypore is not inert. Twentieth-century and later work on the species describes acids and other metabolites; folk European use includes styptic and anthelmintic applications. Laboratory activity against some organisms in a dish is not a Copper Age clinical trial. The whipworm finding made the anthelmintic story almost irresistible in the 1990s press. A man can carry a bitter fungus because his grandmother said to, because it lights, because it is light, or because the thong was already there.

The honest sentence is: **the intent is not labeled.** Archaeology gives association, not a package insert. The same restraint belongs on the tattoos — groups of marks over joints and the lumbar region that invited acupuncture comparisons. Comparisons are not protocols. They are how modern eyes try to keep a dead man busy.

## Deep time is not a pharmacopeia

It is tempting to start every herbal history with "humans have always used plants as medicine," then produce Ötzi as Exhibit A. He is a worse exhibit than that sentence wants. He is one man, on one route, in one season, with a kit assembled for a crossing that failed. Paleolithic residues on stone tools, dental calculus with starch and poppy, charred chenopods in hearths, the Shanidar flower-burial argument (now a much quieter file than the 1970s textbooks), the Neolithic poppy finds in central Europe — those widen the picture and still do not add up to a *De materia medica*.

What the find does smash is the cartoon of prehistoric life as meat and flint. People in the Alps four thousand years before the common era were already sorting fungi by job: one for tinder in the pouch (*Fomes fomentarius*, the hoof fungus, a known amadou), another on a string. Sorting is the beginning of pharmacy even when the shop has not been invented. The South Tyrol Museum's catalogs, and the 1990s papers that named the polypore, are the file. Reconstruction documentaries are not.

## After the thaw

The South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology in Bolzano holds the body and the objects. Reconstructions of his face go around the world on posters; they are artists' arguments. The polypores are easier. They look like what they are. If this series needs an origin image, use the fungus and the pass, not a wax man in a fur cape.

Later essays in this pack deal with texts: clay, papyrus, Sanskrit, Chinese, Greek. Texts lie in their own way — they claim a plant for a fever because a school says so. Ötzi cannot lie about a school. He can only leave mass and a thong. That is why he belongs first. Before there is a canon, there is a pocket.

**Educational note.** Nothing in the kit is an instruction for treating parasites or wounds. Birch polypore can be toxic or simply useless in untrained hands; this essay does not describe preparation.
