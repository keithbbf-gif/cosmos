# The Poppy's Long Administrative Life

The opium poppy is a farm plant. That is the fact that moral history keeps trying to outrun. *Papaver somniferum* has been grown for seed, oil, and latex across a belt from the Mediterranean through Persia and India into China. Ancient authors knew the juice as a sleep and a killer. Islamic physicians wrote it into formularies. Paracelsus and Sydenham had their laudanums. English cottages had Godfrey's Cordial and Mrs. Winslow's Soothing Syrup — opium for children, sold as kindness.

The plant did not become a problem when it became a vice. It became a problem when empires needed a revenue and then a repentance.

## Latex, not legend

Scoring a capsule to collect latex is an agricultural act this essay will not teach. Historically, the dried cake moved as "Turkey opium," "Bengal opium," "Persian," grades that a London chemist could smell. Pharmacopeias specified tests. Those tests were the ancestor of both quality control and the later forensic lab. Dioscorides already distinguished kinds and warned. Warning and selling have always been compatible.

## Laudanum as household furniture

Sydenham's laudanum (opium in wine, with his own aromatics) was a physician's tool. The nineteenth-century British and American bottle was a grocery. Dorothy and William Wordsworth's circle, Coleridge, the Countess of Lovelace, countless unnamed women with "nerves": the literary file is crowded and unrepresentative. The representative file is the working household with a crying infant and a cheap syrup. James Harvey Young's patent-medicine books are ruthless on this. The 1906 U.S. label law (next essays) is partly a poppy law.

## Wars and treaties

The British East India Company's Bengal opium, auctioned at Calcutta and run into China against Qing law, is the largest botanical-revenue story of the nineteenth century. The two Opium Wars (1839–42, 1856–60) were not fought because a plant is wicked. They were fought because a company-state wanted silver and a market, and because a court in Beijing wanted the smoking houses closed. The Treaty of Nanking and the later legalization of the trade under duress are pharmacy history if you think pharmacy includes the port.

Lin Zexu's 1839 destruction of chests at Humen is the Chinese civic monument; the later republican and PRC eradication campaigns are the long remainder. Indian peasant growers, Persian merchants, Ottoman poppy fields, and the twentieth-century Golden Triangle are other chapters of the same crop. Courtwright's *Forces of Habit* puts opium next to alcohol, tobacco, caffeine as a commodity with a career. The 1909 Shanghai Commission, the 1912 Hague convention, and the 1961 Single Convention tried to make the career a permit. Permits create smugglers and they create medical shortages. Both outcomes are in the minutes.

A hospice in 2020s America can still run short of oral morphine while a treaty system congratulates itself. That sentence is not an argument for a kitchen garden. It is an argument that administration has side effects.

## Morphine, heroin, the schedule

Sertürner's crystal (essay 23) and Wright's 1874 diacetylmorphine, later Bayer's Heroin, split the poppy into products with different speeds. Harrison (1914) and then the Controlled Substances Act (1970) in the U.S. folded the medical and the illicit into one police-and-doctor system. A hospice ward and a street market share a plant and do not share a world. History that pretends they do is propaganda. History that pretends they do not share a molecule is sentiment.

## China, the pipe, and the pharmacopeia

Qing officials distinguished medical cake from smoking paste more carefully than later Western cartoons did. The pipe is a Qing-and-after social technology, not an ancient eternal. A London monograph of powdered opium and a Fuzhou smoking house share a plant and do not share a ritual. Republican and PRC campaigns then made the plant a nation-building enemy, which is another administrative life — eradication as patriotism.

## How to write the poppy without a wink

No "ancient painkiller of emperors" perfume. No instructions. No slang tutorial. Name the statute, the war, the bottle, the pharmacopeia monograph. The poppy's administrative life is the life of forms: customs, prescriptions, treaties, exemption certificates for research. Forms are less pretty than petals. They are the truth of how a medicine becomes a controlled substance without the plant changing its mind.
