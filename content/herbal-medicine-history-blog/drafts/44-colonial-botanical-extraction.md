# Ships, Gardens, and the Botanical Ledger

The Banda islands produced nutmeg. The Dutch East India Company produced a monopoly and, in 1621, a massacre. That pairing — a spice that was also a drug in the Galenic shop, and a corporate state that wanted the tree in only one set of hands — is the extreme, honest form of the economic-botany story. Most transfers were quieter: a Wardian case of seedlings, a Kew memorandum, a Spanish *protomedicato* list, a French Jardin des Pépinières. The quiet ones still belong in the ledger.

This essay is the index to a pattern the plant-by-plant pieces have already shown. Cinchona to Java. Rubber to Malaya (not a medicine first, but the same office — Wickham's 1876 seed story is the popular one; the quieter Dutch and Kew correspondences are the truer machine). Tea as diet and as nervous drug, moved from China to Assam and Ceylon by men who thought a leaf could break a monopoly. Coca's later isolate. Orta's Goa as a conversational warehouse. Padua and Chelsea as domestic catalogs; Kew as an imperial one. The Jardin du Roi / Jardin des Plantes in Paris, Buitenzorg on Java, Calcutta's botanic garden under Roxburgh and Wallich: each was a node. A node collects. Collection is not innocent when a navy is attached.

## What a ledger knows

A ledger knows weight, price, grade, port, loss to rot. It does not know a teacher. Schiebinger's *Plants and Empire* is the book that keeps looking for the teacher anyway — the enslaved woman, the unnamed informant, the abortifacient (*peacock flower*, *Poinciana / Caesalpinia pulcherrima*, in her most famous case) that did not transfer into European obstetrics because the empire did not want that particular knowledge to travel. Non-transfer is part of extraction. Some knowledge was left behind on purpose. The ledger's silence about a teacher is not an empty cell. It is a decision.

Spanish *relaciones* and the *protomedicato* in Mexico and Lima produced their own inventories (Hernández in the 1570s; later pharmacy boards). The Spanish file is not a kinder empire. It is a different bureaucracy: more Crown physician, less Company auction, still a ship.

Harold Cook's *Matters of Exchange* puts Dutch medicine in a world of specimens and prices. Richard Grove's green imperialism thesis (that some colonial scientists also invented conservation) is argued and useful if you do not let it wash the blood off Banda. Brockway is blunter about botanic gardens as instruments of expansion.

## Pharmacognosy as a job

The university subject called pharmacognosy — identification of crude drugs, microscopy, assay — was built to stop merchants from selling olive leaves as senna. It is a good subject. It was also built in the same decades as steam and the telegraph, to handle a global sack. A microscope in London looking at a bark from Peru is a moral situation, not only a technique. The technique can be taught without the situation. This series refuses that convenience.

## After the empires

Independent states inherited gardens and the habit of the ledger. Some national research institutes (India, Ghana, Brazil, China) turned the habit toward their own lists — a reversal that is never complete, because journals and patents still often sit in the old metropoles. The Nagoya Protocol is a late, incomplete attempt to put a contract where the ledger had a shrug. Vinca and taxol (essays 33–34) show how much the shrug cost.

## The Wardian case as a machine

Nathaniel Bagshaw Ward's glazed case (1830s) made seedlings survivable on a long wet deck. Kew used it; so did every rival garden. A box of glass and soil is not a metaphor. It is how cinchona and rubber and tea changed continents without a poetic "exchange." Exchange implies two ledgers. The Wardian case had one address on the shipping label.

## How to caption this without a sermon

Use a specific cargo. Twelve words: *Cinchona seedlings, Wardian cases, India Office, 1860s.* Then another twelve: *Nutmeg, Banda, VOC, 1621.* The reader can do the theology. The historian's job is to keep the garden and the massacre from being filed in different wings of the museum.

No plant in this essay is a souvenir to go collect. The souvenir habit is how the ledger stays open.
