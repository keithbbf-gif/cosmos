# Claims guardrails — herbal history blog (staged)

Internal. Not a blog post. Counsel still owns product and ad copy.

This pack is **historical and educational**. It must not read like disease-cure marketing, supplement PDP copy, or implied clinical advice.

## Never publish from this series

- Charts or infographics that imply cure rates, symptom reversal, or “X% effective” for herbs or supplements.
- Before/after patient imagery or fake clinical trial dashboards.
- “Use this plant to treat [disease]” instructions — history of past use is not a modern indication.
- Synthesis or extraction how-tos that could enable illicit drug manufacture.
- Forged herbarium photographs or AI-generated “specimen” photos presented as museum holdings.

## Allowed framing

- Dated regulatory milestones (1906 Act, DSHEA, EU THMPD) as **legal history**.
- Trade-route and pharmacopeia plates labeled **illustrative** or **speculative**.
- Alkaloid discovery timelines with **laboratory dates** cited in prose — no efficacy bars.
- Comparative monograph plates as **line diagrams** for literacy, not botanical identification keys.

## Pairing with Grok drafts

When narrative lands, every health-adjacent sentence still needs a primary source. Graphics captions already say *illustrative* where topology or sequencing is simplified.
