# Herbal Medicine, Drugs, and Supplements: A History Pack

Staged magazine drafts for a history series on how humans have used plants as medicines, commodities, and later as "dietary supplements." Educational framing only. Nothing here is medical advice, a product pitch, or a how-to for making or diverting drugs.

**Pack slug:** `herbal-medicine-history-blog`  
**Status:** draft / staged content  
**Voice:** magazine-serious, human, concrete (see `STYLE_GUIDE.md`)  
**Claims:** historical and legal guardrails in `CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`  
**Sources:** `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`  
**Images:** `PHOTO_NOTES.md`  
**CMS:** `WP_IMPORT.md`

## How to read this calendar

Forty-five essays, one file each under `drafts/`. They are ordered as a reading route — not a syllabus that pretends the world moved in a straight line from "superstition" to "science." Pharmacopeias overlap. Isolation chemistry did not abolish folk use. Regulation created new categories as often as it closed old ones.

Each draft is a standalone feature. Cross-links in the body are optional for the CMS. Do not collapse two essays into one "listicle of traditions."

## Suggested publication waves

**Wave A — Origins and texts (01–12).** Archaeology and the first written pharmacopeias. Publish as a winter series: one essay a week, maps in the photo slot.

**Wave B — Encounter and gardens (13–20).** New World manuscripts, African systems without the safari caption, physic gardens, Goa. Do not title this wave "Discovery."

**Wave C — Isolates (21–25, 31–36).** Dose, foxglove, morphine, aspirin, ephedrine, reserpine, vinca, taxol, qinghao, ergot. The chemistry essays must stay historical: no synthesis steps.

**Wave D — American bottles and statutes (26–30, 37, 40, 43).** Patent medicines, 1906, Harrison, Flexner, Shakers, cannabis scheduling, DSHEA, vitamins.

**Wave E — Late categories (38–39, 41–42, 44–45).** Kava, adaptogens, St. John's wort interactions, WHO strategy, colonial ledgers, scheduled sacraments.

## What this pack is not

- A buyer's guide, protocol, or "stack."
- A ranking of which tradition is "most scientific."
- A reprint of Wikipedia lead paragraphs with adjectives added.
- Forty-five versions of the same willow-bark-to-aspirin anecdote.

## Length and readiness

Each draft is written to web-magazine feature length (roughly 700–1,100 words of body copy): enough for names, dates, and an argument, not a padded 2,000-word recap. Front matter includes `voice_check: human`, slug, summary, tags, and `sources_notes`. Photo slots are placeholders with license hints, not generated "lab" images.

Fact-check against `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` before CMS publish. Weak or contested points are flagged in the essays themselves and in the bibliography's "soft spots" section. Do not invent trial results to fill a hole.
