# Bibliography

Authority file for the pack. Prefer these editions and instruments over a stray web summary. If an essay needs a fact that is not here, add the source to this file when you find it — do not mint a plausible journal name.

Items marked **soft** are incomplete, contested, or commonly mis-cited. Do not launder a soft item into a hard claim.

## Primary texts and early printed books

- *The Ebers Papyrus.* Hieratic medical papyrus, c. 1550 BCE (content older in parts). Standard modern handle: Georg Ebers, *Papyros Ebers* (Leipzig, 1875); English pathway via B. Ebbell, *The Papyrus Ebers* (Copenhagen: Levin & Munksgaard, 1937) — **soft** on some plant identifications; Ebbell's medical glosses are of their decade.
- *The Edwin Smith Papyrus.* Surgical; useful as contrast (wound logic vs. recipe logic). James Henry Breasted, *The Edwin Smith Surgical Papyrus* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1930).
- Hippocratic corpus, esp. *Affections*, *Regimen*, *Diseases of Women*. Loeb or later scholarly translations. The line "let food be thy medicine" is **soft**: not a stable Hippocratic quotation in that form.
- Theophrastus, *Historia plantarum* and *De causis plantarum*.
- Pedanius Dioscorides, *De materia medica* (1st c. CE). Use Lily Y. Beck, trans., *Pedanius Dioscorides of Anazarbus: De materia medica* (Hildesheim: Olms-Weidmann, 2005; later rev.). John M. Riddle, *Dioscorides on Pharmacy and Medicine* (Austin: University of Texas Press, 1985).
- Galen, *De simplicium medicamentorum temperamentis et facultatibus*; *De antidotis*. Avoid citing "Galen said" from secondary listicles.
- *Vienna Dioscorides* (Österreichische Nationalbibliothek, Cod. med. gr. 1), c. 512 CE. Facsimile literature; image rights with ONB.
- Pliny the Elder, *Naturalis historia*, books 12–27 (plants).
- Celsus, *De medicina*.
- Scribonius Largus, *Compositiones*.
- *Caraka Saṃhitā* and *Suśruta Saṃhitā*. Reliable English: P.V. Sharma; or the older but still cited Priya Vrat Sharma / Bhishagratna pathways — note century of translation. Do not treat a 20th-c. popular Ayurveda manual as the classical text.
- *Shen Nong Bencao Jing* (神農本草經). Reconstructed from later citation; Unschuld and others stress that the "author" is a culture hero. Paul U. Unschuld, *Medicine in China: A History of Pharmaceutics* (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1986).
- Li Shizhen, *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目), 1596. Modern studies: Nappi, *The Monkey and the Inkpot* (Harvard, 2009).
- Ibn Sīnā (Avicenna), *al-Qānūn fī al-ṭibb*. Latin reception as *Canon medicinae*. For the plant books, use a named translation or a secondary that cites the book/fen.
- Abū Bakr al-Rāzī (Rhazes), *Kitāb al-Ḥāwī*; *Kitāb al-Manṣūrī*.
- Ibn al-Bayṭār, *al-Jāmiʿ li-mufradāt al-adwiya wa-l-aghdhiya*.
- al-Kindī, *Aqrābādhīn* (formulary) — Martin Levey, *The Medical Formulary or Aqrābādhīn of al-Kindī* (Madison: University of Wisconsin Press, 1966).
- Maimonides, medical writings (especially on hemorrhoids, asthma, poisons) as a medieval Jewish pharmacological witness — use Bos editions where possible.
- Hildegard of Bingen, *Physica*; *Causae et curae*. Critical caution: many "Hildegard medicine" paperbacks are 20th-c. reconstructions.
- *Circa instans* and the Salernitan corpus. See Faith Wallis and the Toronto medieval medicine series.
- Valerius Cordus, *Dispensatorium* (Nuremberg, posthumous 1546).
- *Ricettario Fiorentino* (Florence, 1498 and later).
- Leonhart Fuchs, *De historia stirpium* (Basel, 1542) / *New Kreüterbuch* (1543).
- Garcia de Orta, *Colóquios dos simples e drogas da Índia* (Goa, 1563). Clements Markham's 1913 English is dated; use a modern annotated edition when possible.
- Nicolás Monardes, *Historia medicinal de las cosas que se traen de nuestras Indias Occidentales* (Seville, 1565–74).
- John Gerard, *The Herball* (London, 1597; Johnson rev. 1633).
- Nicholas Culpeper, *The English Physitian* (1652) / *Complete Herbal*. Polemical, not a pharmacopeia.
- *London Pharmacopoeia* (1618 first).
- *Pharmacopoeia of the United States* (1820 first).
- Martín de la Cruz and Juan Badiano, *Libellus de medicinalibus Indorum herbis* (1552). Vatican and later Mexican facsimiles. English: *The Badianus Manuscript*, ed. Emily Walcott Emmart (Baltimore: Johns Hopkins, 1940) — **soft** on some botanical IDs; newer Mexican scholarship should be preferred when available.
- Bernardino de Sahagún, *Florentine Codex*, book 11 (earthly things).
- William Withering, *An Account of the Foxglove, and Some of Its Medical Uses* (Birmingham, 1785).
- Friedrich Wilhelm Sertürner, papers on *principium somniferum* (1805–1817). See the English discussions in Huxtable and in *Journal of the History of Medicine*.
- Thomas Sydenham on laudanum (17th c.) — quote from a scholarly edition, not a meme.

## Isolation, drugs, and 19th–20th c. pharmacy

- John E. Lesch, *The German Chemical Industry and the Rise of Experimental Pharmacology* (relevant chapters across his essays); and *Science and Medicine in France*.
- Walter Sneader, *Drug Discovery: A History* (Chichester: Wiley, 2005).
- John Parascandola, *The Development of American Pharmacology* (Baltimore: Johns Hopkins, 1992).
- John P. Swann, *Academic Scientists and the Pharmaceutical Industry* (Baltimore: Johns Hopkins, 1988).
- F. W. A. Sertürner historiography: R. J. Huxtable and S. K. W. Schwarz, "The isolation of morphine" type reviews — verify the 1804 vs 1805 vs 1817 dating before locking a sentence (the isolation and the accepted publication are not the same year).
- Pelletier and Caventou on quinine (1820): original *Annales de chimie et de physique*; secondary in Honigsbaum, *The Fever Trail* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2001).
- Aspirin: Diarmuid Jeffreys, *Aspirin: The Remarkable Story of a Wonder Drug* (Bloomsbury, 2004); Bayer corporate archive is promotional — **soft** if used alone. Felix Hoffmann 1897 acetylation; Hoffmann vs. Arthur Eichengrün credit is a live historiographic fight — flag it.
- Salicin / Leroux (1829), Piria, Kolbe (synthesis of salicylic acid, 1859–60): standard history-of-chemistry handbooks; confirm pages.
- Coca: Albert Niemann, 1860 dissertation, Göttingen; Sigmund Freud, *Über Coca* (1884) as a primary of medical enthusiasm, not a how-to. Paul Gootenberg, *Andean Cocaine: The Making of a Global Drug* (Chapel Hill: University of North Carolina Press, 2008).
- Ephedrine: Nagai Nagayoshi, 1885 isolation from *má huáng*; K. K. Chen and Carl F. Schmidt, 1920s English-language pharmacology. FDA docket on dietary-supplement ephedra (final rule 2004): 69 Fed. Reg. 6788 (Feb. 11, 2004).
- Reserpine / *Rauvolfia serpentina*: Rustom Vakil, *British Heart Journal* (1949) is the usual bedside citation; Ciba isolation literature 1950s. **Soft:** popular "ancient Indian blood-pressure drug" compresses many indications.
- Vinca alkaloids: Robert Noble, Charles Beer, J. H. Cutts (University of Western Ontario) and Eli Lilly development of vinblastine/vincristine. See *Canadian Cancer Conference* era papers and later reviews in *Journal of the History of Medicine*. Source plant *Catharanthus roseus* (Madagascar periwinkle); benefit-sharing is **soft** / mostly absent in the 1950s record.
- Paclitaxel (Taxol): Monroe Wall, Mansukh Wani; NCI screening; *Taxus brevifolia*. Jordan Goodman and Vivien Walsh, *The Story of Taxol* (Cambridge University Press, 2001).
- Artemisinin: Tu Youyou and Project 523. Tu's Nobel lecture (2015) is a primary of the official story. Elisabeth Hsu and others on *qinghao* in the *Zhouhou beiji fang*. Do not invent a 4th-c. randomized trial.
- Ergot: George Barger; Arthur Stoll (Sandoz); Albert Hofmann, *LSD: My Problem Child* (McGraw-Hill, 1980) as memoir — not a methods manual. Medieval ergotism: *Annals* of the Annales school and later medical-historical reviews; outbreak numbers are **soft**.
- Curare / tubocurarine: Harold King; Griffith and Johnson 1942 anesthesia paper — contrast plant arrow poisons with later OR use; no preparation instructions.
- Richard Evans Schultes and Albert Hofmann, *Plants of the Gods* (1979; later eds.) — use as a map of 20th-c. ethnobotany's imagination; check ethnography against nation-specific work.

## Regulation, patent medicines, supplements

- James Harvey Young, *The Toadstool Millionaires* (Princeton, 1961); *The Medical Messiahs* (Princeton, 1967).
- James C. Whorton, *Nature Cures: The History of Alternative Medicine in America* (Oxford, 2002); *Inner Hygiene*.
- Charles E. Rosenberg, essays on therapeutics in *Explaining Epidemics* and *The Care of Strangers*.
- Harry M. Marks, *The Progress of Experiment* (Cambridge, 1997) — RCT culture.
- Pure Food and Drug Act, Pub. L. 59-384, 34 Stat. 768 (June 30, 1906).
- Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, 1938 (after Massengill elixir sulfanilamide, 1937). FDA historical summaries are usable if paired with the statute.
- Kefauver–Harris Amendments, 1962.
- Harrison Narcotics Tax Act, Pub. L. 63-223, 38 Stat. 785 (Dec. 17, 1914). David F. Musto, *The American Disease* (Yale / Oxford, several eds.).
- Controlled Substances Act, 1970; Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs, 1961.
- Abraham Flexner, *Medical Education in the United States and Canada* (Carnegie Foundation, 1910).
- John S. Haller Jr., *Medical Protestants: The Eclectics in American Medicine, 1825–1939* (Southern Illinois, 1994); *The People's Doctors* (Thomsonianism).
- Shaker herb industry: Amy Bess Miller and later Hancock/Canterbury site scholarship; USDA and Philadelphia College of Pharmacy connections — confirm local museum catalogs.
- John Uri Lloyd: Lloyd Library (Cincinnati) catalogs; *Stringtown on the Pike* is literature, not a methods text.
- Proxmire Amendment (vitamin amendments to the FD&C Act), 1976, 90 Stat. 410.
- Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act, Pub. L. 103-417, 108 Stat. 4325 (Oct. 25, 1994). Legislative history: Hatch, Harkin; contemporary *NEJM* / *JAMA* critiques (e.g. Angell, Relman).
- FDA, "Dietary Supplements" pages and 21 C.F.R. Part 111 (cGMP) — for the legal box, not for product advice.
- German Commission E monographs (Blumenthal / ABC English) — **soft** as evidence; they are expert monographs, not RCTs.
- EU Directive 2004/24/EC (Traditional Herbal Medicinal Products).
- WHO, *Traditional Medicine Strategy* (2002–2005; 2014–2023). Alma-Ata 1978 as the policy ancestor. Treat as policy, not as meta-analysis.
- NCCIH (formerly NCCAM) historical pages: useful for U.S. research-funding history; do not treat a NCCIH summary as a trial.

## Named teaching cases (interactions, bans, trials)

- St. John's wort (*Hypericum perforatum*) and CYP3A4 / P-gp: the ciclosporin and oral-contraceptive case series / studies of the late 1990s–early 2000s (e.g. *Lancet* and *Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics* literature). Name the interaction as a documented clinical problem, not as a scare caption for all herbs.
- Ginkgo GEM trial and other large dementia studies: mixed; do not write "proven" or "disproven" from memory. **Soft** until a named citation is locked for the CMS version.
- Ephedra: GAO reports; RAND review commissioned in the early 2000s; 2004 final rule (above).
- Kava: German/Swiss/UK actions c. 2002; later WHO / FDA advisories. Hepatotoxicity signal is the regulatory fact; mechanism and extract type (acetone vs. water; peeled vs. peel) are **soft** and contested.
- Red yeast rice / Cholestin / lovastatin: Pharmanex *v.* Shalala litigation and FDA's drug/supplement boundary — excellent DSHEA teaching case.
- Elixir sulfanilamide (1937): FDA history; Calvery / DEG. Not herbal, but it is why 1938 looks the way it does.

## Reference tools (identity, not copy)

- *United States Pharmacopeia–National Formulary* (current is a licensed book; historical title pages are the image).
- *British Pharmacopoeia* historical volumes.
- Kew *Plants of the World Online* / IPNI for binomials.
- NAPRALERT (Farnsworth) — existence as a 20th-c. project is the historical point; do not scrape it into medical advice.
- WHO monographs on selected medicinal plants — expert documents, **soft** as "WHO says it works."

## Soft spots (do not fabricate around these)

1. Exact modern binomials for Egyptian, Akkadian, and Nahuatl plant names — often educated guesses.
2. Shennong as a biographical person; Huangdi as an author.
3. Countess of Chinchón as the origin of "cinchona" — Linnaeus named the genus; the countess story is late and doubtful (Haggis and later).
4. "Chinese discovered ephedrine in antiquity" — they used *má huáng*; Nagai isolated the alkaloid.
5. Hippocrates / food / medicine quote.
6. Precise Ötzi therapeutic intent for the polypores (anti-parasitic is a hypothesis).
7. Soviet adaptogen trial quality.
8. Kava hepatotoxicity mechanism and which preparations.
9. Benefit-sharing for vinca and many NCI plants.
10. Any numeric "X% of modern drugs come from plants" — the percentage changes with the denominator (NP-derived, NP-inspired, antibiotics…). If used, cite a named Newman & Cragg review and the year.

## How to add a source

Author, *Title* (Place: Publisher, year), pages if a hard claim. For statutes: popular name, public law number, statutes-at-large cite, date. For cases: italic name, volume U.S. page (year). For journals: author, title, journal, year, volume, pages. If you only have a URL, it is not yet a source.
