This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Two objects, one career. Marsha Dunn Klein is first the co-author of *Pre-Feeding Skills* (1987, 2000) with Suzanne Evans Morris. She is later the Mealtime Notions address and the Get Permission stance — consent as a clinical word, the child allowed to lean in. This pack will not teach the stance. It will say the stance exists because compliance chairs had already existed.

She was living when this pack was staged. Occupational therapist in the public bios. Published work and company-facing teaching only.

## The work

The early book is a sensory-motor shelf. The later work is a relationship claim that can be heard beside Ellyn Satter and against a reinforcement-only room. Permission language traveled because parents were tired of being the problem. It also traveled because it photocopies into a slogan. “We got permission” can mean a child came toward the spoon. It can mean a therapist narrated over a child who had no exit.

Heritage includes the emptied-out version. Klein is not responsible for every emptied-out sentence. She is responsible for putting a word in the field that could be emptied.

## What later people kept

A brake on siege meals. A way to write a note that is not a bite count. What they still argue about: whether permission is enough when a child is not safe to wait, and whether a branded stance is a method or a temperament.

PFD’s psychosocial domain is downstream weather. Klein is one of the people who made that domain sound like English instead of like a token economy.

## Close

Mealtime Notions is a company name that sounds like a kitchen shop. That is not an accident. Klein moved the conversation toward ordinary tables. Ordinary tables are where most feeding happens. They are also where slogans go to die or to rule.

Get Permission, as a published stance, will be emptied out again next year by someone who did not read her. That is the fate of usable words. This pack keeps the word and the warning together. Consent is not a narration. If the child has no exit, the sentence is false no matter how gently it is said.

Occupational therapy’s claim on the table is not a raid on speech-language pathology. It is a reminder that hands, seating, and a Tuesday kitchen were never only a swallow. Klein’s career sits on that reminder without needing a winner.
