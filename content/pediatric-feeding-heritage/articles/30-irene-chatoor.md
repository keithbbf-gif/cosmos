This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Before PFD was a consensus suitcase, infant psychiatry had its own furniture. Irene Chatoor’s work at Children’s National in Washington treated feeding disorders as classifiable objects — including the phrase *infantile anorexia* that later manuals moved, kept, or argued with. The meal, in her papers, is a relationship that can carry a diagnosis without the diagnosis being an insult dressed as Latin.

She was living when this pack was staged. Child psychiatrist in the public bios. Published classifications and clinic-facing papers only.

## The work

Chatoor gave teams a way to talk about a child who would not eat when the swallow study was not the story and the motor story was not enough. DC:0–3 and DSM weather moved around her terms. A magazine that freezes one edition as the eternal name is doing the field a disservice. The historical fact is that a psychiatry address sat at the high chair and refused to call every refusal “behavior” as if behavior were a slur.

She also made research out of interactions that therapists had been describing in anecdotes. Anecdotes do not get funded. Classifications do. That is a civic fact as much as a clinical one.

## What later people kept

Permission to put relationship in the chart without feeling unscientific. What they discarded, or should have: the habit of using *anorexia* in a toddler room as if the adult eating-disorder street meaning had walked in. What they still argue about: how much of PFD’s psychosocial domain is Chatoor’s furniture under a new paint.

This pack will not reprint a diagnostic worksheet. The name on the paper is enough.

## Close

Children’s National sits in a capital that produces manuals. Chatoor’s classifications had to live next to federal language and insurance language. That is a different pressure from a private course. A diagnosis that can be coded will be coded. Coding is how a child gets into a room. It is also how a child gets a label that follows her to school.

*Infantile anorexia* will keep being misunderstood on the street. This pack keeps the phrase in quotation as a period term. Living clinicians may prefer newer words. History does not owe the street a cleanup.

Psychiatry at a high chair makes some therapists nervous. Nervous is useful if it keeps a team from calling every refusal a trick. It is not useful if it sends the child away. Chatoor’s furniture was built to keep the child in the room.
