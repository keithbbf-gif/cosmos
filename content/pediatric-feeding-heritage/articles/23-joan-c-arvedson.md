This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The sibling dysphagia pack already told the portable-team story: *Pediatric Swallowing and Feeding*, Brodsky on the early spines, Lefton-Greif on the later ones, Children’s Hospital of Wisconsin as the address. This page will not copy those sentences. It will stay on a different door: the child who is not in the NICU.

Joan C. Arvedson was living when this pack was staged. Coordinator of feeding and swallowing services in Milwaukee, clinical professor of pediatrics (gastroenterology) at the Medical College of Wisconsin in the public bios. BCS-S, ASHA Honors and Fellow in the publisher copy. Published textbooks and papers only. No family detail.

## The outpatient residue

A textbook wide enough to include the school consult and the child with a tube who is trying a taste is a claim about who counts as a pediatric feeding patient. Narrow books imply narrow children. Arvedson’s project kept the children wide: cardiac, neurologic, the clean swallow study with a falling curve, the infant and the eight-year-old.

That width is why speech-language pathology students who only had Logemann finally had a spine that did not run out of infants on page twenty. It is also why a rural therapist could assign a chapter and pretend, for a semester, that Milwaukee was in the room. Pretending is not nothing. It is also not a team.

## What later people kept

Assignable pediatric dysphagia and feeding as one job with two words. What they discarded, or should have: the idea that owning the textbook is the same as owning competence. What they still argue about: whether “feeding” dilutes “swallow.” The 2019 PFD paper is a later diplomatic answer. Arvedson’s editions are the furniture that made diplomacy necessary.

This pack will not reprint positioning charts or bottle-flow advice. The historical fact is that a Milwaukee service made the non-NICU child citable. Citable children are harder to send away.

## Close

Milwaukee is a Great Lakes children’s hospital, not a coastal myth. That matters in a series whose host brand knows inland contract work. Arvedson’s assignable textbook is how a therapist in a smaller city could cite a team she did not have. Citation is a consolation and a pressure. Consolation: you are not making this up. Pressure: the chapter assumes a hallway.

Living figures get short files on purpose. The work is still moving. This page will not invent a retirement scene. It will leave her where the public bios leave her: a service, a spine, a student who finally has a book that does not run out of children.
