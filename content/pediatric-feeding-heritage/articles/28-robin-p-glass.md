This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Second names vanish in speech-language pathology the way they vanish everywhere. Robin P. Glass is the other author of *Feeding and Swallowing Disorders in Infancy* (Therapy Skill Builders, 1992). Illustrations in some copies are credited to A. Brian Carr under contract. The book is a pair. This file exists so the pair stays a pair.

She was living when this pack was staged. Occupational therapist, Seattle Children’s in the public record. Published work only.

## The work

An infant book written from OT is a political fact in buildings where SLP later claimed the swallow and nursing claimed the clock. Glass’s share of the labor is the reminder that pediatric feeding’s paperback generation was not a single-credential event. The hallway war in essay 07 is downstream of spines like this one.

Bosma’s jacket blessing names “occupational, physical, and speech therapists and nurses.” Glass is in that list by craft even when a later syllabus files the book under “SLP feeding.” Filing is not origin.

## What later people kept

A nursery that can be described without pretending the only professional in it is a speech pathologist. What they still argue about: who bills the infant. The book cannot settle a bylaw. It can only sit on both shelves until someone notices.

This pack will not pull assessment forms out of the 1992 text. The historical object is the shared authorship and the infant who stayed the subject.

## Close

A. Brian Carr’s contracted drawings are a reminder that infant books are made objects. Someone had to decide how to draw a rib cage and a bottle without turning the page into a product shot. Glass’s second-author labor includes living with those decisions.

This file exists because “Wolf and Glass” collapses in speech too often to “Wolf.” Collapse is how women and second authors leave a syllabus. The 1992 title page still has two names. Keep both when you shelve it.

Seattle’s nursery culture in those years included nurses who did not need a paperback to watch an infant. Glass wrote anyway. Writing is how a craft survives a staff change. Staff changes are the real reliability study.

Second authors get asked to introduce the first author. This file refuses that job. Glass wrote an infant book. The hallway that later argued about credentials was already in the chapters if you were willing to see two names.
