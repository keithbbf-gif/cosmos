This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A practice can be common and still thin on evidence. Pamela Dodrill’s pediatric feeding papers — including work that sits next to Memorie M. Gosa’s on thickening — treated that sentence as a research problem rather than as an accusation. Children’s hospitals had inherited adult texture habits and nursery rice-cereal folklore. Someone had to say the inheritance was a literature, not a law.

She was living when this pack was staged. Speech-language pathologist; Brigham and Women’s / Boston children’s-hospital geography in the public bios at various points. Published reviews and studies only. No recipes.

## The work

Dodrill helped make “feeding skill” a phrase you could put in a methods section. That matters for the 2019 PFD domain, which needed predecessors who were not only course teachers. She also helped make pediatric thickening something a careful person would cite instead of something a careful person would just do.

The tone of a good review is unglamorous. Unglamorous is a virtue in a field that sells weekends.

## What later people kept

Permission to ask “says who?” about a sticky bottle. What they still argue about: how to feed a child on Thursday while the review says the Thursday habit is under-studied.

This pack will not convert IDDSI levels in a kitchen. Dodrill is here as the person who made conversion culture look like a question.

## Close

Boston children’s-hospital weather is a high-resource weather. Dodrill’s reviews still get read in places that cannot copy the resource. That is a good thing if the reading is “be careful.” It is a bad thing if the reading is “do what Brigham does.” Reviews are not buildings.

Thickening culture wanted a permission slip. She offered a bibliography instead. Bibliographies are frustrating on a Thursday. Thursday is when folklore wins. This pack is for the Friday meeting that wants to know why Thursday felt uneasy.

Feeding skill, as a research phrase, had to be rescued from workshop slang. Dodrill’s methods sections are part of that rescue. Rescue is slow. Folklore is fast. The reviews exist because folklore won too many Thursdays.

A review that says the evidence is thin is doing civic work. Thin evidence is not an empty Thursday. It is a Thursday that should be able to say it is guessing, out loud, in the note.
