This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A feeding tube is a success that can become a room. Nasogastric tape on a cheek. A gastrostomy that lets a child go home. Calories that no longer depend on a suck. Parents who sleep. Those are not small gifts. The twentieth century’s surgery and plastics made them ordinary enough that a new problem could be named: the child who no longer needed the tube and could not leave it.

Marguerite Dunitz-Scheer and Peter Scheer ran a psychosomatic service at the Pediatric Hospital of the Medical University Graz from the 1990s into the 2010s, with a public reputation for tube-dependency work that drew referrals from far outside Styria. Their later book *Child-led Tube-management and Tube-weaning* (Springer) treats more than three decades and thousands of children as a clinical memory. Dunitz-Scheer was born in 1955 in Washington, D.C., trained in Zurich and Graz, and has also been identified with Notube.com as a medical director from 2009. This pack will not print a wean. It will say a European service made “tube dependency” a phrase American teams had to answer.

## The problem the decade thought it was solving

Neonatology and pediatric surgery got good at staying alive. Fundoplication eras, reflux eras, “failure to thrive” eras put tubes in children whose mouths had not been invited to the meeting. Some children needed those tubes for years. Some children needed them for a winter and still had them at kindergarten. The tube is not the villain. The missing exit interview is.

American services answered with their own wean cultures — inpatient, outpatient, behavioral, hunger-induction, slow taste programs. The names differ. The fight is familiar: Is hunger a tool or a cruelty? Is the parent a partner or a variable? Is the child allowed to lose weight on the way back to a mouth?

## What Graz made visible

That a tube can organize a family’s entire day — pump, backpack, night feed, school nurse, airline letter. That “oral aversion” is sometimes a rational response to a month of tape. That psychosomatics, in the German-speaking sense, could sit next to pediatrics without being a synonym for “it’s in the parent’s head.”

What Graz did not make: a universal method. Exporting a service is how a reputation becomes a brand. Brands travel faster than the staffing that made the service careful.

## Who was left out

Children who still need the tube and whose parents are shamed by wean culture. Children in hospitals with no psychologist and no feeding team, only a surgeon who is proud of the button. Children whose insurance covers the formula in the bag and not the months of meals after the bag. Children in countries where a gastrostomy is not available and the moral problem is the opposite one.

Also left out: the child who eats by mouth and by tube and is fine, and does not need a narrative of liberation.

## Residue

Every modern clinic that says “we don’t wean, we rehabilitate the meal” is still in conversation with Graz whether it cites Styria or not. So is every parent Facebook group that treats a wean as a before-and-after. Before-and-after is a dangerous genre around children.

The honest heritage is double. Tubes are how some children live. Exits are how some children get a childhood that is not a pump. A pack that only tells one of those sentences is running a campaign. This pack will tell both and then stop before the schedule.
