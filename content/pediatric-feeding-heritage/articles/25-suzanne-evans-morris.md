This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The title is the claim. *Pre-Feeding Skills*, with Marsha Dunn Klein, Therapy Skill Builders, 1987; second edition 2000. Suzanne Evans Morris put a brake on a field that wanted to pour. Something has to happen before the sip — tone, sensation, a body that can organize toward a spoon — and that something can be taught without a recipe card if the teacher is careful. Courses were not always careful.

She was living when this pack was staged. New Visions and a long teaching life sit in the public professional record. Published books and course names only. No family detail, no street.

## The work

Morris came out of a sensory-motor and neurodevelopmental weather. The book let occupational therapists and speech-language pathologists share a vocabulary without pretending they had settled the credential fight. Readiness, in her title, is not a moral quality. It is a description of a body in time.

The scar is what happened when chapters became photocopies. Isolated oral-motor homework is a later habit this pack refuses to reprint. A brake title cannot be blamed for every accelerator that used its name.

## What later people kept

Permission to wait without calling waiting laziness. A shelf that made “feeding skill” thinkable before Goday’s 2019 domain. What they discarded, or should have: the mouth-as-gym reading. What they still argue about: how much motor story a meal needs when the problem is fear, hunger, or an airway.

Klein’s later permission language is a fork in the same household. This profile stays on the 1987 brake. The brake is the heritage worth keeping. The photocopy is the heritage worth naming.

## Close

New Visions, as a public teaching name, is part of how a private practice becomes a pilgrimage site. Pilgrimage sites sell weekends. Weekends can keep a brake or sell an accelerator. Morris’s title remains the brake. Readers who only met a later oral-motor marketplace should go back to the words *pre-feeding* and sit with the hyphen.

The hyphen is a pause. A pause is the opposite of a photocopied drill. If this profile has a residue you can steal, steal the hyphen. Leave the drills in the decade that overused them.

A second edition in 2000 is not a confession that 1987 was wrong. It is a publisher saying the hyphen still sold. Selling a pause is a strange success. Keep the strangeness in view when a catalog treats the book as a skills curriculum.
