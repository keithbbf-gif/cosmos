This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Weekend courses have ancestors. Rona Alexander’s name is on a neurodevelopmental and oral-motor teaching lineage that speech-language pathologists and occupational therapists carried through the 1980s and after — the mouth as something that lives on a body, the body as something that lives in gravity, the meal as something you should not start with a tongue in a mirror. Students who only met a later photocopy of isolated drills have already lost her weather.

She was living when this pack was staged. Public teaching and publication record only. This pack will not reprint exercises.

## The work

Alexander is one reason “oral motor” did not always mean a gym. Neurodevelopmental treatment culture — Bobath weather, pediatric physical therapy cousins — is the climate. Feeding therapists who could talk about pelvic stability and a jaw in the same sentence were speaking that climate even when they filed the note under SLP.

The scar is the same scar as the pre-feeding essay: isolation. A whole-body teaching can be reduced to a jaw stretch. Reduction is how a lineage becomes a product.

## What later people kept

A refusal to treat the mouth as a detached appliance. What they still argue about: how much of that refusal is evidence and how much is school-of-thought. The evidence fights of the 2000s and 2010s around oral-motor homework are part of her afterlife whether a particular paper names her or not.

Morris and Klein, Palmer, the NDT-certified NICU therapist — they share a decade’s air. Alexander is the teaching name this pack puts on that air so the air is not anonymous.

## Close

NDT culture has its own saints and its own later prosecutors. Alexander lived in that weather without being reducible to a Bobath footnote. The mouth-on-a-body sentence is the one this pack will keep. The photocopied jaw stretch is the one it will keep naming as a reduction.

Weekend students wanted something to do with their hands. Teachers wanted them to look at a pelvis first. Hands usually won. That win is the oral-motor marketplace. This profile is a dissenting caption under the marketplace photograph.

A whole-body teacher will lose, often, to a handout. Losing is not the same as being wrong. This pack records the loss so the next handout has to walk past a caption.
