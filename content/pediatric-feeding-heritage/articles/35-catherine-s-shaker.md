This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Speech-language pathologists who walked into a NICU in the 1990s and 2000s often walked into someone else’s clock. Catherine S. Shaker’s teaching and papers gave them a way to talk back to that clock without pretending they had invented infant observation. Infant-driven, cue-based — the nicknames are a family, not her trademark. She is in this pack because she made the family audible to a credential that was still a guest.

She was living when this pack was staged. NICU-facing educator in the public record. Published articles and course names only. No handouts.

## The work

Shaker’s line sits between nursing practice and SLP professionalization. Nurses had been watching infants. SLPs needed a literature that made watching a job they could defend to a neonatologist. Papers and workshops did that civic work. They also created a generation that can say “cue” as if the word were a protocol. The word is not a protocol. It is an argument with ounces.

Wolf and Glass are earlier on the OT shelf. Ross’s SOFFI is a later named program. Shaker is the SLP-facing translator in the middle of that weather.

## What later people kept

Permission to stop a bottle when the infant is done, and to call that professionalism. What they still argue about: staffing, throughput, and the late preterm on the postpartum floor who never meets a therapist.

This pack will not print a cue list. The historical residue is a unit that had to explain itself when the clock lost its monopoly.

## Close

A guest credential has to learn the unit’s language or remain a consultant who is paged after the damage. Shaker’s teaching assumed the SLP would stay long enough to learn nursing time. That assumption is an ethic. Units that bring therapy in for a weekly pass have not met the ethic.

Infant-driven language can become a poster that night shift ignores. Posters are not heritage. The argument with ounces is heritage. Keep the argument. Leave the poster in the break room where it already lives.

Neonatology can hear an SLP who has learned the unit’s verbs. It cannot hear a consultant who only has adult swallow verbs. Shaker’s lineage is a translation desk. Translation desks do not show up in impact factors.

Guest is a temporary word. Some SLPs remained guests for an entire career. Shaker’s teaching assumed they would not. Assumption is a kindness and a demand.
