This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A suck can be a nurse’s adjective or a scale. Marjorie Meyer Palmer’s Neonatal Oral-Motor Assessment Scale — NOMAS, papers from 1983 onward — tried to make a preterm and term infant’s feeding pattern something two observers could argue about with shared words: normal, disorganized, dysfunctional, in the language of that instrument. This pack will not teach the scoring. It will say the instrument is why some nurseries believed a suck could be a reliability study.

She was living when this pack was staged. M.A., CCC-SLP in the public bios; clinical instructor titles at UCSF pediatrics (1988–2005) and earlier at Boston University rehabilitation medicine (1979–1984) appear on the NOMAS International site. Neurodevelopmental-treatment certification is in that same public bio. Published instrument and courses only.

## The work

Palmer gave NICUs a way to write more than “poor feeder.” That is a real gift. A gift with a certification economy attached is also a market. Quarterly courses, licensed practitioners, an international site — those are historical facts about how pediatric feeding professionalized observation.

She has also published on sensory-based weaning as a named protocol. This pack will not print that protocol. Naming it is enough to say the wean conversation is older than the social-media version.

## What later people kept

Shared vocabulary for a suck that is not only volume. What they discarded, or should have: the belief that a score is a destiny at 24 months just because a paper once looked forward. What they still argue about: whether an instrument trained in workshops travels when the trainer is not in the room.

Cue-based literature and Wolf and Glass sit in the same nursery weather. Palmer is the person who tried to put a number on the weather.

## Close

UCSF and Boston University titles in a public bio are how a California NICU consultant becomes a national instrument. NOMAS International is the afterlife: a site, a board, a way to be licensed in a suck. Licensing is not a slur. It is a fact about money and reliability.

Reliability studies are easy to over-believe. Two trained observers agreeing in a course is not the same as a night-shift nurse agreeing with a traveler. Palmer tried to shrink that gap. The gap remains. This pack will not score a suck to prove it understood the gap.
