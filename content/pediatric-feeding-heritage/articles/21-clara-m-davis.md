This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A nurse who is not allowed to nod. A tray of unmixed foods. A weaned infant who points. Clara Marie Davis’s 1928 *American Journal of Diseases of Children* paper is small — three infants at Mount Sinai in Chicago, named in the text as Earl H., Donald R., and Abraham G. Her 1939 Montréal address to the Canadian Medical Association is the story people retell: more children, years of ledgers, a ballroom that wanted to hear that the prescription card had been too sure.

She was born in 1878 and died in 1959. Day dates are not forced in this pack. M.D. 1901 in the Hektoen note. Chicago pediatrician. Winnetka on the 1939 byline. The cafeteria is the object.

## The work

Davis thought Holt’s world had made refusal into a disease. She built an institution where the adult’s job was stillness and the child’s job was selection from a list she had already chosen. That last clause is the one popular memory drops. Wisdom inside a curated tray is not a gas-station study.

The infants came from mothers who could not keep them — unmarried young women, widows. Strauss’s later *CMAJ* account is the secondary warrant. The cafeteria was closer to an orphanage than to a family table. After 1959 the raw boxes go missing from the trail he could find. We have papers. We do not have the basement.

## What later people kept

A permission to distrust the percentage card. A story about rickets and cod liver oil that has been doing cultural work for ninety years. What they discarded, or should have: the institutional conditions, the n of 3 versus the later 15, the fact that Davis chose the foods. What they still argue about: whether a child has an inner nutritionist. Satter’s later family-table language is a cousin, not a copy. Instagram “baby-led” is a distant cousin who did not read the methods.

## Close

Winnetka on a 1939 byline is not Mount Sinai’s ward. Davis moved, as successful physicians do, into a geography that could hear a Montréal address. The infants who pointed at the tray did not move with her into the afterlife of the paper. Their names in the 1928 text — initials, a courtesy that is also a veil — are the closest this pack will come to patients.

Do not use those initials as a cute story in a parent handout. They are research subjects from an institution that was not a home. The cafeteria’s power is the stillness of the nurse. The cafeteria’s cost is the stillness of the mother who is not in the room. Both belong on the page.
