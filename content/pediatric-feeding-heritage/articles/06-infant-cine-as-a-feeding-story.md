This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The sibling dysphagia pack already owns James F. Bosma’s atlas and the Karolinska cry studies. This essay will not retell the vita. It will ask a narrower question: what happens to *feeding* when someone can film a swallow.

Between 1959 and 1961 Bosma was in Stockholm with John Lind and H. M. Truby, looking at infant cry as a motion. The 1965 *Acta Paediatrica* work still startles people who expect a speech paper. Cine. Acoustic traces. A neonatal airway that is not a cute small adult. In 1961 he joined the National Institute of Dental Research. In 1986 Johns Hopkins University Press published *Anatomy of the Infant Head*. He died 22 June 2001 in Baltimore. Birth day is not forced in this pack.

The feeding story is this: once the infant head is a film, the meal is no longer only ounces. It is a sequence. Sequences can be paused. Paused sequences become teaching objects. Teaching objects become jobs.

## The problem the decade thought it was solving

Premature infants were surviving in larger numbers. Children’s hospitals had mouths that did not behave like the mouths in Holt. Radiology could already watch an adult barium swallow; Cannon’s bismuth meal is a nineteenth-century ancestor the sibling pack narrates. What pediatric services lacked was a habit of treating the *infant* swallow as a developmental motion rather than as a plumbing failure.

Bosma’s cine did not invent hunger. It made coordination visible to people who were not holding the infant. That is a political fact. A nurse at the bedside already knew the infant who fluttered and the infant who clicked. A film could be carried to a conference. Conferences hire.

## What the film could not hold

A parent’s face. A cultural food. A three a.m. refusal that is not a swallow failure. Radiation and ethics — you do not film every meal. The children who never reached Bethesda or Karolinska. The film is a sample of a sample.

It also could not hold occupational therapy’s later claim on the same high chair, or speech-language pathology’s later claim, or the GI service’s claim that the problem was reflux until proven otherwise. Those fights need a hallway. The hallway is the next essay. The cine is why the hallway had pictures to argue about.

## Who was left out

School-aged children with lifelong disability were being fed, or not fed, while the cameras stayed on neonates. Justine Joan Sheppard’s later line is the reminder. Cardiac infants. Infants with clefts whose bottles were a cottage industry of nipples and obturators — objects this pack will name as heritage, not as a shopping list. The cine years are a NICU-adjacent fame. Fame is not a census.

## Residue

Every later pediatric videofluoroscopy, every FEES argument in a small nose, every student who is told “the infant is not a small adult,” walks through a room Bosma furnished. The sibling pack’s Bosma profile lifts the 1986 book. This essay lifts the *habit*: watching the meal as time.

Watching is not treating. The scar of the cine years is the belief that if you can see a delay, you must act on the delay. Sometimes the honest act is to wait for development. Sometimes waiting is how a child stays hungry. The film cannot decide. People decide. The film just makes them feel they decided with evidence.

A modern feeding team that starts with a picture and ends with a parent who was never asked what the meal is for has used Bosma’s gift as a blunt instrument. The gift was the infant head as a serious object. Serious objects deserve serious rooms. They do not deserve a protocol photocopied from an adult cookie swallow.
