This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The sibling dysphagia pack already lifted *Anatomy of the Infant Head* (Johns Hopkins University Press, 1986) and the Karolinska cry cine. This page will not reprint that vita. It will stay on a single residue: once you can watch an infant swallow twice, the meal is no longer only a number of ounces.

James F. Bosma was born in 1916 — day not forced — and died 22 June 2001 of heart failure at the University of Maryland Medical Center. The Baltimore *Sun* printed the notice on the 28th. NIH after Stockholm. A later seat at the Hopkins Swallowing Center, the adult room, as if air and food did not care about your age.

## The feeding scene

Nurseries in the 1960s and 1970s still lived by volume. Bosma’s films and plates gave therapists and neonatologists a way to say *sequence* in a meeting that only wanted *amount*. Sequence is a different ethics. It can justify waiting. It can justify intervening. It cannot, by itself, choose.

Wolf and Glass’s 1992 jacket used his blessing. That is influence without a royalty. A cine ancestor telling the paperback generation they had already grown a craft is a date in this pack’s calendar. The blessing can be abused. A picture is not a reason to treat every pause as a defect.

## What this pack keeps

The infant as not a small adult — said here as a feeding sentence, not as an anatomy-table sentence. What it refuses: a protocol pulled from a frame. What it still argues about: whether watching a meal makes you responsible for changing it.

Lefton-Greif and the pediatric VFSS generation are downstream. So is every student who is told to look at coordination and then handed a productivity standard that only counts ounces. Bosma cannot resolve that. He can only be the man who made the contradiction visible.

## Close

The Baltimore death notice is a local paper doing civic work. NIH and Hopkins are national addresses. This pack’s Bosma lives in the gap: a federal scientist whose films changed how a bedside nurse could be argued with. Argument is not a protocol. It is a change in who gets to speak in the huddle.

Karolinska, Bethesda, a 1986 book, a 2001 funeral. The sibling pack already walked that timeline as anatomy. Here the timeline is a feeding ethic. If you can see a pause, you may still choose not to treat the pause as a defect. Seeing is not the same as chasing. Bosma made seeing possible. Chasing was other people’s productivity.
