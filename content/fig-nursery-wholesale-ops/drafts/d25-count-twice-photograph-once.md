I count, I write, I photograph the open box. Then I tape. Tape
is the line after which I am arguing with a memory. I have a
bad memory at 10 p.m. The photo does not.

The photo is ugly on purpose. Slip readable. Bags readable.
Count visible if I can get it. No filter. No lifestyle. This
is not FigRoots fruit light. This is a bench receipt.

I do not post these. I file them by date and destination. When
the email comes I do not want to reconstruct a Tuesday from
the invoice alone. The invoice does not show the stick that
stayed on the table.

Second count is after the band and before the bag. Third
count, if I am tired, is from the photo before I walk to the
carrier. I have driven back from town for a short. I would
rather the photo catch it in the shop.

People will say this is overhead. It is thirty seconds. A
claim is an hour and a name. I know which one I am buying
when I skip the camera because my hands are dirty. Dirty
hands can still hold a phone long enough to tap.

If I substitute, the photo must show the substitute card.
A photo of “the box” that hides the one line that changed
is a photo of a story. Stories are how I get polite and
wrong.

Keith gets the file if I am the one who packed and he is
the one who answers mail. We do not want two stories. One
photo, one slip, one invoice. That is the agree check with
a lens.

I do not photograph every retail three. I photograph every
wholesale carton and every mixed box and every lot I would
hate to relitigate. If that is all of them, then that is
all of them.

Receivers should photograph too, before the peanuts hit the
floor. Two photos of the same truth are how a DOA clock
starts. One photo and a paragraph of feelings is a
different clock.

Tape last. Always last. I have taped, then remembered the
slip, then opened a “finished” box and lost a count in the
re-stuff. If the tape is on, the job was supposed to be
over. Let it be over because you finished, not because you
were sick of paper.

The photo folder is dated, not named by variety, because
variety names I am mad at get typos. Date plus destination
plus lot on the slip in the frame is enough. If I cannot
find Tuesday’s Ohio box, I did not file it. Filing is
part of packing. I used to think filing was office work.
It is pack work that happens after tape.

I take the photo in the shop light, not on the tailgate
at dusk. Dusk photos do not read lot codes. Unreadable
photos are theater. I delete theater so I am not tempted
to pretend I have evidence.

I do not photograph a closed box and call it done. A closed box is a secret. The open box is the receipt. Tape after the receipt, always.
