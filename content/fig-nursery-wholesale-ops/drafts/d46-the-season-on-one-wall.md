The wall calendar is the operation. November through
March, then we stop pretending. I write this last so
the stages can hang on one page a person can read with
a coat on.

**Late fall.** Watch the mothers. Do not cut because the
shop is bored. Card the ones that will be on the list.
Kill the names that fruited wrong. Buy boxes, bags,
sleeves, thermometers. Fix the cooler. Write the
no-ship states you already know you cannot satisfy,
then **[VERIFY]** them again in January.

**Early winter.** First clean dry cuts. Grade. Lot
codes. Small bricks of the sure varieties. No Friday
dares. Retail sleeves if we have them. Wholesale only
when the wood is actually asleep.

**Deep winter.** This is the trade. Midweek hops. Shake
tests. Photos. Weather switch on the door. Heat packs
rare. Inventory with throw weeks. Walk the cooler like
it can lie.

**Late winter.** Buds will try. Shorten holds. Stop
calling a waking stick dormant. Offer liners later if
we have them. Do not restock a list with green side
shoots because the fashionable name sold out.

**Spring.** The ship stage shrinks. The land stage
teaches. DOA clocks close. We write what the notebook
learned — which mother gave junk, which pack sweated,
which week we should have refused. Then we go grow
fruit so next December has a factory.

I will not run a “year-round cutting shop” as a
personality. Year-round is how you mail salads and
call them lumber. There is summer work: pots, liners,
the proof row. There is not an honest dormant wholesale
in July in 8a.

Keith hangs the stage list under the calendar: take,
mark, pack, hold, ship, land. If a day’s work does not
fit a stage, it is probably fuss. Fuss is allowed on a
Sunday. Fuss is not a SKU.

If you are new to the trade, do not do all forty-six
drafts in one week. Do the keep four: three labels,
damp is not wet, weather is a switch, this lane is not
a pharmacy. Then add polarity, walls, and the Friday
door. The rest is how those seven get lonely less
often.

The wall is paper. The season is real. When they
disagree, the season wins. I rewrite the wall.

I rewrite the wall every October with last year’s
throws in the margin. The margin is the teacher. If
Celeste P2 went to the pile, P2 gets a July look
before it gets a December SKU. If Friday burned us,
Friday stays on the door even when I am behind.

If you steal one page for a shop that is not ours,
steal the keep four and the month lines, not our lot
codes and not our jokes about the stove. Your cooler
is yours. The physics is shared. The names are not.

When March comes I take the ship marker off the door
so I am not tempted to mail a salad in May and call
it lumber. The marker comes back when the wood goes
quiet. The wall can be empty a while. Empty is a
season too.
