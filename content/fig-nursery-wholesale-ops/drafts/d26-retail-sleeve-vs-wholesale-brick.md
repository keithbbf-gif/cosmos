A retail sleeve is a conversation. A wholesale brick is a
count. I pack them differently because they will be opened
differently.

Retail: three sticks, a note, maybe a mother photo code, a
sleeve they can hold in one hand, a bag they can fridge
without opening a hay bale. The person on the other end
might be new. They might need the FigRoots cuttings post
more than they need my efficiency. I can afford a sentence
on a card: square end down, 6–8 inches, three nodes, do not
add a wet towel.

Wholesale: they have a bench. They have a tray to fill. They
do not need my paragraph if my count is right. They need
tens they can grab, a slip they can tick, a grade that does
not surprise their mist line. I do not tuck a novel into a
fifty. I tuck a slip and a lot code.

The mistake is packing wholesale like retail — fifty little
conversations, fifty tiny sleeves, a price that assumed a
brick. I will do sleeves for a shop that resells singles,
as a named SKU, with time on the invoice. I will not donate
it.

The other mistake is packing retail like wholesale — a brick
of mixed leftovers and a “you sort it” energy for someone
who bought a gift. That person is not a sorter. They are a
beginner you just taught that fig people are chaotic.

Jack writes grower copy on FigRoots. This lane is buyfigs.
The sleeve can point at FigRoots. The brick should not need
to. If my brick needs a tutorial to be usable, I packed a
puzzle.

Weight and postage change. A brick of fifty in one small
carton is usually cheaper and safer than fifty sleeves in
a cavern. A sleeve of three in a giant wholesale box will
rattle unless I fill the air. Match the pack to the unit.

I keep two stations if the week is heavy: sleeve table,
brick table. Different bags, different cards, different
photos. When the stations merge, the leftover shorts wander
into the brick. I have watched them wander. I put them
back.

If you only run one table, run the brick rules and then
add a note for retail. Do not run the retail feelings and
hope the brick inherits them. Feelings do not count to
fifty.

I print two slip templates. The retail one has a sentence
about square ends and no wet towel. The wholesale one has
lines and counts. If I use the retail template on a fifty,
I look like I do not know what a fifty is. If I use the
wholesale template on a gift of three, I look like I do
not know what a gift is. Templates are cheaper than
explanations after the fact.

When the week mixes both, I finish bricks first. Sleeves
can wait an hour. Bricks waiting become totes. Totes
waiting become Friday. Friday becomes the door rule.

If a wholesale buyer wants sleeves, I price the hour. The hour is real. A free hour is how bricks start wearing sleeves and then the week dies.
