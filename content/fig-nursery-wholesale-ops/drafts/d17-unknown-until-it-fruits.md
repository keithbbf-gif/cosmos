“Unknown” on a wholesale invoice is honesty. “Mystery fig” as a
named sale is a different business. I will sell unknown wood. I
will not perfume it.

The word means: we do not have a name we will stand behind. Maybe
the tag died. Maybe the fruit was a crowd. Maybe it is a seedling
that has not plated. Maybe it is a guest that has not earned the
block. The buyer is buying a fig, not a story. Price it like a
fig without a name. Say what we *do* know — dark or light, tight
eye or open, pot or ground, year we took it.

I will not put unknown in a box of named lots without a wall.
The wall is physical. A divider, a separate bag, a slip line
that cannot be missed. Unknown has a way of migrating into the
named pile if you treat it like filler. Filler is how a nursery
loses a year.

Some buyers want unknown on purpose — they are running a trial
row, they like seedlings, they want volume for rootstock. Good.
Write `UNKNOWN` in the same font as `CELESTE`. Do not write it
smaller. Small is how people “fix” it later at their end and
then blame you for their hope.

I will not assign a romantic holding name. “Arkansas Black #3”
is fine as a local trial code on *our* bench. It is not fine as
a sell name unless we are willing to stand behind that code
forever. Codes we are not willing to keep should die at the
gate.

Jack’s grower draft about unknown figs and honest names is the
backyard version. This is the invoice version. The invoice is
where honesty either happens or gets expensive.

If we later prove the unknown, we do not quietly rename last
year’s invoices in our heads. We tell the people who bought
it, if we still can. Some will be glad. Some will have already
sold plants as unknown and will stay that way. Both are clean.
The unclean thing is letting them sell it as a European name
because we got excited in July and posted a photo.

I would rather a short list. A short list with unknown at the
bottom is a nursery. A long list with no unknowns and a row of
maybe-plants is a catalog of wishes. Wishes ship poorly.

Buyers: if you need a name for a customer of yours, do not buy
unknown and invent one. Buy a named lot or wait. The trade is
already too full of invented ones. We do not need to donate
another.

I price unknown below named wood of the same grade, and I
say why on the slip: no name we will stand behind. If a
buyer wants to pay named money for unknown, I still print
UNKNOWN. Their hope is not my adjective. I will take their
money for a fig. I will not take their money for a story
I refused in the row.

If unknown later proves, I send a note, not a silent
relabel of their catalog. Their catalog is theirs. My note
is a gift they can ignore. Ignoring is clean. Quiet edits
of someone else’s list are not.
