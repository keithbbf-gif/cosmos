One label is a wish. Three labels is a habit. I will die on this
small hill because I have already died on it in March, when the
only tag left was a smear in the bottom of a bag.

The three that leave this yard:

1. **On the bundle** — a card or tape under the band, variety,
   count, grade, cut week. If the bag vanishes, the bundle still
   has a name.
2. **On the bag** — the same words, plus the lot code. Bags get
   swapped. Bundles get pulled. The bag has to survive a swap.
3. **On the slip** — variety, count, grade, lot, destination.
   The slip lives in a sleeve, not loose on wet paper.

If those three disagree, we do not tape the box. We sit down.
Disagreement is the system working. Agreement you did not check
is how you ship eighteen as twenty.

I do not count a color tie as a label. A red tie is a hint. Hints
need a legend taped inside the lid, and legends get thrown away
with the peanuts. If I use color, I still write words. Words
photocopy. Hints do not.

The receiver should be able to photograph the open box and see
the same name in three places. That photo is their DOA clock and
my reputation. If my only name is on a packing list in an email
they have to scroll for, I asked them to do my job.

My grower post about labels that stay is about pots in an
Arkansas summer. This is the winter cousin. Rain in a box, crush,
a week in a truck. Paper pulp is not a label. Cheap ink that
smears when it hits condensation is not a label. I like pencil
on card plus a printed slip. Pencil does not run when the bag
sweats. Print does not depend on my handwriting at 9 p.m.

I have tried writing on bark. It looks folkloric and it rubs off
on the next stick. I have tried a single flag in a box of four
varieties. The flag always ends up in the wrong corner. I have
tried “they will know, I told them in the message.” They will
not know. They will guess, and then they will fruit a mix, and
then they will think I am a thief when I was only tired.

Three sounds like overhead. It is two extra minutes on a lot that
took a year to grow. I can afford two minutes. I cannot afford
March.

A fail I still replay: one flag in a four-variety box, peanuts
dumped, flag in the corner, four bags with no words. We
guessed. We guessed wrong on one. That one is why I will
bore you about three labels. Boredom is cheaper than a wrong
row in someone else’s catalog.

I also put the lot code on the outside tape as a *fourth*
hint only if the inside three already exist. Outside tape
alone is a snack for the carrier sticker. Inside three plus
a tape hint is a belt and suspenders. I will wear both in
January.
