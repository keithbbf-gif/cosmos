Every stick you take is a fig you will not eat next August. I say that
out loud in December because the tote does not say it. The tote just
gets heavier and looks like money.

A mother block is not a firewood pile. It is the factory and the proof
tree. If I strip a Celeste for fifty cuttings and leave her a hat rack,
I did not “maximize the variety.” I spent next year’s fruit and next
year’s wood in one afternoon. Wholesale math that ignores August is
hobby math with a bigger invoice.

I take wood the way I prune for a bush I still want: thin the crowding,
keep a frame, leave fruiting wood on the plants that earn their keep in
the ground. The first-year in-ground fig does not get harvested for a
list. It has not earned the zone and it has not proven type. A potted
mother can give more, because we already decided that plant is a
library, not a plate. Even then I do not skin it.

Jack’s length band helps here. Three nodes, 6–8 inches, means I am not
taking four-foot wands for the photo. I am taking units. I can count
units against a plant. “This LSU Purple can spare twelve this week”
is a sentence. “Take all of it, they want it” is how you own a stump
in June and a refund thread in January.

Allocation is the adult word. Rare wood gets a number before the shop
opens. We do not let the first three wholesale accounts empty a mother
that has twelve honest sticks. We also do not invent sticks. Sold out
is a clean status. A late cut from green side shoots is not a restock.

I walk the row with a card: variety, plant ID, last year’s fruit note,
sticks I will allow. If last year the tree was a dud or a mix-up, it
does not get a wholesale name this year. It gets a trial tag or it gets
cut for our own cups as unknown. The trade does not need my embarrassment
gift-wrapped.

People will tell you a fig wants a hard cut and will explode. Sometimes.
A young plant that you harvested like a hay field will also sit there
and sulk, or push a spray of weak wood that is useless next December.
I would rather ship fewer units and still have a mother I can photograph
in July.

A number I will actually write: on an established in-ground bush I
rarely take more than a third of last year’s good wood, and I take
none off a plant that has not fruited true for me. Pots can give more
if that pot is a library plant. Even then I leave a frame. If the
card says twelve and the tote wants twenty, the card wins.

If you are a buyer, ask how the wood was taken. If the seller cannot
talk about the plant, only the listing, you are not in a nursery
conversation. You are in a leftovers conversation. I have been on both
sides. I prefer the side where August still exists.
