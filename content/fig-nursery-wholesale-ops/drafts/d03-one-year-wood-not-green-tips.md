Green tips look generous in a photo and die in a bag. I have thrown
away more “pretty” wood than I have thrown away ugly gray sticks, and
that is not a personality trait. That is moisture and pith.

The cutting we sell as a standard is last season’s growth that finished.
One-year wood. Straight enough to handle. Pencil-thick, or a little
over, with three honest nodes in the 6–8 inch band. Two-year wood can
work — it is often fatter, sometimes slower to callus, sometimes a gift
if the eyes are sound — but I do not mix it into a pencil lot and hope
the receiver sorts it. If I ship two-year, the slip says two-year.

What I will not ship as a named dormant lot is the long green wand from
an October flush. It photographs. It is long. It is also half water.
In a sealed bag it sweats, then it slimes, then someone emails a photo
of a gray sock and asks why my “premium” wood looks like that. Because
it was never wood yet.

Pith tells on you. A fat stick with a huge pale center and a thin ring
of actual tissue is a hose, not a cutting. I split one when I am
unsure. If the ring is a joke, it is a cull, even if the variety is the
one everyone wants. Wholesale does not get the hose. Retail does not
get the hose with a story. The burn pile gets the hose.

I take from the middle of the cane more than from the whip tip. The
base of a long shoot can be good if it lignified. The last four inches
of a late push are almost always a vanity cut. Keith will leave those
tips on a mother he still wants fruit from anyway. The tree needs a
brake in August. I need a brake in December. We are not in a contest
to see who can strip a plant into a coat rack.

Nodes are not decoration. Three nodes in the band means three chances
to wake. A long skinny stick with eyes an inch apart and no caliper is
a different product. I can make an 8-inch stick with one fat node and
two weak ones. I should not. I should cut to the wood I would stick
in our own cup.

I keep a reject bucket labeled TIPS so I can see how often I still
reach for the pretty end. In a good week the bucket is light. In a
week I am tired, it gets heavy, and that is the week I should walk
away from the pruners. The bucket is cheaper than a refund.

If you are buying wholesale and the bundle looks like a florist dumped
a vase, send it back or grade it down before you stick it. If you are
selling, do the grading on your bench. The mail will not thicken a
tip. It will only tell the truth slower.

A January pass I still use: I lay last year’s canes on the
table and I cut from the middle first. The butt that lignified
early goes in the standard pile if the eyes are sound. The
whip tip goes in TIPS even if it would make the photo prettier.
If a buyer asks why their bundle is not two feet long, I tell
them two feet of October is not a winter product. I would
rather explain a shorter honest stick than refund a long one
that slimed. The middle of the cane is where the trade lives.
