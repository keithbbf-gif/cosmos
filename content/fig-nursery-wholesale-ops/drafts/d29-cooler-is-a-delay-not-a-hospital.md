The cooler holds wood. It does not fix a bad cut or a wet
pack. I have to say this to myself when I slide a tote
in and feel the little relief of “it’s put away.” Put
away is not okay. Put away is paused.

If the wood was green, the cooler is a rot timer. If the
wood was unnamed, the cooler is a mystery timer. If the
bag is wet, the cooler is a mushroom house with a nice
door. I do not use the door as a blessing.

Jack’s fridge post on FigRoots is the grower version:
dormant, lignified, wash, dry, parafilm for a long hold,
double-bag, crisper, no free water, thermometer. The
nursery version is the same physics with more totes and
less romance. I still open bags. I still smell. I still
throw.

A walk-in at about 36–40°F is the story a lot of
wholesale lists tell. Figaholics used to print ~37°F in
public. That is a target, not a sacrament. **[VERIFY]**
with a thermometer in *your* air, not the sticker on
the compressor. I have seen “walk-ins” that were a
garage with a window unit and a prayer.

I do not store cuttings next to apples if I can help it.
Fruit breathes a chemistry that wakes things. I do not
store them next to a dead raccoon of leftovers. The
cooler is for wood and for the thermometer. If it
becomes a fridge of everything, it becomes a fridge of
everything.

Hold time is a notebook number, not a hope. I write the
cut week on the tote. I write the throw week. When the
throw week comes I do not bargain. Bargaining is how a
discount mix is born. Discount mixes are how names go
to die in someone else’s tray.

If I need the cooler because the ship window closed, I
treat that as a new decision: recut later, root here, or
throw. I do not treat it as “the product improved in
the dark.” Wood does not improve in the dark. It waits,
or it declines.

Keith will pull a tote if the mother got relabeled while
the wood sat. The cooler cannot freeze a name in place
if the factory changed. Identity lives in the row. The
cooler only lives in time.

Open, smell, throw, or ship. Those are the verbs. “Maybe
by Friday” is not a verb I allow on a wet bag.

I open the cooler with a job, not with a vibe. The job
is: read the thermometer, smell the oldest tote, throw
or recard, close the door. If I open the door to hide
from a phone, I will rearrange totes like that is work.
Rearranging is not holding. Holding is time plus a
throw week.

If the power fails, the cooler is a tote in a dark room.
I move what I can to the household fridge that I have
measured, or I root, or I throw. I do not leave a
weekend of unknown air and then ship Monday as if the
sticker still ran.
