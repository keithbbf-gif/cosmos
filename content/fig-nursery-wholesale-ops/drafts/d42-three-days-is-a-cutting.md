Three days is a cutting. A leafy pot wants a different
clock. I buy service by the object, not by the habit of
“we always do Priority.”

Dormant wood, dry-seal, midweek, cold-to-cool weather:
a two-to-three-day hop is the honest one for most of
the lower forty-eight. Overnight is a luxury for a
rare lot or a bad forecast I still chose to meet. Five
to seven days is a hold I did not admit — a weekend,
a miss, a holiday. I plan like five can happen and I
pack like three should.

Green wood should not be on a five-day clock. Green
wood should not be on this bench for mail. If I have
green, I root it or I skip it.

A leafy pot is produce. It transpires. It wants a short
clock, a box that does not steam for a week, a Monday
or Tuesday drop, a receiver who waters. I will not give
a leafy pot my winter wood habit. I will give it a
refuse if the clock is a joke.

Dormant pots sit between. The top is asleep. The root
still hates a hard freeze through a thin plastic wall
and still hates a cook. I treat them closer to plants
than to pencils. See the pot pack draft.

I do not ship wood and a leafy pot in the same clock
on purpose. If a buyer wants both, they get two cartons
or they wait until the pot makes sense. One carton with
two clocks is how the pot cooks the bags or the bags
wait while the pot dies.

International clocks are not three days. They are a
paper clock plus a week you do not control. I do not
sell that as a winter wood hop. I sell it as a maybe
with a phyto calendar, or I do not sell it.

Keith will pay more postage to save a week when the
object is a plant that cannot wait. He will not pay
more postage to feel fancy on a brick of Celeste that
would have been fine on the cheaper hop in a good
week. Fancy is not a transit time.

Receivers: if tracking says five days and you get a
cutting bag, open it anyway. Five days can still be
sound. If you get a leafy pot on day five, open it
like a guest who has been in a car too long. Water,
shade, no blame-first. Then photograph if blame is
earned.

Clock first. Service second. Habit last. The object
picks the clock. I just listen.

I write the object on the carton in words a sorter can
ignore and a receiver cannot: `dormant fig cuttings` or
`live potted fig`. If I use the same shorthand for both,
I will buy the same service for both. Shorthand is how
clocks blur. Blurred clocks kill the pot first, then
they teach me, late.

If a buyer wants one carton for a stick and a leafy
pot, I price two cartons or I refuse the pot. I do not
discount the refuse. The refuse is the product.
