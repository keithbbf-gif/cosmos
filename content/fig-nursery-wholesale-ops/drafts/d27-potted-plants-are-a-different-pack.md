Dirt is weight, quarantine, and a different box. Do not
treat a pot like a stick. I have, once, because the week
was ugly and the pot was small. The pot arrived on its
side with mix in the peanuts and a broken rim. The
cuttings in the same carton were fine. Of course they
were. They were the right product for that carton.

A potted fig is a plant with a wet climate around its
roots. It wants the pot immovable, the top of the mix
held down, the canes tied so they do not lever the root
ball to death, and a box that does not become a drum.
It also wants me to ask whether the destination even
wants soil.

Some states and islands care about soil more than they
care about your variety name. Hawaii, California, and
others have inbound stories that change **[VERIFY]**. Soil
can be the reason a box dies at an inspection or never
should have been offered. A dormant unrooted cutting
taken well above the dirt line is a different object
than a gallon pot of Arkansas clay. I do not blur them
on a listing because both are “figs.”

Pack for a pot: bag the pot to keep mix in, not to steam
the plant for a week. Vent if the hop is long and the
weather is warm. Tie the cane. Spacer the pot so it
cannot roll. Double-box if the pot is heavy enough to
kill the first carton. No heat pack against the pot
wall — that is a cooked root.

I will not ship a leafy, thirsty pot on a Friday into a
weekend warehouse. Leafy pots are a different clock than
dormant wood. See the transit draft. If the plant is in
active growth I treat it like produce, not like lumber.

Jack packs the wood. I usually pack the pots, because I
am the one who has to answer for soil questions. If I
cannot answer the destination question the same day, the
pot does not leave. A delayed pot is ugly. A seized pot
is uglier.

Do not wedge cuttings around a pot “to fill the air.”
The pot will crush them. Fill with paper. The air-is-a-
hammer draft still applies. The pot is just a heavier
hammer.

If a buyer wants liners in cells, that is closer to a
pot than to a cutting. Cells dry out. Cells freeze.
Cells need a box and a week that makes sense. They do
not ride in an envelope. Nothing living we are proud of
rides in an envelope.

The short version: wood is a bag. A pot is a crate. If
I use the same motion for both, I am not packing. I am
wishing on postage.

I weigh a pot pack once so I remember it is not a cutting
carton. Postage surprises make people squeeze pots into
envelopes in their heads. Heads are not scales. The scale
is the scale. If the number is ugly, the pot waits or the
buyer pays. I do not hide dirt in a cuttings rate.

I also write `soil: yes` or `soil: no` on the slip so a
second pair of hands does not add a pot to a no-soil
route because the pot was sitting there looking ready.
Ready is not the route.
