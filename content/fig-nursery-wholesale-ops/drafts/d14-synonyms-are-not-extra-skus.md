Lattarula and Italian Honey are not two SKUs. I have seen lists
that sell both from the same row and call it depth. That is not
depth. That is a filing error with a price.

Figs collect names the way a shed collects jars. Blanche, Italian
Honey, White Marseilles, Lattarula — some of those conversations
are real regional names for a type, some are hopeful labels on a
cutting that looked pale. I am not the court of last resort on
every synonym. I am the person who will not print two invoices
for one plant.

Our rule: one mother, one sell name, aliases in the notes. If I
believe the trade knows it both ways, the slip can say `Lattarula
(Italian Honey)` once. It cannot say line 4 Lattarula and line 9
Italian Honey from the same tote. A wholesale buyer building a
collection will think they bought two. Then they will fruit two
and think I am a genius or a thief. I am neither. I was greedy
with syllables.

Brown Turkey is a crowd. I will not pretend our Brown Turkey is
your Brown Turkey. I will say “Brown Turkey, our strain, not a
warranty of identity against every Turkey in commerce.” That
sentence loses sales. It keeps friends. If I have a local Turkey
that plates a certain way, I can describe the plate. I cannot
annex every Turkey on the internet.

The same knife cuts the other direction. I will not invent a
house name to hide a common fig. “Saline River Black” as a
mystery when it is a known dark fig I have not proven is a
story. House names are for seedlings we grew, or for a strain
we will stand behind as ours. They are not perfume on a maybe.

Jack wants the sell name short enough to fit on a bundle card.
Good. The notebook can hold the alias list. The card cannot.
If the card is a paragraph, nobody reads it in a cold garage.

When we change a name because fruit proved us wrong, we write
the old name in the lot history. We do not silently reprint.
Silent reprints are how a buyer’s 2024 “X” and 2026 “Y” are
the same stick and they never get told. Tell them. Offer a
make-right if we sold the wrong word. Do not hope they do not
stack the pots.

I keep a one-page synonym graveyard on the shop wall: names we
will not list as separate, names we retired, names we will only
sell as unknown. Visitors think it is fussy. I think it is how
you stay in the trade more than one winter.

If you are buying wholesale, ask what else they call it. If the
seller gets irritated, you learned something. If they can talk
for two minutes without adding SKUs, you learned something
better.

I keep the graveyard list dated. If a synonym fight gets
settled in the trade, I update the wall and I do not sneak
the second SKU back because a buyer searched the other
word. Search is not a reason to split a plant. The notes
field can hold the other word. The invoice line cannot
hold a second plant that does not exist.

If I am unsure whether two names are one plant, I sell
neither as a pair. I sell one name, or I sell unknown, or
I wait. Waiting is an SKU with a price of zero. I can
afford zero better than a twin.
