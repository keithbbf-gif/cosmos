Dead on arrival is a clock. May is a garden problem. I
will make a box right when you show me the box. I will
not make a summer right when you show me a cup you
watered like a fern.

Our clock is short on purpose: photograph the open
carton within a day of delivery, two if the truck sat,
and tell us what the slip said versus what you held.
If you wait a week, I am now guessing about your
fridge, your mix, your heat mat, your cat. I can still
be kind. I cannot be sure.

DOA on dormant wood means: mush, fuzz, a cooked smell,
ice damage that collapses on thaw, a crush that took
the eyes, a short that is not a count. It does not
mean “it has not rooted yet.” Unrooted wood is
supposed to be unrooted. Give it a bench and a month
before you call the stick a ghost.

I refund, replace, or both, when the photo is the box.
I do not refund a style. If you wanted longer wood,
that is a grade conversation before ship, not a DOA.

If you stuck the wood and then it failed, we are in
rooting-failure country. Jack already wrote that for
growers on FigRoots. This lane will still listen if
the wood looked wrong *before* the cup. After the cup,
we are sharing a problem. Sharing is not the same as
a claim.

Keith will not argue about a polite photo. He will
argue about a May essay. Send the photo. Skip the
essay. If you need to write, write the lot code and
the delivery date.

A receiver who runs a nursery should put the clock on
their wall too, for *their* buyers. If they wait until
May to tell us, they also waited until May to tell
themselves. That is how a whole trade gets sloppy.

I keep the pack-out photo so I can match you. If my
photo shows twenty and yours shows eighteen, we have a
conversation. If I have no photo, I have a weaker
hand, and that is my fault. The camera is part of the
DOA system, not extra.

The clock is not me being hard. The clock is how a
cutting remains a cutting in the story. After enough
weeks it is a plant you grew. I will cheer for that
plant. I will not insure it.

I put the clock on the invoice in one line so it is
not a surprise. Surprises feel like fine print. A line
that says `photo the open box within a day` is a bench
instruction. I want it next to the count, not in a
policy novel.

If I miss my own pack-out photo, I say so when I
answer. I do not fake a memory of twenty. I look at
theirs and I make it right if the wood looks like my
fail. Faking a count to win a clock is how you lose
the next winter.

If a photo arrives on day eight, I still look. I may still help. I will not pretend it is the same clock. Day eight is a garden conversation with a picture.
