A wholesale box is not a hobby envelope with a bigger stamp. That is the
first fight on this bench, and we lose it every December when someone
wants “just a few extra” stuffed into a trade carton that already has a
count on the slip.

Retail is a conversation. One variety, three sticks, a handwritten note,
a photo of the mother if we have one. Wholesale is a count. Twenty-five
or fifty of a named grade, same length band, same story on the invoice
and on the bag. If I treat the wholesale carton like a gift box, I will
short a nursery that planned a tray around a number I wrote down and then
forgot.

I already defined the industry cutting on FigRoots: **6–8 inches, three
nodes**, lignified, and if it is going to wait, dormant. Wholesale does
not get to invent a shorter stick because the tote is heavy. It can run
*longer* — 8–12 inches for a liner program that wants a cane, not a
stub — but that is a grade on the slip, not a surprise in the bag. A
box of mixed lengths with no band is not generous. It is a sorting job
I handed to someone else.

The other difference is refusal. A hobby sale can eat a skinny stick if
I say so in the message and the buyer still wants it. A wholesale lot
cannot. The buyer is not potting one cup on a kitchen counter. They are
sticking a tray they will sell. If I put my culls in their fifty, I did
not “use the wood.” I moved my problem across a state line.

We take wood when the plant is actually asleep. We grade it on the bench
the same day. We write the name before the pile exists. Those three
sentences are the whole winter. Everything after — paper, cooler, heat
pack, tracking — is how you keep from undoing them.

I do not care if the internet calls a bag of eight random sticks
“wholesale.” Wholesale is a unit a second nursery can receive without
calling me to ask what they bought. If I cannot say the variety, the
count, the length band, and the week I cut it, I am not in the trade
yet. I am mailing leftovers.

Keith will tell you the mother block is the factory. He is right. My
job is narrower: the stick that leaves here has to be the stick I would
put in our own tray. Not the stick I was tired of looking at. Not the
tip I cut because it photographed green. Not the unlabeled leftover
from Thursday.

A worked Tuesday from last January: a nursery in Ohio wanted fifty
standard Celeste and twenty-five LSU Purple. I had sixty-two Celeste
on the bench after culls and thirty Purple. I shipped fifty and
twenty-five. The extras went into our cups. I did not “make the box
nice” with the leftovers. I also did not take the seven skinny Celeste
I had already pulled and sneak them under the rubber band. That is
the whole religion.

If you only remember one split: retail can explain. Wholesale has to
count. The rest of this folder is how we keep that split from turning
into a March argument.
