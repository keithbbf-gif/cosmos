I pack with paper and a bag. I do not pack with yesterday’s
lunch towel. That sounds like a joke until you smell a box
that borrowed a kitchen rag. Kitchen rags have dinners. Dinners
have biology. Biology is not a grade.

What I use:

- Clean newsprint or kraft, barely damp if anything
- A bag that actually seals — zip or a rolled poly with tape
- Parafilm on a long hold, the way I said on The Fig Jam:
  wrap, double-bag, crisper. Ship-this-week wood often gets
  no film. Film is time insurance, not postage insurance.
- A cardboard carton that is a size, not a cavern

What I skip:

- Grocery bags with last week’s onion weather
- Soaked paper towels from the sink roll, still dripping
- Plastic wrap cinched on bark like a tourniquet
- Foam peanuts as the only cushion against a rattle of
  naked sticks (peanuts plus a bagged bundle is fine)
- Scented anything. The box is not a drawer sachet.

Parafilm holds what is in the wood. It is not a reason to
add water. If I film wet wood I have made a pickle. If I
film crushed wood I have hidden a cull. I film clean dormant
wood that might wait, or a basal I waxed instead.

People love beeswax on ends. I will wax a cut for a hold.
I will not cake a whole stick. Thick wax on eyes is how you
mail a candle. A thin end seal is a choice. A dipped
lollipop is a novelty.

I do not reuse a bag that already grew a fog farm. Bags are
cheap. Reputations are not. If a bag looks used, it is
used. Used goes to the trash or to a dry hardware job, not
back on food-adjacent wood. These are not snacks. They are
also not trash bags’ second career.

Tape: I like paper tape on the carton and clear tape on the
sleeve. I do not tape the wood. Tape on bark is a bandage
that rips eyes when they peel it.

If a buyer specifies a pack — their bag, their film, their
box — I follow it if it does not force a swamp. If their
spec is a wet towel, I push back once, in writing, and I
keep a copy. I will not be the only name on a mold photo
if I was following a swamp spec I never agreed to.

The materials list is short on purpose. Short lists get
done on a Thursday night. Long lists get “good enough”
and a rag from the kitchen.

I restock boxes and bags before I restock toys. Toys are
colored films and novelty sleeves. They eat time. Time is
the scarce thing in a ship week. A plain bag that seals
beats a pretty sleeve that does not. If a retail sleeve
earns a photo on FigRoots, that is a different hour, a
different SKU, and a different table.

I smell a reused box before I trust it. A box that lived
with onions or with a wet plant last year still has that
year in the corrugate. Wood will sit in that air. I
recycle the box to something that is not a nursery.
