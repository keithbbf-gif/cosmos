Pencil-thick is the floor. Two culls sit on either side of it. One is
the twig that looks like a promise and snaps like a herb. The other is
the club that looks expensive and is mostly pith.

I keep an actual pencil on the bench. Not as a joke. As a gauge. If
the stick is thinner than that for most of its length, I do not put it
in a standard wholesale bundle. A thin stick can root. I root thin
sticks here when I am being stubborn about a variety. I do not sell
them as the same unit as a pencil cane. The receiver’s heat mat is
not a charity.

The club is the one people argue about. Fat wood feels like value. In
figs, fat often means a huge pale center and a thin living ring. It
can also mean two-year wood with sleepy eyes. Sometimes it is fine.
Sometimes it sits in the cup and thinks about life until the mix sours.
I split a sample from a fat lot. If I am looking at a hose, the lot
is not a lot. It is firewood that used to be a fig.

Crush is a third cull that pretends to be handling. Pruners that
chew, a tote you sat on, a rubber band that girdled the bark for a
week. The eye you need is the first thing that rubs off. I would
rather cut a clean shorter stick than ship a long bruised one.

I do not rescue a cull with parafilm. Film is for holding the moisture
that is already in sound wood, the way we talked about on the Fig Jam
and on the fridge post. It is not paint. It will not put an eye back.
It will not turn pith into cambium. Wrapping a dead stick is how you
mail a secret.

Wholesale buyers: fan a bundle before you stick it. Pull the twigs
and the clubs into their own tray or into the trash. Do not let a
bad twenty percent set the moisture for the good eighty. Sellers:
do that fanning on your Tuesday, not their Thursday.

The pencil is not a religion. Some varieties run slender. I will say
so on the slip — “slender habit, still three nodes, still lignified”
— and I will not hide those in a box of fat Brown Turkey. Honesty is
cheaper than a claim.

A buyer once sent a photo of a bundle with three clubs, two twigs,
and a dozen honest pencils. They were kind about it. I still refunded
the outliers and changed how I band. Now the culls never reach the
rubber band. They reach the bucket first. Kindness from Ohio is not
a quality system.

If the whole mother only throws twigs this year, I do not wholesale
that variety this year. I root what I can for us. The list gets a
hole. Holes are allowed. Mixed calipers with a smile are not.

I keep the pencil in the tote, not in a drawer. If I have to
walk to find the gauge, I will stop using it. The cull buckets
are labeled TWIG and CLUB so a second pair of hands can finish
the sort. If both buckets are empty and the standard pile is
huge, I am not being kind. I am not looking. Empty cull
buckets during a heavy cut week are a smell I do not trust.
