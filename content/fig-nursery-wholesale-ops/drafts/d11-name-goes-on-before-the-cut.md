I write the name before I cut. After the pile exists, I am guessing.
I can guess well. I have also guessed wrong with a confidence that
still makes my neck hot.

The card starts at the mother. Stake tag, pot tag, notebook. I copy
that name onto a tote card and a bundle card *before* the first
stick falls. If the stake says one thing and the notebook says
another, I do not cut. I go get Keith. Two records in a fight are
not a SKU.

People think labeling is a packing-table job. Packing-table labels
are how you label a mystery. The mystery happened in the row when
you cut three plants “you know by heart” into one bucket because
the bucket was empty and the light was going. I have that bucket
in my head. It is how two dark figs became one argument.

Wholesale makes this louder. A hobby buyer might fruit one plant
and shrug. A nursery will stick twenty and sell them. If my
pre-cut name was a shrug, I just printed a shrug twenty times.

I use a fat marker and a card that does not melt. I do not trust
a name written on my glove. I do not trust a name I will “put on
in the shop.” The shop is where we pack. The row is where we name.

If I am cutting two plants of the same variety, they can share a
tote. If I am cutting two plants I *believe* are the same, they
get two totes until the fruit agrees. Belief is not a merger.

My FigRoots labeling habit is three times — cutting, pot,
and the one you will still read in July. This folder starts one
step earlier: the tote that carries the wood from the plant to
the bench. That tote card is the fourth label people skip. Skip
it and the other three are decorating a guess.

A worked pass: I stand at LSU Purple #2, I write “LSU Purple / P2 /
2026-01-12” on the tote card, I clip the card on, I cut twelve, I
close the tote, I wipe the blade, I walk. I do not carry an open
tote past another variety. I do not set two open totes on the
same tailgate. The tailgate is a blender.

If you take one habit from the mark stage, take this one. Names
are cheap in the row and expensive in the mail. Write first. Cut
second. Argue with the notebook, not with a nursery in another
state.

I keep blank tote cards in my jacket, not on the shop table.
If the cards live in the shop I will cut “just a few” in the
row without them. Jacket cards are how the habit survives a
cold walk. I write with a pencil when the marker is frozen.
Pencil on a damp card still beats a name I meant to add
later.

If a helper cuts with me, they get their own cards. Shared
memory is not a card. I have shared memory with people I
like. I still like them. I do not like the mix-up.
