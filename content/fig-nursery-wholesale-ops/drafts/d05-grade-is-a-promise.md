Grade is what you will still stand behind when the buyer opens the bag
in Ohio. It is not a feeling you had at the table saw. It is not
“premium” because the variety is fashionable. I have seen skinny,
one-node, half-green sticks sold as premium because the name on the
bag was expensive. That is a costume.

On this bench a **standard** cutting is the FigRoots unit: 6–8 inches,
three nodes, lignified, dormant if it will wait, caliper you could
mistake for a pencil. An **long** or **liner** grade is longer wood,
called on the slip, same soundness. A **short** is honest: two nodes,
or a little under the band, priced like a short, never mixed into a
standard fifty. A **cull** is not a grade we sell. It is a pile.

I write the grade on the tote before I write the count. If I count
first I will start promoting culls to make the number. The number is
supposed to serve the grade, not the other way around. Wholesale
buyers build trays around a grade. If half the bundle is shorts, their
mist tent is now a sorting line.

What throws a stick out of standard here: crushed bark, a split from
the pruners, mold I can see, an eye rubbed off, a mix-up I cannot
unsee, pith like a straw, a green tip I should have left on the tree,
an unlabeled piece that landed in the named pile. I do not “wait and
see” on mold in a wholesale lot. I wait and see on a stick in my own
cup, maybe. Their tray is not my experiment.

I will not print A / B / C unless I can define the letters on one
card and tape it in the box. Letters without a card are vibes. If I
use them, A is the standard band and sound eyes. B is sound wood that
missed the band or the caliper. C is not for sale. Other people’s
letters are not my letters. I do not assume.

Keith will kill a lot if the mother is wrong. That is not grade. That
is identity. Grade assumes the name is already true. If the name is
soft, we do not grade it. We stop.

A buyer who wants “ungraded bulk” can say so. I will still pull the
rot and the mystery sticks. Ungraded is not unclean. Ungraded is
“you sort length.” I will not pretend a slime bag is a bulk discount.

I have one rubber stamp I will not buy: PREMIUM. It does no work.
The slip does the work — standard 6–8 / 3 node / dormant / pencil.
If I cannot fit the grade in one line, I do not have a grade. I have
an adjective. Adjectives do not stick trays.

If you only remember the ugly version: the grade is the email I am
willing to answer in March without lying. If I cannot answer it, the
stick never should have left the burn pile.

When a buyer asks for “all A grade,” I ask them to say the
band out loud. If they cannot, we write one together: 6–8
inches, three nodes, pencil, dormant, no fuzz. Then I grade
to that card, not to the letter. Letters wander between
nurseries. Cards sit in the box. I would rather a plain card
than a fancy letter that means something else in Oregon.
