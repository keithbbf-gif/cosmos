If the slip says twenty and the bag has eighteen, you did not
pack. You hoped. Hope is not a count. I have hoped. I have also
opened my own box at the post office in my head and felt the
exact heat of being the person who shorted a nursery.

The slip is the packing-table document. The invoice is the
money document. The stick is the plant. Those three have to
sing the same song in the same key: name, grade, count, lot.
If the invoice has a nickname and the slip has the sell name
and the bag has a synonym, I built a translator and then I
left town.

I count the bundle onto the slip, not from memory of the tote.
I count twice. The second count is after the rubber band, when
I am annoyed and want to be done. That is the count that
catches the stick that stayed on the bench.

If I substitute, the slip says substitute, the invoice says
substitute, and the bag says substitute. A silent substitute
is a different variety with extra steps. I would rather delay
the line than invent a kindness. Kindness that fruits wrong is
not kindness.

I put the slip in a dry sleeve, taped to the lid or standing
in the box, not under a wet brick of wood. A pulp slip is a
missing slip. I keep a photo of the open box with the slip
readable. That photo is for me, not for social media. When
the email comes, I do not want to reconstruct a Tuesday from
vibes.

Keith handles the invoice so I do not invent prices at 10 p.m.
I handle the slip so he does not invent counts from the
listing. The crack between us is a known place. We read it
out loud before tape: “fifty Celeste standard, twenty-five
Purple standard, lots on the cards.” If someone hesitates,
we recount.

A wholesale buyer should do the same on their bench: slip
versus bags versus invoice, before the wood hits mix. If they
wait until the cups are labeled, they have laundered my error
into their system. Catch it at the box. Photograph it. Then
talk.

Short counts happen. I make them right. I do not argue that
eighteen is “about twenty” because some sticks were fat. Fat
is not a count. Count is a count.

If you only install one check this winter, install the agree
check. Labels, lots, and grades all sit on top of it. Without
it, the rest of the folder is decoration.

We read the agree check out loud even when we are alone.
It sounds foolish until you skip it and short a fifty.
The spoken line is a third count you cannot shrug past.
“Fifty Celeste standard, lot CEL-P2-2612-STD50.” If my
mouth hesitates, my hands recount.

If the invoice software wants a short SKU and the slip
wants a long name, I put both on the slip. Software is
allowed to be ugly. The bag is not allowed to be a
different plant than the money.
