Cut-to-order is fresher. The cooler is how you survive the week you
sold too many. I live in both sentences and I do not let them cancel.

If I can cut a named lot the morning I pack, I do. The wood has not
sat in a bag inventing condensation. The label is still the label I
wrote at the mother. The grade is the grade I just saw. That is the
cleanest wholesale I know. It is also the slowest, and it is how you
miss a ship window because you were still walking the row at 2 p.m.
while the forecast was turning into a Thursday freeze.

So we cut some wood ahead. Not a season. A week, maybe two, of the
varieties we already know we will ship, stored like Jack said on the
fridge post: dormant, lignified, dry-seal, crisper or the real cooler,
thermometer in the drawer, no wet towel. The cooler is a delay. It is
not an improvement. It does not make a green stick dormant. It does
not make an unlabeled stick named.

The failure mode of cut-to-order is haste. You cut in the dusk, you
mix a pile, you invent a count. The failure mode of inventory is
pride. You keep a tote because you already cut it, even after it
sweats or the buds start to move. I would rather recut than ship a
proud tote. Wood is cheaper than a name.

We do not pre-cut the rare stuff into a big anonymous brick. Rare
wood is cut when the order is real, or cut into labeled bundles of
ten that never lose their card. Bulk Celeste can sit as a counted
brick if the brick stays cold and dry and I open it twice a week.
Black Madeira does not get to be a brick. It gets to be a card.

Keith’s rule: if the cooler inventory is older than the notebook
says we allow, it becomes our cups or it becomes the burn pile. It
does not become a discount wholesale. Discount is how bad wood
travels farther.

A buyer who wants “whatever you have in the cooler” should still
get a date on the slip. I write the cut week. I write the pack
week if they differ. If I cannot remember the cut week, I do not
remember enough to sell the tote.

Last January we pre-cut too much Lattarula because three people
asked in the same hour. Two orders landed. The third changed their
mind. The leftover brick was still sound on day six. Day twelve it
had a smell. Day twelve went in the trash, not in a “bulk mix.”
The mix is how you teach a second nursery that your name is a
guess.

If you only have a fridge, you do not have a warehouse. You have
a delay drawer. Use it like one. Cut what you can pack this week.
Hold only what you will open and smell. The rest of the row can
wait on the tree, which is still the best cooler I have, as long
as the sap is down and the cows are not chewing the labels.
