If you can wring a drop out of the paper, you packed a mushroom
farm. I do not care how many forum posts say “keep them moist.”
Moist is a feeling. A drop is a measurement. The drop loses.

Dormant lignified wood already has water in it. My job is to
stop that water from leaving, not to add a swamp. We are in the
dry-seal camp: barely damp paper or no free paper at all, a bag
that closes, no puddle in the corner. OurFigs threads are full
of both camps. The wet-towel people lose bags to mold when the
truck sits warm. I have lost bags that way. I stopped.

The squeeze test is the same spirit as the Fig Pop coir test,
different job. Squeeze the paper. If a drop runs, wring it and
wait. If it is bone dry and crispy, I will fog it with a wet
hand and squeeze again. I am aiming at cool cloth, not laundry.

I do not wrap each stick like a burrito unless the lot is
sitting. For a ship-this-week bundle I want the wood dry to the
touch on the bark, ends clean, bag sealed, a scrap of paper
only if the air in the shop is desert-dry. Arkansas in January
is not a desert. The wood will make its own weather in a sealed
bag. That weather should be fog on the plastic, not a pond.

Condensation after a warm pack hits a cold truck is normal. A
pond is not. If I pack in a heated shop, I let the bundled wood
cool before I seal. Sealing warm wood is how I invent rain.

Wholesale bricks are worse than retail threes. Fifty sticks
make more weather than three. I do not pack fifty in one
sopping towel. I pack in tens or twenty-fives with their own
bags so one wet brick does not convict the whole carton.

Mold I can see at pack-out is a cull. I do not “ship it, they
can wipe it.” They can wipe it and then they can tell twenty
friends. I wipe nothing that is already fuzzy. Fuzzy is trash.

People ask about sphagnum. Sphagnum holds a lot of water and
feels professional. It also hides a puddle. If I use it, I
wring it until I am bored. I usually skip it for dormant fig
wood in a short USPS hop. I use it more for longer holds of
other species. Figs in January do not need a bog.

If you remember only the mistake: wet + warm + sealed + time
is not caution. It is a project against your own bark. The
mail will finish it.

A pack I trust in our shop: bark dry, ends dull, bag
sealed on cool wood, a scrap of kraft that cannot wring a
drop, fifty split into two bags so one weather does not
convict all. I shake the bag. I want a soft rustle, not a
slap of wet paper. Slap means I start over.

If a helper was taught the wet-towel gospel, I do not
argue theology. I do the squeeze in front of them and I
point at the drop. The drop is the teacher. I am just the
person holding the paper.
