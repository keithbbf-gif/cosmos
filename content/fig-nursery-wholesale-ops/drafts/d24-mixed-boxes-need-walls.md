Two varieties in one box need a wall, not a prayer. I have
prayed. Prayer looks like two bags that were obviously
different at 10 p.m. and obviously interchangeable at 8 a.m.
in someone else’s garage.

The wall can be a cardboard divider, a second smaller box
inside, or at least two sealed bags with three labels each
and a slip that maps left and right. “You will see” is not
a wall. “I used different color ties” is not a wall unless
the legend is taped to the lid *and* the words are on the
bags.

I pack mixed wholesale as if a stranger will open it with
gloves on, in the wind, and will toss the peanuts before
they read the email. Because they will. The box has to
survive the toss.

Never put unknown in the same bag as named. Never put a
substitute in the same bag as the original line without a
card that says substitute. Bags are where names drown.

If the order is three varieties of ten, I want three bags,
three bundle cards, one slip with three lines, and a photo.
If the order is ten varieties of five, I want to ask why we
are running a retail gift box at wholesale prices. I will
still pack it, slower, with walls. I will not pretend it is
the same hour as a single brick of fifty.

Keith once opened a box we received that had six names and
one rubber band. We fruited three animals from that band.
We did not get mad at the plants. We got mad at the band.
Then we wrote this habit on the wall.

A wall also stops moisture travel. One wet bag should not
convict the dry one. Separate bags are a quarantine for
weather, not just for names.

If I am out of dividers I will not “just nest them.” I will
find a cereal box and cut a wall. The pantry is a packing
store at 9 p.m. The prayer is not.

Receivers: if you open a mixed box with no walls, photograph
before you dump. Dumping is how you become the second
author of the mix-up. I cannot fix a pile on your bench
that used to be two bags.

The wall is cardboard. The prayer is optional. Only one of
them works in a wind.

A wall I will actually build at 9 p.m. is a cereal box
cut to the carton height, taped so it cannot collapse
into a ramp. Ramps are how bags slide into one corner
and marry. I have made ramps by being clever with a
soft divider. Soft is not a wall. Card is a wall.

I also refuse to nest a small bag inside a big bag of
another name. Nesting looks neat and is a single puncture
away from a mix. Side by side, wall between, slip on
top. Neat can wait.

I write LEFT and RIGHT on the slip when there are two names. The words match the photo. If I cannot say left and right, I do not have a wall. I have a hope with peanuts.
