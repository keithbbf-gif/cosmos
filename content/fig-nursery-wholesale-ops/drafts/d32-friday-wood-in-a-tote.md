Friday wood in a tote until Monday is a decision. Write
it down or throw it. I have left a tote on a shop floor
because I was “done for the week” and the floor was not
a cooler. Monday I had a smell and a story. The story
was not good.

If I cut on Friday I either pack and hold in the real
cooler, or I do not cut. The tree is a better weekend
hold than a dark tote in a shop that hits 60°F when the
stove is going. The tree also keeps the name on the
stake. The tote keeps whatever I thought I knew.

A labeled, sealed, cooled Friday brick can be a Monday
ship. An open tote under a bench cannot. I do not
negotiate with the under-bench tote. It taught me.

Write the decision on the cooler card: `hold to Mon,
cut 1/17, Celeste P2, STD 40`. If I cannot write that
sentence, I do not have a hold. I have a pile.

Weekend weather matters. If the shop freezes, an
unsealed tote can ice. If the shop cooks, an unsealed
tote can wake. The cooler is the only weekend I trust,
and I only trust it because I measured it.

I do not hide Friday extras in a “misc” tote. Misc is
where names go to marry each other. If extras exist,
they get a card or they get our cups before I leave.

Keith’s weekend rule: no open blades, no open totes, no
unlabeled anything on a horizontal surface. Horizontal
surfaces collect kids, cats, and elbows. Elbows do not
read lot codes.

If Monday I cannot smell-test a bag without wincing, the
decision is already made. I do not ship a wince. I do
not discount a wince. I throw a wince. Then I recut if
the mother still has wood and the window is still real.

Buyers who receive a Monday box should know some of it
may have sat two nights. Ask for the cut week. If the
seller bristles, you learned something. If they can say
“cut Friday, cooled, packed Monday,” you learned
something better.

The tote is not a plan. The sentence on the card is a
plan. I will not leave a tote to invent the sentence
for me.

The shop floor on a Friday collects half-jobs. I do a
walk with a trash bag and a stack of cards before I lock
the door. Open tote, card it or dump it into cups or
throw it. No third option under the bench. Under the
bench is a Monday smell.

If I am too tired to walk, I am too tired to have cut
that last tote. The last tote is the one that becomes
misc. Misc is the blender. I put the pruners down
before I invent misc.

I set a phone alarm for Friday lockup labeled TOTES. If the alarm fires and I am already in town, I turn around or I throw Monday. I do not invent a third story from the highway.

Monday I smell first. If I bargain first, I am already late.
