A phytosanitary certificate is a document from an
inspector, not a sentence in your listing. I have seen
listings that say “phyto certified” the way people say
“grandma’s variety.” Grandma can be a story. A phyto
cannot.

Nursery-stock certificates and phytosanitary certificates
are not the same paper. Plant-board guidance says so in
plain language: one is closer to a nursery cleanliness
story, the other is a quarantine story **[VERIFY]** for
the actual movement. I do not print either word because
it photographs well.

If a destination or a country wants a phyto, I call the
people who issue them *before* I take the money. In
practice that is a state department of agriculture or
a USDA APHIS desk, not a friend with a printer. I do
not promise a date I have not been given. Inspectors
have weeks. Figs have windows. Those two calendars
fight. I tell the buyer which one is winning.

I do not forge a stamp. I do not photocopy last year’s
paper onto this year’s box. I do not QR-code a
certificate if the destination does not accept
electronic tags — California’s inbound notes have said
they do not accept QR as the certificate **[VERIFY]**.
Paper means paper when they say paper.

Export is a different job than a USPS hop to Ohio.
International buyers get a slower yes or a clean no.
I do not hide a foreign address in a domestic carton.
I do not ask a carrier to be my customs broker.

Jack will pack to a certificate’s conditions if we have
one — unrooted, no soil, labeled, whatever the page
says. He will not pack to a vibe I invented because I
wanted the order.

I will not claim virus-free, nematode-free, or
“certified clean” because I washed a stick. Wash is
wash. A lab is a lab. An inspector is an inspector.
Those are three businesses. We are mostly the first
one.

If we cannot get the paper, we do not ship the route.
We do not whisper the route. We refund. Whispering is
how small nurseries become examples.

The word on the slip is `document on file` only when
the document is on file. Otherwise the word is `no`.
`No` is a professional word. It keeps you in the trade.

I will not let a listing template auto-fill “certified”
because the theme came with the word. Themes are not
inspectors. I strip that word when I see it. If we
have paper, I say the name of the paper and the date.
If we do not, I say nothing that sounds like paper.

A buyer who needs a phyto next week may not be a buyer
this week. I say that early. Early no is cheaper than
a late story about an inspector who does not work my
hope’s hours.

I keep the real paper in a sleeve, not a photo of paper as the only copy. Photos fade into “we had one.” Sleeves still have the page.

No paper, no route. I can say that in four words. I should.
