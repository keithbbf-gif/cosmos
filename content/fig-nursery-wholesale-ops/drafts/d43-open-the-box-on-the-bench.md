Open the box on the bench, not in the driveway. Photograph
before you argue. I write this for the other bench — the
wholesale buyer, the second nursery, the person who is
about to dump peanuts in the wind and then guess.

Bring the carton inside. Dry table. Phone charged. Open
the lid. Photograph the top: slip, bags, walls, any
heat pack. Then take the bags out. Photograph the labels.
Then open a bag. Photograph what you see before you
strip the band.

If you dump first, you become a co-author. I cannot
unmix your table. I can answer a photo of my slip next
to my bag next to a puddle or a fuzz. I cannot answer
a pile.

Count against the slip before you label cups. If you
label cups first you will launder a short into your
own system and then we will both be sure we are right.
The slip is the fight we can still have.

Check polarity. Check the grade band. Pull culls into
their own tray. Do not let a club set the moisture for
the pencils. That is the same fanning I should have
done. If I did not, you still should.

If it is raining in the bag, dry, decide, photograph.
If it is frozen, thaw before you judge. Frozen can
look proud and then sock. If it is warm and waking,
stick sooner and do not fridge a wake.

Write the lot codes into your own book if you will
sell this wood onward. If you skip that, you inherit
my name and drop my history. Then the next person
cannot find a mother. You became a dead end. Dead
ends are how the trade gets noisier.

Keith will take a call after a photo. He will not take
a novel before a photo. I am the same. This is not
rudeness. This is the agree check at the other door.

If the box is good, say so once. People only write
when it is bad. A one-line “landed, counts, dry” is
how I know the week worked. I keep those too. They
are the other half of the notebook.

I tell new wholesale buyers this before the first box,
not after the first fight. After is a lecture. Before
is a bench. I would rather they think I am fussy on
day one than clever on day four.

If they open in a driveway anyway, I still ask for
whatever photo they have. I do not pretend a windy
pile is a lab. I do my best, and I write in the book
that this buyer dumps. Next box I might add a second
inner carton. I will not add a novel about how they
should have known. I should have said it first.

I put a one-page open-the-box card in wholesale cartons the first season I work with someone. After they do it once, I can skip the card. I do not skip the first time.

Wind is not a receiving protocol. A table is.

If the slip is pulp, photograph the pulp. Pulp is still evidence.
