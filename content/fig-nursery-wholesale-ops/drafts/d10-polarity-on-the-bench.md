The basal end is the end that was closer to the ground. Mark it like
you mean it. A wholesale tray of fifty does not have time to study
each stick’s bud direction under a kitchen light.

Figs will sometimes root upside down and then waste a month
correcting themselves. Sometimes they just sit. Either way I did
not do the receiver a favor by handing them a bundle that looks
like a dropped box of pencils.

I cut the basal end square and the tip on a slight slant. That is
an old nursery habit and it is not magic. It is a font. Anyone
on the other bench can read which way the stick lived. If I am
taking a lot of one variety, I keep the squares together in the
bundle — all basals one way, like a deck of cards, not like
kindling.

Some people paint the basal with wax or a color. I will wax a
cut that is going to sit, sometimes. I will not color-code a
dozen varieties with paint and then lose the legend. Paint without
a card is the same sin as zip ties without a card. The slant and
the square do not need a legend.

Buds point. Look at them. If I cannot tell, I have a piece of
junk or a piece of two-year wood that forgot how to be obvious.
I do not put mysterious sticks in a wholesale band. I put them
in my own cup, marked “which way?” and I learn.

Jack’s read-a-cutting draft on FigRoots is the receiver’s version
of this. This is the sender’s version. If I mark polarity here,
they do not have to guess in a garage in Michigan with a kid
asking when dinner is. That is not extra service. That is the
job.

I have opened other people’s boxes where every cut was the same
angle both ends, bark shredded, no band direction. I still rooted
what I could. I did not order fifty again. Direction is one of
those cheap tells, like a packing slip that matches the bag. You
can skip it and still be honest. You cannot skip it and call
yourself careful.

When I band a bundle of ten, the basal ends are the end I tap on
the table. The tap squares them. The rubber band sits in the
middle, not on the eyes. The label faces out. If I cannot tap a
bundle square, I have mixed lengths and I need to regrade before
I pack.

Polarity is not a theory. It is a stack of sticks that can be
read at arm’s length. If your box cannot be read at arm’s length,
you are asking the mail to deliver a puzzle.

If I am teaching a second pair of hands, I make them tap a
bundle square before I let them bag. The tap is the exam.
If the ends look like a broken fan, they re-cut or they
re-sort. I do not bag a fan and hope Ohio can read buds
under a shop light. Ohio has a job. Reading my mess is not
it.

I have shipped a mixed-angle bundle once because I was proud
of the count. The buyer was polite and still asked which way
was up. Polite questions are a fail. The next fifty got the
square and the slant even if the count took longer.
