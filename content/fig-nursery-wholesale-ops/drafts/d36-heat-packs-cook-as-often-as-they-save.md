A heat pack against bark is an oven with no thermostat. I
have used them. I have also opened a box where the bundle
next to the pack looked boiled and the bundle in the far
corner looked fine. That is not mysterious. That is a
warmer touching a bag.

If I use one, it does not touch the wood. Paper wall,
air, pack in its own paper, never on the bag seam, never
under a rubber band. I use the smallest pack that is
still a pack. I do not use three “to be safe.” Three is
a kiln.

A lot of weeks I use zero. Dormant wood in January will
take a cold hop better than a cooked hop. The switch in
the weather draft is the first tool. The heat pack is a
maybe for a destination that will sit below the teens
with a receiver who cannot meet the truck. It is not a
default.

Never a heat pack in a small poly mailer. Never a heat
pack on a pot wall. Never a heat pack plus a wet bag.
Wet plus heat is a spa. Spas wake wood and then kill it.

I write `heat pack: yes/no` on the slip so the receiver
does not think the warm bag is a mystery infection. I
want them to open away from the pack and get the wood
into their weather, not into a second oven on a heater
vent.

Ice packs are the other superstition. An ice pack against
dormant bark can freeze a side. I do not pack salad ice
on fig wood. If I am scared of heat, I change the week,
not the picnic kit.

Keith’s rule: if I need a heat pack to justify a Friday
drop, I do not ship. The pack is not a pardon for a bad
calendar. The calendar is the pardon.

I have skipped packs into Minnesota because the receiver
had a garage protocol and a same-day pickup. I have used
a pack into a porch I did not trust. Both can be right.
The common wrong is a pack as a personality — “we always
include one.” Always is how you cook a mild week.

If the pack activated in the shop and the box sat, I
throw the pack and I re-decide the week. A spent pack in
a box is dirt that used to be chemistry. It will not
help Ohio. It will only take space.

Heat is a tool. Tools need a reason. “I bought a case”
is not a reason.

I store heat packs away from the pack table so they are
not a reflex. If they live under my hand I will toss
one in to feel finished. Finished is a tape job. A pack
is a decision. I walk to the shelf if the decision is
real.

If a receiver asks for a pack in a mild week, I say no
once, with the forecast. If they still want it, I pack
it off the wood and I write their ask on the slip. I
want the photo to show the wall of paper. I do not want
a boiled bag and a “but you asked” fight with no
picture.
