A clean cut ships. Wax is a choice. Sealed wet ends are a
rot plan. I have tried all three in the same week and the
wet ends wrote the review.

The basal square and the tip slant are my default. Dry to
the touch. No milk. If the wood wants to milk, it is not a
ship end yet — see the dormant window. I do not paint sap
with wax and call it dormant. I wait, or I root it here.

Wax: a thin cap on a cut that will sit in the cooler more
than a few days, or a long hop I do not love. I melt a
little, dip, set. I do not cook the bark. I do not wax
over dirt. Dirt under wax is a secret.

Film: a wrap on wood that will wait, or on a bundle that
will be opened and closed. I still want the bark able to
breathe a little in the bag’s air. Mummifying fifty sticks
into one plastic log is how you cook the inside of the log.

Nothing: most of our USPS weeks inside the lower forty-eight
in January, wood cut this week, dry-seal bag, fast pack.
The ends are clean. The truck is cold. The receiver is
ready. Extra coatings are how I feel employed. Feeling
employed is not a spec.

I never seal a wet end in film “to keep it from drying.”
You kept the wet. The wet will do what wet does in the
dark. If an end looks tired at pack-out I recut once, let
it dull over, then bag. If it still looks tired, it is a
cull.

Polarity marks stay readable after wax. If the wax hides
the square, I marked wrong or I dipped too deep. The
receiver should still see which way the stick lived.

Wholesale buyers sometimes ask for waxed ends because a
catalog taught them that is “professional.” Professional
is sound wood and a slip that matches. I will wax if they
ask and the wood is dry. I will not wax a swamp to meet a
catalog adjective.

Keith’s only rule here: do not invent a homebrew goop. No
kitchen recipes, no mystery powders, no “this oil is
antimicrobial” speeches. We are not a pharmacy and we are
not mailing salad dressing. Clean cut. Optional thin wax.
Optional film. Stop.

If the end is brown, dry, and named, it is ready. If I am
decorating it because I am nervous, I should check the
forecast instead. Nervous energy belongs on the ship/no-ship
switch, not on a thicker dip.

I keep the wax pot small so I cannot dip a whole cane
without noticing. Small pot, thin cap, set on kraft, no
drips on eyes. If I am using wax because I am behind, I
put the pot away and I recut later. Behind is a calendar
problem. Wax is not a calendar.

A receiver who asks “why no wax” gets the clean-cut
sermon once. If they still want wax, I wax dry wood and
I note it on the slip. I do not wax a wet end to meet
their catalog memory.
