A liner has roots. A cutting does not. The pack and the
promise change. I will not sell them as the same line
item with a shrug.

Unrooted wood is a delay product. It can wait in a
cooler if it is dormant and dry-seal. It wants a bag,
a band, a slip. It does not want mix in the bag. Mix
in a cutting bag is dirt on a wound.

A liner is a small plant. It already spent energy on
roots. It wants those roots not to dry into wire and
not to sit in a swamp. If it is dormant in a cell, I
still treat the root as the thing I am shipping. Wrap
the cell. Keep the crown from rubbing raw. Do not stack
three crates so the bottom plants become washers.

Callused but unrooted is a third object. I will say
`callused` on the slip. I will not say `rooted` because
I saw a bump. A bump is a bump. Buyers build benches
around words. If I inflate the word I inflate their
calendar.

Wholesale liner lots need a count of *cells that still
have a plant*, not a count of cells. Empty cells travel.
I have received a tray of “fifty” that was forty-one.
I have also shipped a tray I should have walked again.
Walk it. Pull the blanks. Then count.

Do not ship liners in the same bag as unrooted wood.
The moisture story is different. The crush story is
different. The claim story is different. Walls, again.

Price is not a pack. A cheap liner is still a liner.
A expensive cutting is still a stick. I pack the object,
not the invoice total. The expensive stick does not get
to ride in a better physics. It gets the same dry-seal
and a clearer photo.

Keith will not call a rooted pot a liner just to fit a
shipping class. A three-gallon is a plant. A cell is a
liner. A stick is a cutting. If the listing blurs those,
the listing is the first mix-up.

Receivers: open liners before you open wood if both
arrive. Liners can dry on your bench while you admire
a bundle. Water what has roots. Bag what does not.
That order has saved us more trays than any product.

If you only remember one sentence: do not put roots and
non-roots in the same weather. They do not want the
same week.

I walk a liner tray the way I walk a mother row: pull
blanks, pull the ones that wiggle out of the cell like
they were never rooted, count what remains. Then I write
that count. If the tray card still says fifty and I am
holding forty-one, the card is a wish. Wishes do not
ship.

A liner that dried to wire on my hold shelf is not a
discount liner. It is a throw. Discounting a dead root
is how a second nursery learns I sell ghosts.

I will not wet a liner tray and a cutting bag in the same motion. Different cans, different days if I have to. Shared spray is how wood gets a swamp it did not order.
