Empty air in a box is a hammer. Fill it or the post office
will. I have opened cartons where the bundles looked like
they had been in a dice cup. The wood was honest when it
left. The air taught it a new grade.

The carton should be *bored* by how little the contents can
move. Not crushed. Bored. I shake the closed box once at
the table. If I hear a rattle, I open it. Rattle is a
confession.

Fill is crumpled kraft, more newsprint, a second bagged
bundle, a cardboard spacer. I like paper that compresses
and comes back a little. I do not like a single layer of
peanuts around a heavy brick — the brick swims, then it
hits a corner in a truck. Peanuts plus a snug paper nest
is fine. Peanuts as a beach the brick vacations on is not.

Double-boxing a plant or a heavy liner lot is real. Double-
boxing three bags of cuttings in a cavern is laziness plus
postage. Size the box down. The right small box beats the
wrong big box with a poem of paper.

Crush is the other wall. If I fill so hard the bands cut
bark, I failed the other direction. Eyes do not like to
be the cushion. The cushion is paper. The wood is the
reason.

I tape like I mean the corners. A box that blooms in the
sort machine will pick up weather and lose a slip. I do
not use a box that already took a trip and went soft. Soft
boxes are pre-crushed. They are also cheaper than a claim
until they are not.

Write THIS SIDE UP if polarity of a potted plant matters.
For cuttings in bags, up is less holy, but I still keep
heavy bricks on the bottom and slips on top. A brick on a
slip is how the slip becomes pulp.

I do not pack a box to the ceiling with no lid gap and then
sit on it. I have seen that. The person was in a hurry.
The wood was thinner when it landed.

Shake test, corner tape, no rattle, no sit. That is the
crush sermon. The mail is a hammer either way. I get to
choose whether the hammer hits paper or bark.

I keep three carton sizes on the shelf and I force myself
to pick the bored one. The leftover giant box from a pot
shipment is not a cuttings box. It is a drum kit. I have
used it when I was lazy. The rattle taught the grade a
new story I did not write on the slip.

Corner tape is two strips minimum. I have watched a
single strip bloom in a sort and pick up a wet floor.
The slip became pulp. The wood survived. The name almost
did not. Two strips is cheaper than a name.

If I must reuse a carton, I peel the old labels so a
carrier does not sort my box to last year’s city. Old
labels are a second hammer, just a quieter one.

I shake the box in front of whoever packed it. If they hear the rattle too, we open it. Shared ears beat a memo.
