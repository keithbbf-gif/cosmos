Ten is a retail handful. Fifty is a trade unit. Do not mix the
math. I have packed a “fifty” that was two twenties and a ten
in my head and a forty-seven on the table. The head lost.

We band in tens when the wood is going to be counted more than
once — mixed boxes, allocations, anything a second person will
audit. Ten is a number you can see. Fifty loose is a haystack.
I can make a fifty out of five tens and I can still see a miss.

Some nurseries want a brick of fifty in one band. I will do
that if they ask and if the caliper is even enough that the
band does not crush the outsides to save the insides. A tight
band on a fat brick girdles eyes. A loose band on a fat brick
is a spill waiting for the first drop. Five tens in one bag
is usually kinder.

The rubber band sits in the middle of the internodes, not on
the buds. I split bands if the bundle is long. I do not use
wire. I do not use a zip tie cinched like I am closing a
freezer bag of soup. Bark is not soup.

Count onto a towel, band, count the banded, write the card,
then bag. If I bag first I stop seeing. Seeing is the quality
system.

Retail threes can be a sleeve. Wholesale should not arrive as
fifty individual sleeves unless the buyer is a shop that
resells singles. That is a different SKU, a different price,
and a different hour count. I will not donate that hour into
a wholesale invoice because it “looks nice.” Nice is not a
unit.

I photograph the five tens before they go in the bag when the
lot is expensive or the buyer is new. Two extra seconds. The
photo has the slip in the frame. That is the agree check from
the mark stage, sitting on the pack table.

If a stick breaks in the band, it does not stay to “make the
count.” I replace it. A broken stick is a short with extra
steps. The buyer should not have to find it.

Keith will sell a hundred. I still pack it as tens. The
hundred is an invoice number. The ten is a bench number. When
those two fight, the bench number wins until we recount the
invoice.

Mix ten and fifty in the same sentence only if you also say
how many bags. “Fifty” with no bundle story is how people
short you and do not know it. Ask. Or be the person who
already told them.

I keep rubber bands in two sizes so I do not cinch a
pencil bundle with a band meant for a club brick. The
wrong band is a quiet cull machine. It looks tidy. It
girdles. I would rather a slightly sloppy ten than a
tidy fifty with a ring in the bark.

If a buyer insists on one band of fifty, I photograph
the brick and I use two bands spaced off the eyes. I
also tell them their opener will hate me. Some still
want it. Their bench, their haystack. My slip still
says fifty in tens if I packed tens.
