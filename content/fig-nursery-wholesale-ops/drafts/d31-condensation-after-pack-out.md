Warm wood in a cold bag sweats. That water is yours to
manage. I have sealed a bundle in a heated shop, set it
on a cold stoop, and made a rainstorm in a zip bag that
looked dry when I closed it. The bag did not lie. I did.

If I pack in a warm room I let the banded wood sit until
it is cool to the touch, then I seal. If I cannot wait, I
leave the bag cracked in the cooler for an hour and then
seal it there. The second way risks a mix-up if I leave
too many mouths open. I label the open mouths. I do not
leave a row of anonymous open bags. That is a blender.

Fog on the plastic after a night in the cooler can be
normal. A puddle in the corner is a re-pack. I pour
nothing back onto the wood. I recut only if an end looks
tired. I dry the bark with a clean rag. I throw the wet
paper. I start the squeeze test over.

Wholesale bricks make more weather than threes. I check
bricks the next morning if they are still here. I do not
assume a good pack-out stays good after the temperature
change. The hold stage exists because pack-out is not
the end of physics.

Do not put a just-sealed warm bag against a freezer wall
to “hurry.” That is how you ice the outsides and sweat
the insides. Hurry belongs on the ship calendar, not on
a temperature stunt.

If I am holding a week, I burp the bag once in the middle
if I see heavy fog — open, wipe, reseal, still dry-seal.
If I see fuzz, I stop burping and I start throwing. Fuzz
is not a condensation story. Fuzz is a cull story.

Keith will delay a pack-out if the shop is a kiln from
the stove. We cut, we grade, we card, we wait. The
carrier does not get to dictate a sweat pack because
their last pickup is in forty minutes. There will be
another pickup. There will not be another name.

Receivers: if you open a bag and it rains, photograph,
dry, and decide. Do not stick wet slimy wood and then
call it my mix. Some rain is transit. Some rain is my
pack. The photo starts that sentence. The smear on your
towel does not.

Sweat is expected. Puddles are optional. I opt out.

If I packed in a hurry and sealed warm, I mark the tote
`BURP TONIGHT` and I do it. A sticky note on the cooler
door has saved more bricks than any film. If I skip the
burp because I am gone, I assume rain and I open those
bags before they ship. Assuming dry is how a Monday
truck gets a pond.

I do not stack a warm sealed bag against a cold sealed
bag to “even them out.” That is two weathers sharing a
wall. They can sit apart until they are both boring.

I keep dry rags in the cooler that are only for bags. Shop rags stay in the shop. A shop rag in a bag is last week’s juice taking a trip.
