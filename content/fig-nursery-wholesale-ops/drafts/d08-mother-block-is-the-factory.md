If the mother is wrong, the whole winter is a lie. Packing will not
fix it. A pretty label will not fix it. A phytosanitary stamp will
not turn a Brown Turkey into the name you wanted to sell.

I treat the in-ground set as the proof row and the pots as the
library. The proof row has to fruit. Until it does, that plant is
a trial, and trial wood does not go out as a named wholesale lot.
I will root it for us. I will trade it as unknown if the other
bench agrees. I will not print a European name on a stick I have
not eaten.

The factory needs a map. Not a vibe. A card in the pot, a tag on
the stake, and a page that says which stake is which when the tag
bleaches. We have lost names to sun, to string-trimmers, to a kid
who likes shiny tags, to my own hand in a hurry. Two of those
three surviving records is the minimum. One record is a story. Zero
is a seedling with extra steps.

Jack will not cut a plant that is not on the card. That sounds
bureaucratic until you have packed twenty “Smith” and watched them
leaf out as three different animals. I have done that. I still get
hot about it. The fix was not a better Sharpie. The fix was stop
cutting from memory.

A mother I bought as a cutting is not a mother I sell from in year
one. It is a guest. Guests can be wrong. Marketplace wood is a
guest until it plates. Nursery wood from a person I trust is still
a guest, just a politer one. Trust is not type. Type is fruit,
leaf, eye, habit — the same work as the FigRoots true-to-type
draft, done before the wholesale list exists.

I will pull a mother out of the block. Not to the burn pile the
first season it looks odd — figs lie in a bad year — but off the
list. Off the list is cheap. A winter of shipping a mix-up is
expensive in a way money does not cover. People remember the name
you sold them more than they remember your apology.

Sanitation at the factory is plant work, not human medicine. I
clean the blade between varieties. I do not claim the block is
virus-free. Fig mosaic is common enough that I will not print
“clean” for a feeling. I will say what I saw: no obvious canker,
no scale I ignored, no nematode pots mixed into the cutting tote.
If I cannot say that, I do not cut for trade.

Walk the row in July with the December list in your pocket. If you
cannot find the plant, you cannot sell the wood. If you find two
plants with the same name and different fruit, you have a research
project, not a SKU. The factory is allowed to be small. It is not
allowed to be fictional.

A map I will actually keep is ugly: a grid in a notebook,
pot numbers in marker on the rim, a stake that is ugly enough
nobody steals it for a craft. Pretty tags walk. Ugly tags
stay. If I cannot find P2 in July, P2 does not sell in
January. That is not a filing preference. That is the factory
clock. I have rebuilt a map after a storm. I have not rebuilt
a reputation the same week.
