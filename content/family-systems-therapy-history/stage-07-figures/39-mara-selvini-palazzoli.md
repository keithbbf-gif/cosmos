**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Mara Selvini Palazzoli was born in 1916 and died in 1999. She trained in medicine and psychoanalysis in a European world that did not need Palo Alto's permission to look at a household. The Centro per lo Studio della Famiglia in Milan is the shop. *Paradosso e controparadosso* (1975) is the Italian object. The 1978 Aronson translation is the English mailbox. The 1980 *Family Process* paper is the teaching object American students actually cite.

She is often filed as "the Milan team" as if a woman and three colleagues were a single noun. This page files her as a psychiatrist who organized a research on households living with psychosis and anorexia, then watched the English-speaking field steal the rituals and leave the clinical target on the floor. Theft of that kind is a historical event. It is not a compliment.

The later split — Selvini Palazzoli and Prata in one direction, Boscolo and Cecchin in another — is a shop fact. American slides still say "Milan" after the shop became two shops. This pack will not pick a successor. It will keep her name on the 1975/1978 book and on the 1980 paper, and it will keep Giuliana Prata's name beside hers when the later programmatic work is mentioned.

Feminist readers in the United States argued with "neutrality." The argument is in the era essay. This figure page only needs to say she lived long enough to hear it. Hearing a revolt from across an ocean is not the same as joining it. History does not require her to have joined it in order to take the revolt seriously.

No Centro photographs. Type: Mara Selvini Palazzoli, 1916–1999.

A small-city clinician who still thinks a team might know something a solo person in the room cannot see is near this hallway. Thinking that is not a Milan hour. A team is a staffing luxury most county clinics will never have. Luxury is part of the history. So is the 1975 book, which did not ask whether Arkansas had a one-way mirror.

She wrote before and after the team years, including earlier work on anorexia that the later systemic fame sometimes swallows. Swallowing a prior book is a founder disease even when the founder is a woman. This deepen will not swallow. It will point at 1975 and 1980 and stop before a greatest-hits tour.

The English word "systemic" now means, in some American mouths, anything that is not strictly individual. That widening would have puzzled a Milan research meeting. Puzzlement is useful. If a word can mean everything, it no longer names Milan.

## Prior books, later fame

She wrote on anorexia before the English-speaking field decided "Milan" was a ritual of leaving the room. Prior books get swallowed by later fame even when the founder is a woman. This deepen will not swallow. 1975 and 1980 are enough. A tour would be an institute brochure.

Prata's name belongs beside hers when the later programmatic work is mentioned. Boscolo and Cecchin's later teaching is a different shop. American slides that still say one word after a split are syllabus weather. This page prefers the split.

## Anorexia as a prior object

English fame swallowed her earlier writing on anorexia into a systemic brand. Swallowing is a founder disease. This deepen points at the prior object without reprinting a medical protocol. 1975 and 1980 remain the public systemic objects. The Centro remains a shop we will not scrape.

## Four names, then two shops

1975 is four names. The later split is two shops. American slides that keep one noun are weather. This page prefers four names and a split. 1980 remains the English teaching paper. 1999 remains the death year. No Centro scrape. Neutrality-as-side remains the feminist caption, even here.
