**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Don D. Jackson was born in 1920 and died in 1968. He is the name on the 1956 paper who could admit a patient, telephone a hospital, and incorporate an institute. Research cultures need that person. Later memory prefers Bateson's fame and Haley's sentences. This page prefers the organizer.

MRI's founding year — 1958 in his institutional sentences, 1959 in some later encyclopedias — is his birthday dispute. *Pragmatics of Human Communication* (1967) carries his name with Watzlawick and Beavin. The Brief Therapy Center's 1974 books do not, because he was already dead. Afterlives that talk as if Jackson wrote *Change* are sloppy. This pack will not be sloppy.

He took family interaction seriously inside a medical world that still preferred an individual chart. Taking it seriously is not the same as blaming a household for a psychotic illness. He lived in the decade when those two acts were hard to pull apart. The era essays on the double bind and on mother-blame are the caption.

## The condition of a school

Opening a hospital door is not a therapy school. It is the condition of a school. History that forgets the condition becomes a syllabus of geniuses. Jackson was the condition in Palo Alto: admit, telephone, incorporate.

He died at forty-seven. Clean myths grow around short lives. This page prefers the messy year-fight about MRI's founding and the 1967 book he did live to put his name on. 1974 is other people's book. Keep the years.
