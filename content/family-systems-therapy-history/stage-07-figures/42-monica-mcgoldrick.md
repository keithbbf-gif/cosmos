Monica McGoldrick is living at last verification (2026-09-14). This page uses public books only. No institute photographs. No health speculation. No unsourced private life.

*Ethnicity and Family Therapy* (Guilford, 1982), edited with John K. Pearce and Joseph Giordano, is the object that made ethnicity a table of contents instead of a last chapter. *Genograms in Family Assessment* (Norton, 1985), with Randy Gerson, is the object that made a drawing ordinary in American training. Ordinary drawings become worksheets. This pack will not print a blank genogram. The 1985 book is a historical object. A blank form is a protocol.

With Betty Carter she edited *The Family Life Cycle* (1980), later widened as *The Expanded Family Life Cycle*. Developmental time — who is leaving home, who is burying a parent — became a family-therapy object rather than a background. Life-cycle talk can be a relief. It can also become a norm that punishes households that do not look like the diagram. McGoldrick's later editions tried to widen the norm. Widening is a historical process, not a finished virtue.

She is often filed as "Bowen plus culture." The filing is a teaching convenience. The 1982 book is a shop of many authors. Shops of many authors are harder to brand, which is why syllabi still say McGoldrick as if she were a solo method. This page keeps Pearce, Giordano, Gerson, and Carter on the citation list.
