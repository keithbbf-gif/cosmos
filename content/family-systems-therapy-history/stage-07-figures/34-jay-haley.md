**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jay Haley was born in 1923 in Wyoming and died in 2007. He came into the Palo Alto group as a communications student and left it as a man who thought therapy was what you *did*. The 1956 byline is the first public object. The *Family Process* editorship is the second. *Strategies of Psychotherapy* (1963) is the third. After that he is a school.

He watched Milton Erickson and wrote about him. He watched Bateson and argued with him. He watched Minuchin and split from the gravitational field of Philadelphia. Watching is a method. This pack will not teach Ericksonian tasks. It will say Haley made watching into a profession of directives.

He wrote sentences that sound like dares. Dares photograph well in memory. They also license imitators to be cute with people who are already tired. This deepen exists to keep 1963 and 1976 visible as books, not as permission slips.

With Cloé Madanes he built a Washington training shop. Her 1981 book is her object. Living co-founder: public books only. This page will not tell a marriage story. It will tell a shop story.

No workshop stills. Type: Jay Haley, 1923–2007.

A small-city clinician who still thinks a clever instruction will interrupt a loop is near this hallway. Thinking that is not a reason to write a clever instruction on a clinic blog. This series does not publish treatment protocols.

Wyoming to Palo Alto to Philadelphia's orbit to Washington is an American itinerary with no European finishing school. He liked that. The field's later taste for Milan and Adelaide did not erase him.
