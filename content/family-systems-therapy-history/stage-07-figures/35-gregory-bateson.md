**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Gregory Bateson was born in 1904 in Grantchester and died in 1980 in San Francisco. He was William Bateson's son, an anthropologist, a cybernetic conversationalist, Margaret Mead's husband for a stretch, and the most famous name on a 1956 psychiatry paper he did not write as a clinician.

*Naven* (1936) is a New Guinea book. The Bali years with Mead (1936–1938) produced photographs this pack will not scrape and will not use as "family" clip art. *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972) is a late collection that American therapists underlined and then treated as a clinic. Underlining is not a license. Most of that book is not a family hour.

Palo Alto recruited him because he could talk about patterns, logical types, and binds. Recruitment is the historical event. The 1956 paper is the joint object (era essay). This figure page exists so Bateson does not get turned into a family therapist with a better vocabulary. He was a man who let hospitals use him, and who used hospitals as a site for ideas he had already been building on animals, tribes, and machines.

The Imogen Cunningham portrait at the National Portrait Gallery (1963) is © Cunningham Trust. Type is the honest image: Gregory Bateson, 1904–1980. A 2004 CC photograph of Mary Catherine Bateson is not a substitute father.

He was not gentle about sloppy thinking and not always careful about the household damage a sloppy uptake of 1956 would do. Both can be true. Hagiography is one failure. A sneer that ignores *Naven* is another.

A small-city clinician who still says "the pattern, not the person" is near his hallway and also near a dozen other hallways. The sentence is cheap now. In 1956 it was a research bet with a disease word in the title. Keep the title.

## Underlining is not a license

American therapists underlined *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972) and then treated the underlining as a clinic. Most of that book is not a family hour. *Naven* (1936) is not a family hour. Bali photographs are not a family hour and are not ours.

The 1956 paper is the joint object. This figure page exists so the most famous name does not absorb the joint damage or the joint payroll. Hagiography is one failure. A sneer that ignores the anthropology is another.

## Not a clinician, on purpose

The point of this figure page is the negative: he was not, primarily, a clinician. Psychiatrists recruited a pattern thinker. Recruitment had a pagination. The pagination had a walk-back. Walk-backs do not make him a family therapist in retrospect. Type: 1904–1980. No Bali child photographs.
