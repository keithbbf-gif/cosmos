**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The Wiltwyck School for Boys sat up the Hudson and took children the nicer institutions preferred not to see. Salvador Minuchin, an Argentine-born psychiatrist, worked there in the late 1950s and early 1960s with colleagues who already knew how to enter a walk-up. *Families of the Slums* (Basic Books, 1967) lists five authors: Minuchin, Braulio Montalvo, Bernard G. Guerney, Jr., Bernice L. Rosman, and Florence Schumer. Five is the first fact. A later fame that says "Minuchin invented poor families as a clinical object" has already failed the title page.

He then spent the years that made a school at the Philadelphia Child Guidance Clinic. *Families and Family Therapy* (Harvard University Press, 1974) made "structure" a clinical word: boundaries, coalitions, a parentified child, an enmeshed pair, a disengaged pair. He moved chairs. He asked a father to speak to a son while the room watched. He worked, often, with households that the analytic institutes of the same cities did not see. Watching is a method. This page will not give you a chair diagram.

*Psychosomatic Families* (1978), with Rosman and Lester Baker, took the structural eye to households organized around a child's illness — anorexia as the famous example in the later teaching. Illness here is a historical object in a Harvard book, not a prompt for your table. The later field argued about whether those chapters over-read family process into a medical condition. The argument is the history. The book is the object.

Montalvo's name on 1967 is not a courtesy. Structural family therapy as a workforce was a shop: supervisors, tapes, a clinic with a reputation, trainees who could fail in public. Harry Aponte later carried a related structural-pastoral ear. H. Charles Fishman co-wrote *Family Therapy Techniques* (1981). Schools that last are shops. Shops have authorship fights and students who remember different hours. This pack will not settle those memories. It will keep the 1967 title page from disappearing.

Class is not an atmosphere in this story. It is the reason Wiltwyck exists. A theory of boundaries that forgets rent, racism, and a juvenile court is a theory of a white professional's living room. Minuchin knew that more than some of his imitators did. Imitators liked the chair-moving because it photographed well in the mind. The 1967 book is grayer and better.

The one-way mirror at Philadelphia is a neighboring object (stage 2). Tapes taught the world a structural hour. Tapes also made a family into a teaching specimen. Later consent literature is a correction, not a footnote.
