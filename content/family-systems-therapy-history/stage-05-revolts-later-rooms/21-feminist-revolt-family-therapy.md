**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Rachel T. Hare-Mustin published "A Feminist Approach to Family Therapy" in *Family Process* in 1978. The journal that had been a men's research mailbox printed a sentence the mailbox had not been built for: that complementarity, neutrality, and a taste for "the family" as a unit could hide a hierarchy. Virginia Goldner's "Generation and Gender: Normative and Covert Hierarchies" followed in 1988. Two essays do not make a revolution. They date a revolt that was already happening in living rooms, supervision groups, and women's projects.

Family therapy had congratulated itself on not blaming a person. The revolt said: you blamed a system and still sent the mother to the kitchen. Structural work that restored a "parental hierarchy" could restore a patriarchal one. Strategic work that issued directives could issue them toward a quiet wife. Milan neutrality could be a side. Bowen diagrams could treat a woman's cutoff from a dangerous home as a failure of differentiation. None of those sentences is a demolition of every hour those schools ever ran. All of them are reasons a 1978 essay had to be written.

The revolt was not only white American academic feminism, though the two *Family Process* citations are. Women of color had been saying, in social work and in community clinics, that "the family" as imagined in Palo Alto was a particular ethnic and class object. Later essays on Boyd-Franklin and Falicov carry that correction so this page does not become a single-wave story.

Men in the field responded with a mixture of conversion, irritation, and silence. Silence is a historical response. Some institutes added a module. Modules can be a victory and a muzzle: one Friday for gender, four days for the old diagrams. This pack will not grade the modules. It will keep 1978 and 1988 on the table as objects a reader can still open.

Domestic violence is the test the revolt refused to let "circular causality" swallow. If everyone causes everyone, a beating becomes a dance. Goldner and colleagues later wrote more directly about violence; the trail is longer than this essay. The short historical claim is enough: by the late 1980s a serious family therapist could not, in public, treat a slap as a complementary sequence without being answered.

No "diverse hands" stock. No generated consciousness-raising circle. Type: Hare-Mustin 1978; Goldner 1988.

A small-city clinician who still flinches when a colleague says "the couple is the patient" in a house where only one person has bruises is standing in this hallway. Flinching is not a protocol. This series will not teach you a feminist session. It will say the journal of record had to be argued with from inside, and the argument is part of the heritage, not a later politeness.

WOW Therapies does not, by naming 1978, claim a feminist-therapy service line. Naming is literacy. Literacy is the only product this URL is allowed to ship.

## Modules and muzzles

Institutes answered 1978 with a Friday module. Modules can be a victory: the word *gender* now appears on a schedule. They can also be a muzzle: four days of the old diagrams, one afternoon of the revolt. This pack will not grade your institute. It will keep 1978 and 1988 as objects a reader can still open.

Violence is the test circular causality failed in public. A slap is not a complementary dance. Goldner and colleagues wrote more than one later paper; this writer will not invent their titles. The short claim stands: by the late 1980s a serious family therapist could not, in public, treat a beating as a sequence without being answered.

Women of color had been saying, in other buildings, that "the family" in Palo Alto was a particular object. Boyd-Franklin 1989 and Falicov 1998 are the next hallway, not a footnote to Hare-Mustin.

## Complementarity as a trap

The older schools liked complementarity: pursuer and distancer, over-functioner and under-functioner. The revolt said those pairs can hide who is allowed to leave. Pairs are pretty on a slide. Pretty is not an ethic.

This page will not replace complementarity with a new pair for you to try at home. It will keep 1978 on the table as the year the journal of record had to print the trap. Printing a trap is not dissolving it. Dissolving it took longer than an essay, and is unfinished.
