**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas practice brand. Its public face, as last fetched, is occupational, physical, and speech services. That sentence has to appear in this folder more than once, because a family-systems history pack on a clinic domain will otherwise be read as a quiet service launch.

What a small-city clinic inherited from the objects in this folder is not a mirror, not a Milan team, not a Georgetown diagram, not a Dulwich letter. It inherited **words**. System. Triangle. Structure. Brief. Narrative. Parentified. Those words arrive in graduate syllabi, church workshops, school meetings, and insurance portals. Words without a shop are a weather. Weather is not a trained team.

A school counselor who says "the family is the client" may be standing in 1924 child guidance, 1956 Palo Alto, 1974 Philadelphia, or 1985 Milwaukee without knowing which. The sentence sounds the same. The ethics do not. This pack's only local use is to slow that sentence down until a year appears.

Rural households already know more about loyalty, cutoff, and who translates at the hospital than a 1973 hardback can teach them. Knowledge of that kind is not "folk Bowen." Treating it as folk Bowen is a professional theft. The inheritance runs both ways, and the professional side has usually been louder about what it gave than about what it took.

Speech-language pathology, occupational therapy, and physical therapy already sit in family systems without naming them: a feeding hour, a home exercise, a caregiver who must learn a swallow strategy. Those hours have their own ethics and their own SLPWOW history pack. This folder will not raid them. It will say: if a caregiver is in the room, the 1950s argument about the unit is already on the furniture. Naming the furniture is not a family-therapy session.

If you came here to find out whether this clinic "does Bowen," you have come to the wrong URL. This URL does history. History does not bill. If you need a clinician, you need a person, a license, a consent form, and a room that is not a blog.

The 15-second version, if a skimming reader needs one: the field named the family as a unit, then argued for fifty years about blame, glass, gender, race, and money. A small-city clinic inherited the argument as vocabulary. Vocabulary is not competence. Competence is supervised work with people who chose you. This series will not pretend otherwise.

## Phone, not hardware

A clinic that still answers its own phone has already refused one kind of mirror: the call center that treats a household as a ticket. Answering a phone is not family therapy. It is a local fact this pack is allowed to name because the brand is local. Naming a phone is not a photograph of a treatment room. Keep the interiors off the page unless consent exists.

## School meeting as grandchild

A school meeting about a silent child is a grandchild of 1924 child guidance and a distant cousin of 1974 Philadelphia. Grandchildren do not owe loyalty to a Harvard hardback. They do owe a caption: which year is this meeting pretending to be? Pretending is the weather. This URL sells captions, not competence.

## Vocabulary is not a team

Triangle, structure, brief, narrative: four words a rural meeting can say in one hour without a shop behind any of them. This pack's local use is to slow the hour until a year appears. Years are cheaper than a fake service line and more honest than a booking widget under a 1956 citation.
