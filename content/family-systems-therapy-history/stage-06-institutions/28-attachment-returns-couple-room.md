**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Sue Johnson's public books — including *The Practice of Emotionally Focused Couple Therapy* (Brunner-Routledge, 2004) — took John Bowlby's attachment vocabulary into a couple hour and made a teachable sequence out of it. That is a neighboring object, not a family-of-origin school and not a 1954 NIMH ward.

The sister pack already holds Bowlby and Ainsworth. This page exists so a reader who hears "systems" and "attachment" in the same workshop does not think they are one man. Bowen used attachment-adjacent words in a different century and a different science bet. Johnson's EFT is a late-century couple protocol with trials and a training shop. Protocol is the word this pack will not teach. Trials are historical events: year, sample, comparison, limits. This page will not reprint them as a promise.

Why is a couple therapy in a family-systems folder? Because American "family therapy" as a profession always included couple work, and because the later marketplace often sold EFT as the thing that replaced the old schools. Replacement is a brand story. The old schools are still on Guilford shelves. The new shop is a shop. Both can be true.

A small-city clinician who still thinks a couple hour is about a bond and not only about a chore chart is near this hallway. Thinking that is not an EFT certification. WOW Therapies does not, by naming 2004, claim that certification.

The return of attachment is also a return of a developmental vocabulary the 1950s communication schools had sometimes treated as noise. Returns are not restorations. Bowlby did not write 2004. Johnson did not write 1969. Keep the years.
