**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jay Haley published *Strategies of Psychotherapy* in 1963 with Grune & Stratton. He had already been a name on the 1956 Palo Alto paper and an editor of *Family Process*. The 1963 book is the object that made "strategy" sound like a school instead of a personality. A strategy, in his ear, was what the therapist *did* — a directive, an ordeal, a way of changing a sequence — not what the household privately believed about 1908.

*Problem-Solving Therapy* (Jossey-Bass, 1976) and *Leaving Home* (McGraw-Hill, 1980) are later, more clinical objects. They belong to the years after he left the Palo Alto gravitational field and, with Cloé Madanes, built a Washington training shop. Madanes published *Strategic Family Therapy* (Jossey-Bass, 1981). Two books, two names, one brand word. This pack will not collapse her into a spouse credit. Living people get extra care: public books only, no gossip, no unsourced private life.

Directives are the scar. A clinician who tells a household to do something unexpected can interrupt a loop. A clinician who loves the unexpected can become a trickster the family has to survive. Haley wrote as if power in the room were a fact to be used rather than a shame to be denied. Using power openly is one ethic. Denying power while using it is another. Later feminist and cultural critics asked who was being directed, and toward what family order. Those questions belong in stage 5. They were aimed, often, at this hallway.

Haley had watched Erickson and had watched Bateson and had watched Minuchin. He was a synthesizer with a polemicist's sentence. Polemic photographs well in memory. It also makes enemies. Some of the enemies wrote better books because they were angry. Anger is a historical fuel. This page will not take Haley's side against Minuchin or Minuchin's against Haley. The split is a 1970s fact. Rural readers do not need a winner. They need to know why two famous men are not one method.

No Washington institute photographs. Type: Jay Haley, 1923–2007; Cloé Madanes, public books only. Do not scrape a workshop still of a living teacher.

A small-city clinician who still thinks a clever homework task will save a Tuesday is standing in the blast zone of this school. Thinking that is not a reason to assign clever homework from a website. This series does not publish treatment protocols. It publishes 1963, 1976, 1980, and 1981, and the refusal to turn a directive into a blog prompt.

Haley died in 2007. The strategic word outlived him in places that never read 1963 — school-based programs, brief "family" interventions, anyone who likes a task. Afterlives are sloppy. History's job is to put the sloppiness next to the Jossey-Bass spine and let the reader see the distance.

## Directives after the revolt

Hare-Mustin 1978 and Goldner 1988 were aimed, often, at this hallway: who is being directed, toward what family order, and whether a trickster's cleverness is a kindness. The revolt does not erase 1963. It captions 1963. Captions are how a history site stays out of the permission-slip business.

Madanes's 1981 book is a public object by a living writer. This pack will not tell a marriage story to thicken a paragraph. Shop facts are enough: Washington, two names, one brand word, two title pages.

*Leaving Home* (1980) treated a young adult's exit as a family problem a strategist might address. Exit is not a prompt for your Tuesday. It is a 1980 McGraw-Hill object. Keep it on the shelf.

## Editor, then school

Haley's *Family Process* years are a different job from his Washington years. An editor shapes a field's mailbox. A school director shapes a workforce. Confusing the jobs is how a syllabus writes one word — strategic — over a decade of different rooms.

He could be unfair in print. Unfairness is a historical tone, not a method this site will borrow. Readers who only know him as a dare have not opened 1963. Opening 1963 is the assignment. Imitating a dare is not.

## Washington as a shop, not a capital

The Washington Family Therapy Institute is a training address, not a federal fact. Later readers hear "Washington" and smell policy. Smell is a misread. Haley and Madanes built a shop that sold directives the way Philadelphia sold chairs and Milwaukee sold Thursdays. Shops have catalogs. This URL is not a catalog.

A rural reader who meets "strategic" only as a school-based task has met an afterlife. Afterlives are sloppy. 1963 remains the first spine. 1976 remains the more clinical spine. 1981 remains hers. Three spines, no dare on a blog.
