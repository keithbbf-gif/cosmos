**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Don D. Jackson organized the Mental Research Institute in Palo Alto in 1958 — 1958 in Jackson-era institutional sentences, 1959 in some later encyclopedias. This pack will not flatten the year. An institute that cannot agree with its later encyclopedias about its birthday is already a lesson in how origin stories work.

MRI was never only one school. Satir trained people. Bateson haunted the theory. Haley passed through. The object this page holds is the later Brief Therapy Center — John Weakland, Richard Fisch, Paul Watzlawick, Arthur M. Bodin — and the 1974 public objects that made "brief" a word a hospital could hear. *Change: Principles of Problem Formation and Problem Resolution* (Norton, 1974) is a book. "Brief Therapy: Focused Problem Resolution," *Family Process* 13, no. 2 (1974): 141–168, is a paper. The ten-session ambition that attached itself to the project is a research bet. It is not a coupon, and it is not this site's scheduling policy.

*Pragmatics of Human Communication* (1967), with Janet Beavin Bavelas and Jackson, is the earlier, thicker object: interaction as a science of messages, punctuation, symmetrical and complementary dances. Readers who only know MRI through a "paradoxical injunction" slide have skipped the 1967 book. Slides prefer paradox. Books prefer punctuation. This page prefers the book.

Brief interactional work treated the complaint as a loop: the attempted solution as the next turn in the problem. That sentence has been useful and over-sold. Useful, because it can stop a clinician from hunting a 1908 childhood while a 1974 household repeats a failed helpfulness. Over-sold, because a loop can become a cleverness that never asks who has less power in the dance. Feminist readers later said so. They were not spoiling the fun. They were reading the room.

Jackson died in 1968, young, before the 1974 books. The institute outlived him and then had to live without its psychiatrist-organizer. Institutes that lose a founder early become arguments about what the founder would have wanted. This pack will not settle the argument. It will keep 1958/59, 1967, and 1974 on the same shelf.

No MRI staff photographs. Type: Mental Research Institute, Palo Alto. A Stanford or VA *building* only if the file is PD/CC and captioned as architecture, not as "the birthplace of family therapy." Birthplace language is a kinship title in brick.

Milwaukee later took "brief" in a different direction — solutions, exceptions, a question about the future. MRI brief work is not SFBT. Confusing them is how a workshop sells two cities as one weekend. The Milwaukee essay will keep Milwaukee. This essay keeps the Palo Alto loop and the 1974 pagination.

A small-city clinician who still asks what people have already tried, and whether the trying has become the trouble, is near this hallway. Asking is not an MRI protocol. This series does not publish treatment protocols. It publishes the 1974 objects and the refusal to turn a session limit into a brand promise.

Managed care later loved "brief." Love from a payer is a political event. It is not proof that Weakland had insurers in mind when he wrote about focused problem resolution. Collapsing those two briefs is a common slander and a common advertisement. This pack will do neither.

## Two briefs

MRI brief work (1974) and Milwaukee brief work (1985) share a word and not a city. Workshop advertising sands them together because "brief" sells. This pack keeps 1974 as a loop about attempted solutions and 1985 as a question about what is already working. If a later draft in this folder mixes the two without a year, the draft is wrong.

Jackson's 1968 death left the institute arguing. Arguments about dead organizers are ordinary. Ordinary is not a reason to let 1974 talk as if Jackson wrote it. He wrote 1956 and 1967. Weakland, Fisch, Watzlawick, and Bodin wrote the 1974 paper. Authorship is the fence.

Payers later loved session limits. Love from a payer is not Weakland's research bet. Collapsing those two loves is a slander and an advertisement. This page does neither.

## Punctuation, not a slogan

The 1967 book cares about how a sequence is punctuated: who started it, who is reacting, who gets to name the start. Slogans prefer paradox. This page prefers punctuation because punctuation is harder to turn into a party trick.

Bodin’s name on the 1974 *Family Process* paper is easy to drop. This pack will not drop him. Four names, one pagination, a ten-session research bet. Bets are not coupons. Coupons are what later marketers sold.
