**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Lyman C. Wynne was born in 1923 and died in 2007. In 1958, with Irving Ryckoff, Juliana Day, and Stanley Hirsch, he published "Pseudo-Mutuality in the Family Relations of Schizophrenics" in *Psychiatry*. The paper named a brittle, too-agreeable surface in some interviews at NIMH. It was a careful observation. It became, in careless hands, another way to say the house was sick and the patient was its symptom. He spent later decades walking toward communication research and genetics both. A career can revise itself. A phrase, once loose in the culture, does not.

## 1958, a word, a climate

The paper sits on the same shelf as Fromm-Reichmann 1948, Lidz's Yale books, and Bateson 1956. Deborah Weinstein's later history of the "pathological family" is one place a reader can see the climate: postwar funding for a science of the household. Wynne was inside that climate, at NIMH, then at Rochester. He was not a family therapist in the Satir or Haley sense. He was a researcher whose noun entered the clinical vocabulary anyway.

Pseudo-mutuality: a surface of agreement that cannot stand a real difference. The observation can be true of a particular interview. The injury is what happens when the noun becomes a character reference for a mother. This pack's schizophrenogenic-theories essay is the place for that injury. This figure essay is the place for the man who has to live with a 1958 title.

Later expressed-emotion research (Brown, Birley, Wing; Vaughn and Leff) asked a different question — relapse, not origin. Do not collapse Wynne 1958 into EE, and do not use EE to launder 1958. The distinction is the whole ethic.

## Revision as a historical fact

Wynne's later work on communication deviance and on the genetics of psychotic illness is in the public scientific record. This draft will not inventory papers. It will say: he did not stay where 1958 put him. Staying would have been easier for the field's villains-and-heroes story. He complicated the story. Honor the complication. Do not use it as a redemption arc that makes the 1958 paper harmless. The paper is still on the shelf. Women still paid for the popular version.

Rochester, after NIMH, is an institutional address students can check. This page will not invent a colorful department story.

## Rubber fence; Singer; Rochester; a Finnish adoption file

Beside "pseudo-mutuality," Wynne's NIMH group used "rubber fence" for a family that looked open and admitted no new person or idea. The phrase sat in the same 1950s–60s file as Lidz at Yale and Bateson in Palo Alto. Later he worked with Margaret Thaler Singer on communication deviance — odd, hard-to-follow speech in some relatives of people diagnosed with schizophrenia — a research line that is not a dinner-table game and not a parlor diagnosis.

The University of Rochester was the later institutional home after NIMH. Finnish adoptive-family studies, in collaboration with Pekka Tienari and others from the 1970s onward, are the genetic-and-environment wing of the late career. He died in 2007. *Family Process* and psychiatric obituaries are the date sources. Early Wynne and late Wynne are different objects. A research ward is not a family-therapy school.
