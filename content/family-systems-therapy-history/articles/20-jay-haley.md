**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jay Haley was born on 19 July 1923 in Midwest, Wyoming, and died on 13 February 2007 in San Diego. He was the youngest name on the 1956 double-bind paper and the first editor of *Family Process* (1962–1969). He wrote *Strategies of Psychotherapy* (1963) as if therapy were a situation with a power problem. He wrote *Uncommon Therapy* (1973) as if Milton Erickson were a family therapist's secret ancestor. He wrote *Problem-Solving Therapy* (1976) as if a Tuesday needed a plan. He worked at MRI, at the Philadelphia Child Guidance Clinic, and at the Family Therapy Institute of Washington, D.C., with Cloé Madanes. He never sounded grateful to a school for long.

## A strategist, not an epistemologist

Bateson wanted to know how communication could be mapped. Haley wanted to know what to do after the map. That difference made him the writer students read when they were tired of weather and growth. It also made him the writer feminist critics read when they wanted an example of cleverness that could take a household's existing power and call it technique.

He edited the field's journal for eight years. An editor is a founder even when he is not a director. The first volumes of *Family Process* feel like his taste: experiment, film, a revolutionary mood, a later need to walk back a schizophrenia theory that had gotten loose. His 1969 farewell is a primary source. Read it as a man leaving a chair, not as scripture.

Philadelphia put him next to Minuchin's structure. Washington put him next to Madanes's strategic book (1981). The geography is a temperament. He took what he needed and left the church. Students remember sharpness. This page will not collect unsourced corridor stories. Public books only.

## Erickson, and the limits of this pack

*Uncommon Therapy* is a historical object about Milton Erickson (d. 1980). This pack is not a hypnosis series and will not become one. Haley used Erickson to authorize indirection: stories, tasks, a refusal to wrestle a symptom as if it were a confession. Indirection can be kind. It can be a stunt. The 1970s workshop circuit did not always tell those apart. Do not take a trance cue from a heritage essay.

His later writing about "ordeals" is a fight the field is still having. This essay will not revive it as a tip. A clever ordeal in a textbook is how a family gets hurt in a kitchen.

## Inheritance

A small-city clinician can steal one habit. Before you talk for forty minutes about a child's motive, ask what happens after dinner on Tuesday. Then ask who would have to change their Tuesday if the problem left. Then stop. The rest is training, a supervisor, and the 1978 papers Haley did not write.

Do not scrape institute headshots. He died in 2007. Type: Jay Haley, 1923–2007.

If a household is in trouble, this page is not the appointment.

The usable inheritance is a prose style as much as a school. Haley wrote short. He hated sentiment that had not earned its keep. Family therapy needed that hatred because Satir's warmth and Bowen's weather could become weather systems of their own. It also needed the critics who said his shortness could become a man's joke at a woman's week. Keep both on the shelf. *Strategies* and Hare-Mustin 1978 are the same decade's argument.

Midwest, Wyoming, is a useful birthplace for a myth of the American original. Do not lean on the myth. Lean on the dates: 1956 paper, 1962 editor, 1963 book, 1973 Erickson, 1976 problem-solving, Washington with Madanes, death in 2007. The myth will take care of itself. Heritage education should not help it.

He was not "the father of" anything this pack will say. He was a particular writer who made a field less pious and, at times, less careful. Both halves of that sentence are the job.

## Midwest, Wyoming, 1923 — a name that is also a town

He was born in a town actually named Midwest. Later writers cannot resist the joke. Resist it. The useful fact is the 1923 date and the 2007 date, and the 1962–1969 editorship between them. An editor for eight years is a founder even when he is not a director.

*Problem-Solving Therapy* (Jossey-Bass, 1976) is the book agencies could assign. *Strategies* (1963) is the book psychiatrists argued with. *Uncommon Therapy* (1973) is the book workshops sold. Three audiences, one writer who did not want to be grateful to a church. Ungratefulness is a style. It is also why Madanes's 1981 book had to exist: someone had to write the school so it could be taught without only quoting him.

San Diego, 13 February 2007. Public obituaries. This page will not collect them. The prose remains. So does Hare-Mustin 1978 on the same decade's shelf. Assign both.

## 1969, a farewell, a chair left

Haley's editor's farewell in *Family Process* 8, no. 2 (1969) is a primary source a student can read without a biography. He left the chair. The journal did not end. Leaving is a historical act. So is the eight-year run that preceded it. This pack will take the farewell over the workshop legend.

Washington's Family Therapy Institute is the later address. Philadelphia is the middle. MRI is the start. Three addresses, one writer who would not join a church. Madanes's 1981 book is the teachable remainder. Hare-Mustin 1978 is the ethical remainder. Assign the remainder. Do not assign an ordeal.

Wyoming in 1923 is a birthplace. San Diego in 2007 is a death. The prose is what sits between. Short sentences, a power problem, a refusal of sentiment that had not earned its keep. Sentiment that *has* earned its keep — a bruise, a hungry house — is not what he was refusing. Keep that distinction or do not use him.
