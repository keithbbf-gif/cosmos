**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Ivan Boszormenyi-Nagy was born in Budapest in 1920, came to the United States in 1950, and died in 2007. He spent the decades that made family therapy famous at the Eastern Pennsylvania Psychiatric Institute in Philadelphia, in a language that never quite sounded like Palo Alto. With Geraldine Spark he published *Invisible Loyalties* in 1973. With Barbara Krasner he published *Between Give and Take* in 1986. Loyalty, entitlement, ledger, relational ethics, multidirected partiality — the words are easy to poster. They were meant as a claim about justice between generations.

## A Central European gravity

Philadelphia in those years was crowded: Minuchin's clinic, Nagy's EPPI, later Aponte. Students could take a bus between dialects. Histories that treat the field as a sequence of California papers miss the city. Nagy's Hungarian training and the postwar migration are not color. They are why "loyalty" in his pages has a gravity Haley's irony does not.

His unit was the ethic of the relationship: who has given, who has been owed, who is silently paying a parent's unpaid bill. A child can remain faithful to a wounded parent by failing, by staying, by carrying a symptom that keeps the old account open. That sentence can be profound. It can also become a way to tell a woman she owes her father more fairness. Contextual therapy's later teachers had to keep saying: a ledger is not an order to reconcile with someone who hurt you. Fairness includes the right to a balanced account, and sometimes the balance is distance.

## Multidirected partiality, named and not taught

The clinician's attempt to be on more than one person's side, in turn, without becoming a judge who has already ruled — that is the technical name students underline. This page will not teach you how to do it. Doing it badly is how you abandon a child to a parent's charm. Nagy thought a neutral technician was a moral evasion. He was arguing with MRI as much as with psychoanalysis.

Feminist readers asked who keeps the books. If the unpaid labor is already a woman's, a therapy of "give and take" can become a second shift. The revolt essay is the place for that argument. Nagy is not excused from it.

James Framo's family-of-origin sessions sat in the same intergenerational neighborhood without being the same house. Keep the addresses straight.

## Inheritance

A small-city clinician can steal one question. Who would be hurt, silently, if this problem got better? Then stop. The answer may be "no one."

Do not use loyalty language to keep a person in an unsafe house. Do not print a blank ledger. Do not scrape EPPI photographs. Type: Ivan Boszormenyi-Nagy, 1920–2007.

If a household is in trouble, this page is not the appointment.

The usable inheritance is an ethic that is not a technique. Family therapy's other houses offered patterns, structures, tasks, warmth. Nagy offered a word — fairness — that American clinics can find embarrassing. Embarrassment is not a reason to skip the 1973 book. It is a reason to read it without a worksheet.

Budapest to Philadelphia is a short itinerary with a long century behind it. This essay will not embroider the emigration. Public record: 1920, 1950, EPPI, 1973, 1986, 2007. The rest, if it is family lore, stays off a heritage page.

Ordinary counseling still uses "loyalty" without the citation. A teenager who fails a grade the year a parent gets sick; an adult who cannot enjoy a marriage without feeling they have betrayed a lonely mother — those scenes are why the book stayed in print. Honor the scenes. Do not franchise the noun.

## 1920, Budapest; 1950, an arrival; 2007, a death

Those are the hard dates. Hungarian medical and psychiatric training, a postwar crossing, EPPI, two books with women coauthors. The hyphenated name is how American syllabi filed a Central European ethic. Filing is not understanding. Understanding is reading 1973 without a worksheet.

Multidirected partiality is a long phrase for a simple refusal: do not become the judge who has already ruled, and do not become the technician who pretends justice is none of your business. MRI could hear the second half as an insult. Nagy meant it as one. Insults between schools are historical data. This pack will not pick a winner. It will say Philadelphia had both schools on a bus line.

A ledger that sounds like church will be used like church: stay, forgive, owe. His later teachers had to write the un-churching. Distance can be a fair account. A violent home is not a loyalty test. Date 1973. Keep the warning. If you are in danger, call local emergency services.

## EPPI as a payroll, not a myth

Eastern Pennsylvania Psychiatric Institute paid for a research-and-training life that was not a private lodge and not a California nonprofit. State-facing institutes have different samples. A theory of justice between generations written in that payroll will sound different from a theory of paradox written on Middlefield Road. Difference is the point of a crowded Philadelphia.

Geraldine Spark, 1973. Barbara Krasner, 1986. Say the names when you say the books. Hyphenated filing is not solo authorship. A small-city clinician who never says "multidirected partiality" out loud can still refuse to be the judge who has already ruled. Refusal is the 1973 remainder. Reconciliation-as-assignment is the misuse. A violent home is not a loyalty test. If you are in danger, call local emergency services.

Between Give and Take is 1986, thirteen years after Invisible Loyalties. Thirteen years is a revision, not a sequel slogan. Krasner's name is on the later cover. Say it. A small-city hour that never uses his hyphen can still refuse a cheap reconciliation.
