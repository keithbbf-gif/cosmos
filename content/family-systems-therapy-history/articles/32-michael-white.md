**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Michael White was born in 1948 in Adelaide and died in 2008 in San Diego, after a workshop — a public fact reported at the time. He built the Dulwich Centre (from 1983) into a place that published, trained, and argued. With David Epston he published *Narrative Means to Therapeutic Ends* in 1990. The book treated problems as stories that had recruited a person, and it looked for the hours the story did not win. He had read Foucault. He cared about the practices through which people get made into cases.

## Adelaide is not Milwaukee

Narrative therapy is brief in mood and suspicious of excavation, like SFBT. It is not a clone. Externalizing — speaking of a problem as something a person is in relation to, rather than something a person is — is the move students remember. This page will not teach you to externalize. A cute name for a problem on a blog is how a 1990 book becomes a classroom skit. He was more careful than his imitators.

Australia's colonial archive is not a backdrop. White worked in a country still arguing with itself about whose stories get to be official. This essay will not decorate itself with motifs it does not have a license to use. It will say: do not import 1990 as a set of clever questions and leave the politics at the airport.

The Dulwich Centre continued after 2008. So did the habit of citing him as if he could still sign off on every franchise. He cannot.

## Letters, witnesses, thin files

Epston's letters were a clinical act. White's interviews tried to thicken a story a file had thinned. Later narrative practices invited audiences and witnesses to certify a new story. Those later practices have ethics problems (who is in the room, who is asked to applaud). History can name the problem. History cannot run the room.

American family therapy, by 1990, was already splitting into manuals and postmodernisms. Narrative arrived as a postmodernism you could still do on a Tuesday. That is why it was loved and why it was cheapened. A school worksheet that says "name your problem" is not Dulwich.

Do not externalize a person's oppression as if racism were a cartoon character they can outwit in four sessions. Hardy exists in this pack because that sentence needed him.

## Inheritance

A small-city clinician can steal one habit. When a file has already decided who a child is, ask for an hour the file cannot explain. Then believe the answer long enough to write it down. Then stop.

Do not scrape Dulwich or workshop photographs. Type: Michael White, 1948–2008.

If a household is in trouble, this page is not the appointment.

The usable inheritance is a suspicion of thin stories. Child guidance wrote them in 1915. Schizophrenogenic theory wrote them in 1948. Narrative therapy is one of the field's attempts to give the thin story back. Attempts fail in ordinary ways: they become brands, they become cute, they forget a bruise. White is not responsible for every failure. He is responsible for a book and a Centre.

1948–2008 is a short life for a founder of a late school. The San Diego death, after teaching, is the kind of public fact obituaries used. This page will not embroider it. The work is the 1990 book, the Dulwich papers, and a generation of clinicians who learned to ask who owns the meaning.

The general psychotherapy pack does not give him a figure essay; Insoo Kim Berg is that pack's brief-therapy face. This pack gives White and Epston both, because Adelaide and Auckland are not a Milwaukee sequel.

## 1948, Adelaide; 2008, San Diego

Adelaide is a city, not a metaphor for "down under." The Dulwich Centre, from 1983, is an address you could write to. San Diego in 2008 is where he died after teaching — a public fact. This page will not make a symbol of a workshop death. The work is the 1990 book and the Centre's list.

Foucault on a family-therapy syllabus is a risk. The risk is incense. The useful remainder is concrete: a file makes a case; a question can make the case less inevitable. If the hour cannot name the file, it is a skit.

He cannot sign off on franchises. 2008 is why. Living partners and later trainers can speak for themselves. Epston's essay is next door. Do not collapse Adelaide into Auckland. Two cities, one 1990 spine.

## 1989 selected papers; 1995 and 1997 books

White's *Selected Papers* (Dulwich Centre Publications, 1989) is the Adelaide object American libraries sometimes lack. *Re-Authoring Lives: Interviews and Essays* (Dulwich, 1995) and *Narratives of Therapists' Lives* (Dulwich, 1997) show the 1990s circuit-teaching years without treating every workshop as a second 1990 book. *Maps of Narrative Practice* (Norton, 2007) is the late diagram book. He died in San Diego on 4 April 2008, after teaching — a public itinerary fact. Do not make a symbol of a workshop city.

He published in the *Australian and New Zealand Journal of Family Therapy* before Norton made him an American syllabus week. ANZJFT is a regional serial, not a footnote. Students who only cite 1990 have skipped the journal that first carried Adelaide into print.

Community projects reported in Dulwich publications, including work with Aboriginal communities, are part of the archive. They are easy to tokenize. Cite a named Dulwich paper. Do not invent a reconciliation narrative for a header motif you cannot license. He cannot sign a franchise. 2008 is why.
