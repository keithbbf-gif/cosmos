**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Lynn Hoffman was born in 1924 and died in 2017. She published *Foundations of Family Therapy* in 1981 — a book that mapped the field while the field was still arguing with itself — and then spent later papers walking away from the strategic certainties the map had included. She is one of the American receiving stations for Milan. She is also one of the people who told the truth about how a receiving station changes the signal.

## A map, then a revision

*Foundations* is the object a student can still be handed when they want the schools in one place. Maps freeze. Hoffman did not stay frozen. The later work — toward a more collaborative, postmodern, less expert stance — is a historical event in her career, not a betrayal of 1981. People who only assign the textbook miss the revision. People who only quote the revision miss the textbook. She wrote both.

Milan's hypothesizing, circularity, and neutrality landed in American institutes partly through people like her who could translate without pretending they had invented the Centro. Translation is not fidelity. She knew that. Cecchin's later "curiosity" and the Boscolo–Cecchin training path are easier to understand if you have read Hoffman watching herself change her mind.

## From strategy toward not-knowing

The move from a planned intervention toward a conversation that does not pretend to own the meaning sits next to White and Epston, next to Harlene Anderson and Harry Goolishian (Houston Galveston; Goolishian d. 1991), and next to a 1990s mood the manuals were already refusing. Hoffman is a hinge inside one life. Hinge figures get used by both sides. Strategists can cite 1981. Postmodernists can cite the later essays. This pack will cite the hinge.

She wrote as a woman in a field of male founders, without making that the only subject. The Women's Project and Hare-Mustin are neighbors, not identical. Hoffman’s particular job was the history of ideas *inside* the interview.

Do not take a "not-knowing" slogan from this page and use it to sit still while someone is being hurt. Curiosity is not a freeze. Cecchin had to say so. So did she, in so many words, in later work.

## Inheritance

A small-city clinician can steal one habit. When a school you love stops fitting the room, say so on the page, not only in the corridor. Then stop. Changing your mind in public is a clinical ethic if you are a trainer. It is not a brand refresh.

The usable inheritance is a permission to revise. Family therapy's official memory likes people who founded a church and stayed. Hoffman mapped the churches and then walked. Walking is how a field avoids becoming a museum of 1974.

1924–2017 is a long life. She saw the heroic decades, the feminist revolt, the manuals, and the postmodern split. *Foundations* is 1981, the same year Madanes published *Strategic Family Therapy* and Minuchin left Philadelphia for New York. A later editor could make a calendar of 1981 alone. This pack gives 1981 three names and lets Hoffman be the one who would not stay where the year put her.

Harry Goolishian and Harlene Anderson deserve a later wave. This calendar had forty-four chairs. Hoffman sits in one as the American who made Milan thinkable and then made certainty less thinkable.

## 1924–2017, a map that would not stay framed

*Foundations* is 1981, Basic Books, the same hinge year as Madanes and Minuchin's New York move. She mapped the churches. Then she walked. Walking, in later papers, meant a more collaborative stance, less expert knowing, Milan received and then revised. Receiving stations change the signal. She said so.

Harlene Anderson and Harry Goolishian (Houston Galveston; he died 1991) are the American neighbors of that walk. This pack did not have a forty-fifth chair. Naming them here is honesty about a neighborhood.

## 1993 *Exchanging Voices*; 2002 *Intimate History*

*Exchanging Voices: A Collaborative Approach to Family Therapy* (Karnac, 1993) is the essay book that marks the walk away from strategic certainty. Students who only read *Foundations* (Basic Books, 1981) have the map and not the revision. *Family Therapy: An Intimate History* (Norton, 2002) is the late memoir-history: Ackerman Institute years, the MRI and Milan circuits as a receiving station, the later collaborative turn. Cite the year so readers know which Hoffman they have.

She worked in Amherst, Massachusetts, in the later decades — a small-city address, not a second Centro. Obituaries in 2017, including notices in the AFTA and *Family Process* worlds, are the date sources. Do not invent a last theory she never published.

Harlene Anderson and Harry Goolishian (Houston Galveston Institute; Goolishian died 1991) remain the American neighbors of the 1993 book. Forty-four chairs were not enough for their own essays. Naming them is honesty about a street. Assign 1981 and 1993, or admit you wanted a museum of strategy.
