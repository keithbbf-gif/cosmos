**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Jay Haley was born in 1923 in Midwest, Wyoming, and died in 2007 in San Diego. Between those dates he became the writer other family therapists read when they wanted to be less sentimental. *Strategies of Psychotherapy* (1963) treated therapy as a situation with a power problem, not as a confession booth. *Uncommon Therapy* (1973) made Milton Erickson's hypnosis and paradox available to people who would never become hypnotists. *Problem-Solving Therapy* (1976) and the Washington years with Cloé Madanes made "strategic" a school you could train in. The school is easy to parody as tricks. The parody exists because some of the tricks worked on the page better than they worked on a frightened family.

## From Palo Alto to Philadelphia

Haley was the youngest of the 1956 double-bind authors and the one who most wanted a method. Bateson wanted an epistemology. Jackson wanted an institute. Weakland wanted to watch. Haley wanted to know what to *do* on Tuesday. That difference made him useful and made him restless. He edited *Family Process* from its 1962 first issue through 1969, which meant he also decided, for eight years, what the new field would see of itself.

He worked at MRI, then at the Philadelphia Child Guidance Clinic under Minuchin, then, with Madanes, at the Family Therapy Institute of Washington, D.C., from the mid-1970s. The geography is a temperament: he took what he needed from communication theory and from structure and refused to join anyone's church. Students remember a man who could be generous on the page and sharp in a corridor. This essay will not collect corridor stories. Public books only.

Strategic therapy, as he and Madanes wrote it, planned an intervention. The clinician formed a hypothesis about what the symptom was *doing* in the household and assigned a task that would make the old pattern harder to keep. Sometimes the task was straightforward (parents who had stopped parenting were asked to parent at a specific hour). Sometimes it was paradoxical in the MRI sense (a demand that the symptom be produced on purpose). This essay will not give you a task. A task without a license, a supervisor, and a consent process is how a clever idea becomes a mess in a kitchen.

## Madanes, and a different accent on power

Cloé Madanes, Argentine-born, published *Strategic Family Therapy* in 1981. She had worked in Philadelphia and then in Washington. She wrote about power with less of Haley's Erickson romance and more attention to the uses of helplessness, to children who protect parents, to the difference between a playful directive and a humiliation. Later she wrote about violence and about the ethics of who gets protected in an hour. Living writers get extra care here: public books, no gossip, no health speculation. She was alive at last verification in 2026.

The Haley–Madanes partnership (they were married for a time; that is a public fact, not a plot) trained a generation that liked plans. Plans are not sins. Plans become sins when they treat a family as an opponent to outwit. Feminist critics in the late 1970s and 1980s said the quiet part: a therapy of directives will take the existing power in the house — usually male, usually adult — and call the result "strategy." Hare-Mustin (1978, 1980) belongs on the same shelf as *Problem-Solving Therapy*. If your training program assigned Haley and not Hare-Mustin, the program made a choice.

## Erickson as a historical object

*Uncommon Therapy* is a book about Milton Erickson, who died in 1980. Erickson is not a family therapist in the sense of this pack, and this pack will not become a hypnosis primer. The historical point is that Haley used Erickson to authorize indirection: stories, tasks, a refusal to wrestle a symptom head-on. Indirection in the hands of a gifted clinician can be kind. Indirection in the hands of a show-off is a stunt. The 1970s did not always tell those apart. Workshops sold out either way.

Do not take a trance instruction from this page. Do not take a paradox. If someone in a household is being hurt, cleverness is not the first verb.

## What not to do with strategy

Do not assign a "ordeal" you read about in a textbook. Haley's later writing about ordeals is a historical object and a fight the field is still having. This series will not revive it as a tip.

Do not use "resistance" as a way to win. A family that will not do your task may be telling you the task was stupid, unsafe, or aimed at the wrong person.

Do not scrape institute headshots. Haley died in 2007. Madanes is living. Type only.

A small-city clinician can steal one habit. Before you talk for forty minutes about a child's motive, ask what happens, Tuesday, after dinner. Then ask who would have to change their Tuesday if the problem left. Then stop. The rest is training, not a heritage page.

If a household is in trouble, this page is not the appointment.

## 1963, Grune & Stratton, a short book with a long temper

*Strategies of Psychotherapy* is not only a family book. Haley was writing about therapy as a situation — hypnosis, directives, a power problem — before he was only a family-therapy brand. The 1963 imprint (Grune & Stratton) is a medical-house object. The 1976 Jossey-Bass *Problem-Solving Therapy* is a training-house object. The shift in publishers is a shift in audience: from psychiatrists who might still be arguing with psychoanalysis to counselors who needed a Tuesday plan.

Milton Erickson died in 1980. *Uncommon Therapy* (1973) had already made him a character in family therapy's story. Characters get worshipped. This pack will not worship. It will say: Haley needed an ancestor who authorized indirection, and Erickson was available. Available ancestors are a historical fact, not a reason to run a trance in a kitchen.

Washington, D.C., in the mid-1970s was a city of institutes and associations. AAMFT's 1978 name change is next door in the calendar. Haley and Madanes were teaching a school while the association was deciding what to call the profession. Schools and associations are not the same object. A license does not make a directive kind.

The useful dated remainder is Haley's eight years as editor. An editor who is also a combatant will print the fight he is having. The first volumes of *Family Process* are that fight, bound.
