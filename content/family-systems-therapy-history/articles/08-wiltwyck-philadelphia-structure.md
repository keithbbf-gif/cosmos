**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1965 Salvador Minuchin left the Wiltwyck School for Boys, a residential school in Esopus, New York, and became director of the Philadelphia Child Guidance Clinic. He was forty-four, Argentine-born, trained at the William Alanson White Institute in Sullivan's interpersonal psychiatry, and already convinced that a long interpretive hour did not reach the action-oriented boys Wiltwyck housed. With Dick Auerswald, Charles King, Braulio Montalvo, Clara Rabinowitz, and later Bernard Guerney, Bernice Rosman, and Florence Schumer, the Wiltwyck group had installed a one-way mirror and taken turns learning how to interview families. *Families of the Slums* (1967) is the book of that experiment. *Families and Family Therapy* (1974) is the book that made "structure" a word students could underline.

## Wiltwyck was not Palo Alto

Wiltwyck's boys came, many of them, from poor New York neighborhoods. The staff, at first, did not. Minuchin later said the long, protected, interpretive techniques he had learned as an analyst did not help. The team went to the families. They watched organization: who spoke, who was left out, which child had been promoted into a spouse's job, where a boundary was so thin that a parent and child formed a pair against the other parent. They called the work, in time, structural. The word means, in this school, the invisible rules of who is with whom. It does not mean a lecture on architecture.

*Families of the Slums* is a dated study of a specific sample. It is not a character reference for a social class. Later readers, including Minuchin in later moods, had to watch how easily "disorganized" became a polite word for poor and Black and Latino households seen from a white clinic. The Philadelphia years made that problem louder. The clinic's clients were mostly African American or Latino; the staff, when he arrived, was mostly not. He recruited minority professionals and invented training paths for people the psychiatric establishment had not invited. That fact belongs in the same paragraph as the theory. A method that ignores who is on staff will misread who is in the room.

Do not use Wiltwyck photographs of boys as thumbnails. They were children in an institution. They did not sign a web license.

## Philadelphia, 1965–1975

Minuchin directed the Philadelphia Child Guidance Clinic from 1965 and resigned as director in 1975, remaining director emeritus until 1981. He also held appointments at Children's Hospital of Philadelphia and the University of Pennsylvania. The clinic grew from a small staff into a training center the field had to visit. Jay Haley worked there for a stretch; so did Cloé Madanes; so did a generation of people who later took structural ideas into community clinics. Harry Aponte's later ecological-structural work is one of those lines. It has its own essay.

Minuchin moved chairs. He asked a father to speak to a son while the room watched. He unjoined a pair that had gotten too tight and looked for a parental team that could hold a boundary. The theater of the hour was in service of a map: enmeshment, disengagement, coalition, triangulation, a parentified child. This essay names the words so a reader can hear them in the wild. It will not teach you to rearrange your living room. Rearrangement without a license and a supervisor is a parlor stunt. In a violent home it is worse.

*Psychosomatic Families* (1978), with Rosman and Baker, took the map into anorexia and other illnesses the era called psychosomatic. The claim — that family patterns and a dangerous symptom can keep each other going — is a research bet of its decade. Later eating-disorder medicine does not treat 1978 as a manual. History can say the book existed, that it made family interviews ordinary in some pediatric wards, and that "psychosomatic" is a period word that has been used to dismiss a body's pain. All three sentences can be true.

## After Philadelphia

In 1981 Minuchin left Pennsylvania and established Family Studies, Inc., in New York. After his retirement in 1996 the center took his name. He died in 2017 in Florida. The Minuchin Center still teaches a version of the model. Training centers outlive founders. They also decide, year by year, how much of 1967 they still want.

Structural therapy's honesty is that it would not pretend the rest of the house was scenery. Its scar is the temptation to treat a fifty-minute newscast of family week as an intervention, and to treat cultural closeness as "enmeshment" you have not earned the right to judge. A clinician who uses "boundaries" about a household whose language of kinship they do not speak is doing 1967 without the part where Minuchin at least knew he was a visitor.

## What not to do with structure

Do not use "parentified" as a joke about a competent oldest daughter in a working family. Sometimes a child has been asked to carry an adult. Sometimes a historian of the middle class has mistaken labor for pathology.

Do not use "enmeshment" as a verdict on an immigrant household that sleeps closer and calls more. Celia Falicov and Monica McGoldrick exist in this pack because this sentence needed them.

Do not scrape Philadelphia Child Guidance or Minuchin Center photographs. There is no clean licensed portrait. Type: Salvador Minuchin, 1921–2017.

A small-city clinician can steal one habit. When a child is the only name on the form, look at the pairs. Then look at your own pair with the parent who talks most. Then stop. The rest is training.

If a household is in trouble, this page is not the appointment.

## 1974, a Harvard spine, a room that moved

Harvard University Press, 1974: *Families and Family Therapy* is a university object, not a workshop handout. That fact changed who could assign it. A Harvard spine in a social-work program is a different permission than a 1967 Basic Books study of Wiltwyck. Both books are his. The 1974 one is the one that made "joining" and "unbalancing" words a student could be examined on. This page will not examine you.

Charles King and Clara Rabinowitz are easy to lose when the sentence becomes "Minuchin and Montalvo." The Wiltwyck team was larger than a pair. Dick Auerswald's later ecological work is a cousin of Aponte's. Cousins belong in a sources list even when they do not get a chair.

Philadelphia's Children's Hospital appointment meant pediatric wards heard family interviews as ordinary earlier than some adult psychiatry departments did. Ordinary is how a method becomes a default. Defaults need the 1978 psychosomatic book dated, and they need later medicine allowed to disagree.
