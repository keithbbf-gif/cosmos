**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Steve de Shazer was born in 1940 and died in 2005. With Insoo Kim Berg he founded the Brief Family Therapy Center in Milwaukee in 1978. He wrote *Patterns of Brief Family Therapy* (1982), *Keys to Solution in Brief Therapy* (1985), and *Clues* (1988). He had MRI in his rearview mirror and Wittgenstein on the desk. He wanted a therapy that did not pretend to see behind language. He liked exceptions, small changes, and the idea that you do not need a cathedral-theory of the problem in order to build a solution.

## Not a solo

Berg is not a coauthor in the courtesy sense. The Center was a partnership. This figure essay exists because de Shazer's books are the ones philosophy-minded students cite. Berg's figure essay exists because county buildings remember her ear. Read both.

MRI had said the attempted solution could be the problem. Milwaukee said: then stop studying the problem as if it were a monument. Look at the hours the monument is closed. The so-called miracle question, scaling questions, exception questions — this page names them as historical objects. It will not teach you to ask them. A numbered scale on a blog is a worksheet.

He wrote like a man who would rather be precise than warm. Precision is an ethic. It can also sound like a refusal to hear a bruise. Berg's clinical practice is part of why the model survived contact with mandated clients. De Shazer's prose is part of why the model survived contact with graduate school.

## What got cheap

Agencies wanted three sessions and a "strengths-based" sentence in every chart. Solution-focused work can be serious in ugly corners. It can become a cheerleader. De Shazer is not responsible for every flyer that used his name. He is responsible for a literature that was available to become a flyer.

Feminist and trauma clinicians asked the right rude question. If the problem is a violent partner, "what is already working" can keep a woman in a house. His better teachers built in safety. This pack will not pretend a 1985 book replaces a shelter. If you are in danger, call local emergency services.

## Inheritance

A small-city clinician can steal one habit. Ask when the trouble is less, and be genuinely interested. Then stop.

The usable inheritance is an anti-excavatory temperament that is not cynicism. People cannot always afford a five-year archaeology. Shortness is not a slogan. A slogan is "focus on solutions" said to a person who is still bleeding. Keep the temperament. Leave the slogan in the agency newsletter.

## 1940–2005, Milwaukee, not a punch line

Milwaukee is a city with a lakefront and county buildings. The Brief Family Therapy Center was a real address, not a metaphor for "the Midwest." European readers who met SFBT as an Italian or German training product still owe the address.

Wittgenstein on the desk is easy to parody. The parody exists because some later slides used "language game" as incense. His better pages are more concrete: what did you do when it was less bad. Concrete is the test. If a solution-focused hour cannot name Tuesday, it is a slogan.

## 1985, Norton, a key that is not a keychain

*Keys to Solution in Brief Therapy* is the spine agencies remember. *Patterns* (1982) is earlier and more family-institute. *Clues* (1988) is later and more language. Three books, one Center, a 1978 founding. He did not sit on the 1956 paper. He inherited it and moved the question. Moving the question is how a field avoids a Palo Alto museum.

Wittgenstein on the desk is a fact about his reading, not a license for incense. Berg's ear is a fact about the Center, not decoration. 2005 is a death. Coaching is a migration he cannot sign. ## 1991 and 1994 books; a 2007 posthumous spine

After *Clues* (1988) he published *Putting Difference to Work* (Norton, 1991) and *Words Were Originally Magic* (Norton, 1994). The 1994 title is the Wittgenstein book in public. It is also the book later slides reduced to "language games" incense. The pages themselves stay closer to Tuesday: what did you do when it was less bad. *More Than Miracles: The State of the Art of Solution-Focused Brief Therapy*, with Berg and colleagues, appeared from Haworth in 2007 — after his 11 September 2005 death in Vienna, on the road, and in the same season as Berg's last working years. A posthumous "state of the art" is a historical object. It is not a license for a living franchise to speak as if he still chaired the Center.

The Solution-Focused Brief Therapy Association (SFBTA) formed after the bricks-and-mortar Milwaukee address closed. Associations are not clinics. Coaching icebreakers that borrowed scaling questions are not the 1978 Center. Date 1978, 1982, 1985, 1988, 1991, 1994, 2005, 2007. Skip a flyer that skips the dates.

European institutes later taught SFBT as if it had been born in a translation. The birth address is Milwaukee. MRI is the grandparent. Wittgenstein is a reading list, not a mascot.
