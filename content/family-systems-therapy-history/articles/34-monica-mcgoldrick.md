**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

Monica McGoldrick published *Ethnicity and Family Therapy* in 1982, with John K. Pearce and Joseph Giordano, and *Genograms in Family Assessment* in 1985, with Randy Gerson. With Betty Carter she edited the family-life-cycle volumes that trained a generation to think in stages without thinking only in white nuclear ones. She was alive at last verification in 2026. Living writers get extra care: public books, the Multicultural Family Institute as a public address, no gossip, no scraped photographs.

## 1982, a syllabus changes

*Ethnicity and Family Therapy* made ethnicity ordinary on American training lists. Ordinary is not the same as done. A syllabus that assigns McGoldrick in week fourteen and Bowen in weeks one through six has told you which map is real. The 1982 book is a historical event: the field could no longer pretend "the family" was an unmarked ethnicity without at least seeing a chapter title that said otherwise.

She is not a Bowen clone, though the genogram has a Bowen-era grandfather. She pushed gender and culture into a drawing that could have stayed a Tennessee diagram. Carter's life-cycle work sits next to hers; they are partners on a spine, not a footnote and a star.

## The genogram as a historical object

The 1985 book taught a drawing tool. This series will not print a blank genogram for a reader to fill in. That would be a worksheet. The historical point is that American counseling made a family map ordinary, and that ordinary maps carry ordinary biases: who counts as kin, whose marriage is drawn in a solid line, whose cutoff is a moral story. Boyd-Franklin and Falicov exist in this pack so that sentence has somewhere to go besides a McGoldrick chapter.

Do not treat a genogram as a verdict. Date it. Erase it. The Bowen era essay already said so. McGoldrick's later editions kept adding households the first edition could under-see. Later editions are a form of intellectual honesty. They are also a reminder that 1985 is a date.

## Later editions; *Revisioning Family Therapy*, 1998

*Ethnicity and Family Therapy* went to a second edition in 1996 and a third in 2005, still Guilford, still many chapter authors. A sentence that says "McGoldrick says Irish families…" must check whether the chapter is hers or a contributor's. *Genograms: Assessment and Intervention* is the later title of the 1985 Gerson book; Norton reprinted and expanded it in 1999 and again in the 2000s. Cite the edition on the desk. This series still will not print a blank chart.

She edited *Revisioning Family Therapy: Race, Culture, and Gender in Clinical Practice* (Guilford, 1998; later editions). Hardy appears among the contributors who rewrote the field's racial silence. The 1998 volume is a syllabus event in the same decade as Boyd-Franklin's second edition (2003) and Falicov's 1998 spine. Date the contents page.

The Multicultural Family Institute in Highland Park, New Jersey, is the later public training address. It is not a second AAMFT. Do not invent accreditation.
