## Resilience after the heroic decades

By 1998 the field had blamed mothers, mapped structures, assigned tasks, revolted about gender, and begun to write manuals. Walsh's book is a late-century attempt to talk about strength without becoming Milwaukee's cheaper cousin. Resilience, in her usage, is not a greeting card. It is a set of processes — belief systems, organization, communication — that a household may already be using. This page names the 1998 event. It will not print a resilience checklist. A checklist is a worksheet.

She sits next to Boss (ambiguous loss, 1999) and McGoldrick (ethnicity, life cycle) as a Chicago-and-syllabus neighbor. She sits next to Berg as someone who asked what was already working, without being SFBT. Keep the addresses straight. Cheerleading is the failure mode all of these share. Walsh's better pages know a household can be resilient and still be in an unjust order. Boyd-Franklin and Hardy exist so that sentence has race in it. Aponte exists so that it has poverty in it.

Later editions kept expanding whose families count as the ones that "come through." Later editions are a form of honesty. They are also a reminder that 1998 is a date, not a timeless noun.

## Against the deficit file

## 1982 *Normal Family Processes*; 1999 spiritual resources

Walsh edited *Normal Family Processes* (Guilford, 1982; later editions through the 2000s and 2010s). "Normal" in that title is a fighting word: she meant diversity of form, not a 1950s sitcom.
