**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In 1948 Frieda Fromm-Reichmann published "Notes on the Development of Treatment of Schizophrenics by Psychoanalytic Psychotherapy" in *Psychiatry*. Buried in that paper is a sentence that named a mother as a kind of climate. The adjective — schizophrenogenic — left the paper and went to work in hospitals, textbooks, and living rooms. This pack will print the citation. It will not reprint the theory as a finding.

The 1950s and early 1960s were crowded with household etiologies of psychosis. Theodore Lidz and colleagues at Yale wrote about marital "schism" and "skew." Lyman Wynne, Irving Ryckoff, Juliana Day, and Stanley Hirsch published "Pseudo-Mutuality in the Family Relations of Schizophrenics" in *Psychiatry* in 1958. Bateson, Jackson, Haley, and Weakland published the double-bind paper in 1956. Different vocabularies. A shared temptation: to locate a terrible illness in a pattern of talk at the dinner table.

Some of those writers later qualified, recanted, or watched biology return to the conversation with a force that made the dinner table look small. Some families never received the qualification. A mother who spent 1962 being treated as a toxin does not get those years back because a 1980 textbook grew polite. History's job is to keep both the paper and the injury visible. A clinic site's job is to refuse to sell the injury as insight.

Women who were already doing the unpaid work of hospitals — visiting, translating, taking the patient home — were the easiest people to turn into causes. Fathers appeared in some of the theories as absences or as tyrants. The child with the diagnosis appeared as a messenger. The messenger theory later became a family-therapy habit even in rooms that had dropped psychosis: the identified patient as the family truth-teller. That habit can be a kindness. It can also be a script that makes a child's symptom do too much moral work. This page will not assign that script.

Race and class again. A theory that prosecutes a mother is not evenly distributed. Poor mothers met it as casework. Middle-class mothers met it as analysis. Black mothers in the United States met it on top of a longer American habit of treating their households as problems for the state. A later essay on Boyd-Franklin exists so this paragraph does not become the only sentence.

Why include these papers in a pack that refuses their conclusions? Because family therapy did not appear in a clean room. It appeared in a decade that was already staring at households for an explanation of madness. Some of the same people — Jackson, Wynne, Lidz as neighbors — sit in both stories. If we skip the prosecution, the later "systems" language sounds like a gift. It was also a continuation. A system can be a way of not naming a villain. It can also be a way of renaming her.

Biological psychiatry's later rise is not this pack's victory lap. Brain stories can be crude too. The point is narrower. A history site does not get to be nostalgic for a clever family theory of schizophrenia. Clever is not a clinical ethic. The families who lived under those theories are not a seminar.

Fromm-Reichmann was a serious clinician, a refugee, a Chestnut Lodge figure. Holding her seriousness and holding the damage of a word she loosed is the same task. Hagiography is one failure. A sneering demolition that forgets the hospital world of 1948 is another. The word did the harm. The word is in *Psychiatry*. The word is not coming to Southeast Arkansas as advice.

If a later popular article still talks as if cold parenting caused a psychotic illness, that article is not "classic family therapy." It is a 1948 sentence that outlived its paper. This folder will not give it a second life under a warmer brand.

## What a walk-back does not repair

Some of the 1950s authors later said they had overreached. Walk-backs belong in the record. They do not refund a decade of visiting hours in which a mother was treated as a climate. A clinic site that prints the walk-back as if it were a happy ending has failed the families who lived under the adjective.

Biological psychiatry's later confidence is not this pack's ally. Brain stories can be crude, and they can erase a household that still has to live with an illness. The point is narrower and meaner: a clever family theory of madness is not a heritage to warm up. Cleverness is how 1948 traveled.

Lidz's Yale shop and Wynne's NIMH and Rochester years are neighboring objects, not villains this page will try for sport. Neighboring is accurate. Sport is a seminar vice.
