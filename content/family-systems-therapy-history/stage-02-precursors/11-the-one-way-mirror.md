**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

A one-way mirror is a piece of architectural glass with a lighting trick. In family therapy it became a school trademark: the family in the bright room, the team in the dark one, a phone that could ring in a suggestion, a tape that could be replayed in a conference. The glass is easy to describe. The power is easier to forget.

Live supervision did work that a process note cannot. A trainee could be stopped mid-sentence. A senior person could see a coalition form in a chair arrangement. Minuchin's Philadelphia hours, MRI's brief projects, the Milan team's later ritual of leaving the room to consult — different cities, same appetite for a second pair of eyes. The appetite is pedagogical. It is also hierarchical. Someone is unseen. Someone is seen.

Families were not always told, in the early years, what the glass was doing. Consent forms thickened later, under ethics codes, lawsuits, and a general American tightening around recording. This pack will not pretend to have audited every 1968 clinic. It will say the obvious thing: a technology of secret seeing will, unless watched, treat secrecy as method. Secrecy can protect a trainee's nerve. It can also make a family into a specimen.

Tom Andersen's "The Reflecting Team," *Family Process* 26, no. 4 (1987): 415–428, is one later answer. The team spoke in front of the family. The glass, in that telling, became a conversation instead of a hide. Andersen was writing from northern Europe, from a different welfare and courtesy culture, and the American import of the reflecting team became, like every import, a workshop module. This page will not teach the module. It will keep 1987 as a date when the field had to argue with its own favorite wall.

Tapes are the mirror's cousin. A tape can be a kindness to a trainee. A tape can travel to a conference without the family's last name and still carry their walk. Minuchin tapes taught a generation how a structural hour looked. They also taught a generation that looking was allowed. Boyd-Franklin's later work, and a wider Black-family scholarship, asked who was safe in front of a lens. That question is not an "also." It is a correction to the romance of the glass.

Stock photography loves a mirror. This series will not. A photograph through glass of a family, even a hired family, even a smiling family, reenacts the specimen. Graphics agents get type, or an original diagram of a *room plan* with no people in it. No faces. No children. No "diverse family" silhouettes as a workaround. Silhouettes are still bodies.

A small-city clinic may never have owned a mirror. Most will not. The inheritance is not the hardware. It is the habit of talking about a household as something a professional team has the right to see whole. Sometimes that habit is the only way a silent child gets an adult conversation around them. Sometimes it is a crowded ambush. History cannot decide your Tuesday. It can keep the glass from sounding like a neutral scientific instrument.

WOW Therapies, on this page, installs no mirror and offers no live-supervision service. The sentence is dull on purpose. Dullness is how a claims fence sounds when it is working.

If you came here for a floor plan you can build, you will not get one. If you came here to understand why a 1970s training film still circulates with a reverence that would be strange in any other branch of medicine, the reverence is the object. Glass made family therapy look like a laboratory. Laboratories have subjects. Subjects have a later literature called consent. This folder would rather be late to the laboratory and early to the literature.

## Hardware and habit

Most American clinics never bought the glass. They bought the habit: talking about a household as something a team has the right to see whole. Habits travel farther than hardware. A rural staffing pattern of one clinician and a borrowed office can still talk as if a team were watching. That talk is the inheritance.

Consent forms thickened under ethics codes and under lawsuits this writer will not invent. Thickening is incomplete. Workshop videos still move. This pack will not link them. Linking is a second seeing.

Andersen 1987 is one answer. Boyd-Franklin 1989 is another. A 2026 trainee who has never seen a mirror still lives in their weather. Weather is not a floor plan.

## Portrait

Never a photograph through glass of a family. Type, or an empty room-plan drawing.
