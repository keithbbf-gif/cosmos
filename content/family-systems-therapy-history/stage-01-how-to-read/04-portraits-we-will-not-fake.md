**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The assignment for this pack is narrower than a magazine's usual hunger for faces. **Portrait notes, public domain or Creative Commons only.** If the file page is not clean, the post is type. A generated "Virginia Satir in 1964" is a lie with good lighting.

That rule is not an aesthetic preference. It is the only way a history series on a clinic site stays out of two traps at once: copyright theft, and the softer theft of inventing a witness.

## What we opened

The Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery holds Imogen Cunningham's 1963 gelatin-silver portrait of Gregory Bateson (NPG.2003.8). The museum page says usage conditions apply. The copyright line is © 1963 Imogen Cunningham Trust. That is a museum object a reader may look at. It is not a featured image this folder will upload.

The Library of Congress holds Mead–Bateson field photographs from Bali and the Sepik, 1936–1938. Many of those frames show other people — children, a puppet maker, a village hour. They carry usage conditions. They are not Bateson's headshot. They are not clip art for "the family." Using a Balinese child's body to decorate an essay about an American clinic would be a third ethical failure on top of license and caption.

Wikimedia Commons has a Satir gallery that, as last opened, surfaces grave photographs and thin or mislabeled files. A grave is not a portrait license for a living memory, and it is a cruelty to use as a thumbnail. Commons also holds a 2004 Festival della Scienza photograph of Mary Catherine Bateson, CC BY-SA 2.0. That file is not Gregory Bateson. Cropping a daughter into a father is the kind of "historical composite" this pack exists to refuse.

Bowen Center, Satir Global / Avanta, the Minuchin Center, MRI, the Ackerman Institute, Dulwich Centre, Milwaukee BFTC, AAMFT, AFTA: their photographs are institute and workshop property. A "headshot" page is not a Commons file. Scraping one because the person is dead is still scraping.

## Why the table is almost empty

Family therapy became a public field after 1950. U.S. copyright for a 1960s press photograph is still in force in 2026 unless the file was dedicated or published as U.S. government work. Bell's 1961 monograph is a public-health object; its *text* is easier to quote than its *plates*, if any. *Family Process* covers are journal property. Guilford, Norton, and Harvard jackets are not public-domain art.

Living writers — McGoldrick, Boyd-Franklin, Falicov, Boss, Walsh, Hardy, Johnson, Epston, Madanes — are not public property because they publish. Their faces stay off this site unless a clean CC file appears and a human re-reads the file page on upload day.

## What to print instead

Type. "Murray Bowen, 1913–1990." "Virginia Satir, 1916–1988." "Salvador Minuchin, 1921–2017." Dates are facts. A likeness we do not own is not a fact we are allowed to borrow.

Period objects, if the scan is clean: a pre-1930 title page of a book that is actually old enough; a GPO or museum photograph of a *building* captioned as architecture; a typographic treatment of *Family Process* 1(1), 1962, that does not scrape the cover. An original diagram drawn for this series — a map of Palo Alto, Georgetown, Philadelphia, Milan, Milwaukee, Adelaide — is a drawing, not a face.

Alt text should name the frame. "Type treatment: Salvador Minuchin, 1921–2017, cream field, black letters." Not "the man who saved poor families." Captions that editorialize are how a graphic becomes a claim.

## What a graphics agent must not do

Do not ask an image model for "a compassionate 1970s family therapist with a genogram." Do not colorize a PD building and call it Philadelphia Child Guidance. Do not crop a group photograph so a bystander becomes "the" founder. Do not flip a face for layout. Do not use a one-way-mirror photograph of a family, ever. The glass was a clinical technology. It was not a stock category.

If the writer and the graphics agent disagree, `PORTRAIT_SOURCES.md` wins. A beautiful unlicensed face does not ship. A series template — wordmark plus essay title — ships every time the table says `no-portrait`.

This is the least glamorous essay in the folder. It is also the one that keeps the rest from becoming a costume department. Family therapy already has a history of seeing people without their consent. We will not add a second seeing, in pixels, on a site that answers a phone in Southeast Arkansas.

The later figure essays will repeat a short Portrait section. The repetition is the fence. If you are tired of reading "no clean licensed photograph," you have understood the assignment.
