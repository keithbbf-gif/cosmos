**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

The survey pack already has an essay that walks from Bateson to Satir to Minuchin to Milwaukee in the space of one sitting. Its slug is `families-systems-therapy`. Its job is a calendar of the whole psychotherapy house: Pinel, Freud, Rogers, Beck, Bowlby, Fanon. Family systems appears there as one hallway.

This folder is not a second hallway painted the same color. It is the rooms off that hallway, with the doors open long enough to see the furniture.

If you came here for Freud's century, go back. If you came here for Van Riper, you are on the wrong site lane — that is SLPWOW. If you came here for Beck's 1979 manual, there is a CBT deepen pack that will not be rewritten here. If you came here for Hayes and a Worcester basement, that is the ACT and mindfulness pack. Each of those folders has a fence of its own. This one is narrower and, on purpose, more stubborn.

## What the survey already said

The sister essay opens on a child who will not speak at dinner and refuses to let that sentence become a liberation slogan or a prosecution. It names the 1956 Palo Alto paper and the walk-back. It gives Satir her growth language and Minuchin his chairs. It gives Milwaukee a last movement. It warns that a theory of families can injure.

Those sentences stay true. This pack will not "correct" them by retelling them with more adjectives. It will sit longer in the objects the survey had to pass: *Family Process* 1(1); Bell's 1961 public-health monograph; the Wiltwyck authorship line; Hare-Mustin's 1978 essay; the Women's Project's 1988 book; Andersen's 1987 paper; Boyd-Franklin 1989; Falicov; Boss's ambiguous loss; Hardy's public work on race.

The survey figure essays on Satir, Minuchin, and Insoo Kim Berg are biographies at calendar scale. The figure essays here are deepens. They assume a reader can hold two pages on the same person without needing one of them to die.

## What this pack will not steal

It will not steal Pinel's asylum, Dix's petitions, or Clifford Beers's memoir. Those belong to the heritage calendar.

It will not steal a thought record, an ABC, or an IAPT graph. Those belong to the CBT deepen.

It will not steal a body scan or a hexaflex. Those belong to the ACT pack, which already refused to teach them.

It will not steal fluency shaping, an Iowa laboratory, or a swallowing heritage. Those belong to SLPWOW.

Theft in this house looks like a cross-link that pretends to be a new essay. The honest cross-link names the slug and sends the reader. This page is that link, written once so later drafts can point here instead of repeating the map.

## Why a practice site has two family pages

Because a survey that must also reach Freud and Fanon cannot pause for Georgetown's 1975 Family Center or for the difference between *Peoplemaking* and *Conjoint Family Therapy*. Because a rural reader who has heard "Bowen" in a church workshop and "Minuchin" in a graduate syllabus deserves to know they are not one man with two last names. Because mother-blame has a paper trail, and a single paragraph in a survey cannot carry that trail without flattening it into a scold.

WOW Therapies is still a Southeast Arkansas practice brand whose public face, as last fetched, is occupational, physical, and speech services. Two history folders about families do not secretly add a marriage-and-family department. They add literacy. Literacy is cheaper than a fake service line and more honest than a booking widget under a 1956 citation.

## How to move between the pages

If you want the whole house, start at the survey INDEX. If you want this hallway, stay. If you want Satir as one figure among forty, read the survey portrait. If you want Satir as MRI's first training director and as a woman whose public book outran her training book, read the deepen. The same rule applies to Minuchin and to Berg.

If you want attachment, the survey has Bowlby and Ainsworth. This pack has a later essay on what happened when attachment language returned to a couple room under Sue Johnson's public books. That essay is a neighbor, not a raid on the Bowlby page.

If you want trauma, the survey has Herman. This pack will mention tapes, consent, and the one-way mirror. It will not rewrite *Trauma and Recovery*.

The test of a good sister is that the two pages can sit on a table without either pretending to be the only one. This folder fails that test if it repeats the survey's first three movements and calls the repetition depth. Depth is a 1962 masthead, a 1967 five-author Wiltwyck book, a 1978 feminist essay, a 1987 reflecting-team paper — objects the survey did not have room to live inside.

A later editor who finds a paragraph here that is only the survey rewritten should cut the paragraph. The sister already did that work.
