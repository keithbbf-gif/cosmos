# Claims guardrails — microbiome / probiotic research pack

Internal. Not a blog post. Counsel still owns the last read.

These essays teach how the research is built. They must not become intended-use documents for a live-microbe SKU. A sentence that would be illegal next to an add-to-cart button is illegal as a "science explainer" on the same site.

## The statute, not the vibe

Under DSHEA (Pub. L. 103-417) and 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff), a dietary supplement is a food category. It is not a drug. A product is a drug if it is intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease (21 U.S.C. § 321(g)(1)(B)). Intended use is read from labels, websites, social posts, and customer-service scripts.

Structure/function claims are allowed if they are truthful, not misleading, and substantiated, and if FDA is notified within 30 days and the DSHEA disclaimer appears (21 CFR 101.93). "Supports a healthy gut flora during antibiotic use" is a different legal object from "prevents *C. difficile* infection."

FTC's *Health Products Compliance Guidance* (December 2022) is the advertising rule. Competent and reliable scientific evidence, usually well-controlled human studies, for the claim as a reasonable consumer would read it. A mouse paper and a podcast do not get you there.

FDA does **not** treat "probiotic" as a defined product class. Live microbes sold to treat disease are **live biotherapeutic products (LBPs)** and are regulated as drugs (FDA guidance, June 2016, *Early Clinical Trials With Live Biotherapeutic Products*). Fecal microbiota products cleared or approved for recurrent *C. difficile* (Rebyota 2022; Vowst 2023) are drugs. They are teaching cases, not aisle analogues.

## Never say (or imply)

### Disease and infection

- Prevents, treats, cures, mitigates, or diagnoses *C. difficile*, IBD, IBS-as-disease, NEC, pouchitis, SIBO, SIFO, leaky gut-as-diagnosis, autism, depression, anxiety, obesity, diabetes, NAFLD, cancer, COVID-19, UTI, bacterial vaginosis, or any other disease.
- "Natural FMT," "at-home transplant," "replaces vancomycin," "C. diff protocol."
- Quoting an AGA disease-prevention row next to a consumer SKU as if the bottle inherited the guideline.

### Infants and pregnancy

- Home use of probiotics to prevent NEC, sepsis, or colic-as-disease.
- Dosing a preterm infant from a DTC site.
- "Safe in pregnancy / breastfeeding" as a blanket.

### Brain, mood, weight

- "Psychobiotic" as a clinical class that treats depression or anxiety.
- "Fixes your metabolism," "obesity bacteria," "lean microbiome in a capsule."
- Personalized stool or DNA kits that "tell you which probiotic to take" as a diagnosis or prescription.

### Quality theater

- "Pharmaceutical grade" as a consumer claim.
- "FDA approved probiotic supplement."
- "Clinically proven gut health" as a category claim for an untyped blend.
- "50 strains is better."
- A supplier CFU-at-manufacture sheet treated as end-of-shelf finished-product evidence.
- "Heavy-metal free" / "pathogen free" without method, limit, lab, and lot.

### Restoration myths

- "Repopulate your microbiome after antibiotics" as a guaranteed rebuild.
- "Balance" / "reset" as if there were a reference microbiome for that reader.
- Treating "dysbiosis" as a billed diagnosis.

## Allowed shapes (still need substantiation if used on a SKU)

- "Contains [genus species strain] at [CFU] through end of shelf, lot-tested for identity and [named contaminants]."
- "This strain was used in [named human study] at [dose] for [duration] measuring [named structure/function endpoint]." Hedged. Disclaimer on.
- "Live microorganisms, when administered in adequate amounts, are the scientific definition of a probiotic (Hill et al., 2014). This product's file [does / does not] meet those three words."
- Educational description of hospital research, drug approvals, and guideline rows — clearly labeled as *not* supplement indications.
- "Not evaluated by FDA. Not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."

## Process

1. If a sentence would be illegal on a label, it is illegal in a blog caption that sits next to a bottle.
2. Influencer briefs get the same list. A Reel is labeling if you paid for it.
3. When evidence is mixed (IBS meta-analyses; metabolic taxa), say so. Do not average a drug trial into a 2-billion-CFU gummy.
4. New isolates, next-gen anaerobes, phage SKUs, and pasteurized *Akkermansia*: check the *dated* regulatory category before anyone writes "probiotic" on the pack.
5. No reader dosing. No stacking instructions. No "start with…".
