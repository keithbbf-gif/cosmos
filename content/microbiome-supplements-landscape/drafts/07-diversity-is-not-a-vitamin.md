**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). A diversity index is not a nutrient and not a treatment target for a consumer product.

Stool-kit PDFs love a rainbow and a number: your Shannon index, your Simpson, your observed features. The number is a summary of how mixed the *reads* look after a pipeline. It is not a vitamin. You cannot be deficient in Shannon. You cannot replete Shannon with fifteen unlabeled strains. Shade's 2017 *ISME Journal* essay (PMID 27482925) put the warning in the title: diversity is the question, not the answer.

## What the index is

Alpha diversity summarizes one sample: how many kinds, and how evenly they are read. Beta diversity compares samples. Both depend on depth, rarefaction, and whether you are counting OTUs, ASVs, or species-level bins. Change the region of 16S and the number moves. Compare your 2026 kit to an HMP table and you may have compared two different instruments (draft 04).

A drop in stool alpha diversity after broad-spectrum antibiotics is a repeatedly observed *direction*. That does not make "increase diversity" a legal or scientific endpoint for a gummy. Observed direction in a research setting is not a structure/function claim.

## Sites that refuse the rainbow

Ravel et al., 2011 (PMID 20534435), described vaginal community state types in reproductive-age women. Several common types are dominated by one *Lactobacillus* species. That is low diversity. It is also a typical pattern, not a failure state. Calling those communities "dysbiotic" because a stool-trained intuition wants more colors is a category error (drafts 06 and 35).

Skin sites differ from each other. The mouth is not the colon. HMP 2012 made site one of the first facts. A brand that prints "supports microbial diversity" without naming a site is talking about a statistic that does not live in one place.

Even in stool, more is not a virtue in every story. A community can be diverse and include organisms you would not celebrate. A community can be less diverse and metabolically capable because of functional redundancy (draft 03). If function can be shared, taxon counting is a shadow.

## The 15-strain non sequitur

Marketers treat strain count as a diversity supplement. Swallow fifteen names, get fifteen bars. That is not how engraftment works, and it is not how an index is built. Most swallowed isolates do not take up long-term residence. A transient pass-through can still be studied for a host endpoint — that is a trial design, not a diversity play. If the cells do not persist, yesterday's Shannon is mostly still yesterday's residents plus some DNA you ate.

A mix also has no claim on "better than one" without a trial of the mix. Hill 2014's default is strain specificity. A pile of strains is a pile of untested interactions plus a larger QC problem (draft 22).

## What a paper can honestly do with the number

Pre-specify it as an exploratory ecological readout. Report the metric and the pipeline. Do not hide that it is relative to sequencing depth. Pair it, if you can, with a host endpoint that a person would recognize: a symptom score in a trial, a tolerance endpoint, a biomarker you locked. If the only thing that moved is Shannon, you have an ecology note. Ecology notes do not go on the front of the bottle.

If a kit prints you in the "low" tercile of its own customers, you have learned how you sit in their marketing database. You have not learned that you need a product.

## Language that survives

Say "alpha diversity (Shannon) on this V4 pipeline." Say "we did not use diversity as a sales target." Do not say "restores a rich microbiome." Do not say "more strains, more diversity, more health." That sentence has three leaps and no citation.

The reader habit is the same as in the enterotype piece (draft 05): high-dimensional data, humans want one number, the colon did not agree to be graded. When later drafts talk about fiber or diet moving a community (drafts 29–30), they will talk about named taxa or named functions where the papers do — not about buying a rainbow.

## Evenness, richness, and the number people mean

Shannon mixes richness and evenness. Simpson leans on dominants. Observed features lean on depth. A kit that prints "diversity" without naming the metric is already fog. A kit that compares your Shannon to a cohort sequenced at a different depth is comparing instruments. Rarefaction is not a moral act. It is a way to stop pretending a 80,000-read sample and a 8,000-read sample saw the same world.

Faith's phylogenetic diversity adds branch length. It is still a summary of a tree you chose. Change the tree, change the virtue.

## Antibiotics, recovery, and the "repopulate" reflex

Stool alpha diversity often drops after broad-spectrum antibiotics and then recovers on a clock that depends on the drug, the diet, and the person. That direction is real in many papers. It does not make "increase diversity" a legal endpoint for a gummy. It does not make a 15-strain pile a rebuild kit. Recovery, when it happens, is mostly the residents returning — not a tourist isolate taking a lease (draft 30).

If a trial uses Shannon as a primary endpoint in healthy adults, ask why a person should care. If the answer is "because the kit is red," you have a circular product.

## What to print instead

Print the metric, the pipeline, and "exploratory." Print a host endpoint if you have one. Do not print "rich microbiome" as a compliment that implies a purchase. Do not print strain count as a diversity supplement. Draft 35 is the site that breaks the rainbow. Draft 03 is the functional redundancy that makes taxon counting a shadow.
