In the European Union, the nutrition and health claims regulation sent companies to EFSA with dossiers. For live microbes, the 2009–2010 wave is famous for how often the answer was no: strain characterization incomplete, benefit not specific, population wrong, studies not transferable. The word "probiotic" itself became a problem in some member states because it was read as a health claim. Italy and a few others later wrote national ways to keep a word on a pack under conditions. `[VERIFY]` the dated Italian Ministry of Health page before an editor prints a rule. The teaching fact is not a country league table. It is that **Hill’s scientific compliment and a European claim authorization are different objects.**

## What EFSA was asking for

A defined strain, a defined benefit, a defined population, and human studies that actually tested that chain. Species-level blobs failed. "Gut health" failed for being a blob. Disease claims belong in a different EU legal box anyway. If a founder says "EFSA-approved probiotic," they are usually wrong. If they say "EFSA-evaluated and not authorized," they may be describing the modal outcome of that wave. Name the opinion if you name an outcome.

Later dossiers sometimes did better on characterization and still failed on benefit. Characterization is necessary and not sufficient. That sentence should sound familiar (drafts 01 and 09).

## FOSHU is not a loophole

Japan’s Foods for Specified Health Uses is a permission system with its own science bar, its own logo rules, and its own list. A FOSHU-approved product is a Japanese regulatory object. It does not move a US 101.93 file. It does not move an EFSA authorization. Printing a Japanese seal on an English DTC page is how you import a confidence you did not earn in the jurisdiction where the cart sits.

Other Asian systems (Korea’s health-functional foods, China’s blue-hat, and so on) are their own homework. This pack will not fake fluency. It will say: bring the dated page or do not imply a blessing.

## The US, again, for contrast

DSHEA lets you *speak* a structure/function sentence if you can substantiate it and notify and disclaimer. It does not make the sentence true. FTC still reads the ad. The word probiotic is not banned. That is why the US aisle is loud, and why this pack spends so many pages on the scientific definition: the statute will not do the cleanup for you.

Canada’s NNHPD, Australia’s TGA listed complementary medicines — same moral. A license number is a local object.

## House style

Never "approved in 73 countries." Never a collage of seals. If a piece mentions EFSA, mention the refusal wave and the characterization-plus-benefit bar. If it mentions FOSHU, mention jurisdiction. If a founder wants a global SKU, they want counsel in each place, not a prefix.
