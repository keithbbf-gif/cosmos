**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). A heat-killed preparation is not a probiotic as Hill 2014 uses the word.

Hill et al. 2014 put "live" first on purpose. A dead cell can still present molecules to an immune system, can still be a dose of fragments, can still show up in a trial. It cannot meet the FAO/WHO–ISAPP definition of a probiotic. The aisle has spent a decade trying to keep the word anyway: tyndallized, heat-killed, inactivated, "ghost," "postbiotic" as a synonym for last year's overstock after a temperature excursion. Salminen 2021 is the cleanup on the last of those words (draft 26). This essay is the fork.

## Why houses kill cells on purpose

Stability. Allergy to the idea of live organisms in some channels. A trial arm that surprised everyone (Depommier 2019's pasteurized *Akkermansia*; draft 21). A desire to sell into conditions where live cells make clinicians nervous. Those are reasons. They are not a license to keep the live-cell papers.

Tyndallization is a specific, old sterilization idea (repeated heat to catch spores that germinate between cycles). Marketing uses it to mean "kindly killed." Ask for the actual process: temperature, time, whether anything is still plateable, how identity is confirmed after death. qPCR will still see the DNA (draft 13). A plate should not.

## What dies with the cell, scientifically

Engraftment stories die. "Repopulation" dies twice — it was already a bad noun (draft 06). Any mechanism that required metabolism in the lumen dies. Mechanisms that were about a cell-wall molecule or a leftover metabolite might live. "Might" is a new trial, not a highlighter on the old one.

CFU on a dead product is a category error. If they print AFU or genome copies instead, read draft 13. If they still print CFU, the file is confused or the kill step failed.

## What dies with the cell, legally

In the US, you still have a food or supplement or, if you claim to treat disease, a drug. The kill step does not get you out of intended-use law. It may change GRAS/NDI and novel-food conversations because the article of commerce changed (draft 40). A live isolate's GRAS notice is not automatically a killed preparation's notice.

Calling a dead powder a probiotic is, on ISAPP's terms, false. Calling it a postbiotic is only true if it matches Salminen's definition: a preparation of inanimate microorganisms and/or their components that confers a health benefit on the host — with the preparation characterized, not a random lysate soup. Draft 26.

## How to write the carton if you are being careful

Name the organism that was grown. Name that it is inactivated, and how. Name the human study on the *inactivated* article. Do not print "live and active." Do not print a live-cell trial. Do not print a CFU that is a memory.

If you are an editor: treat "tyndallized probiotic" as a banned phrase in this pack. It is two words that cancel each other, plus a third that hopes you will not notice.

## A process card the COA should be able to fill

Temperature, time, whether anything is still plateable, how identity is confirmed after death, how potency is now expressed (not CFU), whether the kill step is validated on this matrix. qPCR will still see DNA (draft 13). A plate should not. If the plate lights up, the kill failed or the plant is dirty.

Tyndallization as marketing means "kindly killed." Ask for the actual cycle. Repeated heat to catch germinating spores is an old idea. Most "tyndallized probiotic" lines are a contradiction plus a third word that hopes you will not notice.

## What a new trial must be about

The inactivated article. Not the live cousin. Mechanisms that required metabolism are gone. Mechanisms about a leftover molecule might remain — "might" is the trial. Depommier 2019's pasteurized arm is a celebrity pointer, still exploratory (draft 21). Do not let celebrity characterize your powder.

GRAS/NDI and novel-food files can reset because the article changed (draft 40). Intended-use law did not get kinder because the cells died (draft 38).

## House ban

"Tyndallized probiotic," "live postbiotic," and "dead but still probiotic" are fail phrases in this pack. Use inactivated, name the method, or use postbiotic only when Salminen 2021 is met (draft 26). Wave 4 is the prefix cleanup that this fork made necessary.

## Inventory that cooked

A stability failure is a deviation, not a launch. Relabeling last year's live SKU as a postbiotic after a warehouse event is how a prefix launders a CAPA. Preparation, in Salminen 2021, is designed. Accidents are accidents. Part 111 wants the deviation file, not a rebrand calendar.

If you meant to kill the cells, the process card and the zero plate come first. Then a trial of the inactivated article. Then a naming decision that does not keep "probiotic." Then a regulatory check because the article changed (draft 40). That order is the opposite of a prefix in search of a leftover pallet.

## Pocket card

Live is the first word. Killing it is a new article. Process card: temperature, time, zero plate, new potency unit (not CFU), identity after death. A warehouse cook is a deviation, not a postbiotic launch (draft 14). Salminen 2021 is a designed preparation (draft 26). GRAS/NDI may reset (draft 40). Intended use did not get kinder (draft 38). Banned phrases: tyndallized probiotic, live postbiotic, dead but still probiotic. Celebrity pointer: Depommier pasteurized arm, still exploratory (draft 21). Order: process, trial of the inactivated article, naming, regulatory check — not a prefix on a leftover pallet.
