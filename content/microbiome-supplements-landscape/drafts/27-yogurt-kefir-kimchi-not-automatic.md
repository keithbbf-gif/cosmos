Hill et al. 2014 did a useful, unpopular thing: they said fermented foods with undefined microbial content are not automatically probiotics. Live cultures can still be there. The food can still be part of a reasonable diet. The three load-bearing words (draft 01) are not a souvenir you get for souring milk. Metchnikoff's yogurt (draft 02) sits on the far side of that sentence — a practice and a theory, not a strain file.

## Yogurt, the teaching food

Traditional yogurt is *Streptococcus thermophilus* and *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* (names subject to the usual nomenclatural weather). Those cultures ferment lactose. Some people who are lactose intolerant tolerate yogurt better than milk because of that work — a food-science sentence with a long, specific literature. `[CITE NEEDED]` if an editor wants a named EFSA or NIH ODS paragraph on yogurt and lactose, rather than a vibe.

The US "Live & Active Cultures" seal is an industry threshold for certain organisms at manufacture, not an ISAPP benefit file, and not end-of-shelf CFU of a named probiotic strain. Heat-treated yogurt after fermentation is a different object: flavor without the live line.

A yogurt that has had GG or another studied isolate *added* at a studied dose is a food plus an isolate. The isolate can have a file. The yogurt vehicle is then part of the matrix (draft 09). Most grocery cups are not that.

## Kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut, kombucha

Kefir grains are a mixed community that is not the same from fridge to fridge. Kimchi and sauerkraut are house and season. Kombucha is a SCOBY plus sugar, and sometimes leftover alcohol. These are cultural and sensory objects. They are poor capsule analogues. "Contains probiotics" on a kombucha can is often a prefix on a ferment with no strain ID and no Hill benefit.

Home ferments vary more. That is the pleasure and the QC nightmare. This pack will not give you a recipe, a safety protocol, or a CFU target for a crock.

## What a food label can honestly say

"Fermented." "Contains live cultures." The organism names if you know them and they are still true at sale. A studied isolate, named, if you added one and can show the dose. "Probiotic" only if Hill's words are true of a defined microbe in that food. The 2014 paper was written because that last sentence was being skipped.

Do not say "more probiotic than a supplement" because the jar is large. Dose is not jar size. Do not say "the original probiotic" because a cuisine is old. Age is not a definition.

## Live & Active, and other seals that are not Hill

Industry seals that set a manufacture-day threshold for certain yogurt cultures are food-channel objects. They are not end-of-shelf CFU of a named probiotic strain. They are not ISAPP benefit files. Heat-treated yogurt after fermentation is a different object: flavor without the live line. A cup that later receives GG at a studied dose is food plus an isolate (draft 16). Most grocery cups are not that.

Lactose digestion in yogurt has a specific food-science literature. `[CITE NEEDED]` if an editor wants a named ODS or EFSA paragraph rather than a vibe. That literature still does not make every sour cup a probiotic under Hill 2014.

## Home crocks and kombucha cans

House kimchi and sauerkraut vary. That is cuisine. It is a QC nightmare if you try to turn it into a capsule analogue. Kombucha cans that print "contains probiotics" are often a prefix on an undefined SCOBY plus leftover sugar and sometimes alcohol. This pack will not give a recipe or a CFU target for a crock. It will refuse the prefix.

## A grocery-aisle script

Cup: live cultures named, or heat-treated and honest about it. Seal: industry threshold at manufacture, not Hill. Isolate added: then the isolate can have a file, and the cup is a matrix (draft 09). Kefir: mixed community, fridge-to-fridge. Kimchi: house and season. Kombucha: SCOBY, sugar, sometimes alcohol, often a prefix. None of these automatically meet the three words.

A gummy that prints kimchi is a flavor. A kit that prescribes sauerkraut from a bar is a costume (draft 31). Metchnikoff remains a book (draft 02). The honest prestige of a jar is that it is a food. Leave it that way.
