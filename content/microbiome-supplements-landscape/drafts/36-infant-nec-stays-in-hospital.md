This essay will not discuss home use of live microbes in infants. Necrotizing enterocolitis is a hospital disease. Colic, allergy, and sepsis are not cart nouns.

AGA 2020’s most serious probiotic rows sit in neonatology (Su et al.; Preidis et al.; draft 10). Preterm, low-birth-weight infants and NEC are the words. Those words are why a careful pack mentions the guideline at all — and why a careful pack then shuts the door. A NICU protocol, a hospital pharmacy, a neonatologist, and a product that may not even be a dietary supplement in that use: that is the room. A parent, a phone, and a dropper from a wellness site: that is a different room. This essay exists to keep them from sharing a sentence.

## Why the evidence is the least borrowable

The population is not your customer. The endpoint is a disease. The administration is clinical. The organisms, when named, are specific combinations, not a grocery blend. The risk conversation includes infection in a fragile host. Rare harms that are acceptable to discuss in a NICU consent are not "safe for the whole family" on a pouch.

Society documents besides AGA (ESPGHAN and others) have their own dated pages. `[VERIFY]` before an editor adds a named row. None of them, read honestly, are Amazon copy.

## The guilt products that try anyway

C-section, formula, "the birth microbiome," a drop that "gives back what the canal would have given." Delivery mode does move early communities in research (draft 19). That is not a moral failure and not a SKU. Bode’s HMO story is milk science (draft 28), not a parent-shaming powder. "Pediatric gummy" versions of adult blends are a QC and dosing problem even before claims (the sister supplements pack has a kids-gummy piece; this pack will not add a flavor).

Colic-targeted products sit in a grey of parental despair. This pack will not review them. It will say: infant fuss is a clinician conversation. Disease-shaped endpoints are drugs.

## Safety, said once

Live microbes have been associated, in case reports, with infection in vulnerable hosts. That sentence is enough for an educational wall. We will not inventory cases. We will not print a dropper schedule. We will not say "generally recognized as safe in babies." GRAS is not a baby (draft 40).

The dropper that wants NICU prestige is the object this wall was built for. Serious pediatric rows exist because the population is fragile and the endpoint is a disease.
