**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). Bacterial vaginosis, yeast infection, UTI, and fertility are outside this essay’s mouth. A swab kit is not a diagnosis.

Ravel, Gajer, Abdo, and colleagues (*PNAS*, 2011; PMID 20534435) described the vaginal microbiome of reproductive-age women as community state types, several of them dominated by a single *Lactobacillus* species (*L. crispatus*, *L. iners*, *L. gasseri*, *L. jensenii* in the paper’s language — nomenclatural weather applies). Low diversity is common. That sentence should have killed a decade of stool-trained "raise your diversity" copy in this site (draft 07). It did not, because kits and capsules needed a deficiency.

## What CST language is

It is a clustering-and-ecology tool for a specific site, a specific population, a specific set of methods. It is not a diagnostic code. It is not stable in every woman every week of a cycle. It is not a moral ranking, though some research programs treat *L. crispatus*-dominant communities as a state they want to study in relation to clinical outcomes. Study is not a DTC script. Outcomes in those papers are often disease or obstetric — **clinic room**.

*L. iners*-dominant states have a more mixed reputation in the literature. Mixed reputation is not "buy our crispatus." Identity, live dose, and a legal endpoint would still be required, and disease endpoints would still be drugs.

## Oral versus vaginal products

An oral capsule that claims to "support vaginal flora" is making a story about translocation or systemic effects that needs a file, not a vibe. A vaginal product may be a cosmetic, a device, a drug, or something a regulator will re-label when they read the box. This pack will not sort a catalog. It will say: site of administration changes the object. Stool papers do not travel. GG’s gut file does not become a vaginal file because the genus used to be *Lactobacillus* (drafts 08 and 16).

## Kits

A consumer vaginal swab sequenced like a stool kit inherits every 16S limit plus a more intimate intended-use risk (drafts 04 and 31). If the PDF says BV, it is speaking a diagnosis. If it says "unbalanced" and sells a product, it is speaking dysbiosis in a new chair (draft 06). If it says CST-III and a shopping list, it has turned Ravel 2011 into a vending machine.

## House style

No deficiency language. No "restore your feminine microbiome." No implied STI or fertility claim. No cute branding of a species as a personality. Cite Ravel when you need the typology. Cite HMP when you need "site first." Send clinical questions to clinicians.

Draft 36 is the other population this pack will not let a DTC site swallow: preterm infants and NEC rows.

## Cycle, population, and the statue problem

Ravel 2011 described reproductive-age women with specific methods. Communities move across a cycle. A single swab is a single swab. Treating a CST badge as a standing diagnosis is the enterotype error in a more intimate chair (draft 05). *L. iners*-dominant states have a mixed research reputation. Mixed reputation is not "buy our *crispatus*." Identity, live dose, and a legal endpoint would still be required. Disease and obstetric endpoints stay in clinic.

Nomenclatural weather applies to the 2011 species words. Print current combinations plus strain if you have an isolate. Species-level morality is still a bad habit.

## What a swab PDF should not contain

BV as a verdict. Fertility nouns. STI nouns. A shopping list mapped to a CST badge. "Restore your feminine microbiome." A cute species personality. An oral GG citation used as if site did not matter. Cycle-blind statue language.

What it may contain, if anyone insists on selling a research-shaped report: methods, a snapshot, a refusal to diagnose, a pointer to a clinician. Ravel 2011 is a typology for a population and a method. It is not a report card. Low diversity can be typical here (draft 07). Draft 36 is the other population a cart must not swallow.
