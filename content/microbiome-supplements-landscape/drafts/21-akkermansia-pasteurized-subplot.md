Muriel Derrien, Elaine Vaughan, Caroline Plugge, and Willem de Vos described *Akkermansia muciniphila* in 2004 (*IJSEM*; PMID 15388697): a mucin-degrading inhabitant of the human gut. The organism became a celebrity because observational work tied its relative abundance to metabolic traits people want, and because Patrice Cani's group and others pushed mechanistic and then human work. Celebrity is a research fact. It is not a reference range.

## What Depommier 2019 actually is

Depommier, Everard, Druart, and colleagues published a proof-of-concept exploratory study in *Nature Medicine* (2019; PMID 31263284) in overweight and obese volunteers, with placebo, live, and pasteurized arms. Read the paper for the endpoints they measured and for the limits they stated. Do not let a deck turn an exploratory n into a category claim. This pack will not reprint metabolic numbers as if they were a label.

The teaching fork is the pasteurized arm. Heat-killed cells are not "live microorganisms." If that arm is the interesting one, you have left Hill 2014's first word. You may have entered Salminen 2021's postbiotic conversation — *if* the preparation matches that definition, which is stricter than "we cooked a probiotic" (draft 23 and 26). Or you have a novel food / ingredient file in a particular jurisdiction. `[VERIFY]` the dated category before anyone prints "probiotic *Akkermansia*" on a pasteurized powder.

## The kit problem

Consumer 16S reports love a red *Akkermansia* bar. V4 may not even treat the genus fairly. Relative abundance is not cell count. "Low" against an HMP-ish mean is a methods choice (drafts 03–04). Selling a bottle from that bar is the prescription pattern draft 31 exists to refuse.

Even a beautiful shotgun strain-level call does not tell you a commercial isolate will engraft, or that engraftment would do a thing you want. Mucin dwellers live in a niche. A capsule is not that niche with a lid.

## Live manufacturing is not a slogan

*A. muciniphila* is anaerobic enough to humble a plant that was built for *Lactobacillus*. People who actually culture it talk like process engineers, not like lifestyle brands. If a supplement house added the name last quarter and cannot show anaerobic process control and end-of-shelf identity, the name is ink (draft 20).

Safety and novel-food files differ by country. Europe and the United States do not share a brain. A press release about one jurisdiction is not a US DSHEA sticker.

## How to keep the organism in an educational pack

Name the 2004 description. Name the 2019 study as exploratory, with a pasteurized arm that complicates the slogan. Refuse lean-microbe folklore (draft 34). Refuse kit-to-cart. Refuse to call a pasteurized powder a probiotic.

If a later human file on a *named* preparation is larger and clearer, an editor can add a dated note. Until then, *Akkermansia* is a research organism with a famous subplot — not a personality type, not a deficiency, and not a reason to trust a red bar.

## Jurisdiction as part of the name

A press release about a novel-food or GRAS-shaped conclusion in one country is not a US sticker and not Hill's word. Pasteurized powder and live powder are different articles (draft 40). If the carton says probiotic and the process says cooked, the carton is false on ISAPP's terms. If the carton says postbiotic, Salminen 2021 still wants a characterized preparation and a host benefit (draft 26).

Lean folklore will try to sit on this organism every year. Send it to draft 34. Kit bars will try to sit on it every quarter. Send them to draft 31. Keep Derrien 2004 and Depommier 2019 as the dated objects. Do not let a red bar become a personality.
