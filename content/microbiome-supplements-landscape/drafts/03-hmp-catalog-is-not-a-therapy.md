In June 2012, *Nature* published two Human Microbiome Project consortium papers that still get paraphrased on sales decks: one on the structure, function, and diversity of the "healthy" human microbiome (PMID 22699610), one on the research framework (PMID 22699609). NIH had stood the project up in 2007. The point was to *describe* — body sites, healthy adult volunteers, 16S and shotgun data, a shared methods spine — so later work had something other than folklore to stand on. Two years earlier, the European MetaHIT consortium had published a gut microbial gene catalogue (Qin et al., *Nature* 2010; PMID 20203603). Catalogs are tools. They are not therapies.

## What those papers measured

They measured who was present, at what relative abundance, in stool and several other sites, in people who had been screened as healthy by the project's rules. They showed that two healthy people can look unalike at the taxon level and still share a lot of *functions* — metabolic pathways that different species can perform. That sentence is the one marketers skip. If function can be shared across taxa, then "missing *Akkermansia*" on a consumer 16S report is not automatically a deficiency, and "adding *Akkermansia*" is not automatically a repair. Draft 21.

They also showed that body site dwarfs many other variables. Oral communities are not stool communities. Skin is not vagina. A capsule aimed at "the microbiome" as if there were one is already lost. Draft 35 is the vaginal version of that warning; this piece is the general one.

What they did not measure: a reference range you can bill against. A dose of anything. A ranking of probiotics. A proof that higher diversity is healthier in every site (it is not; draft 07). A list of species a supplement must contain to be "complete."

## Healthy cohort is a methods choice, not a moral state

HMP's "healthy" was inclusion and exclusion criteria — not a cosmic baseline. People with certain diagnoses, certain medications, certain body-mass cutoffs were out. That is how you build a clean first catalog. It is also how you guarantee the catalog is not "America," not "the species," and not your customer who just finished doxycycline.

When a stool-kit company colors your chart against "HMP healthy," ask which release, which body site, which pipeline, and whether the comparison is even licensed. Then ask what decision the color is trying to force. If the decision is "buy this blend," the catalog is being used as a vending machine. Draft 31.

iHMP (2019 *Nature*; PMID 31142853) followed people through pregnancy, inflammatory bowel disease, and prediabetes — condition-specific, longitudinal, multi-omic. That is closer to clinic-adjacent research. It still does not authorize a DTC capsule named after a pathway.

## MetaHIT's gene book

Qin 2010 reported a few million non-redundant gut genes from a defined set of stool samples, mostly European, with some comparison cohorts. The number people remember (often "3.3 million") is a catalogue size, not a vitamin. Later work enlarged and reclustered the book. If you cite a gene count, cite the paper and the year. Do not treat the first number as eternal.

A gene catalogue lets you ask "is this function present in this stool." It does not tell you the organism is alive in the person, or that a capsule of a distant cousin will install the function. Shotgun data can infer function better than 16S. Inference is not colonization. Draft 04.

## How the aisle borrowed the logo

"Based on the Human Microbiome Project" has appeared on copy that means, at best, "we used public taxon names." That is like printing "based on the Human Genome Project" on a multivitamin. The project sequenced. It did not formulate.

A worse borrow: "restores the microbiome the HMP found." HMP did not find one microbiome. It found variation, site specificity, and a lot of genes. Restoration implies a prior state you should return to. For a healthy adult who was not sampled last year, that prior state is a myth. For a patient in a transplant protocol, the prior state is a clinical object in a drug file. Different rooms. Draft 37.

## What to take, as a reader of later papers

Take the habit of asking which cohort, which site, which method, which year of the database. Take the humility that "healthy" was a screen. Take the functional redundancy point: taxon charts lie about uniqueness of function. Leave the fantasy that a 2007–2012 methods program secretly published a recipe for a 15-strain bottle.

If a founder wants a true sentence for a deck, here is one: public catalogs made it possible to *name* organisms and genes more consistently. Naming is not dosing. The dose, if any, still has to come from a human study on the isolate you put in the hopper — or you stop using the catalogs as if they were a pharmacopeia.

## Sample size, geography, and the quiet "healthy"

HMP Phase 1 was a US-centered methods program on screened adults. It was never a world atlas. MetaHIT was mostly European stool plus comparison cohorts. Later catalogs — including larger gene books and the expanding GTDB — changed counts and names. If a kit still says "compared to HMP," ask which release, which body site, and whether the kit's region of 16S even matches the table. A mismatch is not a diagnosis. It is a methods argument wearing a traffic light.

The word "healthy" in a consortium title is an inclusion screen. Medications, BMI cutoffs, and diagnosed disease were out for a reason. Your customer on a PPI, or six weeks after travel, or pregnant, was not the object. iHMP tried to follow people through specific conditions. That is a different design. It still does not publish a capsule recipe.

## Function versus taxon, again, because decks skip it

The 2012 structure paper's useful shock was functional redundancy: different taxa, similar pathway inventories. A founder who wants to "replace missing *Faecalibacterium*" is betting that the function is unique and that the bottle can install it. Both bets are empirical. The first is often false. The second is draft 20. A catalog that lists genes is a way to *ask* the function question. It is not a yes.

Shotgun depth, dehosting, and the database year change which genes you think you saw. Cite the year. Do not treat Qin 2010's first headline number as a constant of nature.

## What a methods program owes a later brand

Naming. Shared pipelines. A public argument about what "healthy" meant. That is a lot. It is not a SKU. The honest deck sentence is: public catalogs made it cheaper to be wrong in a more specific way. Specific wrongness is progress. It is still wrong to print "HMP-based formula."

Draft 04 is the instrument that made the catalogs. Draft 31 is the checkout that pretends the catalogs were a pharmacy. If you need a photo caption that will not get you in trouble: "A sampling program, 2007–2012. Not a recipe."
