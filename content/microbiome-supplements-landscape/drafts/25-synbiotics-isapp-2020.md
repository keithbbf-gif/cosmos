In 2020, Swanson, Gibson, Hutkins, and colleagues published the ISAPP consensus on synbiotics (*Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology*; PMID 32826966). The word is older than the paper. The paper was an attempt to stop the word from meaning "we put a fiber in the probiotic bottle." A synbiotic, on their terms, is a mixture comprising live microorganisms and substrate(s) selectively utilized by host microorganisms that confers a health benefit on the host. The benefit has to be measured. The live part has to be live. The substrate has to be a substrate in the 2017 prebiotic sense, or at least selectively used — not a random excipient.

## Complementary versus synergistic

A **complementary** synbiotic is a probiotic and a prebiotic that each have their own evidence; they are co-administered because you want both jobs, not because you have proven a duet. A **synergistic** synbiotic is designed so the substrate is used by the co-administered microbe (or by the host community in a way that depends on the pair) to produce a benefit you would not claim for the parts alone. Synergistic is a harder trial. It is also the version marketers imply when they say the fiber "activates" the strain.

If you have not run the pair, you do not have synergistic. If you have not even run the parts, you have a hopper.

## Co-packaging is a process decision

Inulin in the same capsule can kill a live isolate with water activity (draft 14). It can also be fine. That is a stability file, not a synbiotic file. A sachet with two compartments is a different process object than a blended powder. The 2020 definition is about biology and benefit, but Part 111 will still ask whether the finished product meets specs. A definition from ISAPP does not overfill your bottle.

## The live word still applies

If the cells are dead, you do not have a synbiotic with a probiotic component. You might have a substrate plus a postbiotic-ish powder — which is a new naming problem (drafts 23 and 26). Do not stack prefixes to hide a kill step.

The microorganism still wants a strain ID. "Synbiotic blend" is "clinically studied blend" with a fiber (draft 15).

## How to read a carton

Look for the isolate, the substrate chemistry, the doses of both at end of shelf, and a human study of the *combination* if they said synergistic — or of each part if they said complementary, plus a reason they are together. If you find two prefixes and a billion-count, you have found a vibe.

Editors in this pack: do not use synbiotic unless you mean Swanson 2020. Do not use it as a transition word between probiotic and prebiotic paragraphs. The next essay is postbiotic, which is the prefix people reach for when live becomes inconvenient.

## Two protocols, written as cartoons

Complementary: isolate A has a human file; substrate B has a human file; you put them in one sachet because a person might want both jobs. You do not claim a duet. Synergistic: you designed B to be used by A (or you designed the pair so the community does a thing the parts did not), and you ran the pair. Marketers imply synergistic when they say the fiber "activates" the strain. If you have not run the pair, you do not have synergistic. If you have not run the parts, you have a hopper.

## Process can veto a definition

Inulin in the same capsule can kill the live line with water (draft 14). Two compartments can save both specs and still fail Swanson if there is no benefit file. Part 111 does not care that ISAPP published a word in 2020. It cares whether the finished product meets specs.

Dead cells plus a substrate are not a synbiotic with a probiotic component (drafts 23 and 26). Do not stack prefixes to hide a kill step. "Synbiotic blend" is draft 15 with a fiber.

## Reading a carton in thirty seconds

Isolate, substrate chemistry, both doses at expiry, and a combination study if they said synergistic. If you find two prefixes and a billion-count, you have found a vibe. Editors: do not use synbiotic as a transition word between paragraphs. Use it when you mean Swanson 2020.

## Activation as a verb to delete

"The fiber activates the strain" is synergistic copy without a synergistic trial. Delete the verb unless the pair was the intervention. Complementary is allowed when both parts have files and you do not claim a duet. Co-packaging without files is a hopper. Water activity can veto a pretty pair (draft 14). Dead plus substrate is a different naming problem (draft 26).

Thirty-second carton read: isolate, chemistry, both expiry doses, combination study if they said synergistic. Two prefixes and a billion-count is a vibe. Editors will not use the word as furniture between paragraphs.

## One more refusal

A sachet with a live line and a sweet fiber will be called synergistic in the first sales meeting because the words sound like they belong together. Swanson 2020 does not care about the meeting. If the pair was not the intervention, delete "activates." If the parts were not studied either, you have a hopper with two prefixes. If the fiber killed the cells with water, you have a process failure wearing a 2020 word. Print the isolate, the chemistry, and the two expiry numbers, or do not print the word.
