**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). "Postbiotic" is a scientific definition in this pack, not an FDA category.

ISAPP convened a panel on inanimate preparations because the aisle needed a word and was using several badly. Salminen, Collado, Endo, and colleagues published the consensus in 2021 (PMID 33948025). A postbiotic, as they used the term, is a preparation of inanimate microorganisms and/or their components that confers a health benefit on the host. Preparation is load-bearing. Inanimate is load-bearing. Benefit is load-bearing. A broth of unknown fragments is not a preparation in their sense. A live product that cooked in a warehouse is not a postbiotic; it is a deviation (draft 14).

## What the panel was trying to keep out

Random metabolites in a dropper ("our postbiotic butyrate") — butyrate is a chemical; it may be a metabolite; it is not automatically a postbiotic under a definition that starts with microorganisms and their components. Cell-free supernatants with no characterization. Last year's probiotic, relabeled. "Paraprobiotic" and other competing coinages used as if they were interchangeable without a file.

Read the paper's scope notes if you need to argue a metabolite-only product. This pack will not stretch the word to cover a short-chain fatty acid gummy. Draft 29 talks about butyrate as a research object without the prefix.

## Relationship to Hill 2014

A postbiotic is not a probiotic. The first word died on purpose. A trial of the live isolate does not move (draft 23). A trial of the inactivated preparation might exist — Depommier's pasteurized arm is the celebrity example people reach for, and it is still exploratory (draft 21). Do not let celebrity do the characterizing.

Safety files change. Dead cells can still present antigens. They can still be contaminated with live organisms if the kill step failed. Sterility or a plate-count-of-zero spec is part of the identity of the article.

## Regulatory box, still intended use

FDA does not hand you a postbiotic monograph. You have a food ingredient, a supplement ingredient, or a drug, depending on intended use and on whether the article is new (drafts 38 and 40). Europe's novel-food path has already met some inactivated organisms. `[VERIFY]` the dated opinion before a sentence names a jurisdiction as settled.

Structure/function copy still needs substantiation on the inactivated article. "Supports immune function" is not inherited from a live cousin, and it is still a claim FTC can read.

## House style

Use postbiotic only when you mean Salminen 2021 and you can point at a preparation. Do not use it as a soft synonym for "extract," "fermentation filtrate," or "heat-killed probiotic." Do not stack it with probiotic on the same line. If an editor sees "live postbiotic," the draft has failed.

Foods with dead cultures after cooking are still foods. They are not postbiotics by cuisine. Draft 27 is the living-food version of that refusal.

## Metabolites are not a free upgrade

A butyrate salt is a chemical. A fermentation filtrate with no characterized cells or components is a soup. Salminen 2021 starts with inanimate microorganisms and/or their components. Read the scope notes before you stretch the word over a dropper. Draft 29 talks about butyrate without the prefix. This essay will not gift the prefix to a short-chain fatty acid gummy.

Last year's live SKU after a temperature excursion is a deviation (draft 14), not a postbiotic launch. Preparation is a designed process, not an accident you decided to brand.

## Kill-step identity

Dead cells can still present antigens. They can still be contaminated with live organisms if the kill failed. A plate-count-of-zero spec is part of the article. qPCR identity without a plate is how a failed kill hides (draft 13). Safety did not get simpler (draft 41).

Regulatory boxes remain intended use plus whether the article is new (drafts 38 and 40). Europe has already met some inactivated organisms as novel foods. `[VERIFY]` the dated opinion. A press release is not a US sticker.

## House ban, restated

Do not stack postbiotic with probiotic on one line. Do not use the word for "extract" or "filtrate" unless the preparation matches. If an editor sees "live postbiotic," the draft has failed. Draft 27 is the food version of refusing a prefix: ferments are ferments.

## Filtrate, extract, ghost, para-

Competing coinages exist because the aisle needed more nouns. This pack uses Salminen 2021 or it uses a chemistry word (filtrate, inactivated cells, butyrate salt). "Ghost" and "para-probiotic" will not be treated as synonyms without a file. A dropper of unnamed metabolites is a soup. Draft 29 will talk about butyrate without gifting this prefix.

Kill-step identity plus a zero plate plus a new potency unit is the minimum process card (draft 23). Intended use did not get kinder because the cells died (draft 38). Novel-food and GRAS/NDI may have reset (draft 40). `[VERIFY]` the dated opinion before a jurisdiction is named as settled.

## Pocket card

Preparation of inanimate microorganisms and/or their components plus a host benefit. Not a eulogy. Not last year's excursion. Not a butyrate salt by default (draft 29). Not a random filtrate. Kill-step identity plus zero plate plus new unit (drafts 13 and 23). Safety did not get simpler (draft 41). Intended use plus novelty still apply (drafts 38 and 40). Dated novel-food or GRAS opinions need a `[VERIFY]`. Banned: live postbiotic; stacking with probiotic on one line; using the word for "extract" without a match. Foods with cooked cultures are foods, not postbiotics by cuisine (draft 27).

## One more refusal

A filtrate dropper will arrive with this prefix because "probiotic" became hard and "metabolite" sounded small. Salminen 2021 starts with a preparation of inanimate microorganisms or their components, characterized, with a host benefit. A mystery dropper is a soup. A butyrate salt is a chemical (draft 29). Last year's cooked pallet is a deviation (draft 14). Use a chemistry word until the preparation matches. Do not stack prefixes to hide a missing zero plate.
