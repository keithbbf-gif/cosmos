A clustering label from a research paper is not a personality type, a diagnosis, or a reason to buy a live-microbe product.

In 2011, Arumugam, Raes, Pelletier, and colleagues published "Enterotypes of the human gut microbiome" in *Nature* (PMID 21508958). They took stool metagenomes from a few hundred people across several countries, ran dimensionality reduction and clustering, and reported three community types driven by *Bacteroides*, *Prevotella*, and a *Ruminococcus*-leaning group. The figure was clean. The press was cleaner. For a decade, consumer reports have handed people a type as if it were an astrological sign with a shopping list.

## What the 2011 paper did

It asked whether gut communities fall into discrete classes the way blood types do. On that dataset, with those methods, three clouds appeared. The authors were careful enough to say the types were "densely populated areas in a multidimensional space" — not passports. Readers heard passports.

*Prevotella* clouds associated, in other work, with diets higher in fiber and certain non-Western patterns. *Bacteroides* clouds associated with more typical Western diets. Those are population hints. They are not a moral ranking and not a prescription to "switch enterotypes" with a capsule. Diet can move taxa (David 2014; draft 30). A 15-strain bottle is not a diet.

The *Ruminococcus* type was the shakiest of the three even in early commentary. If your kit still prints it as a firm identity, ask which paper they think they are citing.

## What later papers did to the sketch

Knights, Ward, McKinlay, and colleagues (*Cell Host & Microbe*, 2014; PMID 25299329) wrote the sentence the aisle ignored: rethink enterotypes. Clustering depends on distance metrics, the number of clusters you ask the algorithm for, and the cohort. Many datasets look more like a gradient — especially along a *Bacteroides*–*Prevotella* axis — than like three bins. If you force three bins, you will get three bins. That is a property of k-means, not of the colon.

Costea et al. (*Nature Microbiology*, 2018; PMID 29255284) tried to stabilize the conversation: enterotypes can be useful *descriptors* if you report methods and do not pretend the universe has voted. Useful descriptor is a long way from "you are a Prevotella person; buy our Prevotella support blend." There is no such blend that inherited the 2011 figure.

Longitudinal sampling made the sketch softer still. People drift. Antibiotics shove. A travel week shoves. A single stool is a single stool. Typing a customer from one kit and then selling a quarterly subscription to "maintain your type" is a business model, not a finding.

## Why the aisle loved a type

Types are shareable. They fit a PDF cover. They make a 16S table feel like a blood test. They also create a fake deficiency: if you are Type 2, something must be wrong with Type 2, or something must be missing that would make you Type 1. HMP already showed healthy people can sit far apart (draft 03). Enterotype marketing quietly walked that back.

The scientific temptation is real: discrete classes would make trials easier. You could stratify. Some researchers still use enterotype-like strata as *exploratory* covariates. Exploratory covariate is allowed. Predetermined shopping list is not.

## What a founder or editor should refuse

Refuse copy that says "restore your enterotype." There is no restoration target in the 2011 paper. Refuse "the healthy enterotype." Arumugam et al. did not crown one. Refuse strain lists mapped to types without a trial that enrolled people by that type and measured a named endpoint on that SKU. I have not seen that trial as a standard of the trade. If you have one, name it. `[CITE NEEDED]`

Refuse to treat *Prevotella* as a villain or a hero. The same genus contains organisms people worry about in other body sites and organisms that look ordinary in stool. Genus-level morality is how 16S dashboards become folklore.

## How to read an enterotype on a report you already paid for

Read it as: this pipeline, this day, placed your sample nearer one cloud than another on a plot trained on someone else's cohort. Then ignore the cloud for any purchasing decision. If a clinician is using community composition inside a research protocol, that is a protocol. If a brand is using it to move units, that is a claim. Claims need human evidence on the product, not a 2011 cluster cartoon.

The useful residue of the enterotype fight is methodological humility. Community data are high-dimensional. Humans like three. The colon did not agree to three. When a later piece in this pack says "the community moved," it means a measured shift in a named assay — not a personality change.

## How a cluster becomes a personality

Marketing needs a noun a customer can say at dinner. "I'm Prevotella" is that noun. Blood types trained the public to expect a small number of buckets that matter. Stool communities did not sign that contract. When a kit prints a type and a shopping list, it is using a 2011 figure as a personality test. Personality tests sell. They do not enumerate a hopper.

A slightly more careful kit prints a probability of membership. That is still a forced geometry. If the underlying data are a gradient, a 70 percent badge is a story about the algorithm's prior. Ask whether they tried two clusters, four, and a no-cluster model. If they only ever tried three, they have not tested Arumugam. They have illustrated it.

## Diet, geography, and the *Prevotella* shortcut

Papers that followed 2011 often saw *Prevotella*-high stools in people eating more fiber or in cohorts outside a narrow Western clinic. That is a population hint. It is not a reason to sell a *Prevotella* capsule to a Type-1 customer, or a *Bacteroides* capsule to a Type-2 customer. You would need a trial that enrolled by the type and measured a host endpoint on the SKU. Name it or drop the idea.

Travel, antibiotics, and a week of David 2014's menus (draft 30) will shove a point along the axis. Quarterly "type maintenance" subscriptions are a business model that assumes a statue. David et al. published a mobile.

## What to keep from the fight

Keep the *Bacteroides*–*Prevotella* axis as a way to read some plots. Keep Knights 2014 as the paper you send to a founder who wants three badges. Keep Costea 2018 as proof that serious people still argue about descriptors without turning them into passports. Drop the shopping list. Drop "the healthy type." Drop any sentence that uses enterotype as a verb.

If an editor needs a hed that will not lie: **a cluster is a choice.** The colon did not choose.
