**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). A purchased stool report is not a diagnosis and not a recommendation to take a product.

Draft 04 was the instrument. This is the checkout. A consumer 16S kit produces a relative-abundance table, a diversity number, and — if the business model needs it — a list of foods and capsules. The first two objects are already limited: region, pipeline, database vintage, no live/dead, rarely a strain, no clinical decision limits (drafts 04 and 07). The third object is the intended-use problem. When a lab-developed wellness test tells you what to swallow, a reasonable person hears a prescription. FTC 2022 reads the ad that way. If the nouns are diseases, 21 U.S.C. § 321(g) is in the room.

## What the report cannot do

It cannot say you have dysbiosis (draft 06). It cannot say your enterotype is your fate (draft 05). It cannot say HMP is your reference range (draft 03). It cannot say a red *Akkermansia* bar is a deficiency (draft 21). It cannot say a house blend is Hill’s object (draft 01). It cannot tell a clinician to stop a drug. It cannot tell a parent to dose a child.

It can say: under this pipeline, on this day, these groups of reads looked like this. That sentence rarely sells a subscription.

## Food lists and the David 2014 laundering

David et al. showed diet moves communities (draft 30). A kit that turns your bars into a grocery list is borrowing the *direction* of that paper without the controlled menus or the research context. "Eat more artichokes" may be harmless. "Eat more artichokes because your chart is sick" is a diagnosis-shaped grocery list. Harmless advice plus a medical costume is still a costume.

## House-brand loops

The clean design, if anyone insists on selling both a kit and a live SKU, is a firewall: the report does not name the SKU; the SKU has its own strain, CFU-through-shelf, and human endpoint; the kit’s terms of use do not promise a treatment. The common design is the opposite. uBiome is the expensive version of what happens when testing, billing, and story get ahead of the instrument (draft 32). Most current kits will not become DOJ exhibits. They can still become FTC exhibits.

## Clinical tests are a different object

A stool pathogen panel, calprotectin, or a clinician-ordered assay in a workup is not this essay. Those have intended medical uses and their own regulations. Do not launder a wellness amplicon into that authority by using a similar PDF layout.

## House style

No reconstructed dashboards. No "we ranked these companies." Teach the firewall. If a founder wants a sentence for a deck: **a census is not a pharmacy.** Wave 5 will now spend one essay on the company that made that sentence expensive.

## A firewall, written as a contract

If a company insists on selling a kit and a live SKU: the report does not name the SKU; the SKU has its own strain, end-of-shelf dose, and human endpoint; the terms of use do not promise a treatment; the PDF does not say dysbiosis; the colors are not a reference range borrowed from HMP without methods. The common design is the opposite. uBiome is the expensive illustration (draft 32). FTC 2022 reads the common design as a person would.

A clinician-ordered pathogen panel or calprotectin is not this object. Similar PDF furniture is how wellness amplicons borrow authority. Do not launder.

## Food lists as costumes

David 2014 licensed the *direction* that diet moves communities (draft 30). A grocery list that treats your bars as sick is a diagnosis-shaped menu. "Eat more artichokes" may be harmless. "Eat more artichokes because your chart is sick" is a costume. Harmless advice plus a medical hat is still a hat.

## What the report can keep

A snapshot of DNA, this pipeline, this day, with methods printed. That sentence rarely sells a subscription. It is also the only sentence this pack will defend. Draft 04 is the instrument. This is the checkout. If a founder wants a deck line: a census is not a pharmacy.

## Terms-of-use lines that do not save a banner

A footer that says "not a diagnostic" under a red disease-shaped noun and a cart is a contradiction. FTC reads the banner. 21 U.S.C. § 321(g) reads the disease noun. The firewall is the design, not the footer. No SKU named on the PDF. No HMP reference range without methods. No dysbiosis. No subscription that assumes a statue (draft 30).

If you already bought a report, keep the methods and the FASTQ if offered. Ignore the cart. Clinical tests ordered in a workup are a different object. Do not let similar furniture confuse them. Draft 32 is the expensive illustration of beauty plus billing plus costume.

## One more refusal

The prettiest PDF in the category is still a census of one gene region on one day under one pipeline (draft 04). Beauty does not add live/dead, strain ID, or a reference range. A house blend at the bottom is speech. Speech is intended use. If you want to sell a live SKU, sell it with its own file and a firewall. If you want to sell a snapshot, sell a snapshot. The hybrid is how this category keeps meeting FTC and, in one expensive case, worse (draft 32).
