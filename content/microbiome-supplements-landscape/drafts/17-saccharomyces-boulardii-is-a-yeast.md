**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). Reviews of *S. boulardii* in gastrointestinal disease are not consumer indications.

Trade history says Henri Boulard isolated a yeast from lychee (or mangosteen — the fruit moves in the telling) in Indochina around 1923 while watching people chew the peel during a cholera outbreak. That story is useful the way Metchnikoff is useful: as origin folklore. It is not a methods section. The organism that later sat in collections and in trials is a *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* variant sold as *S. boulardii* (sometimes *S. cerevisiae* var. *boulardii*). It is a fungus. It is not a *Lactobacillus*. A surprising number of decks treat it as one more "probiotic bacteria."

## Why the kingdom matters on a factory floor

Antibacterial preservatives and antibiotics do not hit a yeast the way they hit a lactic isolate. A CMO that "validates probiotic lines" with a bacterial SOP will miss yeast viability, yeast contaminants, and yeast-specific enumeration. CFU methods differ. Water activity tolerances differ. Heat differs. A shared hopper is a contamination story waiting for a Monday.

People on antibacterial antibiotics sometimes hear that a yeast "isn't affected." That is a mechanistic cartoon, not a consumer instruction, and it sits next to disease-prevention papers this pack will not turn into aisle copy. Do not dose from an essay.

Fungemia in seriously ill or catheterized patients appears in the safety literature as a rare, ugly case type. `[CITE NEEDED]` for any specific case series an editor wants to name. The educational point: "natural" and "food organism" do not mean "safe in every host." Immunocompromised use is a clinician room, not a DTC room.

## The research file is large and the wrong shape for a PDP

McFarland 2010 (*World Journal of Gastroenterology*; PMID 20458757) is one of the reviews people still pull for *S. boulardii* and gastrointestinal conditions. AGA 2020 discusses specific organisms, including this yeast, in *disease* rows (draft 10). Those documents are how a society and a reviewer talk. They are not how a supplement page talks. Educational description is allowed. "Prevents *C. diff*" on a carton is not.

If a house sells *S. boulardii* as a dietary supplement, the legal box is DSHEA: identity, safety for intended use, structure/function if they have a file, no disease intended use. The size of the clinical literature does not move the box. It may move counsel's blood pressure.

## Naming and the cousin problem

Not every *S. cerevisiae* is *boulardii*. Baking yeast is a cousin. Identity testing has to say which. Strain designations exist; use them. "Saccharomyces" on a blend line is the yeast version of "lactobacillus blend."

Rename weather exists here too, though it is milder than Zheng 2020. Print the name a collection uses and the name the trial used, once each, if they differ.

## Folklore versus a collection vial

Boulard's 1923 isolation story moves fruit and country depending on who tells it. Treat it as trade folklore unless a primary sits in the file. The collection vial is the object. The story is a caption. Metchnikoff taught the same split (draft 02). Do not shoot a cholera-adjacent hero video. Do not invent a notebook.

*S. cerevisiae* var. *boulardii* versus a baking strain is an identity problem. Ask for the designation. "Saccharomyces" on a blend line is the yeast version of a lactobacillus blend.

## Antibiotics, cartoons, and the sentence we will not write

A cartoon says antibacterial drugs do not hit a yeast, so a yeast is the "right" companion. That cartoon sits next to disease-prevention papers. This pack will not turn it into a consumer instruction or a dose. People on antibiotics need a clinician, not a kingdom trivia fact.

Fungemia case types in fragile hosts are the safety wall (draft 41). "Food organism" does not mean every host. DTC copy that says "safe for everyone" has left the file.

## CMO SOP, written as a punch list

Enumeration method for a yeast. Water-activity spec. Heat. Whether the hopper is shared with lactic lines. Whether antibacterial preservatives in a gummy were validated against a fungus. Whether the trial's matrix matches. If four of those are blank, the kingdom fact has not entered the plant yet. Draft 14 will kill the rest in the warehouse.

## Label lines that confuse kingdoms

"Probiotic bacteria including *S. boulardii*." That line is false in a biology class and sloppy in a plant. "50 billion probiotic cultures" that mixes a yeast CFU with a lactic CFU as if they were one virtue is draft 13's unit error plus a kingdom error. Separate the lines. Separate the methods. Separate the hoppers if the plant is grown-up.

Travelers' diarrhea and antibiotic-associated papers exist in the review literature. They are research rooms, often disease-shaped. They stay behind the wall (drafts 10 and 38). Educational description is allowed. A carton that says "the yeast for antibiotics" is a protocol. We will not write it.
