Lars Bode's 2012 *Glycobiology* review (PMID 22513036) is the teaching map of human milk oligosaccharides: odd, abundant, barely digested by the infant, and highly interesting to certain microbes and to researchers who care about the infant gut and immune development. "Every baby needs a sugar mama" is his title, not a slogan for an adult tub. The jump from that literature to a scoop of 2′-fucosyllactose (2′-FL) next to a creatine display is a category jump. Chemistry can travel. The rest of the milk cannot.

## What 2′-FL is doing in a plant

It is a defined oligosaccharide that can be made by fermentation or other industrial routes. It is a substrate some *Bifidobacterium* lines are good at using (draft 19). That can be studied in adults as a prebiotic-shaped question (draft 24): does this substrate get used, and is there a host endpoint? Those papers, when they exist, are adult papers at adult doses. They are not Bode-in-a-scoop. `[CITE NEEDED]` for any specific adult RCT an editor wants to attach to a SKU.

Infant formula that includes HMOs is a regulated food for a specific population. Do not confuse that file with a wellness powder. Do not put formula claims, implied or cute, on an adult pouch.

## What the hero copy wants

It wants breast-milk holiness. It wants "as in mother's milk" on a 40-year-old's coffee. It wants a bifidobacteria bar on a stool kit to complete the sale (draft 31). Those wants are the reason this essay is claims-guarded. Milk is a tissue product of a mammal. A purified sugar is a purified sugar. The holiness does not commute.

Secretor status (FUT2) changes which HMOs a woman produces. That is a human-genetics fact in the milk literature. It is not a DTC kit-to-powder story. Do not build a FUT2 upsell.

## Prefixes

2′-FL may be argued as a prebiotic if the 2017 words are met. It is not a probiotic. It is not a synbiotic unless someone designed and tested a pair (draft 25). It is not an HMO *mixture* — human milk has many structures. Printing "HMOs" for a single 2′-FL is a plural that does work the chemistry did not pay for. Print the name of the sugar.

## Safety and GI tolerance

Oligosaccharides cause gas in some people. That is a host effect. Dose in a paper is not a serving suggestion here; we will not print one. Infant safety files do not automatically cover adult chronic use at supplement-store grams. If the article is new to the food supply in a jurisdiction, draft 40's NDI/GRAS/novel-food questions apply.

## House style

No baby in the hero. No "liquid gold." No implied feeding claim. Name the sugar, the population in the study, and the endpoint. If the file is thin, say the file is thin. The next essay stays with substrates that never borrowed a mammal — resistant starch and the cross-feeding story — and still refuses to become a SKU.
