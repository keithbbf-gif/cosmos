**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). This is not legal advice. Counsel owns the last read.

A live microbe does not arrive at the border with a passport that says probiotic. It arrives with a name, a process, and a pile of sentences: label, site, Amazon bullets, a paid Reel, a script a founder mailed to an influencer. Those sentences pick a box. In the United States the common boxes are **dietary supplement** (a food category under DSHEA, Pub. L. 103-417; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff)) and **drug** (21 U.S.C. § 321(g)(1)(B) — intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease). FDA’s June 2016 guidance on early clinical trials with live biotherapeutic products is how the agency talks when the live article is in the drug box. Hill 2014 is how scientists talk when they want a word. The three documents do not share a brain.

## What the supplement box actually is

FDA does not pre-approve the SKU. The manufacturer is responsible for safety and for claims. Part 111 is the cGMP. Structure/function claims, if used, need substantiation, a 30-day notification, and the 101.93 disclaimer. "Supports a healthy gut flora" is the *shape* of a structure/function sentence. Whether it is true of *this* isolate is a file. "Prevents *C. diff*" is not that shape. It is a disease sentence, even if AGA said something in a hospital row (draft 10).

"Probiotic" on a supplement is a scientific compliment you may or may not have earned (draft 01). It is not a monograph. It is not "FDA approved probiotic."

## What the LBP box actually is

A live biotherapeutic product is a biological product containing live organisms, used to prevent or treat a disease or condition — read the guidance for the agency’s wording. IND-enabled trials, CMC that looks like a biologic, and, if someone wants to sell it for that intended use, a licensure path. Rebyota and Vowst already walked a version of that path (draft 37). A founder cannot get there by printing "clinical grade" on a supplement.

If you run a disease-endpoint trial on a live isolate and also sell a lookalike tub online with the trial’s sentence, you have not found a clever dual-use. You have handed intended-use evidence to a plaintiff and a reviewer.

## FTC still sits on the supplement

The 2022 *Health Products Compliance Guidance* is the advertising rule. Competent and reliable scientific evidence, usually well-controlled human studies, for the claim as a person would read it. Mice, 16S shifts, and cousin isolates fail that sentence (drafts 11, 04, 15). A disclaimer in 8-point type does not save a banner.

## Other countries are not a loophole

EFSA’s refusals and Japan’s FOSHU (draft 39) do not rewrite DSHEA. A FOSHU logo on a US landing page is a confusion. A Canadian NNHPD number is a Canadian object. Print the jurisdiction you are actually in.

## House style

When an essay in this pack says "supplement," it means the DSHEA box. When it says "LBP" or "drug," it means the other box. When it says "probiotic," it means Hill 2014 unless it is teaching a misuse. Do not write "regulated like a drug" about a supplement that is merely cGMP-compliant. Part 111 is not a BLA.

Draft 39 is the comparative-label essay: what happens when a region will not let you say the word at all.

## Dual-use is not a strategy

A disease-endpoint trial on a live isolate plus a lookalike tub online with the trial's sentence is intended-use evidence handed to a reviewer. "Clinical grade" on a supplement is not a BLA. Part 111 is not a licensure path. Rebyota and Vowst already showed what the drug box looks like (draft 37).

Reels, influencer scripts, and customer-service macros count. Intended use is a pile of sentences, not a formulator's private hope. FTC 2022 still sits on the supplement box even when FDA has not written a warning letter yet.

## Other countries are not a tunnel

EFSA refusals and FOSHU permissions do not rewrite DSHEA (draft 39). A Canadian number is a Canadian object. Print the jurisdiction where the cart sits. "Regulated like a drug" about a cGMP supplement is a confusion this pack will not print.

## Vocabulary lock

Supplement means the DSHEA box. LBP or drug means the other box. Probiotic means Hill 2014 unless we are teaching a misuse. When a guideline row is a disease row, the box is not a supplement even if the isolate could have been sold as one under quieter sentences. Draft 10 is the row. This is the border agent.

## A pile-of-sentences audit

Label. Amazon bullets. Site banner. Paid Reel. Influencer brief. Customer-service macro. Sales-deck speaker notes. If any one of them is a disease sentence, the pile is a disease sentence. Dual-use — IND trial plus a loud tub — is evidence, not cleverness. "Clinical grade" is not a BLA. Part 111 is not licensure.

Hill 2014 can be true of an isolate that is still in the wrong box because the website went to a hospital row (draft 10). The border agent is intended use, not the Latin. Draft 39 is what happens when a region will not even let you say the word.
