**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). A fiber is not a drug. "Feeds good bacteria" is not, by itself, a finished claim.

Glenn Gibson and Marcel Roberfroid introduced "prebiotic" in *The Journal of Nutrition* in 1995 (PMID 7782892): dietary modulation of the colonic microbiota, a substrate that selectively stimulates organisms already present. ISAPP's 2017 consensus (Gibson, Hutkins, Sanders, et al.; PMID 28611480) restated the idea for a broader host and a stricter mouth: a substrate that is selectively utilized by host microorganisms conferring a health benefit. Three ideas, again load-bearing: **substrate**, **selective use**, **host benefit**. A powder that is sweet, fermentable, and sold next to a probiotic is not automatically in the set.

## Selective is the word people skip

Lots of fibers are used by lots of organisms. Selectivity is a pattern, not a brand color. If a paper shows a rise in bifidobacteria after inulin, that is a commonly reported direction — and it is still a paper, a dose, and a cohort, not a law. If the same powder also feeds organisms you did not come to praise, the adjective "prebiotic" is doing image work.

Selective use without a host benefit is an ecology note. Host benefit without evidence of microbial use is, at best, a fiber story. ISAPP wanted both. Copy usually wants the prefix.

## Inulin, FOS, GOS, and the rest

Chicory inulin, fructo-oligosaccharides, galacto-oligosaccharides, and later entries (PHGG, some resistant starches, some HMOs; drafts 28–29) have unequal files. Name the chemistry. Name the dose that was studied. Name the endpoint. Gas and bloating are host effects too; they belong in a honest methods conversation even when they will not go on the front of the pouch.

Psyllium and some other fibers have walked into FDA labeling conversations as fibers, not as prebiotics. Those are different legal objects. Do not launder a fiber authorized-health-claim file into a microbiome-prefix file, or the reverse.

## Not a probiotic, not a smaller probiotic

A prebiotic is not live. It does not inherit Hill 2014. It does not get CFU. Combining one with a live isolate is a synbiotic *only* if the combination meets Swanson 2020 (draft 25). Sprinkling inulin into a 15-strain tub and printing both prefixes is the blend trick with a second adjective (draft 15).

Diet already supplies microbiota-accessible carbohydrates (Sonnenburg; draft 30). A sachet can be a tool. It is not a moral upgrade on beans. When a kit says "your bifidobacteria are low; buy our FOS," you have the prescription pattern again (draft 31).

## How to keep the word honest

Use it when the substrate is characterized, the selective-use story is in a paper you can name, and a host benefit was measured — or do not use it. "Contains chicory-root fiber (inulin), 5 g per serving" is a smaller, better sentence when the prebiotic file is thin. Smaller sentences are this pack's religion.

Wave 4 will now add synbiotic and postbiotic, which exist because the aisle needed more prefixes after probiotic and prebiotic got crowded. The definitions are trying to stay narrower than the aisle.

## A worked refusal

A powder is sweet, fermentable, and sits next to a probiotic. The carton says "prebiotic fiber blend." Chemistry is unspecified. Dose is "proprietary." Endpoint is "feeds good bacteria." Gibson 1995 and Gibson 2017 both fail. Print "contains chicory-root fiber (inulin), grams per serving" if that is true and that is all you have. The prefix is not a halo for a sweet powder.

Selective use without a host benefit is an ecology note. Host benefit without microbial use is, at best, a fiber story. Gas is a host effect and belongs in the methods conversation even when it will not go on the pouch.

## Dose, chemistry, and the unnamed blend

"Prebiotic blend" with no chemistry and no grams is a prefix on a mystery. Inulin, FOS, GOS, PHGG, RS, 2′-FL are different objects (drafts 28–29). Each wants its own dose and its own paper. Gas belongs in the conversation. Selectivity that also feeds organisms you did not come to praise belongs in the conversation. A sweet powder next to a probiotic is a planogram, not a definition.

Kit-driven FOS upsells are draft 31. Synbiotic stacking without Swanson is draft 25. Diet already bringing MACs is draft 30. Keep Gibson 1995 for the coinage and Gibson 2017 for the tighter mouth.
