Named multi-strain combinations that appear in disease guidelines are research/clinic objects, not aisle analogues.

Claudio De Simone published a 2019 commentary in *Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology* (PMID 29378315) on an unregulated probiotic market and on the fate of an eight-strain combination associated with his name. The brand-name fights around VSL#3 and successor products are a matter of public dockets and society notes. This pack will not referee a trademark. It will take the scientific moral that made the fight possible: **a combination is an intervention.** Change an isolate, a ratio, a CMO, or a freeze-dry curve, and you have a new object. Papers about the old object do not automatically move.

## Why mixes feel like wisdom

More names look like more coverage — like a broad-spectrum antibiotic for a community you have decided is broken (draft 06). There is no general law that eleven strains beat one. Interactions in the hopper and in the gut can go the other way. QC gets harder: can you enumerate each isolate? Does one die and leave the label lying (draft 15)? Hill 2014's default remains the strain. A mix can be studied *as a mix*. That study is the file. There is no participation trophy for arithmetic.

## How guidelines accidentally feed the cousin

AGA 2020 and other documents sometimes name combinations at a level that is not a deposit-by-deposit recipe (draft 10). Implementation then splits: clinicians who used a specific product in a trial, and marketers who hear "eight lactic strains" and build a lookalike. Lookalikes are the De Simone problem at retail scale. If a society later clarifies a product name, that clarification is part of the dated file. `[VERIFY]` before anyone claims alignment.

Pouchitis and other disease rows that mention combinations are **not** supplement copy. They are the reason this essay exists next to drafts 10 and 38.

## What a mix file has to contain

Identity of each isolate, ratios, the process that produces those ratios at fill *and* at expiry, a human trial of that finished combination, and a change-control procedure that triggers new evidence when the recipe moves. That last sentence is where most 15-strain tubs leave the conversation. Supplier swap without a new plate and a new thought is how a "clinically studied blend" becomes a scrapbook (draft 15).

## Legal history as a methods lesson

When two products share a family story and split, the papers do not know which hopper you own. Citations must follow the intervention, not the nostalgia. Editors should refuse "the VSL#3 literature" as a blob unless the author can say which object, which years, which formula. Readers should refuse a landing page that cites a blob.

This is not unique to one man's recipe. Every house blend with a sliding SKU is a quieter version. The quiet ones do not make *CGH*. They still fail Hill.

## What to steal

Steal change control. Steal "the mix is the drug-shaped object even when it is sold as food." Steal the refusal to let a lookalike inherit. Leave the lawsuit play-by-play to reporters. Leave disease indications on the clinic side of the wall.

Draft 23 will now take the opposite cheat: not a cousin mix, but a dead version of a famous live thing, hoping the citations survive the autoclave.

## What "eight strains" hides

Ratios. Process. Which freeze-dry curve. Whether expiry still has eight. Whether the trial used this CMO. Whether a society row named a product or a sketch of a combination (draft 10). Lookalikes are the retail version of the naming fight. Citations must follow the intervention, not the nostalgia. "The VSL#3 literature" as a blob is an editor fail unless the author can say which object, which years, which formula.

## Change control, written as a trigger list

Isolate swap. Ratio change. CMO change. Medium change that alters carryover. A dating period that drops a line. Each trigger wants a new plate and a new decision about the citation. If that sounds like a drug-shaped quality system, welcome to why mixes are expensive. Hill 2014's default remains the strain. A mix can be studied as a mix. There is no arithmetic trophy.
