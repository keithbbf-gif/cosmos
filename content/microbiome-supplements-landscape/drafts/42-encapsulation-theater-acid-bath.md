**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). "Survives stomach acid" is a process claim until a file makes it a fact about this matrix.

Every live-microbe deck has a slide: a beaker, a pH, a heroic percent survival, a coating that "delivers to the intestine." Sometimes the slide is from a supplier’s 2011 white paper. Sometimes it is a Caco-2 picture wearing a cape. In-vitro acid resistance can be a reason to *choose* a process. It is not a host benefit under Hill 2014. It is not a substitute for the dose the person actually swallows at expiry (draft 14). It is not a human RCT (draft 09). It is draft 11’s leap in a smaller glass.

## What a coating changes

Water activity. Dissolution. Enumeration — you have to get the cells out of the coat to plate them, and the method can invent or hide CFU (draft 13). Allergens in the coat. Whether the trial’s uncoated isolate is still the intervention. A coated SKU is a new matrix. New matrix, new object (draft 15).

Coatings also fail. Heat, moisture, and a cheap tablet press will find the failure before a customer review does. Part 111 wants a spec for the finished thing, not for the idea of a coat.

## What the acid-bath graph hides

Static pH is not a fed stomach. Pepsin, meal, transit time, and the fact that some isolates are meant to work *in* the small intestine or *as* they pass — the graph rarely matches the mechanism the marketing just claimed. Spores will look unbeatable in a beaker and still need a human file (draft 18). Anaerobes will look dead because the bench was sloppy (draft 20). Yeast will need a yeast method (draft 17).

A percent survival without a method, a strain, and a matrix is a decoration. This pack will not invent a typical percent. If a founder wants one on a slide, it comes from *their* report, dated, or it stays off the slide.

## "Targeted delivery" as a claim

If the sentence is about a process ("enteric-coated capsule"), it can be a truthful manufacturing description when true. If the sentence is about a benefit ("targets your colon for immunity"), it is a health claim wearing a process hat. FTC reads the hat. So does 101.93 if you are in the supplement box.

## House style

No borrowed supplier graphs without the strain matching the hopper. No 2011 dates hidden in the fine print. No "clinically coated." If the coating is the proud thing, the stability and enumeration methods should be prouder. Draft 43 is the document where those methods either appear or do not: the live-organism COA.

## A graph intake form

Year. Isolate (does it match the hopper). Matrix (coated or not). Static pH or a fed model. Pepsin or not. How cells were recovered. Whether expiry plates exist in the marketed coat. Whether the trial used the coat. If three of those are blank, the graph is furniture. A 2011 supplier PDF on a 2026 cousin is draft 15 in a beaker.

"Targets your colon for immunity" is a benefit claim wearing a process hat. "Enteric-coated" can be a manufacturing fact when true. Do not confuse them. Draft 43 is the COA rows that make the fact checkable.
