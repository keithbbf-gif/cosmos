**Disclaimer.** Educational only. Not medical advice. Dietary supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease (DSHEA; 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff); 21 CFR 101.93). "Dysbiosis" is not a diagnosis this pack recognizes for consumer use, and it is not a structure/function endpoint.

Open a supplement brief, a stool-kit PDF, or a mid-tier review and the word arrives as if it were glucose: *dysbiosis*. Hooks and O'Malley, writing in *mBio* in 2017 (PMID 29018121), did the unfashionable thing. They read how the word was being used and found a pile of meanings that slide into each other: imbalance, loss of diversity, bloom of a taxon someone dislikes, difference from a control group, a causal engine of disease. When a word means all of that, it can explain anything and test nothing.

## Circular comfort

A common loop: disease X is associated with a community that differs from controls; that difference is named dysbiosis; dysbiosis is then said to contribute to disease X. The name has been inserted as a mechanism. Association is still all you have. HMP already showed that healthy communities differ hard from each other (draft 03). Difference is the background weather. It is not a diagnosis.

A second loop: a product is said to "correct dysbiosis." Correct toward what? Whose stool? Which pipeline? Which week? If the answer is "toward the HMP mean," you have chosen a methods artifact as a treatment target. If the answer is "toward last year's kit," you have chosen a subscription.

## What a careful paper does instead

It names the assay. It names the taxa or functions that moved. It names the cohort and the controls. It says whether the shift was pre-specified. It does not ask the word dysbiosis to carry the causal load. Some good papers still use the word in a title because editors like it. Read the tables.

If a trial's primary endpoint is "dysbiosis index improved," demand the index: which taxa, which weights, whether it was locked before unblinding, whether anyone has shown the index tracks a host outcome that matters to a person. An index invented in the same paper that tests the capsule is a circular comfort with a heatmap.

## Why the aisle will not give the word up

It sounds clinical. It does not name a disease, so it feels like it might slip through 21 CFR 101.93. It probably will not, if a reasonable consumer hears "your gut is sick and this bottle fixes it." FTC reads the ad the way a person reads it, not the way a copywriter footnotes it. "Restores balance" is the same noun in softer shoes.

It also flattens body sites. Vaginal communities that are *Lactobacillus*-low are not "dysbiosis" in the same breath as a stool chart that lost *Faecalibacterium*. The word erases the site (draft 35) and erases the fact that some low-diversity states are typical, even preferred, in specific niches (draft 07).

## A vocabulary that can survive counsel

Prefer: "In this study, relative abundance of *taxon* was lower in *cohort* than in *controls* on *assay*." Prefer: "This strain was tested for *named endpoint* in *n* adults for *k* weeks." Prefer: "We do not have a reference microbiome for you." Those sentences are longer. They are harder to put on a pouch. That is the point.

If you must explain the word to a reader who has already been trained by Instagram, explain it as jargon for "different from a comparison group," then drop it. Do not build a product line on it. Do not let a kit print it in red.

## Research use, tightly held

Clinicians and researchers will keep saying dysbiosis in meetings. This pack does not police meetings. It polices sentences that sit near a cart. A hospital protocol that uses community metrics as exploratory readouts is a protocol. A DTC page that tells a customer they *have* dysbiosis and sells them a blend is an intended-use document. The same five syllables. Different rooms.

Metchnikoff had autointoxication (draft 02). The twentieth century had "intestinal toxemia." We have dysbiosis. The job of this essay is to keep the new word from inheriting the old trick: a theory of poisoning that sells a ferment.

## Indexes that arrive with the product

A "dysbiosis index" that is published in the same paper that tests the capsule is a circular comfort. Ask whether the weights were locked, whether the index tracks a host outcome that matters to a person, and whether anyone outside the house has used it. A house index that only moves when the house product is swallowed is a sales instrument.

Some clinical research programs use community metrics as exploratory readouts next to a real endpoint. That can be honest. The honesty is the pre-specification and the refusal to put the index on a pouch. If the press release leads with the index, the honesty left the building.

## Site errors the word invites

Stool-trained intuition wants more colors (draft 07). The vagina often does not (draft 35). The mouth has its own typical communities. Skin sites differ from each other. One word that means "different from a control I liked" will call a typical vaginal community sick and a diverse stool community well, then sell two products. HMP 2012 already said site first. Hooks and O'Malley said the word was doing too much. Listen to both.

## A paragraph you can give a copywriter

"We will not say dysbiosis. We will say what was measured, in whom, against whom, on which assay. We will not correct a customer toward a mean. We will not print the word in red. If a paper we cite uses it in the title, we will explain it as jargon for difference and then use the table."

That paragraph will lose some pitches. It will also lose some letters. Draft 31 is the kit version. Draft 33 is the mood version. Same noun-shaped hole.
