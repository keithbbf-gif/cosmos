Resistant starch is starch that reaches the colon. Food chemists divide it into types (RS1–RS5 in common teaching): physically inaccessible, granular, retrograded, chemically modified, amylose–lipid complexes. Those types are not brands. They are how a potato, a bean, a green banana, or an engineered ingredient behaves when a person eats it. Some of that carbohydrate becomes a substrate for organisms that make short-chain fatty acids, including butyrate. Cross-feeding is the unglamorous truth: one taxon’s leftovers are another taxon’s lunch. A single-name capsule is a poor cartoon of that table.

## Why butyrate got a fan club

Colonocytes use butyrate. Papers about barrier function and immune tone mention it. Marketers heard a molecule they could put on a pouch. A butyrate salt is a chemical product. A resistant-starch powder is a substrate product. A live butyrate-producer (*Faecalibacterium*, *Roseburia*) is usually an oxygen-shy wish (draft 20). These are three articles of commerce. They do not inherit one another’s papers.

Salminen 2021 does not automatically make a butyrate gummy a postbiotic (draft 26). Gibson 2017 does not automatically make every RS a prebiotic (draft 24). Both definitions still want a characterized article and a host benefit, not a pathway diagram.

## Cross-feeding as a humility device

If organism A eats a starch and organism B eats A’s lactate or succinate and then makes butyrate, a kit that says "low *Faecalibacterium*; buy *Faecalibacterium*" has skipped the table. Fiber, not a celebrity taxon, may be the lever that exists in a kitchen (draft 30). A next-gen bottle may not exist as a live object at all.

Cross-feeding also ruins simple "this strain does this metabolite" copy when the strain is swallowed into a crowded colon. In vitro metabolite tables are cage-adjacent (draft 11).

## Food versus sachet

Potatoes cooled overnight, legumes, and some whole grains are how people have always eaten RS without a SKU. A sachet of RS2 from high-amylose maize is a defined ingredient that *can* be studied at a defined dose. Those studies, when they exist, should be named. `[CITE NEEDED]` before a founder prints a specific glucose or stool endpoint. Gas is a host endpoint too.

This pack will not tell you to eat cold potatoes. It will tell you that a kitchen intervention and a sachet intervention are different files, and that both are closer to Sonnenburg’s microbiota-accessible carbohydrate idea (*Cell Metabolism*, 2014; PMID 25156449) than a butyrate-producer that dies in the dock.

## House style

No "butyrate booster" as a disease-adjacent wink. No single-taxon hero. No implied IBD story (Sokol’s organism stays in draft 20). If you mention a type of RS, define it in food-chemistry English. The next essay pulls the camera back: diet moves communities on a timescale of days. Capsules are a narrower tool. That is not a moral ranking. It is a methods fact from David 2014.
