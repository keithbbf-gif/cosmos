# Style guide — microbiome / probiotic research pack

Voice is the product. A clinician-curious reader or a founder who has to sit next to a COA should finish a piece and know what the paper actually measured. If it sounds like a wellness mill, it fails even when a PMID is real.

`voice_check: human` means the draft was written against this file. It is not a byline.

## Who is speaking

A careful reader of primary sources: ISAPP consensus statements, AGA GRADE tables, FAO/WHO consultation text, FDA live-biotherapeutic guidance, HMP methods papers, Cochrane reviews. Not a brand mascot. Not a medical-advice bot. Not a "microbiome coach."

Do not invent a lab, a chief scientist, or a corporate parent. Do not put Unilever, ElitElixirs, or a white-label house on the page as hero copy.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" (as metaphor for a market or field). The *folder* may keep the path name. The *prose* may not.
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- symmetrical list-filler ("empower / enhance / elevate")
- fake confidence on thin evidence
- "dysbiosis" as if it were a lab analyte with a reference range
- "the microbiome" as a single organ with a single pill

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "empower," "in this article we will explore," "gut health journey," "balance your biome."

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a named paper, a guideline row, a statute, or a number a reader can check.
2. Mix sentence length. Short after a long one.
3. Name the document. Hill 2014, Su 2020, Salminen 2021, HMP 2012, Zheng 2020. Do not say "a recent study" if you have the name.
4. Cite or mark `[CITE NEEDED]` / `[VERIFY]`. No invented n, p-values, responder rates, or market-share figures.
5. Say when the file is thin. "One 40-person trial" is a fact. "Emerging science suggests" is a dodge.
6. Prefer methods, nomenclature, trial design, viability, labeling, and regulatory *category* over disease stories.
7. Keep the DSHEA disclaimer on every draft. Educational only. Not medical advice. Supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent disease.
8. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target **1,000–1,600 words** of body copy. Under 1,000 is a stub in this pack. Over 1,800 needs two archives, not a recap.

## Claims posture

- Efficacy and safety sentences need a PMID, DOI, FDA/FTC/ISAPP/AGA URL, or a named review — or a flag.
- Hospital and drug documents (AGA *C. difficile* rows, NEC, Vowst, Rebyota, FMT) may be *described as research and regulation*. They must not be rewritten as supplement copy.
- Do not convert an association (BMI and taxa; mood scores in mice) into a capsule claim.
- "Probiotic" is a scientific definition with load-bearing words. It is not an FDA product category.

## Headings

H2s should be specific ("Hill 2014 kept three load-bearing words") not thematic ("The microbiome conversation"). No "Introduction," "Conclusion," "In Summary."

## Photo slots

Every draft carries a photo/diagram slot after the lede or at the first break (`PHOTO_NOTES.md`). Captions are specific. No happy-gut cartoons.

## Voice check before `voice_check: human`

Read the draft out loud. If a sentence could sit under any other supplement brand without changing a noun, rewrite it. If two adjacent paragraphs start with the same syntactic shape, break one. If you cannot point to a source for a number, flag it or delete it.
