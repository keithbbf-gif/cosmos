# Card — do not conflate

| | Solid steam-bent | Laminated / molded veneer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What moves | One piece, plasticized, crumpled on the inside | Thin leaves, glued on a form (or molded as a shell) |
| Grain on the edge | Continuous | Layers, glue lines |
| Tight radius | Limited by species, thickness, strap | Much tighter, stacked |
| Strength | A little down from the same piece left straight | Generally stronger than solid steam-bent of the same section |
| Moisture later | Curve can open, tighten, or slowly straighten | More stable, still not magic |
| Names people mix up | Thonet solid beech, Windsor bow, boat rib | Aalto birch, Eames shell, most “bent plywood” chairs |
| Glue | None in the bend | The bend *is* glue |

Kerf-bending and a hot pipe (guitar sides) are other jobs again.

If a caption says “steam-bent” on a plywood seat, the caption is wrong.

We will use steam when we want one fiber around a turn and no glue line. We will laminate when the radius or the duty says so. We will not advertise one as the other.
