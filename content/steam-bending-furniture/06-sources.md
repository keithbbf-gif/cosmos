# Sources

Only what the drafts are allowed to lean on. If a sentence wants a fact that is not here, it does not get the fact.

## Primary / institutional

1. **USDA Forest Products Laboratory, *Wood Handbook*, General Technical Report FPL-GTR-190, Chapter 19, “Specialty Treatments”** (Rebecca E. Ibach). Plasticizing, Tg of lignin ~170 °C dry, steam times, 12–20% MC, 25–30% compression vs 1–2% tension, 3% rule, species list, green-wood hydrostatic warning, strength loss, springback-with-moisture.
2. **USDA / FPL older bending bulletin** (govinfo: “BENDING”). Air-dry 12–20%; storage at 70–80 °F / 80% RH ≈ 15–16% MC; 65% RH ≈ 12%. Southern oak chair-back posts 12–15% in a hot-plate press.
3. **Victoria and Albert Museum, “Thonet and the invention of bentwood furniture”** (page updated 17 April 2024). Dates, Koritschan, production 10,000 (1857) → 1,810,000 (1913), 50 million No. 14 by 1930, Crystal Palace 1851, strap breakthrough ~1855, Gebrüder Thonet 1853.
4. **Thonet (company) — bentwood material page and brand history.** Veneer-and-glue first; solid plus steam plus metal band; 1856 privilege; two people on the mold; Frankenberg still doing it; pressurized boiler, ~6 hours, >100 °C — *their* factory, not our box.

## Shop writing

5. **Michael Fortune, “7 Secrets of Steam-Bending,” *Fine Woodworking* 238 (Jan/Feb 2014).** Air-dried 12–16%; 200–210 °F; 1–2 h/in; wrinkle warning at 3 h on 1"; strap; 10-minute working window; strap off at 1 hour; oak blanket; dry to 7–8%; fan.

## Local / house

6. **Encyclopedia of Arkansas, Bradley County.** Three Warren mills, 1901–1902; Bradley Lumber by Samuel H. Fullerton; ~100,000 bd ft/day and ~350 employees by 1907; Potlatch in the 1950s.
7. **Bradley County ARGenWeb / RootsWeb postcard text.** “Sawmilling Arkansas Soft Pine, mild-textured Oak, and other native hardwoods. Producers and fabricators of hardwood flooring, furniture stock, Pine millwork and construction materials.”
8. **Rand Wood Products history page.** Fullerton 1901; later Potlatch, Wood Lumber, Ashcraft, Rand. Pine-forward telling. Use for the mill’s afterlife, not for furniture theology.
9. **BBF house paper (Drive):** Home Depot tax-exempt 2024 — Bradley Brand Furniture, 854 Rock Springs Road, Wilmar, AR, Keith Chambers owner, furniture manufacturer. Arkansas sales-tax permit: LLC, site opened June 5, 2017 on that permit’s telling, NAICS 44211 on the permit (stores — the permit’s box, not a reason to call us a mall kiosk). Older Cresco PO: 501 Pennington, Warren. Order sheets: Moro, Caney, Shiloh, Honeysuckle, gum / oak / walnut-ash / sycamore boards.
10. **Public business listings.** BBF LLC, 501 Pennington St, Warren, AR 71671. Year established on one bidder listing: 2008. David Chambers listed on one government page; Keith as owner/CEO on the company side.

## Useful, not loaded into the essay unless needed

11. Peter Lanyon, “What is Steam Bending?” (2024). Green-wood school: split grain, steam also drives water out. Good for the green/air-dry fight. Not a number source.
12. Thonet chair 214 / 210 R process notes (company magazine). Two to four specialists on a back. Atmosphere of a factory, not a recipe.

## Will not use as fact

- Blog posts that say “Thonet invented steam bending in 1830.”
- Any page that calls an Eames shell steam-bent solid wood.
- AI summaries that merge 1901, 1903, and 2008.
- Memory of a YouTube video we did not re-open.
- A minimum-radius chart with no species, no thickness, no strap.

## How a draft should cite

In the long piece: name the institution in English (“the Forest Service lab,” “the V&A,” “Fortune in *Fine Woodworking*”) once per subject, then just use the number. The wall card gets no footnotes. The cards in 31–35 can carry a one-line “FPL / Fortune” where a number would otherwise look like we made it up.
