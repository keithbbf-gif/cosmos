# Brief

**Ask:** Steam bending — history plus shop practice — for BBF. Human voice. Staged. Quality over speed.

**Who it is for**

Bradley Brand Furniture. Keith Chambers, owner. Hardwood shop in Bradley County: Warren on Pennington, later Rock Springs Road in Wilmar. The company sold case goods and boards — kitchen islands named for the water around here, cutting boards in gum, oak, walnut, ash, sycamore — through the usual catalog and store doors. The mill town behind the name is older than the LLC.

**What the piece has to do**

1. Tell the history without museum velvet. Steam is older than furniture catalogs. Thonet industrialized it. Windsor chairs and boat shops never waited for Vienna.
2. Put shop practice on the bench: blank, moisture, box, strap, form, time, dry, fail.
3. Speak as this shop, not as a content mill. Short sentences. Specific wood. No “whether you are a beginner or a master.”
4. Leave something a person can hang by a steam box and not be embarrassed.

**What it is not**

- Not a Wayfair product blurb.
- Not a Fine Woodworking reprint with our logo on it.
- Not a claim that BBF invented bentwood, or that we currently ship a steam-bent line.
- Not laminated plywood passed off as steam-bent solid.
- Not a safety lecture written by a lawyer who has never opened a box door.

**Length we are hunting**

The long piece should sit in the 2,500–3,500 word range when it is done. The wall card should fit on one sheet, big type, readable with gloves on.

**Proof the brief was heard**

If a reader from Warren finishes it and thinks “that is us, and those numbers are ones I can check,” it worked. If a reader from anywhere finishes it and could swap “BBF” for any brand in a listicle, it failed.
