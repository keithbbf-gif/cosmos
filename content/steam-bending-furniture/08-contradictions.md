# Contradictions we will keep visible

A clean article that hides these is a worse article.

## 1. Green versus air-dried

**Green school:** more water, more heat transfer, tighter bends, and if you rive it you get continuous fiber. Steaming also drives water *out*, so a green-woodworker can call the box a dryer.

**FPL:** green stock makes hydrostatic pressure. The concave face can crush. Drying a hard bend from green is a long argument with checks and a moving curve.

**Fortune:** do not even start on kiln-dried; air-dry to 12–16%.

**House:** we are not a pole-lathe school and we are not Madison, Wisconsin. We will air-dry to the middle of the FPL window (15–18%) and rive or pick grain as if we were the green school. We will not start a paid curve on a dripping stick.

## 2. How long in the box

**Rhyme:** one hour per inch.

**FPL:** 38 minutes or 75 minutes, depending on how wet you started.

**Fortune:** one to two hours, and a third hour on a one-inch part is how you buy wrinkles.

**Thonet factory:** six hours in a pressurized boiler on their rods.

All four can be true in their rooms. The essay will say the rhyme, then the two lab times, then “our box is not their boiler.”

## 3. Kiln-dried

Fortune: lignin set, fibers tear.  
FPL: add time and water.  
Chairmakers: some of them soak kiln-dried oak for days and still break a third.

We will not referee a religion. We will say: buy air-dried when you can; if you cannot, soak and budget scrap.

## 4. Who invented what

Museum copy likes “Thonet invented bentwood.”  
Boat shops laugh.  
V&A is more careful: he industrialized solid bentwood with a strap after failing at veneer packets.

We follow the V&A, not the museum gift shop.

## 5. Strength

A bent piece is weaker than the same piece left straight. FPL says the loss is usually not enough to matter. A designer who puts a steam-bent 5/8" towel bar under a 250-pound lean is not covered by that sentence. Geometry still matters.

## 6. Sweetgum

FPL: on the list.  
Southern shops: gum moves, gum warps, gum is what you make a board out of and hope.  
Both. It is on the list. It is not the first bow.

## 7. “Since 1903”

Brand wants one number. History has three. The essay will spend one short paragraph telling the truth and then get back to the box. If marketing needs a single year later, that is a different mouth.

## 8. Laminate versus steam

Laminate is stronger and can take a tighter radius. Steam is one piece of grain and no glue line on the edge. We will say that once, clearly, and not again. People who want a compound seat shell should not be in this essay.
