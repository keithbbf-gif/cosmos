# Reader pass

Read draft 07 out loud. Then the long form has to answer these, or refuse them on purpose.

## Shop reader (gloves still on)

- Can I hang part of this by the box? **Not 07. That is what 41 is for.** Long form should not try to be the card.
- Do I know what to do with a 1-inch oak stick tomorrow? **Yes. Hour, pull, twenty more, not three. 16 percent. Strap. Blanket.**
- Did you invent a broken bow? **No.**
- Are you selling me a café chair? **No.**
- Gum: am I being talked into it? **No. Good.**

## County reader

- Did you make the mill into a myth? **Almost, then you said stock.** Keep stock.
- 1903 / 1901 / 2008: still distinct? **Yes.**
- Will a cousin say we never steamed? **They would be right. The piece already says we made rectangles.** Do not add a defensive paragraph.

## Museum reader

- Thonet fair? **Yes. Veneer first, strap second, no invention of steam.**
- Aalto/Eames? **Named and set down. Enough.**
- V&A numbers? **Used. Attributed once.**

## Pedant

- 1,810,000 vs “more than 1.8 million.” **Honest.**
- Dry Tg 170 °C next to a 210 °F box. **Will confuse if we do not keep the water sentence.** Keep it in the long form, one breath.
- Fortune unnamed. **Fine. Numbers are on the sheet.**

## Voice

Read the first page without looking at the rest. Does it still sound like a shop in Warren, or like a careful summary of a shop in Warren? **07 is a little smooth.** The long form should put back two rough edges from 06: “conversation pit” (already in 07), “curve is a rumor,” “will not hero.” Those are ours. Do not add new slang to make up for smoothness.

## What the long form must do that 07 does not

07 is the piece you send in an email. The long form is the piece you keep in the shop book: more box, more pull, more wait, the day attached to the same stick, history with enough bone that a caption-writer cannot flatten it. Not a third of extra adjectives. Extra *work*.
