# Research — shop practice

Numbers first. Opinions tagged.

## What the stick is doing

Wood is cellulose rods in a lignin–hemicellulose glue. FPL (Wood Handbook, ch. 19): dry lignin matrix glass transition is about **170 °C / 338 °F**. That is too hot to live in. Water drops the glass transition. Atmospheric steam (~100 °C / 212 °F) plus moisture already in the stick is enough for the species that want to bend.

Plasticized hardwood can take **25–30 percent compression** along the grain and only **1–2 percent stretch**. That ratio is the whole craft. You are not stretching a bow. You are crumpling the inside and keeping the outside at zero.

If the outer face and inner face would have to differ by **more than about 3 percent** in length, FPL says you need end pressure — a strap with stops.

## Moisture

| Situation | MC before steam | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild chair slat, hot-plate press | ~12% | FPL |
| Southern oak chair-back *posts*, hot-plate | 12–15% | FPL older bending bulletin |
| Furniture part over a form, real curve | **15–20%** | FPL |
| Fortune’s shop rule | **12–16%**, air-dried | FWW 238 |
| Wet enough to heat without adding water | 20–25% | FPL |
| Green | 30% to >100% | Problem: hydrostatic crush on the concave face; long, cranky drying |

**Our house pick:** air-dried 15–18 percent for anything we would strap around a form. Meter it. South Arkansas will try to hand you 18 percent and call it dry. Fine for this. Not fine for a finish room.

Kiln-dried: Fortune says the lignin is set and the fibers tear. FPL says put the water back with a long steam or a soak. House: if the sticker is kiln-dried, soak it, expect a higher scrap rate, do not start a customer part on the first stick.

## Time and heat

| Source | Heat | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FPL, stock at 20–25% MC | steam or boil | ~**38 min/inch** (15 min/cm) |
| FPL, drier stock | steam or boil | ~**75 min/inch** (30 min/cm) |
| Fortune | 200–210 °F in the box | **1–2 hours/inch**; 3 hours on 1" is too long (wrinkles) |
| Thonet factory (their telling) | >100 °C, pressurized vessel | ~6 hours on their beech rods |

High-pressure steam makes wood plastic. FPL says it does **not** bend as successfully as atmospheric or low gauge. Do not build a pressure cooker.

**Working time out of the box (Fortune):** ~10 minutes on 1-inch stock, ~20 minutes on 2½-inch. You can put a stubborn one back in.

## Species that will and will not

FPL “commonly used”: white oak, red oak, elm, hickory, ash, beech, birch, maple, walnut, **sweetgum**, mahogany.

Softwoods: generally poor. Exceptions: Pacific yew, yellow-cedar. Boat-mild: Douglas-fir, southern yellow pine, northern and Atlantic white-cedar, redwood.

Fortune: start on ash, oak, hickory, elm, walnut. Softwoods too short in the fiber. Tropicals interlocked and tear.

**BBF bench, local and already in the building:**

| Wood | Bend? | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White oak | Yes | Ring-porous. Checks if you fan it wet. Cover the first day. Tannin + steel = blue-black. |
| Red oak | Yes | Same family of problems. More porous. |
| Hickory | Yes | Windsor people’s bow wood. Hard to mill, honest to bend. |
| Ash | Yes | Getting scarce and unsound in some lots. Inspect. |
| Walnut | Yes | Fortune puts it in the learning pile. Dark, expensive, worth not wasting. |
| Sweetgum | Yes (FPL) | We already cut it for boards. Not the first customer curve. |
| Sycamore | Maybe, not a star | We used it for carving boards. Do not advertise it as a bender. |
| Southern yellow pine | Mild boat curves | Not a chair bow. |
| Cherry, figured maple | Later | Fortune says after you know what you are doing. |

Grain: slope no steeper than about **1:15**. No pith, no shake, no rot, no knot in the bend, no “light and brittle” stick. Riven beats sawn. If you must saw, read the grain on the edge like you are looking for a liar.

## Strap, form, dry

- Strap on the **convex** face. End stops so the blank cannot grow. Snug until the strap takes a slight arc — Fortune. Too loose: outside tears. Too tight: inside wrinkles.
- Form can be stacked particleboard, MDF, or plywood, cut to the curve **plus springback**. We do not have a universal springback percent. Overbend a little, write down what that blank did, keep the note on the form.
- One person can pull up to about 1½ inch (Fortune). Thicker: block and tackle or a second body.
- If a kink starts, clamp behind it. Do not muscle past a kink.
- **Strap off at about one hour** or tannin and steel stain the face. Scraper will get a little. It will not get a lot.
- Dry in the curve until the piece is at the MC you will use it at — Fortune wants ~7–8 percent. FPL: cool and dry while restrained, then the stays can come off. A week is a shop guess. A meter is the answer.
- Oak and other ring-porous: blanket the first day, then air. Fortune. Fan is fine on walnut and the rest once it is on the form.
- After it is dry, more moisture can open the curve. Less can tighten it. Wet/dry cycling slowly straightens it. That is FPL, and it is why a towel bar over a stew pot is a bad design.

## Failures, named

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Break on the outside | No strap, runout, kiln-dried and proud, too tight a radius |
| Wrinkles on the inside | Oversteamed, strap too tight, green and hydrostatic |
| Checks | Dried too fast, especially oak; fan on a wet ring-porous face |
| Blue-black stain | Steel strap left on |
| Curve relaxes | Taken off the form wet; later MC went up |
| Curve gets sharper | Dried past the room it will live in |
| “It just won’t” | Cold, understeamed, or you waited fifteen minutes on a ¾-inch stick |

## Safety, short

Steam is invisible at the door. Gloves. Face to the side. Box sloped to a drain. Vent at the far end. Inlet low. No pressure. No kids wandering through a cycle. The water in the kettle is as dangerous as the vapor.

## What we still do not know from a BBF bench

We do not have a logged scrap rate on gum bows. We do not have a house form radius library. Those get written when someone bends the first twenty sticks and keeps the breaks.
