# BBF house — what we actually are

Write from the floor, not from the LinkedIn sentence.

## The name

Bradley is the county. Warren is the town that doubled when the mills came in. Wilmar is down the road. BBF is Bradley Brand Furniture, an LLC Keith Chambers opened in 2008. The “since 1903” line on the company page is brand language. The courthouse on the square is 1903. The Bradley Lumber Company that put this place on the hardwood map was incorporated September 1901 by Samuel Holmes Fullerton of St. Louis. Postcard copy from the mill said it sawed Arkansas soft pine, mild-textured oak, and other native hardwoods, and that it made flooring, furniture stock, pine millwork, and construction lumber. Furniture *stock*. Not café chairs.

Do not flatten those dates into one origin myth. A mill town that cut oak and gum for other people’s factories is a different ancestor than a Viennese chair works. It is still the right ancestor. We already know these species in the hand.

## What we shipped

From the old order sheets and partner SKUs, not from memory theater:

- Kitchen islands: Moro, Canyon Creek, Caney Creek, Little Red River, Shiloh, Honeysuckle. Stationary and on casters. Alpine White, Mocha Brown, Red Mahogany, Onyx Black.
- Boards: gum bread boards, walnut/ash, white oak, sycamore carving boards.
- Sold through Hayneedle, Overstock, Wayfair, Plum Creek, Jet, Walmart, Lowes, Dillard’s, Cresco/Resco, Macy’s, and a pile of independents.
- NAICS on the government listing: wood product manufacturing and nonupholstered wood household furniture.
- Addresses that show up in the paper: 501 Pennington Street, Warren; later 854 Rock Springs Road, Wilmar.

Those islands were casework. Square corners, panels, a top. Nobody was steaming a Moro apron into a parabola. If this piece starts talking as if we have been bending No. 14 backs since Fullerton, throw it out.

## Why steam belongs in this shop anyway

We already handle the woods the Forest Products Laboratory lists as willing: white oak, red oak, hickory, ash, walnut, sweetgum. Gum is not a Yankee craft-school novelty here. It is what we cut for bread boards. White oak is what we already know will move if you treat it wrong. Ash is getting harder to trust in the woods because of the borer, which is a fact, not a vibe.

A curve in a kitchen piece — a towel bar, a stretcher, a chair if we ever build one, a bow on a hall bench — is cheaper and stronger if the grain runs around the bend instead of being sawn out of a plank and left short on the edges. That is the only commercial reason that matters. History is how you keep from paying tuition Thonet and the boat shops already paid.

## Climate

South Arkansas air does not help you the way a Colorado shop’s air helps. Air-dried stock sits wetter here. That is not a problem for bending. It is a problem for believing a stick is ready to finish because it feels dry on Tuesday. Meter it.

## Voice of the house

Keith writes like a man who has been lied to by green logs and by software. Short. Numbered when it helps. He will not tolerate a claim without an artifact. Same rule here. If we say “one hour per inch,” we say who said it and what they assumed about moisture. If we do not know whether a particular blank will take a 4-inch radius, we say so and tell you how to find out with a scrap.
