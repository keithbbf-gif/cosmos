# What we will not claim

A list so the drafts cannot “improve” their way into a lie.

1. **We did not invent steam bending.** Egyptians, boatbuilders, Native bowyers and canoe-makers, English and American Windsor shops, and a bankrupt cabinetmaker from Boppard all got there first.

2. **Thonet did not invent moisture and heat.** He invented the metal strap on solid wood, a factory in the beech, a numbered catalog, and a shipping box of parts. That is enough. Do not give him the bow and the snowshoe.

3. **Alvar Aalto’s birch and the Eames shells are not this process.** Those are laminated and molded veneers. Strong. Beautiful. Different. If a caption says “steam-bent” on a plywood seat, it is wrong.

4. **BBF did not run a Thonet line.** We made islands and boards. This folder is practice and history for the house, not a rewritten catalog.

5. **“Since 1903” is not the mill’s incorporation date.** Bradley Lumber Company: 1901. Courthouse: 1903. BBF LLC: 2008. Use the dates that belong to the sentence you are in.

6. **Green wood is not automatically better.** Chairmakers who rive green oak and hickory get clean grain and a lot of water already in the cell wall. The Forest Service warns that green stock is a drying and hydrostatic-pressure problem when the bend is severe. Both things can be true. Do not pick a camp for the sake of a heading.

7. **Kiln-dried is not always dead.** Fortune (Fine Woodworking) is hard against kiln-dried because the lignin is set. FPL says low-MC stock just takes a longer steam or a soak to put water back. We will report both and say what we would do on Pennington: prefer air-dried 12–20 percent; if all you have is kiln-dried, soak and expect more breakage.

8. **“One hour per inch” is a shop rhyme, not a law.** FPL: about 38 minutes per inch at 20–25 percent MC, about 75 minutes per inch when drier. Fortune: one to two hours per inch at 200–210 °F, and three hours on a 1-inch stick is too much — the inside wrinkles. We will print the rhyme and the footnotes.

9. **We will not quote a minimum bend radius as if it were universal.** FPL’s useful number is this: if the outer and inner faces differ by much more than 3 percent in length, you need end pressure (a strap). Tighter than that without a strap is hope.

10. **We will not write “perfect parts every time.”** Fortune wrote that. He has thirty-five years and a strap. We will write “most parts, if you do the dull work.”

11. **We will not invent a broken bow from last Tuesday.** Failure modes are real. Fake shop memoir is not.

12. **We will not tell anyone to pressurize a homemade box.** Atmospheric steam. A vent. A drain. Gloves. Face out of the door.

13. **Sweetgum will not be talked down as trash wood.** USDA lists it with the bending hardwoods. We already sell it as a board. It is not white oak. It is not useless.

14. **Ash is not promised forever.** The borer is in the country. Use what is sound. Do not write a eulogy in the middle of a how-to.

15. **A bent piece is a little weaker than a straight one.** FPL says so, and says it is seldom enough to matter. Do not hide it. Do not dramatize it.
