# Card — species

Hang this by the rack. FPL list plus this shop’s mouth.

| Wood | Will it bend? | This shop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White oak | Yes | First customer curve. Ring-porous. Blanket day one. Steel stains it. |
| Red oak | Yes | Same family of trouble. More porous. |
| Hickory | Yes | Honest bow wood. Hard to mill. |
| Ash | Yes | Sound only. Look for the borer. |
| Elm | Yes | Not always in our rack. |
| Walnut | Yes | Expensive. Do not learn the box on it. |
| Beech | Yes | Thonet’s wood. Not a Bradley County staple. |
| Birch | Yes | Same. |
| Maple | Yes | Later. Figured maple later than that. |
| Sweetgum | Yes (lab) | We already cut boards. Not the first bow. Keep a tally if we try it. |
| Mahogany | Yes (lab) | Not our woods. |
| Sycamore | Not a star | Carving boards. Do not advertise. |
| SYP | Mild only | Boat-plank curve. Not a hoop. |
| Other softwoods | Mostly no | Short fiber. Yew and yellow-cedar are someone else’s exceptions. |
| Tropicals | Mostly no | Interlocked. They tear. |

**Grain:** no steeper than 1 in 15. No pith, shake, rot, knot in the bend. Riven beats sawn.

**Moisture, house:** 15–18% for a form bend. Meter.

**Sources:** USDA FPL *Wood Handbook* ch. 19; Fortune, *Fine Woodworking* 238, for the learning list and kiln caution.
