# Section try — the wait

The bend is the loud part. The wait is the part that keeps it.

The glue in the stick has to set in the new shape, and the water has to leave until the piece is as dry as the room it will live in. Fortune likes seven to eight percent. A kitchen in Warren in August may not be seven percent. Meter the room. Meter the piece. The calendar is a guess. He guesses a week. The meter is the boss.

While it is wet and restrained, the curve is a rumor. Take the stays off early and you will watch it open. Leave it until it is at service moisture and it will hold — not forever, not against a steam kettle, but hold.

Moisture after the fact still has an opinion. The handbook is plain. More water later and the piece loses some curve. The wrinkled inside face swells along its length. Less water and the curve can tighten. Wet and dry, wet and dry, and the piece slowly remembers the log. So you do not hang a steam-bent rail over a pot of simmering stock and then act surprised. And you do not finish a piece in a dehumidified room and ship it to a house that is never air-conditioned in July without thinking about the move.

Oak: blanket the first day. Ring-porous wood will check if you hurry the skin. Then air. A fan is a tool, not a personality.

Steel strap: off at about an hour. Tannin and iron make ink. A card scraper will get a blush. It will not get a night’s sleep.

A bent piece is a little weaker than the same piece left straight. The lab says so, and says it is seldom enough to matter. Geometry still matters. A 5/8-inch towel bar with a long unsupported run is not “seldom.” Design the section for the lean, then bend it.

When you finally take it off the rack, do not celebrate with a moisture cycle. Let it sit in the room it will be finished in. Then cut the joinery. A stick that is still arguing will open a shoulder you already cut square.
