# Section try — Warren, and what we actually made

Bradley County is named for Hugh Bradley. Warren is the county seat. The courthouse on the square is 1903, which is probably why a company page likes that year. The mill that put hardwood in the town’s mouth is older by two years.

In September 1901 Samuel Holmes Fullerton of St. Louis stood up the Bradley Lumber Company here. Southern Lumber and Arkansas Lumber came in right around the same season. By 1907 the Bradley mill was on the order of a hundred thousand board feet a day and about three hundred fifty men, and it was a serious hardwood dealer, not a hobby. A postcard from the plant said the quiet part: they sawed Arkansas soft pine, mild-textured oak, and other native hardwoods, and they made flooring, **furniture stock**, pine millwork, and construction lumber. Stock. Other people’s factories bought the rectangles.

Potlatch took the Warren mills in the 1950s. The ground has had other names since. That is their story.

Ours starts in 2008 as Bradley Brand Furniture, LLC. Pennington Street in Warren — Pennington is an old name in that plat — and later Rock Springs Road in Wilmar. We built kitchen islands and we built boards. The islands had creek and river names because the water here already had them: Moro, Caney Creek, Canyon Creek, Little Red River, Shiloh, Honeysuckle. Mocha Brown and Alpine White. Some on casters. The boards were gum, walnut with ash, white oak, sycamore. They went out through the catalog houses and the stores that would have us.

Those were boxes of rectangles. That was the job, and it was an honest job. A kitchen island does not need a steamed stretcher to be real furniture. It needs to be square, finished, and still flat in August.

So why write this folder?

Because the same woods the lab lists as willing are the woods this county already cut for other people, and the woods we already put a knife on. White oak. Red oak. Hickory. Ash. Walnut. Sweetgum. A curve that keeps its grain is a stronger way to turn a corner than a sawn bracket with short fiber on the edge. If we ever put a bow on a bench, or a bent towel bar that is not a piece of iron, or a chair that belongs in the same house as a Moro island, this is the process. Not a rebrand. A tool we refused to treat as a museum trick.

We will not say we have been doing it since Fullerton. We will not say 1903 unless we are talking about the courthouse. We will say 1901 when we mean the mill, 2008 when we mean the LLC, and today when we mean the box.
