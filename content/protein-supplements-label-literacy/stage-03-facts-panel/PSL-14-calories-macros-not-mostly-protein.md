# Calories and macros — when the powder is not mostly protein

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

The category word on the front is **protein**. The energy in the scoop might not be. Calories come from protein, carbohydrate, fat, and (in smaller, annoying ways) from some sugar alcohols and fermentation leftovers. If you only read the protein line, you will mis-sort a **mass gainer**, a **flavored meal mix**, and a **lean isolate** into the same mental bin. The panel already sorted them. You have to look.

## A pocket calculation that does not need a brand’s blog

Per serving, write:

- Protein grams × 4  
- Total carbohydrate grams × 4  
- Fat grams × 9  

Add those three. Compare the sum to the **calorie** line.

You will not get a perfect match. Rounding, fiber, sugar alcohols, and the way organic acids are handled create slack. You are looking for **shape**, not forensic accounting:

- If protein-calories are most of the sum, you are in “protein powder” territory.  
- If carbohydrate-calories dominate, you are in gainer / dessert / meal-mix territory.  
- If fat-calories are large, read the ingredient list for oils, nuts, MCT, or dairy fat that the front did not mention.

This pack does not tell you which shape to buy. It tells you not to let the word PROTEIN pick the shape for you.

## Protein-by-weight is the other honest fraction

Protein grams ÷ serving grams × 100 = **percent protein by weight** (of the powder, not of your diet).

Examples of the kind of range you will see (illustrative, not a standard):

- A whey isolate serving of 30 g powder / 27 g protein ≈ 90% protein by weight.  
- A whey concentrate 32 g / 24 g ≈ 75%.  
- A plant blend 35 g / 20 g ≈ 57%.  
- A gainer 150 g / 25 g ≈ 17%.

The last product can still declare 25 g of protein. It is also a carbohydrate tub. Both statements can be true. Only one of them is in 80-point type.

## Why isolates can still have calories that “don’t look like protein”

Flavored isolates carry sweeteners, gums, and lecithin. Unflavored isolates can still carry a gram or two of leftover lactose or fat, depending on the process. A zero-sugar claim is not a zero-calorie claim. Read the calorie line.

Calorie rounding can make a serving look like 110 when your 4-4-9 sum is 118. Do not start a conspiracy thread over eight calories. Do start a thread with yourself if the 4-4-9 sum is 280 and the front says “clean protein.”

## Mass gainers are not a moral failure

They are a **labeling identity**. A product designed to deliver a lot of energy will show a lot of energy. The literacy failure is buying one because the word protein was larger than the word carbohydrate. PSL-45 is a worked fictional gainer. This lesson is the habit: **calories first, then the split, then the protein line.**

## What we are not doing with calories

We are not assigning you an energy budget.  
We are not calling carbohydrate “bad.”  
We are not calling fat “hormonal.”  
We are not saying a low-calorie scoop treats a condition.

Calories on a panel are an **energy declaration**. They are not a verdict on your body.

## Label drill

Complete this card for one product:

| Item | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Serving weight (g) | |
| Calories | |
| Protein (g) | Protein kcal (×4) |
| Total carb (g) | Carb kcal (×4) |
| Fat (g) | Fat kcal (×9) |
| 4-4-9 sum | |
| Protein / serving weight (%) | |
| Dominant energy source (P/C/F) | |

If the dominant source is not protein, write the front-of-tub word that hid that fact.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say you should cut gainers or chase isolates. It will not say calories from powder are different from calories from food as a law of nature. It will not use “clean” as a nutrient. It will not connect calorie counts to a diagnosis.
