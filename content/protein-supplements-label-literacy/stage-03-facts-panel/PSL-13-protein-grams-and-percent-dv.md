# Protein grams, % Daily Value, and the 50 g reference

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

The protein line is two different measurements pretending to be one nutrient. **Grams** are mostly a nitrogen story (PSL-17). **% Daily Value**, when it appears, is a **quality-adjusted** story that uses a Daily Reference Value of **50 grams** for adults and children 4 years of age or older (21 CFR 101.9(c)(7)). If you divide the gram number by 50 and the %DV does not match, the label may still be right. You may have just met PDCAAS.

## The gram number

21 CFR 101.9(c)(7) lets protein content be calculated as nitrogen × 6.25 unless an AOAC method requires a different factor. The declaration is rounded to the nearest gram (with small-amount exceptions). That gram number is what shoppers compare. It is also what “25g PROTEIN” on the front is supposed to agree with — **per the same serving**.

The gram number does **not**, by itself, tell you:

- whether the nitrogen came from intact protein or from free amino acids,
- whether the amino acid pattern is complete,
- or whether the powder is mostly protein by weight.

Those are later lessons. Here: **grams are the quantity line.**

## The 50 g Daily Reference Value

The 50 g figure is a **labeling reference**, not a personal target this pack is assigning to you. It is not a clinician’s assessment. It is not a training plan. It is the denominator FDA chose so %DV has a common stick.

Other reference numbers exist in the same paragraph for other age/life-stage representations (infants, children 1–3, pregnant or lactating women). If a product is represented for those groups, the stick changes. Most gym tubs are represented for adults. Read the “purported to be for” language, not your assumption.

This pack does not tell those groups to use or avoid powder. PSL-47 exists to send personal questions to a clinician.

## When %DV must appear — the trigger

Protein %DV is not always printed the way sodium %DV is. For foods, 21 CFR 101.9(c)(7)(i) requires the percent of the DRV when a protein **claim** is made. Front-of-pack “20g protein” is a claim. That claim can **force** the panel to show a PDCAAS-corrected %DV.

This is the mechanic the Ninth Circuit walked in the 2023 protein-%DV cases: grams on the front can match grams in the box, while %DV is lower than grams ÷ 50 because **quality correction** reduced the numerator.

Literacy: if you see 20 g protein and 30% DV, do the arithmetic.

- 20 ÷ 50 = 40%.  
- Printed %DV = 30%.  
- Implied PDCAAS ≈ 30/40 = 0.75.

That back-of-envelope is a **reading tool**, not a lab certificate. Rounding exists. Still: a large gap is information.

## PDCAAS in one paragraph

Protein digestibility-corrected amino acid score compares the amino acid pattern and digestibility of the protein to a reference pattern, then caps at 1.00. High-quality animal proteins often sit at or near 1. Many single plant proteins sit lower. Blends can raise the score. **Collagen** is a famous incomplete protein (PSL-23). U.S. **%DV uses PDCAAS**, not DIAAS. If a brand brags DIAAS, they are giving you a scientific score that is **not** the label method unless they also did the PDCAAS math.

## Products that contain only individual amino acids

21 CFR 101.36 says protein shall **not** be declared on labels of products that, other than technological ingredients, contain only individual amino acids. A “BCAA powder” that is not intact protein should not be wearing a protein gram line as if it were a steak. If it does, you are looking at a category or identity problem, not a serving-size problem.

## What %DV is not

It is not a grade for flavor.  
It is not “how much of your life this scoop should be.”  
It is not a disease-risk reduction claim.  
It is not permission to ignore the rest of the diet.

A 50% DV scoop is 25 g of quality-adjusted protein on the adult stick — if the math was done right. It is not a treatment.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say a PDCAAS below 1 makes a powder worthless. It will not say you must eat 50 g of protein from powder. It will not say plant proteins are inferior as foods. It will not convert %DV into a clinical promise.
