# Enzyme blends and “absorption” language

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

A lot of modern protein tubs include a milligram-scale **protease / lactase / “digestive enzyme blend.”** The blend is small. The **speech** around it is large: absorbs better, no bloat, works even if you have trouble with dairy. That speech is where category problems start. This lesson teaches you to find the milligrams, name the enzymes, and send the speech to stage 6. It does not teach you that enzymes treat a digestive diagnosis.

## What you will see on the panel

Sometimes a named enzyme (papain, bromelain, lactase, protease) with an amount or an activity unit (HUT, ALU, FCC). Sometimes a **proprietary digestive blend** at 100 mg that lists five enzymes without amounts. Sometimes nothing on the facts box and a sentence on the web only.

Activity units are not grams of protein. A number in HUT is a **lab activity**, not a scoop of whey. If you cannot find any amount at all, you have fog.

## Lactase next to whey

Lactase is the enzyme that hydrolyzes lactose. Its presence is a **formulation hint** that the concentrate still has lactose worth the firm’s attention, or that the brand wants a story. It is **not** a promise that the product is safe for someone with a milk allergy (allergy is not lactose). It is not a diagnosis of lactose intolerance, and it is not medical advice to try the tub if dairy has been a clinical problem for you.

If a brand says the enzyme “lets you use this whey even with lactose intolerance,” they have started talking about a **condition**. That is the cliff. This pack will not finish the sentence for them.

## “Absorption” and “bioavailability”

Those words are structure/function-shaped when they stay with **normal** digestion. They become disease-shaped when they attach to a pathology, a drug, or a lab deficiency. They become **ordinary misleading** when a 50 mg blend is asked to carry a 25 g protein claim’s entire credibility.

Literacy: the **protein grams** are still the protein grams. An enzyme line does not raise them. It does not change PDCAAS. It does not delete a spike. It is a **side character**.

## Protease and “pre-digested”

Hydrolysate (PSL-20) is a process done **in the plant**. A protease in the tub is a process the brand hopes happens **in the glass or the gut**. They are not the same object. Neither object is a treatment. “Pre-digested” on a hydrolysate plus “digestive enzymes” on the same lid is stacked speech. Read it as stacked speech.

## Bromelain and papain as halo herbs

Pineapple and papaya enzymes are real enzymes and also **story ingredients**. If they appear without activity units, they may be there for the story. If they appear with a joint or sinus sentence, you have left protein literacy and entered a different supplement category that this pack will not market.

## Label drill

On one protein that mentions enzymes (tub or page):

- Blend name and total milligrams (or “not stated”):  
- Enzymes named:  
- Activity units (or “none”):  
- Exact absorption / digest / bloat sentence:  
- Disclaimer next to that sentence?  

Classify the sentence: *process / structure-function-shaped / condition-shaped / decoration*. If you chose condition-shaped, write the disease-claim lock in your own hand. That is the drill.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say enzyme blends work or do not work. It will not say they treat bloating as a diagnosis. It will not say you should add lactase drops to a shake. It will not say hydrolysate plus enzymes is a clinical protocol.

## Carry forward
