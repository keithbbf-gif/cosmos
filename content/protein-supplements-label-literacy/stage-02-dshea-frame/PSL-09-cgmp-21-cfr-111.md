# cGMP (21 CFR 111) — what a lid badge cannot prove

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

If the product is a dietary supplement, the firm that manufactures, packages, labels, or holds it is supposed to follow **current good manufacturing practice** in 21 CFR part 111. That part is about **systems**: people, plants, equipment, production, quality control, packaging and labeling control, holding, returns, and records. It is not about whether protein “works.” It is not a disease claim, and it is not a consumer medal.

When a lid says “GMP certified,” you are looking at **marketing about a system**. The regulation itself does not issue a cute badge.

## What part 111 is trying to prevent

In plain language, part 111 is trying to make it more likely that:

- the powder in the bag matches the identity on the label,
- the strength (how much of the dietary ingredient is there) is what the label said,
- the product is not filthy or contaminated in ways a quality system should have caught,
- the wrong label does not go on the right bag,
- and someone can reconstruct what happened when a lot goes wrong.

Those are **manufacturing** aims. A shopper who converts them into “this scoop is clinically ideal for me” has left the regulation.

## What part 111 does not do

It does not:

- pre-approve the formula,
- evaluate a structure/function sentence for FDA,
- set a federal therapeutic dose of protein,
- replace allergen labeling rules,
- or turn a supplement into a drug.

A facility can have a quality-control unit and still print a disease claim. Those are different failures. Literacy keeps them in different drawers.

## How to read “GMP” on a protein tub

Ask four questions, in order:

1. **Does the mark name a program?**  
   “GMP” alone is a fog. “Certified to NSF/ANSI 173” or another named standard is at least a pointer (PSL-36).

2. **Who did the certifying?**  
   Self-declared GMP is a press release. A third party you can look up is a different object.

3. **What was in scope?**  
   Facility? Product? Only packaging? A certificate about a building is not a certificate about your flavor.

4. **Is it current?**  
   Certificates expire. A 2019 PDF in a 2026 product-image carousel is a prop.

If you cannot answer those from the label or a page that loads without a salesperson, you do not have a GMP fact. You have a word.

## Labeling control is the part shoppers forget

Part 111 is not only mixers and sieves. It includes **packaging and labeling control**: the system that is supposed to keep last month’s flavor sticker off this month’s bag, and last year’s Supplement Facts off this year’s reformulation. When the scoop in your kitchen does not match the panel you photographed in the store, you are in that neighborhood — plus ordinary commerce problems like a website image that was never updated.

Literacy move: when numbers feel impossible, check whether you are looking at **the same lot’s** label. PSL-39 is about lot codes. This lesson is why lot codes exist in a quality system at all.

## Food cGMP vs supplement cGMP

Conventional foods have their own current good manufacturing practice world (21 CFR part 117 and related rules). A protein sold as a **food** is not automatically on part 111. A protein sold as a **supplement** is. Category (PSL-05) decides which manufacturing book the firm is supposed to be holding. A brand that sells the same powder both ways does not get to pick the easier book in an inspection because the Instagram tile was prettier.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say part 111 makes supplements safe. It will not say a facility without a badge is illegal. It will not say FDA “GMP-certifies” tubs. It will not treat a quality system as a reason to use a powder for a medical condition.

## Carry forward

PSL-10 is the duty that barely appears on labels: serious adverse event reporting. Silence on the panel is not silence in the statute.
