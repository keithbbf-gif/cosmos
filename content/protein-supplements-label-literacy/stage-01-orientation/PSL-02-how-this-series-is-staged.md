# How this series is staged

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

“Staged” here is a **curriculum word**, not a compliment. The forty-eight lessons are drafts, in order, with prerequisites. Stage 8’s fictional labels will punish you if you skipped the panel math in stage 3. Stage 6’s claim types will sound like word games if you skipped the DSHEA frame in stage 2. The pack is built so each file can be reviewed alone — and so a reviewer can still see the spine.

## Why a protein-powder topic needs stages

A tub is a small object that sits on four different legal and scientific floors at once:

1. **Identity** — is this a food, a supplement, or (illegally) talking like a drug?
2. **Quantity** — what is a serving, and what was actually measured?
3. **Composition** — which proteins, which extras, which allergens?
4. **Speech** — which sentences on the canister are nutrient-content claims, which are structure/function claims, which are decoration, and which would be disease claims?

If you start at speech, you will argue about adjectives. If you start at identity, the adjectives shrink to their proper size. That is why the stages run **orientation → statute → panel → protein identity → other ingredients → claims → quality signals → practice**.

## The eight stages in one screen

**Stage 1 — Orientation.**  
What a label is; how to walk this pack; the disease-claim lock; who regulates the tub; food vs supplement vs drug.

**Stage 2 — DSHEA frame.**  
What 1994 changed; what FDA does not pre-approve; new dietary ingredients; manufacturing practice; why adverse-event duties barely appear on the panel.

**Stage 3 — Facts panel.**  
The box. Scoops. Protein grams and % Daily Value. Calories that are not protein. Sugars. Minerals that rode along with the dairy or the pea.

**Stage 4 — Protein identity.**  
Nitrogen math, amino lists, “complete,” whey classes, casein, plants, collagen, other animals, free amino acids.

**Stage 5 — Other ingredients.**  
Sweeteners, lecithin and gums, enzyme talk, allergen lines, vegan/kosher/halal marks.

**Stage 6 — Claims.**  
Structure/function versus disease; the mandatory disclaimer; “clinically studied”; “high protein”; pictures and implied claims.

**Stage 7 — Quality signals.**  
Seals, “natural,” metals warnings, lot codes, certificates of analysis.

**Stage 8 — Practice.**  
Arithmetic, cost per 25 g protein, three worked fictional tubs, other-country panels, special populations, a red-flag checklist.

Canon files (`_canon/`) sit beside the stages. They are locks and references, not lessons. The test suite counts `PSL-*.md` files, not the glossary.

## How a single lesson is built

Every lesson repeats the same bones so a tired reader still knows where the drill is:

1. **The move** — one job this file exists to do.
2. **Teaching body** — statute, panel mechanic, or food-science distinction. No brand prosecution.
3. **Label drill** — something you can do with a real or photographed tub, or with a pencil.
4. **What this draft will not say** — a local restatement of the pack lock.
5. **Carry forward** — the next `PSL` id, not a shopping list.

If you are reviewing this pack as a writer or counsel, read the local “will not say” before you edit the teaching body. Edits that “punch up” a sentence into an implied disease claim fail the pack even if they read better on social.

## Draft, not published labeling

These files are **staged drafts** in a repository. They are not:

- FDA-notified structure/function claims,
- camera-ready copy for a canister,
- medical-nutrition therapy,
- or a COSMOS Core module.

“Staged” also means **not secretly live**. Nothing about a folder existing on a branch makes these sentences a product label. If someone copies a paragraph onto a tub, they have left this pack and entered their own compliance problem.

## Suggested sitting sizes

- **One lesson:** the drill plus the move. Fifteen quiet minutes.
- **One stage:** an evening. Do not binge stage 6 without stage 2.
- **The whole pack:** treat it as a short course, not a thread. Stage 8 is an exam you give yourself.

Skipping ahead is allowed for a specialist who already owns the earlier floor. It is not allowed as a way to harvest adjectives.

## Label drill

Open `MANIFEST.toml` in this folder. Write the stage number that owns each of these questions (no peeking at later files if you can help it):

1. Why two protein numbers — grams and %DV — can disagree.  
2. Why “GMP certified” on a lid is not the same as FDA approval.  
3. Why a plant-blend tub can say “20g protein” and still show a %DV that is not 40%.  
4. Why a mass-gainer’s front can say PROTEIN while most of the calories are carbohydrate.

Then check your answers against the stage table in the README. The point is not a grade. The point is to feel the spine before you walk it.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say you must finish the pack before you buy groceries. It will not say later stages “unlock benefits.” It will not imply that reading labels is a therapy.
