# Metals, Proposition 65, and what the front panel hides

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

Protein fractions come from soil-grown plants and from animals that eat plants. **Minerals** in soil include ones shoppers do not want in large amounts: lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury — the “heavy metals” conversation. The **front** of a tub will not give you a microgram table. A **California Proposition 65** warning sometimes will give you a sentence. A COA (PSL-40) sometimes will give you a table. Literacy is knowing what each of those objects is — and what it is not.

This lesson will not tell you a powder is toxic. It will not tell you a powder is clean. It will not turn a metal microgram into a diagnosis.

## There is no single federal “protein powder metal limit” on the lid

FDA has action levels and guidances in various food corners, and it can treat a product as adulterated if it is contaminated. That is not the same as a consumer-facing metals box on every whey. Absence of a metals line is **category-typical**, like the thin adverse-event story in PSL-10. Do not infer zero. Do not infer a crisis.

Independent journalists and labs have published surveys of metals in protein powders. Those surveys are **snapshots of lots**, not a statute. Use them as a reason to ask for a COA, not as a reason this pack will name a winner.

## Proposition 65 is a state warning regime

California’s Safe Drinking Water and Toxic Enforcement Act requires a warning before exposing people to listed chemicals above certain safe-harbor levels. Protein powders that sell into California often carry a **lead** (or other listed chemical) warning.

What the warning **is**:

- a **state** compliance sentence,
- triggered by a listed chemical and a numeric safe harbor,
- often written in a standard font you will start to recognize.

What the warning **is not**:

- an FDA potency rating,
- a finding that the product is illegal in the other forty-nine states,
- a finding that a product **without** the warning is “cleaner” (the firm may not sell in California, may have a different lot, or may have made a different legal judgment),
- or a disease claim that the powder treats metal toxicity.

A warning next to a front panel that says “clean” is a **tension**. Note it. Do not resolve it with a meme.

## Plant vs dairy, cocoa vs unflavored (patterns, not verdicts)

Public surveys often show **higher** metal numbers on some **plant** and **cocoa-heavy** products than on some unflavored dairy isolates. That is a **pattern in those snapshots**, explained in part by plants being closer to soil and cocoa being a metal-accumulating crop. It is not a law that every pea powder is worse than every whey. It is a reason to **ask for the table** on the specific lot, especially if you eat many servings a day — a question for you and, if needed, a clinician, not for this file’s dosage advice.

## What the front hid

The front hid:

- the micrograms,
- the test method,
- the serving used for the warning calculation,
- and whether the warning is for lead, cadmium, or a list.

The back hid it too, unless Prop 65 or a QR-to-COA is there. That is why stage 7 exists.

## Label drill

On one protein:

- Prop 65 / metals warning text (or “none”):  
- Listed chemical (if named):  
- Front words that sit in tension (“clean,” “pure,” “tested”):  
- Any QR or URL that claims a metals test:  
- Did that URL show **this product** and a **date**?  

If the URL is a blog about metals in “protein powders” as a class, it is **not** a lot test.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say you should stop eating plants. It will not say whey is free of metals. It will not say a Prop 65 warning means the product will cause a listed harm — the statute is a warning regime, and this pack will not finish a disease sentence the warning started. It will not set a microgram budget for you.

## Carry forward

PSL-39 is the small print that makes every quality story lot-shaped: lot codes, best-by dates, and storage lines.
