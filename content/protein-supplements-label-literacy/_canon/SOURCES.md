# Sources — protein powder label literacy

Authority is the **official text**, not this pack. Links were checked as teaching pointers in September 2026. Regulations move. If a URL and the eCFR disagree, believe the eCFR.

## Statute and rules (United States)

- Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA), Pub. L. 103-417.
- Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act § 201(g) (drug definition); § 201(ff) (dietary supplement); § 403(r)(6) (statements of nutritional support); § 403(w) (major food allergens).
- 21 CFR 101.9 — Nutrition labeling of food (protein grams, nitrogen factor, PDCAAS-corrected %DV, Daily Reference Value 50 g for adults and children 4+).
- 21 CFR 101.36 — Nutrition labeling of dietary supplements (Supplement Facts; protein not declared on products that contain only individual amino acids).
- 21 CFR 101.54 — Nutrient content claims (“good source,” “high” / “excellent source”).
- 21 CFR 101.93 — Structure/function and related statements; disclaimer; disease-claim criteria.
- 21 CFR part 111 — Current good manufacturing practice for dietary supplements.
- Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act of 2004 (FALCPA).
- Food Allergy Safety, Treatment, Education, and Research Act of 2021 (FASTER Act) — sesame as the ninth major food allergen, effective 2023-01-01.
- Dietary Supplement and Nonprescription Drug Consumer Protection Act (2006) — serious adverse event reporting.

## Other U.S. agencies the label does not replace

- Federal Trade Commission, *Health Products Compliance Guidance* (2022) — advertising substantiation. A claim that never appears on the tub can still appear in an ad, a landing page, or a creator script. FTC still owns that.
- California Proposition 65 (Safe Drinking Water and Toxic Enforcement Act of 1986) — a **state** warning regime. A Prop 65 line is not an FDA potency statement.

## Protein measurement and quality (food science, not disease claims)

- AOAC Official Methods — nitrogen determination; protein often calculated as nitrogen × 6.25 unless a food-specific factor is required.
- FAO/WHO — Protein Digestibility-Corrected Amino Acid Score (PDCAAS) as used in 21 CFR 101.9(c)(7).
- FAO — Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score (DIAAS) as a later scientific score. **DIAAS is not the U.S. label %DV method.** Lessons that mention DIAAS say so.

## Third-party marks (what a seal is, not an endorsement)

Program rules change. Treat the **current** public standard as authority:

- NSF International — Dietary Supplement and Certified for Sport programs.
- Informed-Sport / Informed-Choice (LGC).
- U.S. Pharmacopeia (USP) Dietary Supplement Verification.
- Banned Substances Control Group (BSCG).

This pack does not certify any product and does not say a seal makes a powder “safe,” “effective,” or lawful to use as a drug.

## Court and commentary used only to teach a labeling mechanic

- Ninth Circuit discussion of the protein-claim “trigger” that requires PDCAAS-corrected %DV when a protein claim appears outside the panel (useful for literacy; not a brand accusation). See the 2023 protein-%DV labeling opinions discussing 21 CFR 101.9(c)(7).

## What we do not treat as sources

- Affiliate roundups, “best protein 2026” listicles, and forum nitrogen-spike lore without a method.
- A brand’s own “clinically studied” PDF used as if it were a monograph.
- This pack citing itself.
