# Red-flag checklist

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

This is the aisle card. It does not replace stages 1–7. It is the **fail-closed** list: if a box trips, you slow down or you put the tub back. A flag is not a proof of crime. A flag is a reason the easy story died.

## Stop flags (put it back, or take the photo to a clinician / counsel — not to this pack)

1. **Disease claim** on the tub or the first screen of the product page: diagnose / treat / cure / prevent a disease; a characteristic symptom used as the target; a therapy substitute; a glucometer-and-ribbon context (PSL-31, PSL-35).  
2. **Allergen you must avoid** is an **ingredient** (Contains or parenthetical) (PSL-29).  
3. **No responsible U.S. firm** you can name (PSL-10).  
4. You are buying for a **child, pregnancy, or clinical care** and you do not have a clinician in the loop (PSL-47).

## Slow flags (do the math before you believe the front)

5. Front protein grams ≠ panel protein grams for the **same** serving (PSL-12).  
6. Serving on the panel ≠ serving in the directions (double-scoop “results”) (PSL-12).  
7. Net weight ÷ serving grams is **far** from declared servings (PSL-41).  
8. Protein-by-weight is **gainer territory** while the aisle word is isolate (PSL-14, PSL-45).  
9. %DV is **far** from grams ÷ 50 and the front says “complete” (PSL-13, PSL-19).  
10. Fit test fails: named extras cannot fit the serving after the protein line (PSL-25).  
11. First ingredient is not the protein the front named (PSL-20, PSL-22).  
12. Collagen or “beef isolate” wearing a complete-protein costume without a tryptophan/%DV story (PSL-23, PSL-24).

## Speech flags (sort, then decide whether you still want the brand)

13. Structure/function sentence **without** a bold disclaimer pair on a U.S. supplement (PSL-32).  
14. Disclaimer **plus** a disease claim (believe the disease claim as intended use) (PSL-32).  
15. “Clinically studied / doctor recommended / university” with no openable citation to **this** product (PSL-33).  
16. “FDA approved” for a routine powder (PSL-07).  
17. Enzyme or “absorption” speech attached to a **condition** (PSL-28).  
18. Identity mark fighting the list (vegan + whey; kosher-pareve + milk) (PSL-30).

## Quality flags (look up, do not worship)

19. Seal that **fails** the certifier directory (PSL-36).  
20. “GMP certified” with no program or certifier (PSL-09).  
21. Prop 65 / metals warning next to “pure / clean” with no COA (PSL-38, PSL-40).  
22. No lot code you can find (PSL-39).  
23. Website facts panel that does not match the bag in your hand (PSL-39).

## Not flags (do not teach yourself to panic)

- A short Supplement Facts box that omitted zeros (PSL-11).  
- A missing %DV on a product that makes **no** protein claim (still look) (PSL-13).  
- Sunflower lecithin, sucralose, or xanthan as such (PSL-26, PSL-27).  
- A facility statement **without** the allergen as an ingredient, if your clinician and you treat those as different rows (PSL-29).  
- A foreign panel that lacks a DSHEA disclaimer (PSL-46).  
- Price higher than the last tub you loved (PSL-42 is a stick, not a virtue).

## How to run the card in two minutes

1. Cover the front. Read serving g, protein g, calories, first ingredient, Contains.  
2. Uncover the front. Check flags 5, 11, 1.  
3. Flip for disclaimer, lot, firm name.  
4. If a seal or “FDA” word appears, treat it as a **lookup**, not a halo.  
5. If any stop flag is true, stop.

## Label drill

Take this checklist to one tub. Mark each of the 23 numbered flags **Y / N / n.a.** Do not skip the “not flags” section — write one thing you **almost** panicked about that is in the not-flag list.

Keep the card. Formulas change. The next lot is a new drill (PSL-39).

## What this draft will not say

It will not say a flagged tub is illegal. It will not say an unflagged tub is appropriate for you. It will not convert the checklist into a purchase recommendation. It will not diagnose a reaction.
