# Worked example — fictional plant blend

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

**FICTIONAL** brand: “Leaf Ledger Protein.” The blend is pea-forward with rice and a hemp dusting, a dessert flavor, and a %DV that does not match naive grams ÷ 50. The point is to practice **quality-adjusted reading** and **identity-mark tension**, not to rank plants against dairy.

## FICTIONAL principal display

**LEAF LEDGER**  
Complete Plant Protein · Vegan · Dietary Supplement  
**20g PROTEIN** · Salted Caramel  
Net wt 2 lb (907 g)  
Leaf icon. “Clinically studied pea protein.”

## Annotation walk

**Serving (PSL-12).** The serving is **2 scoops / 36 g**. A shopper who costs “per scoop” will halve the protein to 10 g and still see 20 g on the front. Front 20 g matches the **panel serving**, not one scoop. Write that down.

**Servings math (PSL-41).** 907 ÷ 36 = 25.2. Declared 28. **Miss ~10%.** Flag. Could be rounding plus a 32 g reality hiding in a 36 g line — weigh a level two-scoop if you owned it.

**%DV (PSL-13).** 20 ÷ 50 = 40%. Printed 32%. Implied PDCAAS ≈ 0.80. The word **Complete** on the front is doing adjective work the %DV only partly supports. Not a 1.0 story. Not a worthless story. A **blend** story (PSL-19).

**Energy (PSL-14).** Protein 80 kcal; carb 32; fat 27; sum 139 vs 140. Protein is the largest slice but not the isolate’s 90% by weight. 20/36 = 56% protein by weight. The 8 g carbohydrate plus 3 g fat are the rest of the seed and the flavor system.

**Sodium (PSL-16).** 320 mg is the salted-caramel bill. A “plant = low salt” assumption dies here.

**Identity (PSL-22).** Order: pea, rice, hemp. Hemp is last — a dusting until proven otherwise. Completeness, if any, is pea+rice work, not the hemp photograph.

**Sweeteners (PSL-26).** Front may feel “stevia.” List is stevia **and** sucralose **and** monk fruit. Story vs labor.

**Enzymes (PSL-28).** 100 mg proprietary + lactase on a vegan tub is **speech**. Lactase is a dairy-sugar enzyme on a product that is not dairy protein. Fog + costume. Absorption sentence belongs on the **website**, and “metabolic health” is where you slow down (PSL-31). “Metabolic health” can be soft — or it can be a disease-adjacent wink. Sort it as **at least structure/function-shaped, watch for disease context**. This pack will not clean it up for the brand.

**Vegan vs facility (PSL-29, PSL-30).** No milk ingredient. Facility milk/soy/sesame. Two rows. A vegan shopper who treats facility as a stop has a stop. A vegan shopper who only refuses ingredients does not. This pack does not choose.

**Clinically studied pea (PSL-33).** Borrowed literature unless a citation on *this formula* opens. Not a %DV.

## Cost stick (PSL-42), fictional $38

Total protein ≈ 20 × 25.2 = 504 g (using computed servings).  
$38 / 504 × 25 ≈ **$1.88 / 25 g declared protein**.

## Label drill

Without the annotation, sort:

1. Why %DV is not 40%.  
2. Why 2 scoops is the whole game.  
3. Whether “vegan” fights Contains or only the facility line.  
4. The claim class of the web-only “metabolic” sentence.

Then compare.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say plant blends are inferior foods. It will not say 32% DV is a failure. It will not say facility milk makes the product whey. It will not say “metabolic health” is a disease this powder addresses — we refuse that reading as a product claim.
