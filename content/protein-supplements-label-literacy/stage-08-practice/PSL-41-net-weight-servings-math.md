# Net weight vs servings math

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

The canister has a **net quantity** (e.g. 907 g / 2 lb) and a **servings per container**. Those two numbers plus **grams per serving** should tell a single story. Rounding, “about,” and settling create slack. Literacy is doing the division **before** you believe a cost-per-serving tweet, and knowing how much slack is ordinary.

## The three-line identity

1. Net weight (g) — convert pounds: lb × 453.6 ≈ g.  
2. Serving size (g).  
3. Servings per container (declared).

**Computed servings** = net g ÷ serving g.

Compare computed to declared. Example:

- Net 907 g, serving 30 g → 30.2 servings.  
- Declared “about 30” → ordinary.  
- Declared 38 → the story broke; you are either looking at a different serving than the panel, or a marketing servings number from an old formula, or a net-contents problem.

Do the same after you **weigh a level scoop** (PSL-12). If your scoop is 36 g and the panel said 30 g, your **true** servings from a 907 g bag is about 25, not 30. Your cost math in PSL-42 must use the serving **you actually use**, and you should still know the **legal** serving.

## “About” is a word

Firms print “about 28 servings” because powders settle and because rounding is real. A 10% miss can be heaping plus humidity. A 30% miss is not humidity. Do not start a class action in a comment box from this file. Do start treating the **declared** servings as a claim you are allowed to check.

## Dual tubs and “family size”

A 5 lb bag that uses the same serving as a 2 lb bag should scale. If the family size quietly enlarged the serving, the front “25g” stayed put while calories doubled. Always reread the serving on the bag you are holding.

## Ready-to-drink cartons

Net quantity is mL or fl oz. Servings may be 1 or 2.5. The same identity applies: net ÷ serving volume ≈ servings. Drinking the carton is drinking the **declared servings**, not “one bottle = one number I liked.”

## Worked arithmetic (fictional numbers)

**FICTIONAL tub A:** Net 2.0 lb. Serving 32 g. Declared 28 servings.

- 2.0 × 453.6 = 907 g.  
- 907 ÷ 32 = 28.3.  
- Declared 28. **Match.**

**FICTIONAL tub B:** Net 5.0 lb. Serving 31 g. Declared 80 servings.

- 5 × 453.6 = 2268 g.  
- 2268 ÷ 31 = 73.2.  
- Declared 80. **Miss of ~9% the other way** — too many servings promised. Flag it. Do not invent a reason until you recheck the serving line; some bags declare 28 g servings in one place and 31 g in another.

These tubs do not exist. The **method** does.

## Label drill

On a real bag:

- Net (as printed) and net (g):  
- Serving g:  
- Declared servings:  
- Computed servings:  
- Difference:  
- Level-scoop g (or “no scale”):  
- Computed servings at your scoop:  

Circle a difference larger than two servings on a ~30-serving tub, or larger than ~10% on a big bag. That circle is the drill’s product.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say a miss means you were poisoned. It will not say a match means the protein line is honest (it could still be N × 6.25 theater). It will not tell you to eat a certain number of servings.
