# U.S. panels vs other-country panels

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

This pack is **U.S.-DSHEA literate**. A tub bought in Toronto, Berlin, or Melbourne is a **different legal object** that may share a flavor name with a U.S. SKU. Travelers and importers who read a foreign panel with 21 CFR eyes will invent %DV duties and disclaimer duties that are not there — and miss duties that are. Literacy is **naming the country of the panel** before you argue with the numbers.

This lesson is a map, not a treatise on every market.

## What usually travels with the powder, and what does not

**Travels:** protein is still a nutrient; scoops still lie; allergens still exist; nitrogen methods still exist; dessert gainers still hide behind the word protein.

**Does not travel:**

- The **50 g** adult protein DV and the PDCAAS %DV trigger (U.S. 21 CFR 101.9).  
- The **DSHEA disclaimer** pair (U.S. supplements).  
- FALCPA/FASTER’s exact nine-allergen machine (other countries have lists that overlap and differ — sesame, for example, has a different history in the EU).  
- 21 CFR 111 as the manufacturing book.  
- Proposition 65.

If you are holding a bilingual NAFTA-era leftover or a gray-market import, you may be holding **two** systems that do not agree. Believe the panel that matches the **country of sale**, and do not eat a gray-market tub because the U.S. cousin was sealed.

## European Union (shopper-level)

EU food law uses **nutrition declarations** per 100 g and per portion, reference intakes that are not the U.S. DV table, and a **list of authorized health claims** (the 432/2012 world and its updates). Protein has authorized claim language in that system about contributing to the maintenance of muscle mass and to the growth of muscle mass — **as EU-authorized wording**, not as something this pack is filing in the United States.

A U.S. brand that pastes EU claim wording onto a U.S. supplement without the DSHEA machine is not “being European.” It is being a U.S. label. Sort it with PSL-31.

EU supplements (food supplements directive) are not DSHEA. Do not look for the FDA disclaimer as proof of seriousness.

## Canada

Canada has a **Natural Health Product** pathway with product licenses (NPN) for many supplements, and a food pathway for others. An NPN on a protein product is **not** an FDA NDA. A Canadian Nutrition Facts table is not 21 CFR 101.9. Allergen and precautionary rules differ. If you mail-order across the border, you have two chairs again (PSL-04), plus customs.

## Australia / New Zealand

The Food Standards Code and, for many sports products, a **food** rather than complementary-medicine identity will show a different panel and a different claim culture. “Formulated supplementary sports food” is a category word you will not find in DSHEA. Do not translate it as “FDA dietary supplement.”

## Gray market and “same tub”

A U.S. seal (NSF Sport) on a foreign-sold bag may still be look-uppable. A U.S. Supplement Facts on a bag sold in a country that required a different panel is a **compliance smell**. This pack will not tell you to import. It will tell you: **the country of the receipt is the country of the rules.**

## Label drill

Find one non-U.S. protein panel (photo from a trip, an import site, or a bilingual bag). Write:

- Country of the panel:  
- Title of the box (Nutrition Facts / Supplement Facts / Nutrition Information / other):  
- Protein declared per 100 g and per serving (if both):  
- A %DI / %RI / %DV line (or “none”):  
- A DSHEA-style disclaimer (yes/no):  
- Allergen line style:  

Write one sentence: “If I applied PSL-13’s 50 g stick here, I would be _____.” The correct completion is often “using the wrong stick.”

## What this draft will not say

It will not say U.S. law is stricter or looser as a moral fact. It will not say EU authorized claims are disease claims — they are a **different** authorized-health-claim culture. It will not tell you to buy across a border to dodge a warning. It will not treat a foreign panel as medical advice.
