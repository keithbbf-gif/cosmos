# Worked example — fictional mass gainer

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

**FICTIONAL** brand: “Warehouse Hill Mass.” The front says PROTEIN because that is the aisle. The panel says **carbohydrate**. If you can annotate this tub, PSL-14 has landed.

## FICTIONAL principal display

**WAREHOUSE HILL MASS**  
Serious Protein · 25g · Dietary Supplement  
Cookie Crunch · 10 lb (4.54 kg)  
A muscular silhouette. “Up to 1,250 calories per serving for hard gainers.”

## FICTIONAL Supplement Facts (per 2 scoops, 333 g)

- Servings per container: 13  
- Calories: 1,250  
- Total fat: 16 g  
- Saturated fat: 4 g  
- Cholesterol: 80 mg  
- Sodium: 410 mg  
- Total carbohydrate: 252 g  
- Dietary fiber: 4 g  
- Total sugars: 28 g  
- Added sugars: 22 g  
- Protein: 25 g  
- Protein %DV: 50%

Disclaimer present.

Ingredients (abridged, fictional): Maltodextrin, whey protein concentrate, cocoa, cookie pieces (wheat flour, sugar, palm oil, cocoa, soy lecithin), medium-chain triglycerides, natural and artificial flavors, xanthan gum, sucralose, acesulfame potassium.

Contains: Milk, wheat, soy.

## Annotation walk

**Category.** Still a dietary supplement on this fictional lid, which is a **choice**. A product this calorie-dense could have been a food. The brand wanted supplement speech tools. The panel still has to tell the truth about energy.

**Serving shock (PSL-12).** 333 g is not a 30 g isolate scoop. Two **heaping** restaurant scoops. Directions that say “add 16 oz of milk” add still more energy **not** on this powder panel.

**Servings math (PSL-41).** 4540 g ÷ 333 g ≈ 13.6. Declared 13. Close enough.

**Energy shape (PSL-14).** Protein 100 kcal; carb 1,008; fat 144; sum 1,252. **Carbohydrate is the product.** Protein-by-weight 25/333 ≈ **7.5%**. The front “25g” is true and also a costume.

**%DV (PSL-13).** 25 ÷ 50 = 50%. Printed 50%. Quality looks ~1.0, consistent with whey concentrate as the protein — even while the powder is mostly maltodextrin.

**Sugars (PSL-15).** 22 g added sugars is a dessert. Cookie pieces explain wheat and some of the sugar. “Serious protein” does not appear on the added-sugars line.

**Allergens (PSL-29).** Milk **and** wheat **and** soy. A shopper who grabbed this as “just whey” gets a cookie.

**Claims.** “25g” is a true nutrient-content number **per a meal-sized serving**. “High protein” would need the RACC analysis (PSL-34); do not grant the adjective because 25 is bigger than 20. The calorie boast is an energy boast, not a disease claim. A website that said this product “fixes skinny” as a diagnosis would be a different sort — this fictional front did not go there.

**Cost (PSL-42), fictional $36 / 10 lb.**  
Total protein ≈ 25 × 13.6 = 340 g.  
$36 / 340 × 25 ≈ **$2.65 / 25 g protein** — *worse* than the fictional isolate in PSL-43 once the bag is not a fantasy $1.20 from a smaller gainer. Big bags are not automatically cheap protein. Run the numbers.

If you instead compute **cost per 100 kcal**, this product can look inexpensive. That is the honest stick **if energy was what you wanted**. This pack does not tell you to want it.

## Label drill

Cover the facts panel in your mind. From the front words alone, estimate protein-by-weight. Then uncover:

1. Your estimate vs 7.5%.  
2. Dominant energy source.  
3. Allergens a whey-only shopper missed.  
4. Whether $ / 25 g protein or $ / 100 kcal is the stick that matches **your** reason for looking.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say gainers are bad. It will not say 1,250 calories is a goal. It will not say this product treats underweight as a diagnosis. It will not say maltodextrin is a toxin.

## Carry forward

PSL-46 is the traveler lesson: a U.S. tub is not an EU tub, and “protein” on a foreign panel is a different grid.
