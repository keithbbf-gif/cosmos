# Worked example — fictional whey isolate

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

This label is **FICTIONAL**. The brand “North Parcel Isolate” does not exist. The numbers are a teaching canister so we can annotate a clean-ish isolate without prosecuting a living company. If a real tub looks similar, that is coincidence plus the fact that isolates cluster.

## FICTIONAL principal display

**NORTH PARCEL**  
Whey Protein Isolate · Dietary Supplement  
**27g PROTEIN** · Chocolate  
Net wt 2 lb (907 g)

No disease words. No joint photos. A gram claim that we will check.

## FICTIONAL Supplement Facts (per 1 scoop, 30 g)

- Servings per container: about 30  
- Calories: 110  
- Total fat: 0.5 g  
- Cholesterol: 10 mg  
- Sodium: 80 mg  
- Total carbohydrate: 1 g  
- Total sugars: 1 g (includes 0 g added sugars)  
- Protein: 27 g  
- Calcium: 120 mg  
- Potassium: 150 mg  
- **Protein %DV: 54%**

Disclaimer on the back (bold): *These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.*

Structure/function-shaped sentence on the back only: “Protein contributes to the maintenance of muscle mass.”  
(That sentence is **shown as a class of speech**, not as this pack’s claim or a notification.)

## FICTIONAL other label text

- Ingredients: Whey protein isolate, cocoa powder (processed with alkali), sunflower lecithin, natural flavors, salt, sucralose, acesulfame potassium.  
- Contains: Milk.  
- Manufactured in a facility that also processes soy and tree nuts.  
- Lot: NP-20260914-A · Best by: 2027-09-01  
- Store closed, cool, dry.  
- Distributed by North Parcel LLC, Boise, ID.  
- NSF Certified for Sport mark (fictional placement — in real life you would directory-check).

## Annotation walk (stages 1–7)

**Category (PSL-05).** Supplement Facts + “Dietary Supplement” + disclaimer = occupying the supplement path.

**Serving math (PSL-12, PSL-41).** 907 ÷ 30 = 30.2. Declared “about 30.” Match.

**Protein / %DV (PSL-13).** 27 ÷ 50 = 54%. Printed 54%. Implied PDCAAS ≈ 1.0. Consistent with a whey isolate story.

**Energy shape (PSL-14).** Protein kcal 108; carb 4; fat 4.5; sum ~117 vs 110 calories. Ordinary rounding. Protein dominates. Protein-by-weight 27/30 = 90%. Isolate territory.

**Sugars (PSL-15).** 1 g sugar, 0 added — leftover lactose and/or cocoa, not a dessert syrup. Sweetness is high-intensity (sucralose + ace-K). “No added sugar” would be a checkable claim; this fictional front did not print it.

**Leftovers (PSL-16).** Calcium and potassium look like dairy leftovers plus cocoa. Sodium 80 mg is modest. Cholesterol 10 mg is a declaration, not a morality play.

**Identity (PSL-20).** First ingredient is whey protein isolate. Front word matches. No collagen, no glycine in the list. Fit test (PSL-25): 3 g of room for cocoa, lecithin, flavors, moisture — plausible.

**Allergens (PSL-29).** Contains milk. Facility soy/tree nut is a **second row**, not an ingredient.

**Claims (PSL-31–34).** Front is nutrient-content (27 g). 27 g at PDCAAS 1 is well above 20% DV — “high/excellent” would be arithmetically easy if they printed it. The muscle-mass sentence is structure/function-shaped and **paired** with the disclaimer. No disease pile on this canister. You would still check the website (PSL-35).

**Quality (PSL-36–39).** A sport mark that must be directory-checked. Lot and best-by present. No Prop 65 on this fictional bag — that absence is not a purity medal.

## What a shopper still does not know

Whether the NSF mark is in the real directory. Whether a COA exists for NP-20260914-A. Whether the website added a disease testimonial. Whether you should eat this — **not this pack’s question.**

## What this draft will not say

It will not say you should buy an isolate. It will not say this fictional product is safe or effective. It will not say the muscle-mass sentence is authorized by this pack. It will not treat the fictional NSF mark as a real certification.
