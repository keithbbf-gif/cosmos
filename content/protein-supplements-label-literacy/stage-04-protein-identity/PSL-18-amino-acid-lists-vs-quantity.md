# Amino acid lists vs protein quantity

> **Pack:** Protein powder label literacy — staged draft.  
> **Jurisdiction:** United States (FD&C Act as amended by DSHEA, 1994).  
> **Class:** Consumer education. Not a product label. Not medical advice. Not an advertisement.  
> **Lock:** This draft does not claim that any protein powder diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.  
> **Statute (teaching):** Under DSHEA, a dietary supplement label may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

## The move

Many tubs print a chart of leucine, lysine, valine, and a dozen other names with milligrams beside them. Shoppers add the milligrams in their head and expect the sum to equal the protein grams. Sometimes it almost does. Sometimes the chart is **typical analysis**, not this lot. Sometimes the chart is a **marketing poster** for BCAAs. The amino list is a **composition story**. The protein line is a **quantity story**. They are related. They are not interchangeable.

## What an amino acid profile can honestly be

A profile can be:

1. **Typical values** for that protein type (whey isolate from a spec sheet).  
2. **Lot-specific** analysis (rare on a consumer lid; more common on a COA).  
3. **A highlight reel** — only leucine, or only “BCAAs,” printed in gym type.

(1) is useful for literacy if it is labeled “typical.” (2) is better and rarer. (3) is a claim-shaped decoration. Leucine milligrams are not a second protein line and they are not a disease claim we will make. They are a **fraction** of the protein, if the number is real.

## Why the milligrams will not add like grocery math

- Amino acids in intact protein are bound. Hydrolysis and analysis have recovery issues (tryptophan is famously annoying).  
- Some charts report **free** amino acids plus bound, or only the ones in a spike.  
- Water in the powder and rounding on the protein line create slack.  
- A “per 100 g of product” chart next to a “per serving” protein line is a unit trap.

If you add every amino acid on a complete typical whey profile, you should land **near** the protein grams, not necessarily on them. A sum that is **far below** the protein line with a complete-looking list is a unit or “typical” problem. A sum that is **far above** the protein line is a chart that is not talking about the same serving — or a chart that is fantasy.

## BCAA callouts

Leucine, isoleucine, and valine appear in whey in substantial amounts without anyone adding them. A brand can print “5.5 g BCAAs” on an isolate that simply **is** whey. That can be honest typical math. The same callout on a collagen tub (PSL-23) should make you suspicious, because collagen’s pattern is not whey’s pattern. The same callout on a product that **adds** BCAA powders should show those amino acids in the ingredient list, not only in a firework.

This pack will not say BCAAs “trigger muscle.” That sentence is a structure/function-shaped marketing object you will meet in stage 6, and it is not a disease claim we will upgrade into. Here: **callouts are not quantity of intact protein.**

## Essential vs “all 9”

Charts that shout “all 9 essential amino acids” are stating a **completeness** idea (PSL-19). Collagen charts that shout the same words need a close look at tryptophan. A list can include a dash, a “<10 mg,” or a quiet omission. Read the quiet part.

## Amino acids as separate dietary ingredients

Supplement Facts may list L-glutamine, L-glycine, or a “recovery blend” **under** the protein line, with their own milligrams. Those milligrams may be **inside** the nitrogen-based protein number, **beside** it, or double-counted in a careless design. There is no shopper-facing statute that forces a single presentation. Your move: **see whether the blend’s grams could fit in the serving after you subtract the named protein isolate/concentrate.** If they cannot fit, the panel is confused or the serving is not the serving.

2. Typical vs lot-specific language?

6. Ingredient-list free amino acids:  

If (1) is per 100 g and you compared it to a 30 g serving, redo the arithmetic before you decide the brand cannot add.

## What this draft will not say

It will not say an amino acid chart makes a powder superior. It will not prescribe leucine milligrams. It will not say “essential” means “you will get a disease without this tub.” It will not treat a missing tryptophan line as a medical diagnosis.
