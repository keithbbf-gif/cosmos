# CRL-21 — Carbohydrate and protein drinks as retention maneuvers

> **Educational landscape only.** Co-ingestion as a methods variable.
> Not a shake recipe, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to
> use any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats,
> cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Restate Green 1996 and Steenge 2000 as retention experiments.
- Treat insulin as a proposed mediator, not as a command to drink
  sugar.
- See co-ingestion as a second intervention that complicates
  interpretation.

## Green et al., 1996

Twenty-four men. 5 g creatine, four times a day, for 5 days. One arm
took creatine alone in solution. The other took creatine and, about
30 minutes later, about 93 g of simple carbohydrate in solution. Muscle
TCr rose in both arms; the carbohydrate arm's TCr rise was reported as
about 60% larger. Urinary creatine fell in that arm. Creatine alone did
not move serum insulin; creatine plus the carbohydrate drink did.

The paper's own mechanism sentence is insulin-mediated uptake. That is
a plausible transporter story (CRL-12). It is also a **huge sugar
intervention** sitting on top of the creatine intervention. Any later
task difference in a study that copies this drink is not a pure
creatine contrast.

## Steenge, Simpson, and Greenhaff, 2000

Asked whether protein plus carbohydrate could augment whole-body
creatine retention compared with carbohydrate patterns. The educational
point is not the exact gram recipe. It is that **the 1990s group
treated the vehicle as something they could titrate**, using urine and
blood as retention readouts when they were not repeating every biopsy.

## What later practical writing did with this

Handouts turned Green 1996 into "always take creatine with juice" or
"always take it with a meal." The study used a laboratory carbohydrate
load, not a sip of juice. A meal might raise insulin; it is not 93 g of
simple carbohydrate in a beaker. The direction of the idea (insulin,
uptake) can survive. The dose in the paper often does not, and need
not, if the scientific question is only "did TCr rise at all." Hultman
1996 already raised TCr without that drink.

## Design leaks this creates

- Energy surplus in one arm only.
- Different GI comfort and different blinding.
- Glycogen and water changes that can move body mass (CRL-24).
- Training-study calories that were not in the protocol's "control
  diet" paragraph.

If both arms get the same drink and only creatine vs placebo differs,
the Green maneuver is controlled. If only the creatine arm gets the
shake, you have two treatments.

## What this draft does not claim

- That a reader should combine creatine with a large carbohydrate
  load.
- That insulin is the only uptake modulator.
- That skipping the drink means a protocol "failed." Hultman's arms
  did not depend on it.
