# CRL-18 — What human trials actually put in the bottle

> **Educational landscape only.** A methods inventory. Not a shopping
> list, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to use any product.
> Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any
> disease.

## Learning aims

- Expect creatine monohydrate unless the paper works to say otherwise.
- Read dose, split, vehicle, and duration as four separate variables.
- Notice when a title says "creatine" and the capsule says a blend.

## The default solid

The biopsy-era human literature is, overwhelmingly, **creatine
monohydrate** in water, warm tea, or a flavored drink. That is the
material Harris, Hultman, Greenhaff, and Green used. Later training
studies usually inherit it. When a paper uses another salt or an ester,
it is a different methods object until it shows a pool change (CRL-39).

Monohydrate is about 88% creatine by mass. Honest methods say
"creatine monohydrate, 5 g" or "5 g creatine as the monohydrate."
Vague methods say "5 g creatine" and leave the hydrate implicit.

## The variables that are not the molecule

**Dose per day.** Classic cluster: 20 g/day for several days, or 3 g/day
for several weeks (Hultman 1996). Training studies often use a daily
amount in the 3–7 g neighborhood after an optional short high period.
Military and some laboratory cognitive-task papers have used other
totals. The number is a design choice.

**Split.** Four 5 g servings appear because of plasma kinetics and gut
comfort, not because a tribunal required it (CRL-12). Some trials use
one daily dose in the slower-accumulation designs.

**Vehicle.** Water vs a large carbohydrate drink is Green 1996. Protein
plus carbohydrate is Steenge 2000. Fruit juice appears as a kitchen
stand-in for "some sugar." Vehicle is a co-intervention (CRL-21).

**Duration.** Five to seven days for a high-intake week; four weeks for
the slow path; eight to twelve weeks when the outcome is a training
adaptation. Duration has to match the claim. A 5-day study cannot carry
a hypertrophy story except as water and maybe a first load (CRL-24,
CRL-28).

**Placebo.** Maltodextrin, glucose, cellulose, flavored drink. The
placebo has to match taste, texture, and GI tell. Poor placebos unblind
(CRL-31).

## What often fails to appear in the methods

Third-party assay of the powder. Batch number. Creatinine
contamination. Whether the capsule also contained caffeine or a
proprietary blend. Cross-title confusion ("CREATINE" on the tub,
several molecules in the scoop). A landscape reader treats an
unassayed grocery tub as a **weaker methods object** than a lab-weighed
monohydrate.

## Grams are not milligrams per kilogram

Some papers dose by grams. Some dose by g/kg or by g/kg lean mass
(Burke 2003 used lean-tissue mass). Those are not interchangeable
across body sizes. If a meta-analysis ignores the unit, the diamond
is mixing rulers.

## What this draft does not claim

- That any schedule in a methods table is a recommendation.
- That monohydrate is the only legal or the only absorbable form. It is
  the documented default.
- That a clean methods paragraph makes a product on a shelf identical
  to the study material.
