# CRL-43 — What healthy-volunteer safety papers actually measured

> **Educational landscape only.** A reading of a marker literature.
> Not a safety clearance for any person, not medical advice, and
> not a recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that
> creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- List the marks these papers usually collect (symptoms, dropouts,
  selected blood and urine numbers).
- See why serum creatinine is a tangled mark (CRL-16).
- Refuse the leap from "this sample of healthy athletes" to "you."

## What the file is

There is a set of papers, and a set of review paragraphs, that
report on healthy people — often athletes — who took monohydrate
for weeks to years and gave blood, urine, and symptom lists.
Poortmans and Francaux (1999) is a short, famous example in
athletes. Later reviews (including ISSN-style secondary papers)
collect more of the same class.

Those papers can support sentences like: **in this sample, these
marks did not move in the way the authors were watching, except
where creatinine chemistry predicts a move.** That is a real,
limited sentence.

## What the file is not

It is not a license for a person with a medical history. It is
not a pediatric clearance. It is not a pregnancy file. It is not
a drug approval. It is not a proof that a contaminated tub is
gentle. It is not a proof that a 30 g research day is a lifestyle.

GI symptoms (heaviness, loose stool) appear often enough in
loading-week notes that a methods reader expects them as a
tolerability mark, especially with large single servings of
undissolved powder. Tolerability is not the same as a chemistry
panel.

## The creatinine entanglement, one more time

A larger creatine pool yields more creatinine (Hultman 1996).
Equations that treat serum creatinine as a straightforward input
to estimated filtration will see that entanglement. Papers that
call every creatinine tick an organ event are not reading their
own metabolite. Papers that ignore a mark a clinician would care
about in a different context are not writing a medical chart
either. This series writes neither chart.

## How a careful educational sentence sounds

"Healthy-volunteer papers have reported symptom lists and selected
laboratory marks under named schedules; those papers do not
evaluate disease, do not clear individuals, and do not replace a
clinician who knows a person's history."

If a review adds clinical populations, we stop citing it for that
part (CRL-02, CRL-32).

## What this draft does not claim

- That creatine is "safe" as a one-word sticker.
- That creatine is "unsafe" as a one-word sticker.
- That any reader should start, continue, or stop a powder.
