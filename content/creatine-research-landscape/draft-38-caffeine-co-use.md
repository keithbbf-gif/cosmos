# CRL-38 — Caffeine in the same week: Vandenberghe and the mixed follow-ups

> **Educational landscape only.** A co-use controversy as methods
> history. Not a stacking guide, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Restate Vandenberghe et al. (1996) as a specific protocol, not as
  a forever ban.
- See later mixed papers as a file that did not close.
- Treat coffee, energy drinks, and pre-workouts as unmeasured
  co-interventions in many "creatine" trials.

## The paper that started the argument

Vandenberghe, Gillis, Van Leemputte, Van Hecke, Vanstapel, and
Hespel (1996, *Journal of Applied Physiology*) reported that
caffeine taken with a creatine-loading week **counteracted** the
expected ergogenic effect of the load on muscle torque, even though
muscle PCr still rose. That is a sharp, annoying result: Layer A
moved and Layer B did not in the way the creatine-only arm did.

If you cite "caffeine cancels creatine," you are citing this
neighborhood. A careful citation also names the protocol (doses,
timing, task), because later papers did not all copy it.

## Why follow-ups mixed

Caffeine is its own ergogenic file, with a different time course
(hours, not weeks) and a different habituation story. Combining:

- a week of creatine,
- a week of caffeine at a named mg/kg,
- a torque or sprint test,
- habitual coffee users vs withdrawn users,

…gives a lot of moving parts. Some later studies reported no
interference; some reported GI discomfort that could unblind or
reduce training quality; some never measured the pool. Trexler and
colleagues have reviewed the file; open the review for the table,
not for a rule.

## The practical leak in ordinary trials

Many participants drink coffee. Many "creatine" tubs sold as
pre-workouts already contain caffeine. A methods section that
says "no other supplements" and does not log caffeine is leaving
Vandenberghe's variable on the table. This is a **co-use**
problem, not a morality problem.

## How a careful paper talks

Caffeine abstention rules, habitual intake, whether the powder
was assayed for other stimulants, and whether the Vandenberghe
protocol was copied or only mentioned. "We banned coffee" is a
design choice with its own withdrawal effects.

## What this draft does not claim

- That caffeine always cancels a creatine task effect.
- That readers should combine or separate the two.
- That Vandenberghe 1996 was wrong. It was one clear protocol
  with a surprising Layer B.
