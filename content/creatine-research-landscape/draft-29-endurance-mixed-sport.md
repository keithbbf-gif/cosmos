# CRL-29 — Endurance and mixed-sport papers: where the signal thins

> **Educational landscape only.** A border of the task map. Not a
> race plan, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to use any
> product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- State why long aerobic tasks are a weak a priori test of extra PCr.
- See how extra body mass can work against a runner or climber.
- Treat team-sport simulations as mixed files.

## The prediction, said before the results

A 40-minute time trial at a sustainable power is paid mostly by
oxidative ATP and carbohydrate, not by the size of the PCr tank.
Extra TCr might still matter at surges, hills, or a final sprint, or
it might not show in the mean power. Extra kilogram of water-mass
(CRL-24) can matter in the wrong direction when the task is carrying
the body.

So the endurance file is not "creatine failed." It is **a file where
a phosphagen story has less room and a mass story has more room to
hurt.**

## What papers in this border actually do

Some measure economy or time-trial performance after a load. Some
measure lactate, which is a noisy middleman. Some add a sprint at the
end of a long ride — a more eligible tail. Some study rowers or
swimmers, where mass penalties differ from running.

Results are mixed in the way a cautious map expects: small effects,
nulls, and occasional positives tied to the anaerobic tail of the
event. A review that folds these into the same diamond as 6-second
sprints is blending neighborhoods (CRL-30).

## Mixed sport

Soccer, rugby, hockey, and basketball tests often include repeated
sprints plus a distance total. They are closer to CRL-27 than a
marathon is, and they still include running that carries any added
mass. Protocol details (how many sprints, how much jogging) decide
which neighborhood dominates.

## Heat and long tasks

Long tasks in heat add hydration and thermoregulation outcomes
(CRL-37). Those outcomes are easy to over-interpret. Keep them as
separate measurements: core temperature, sweat, and time-trial power
are not one thing.

## How to write a careful sentence

Allowed: "In this 10-km protocol, the creatine arm did not differ
from placebo on finish time; body mass rose 1.1 kg."
Not allowed in this series: "Creatine is useless for endurance
athletes" or "Creatine is required for the last lap."

## What this draft does not claim

- That no endurance protocol can ever move. Weak prediction is not
  prohibition.
- That added mass always hurts. In some seated tasks it barely
  appears.
- That team-sport players should do anything in particular.
