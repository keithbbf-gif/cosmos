# CRL-04 — Glossary for reading the creatine literature

> **Educational landscape only.** Definitions for reading papers. Not medical
> advice, not a supplement label, and not a recommendation to use any product.
> Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any
> disease.

## Learning aims

- Attach one meaning to each crowded word (creatine, creatinine,
  phosphocreatine, loading).
- Keep assay units from collapsing into each other.
- Notice which glossary terms are *protocol slang* rather than physiology.

## Compounds and pools

**Creatine (Cr).** N-methylguanidinoacetic acid. Endogenous metabolite and
food constituent. In papers, "creatine" may mean the free compound, the sum
with phosphocreatine, or the powder in a bottle. CRL-05.

**Phosphocreatine (PCr; creatine phosphate).** The phosphorylated form that
can donate a phosphoryl group to ADP through creatine kinase. Not the same
word as creatine, and not interchangeable in a results table.

**Total creatine (TCr).** Usually PCr + free Cr in a tissue. The quantity
Harris and Hultman made famous in muscle. If a paper says "creatine content"
check whether it means TCr or free Cr only.

**Creatinine.** The cyclic anhydride that forms from creatine/PCr by
non-enzymatic conversion and is excreted in urine. A **downstream signal**,
not a stored fuel. High-dose oral creatine can raise creatinine excretion
because the pool is larger. CRL-16.

**Creatine monohydrate (CrM).** Creatine crystallized with one water.
Roughly 88% creatine by mass. The form behind most biopsy-era human papers.
CRL-18, CRL-39.

**Other salts and esters.** Citrate, malate, hydrochloride, nitrate, ethyl
ester, "buffered" forms. Different papers, thinner pool evidence. CRL-39.

## Enzymes and carriers (as biochemistry, not as conditions)

**AGAT (arginine:glycine amidinotransferase).** Makes guanidinoacetate,
mainly in kidney in standard textbook accounts. CRL-06.

**GAMT (guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase).** Methylates guanidinoacetate
to creatine, mainly in liver in those same accounts. Consumes a methyl group
from S-adenosylmethionine.

**Creatine kinase (CK).** Catalyzes PCr + ADP + H+ ⇌ Cr + ATP. Cytosolic and
mitochondrial isoforms exist. Serum CK in a blood panel is a **different
use of the same enzyme name** (a leakage mark) and is not a measure of the
phosphagen system. CRL-10, CRL-13.

**SLC6A8 / CreaT.** Sodium- and chloride-dependent creatine transporter that
moves creatine across plasma membranes. This series discusses it as a
carrier protein. It does not discuss transporter-related medical conditions.
CRL-12.

## Assay language

**Dry mass vs wet mass.** Muscle metabolites are often reported per kg dry
muscle after freeze-drying. Wet-mass numbers look smaller. Do not meta-analyze
them as if they matched.

**Needle biopsy.** Usually vastus lateralis in this literature. A local
sample, not a whole-body creatine census. CRL-14.

**31P-MRS.** Phosphorus magnetic resonance spectroscopy. Can watch PCr
kinetics in a limb during and after a contraction. Different assumptions
from a biopsy. CRL-15.

**1H-MRS.** Proton MRS. Used in some brain-creatine papers to estimate total
creatine in a voxel. CRL-44.

**Loading (protocol slang).** A short, high daily intake used in many 1990s
methods (famously 20 g/day for about 6 days in Hultman et al., 1996). A
**schedule in a paper**, not a personal instruction. CRL-19.

**Maintenance (protocol slang).** A lower daily intake used after a load, or
instead of a load, to hold or creep toward a higher muscle TCr. Hultman et
al. reported 2 g/day after a load, and 3 g/day as a slower path. Again:
methods, not advice. CRL-20.

**Responder / non-responder.** Informal labels for people whose measured TCr
rose a lot or a little. Often a stand-in for baseline saturation, adherence,
or assay noise. CRL-23.

## Task language

**Phosphagen-demanding task.** Short, very hard efforts with incomplete
recovery, where PCr is a plausible bottleneck. Repeated sprints, repeated
maximal isokinetic bouts. CRL-27.

**Ergogenic.** Jargon for "the task score moved in the hoped-for direction."
It is not a legal or moral category.

**Familiarization.** Practice trials so that learning is not mistaken for a
treatment effect. Missing in more papers than it should be.

## Speech and statute

**Dietary supplement (U.S. category).** A legal marketing category under
DSHEA, not a proof of effect or of purity. CRL-42.

**Disease claim.** Speech that a product diagnoses, treats, cures, or
prevents disease. Banned in this series. CRL-02.

**Structure/function claim.** A narrower style of supplement speech about
normal structure or function. This series does not write label claims of
either type; it describes papers.

**Position stand.** A society document. Secondary literature. CRL-32.

## What this draft does not claim

- That the glossary is complete or that units are standardized across every
  journal.
- That protocol slang ("load," "maintain") is a recommendation.
- That any enzyme or transporter name implies a medical use.
