# CRL-45 — Cognitive-task and sleep-restriction designs (methods only)

> **Educational landscape only.** How thinking-task papers are
> built. Not a nootropic claim, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Treat a cognitive battery as a **task class** with its own
  leaks (practice, sleep, caffeine, belief).
- Notice when brain TCr was not measured (most of the file).
- Keep sleep restriction as a laboratory stressor, not as a
  treatment story.

## Why this file exists at all

Brain tissue runs CK and holds a creatine pool (CRL-44). It was
predictable that someone would give oral monohydrate and then
give a digit-span or a matrix test. Rae, Digney, McEwan, and
Bates (2003) is a widely cited example in vegetarians — a
sample whose muscle (and possibly brain) baseline might sit
lower (CRL-33) — using tests such as backward digit span and
Raven's matrices after weeks of 5 g/day. That paper is a
**healthy-volunteer task paper**. It is small. It is not a
license to talk about a condition.

Later papers add sleep restriction or a long waking period as a
stressor, on the idea that a stressed brain might show a
bottleneck a rested brain would not. That is a design idea. It
is not a medical indication.

## Leaks that dominate this class

- **Practice effects** on the same battery in week four.
- **Caffeine** (CRL-38) the morning of testing.
- **Blinding** (CRL-31): belief is a cognitive intervention.
- **Sleep** as both a designed stressor and an unmeasured
  confounder.
- **Unanchored Layer A:** no MRS, no muscle biopsy either.
- **Multiple outcomes** without a primary: one pretty p-value
  among twelve tests.

A positive battery after a 5-day load, without MRS, is a weak
brain-pool claim even if the psychology is clean. Dechent
needed weeks for a ~9% voxel change.

## How this series writes about it

Allowed: "Authors reported a change on a named test, in a named
sample, after a named schedule; brain creatine was / was not
measured."
Not allowed: "Creatine is a brain supplement," "use this for
exams," "use this after bad sleep," or any condition sentence.

## What this draft does not claim

- That cognitive effects are established as a general fact.
  The file is mixed and small.
- That vegetarians uniquely need a powder for thinking.
- That a reader should change sleep or study habits because of
  this page.
