# CRL-22 — Washout, crossover, and the month-long tail

> **Educational landscape only.** Trial-architecture literacy. Not
> advice to start or stop any powder. Not medical advice. Does not
> claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any
> disease.

## Learning aims

- Treat washout as a measured decline of a pool, not as a weekend off.
- Spot crossovers that are still contaminated.
- Know what a parallel-group design is buying when washout is long.

## The empirical tail

Hultman et al. (1996) reported that after a 6-day high-intake period,
muscle TCr declined toward baseline across weeks, and at about 30 days
without a hold dose it was no longer different from the starting
value. That is the best-known human washout clock in this file. It
matches a slow creatinine leak from a large tank (CRL-17). It does not
match a drug with a 6-hour half-life.

Any crossover that switches powder to placebo at day 8 and tests again
at day 15 is asking the second arm to work on a muscle that may still
be carrying the first arm.

## Why authors still crossover

Sample size. Each person is their own control. Familiarization can be
shared. Those are real pressures. The honest versions either:

- wash out for a month or more and say so,
- use a very long lead-in and randomize order with a test for period
  effects,
- or admit they are not studying a clean on/off of the pool.

The dishonest version is a 7-day washout and a confident paired
t-test.

## Carry-over is not only TCr

Body mass and muscle water can still be up (CRL-24). A participant who
felt heavier or crampier may be unblinded for the second arm. Training
state changes across a semester. Menstrual phase, if unrecorded, is
another period effect (CRL-34). Crossover is a bundle of clocks.

## Parallel groups are not automatically cleaner

They avoid carry-over of the pool. They reintroduce between-person
baseline TCr, diet, and training noise. That is why randomization and
a baseline biopsy (rare) or at least a diet/training log (common,
often thin) matter. A small parallel study can be cleaner than a
contaminated crossover and still be too small.

## Reading the methods sentence

Reward papers that:

- name the washout in days,
- cite Hultman 1996 or an equivalent measurement when they claim the
  pool is back,
- test order effects,
- do not call a design "double-blind crossover" when the washout is a
  week and the outcome is a muscle-dependent task.

## What this draft does not claim

- That 30 days is a universal clean slate. It is one paper's group
  statement.
- That crossover is always the wrong design.
- That stopping a trial powder has a medical withdrawal meaning here.
  The described phenomenon is a declining metabolite pool.
