# CRL-26 — A map of the exercise literature by task, not by slogan

> **Educational landscape only.** A geography of outcomes. Not a
> training program, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to
> use any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats,
> cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Sort exercise papers by the metabolic shape of the task.
- Predict, before opening the results, where a phosphagen story is
  even eligible.
- Resist one-diamond summaries of unlike tasks.

## The eligible neighborhood

If the mechanism cartoon is extra PCr buffering short, severe ATP
turnover (CRL-10), then the tasks that could show a difference are
tasks that:

- last seconds rather than tens of minutes per effort,
- repeat, with recovery that is incomplete enough for PCr to still
  be rebuilding,
- are measured as work, torque, power, or repetitions — not as a
  feeling.

Greenhaff 1993 (repeated maximal isokinetic bouts) sits in the
center of that neighborhood. Repeated-sprint protocols sit next to
it (CRL-27). Heavy resistance training across weeks sits beside it
for a different reason: each set is a short severe effort, and extra
work per set might accumulate as a training stimulus (CRL-28).

## The thin neighborhood

Long steady endurance, where oxidative phosphorylation and glycogen
are the headlines, is a poor a priori home for a PCr-reservoir story.
Papers exist anyway. Some find small changes, some find none, some
find a mass penalty on hills. CRL-29 is that border.

Team-sport simulations mix the two neighborhoods in one protocol.
They are realistic and hard to interpret.

## What "the literature shows" usually hides

A review that says "creatine improves exercise performance" has
averaged:

- a 6-second cycle sprint,
- a 1RM bench after 8 weeks,
- a 10-km time trial,
- a laboratory jump,
- sometimes a handgrip.

Those are not one performance. Branch (2003) and later meta-analyses
are useful when they **stratify** and less useful when they do not
(CRL-30).

## Sample, not just task

The same sprint protocol in untrained students, in already-loaded
omnivorous sprinters, and in people coming from a low-creatine diet
is three experiments (CRL-33, CRL-36). A map that ignores who was
enrolled is a poster, not a landscape.

## How to place a new paper in five minutes

1. Name the task duration and rest.
2. Name the outcome unit (watts, kg, seconds, reps).
3. Name whether TCr was measured.
4. Name n, sex, diet, training status.
5. Only then read the adjective in the abstract.

If you cannot finish (1)–(2), the paper is not yet on the map.

## What this draft does not claim

- That creatine "works" or "does not work" as a global verb.
- That endurance papers are all failed experiments. They are tests
  of a weaker prediction.
- That laboratory tasks equal sport results on a Saturday.
