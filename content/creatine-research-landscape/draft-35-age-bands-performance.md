# CRL-35 — Age bands in performance research

> **Educational landscape only.** Age as a sampling axis. Not a
> healthy-aging program, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- See "college sample" as an age band, not as "humans."
- Read older-adult lifting trials as **performance and function
  assays in a band**, not as a condition file.
- Keep adolescent research in the ethics-and-consent box, not in a
  product box.

## Young adults are the default animal

The 1990s cluster and most sprint papers are men in early adulthood.
Washout clocks, percent TCr rises, and GI notes from those papers
are not automatically the numbers for a 75-year-old or a 15-year-old.

## Older adults as a performance band

There is a file of resistance-training trials in adults often
labeled 50–80+ years that measure 1RM, chair-stands, grip, or lean
mass. Those are **the same outcome classes as CRL-28**, in a
different band. This series reads them that way.

What this series will not do: name age-related medical conditions,
discuss "treating" them, or treat a chair-stand as a clinical
endpoint. A chair-stand is a task. If a paper's title leans on a
condition word, we still only borrow the **task and the sample
age**, or we do not borrow the paper.

Baseline TCr may differ by age in some reports; treat that as a
modifier hypothesis (room to rise) rather than as a diagnosis of
the band.

## Adolescents and children

Consent, assent, unnecessary invasive assays, and the fact that
this series does not give product advice all point the same way:
we note that some sport organizations and position stands have
discussed young athletes, and we do **not** turn that discussion
into a recommendation. Biopsy in minors is a high bar. Most youth
papers, when they exist, are task-only and small.

## How a careful paper talks

Mean age and range, not "adults." Separate older-adult trials from
college trials in a meta-analysis (CRL-30). No slide that says
"creatine reverses aging." Aging as a word is a time axis, not a
target in this folder.

## What this draft does not claim

- That older adults must or must not use a powder.
- That a function test is a medical treatment.
- That college-male percentages apply at every age.
