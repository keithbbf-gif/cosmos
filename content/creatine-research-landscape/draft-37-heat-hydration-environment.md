# CRL-37 — Heat, hydration, and environmental study designs

> **Educational landscape only.** Environment as a design factor.
> Not a heat-acclimation plan, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- See why water stored with extra TCr shows up in heat protocols.
- Separate core temperature, sweat rate, and task performance as
  three outcomes.
- Avoid folklore that turns a laboratory chamber into a warning
  label or a sales pitch.

## Why environment entered the file

Creatine loading often raises body mass and total body water
(CRL-24). Heat-and-exercise laboratories already care about water.
It was inevitable that someone would put a loaded volunteer in a
chamber and measure temperature and sweat.

Those papers are **environment × pool** designs. They are not,
in this series, about heat illness as a condition to treat or
prevent. We read them as: what marks moved, in what chamber, after
what schedule.

## Three outcomes that get flattened

**Core or skin temperature.** A thermal mark.

**Sweat rate or plasma volume estimates.** A water mark.

**Time-trial power or repetitions in the heat.** A task mark, now
with a mass penalty and a thermal load on top of any phosphagen
story.

A paper can move one and not the others. Abstracts that say
"hydration" without saying which mark are doing folklore.

## Folklore that grew here

One folk pole: extra water is protective in the heat. Other folk
pole: extra water is dangerous in the heat. Both poles are
**medical-adjacent** if they start talking about emergencies. This
series will not walk either pole. It will say: chamber papers exist;
they are small; they do not agree in every mark; they do not write
a field policy from here.

## Design leaks specific to chambers

Clothing, fan speed, drinking rules, and whether the protocol
allowed ad libitum water can dominate a creatine contrast. So can
the extra kilogram on a treadmill. Familiarization in the chamber
is as important as familiarization on a sprint ergometer.

## How a careful paper talks

Dry-bulb and wet-bulb, clothing, drinking schedule, mass change,
and the primary outcome named in advance. No subtitle about
"protecting athletes from [an emergency]."

## What this draft does not claim

- That creatine is a heat aid or a heat risk as a general statement.
- That a reader should change summer practice because of this page.
- That sweat rate is a proxy for muscle TCr.
