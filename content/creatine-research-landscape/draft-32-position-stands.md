# CRL-32 — Position stands as secondary literature

> **Educational landscape only.** How to read a society document.
> Not an endorsement of a society, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Place ISSN 2007 and ISSN 2017 on the map as **position stands**,
  not as experiments.
- Separate the stand's exercise sentences from any clinical tour
  this series will not follow.
- Use a stand as a bibliography generator, then go to the primaries.

## What a position stand is

A group of authors, under a society's name, reads a literature and
states numbered positions. The 2017 International Society of Sports
Nutrition paper (Kreider, Kalman, Antonio, Ziegenfuss, Wildman,
Collins, Candow, Kleiner, Almada, Lopez) is the document most
people mean when they say "ISSN on creatine." There was a 2007
stand (Buford et al.) before it. Other groups have written shorter
statements.

A stand can be careful, dated, and useful. It is still **Layer D
adjacent** in the CRL-03 sense: it is a community's willingness to
speak, not a new biopsy.

## How this series uses ISSN 2017

Allowed uses:

- As a pointer to common research doses and to the claim that
  monohydrate is the best-documented form in sport papers.
- As a dated snapshot of what a sport-nutrition society was
  prepared to assert about exercise and training outcomes.
- As a door into the reference list.

Disallowed uses in this folder:

- As a medical guideline.
- As a substitute for Harris, Hultman, Greenhaff, Green, Branch, or
  Lanhers.
- As a license to repeat any clinical or injury-indication language
  the stand may contain. Those sections exist in the document. They
  are out of scope here (CRL-02).

If you need to say "a 2017 society stand reviewed exercise
literature and stated positions," say that. Do not say "ISSN proved."

## Other secondary objects

Antonio et al. (2021) and the later "Part II" misconceptions papers
are narrative Q&A reviews. They are helpful for listing folklore
(water, creatinine, hair, and so on) and for pointing to studies.
They are still secondary. They also wander toward aims this series
will not adopt. Take the folklore list; leave the indication list.

## Authorship and industry as a reading condition

Sport-nutrition stands often include authors with product, patent,
or consulting ties. That does not shred the citations. It is a wind
direction (CRL-51). Read the conflicts paragraph the way you would
in any journal.

## What this draft does not claim

- That societies should not write stands.
- That 2017 is the last word. Literature continues.
- That a reader should follow any numbered position as personal
  advice.
