# CRL-33 — Habitual diet as a baseline machine

> **Educational landscape only.** Diet as a design variable. Not a
> diet prescription, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to
> use any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats,
> cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Connect low dietary creatine to a lower starting muscle store in
  named papers.
- See why a mixed-diet trial can hide a larger delta that would
  appear in a low-meat arm.
- Keep "vegetarian" as a diet descriptor, not as a moral or medical
  category.

## The compositional fact, restated

Plant foods do not supply meaningful creatine. Animal muscle foods
do (CRL-07). A habitual pattern that excludes those foods therefore
asks endogenous synthesis to cover almost the whole leak (CRL-06,
CRL-17). Several biopsy papers report lower muscle TCr in vegetarian
cohorts — reviews often sketch 90–110 mmol/kg dry versus a mixed-
diet average nearer 120.

Watt, Garnham, and Snow (2004) measured TCr and transporter gene
expression before and after monohydrate in vegetarians and
non-vegetarians. Burke et al. (2003) combined creatine or placebo
with resistance training in vegetarian and omnivorous recreational
lifters and reported larger muscle PCr/TCr changes in the
vegetarian creatine arm, along with training-outcome differences
that belong to that paper's table, not to a slogan.

## Why this is a modifier, not a morality play

A lower baseline is extra room under the ceiling (CRL-23). It is
not a diagnosis. It is not a requirement to use a powder. It is not
a claim that a plant-forward diet is inadequate as a diet. It is a
**starting-store difference** that a methods section can record or
ignore.

Trials that enroll only meat-eating varsity athletes and then
announce a small group effect have sampled the harder end of the
room-to-rise spectrum. Trials that do not record diet at all have
left a modifier on the floor.

## What "vegetarian" hides

Lacto-ovo vs vegan, duration of the pattern, occasional fish, and
prior powder use all move the baseline. Burke et al. had to define
the pattern (they reported years vegetarian and mixed lacto-ovo /
vegan). Copy the definition when you cite them.

## How a careful paper talks

"Diet was logged; n in each pattern is reported; baseline TCr is
shown by diet." That is the gold sentence. "May work better in
vegetarians" without a measurement is a poster.

## What this draft does not claim

- That people on low-animal diets should take creatine.
- That omnivores cannot raise TCr. They can; the mean room is
  smaller.
- That a diet pattern is a medical condition.
