# CRL-34 — Sex as a design variable and the evidence gap

> **Educational landscape only.** Who was enrolled. Not a sex-
> specific program, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to
> use any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats,
> cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Notice how much of the biopsy cluster is men.
- Treat lean mass, diet, and cycle phase as plausible modifiers
  without inventing a second physiology.
- Reward papers that report sex-disaggregated outcomes.

## The sampling fact

Harris 1992, Hultman 1996, Green 1996, and much of the early task
file enrolled men. That is a **who-was-measured** fact. It does not
mean women lack a creatine pool or a CK reaction. It means the
load-bearing percentages (the 20% TCr rise, the 30-day washout, the
60% extra retention with a huge carbohydrate drink) are
**male-sample numbers** until someone repeats the assay.

Later training studies and some meta-analyses include women, often
as a minority in a mixed group, often without a disaggregated
table. Mixed-group diamonds can hide interaction.

## What could differ without needing a myth

- **Muscle mass and the size of the tank** (CRL-17), which changes
  grams leaked per day and maybe the grams needed to hold a rise.
- **Habitual diet** in a given cohort, which changes baseline TCr
  (CRL-33).
- **Menstrual-cycle phase** as a period effect in crossovers
  (CRL-22), mostly unrecorded.
- **Task selection** (some protocols use loads or ergometers that
  were normalized poorly).

None of those items requires a claim that creatine is "for men."
They require a claim that **the file is uneven**.

## What this series will not do

It will not turn estrogen or progesterone into a mechanistic novel.
It will not discuss pregnancy, which some reviews mention; that
topic sits on the other side of the claim fence (CRL-02). It will
not offer a women's dose.

## How a careful paper talks

n by sex. Primary outcome by sex, or a stated reason why not
(power). Cycle phase or contraception noted if the design is a
short crossover. Diet noted. No subtitle that says "finally, the
truth about women and creatine" unless the assay is new.

## What this draft does not claim

- That women do not raise muscle TCr. Some papers show they do;
  the cluster is still thinner.
- That sex is the most important modifier. Diet and training
  status compete.
- That anyone should take or avoid a powder because of sex.

## Sources

- Hultman E et al. *J Appl Physiol.* 1996;81(1):232-237.
- Harris RC, Söderlund K, Hultman E. *Clin Sci (Lond).* 1992;83(3):367-374.
- Smith-Ryan AE et al. appear in later narrative reviews on women
  and sport nutrition; when you want a **primary**, prefer training
  trials that actually enrolled and reported women rather than a
  review's subtitle. Lanhers et al. 2015/2017 inclusion tables are
  a place to see the mix.
