# CRL-17 — Turnover: a pool that leaks creatinine

> **Educational landscape only.** Mass-balance vocabulary. Not medical
> advice, not a supplement label, and not a recommendation to use any
> product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- See the body creatine pool as a tank with a slow leak and two inlets.
- Treat 1–2% per day as a review-level leak rate, not as your
  measurement.
- Connect washout times to that leak rather than to magic.

## The tank cartoon, used carefully

Call the tank **whole-body creatine + phosphocreatine**. Most of it is
in skeletal muscle (CRL-11). Each day a small fraction converts to
creatinine and leaves in urine. Reviews often place that fraction near
**1–2% of the pool per day**. For many adult bodies the replacement
need then lands near **1–3 g/day**, met by endogenous synthesis plus
food (CRL-06, CRL-07).

Inlets: AGAT/GAMT synthesis, dietary creatine, and — in a trial —
oral monohydrate. Outlets: creatinine, and, when intake is high,
urinary creatine itself (spill).

The cartoon explains three otherwise mysterious methods facts:

1. **A short high intake can fill a tank that only leaks a couple of
   percent a day.** You are pouring faster than the leak.
2. **A low daily intake can fill the same tank more slowly** (Hultman's
   3 g × 28 day arm).
3. **When the extra inlet stops, the tank takes weeks, not hours, to
   look like the old tank.** Hultman et al. (1996) saw about a month.
   That is washout (CRL-22).

## Why the leak is chemical

The creatine-to-creatinine conversion is largely non-enzymatic. Warmer,
more acidic conditions run it faster in a flask (CRL-40). In a person,
the leak is a property of the pool's size and environment, which is why
a larger pool yields more urinary creatinine (Hultman 1996). That is
mass balance, not a morality play about organs.

## Whole-body grams vs muscle millimoles

Muscle papers speak mmol/kg dry mass. Whole-body sketches speak grams.
Converting between them requires a muscle-mass assumption and a wet-dry
assumption. Two reviews can disagree by tens of grams and both be
doing arithmetic, not discovering a new organ. When a popular article
says "the body holds 120 grams of creatine," treat it as a **rounded
model**, then go back to the biopsy unit the methods used.

## What feeding does to the inlets

Oral monohydrate is a temporary extra inlet. Endogenous synthesis may
down-regulate (AGAT brake; CRL-06). Food intake may stay the same.
After the extra inlet stops, synthesis and food are asked to cover the
leak of a tank that may still be larger than baseline for weeks. That
is another reason washout is long.

## What this draft does not claim

- That any reader should size an inlet to their tank.
- That leak rates are known per kilogram for every age and sex.
- That creatinine output is a fitness test.
