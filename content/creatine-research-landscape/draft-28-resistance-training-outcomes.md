# CRL-28 — Resistance-training studies: strength and mass as outcomes

> **Educational landscape only.** How lifting trials are built and
> read. Not a hypertrophy program, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Separate week-one water from week-eight training outcomes.
- See extra repetitions as a possible mediator, not as magic mass.
- Read 1RM, repetitions-to-failure, and imaging as different claims.

## Two stacked experiments

A resistance-training creatine paper is usually two experiments glued
together.

**Experiment 1 — the pool.** Did muscle TCr rise? Often unmeasured.

**Experiment 2 — the training.** Did strength or mass outcomes differ
after weeks of lifting *while* one arm had a higher pool (or at least
a different powder)?

If Experiment 2 is positive, authors often say the powder "increased
hypertrophy." A more careful sentence is: **the powder arm trained in
a different internal environment and sometimes did more work, and the
chosen mass/strength assay moved.** Mediation through extra training
volume is a hypothesis that only some papers can test (they have to
log successful repetitions).

## Outcomes that are not interchangeable

**1RM or isometric force.** A neural and a hypertrophic mixture,
especially in novices. Familiarization matters.

**Repetitions at a fixed load.** Closer to the repeated-high-intensity
file (CRL-27). Sensitive to the last-set phosphagen story.

**Scale mass and DXA lean mass.** Water-entangled (CRL-24).

**Fiber cross-section or MRI muscle volume.** Harder, rarer, more
convincing when present, still not a whole-body moral.

Branch (2003) and the Lanhers meta-analyses (2015, 2017) try to pool
strength-shaped outcomes. Read their inclusion rules before quoting
the diamond (CRL-30).

## Time: the variable people skip in conversation

A 5-day loading study can move mass and maybe a repetition test. It
cannot carry an 8-week hypertrophy paragraph. An 8–12 week training
study can carry that paragraph *as an outcome difference*, still
without proving the tissue composition of the difference.

## Who was already lifting

Untrained arms have large 1RM learning. Highly trained arms have
small margins and often higher baseline TCr from diet and prior
self-use (CRL-36). A positive novice study and a null elite study
can both be true.

## What this draft does not claim

- That creatine "builds muscle" as a slogan. The careful claim is
  about **outcomes in named protocols**.
- That extra lean mass on DXA is contractile protein.
- That any reader should change a lifting plan because of this page.
