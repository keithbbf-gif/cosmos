# CRL-20 — Maintenance doses as a design choice, not a lifestyle

> **Educational landscape only.** How trials hold a raised pool. Not a
> lifestyle program, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to use
> any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- See "maintenance" as a second phase in a methods table.
- Connect the size of a hold dose to the leak rate of the pool.
- Notice when a training study changes the dose in week two without
  saying why.

## The word is protocol slang

After a short high-intake week, many papers switch to a smaller daily
amount "to maintain" elevated muscle creatine. Hultman et al. (1996)
used 2 g/day for 30 days after 20 g/day for 6 days and reported that
the elevation held. Later sport-nutrition writing often quotes a hold
in the 3–5 g/day neighborhood. Those are **inherited design numbers**.
They are not a finding about what a named person requires, and they
are not a finding that the hold must follow a load (the slow path had
no load).

## Why a hold dose has a theoretical size

If the tank leaks about 1–2% per day (CRL-17), a larger tank leaks more
grams. A hold dose is an extra inlet meant to match the extra leak plus
whatever spill and down-regulated synthesis do. That is back-of-the-
envelope physiology. It explains why authors did not drop to zero after
a load if they wanted the pool high in week four of a training study.
It does not compute your scoop.

Some reviews say larger, heavily muscled athletes may have been given
higher daily amounts in practice. That sentence is about **sample and
habit**, and it is one of the easier places for a review to slide from
description into a prescription. This series stays with description.

## Design patterns to recognize

**Load then hold.** Common in studies that want a high pool before the
first training test.

**Hold only (slow path).** Common when the authors have weeks and do
not want a high-intake week, or when they are copying Hultman's 3 g
arm without saying so.

**Load then nothing.** A short study that only needed the Saturday
test. Fine if the claim is short. Misleading if the discussion talks
about month-long adaptations.

**Changing the dose when GI complaints appear.** Sometimes reported,
often not. Adherence leak (CRL-23).

## What a hold does not guarantee

It does not guarantee that TCr stayed high unless someone measured it.
It does not guarantee that body mass stayed put (CRL-24). It does not
guarantee blinding (daily drinks can still taste like the thing). It
does not turn a 6-day paper into a 12-week paper.

## What this draft does not claim

- That a hold dose is required for any reader.
- That 2, 3, or 5 g is "the" human dose.
- That stopping a trial powder is dangerous. Washout is a decline, not
  a withdrawal syndrome in this literature.
