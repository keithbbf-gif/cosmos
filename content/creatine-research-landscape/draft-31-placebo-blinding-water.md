# CRL-31 — Placebo, blinding, and the water-weight tell

> **Educational landscape only.** Bias and sensory leakage. Not a
> how-to for running a secret trial, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- List the ways a creatine arm unblinds itself.
- See why subjective and self-paced outcomes suffer first.
- Treat a well-matched placebo as part of the science, not as
  decoration.

## The powder has a personality

Creatine monohydrate is poorly soluble in cold water, can grit, and
at high split doses can announce itself in the gut. A placebo that is
fully dissolved glucose in a clear bottle is not a match. A placebo
that matches bitterness, grit, and volume is harder and rarer.

Participants also own scales. A 1 kg rise in a week is a clue
(CRL-24). So is a rumor in a locker room. "Double-blind" in a
methods line is a hope until the authors test whether people could
name their arm.

## Which outcomes care

A biopsy TCr is relatively indifferent to belief. A maximal 6-second
sprint on a braked ergometer is less indifferent (arousal still
exists) but better than a self-paced time trial or a wellness
questionnaire. Cognitive-task batteries (CRL-45) sit toward the
belief-sensitive end. Occupational papers that use effort ratings
sit there too (CRL-46).

If the headline outcome is a feeling, demand a better blind.

## Water as both confounder and tell

The same water that inflates lean-mass graphs tells the participant
something happened. You cannot wish one away and keep the other.
Designs that measure mass but never ask "which arm do you think you
were in?" leave a leak unmeasured.

## Placebo choice is a scientific choice

Maltodextrin adds carbohydrate. That can matter in a training study
if the amounts are large. Cellulose adds no energy and may not match
mouthfeel. Flavored commercial placebos may contain caffeine or
other surprises if the authors bought a tub instead of compounding.
Assay the placebo too (CRL-41).

## What this draft does not claim

- That every positive creatine paper is an unblinded placebo effect.
  Pool changes are chemical.
- That blinding is impossible. It is expensive.
- That a reader should try to guess their own powder. This is not
  that kind of page.
