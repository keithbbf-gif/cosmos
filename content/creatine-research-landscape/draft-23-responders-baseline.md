# CRL-23 — Responders, non-responders, and the starting store

> **Educational landscape only.** A warning about a popular label.
> Not a test you should take, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Treat "non-responder" as a description of a measured change, not as
  a type of person.
- Put baseline TCr at the front of the explanation list.
- Keep adherence, assay noise, and diet on that list.

## Harris already said the important sentence

Harris, Söderlund, and Hultman (1992) reported that the rise in muscle
creatine was greatest in people whose starting total creatine was
lower. That is a ceiling-and-room story (CRL-11). It is the least
mystical account of why two volunteers on the same schedule do not
show the same delta.

Reviews later quote mixed-diet muscle TCr near 120 mmol/kg dry and an
upper neighborhood near 160, and vegetarian cohorts nearer 90–110
(CRL-07, CRL-33). A person who starts at 150 has little room. A person
who starts at 100 has a lot. Both can "take the powder." Only one
looks like a "responder" on a biopsy.

## The label is usually applied without the biopsy

Popular talk calls someone a non-responder because a lift or a sprint
did not move. That skips Layer A. Possible mundane reasons:

- The pool was already high.
- The powder was not swallowed as written (travel week, GI skip).
- The task was not phosphagen-limited (CRL-26).
- The study was small and the person's noise ate the signal.
- Body mass rose and the task was weight-bearing in an unhelpful way
  (CRL-24).
- The "creatine" was a blend with little monohydrate (CRL-39, CRL-41).

Greenhaff and later writers sometimes used a threshold (for example,
a TCr rise of a certain mmol/kg) to split responders for a secondary
analysis. That is a **post-hoc slice**. It can be honest if labeled.
It becomes folklore when the threshold leaves the paper and becomes a
personality type.

## Diet, sex, and training as baseline machines

Low dietary creatine is a way to start lower (Watt 2004; Burke 2003).
High meat plus prior self-supplementation is a way to start higher.
Sex-related differences in lean mass and in who gets enrolled change
the baseline distribution (CRL-34). None of that requires a mysterious
responder gene, though transporter abundance could still differ
(CRL-12) and is rarely measured.

## How a careful paper talks

"Change in TCr ranged from X to Y; people with lower baseline tended
to rise more." That sentence is enough. "12% were non-responders"
without a definition and without a baseline is not enough.

## What this draft does not claim

- That everyone with a low-meat diet will show a large rise.
- That a genetic test of "creatine response" exists as a validated
  consumer object in this literature.
- That a small task change means the person should do anything in
  particular. This folder does not give that advice.
