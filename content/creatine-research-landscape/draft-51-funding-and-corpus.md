# CRL-51 — Funding, affiliation, and how the corpus gets its shape

> **Educational landscape only.** Sociology of a literature, not
> a conspiracy story. Not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Treat conflicts paragraphs as data, not as a shredder.
- See which experiments are cheap to repeat and therefore
  over-represented.
- Separate a society stand's authorship from a 1992 biopsy.

## Wind, not weather control

Many modern creatine authors have consulted for, owned, or
formulated powders. Many have not. Industry can fund a clean
biopsy or a sloppy sprint. Public funders can do the same.
The reading move is: **mark the wind, then still do the
worksheet** (CRL-03).

A conflicts sentence that is missing is itself a mark.

## Why the corpus looks like college sprints

Cheap, fast, publishable: young men, 5–7 days, a cycle
ergometer, no biopsy, a commercial tub. Expensive, slow,
rarer: sex-balanced biopsy, long washout, assayed material,
registered primary, diet strata (CRL-47). The literature's
center of mass is not a finding about the molecule. It is a
finding about **cost**.

Position stands then summarize the cheap center with
confidence and mention the expensive corners as "more
research is needed," which is true and also convenient.

## Duplicate publication and the friendly outcome

The same cohort can appear as a performance paper and a
body-composition paper. Meta-analysts who miss the overlap
double-count (CRL-30). Outcomes that look pretty get a
second conference abstract. This is ordinary human-literature
behavior, not unique to creatine. It is louder here because
the consumer market is loud.

## How a careful paper talks

Funder, in-kind powder, author equity, and a data-availability
sentence. Pre-registration. A primary outcome that was not
chosen after twelve looks. None of that guarantees truth.
All of it is better than a highlight reel.

## What this draft does not claim

- That industry-adjacent authors cannot measure TCr honestly.
  Harris-era work predates the current market shape.
- That public-university papers are pure.
- That a reader should discard the file. They should read it
  with the cost structure in view.
