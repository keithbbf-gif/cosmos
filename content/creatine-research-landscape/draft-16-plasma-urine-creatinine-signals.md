# CRL-16 — Plasma creatine, urine creatine, and creatinine as research signals

> **Educational landscape only.** Assay literacy. Not a guide to reading
> anyone's lab printout, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to
> use any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures,
> or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Separate plasma creatine, urinary creatine, and creatinine.
- See why a rise in creatinine after a feeding protocol can be a
  **pool-and-breakdown** signal.
- Refuse the habit of treating a research blood mark as a diagnosis.

## Three different sentences

**Plasma creatine** rises after an oral dose and then falls as the
compound is taken up, excreted, or converted. Harris et al. (1992)
reported large plasma increases after 5 g. That curve is evidence that
the dose left the gut in that experiment. It is not a muscle biopsy.

**Urinary creatine** rises when intake exceeds uptake. Green et al.
(1996) used lower urinary creatine in the carbohydrate arm as a
converging sign that more of the dose had been retained. Urinary
creatine is a leftover-and-spill signal.

**Creatinine** is the breakdown product. A larger creatine/PCr pool
produces more creatinine over days. Hultman et al. (1996) reported
higher urinary creatinine while the muscle pool was held high. Serum
creatinine, in some feeding papers, also ticks up. That tick is
**entangled** with how clinicians often use serum creatinine as an
input to estimated filtration equations. Entanglement is a methods
warning. This series does not interpret any person's kidney status.

## Why folklore grows here

A volunteer starts a loading week. A blood panel drawn for another
reason shows a higher creatinine. Someone says a sentence about organs.
A landscape reader says: **the assay is downstream of the pool you just
enlarged, and the equation you are about to use was not built for this
maneuver.** Poortmans and Francaux (1999) and later safety reviews
exist because this confusion is old. CRL-43 stays with what healthy-
volunteer papers measured. None of those papers is a clearance for a
reader with a medical history.

## What these signals cannot do

- They cannot replace TCr in muscle.
- They cannot tell you a task time.
- They cannot certify a powder's identity (a spiked urine could be many
  things).
- They cannot, in this folder, become a diagnosis.

They *can* support adherence ("plasma rose, so something was
absorbed"), support retention comparisons (urine creatine down), and
explain why a chemistry panel moved during a protocol.

## Reporting habits to reward

Papers that show the time of the blood draw relative to the last dose.
Papers that do not call creatinine "kidney function" in the abstract
when they mean "creatinine concentration." Papers that keep estimated
filtration equations out of the highlight reel, or that discuss the
entanglement explicitly.

## What this draft does not claim

- That creatinine is meaningless. It is a real analyte.
- That feeding is "bad for filtration" or "good for filtration." Those
  are medical claims and are out of scope.
- That a reader should start or stop a powder based on a number in this
  draft. There is no such number here.
