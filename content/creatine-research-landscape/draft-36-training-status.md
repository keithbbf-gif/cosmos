# CRL-36 — Training status and sport type as moderators

> **Educational landscape only.** Who already trained the test. Not
> a periodization plan, not medical advice, and not a recommendation
> to use any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats,
> cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Separate novice learning from a powder effect.
- See why already-loaded, already-trained sprinters are a hard test.
- Keep sport type tied to task class (CRL-26), not to a brand.

## Three kinds of "trained"

**Skill-trained.** The person has practiced the test. Familiarization
is short. Learning is a smaller leak.

**Strength- or sprint-trained.** The person already does the
phosphagen-shaped work. Margins are small. Baseline TCr may be
higher from diet and from prior self-administration that the intake
log missed.

**Endurance-trained.** The person is adapted to long oxidative work.
The eligible neighborhood for extra PCr is the surge, not the long
mean (CRL-29).

A paper that says "athletes" without saying which of the three is
using a poster word.

## Why novices inflate 1RM stories

Untrained 1RM rises because of skill and because of the program.
Both arms of a training study can rise a lot. The between-arm
difference is the creatine contrast. If the paper reports only
within-arm change and a p-value against baseline, it has not yet
done the contrast (CRL-28).

## Why elites disappoint slogans

If Harris 1992 is right that high baseline TCr leaves little room,
and if elites eat muscle foods and sometimes already use the powder,
a new tub in a study may be a small chemical event. A null elite
sprint paper can be a ceiling story rather than a refutation of
Greenhaff 1993 in students.

## Sport type is task type plus culture

Weightlifting and track sprint sit near CRL-27/28. Road cycling sits
near CRL-29 except for the finish. CrossFit-like tests mix
everything and add bodyweight movements that punish extra mass.
Culture matters for blinding (locker-room tells) and for
contamination of the "placebo" arm with leftover powder at home.

## How a careful paper talks

Training years, a performance qualifier, a diet and supplement
history, and a familiarization count. "Recreationally active" is
defined in hours per week or it is fog.

## What this draft does not claim

- That trained people "don't respond." Some do; the mean room may
  shrink.
- That novices should be given a powder to speed learning. Learning
  is not a creatine claim.
- That a sport should add or ban the compound because of this page.
