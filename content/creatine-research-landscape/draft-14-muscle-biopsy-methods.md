# CRL-14 — How researchers measure muscle creatine: the needle biopsy

> **Educational landscape only.** An assay chapter. Not a procedure
> guide, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to use any
> product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Know what a vastus lateralis needle biopsy can and cannot say.
- Keep dry-mass units, extraction, and fiber mix in view.
- Treat biopsy as the load-bearing assay of the 1990s cluster, with a
  cost.

## Why this assay still sits at the center

Harris 1992 and Hultman 1996 are believed because someone froze a piece
of human muscle and measured creatine and phosphocreatine in it. Blood
creatine is not that measurement. Body mass is not that measurement. A
sprint time is not that measurement. If you want Layer A (CRL-03), you
are in the biopsy business or in a spectroscopic cousin (CRL-15).

The human method descends from the Bergström needle and from the
Hultman laboratory's long practice with muscle metabolites (including
Harris, Hultman, and Nordesjö 1974 on variance of high-energy
phosphates in quadriceps).

## What the sample is

Usually **vastus lateralis**, a mixed-fiber locomotor muscle that is
reachable. A few tens of milligrams, freeze-clamped or frozen at once,
later freeze-dried for many metabolite papers so that water content does
not swing the denominator. Extracts are assayed for PCr, Cr, and often
ATP and glycogen in the same tradition.

Because PCr is labile, a slow freeze or a messy extraction can turn PCr
into Cr and flatten the ratio while leaving TCr more stable. When a
paper reports a huge swing in PCr/Cr at "rest," ask about handling.
TCr is the more forgiving headline number for feeding studies; PCr is
the more fragile one.

## Variance is not an insult; it is a design input

Harris, Hultman, and Nordesjö (1974) exist so that later writers cannot
pretend a 4% change in one person is a discovery. Fiber-type mix
differs between sticks. A second stick is not a clone of the first.
Group sizes in the 1990s cluster look small by modern RCT fashion
because the assay is loud and expensive. Small-n biopsy can still be
decisive when the effect is a 20% rise in TCr. It is weaker when the
effect is a 2% maybe.

## Locality and the exercised-limb problem

Harris et al. (1992) discussed larger increases in muscle that had been
exercised. If one leg was trained or loaded during the feeding week and
the biopsy came from that leg, you are reading a **local** story. Some
later designs use that on purpose. Some ignore it.

You also do not know gluteal or cardiac muscle from a vastus stick.
Extrapolation is a sentence, not a chromatogram.

## Ethics and burden, as a methods fact

Biopsy is an invasive research procedure. That is why many modern
training studies skip it and report only task outcomes or DXA. Skipping
it does not make those studies worthless. It changes the claim they can
carry. A landscape reader marks **unanchored task papers** instead of
scolding them for not cutting skin.

This draft is not a how-to. It does not describe needle gauge, lidocaine
routines, or aftercare. Those belong in a protocol approved for a study,
not in an educational folder.

## What this draft does not claim

- That every credible creatine paper must include a biopsy.
- That dry-mass units from 1974 are identical to every later lab's
  units.
- That a reader should seek a biopsy. They should not, outside a study.
