# CRL-06 — Endogenous synthesis: AGAT, GAMT, and a methyl group

> **Educational landscape only.** Pathway geography. Not medical advice, not
> a supplement label, and not a recommendation to use any product. Does not
> claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.
> Enzyme names are biochemistry, not a discussion of inborn conditions.

## Learning aims

- Sketch the two-enzyme route from arginine + glycine to creatine.
- See why creatine synthesis is a methyl-budget item, not only a nitrogen
  item.
- Keep "the body makes about a gram" as a **review-level range**, not as
  your personal output.

## The route in one paragraph

Textbook human synthesis is a relay. In the first reaction, arginine:glycine
amidinotransferase (AGAT) transfers the amidino group of arginine to
glycine, producing guanidinoacetate (GAA) and ornithine. In the second
reaction, guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase (GAMT) transfers a methyl
group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to GAA, producing creatine and
S-adenosylhomocysteine. Standard accounts place much of AGAT activity in
kidney and much of GAMT activity in liver, with creatine then released to
blood and taken up by tissues that express the transporter (CRL-12).
Tissue maps in reviews are cleaner than the living organ.

Walker (1979) and Wyss and Kaddurah-Daouk (2000) remain the long-form
pathway reviews. They are physiology, not product literature.

## Why the methyl step matters to readers of feeding papers

GAMT consumes SAM. Creatine synthesis is one of the larger users of labile
methyl groups in whole-body accounting exercises that nutrition
biochemists publish. That fact is sometimes inflated into a scare
("creatine depletes methylation") or into a sales pitch ("therefore take
more methyl donors"). Neither inflation is a finding of this series.

What a landscape reader can keep: if someone is writing about homocysteine,
folate, or methionine in the same sentence as creatine, they are at least
touching a real intersection. Whether a given oral schedule moves those
marks in a given sample is a **trial question**, and the trials are not
all the same. Do not let a pathway diagram write the results table.

## Endogenous rate vs dietary addition vs oral research intakes

Reviews commonly say that an adult degrades on the order of **1–2% of the
creatine pool per day** to creatinine, which for many bodies lands near
**1–3 g/day** that must be replaced by synthesis plus diet (see Wyss &
Kaddurah-Daouk; also the numbers restated in later sport-nutrition
reviews). Habitual omnivorous food may supply on the order of **1 g/day**,
with synthesis covering the rest. Plants contribute essentially no
creatine, so a low-animal-product diet shifts the balance toward
synthesis and toward a lower muscle store in several biopsy papers
(CRL-07, CRL-33).

Oral research schedules in the 1990s (5 g, four times a day, for several
days) are **many times** those replacement rates. That is why they can
raise the muscle pool. It is also why they are methods, not "what the
body wants."

## Regulation, as far as a non-specialist needs

AGAT is the step most often described as regulated. Creatine feeding can
reduce AGAT expression or activity in animal and some human-adjacent
accounts — an end-product brake. If a paper says endogenous synthesis
"shuts off" during supplementation, read for whether they measured AGAT,
GAA, or just assumed the brake from the pathway cartoon.

GAMT is less often the headline regulator in those same reviews. The
methyl supply can still be discussed as a constraint in unusual diets;
that is a different claim than "ordinary food fails."

## What this draft will not do with the enzymes

AGAT, GAMT, and the creatine transporter appear in medical genetics. This
series does not review those conditions, does not discuss management, and
does not treat oral creatine as a therapy. The enzymes are here because
you cannot read a synthesis figure without their names.

## What this draft does not claim

- That a reader should modify protein, methionine, or creatine intake to
  "support methylation."
- That synthesis rates are known for every age, sex, and diet to the gram.
- That GAA feeding is interchangeable with creatine feeding. GAA is a
  different compound with its own papers.
