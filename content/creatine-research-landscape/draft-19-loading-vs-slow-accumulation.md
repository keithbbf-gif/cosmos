# CRL-19 — Two schedules, one pool: Hultman 1996 as methods history

> **Educational landscape only.** History of a protocol contrast. Not a
> personal schedule, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to use
> any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Restate Hultman et al. (1996) as two paths to a similar muscle TCr.
- See why later writers kept the fast path and forgot the slow path.
- Use the paper as a warning about washout clocks.

## What the paper set out to do

Hultman, Söderlund, Timmons, Cederblad, and Greenhaff asked how dietary
creatine, given in different daily amounts for different lengths of
time, changed muscle total creatine and how the extra pool later
declined. Thirty-one men. Several arms. Muscle biopsy and urine.

The educational yield is a **fork**:

- **Fast path:** about 20 g/day for 6 days raised muscle TCr by about
  20% in that sample.
- **Slow path:** about 3 g/day for 28 days produced a similar elevation
  without the short high week.

A hold of 2 g/day kept the fast-path elevation for another 30 days in
the arm that continued. Without a hold, the concentration declined, and
at about 30 days it was no longer different from the starting value.
Urinary creatinine tracked the larger pool.

## What the fork is for, in a landscape

If your scientific question is "does an elevated TCr change this
Saturday's repeated-sprint test," the fast path is a way to get the
pool up before the test week. If your question is "what happens across
a month of training," the slow path may put the pool and the training
on a similar clock. Those are **design reasons**. They are not
preferences about a reader's week.

The forgotten slow path is the most useful correction to internet
folklore. Folklore says you must load. The 1996 paper says you can
arrive at a similar muscle number more slowly. Arrival time is the
difference it actually measured.

## What the paper did not decide

It did not decide GI comfort, although later writers treat high split
doses as more often unpleasant. It did not decide women, adolescents,
or low-meat diets (those samples were not this sample). It did not
decide a task. It did not decide a product. It did not decide that 20%
is the rise you will see; Harris 1992 already said the rise depends on
the starting store.

## How later methods froze the fast path

Grant calendars like a one-week preload. Sport calendars like a number
that fits on a handout. So "20 g × 6 days" became the default, and
"3 g × 28 days" became a footnote. A landscape reader who only knows
the handout has not read the paper.

## Washout is the other half of the result

If TCr is still up at two weeks and mostly back at four, a crossover
with a 7-day washout is not a clean crossover. CRL-22 exists because
this paragraph gets ignored.

## What this draft does not claim

- That you should load, or that you should not load.
- That 2 g/day or 3 g/day is a personal maintenance need.
- That a 20% rise is guaranteed, desirable, or detectable without an
  assay.
