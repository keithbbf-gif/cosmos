# CRL-46 — Occupational and military performance papers as a map

> **Educational landscape only.** A workplace-and-field corner of
> the task map. Not a deployment guide, not medical advice, and
> not a recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that
> creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Place occupational papers on the same task map as sport papers.
- See sleep, load carriage, and heat as stacked modifiers.
- Refuse readiness slogans that skip the outcome table.

## Why the corner exists

Armies, fire services, and labor laboratories care about repeated
high-intensity efforts, loaded marches, and long days. Those are
CRL-27 and CRL-29 tasks wearing a uniform. It is not a new
molecule. It is a new **context of measurement**.

Papers in this corner often combine:

- a creatine schedule copied from sport,
- a field or laboratory battery (lifts, sprints, marksmanship
  analogs, cognitive tests),
- sleep restriction or a long wake (CRL-45),
- heat or hydration rules (CRL-37),
- a sample of already-trained young adults, usually men
  (CRL-34, CRL-36).

Each extra factor is a second intervention. A "military creatine
study" with four moving parts is easy to over-quote.

## What to harvest

Task definitions that sport labs do not use (load carriage,
repeated casualty-drag analogs). Honest nulls on long aerobic
pieces. Notes on palatability and GI complaints when the powder
is issued in the field. Those are methods treasures.

## What not to harvest

Readiness as a medical word. Any implication that a powder is
part of a health program. Injury-prevention language, which
some field papers and some position stands reach for. This
series leaves that language on the table (CRL-02).

## How a careful paper talks

The battery is listed. The primary outcome is named. Sleep and
caffeine are logged. The powder is assayed (CRL-41) — field
tubs are Object B until proven otherwise. Sex and diet are not
"too complicated to record."

## What this draft does not claim

- That occupational use is indicated.
- That a field battery equals a sport result or the reverse.
- That any organization should issue a powder.
