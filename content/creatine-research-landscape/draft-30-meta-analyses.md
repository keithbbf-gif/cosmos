# CRL-30 — Meta-analyses: what pooling hides and what it shows

> **Educational landscape only.** A reader's guide to forest plots
> in this file. Not a ranking of products, not medical advice, and
> not a recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that
> creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Open the inclusion table before the diamond.
- Ask whether the pooled outcome is one task or a mixture.
- Treat industry-adjacent reviews as maps with a wind direction.

## What a creatine meta-analysis is trying to do

A single trial is small, local, and noisy. Pooling asks whether the
sign of an effect repeats. That is a good question when the papers
share a **task class**, a **dose class**, and an **outcome unit**.
It is a worse question when the analyst converts everything to a
standardized mean difference and throws 6-second sprints in with
12-week bench-press 1RMs.

Branch (2003) is an early, still-cited attempt to pool body-
composition and performance papers. Lanhers et al. (2015, 2017)
narrow toward lower- and upper-limb strength. Narrower is usually
more honest. It is also less exciting on a poster.

## Leaks that survive into the diamond

- **Unanchored trials** (no TCr) weighted the same as biopsy trials.
- **Short washout crossovers** treated as independent (CRL-22).
- **Water-mass outcomes** coded as hypertrophy (CRL-24).
- **Duplicate cohorts** published as two papers.
- **Publication bias** toward the pretty sprint.
- **Dose unit mixing** (g/day vs g/kg).

A funnel plot is not a cleansing ritual. It is a hint.

## How to read one in this series

1. What was eligible? (form, days, task)
2. What was the primary outcome in the pre-registration, if any?
3. Are women and low-meat diets present or just mentioned?
4. Is heterogeneity (I² or τ) large enough that the diamond is a
   polite average of disagreements?
5. Who funded the review, and do authors sell the powder?

None of those questions deletes a meta-analysis from the map. They
decide how hard you lean on the last line of the abstract.

## Position stands are not meta-analyses

ISSN 2017 is a society document that cites many papers and states
positions. It can include a narrative of consistency. It is not a
reproducible pooled estimate (CRL-32). Do not cite it as if it were
Branch or Lanhers.

## What this draft does not claim

- That meta-analysis is corrupt. It is a tool.
- That a high I² means "no effect." It means "not one effect."
- That a diamond is a personal expectation.
