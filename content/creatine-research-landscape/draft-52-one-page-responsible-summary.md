# CRL-52 — What a responsible educational summary can say in one page

> **Educational landscape only.** A speech ceiling. Not medical
> advice, not a supplement label, and not a recommendation to
> use any product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses,
> treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Leave the folder with sentences you can say in public
  without turning them into a use protocol.
- Keep Layer A, Layer B, and Layer D from collapsing.
- End where we began: description, not indication.

## Sentences that survive the fence

Creatine is a small guanidino compound humans synthesize and
also eat in animal muscle foods. In muscle it exists in
equilibrium with phosphocreatine through creatine kinase, a
fast reaction that can buffer ATP in short, severe work.

In 1992, Harris, Söderlund, and Hultman showed that oral
creatine can raise the muscle store in healthy people, more
so when the store starts lower. In 1996, Hultman and
colleagues showed a short high-intake week and a longer
moderate-intake month can arrive at a similar elevation, that
a smaller daily amount can hold it, and that the extra pool
takes on the order of a month to fade when the extra inlet
stops.

Those are **pool facts** from named samples, mostly men,
mostly vastus lateralis, mostly monohydrate.

Task papers, starting with Greenhaff's repeated maximal
efforts, ask whether a phosphagen-shaped test moves after
the pool is up. That file is thickest for repeated
high-intensity and resistance-training outcomes, thinner for
long steady endurance, and easy to mis-pool in a forest
plot. Water and body mass rise early and confuse lean-mass
headlines. Habitual low-creatine diets tend to start with
more room. Other salts owe us equimolar pool evidence.
Healthy-volunteer marker papers exist and do not clear a
person. Brain MRS shows a smaller, slower, regional change
in voxels; that is not a thinking product.

Creatine as a named ingredient is not on the 2026 WADA
Prohibited List; a tub can still be contaminated. "Dietary
supplement" is a statute box, not a medal.

This series does not tell you to take it. It does not tell
you not to. It tells you how the file was built.

## Sentences that do not survive the fence

Anything with treat, cure, prevent, or a condition.
Anything that begins "you should."
Anything that turns ISSN into a prescription pad.
Anything that turns a 5 g scoop into a health practice.

## If you remember four pointers

- Harris 1992 — the pool can move.
- Hultman 1996 — two paths, a hold, a month-long tail.
- Greenhaff 1993/1994 — task and PCr kinetics.
- CRL-03 — the worksheet that keeps you honest.

## What this draft does not claim

- That the paragraph above is complete.
- That completeness would become advice.
- That silence about disease is a hidden negative trial.
