# CRL-24 — Body mass, water, and what a scale cannot tell you

> **Educational landscape only.** A measurement confounder chapter.
> Not a body-composition program, not medical advice, and not a
> recommendation to use any product. Does not claim that creatine
> diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Expect a short-term body-mass rise in many loading-week papers.
- Keep water, glycogen-associated water, and contractile protein from
  collapsing into "lean mass."
- Read DXA and two-compartment estimates as instruments with a water
  bias.

## The scale moves early

Loading-week studies often report a rise in body mass of about
**0.5–2 kg** in the first days to a week, with a wide spread. The
usual account is water: creatine is osmotically active, muscle water
rises as TCr rises, and carbohydrate co-ingestion (CRL-21) can add
glycogen-bound water on top. Hultman-era and later training papers
repeat the mass change often enough that a paper which loads and
reports *no* mass data should be asked why.

That mass change is a **real measurement**. It is not automatically
new myofibrils. A 6-day study does not have time to tell a hypertrophy
story except as a joke.

## Why "lean mass" gets in trouble

Two-compartment models and many DXA readouts treat hydrated lean
tissue as one box. Extra intracellular water lands in that box. A
4-week training study that reports +1.5 kg "lean mass" on creatine
versus +0.8 kg on placebo may be looking at water, protein, or both.
Longer studies with training have a better chance of including
contractile protein, but the instrument still does not separate the
water that arrived in week one from the protein that arrived in week
eight unless the authors work at it (and most cannot).

Underwater weighing and older density methods have their own water
assumptions. Bioimpedance is often worse, not better, when electrolyte
and water shift.

## Task consequences that are not "anabolism"

Extra mass can:

- make a bodyweight pull-up or a hill run harder,
- make an absolute strength task look better if the person is simply
  heavier and leaning into a grounded lift,
- change heat dissipation and sweat (CRL-37),
- unblind a participant who watches the scale.

So the same water that inflates a lean-mass graph can hide or mimic a
performance effect depending on the task. CRL-31 treats this as a
blinding and placebo problem as well.

## What careful papers do

They report body mass at the same time of day, after the same voiding
routine, and they do not call a 7-day mass change hypertrophy. They
separate absolute and relative (per kg) performance. They mention
whether the carbohydrate drink differed between arms. They do not
treat a DXA delta as a biopsy of myosin.

## What this draft does not claim

- That all of the mass change is water in every person. It is the
  leading explanation for the *early* change.
- That later training adaptations are "only water." That would be the
  opposite over-claim.
- That a reader should chase or fear a number on a scale because of
  this folder.
