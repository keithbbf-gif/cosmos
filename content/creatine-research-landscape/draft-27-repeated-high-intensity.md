# CRL-27 — Repeated high-intensity tasks: the phosphagen-shaped file

> **Educational landscape only.** A task-class chapter. Not a sprint
> program, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to use any
> product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Describe the protocol shape that matches a PCr story.
- See why the *later* bout, not the first, is often the interesting
  one.
- Keep laboratory isokinetics and field sprints as cousins, not
  clones.

## Why later bouts are the test

PCr falls in a few seconds of maximal work and rebuilds over minutes.
A single first sprint can be paid from a pool that was already large.
The fourth or fifth sprint, started before rebuild is finished, is
where an extra reservoir *could* show. Greenhaff et al. (1993) looked
at repeated maximal voluntary bouts for that reason. Greenhaff et al.
(1994) looked at PCr resynthesis for the same reason.

If a paper only reports sprint 1 and calls the study negative, ask
whether they ever gave the mechanism a chance.

## Common laboratory shapes

- Repeated maximal isokinetic knee extensions with fixed rest.
- Repeated cycle sprints (for example 6–30 s) with rest that is
  shorter than full PCr recovery.
- Repeated bench- or leg-press repetitions to a failure rule.

Casey et al. (1996) is a frequently cited maximal-exercise metabolism
paper in this neighborhood. Open it for the combination of
performance and muscle metabolites, not for a slogan.

## Field and sport cousins

Repeated-sprint ability tests in team-sport athletes try to bring the
same shape outdoors. Surfaces, turns, and pacing add noise. A
meaningful field test that cannot be blinded well (CRL-31) is still
worth mapping; it just carries a wider error bar.

## What a positive paper still has not shown

That the person became a better player. That the first peak power of
the season moved. That the effect survives when the athlete already
eats a high-creatine diet and already trained the test. Task evidence
is local to the task.

## What a negative paper still has not shown

That the pool did not rise. Many task papers never measure TCr. A
negative unanchored paper is "this protocol, this sample, this
outcome did not move," which is a real sentence and a smaller
sentence than "creatine does nothing."

## Design details that decide the file

Rest interval. Number of bouts. Whether total work or best-bout is
primary. Familiarization (sprint tests have a learning curve). Whether
body mass rose into a weight-bearing sprint. Whether caffeine sat in
the same scoop (CRL-38).

## What this draft does not claim

- That every repeated-sprint study is positive. They are not.
- That a laboratory torque curve equals a match-winning action.
- That readers should adopt a sprint protocol from this page.
