# CRL-42 — Statute category: supplement speech is not drug speech

> **Educational landscape only.** A public-language map of U.S.
> categories, not legal advice for any product or person, not
> medical advice, and not a recommendation to use any product.
> Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- See "dietary supplement" as a **marketing-and-statute box**,
  not as a quality or efficacy badge.
- Distinguish disease claims from the narrower structure/function
  style of supplement speech.
- Keep WADA listing, FDA category, and journal evidence as three
  different axes.

## Three axes that get mashed

**Axis 1 — Evidence.** Did a named paper measure a named thing?
That is this series.

**Axis 2 — Food/drug statute (U.S.).** The Dietary Supplement
Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA) sits inside the Federal
Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. A product marketed as a dietary
supplement is not approved as a drug for any condition by virtue
of being sold. Disease-claim speech on a label is the line FDA
has long drawn. This folder uses that line as a **teaching
discipline** (CRL-02). It is not a law office.

**Axis 3 — Sport lists.** WADA's Prohibited List is about sport
eligibility, not about FDA category. Creatine is not a named
listed substance on the 2026 list. A tub can still be a
contamination risk (CRL-41).

A powder can be legal to sell as a supplement, unlisted by WADA,
and still unsupported for a task someone cares about — or the
reverse on the task, without becoming a drug.

## What this series will not write

We will not write structure/function label copy. We will not
write "supports healthy…" sentences that are label-shaped. We
describe papers. If a reader needs a label, they need their own
counsel and their own regulator, not this folder.

We will not interpret 21 U.S.C. § 343(r) for a commercial
launch. The citation exists so students know the public
boundary has a statute number.

## Other countries

Categories differ. A compound sold as a food supplement in one
place can sit in another box elsewhere. Papers are usually
silent on that. Do not export DSHEA as world law.

## What this draft does not claim

- That DSHEA "proves creatine is safe" or "proves it works."
  Statute is not a trial.
- That this page is legal advice.
- That an unapproved drug would be a better category. Category
  is not a compliment.

- Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, 21 U.S.C. § 343(r) and
