# CRL-03 — How to read a creatine paper

> **Educational landscape only.** This draft is a reading method. It is not
> medical advice, not a supplement label, and not a recommendation to use any
> product. It does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or
> prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Open a creatine paper at the methods table, not at the abstract's last line.
- Separate pool change, task change, and mechanism story.
- Spot the usual design leaks: washout, water mass, blinding, and baseline
  store.

## Read the verb before you read the adjective

Abstracts sell adjectives ("effective," "significant," "ergogenic"). Methods
sections sell verbs: *ingested*, *biopsied*, *sprinted*, *recovered*,
*excreted*. A landscape reader collects verbs first.

Ask, in order:

1. **What entered the mouth, and as which salt or hydrate?**
2. **For how many days, split how, with what else in the drink?**
3. **Who swallowed it — sex, age band, diet, training history, body mass?**
4. **What tissue or signal was assayed, and when relative to the last dose?**
5. **What task, if any, sat on top of that assay?**
6. **What was the comparator — placebo, nothing, another salt, another
   schedule?**

If a paper cannot answer (1)–(4), it cannot carry a pool claim. If it cannot
answer (5)–(6), it cannot carry a task claim. Many popular citations fail one
of those gates and survive as slogans anyway.

## Four layers that get flattened

**Layer A — Pool.** Muscle total creatine (TCr) or phosphocreatine (PCr)
changed. Harris et al. (1992) and Hultman et al. (1996) are the modern
anchors. Units are often mmol per kg dry muscle. Wet-muscle units are not
interchangeable without water content.

**Layer B — Task.** A performance or training outcome moved. Greenhaff et al.
(1993) on repeated maximal torque is an early task paper sitting on Layer A.
A task paper that never measured the pool is not automatically false; it is
*unanchored*.

**Layer C — Mechanism story.** Authors infer that extra PCr buffered ATP
turnover, or that extra water changed training volume, or that a transporter
saturated. Stories should be marked as stories.

**Layer D — Extrapolation.** "Therefore athletes / older adults / vegetarians
will…" Extrapolation is not a result. CRL-33–36 exist because Layer D is
where most folklore is born.

## The leaks that repeat

**Baseline store.** People who start nearer the upper published range of
muscle TCr have less room to rise. Harris et al. already noted larger
increases in those with lower starting content. A trial that never measures
baseline TCr cannot sort "non-responder" from "already full" from "did not
swallow the powder."

**Water and the scale.** Creatine loading is often accompanied by a rise in
body mass. If the only hypertrophy assay is a scale or a two-compartment
lean-mass estimate, you are at least partly watching water. CRL-24 and
CRL-31.

**Washout.** Muscle TCr does not snap back in a week. Hultman et al. (1996)
reported a decline toward baseline over about a month after a load when
intake stopped. Crossover trials with short washouts can contaminate the
second arm. CRL-22.

**Blinding and GI tell.** Large doses of poorly dissolved powder can unblind
a participant. If the outcome is a feeling or a self-paced task, that leak
matters more than if the outcome is a biopsy.

**Creatinine confusion.** Serum creatinine can rise when the creatine pool
and creatinine excretion rise. That is a measurement entanglement, not a
secret organ story. CRL-16 and CRL-43 stay on the assay, not on diagnosis.

**Which creatine.** "Creatine" in a title may mean monohydrate, a different
salt, an ester, or an undeclared blend. Pool literature is overwhelmingly
monohydrate. CRL-39.

## A one-page worksheet

Copy this into your notes for any paper:

```
Paper:
Salt / hydrate / dose / days / split / co-ingest:
Sample (n, sex, diet, training, age band):
Pool assay (site, unit, timing):
Task assay (protocol, rest, familiarization):
Comparator and blinding:
Washout / crossover:
Industry or author-product tie:
What the paper can support:
What the abstract implied beyond that:
```

If "what the paper can support" is empty, do not cite the paper in a landscape
sentence. Cite it as a specimen of weak reporting instead (CRL-50).

## Reviews and position stands

Read reviews backward. Start at the inclusion table. If the review mixes
5-second sprints, 10-km time trials, and 12-week hypertrophy programs under
one forest plot, the diamond is a mixture, not a molecule. CRL-30 and CRL-32.

## What this draft does not claim

- That a perfect methods table makes a finding true.
- That industry funding automatically falsifies a result (it is a leak to
  mark, not a shredder).
- That readers should ignore papers that lack biopsy. Lack of biopsy limits
  the *kind* of claim, not the right to exist.
