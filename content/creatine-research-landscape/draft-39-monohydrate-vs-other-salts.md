# CRL-39 — Monohydrate versus other salts and esters

> **Educational landscape only.** A forms inventory. Not a product
> review, not medical advice, and not a recommendation to use any
> product. Does not claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures,
> or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Treat monohydrate as the documented default, not as a brand.
- Demand pool evidence from any other solid.
- See "more soluble" as a kitchen property until a biopsy or a
  careful retention study says otherwise.

## The default, again

Harris, Hultman, Greenhaff, Green: creatine monohydrate. Most
training trials: the same. Jäger et al. (2011) inventoried novel
forms and, read as a forms paper, is useful because it forces the
question: **did this solid raise muscle TCr as monohydrate does?**

## What other labels usually mean

**Different salts** (citrate, malate, hydrochloride, nitrate,
magnesium chelate): creatine plus a counter-ion. Creatine fraction
per gram of powder changes. Solubility and taste may change. Pool
evidence is thinner and mixed; some papers exist, few have the
1990s biopsy weight.

**Buffered / alkaline products.** Jagim et al. (2012) compared a
buffered form to monohydrate and did not find a greater rise in
muscle creatine or better training outcomes in that design. One
paper is not the whole sky. It is a warning that pH marketing is
not a pool assay.

**Ethyl ester.** Spillane et al. (2009) is the often-cited training
study that did not find an advantage over monohydrate and raised
questions about the ester as a delivery story. Read the paper; do
not recycle a forum summary.

**Liquid "creatine shots."** Stability is the issue (CRL-40). A
molecule that has become creatinine in the bottle is not a clever
form. It is a different molecule.

## The marketing move to refuse

"More bioavailable" without a plasma curve, a urine retention
table, or a muscle assay. Solubility in a glass is not
bioavailability in a fiber. "No loading needed" as a form claim
is especially noisy, because monohydrate already has a slow path
(Hultman 1996) that needs no new salt.

## How a careful paper talks

Exact chemical name, creatine fraction, assayed content, and a
comparator arm that is monohydrate at an **equimolar creatine**
dose, not an equal gram of powder. If the doses are equal grams
of different solids, the creatine inlet is already different.

## What this draft does not claim

- That every non-monohydrate solid "does nothing." Some may raise
  the pool; they have to show it.
- That monohydrate is the only legal form.
- That a reader should buy or avoid a named brand.
