# CRL-05 — What creatine is, chemically

> **Educational landscape only.** A molecule brief. Not medical advice, not a
> supplement label, and not a recommendation to use any product. Does not
> claim that creatine diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents any disease.

## Learning aims

- Name the compound without collapsing it into "protein" or "amino acid
  supplement" folklore.
- Keep creatine, phosphocreatine, and creatinine as three structures.
- Know why monohydrate mass is not creatine mass.

## A small, polar, named thing

Creatine is N-(aminoiminomethyl)-N-methylglycine, often written as
N-methylguanidinoacetic acid. Formula C4H9N3O2. The textbook molecular
weight of the anhydrous compound is 131.13 g/mol. It is a guanidino
compound built on a glycine backbone with a methyl group on the nitrogen
that faces the acetate carbon.

It is **related to amino acids** (it is assembled from arginine, glycine,
and a methyl group that originated on methionine) and it is **not a
proteinogenic amino acid**. It does not get inserted into peptide chains
by the ribosome. Calling a creatine powder "just an amino acid" is a
marketing compression, not a structure.

The molecule is polar and small. In water it exists in a zwitterionic
equilibrium. It is not a steroid, not a secretagogue, and not creatine
kinase. Those three mix-ups still appear in comment threads.

## Three structures that share a root

**Creatine** is the free base (or its zwitterion).

**Phosphocreatine** carries a high-energy N-phosphoryl group. In the
creatine kinase reaction, that phosphoryl can be transferred to ADP to
make ATP, with a proton involved in the balanced equation. The chemistry
is reversible and fast relative to most metabolic pathways, which is why
physiologists treat the pair as a near-equilibrium buffer rather than as
a slow storage fat.

**Creatinine** is a cyclic product. Creatine and phosphocreatine convert
non-enzymatically to creatinine in a first-order way that depends on
temperature, pH, and time. That is why a solution left on a warm bench
is not the same material as the powder in a dry bottle (CRL-40), and why
urinary creatinine tracks pool size and breakdown rather than "kidney
folklore" by itself (CRL-16).

If a paper's figure labels all three with the same abbreviation, stop and
relabel them before you quote a number.

## Hydrates and the mass that is not creatine

The form that dominates human feeding studies is **creatine
monohydrate**: C4H9N3O2·H2O, molecular weight 149.15 g/mol. The water
is lattice water. On a mass basis, monohydrate is about 87.9% creatine.
A methods line that says "5 g creatine" may mean 5 g of monohydrate or
5 g of creatine from monohydrate. Those are not identical intakes. Careful
papers specify the hydrate.

Other commercial solids exist (citrate, malate, hydrochloride, and so
on). They change the counter-ion and the creatine fraction per gram of
powder. They do not automatically change the physiology. Pool evidence
still has to be measured (CRL-39).

## What the name remembers

The word comes from Greek *kreas*, flesh. Michel Eugène Chevreul isolated
the compound from meat extract in 1832. The name is a history tag: this
was first a meat crystal, later a muscle metabolite, later an oral
research powder. CRL-08.

## What chemistry does not decide

Structure does not decide a task outcome. The fact that PCr can
phosphorylate ADP does not prove that a 10-km time trial will change
after a week of powder. Chemistry licenses a *plausible bottleneck* for
short, hard, repeated work. Task papers still have to run the task
(CRL-26–29).

Chemistry also does not decide safety for a person. Solubility, pH of a
solution, and degradation to creatinine are material properties.
Tolerability is a human-trial property (CRL-43).

## What this draft does not claim

- That creatine is an essential nutrient in the dietary-reference sense.
  Humans synthesize it; diet adds to the pool.
- That monohydrate is "the only real creatine." It is the best-documented
  oral solid in this literature.
- That a structural analogy to amino acids implies a protein-synthesis
  mechanism.
