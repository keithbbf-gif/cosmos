# The FBI background-check gate is why enacted states sit dark

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

Ask a board why the compact map is still mostly “enacted, not issuing,” and the honest ones point at fingerprints. The Commission FAQ and the October 2023 FAQ PDF say the same thing in bureaucratic English. Member states must consider criminal-history records for an initial privilege to practice. That includes fingerprints or other biometrics to pull FBI and state records. A member state **cannot** participate in authorizing privileges until it has fully implemented the FBI criminal-background-check requirement in Section 3.

“Full implementation,” the FAQ defines, means the licensing board is using FBI record-search results in licensure decisions for **all** applicants seeking an initial audiology or SLP license in that state — not a special compact-only window. States must also keep the Commission posted on progress, and they must finish the request process to receive FBI results or they risk default.

Communication among states and the Commission about eligibility **must not** include information received from the FBI under the Public Law 92-544 channel. The rap sheet stays with the state that pulled it. That is why CompactConnect can say yes or no without becoming a federal criminal-record cloud.

## What a clinician feels

If you were licensed before your state started FBI checks, your board may ask you to be printed again before it will sponsor a privilege. Ohio’s page did exactly that for licenses first issued before 18 January 2018. If your state has not stood up FBI checks for every new SLP, you can live in an enacted compact state and still have nowhere to apply.

Do not mail a paper fingerprint card to the Commission. They have told you they are not allowed to hold that file.

## What this is not

Not a moral judgment of old licenses. Not a substitute for a state-only background check where a board still uses one. Not immigration enforcement. Not a reason to post your arrest record in a Facebook group “for transparency.”

If a bill sponsor promised “the compact will go live the week we sign,” they skipped Section 3. The map is the evidence.

Boards that already printed every new SLP for years will onboard faster. Boards that used a state-only check will have to change the application for *everyone*, including people who will never compact-practice. That political pain is why some enacted states stay dark. It is also why a compact is a public-protection machine and not a travel coupon.

If your prints are delayed at a contractor site, that delay is now on the critical path to a privilege. Schedule them before you sign a contract that assumed a two-week start.
