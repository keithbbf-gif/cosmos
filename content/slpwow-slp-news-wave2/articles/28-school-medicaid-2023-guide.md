# School Medicaid is not IDEA — CMS’s 2023 guide is the federal floor

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

On 18 May 2023 CMS issued a CMCS Informational Bulletin and a long guide, written with the Department of Education: *Delivering Services in School-Based Settings*. The packet is still the federal document districts should open before they believe a vendor’s claiming app. It sits on top of older school-based administrative claiming guidance and on section 110 of the Bipartisan Safer Communities Act. It is not a 2026 rewrite. It is the floor this desk will keep citing until CMS replaces it.

The idea that surprises people is older than the guide. Medicaid can pay for medically necessary speech-language pathology furnished at school to an enrolled child when the service is in the state plan and the provider meets 42 CFR 440.110. Since 2014, school-based services can also reach Medicaid-enrolled students *outside* an IEP for covered benefits, including preventive and behavioral health. IDEA is a civil-rights and education statute. Medicaid is a payer. They shake hands. They are not the same office.

## What the 2023 packet added

The bulletin advertised claiming flexibilities: roster-style interim payments reconciled to cost, optional per-child-per-month interim rates, and a friendlier posture toward random-moment time studies so a district does not have to diary every minute. The guide walks PT, OT, and speech/audiology through 440.110 — physician or other licensed-practitioner orders where required, “by or under the direction of” a qualified therapist, qualifications that often orbit the CCC or an equivalent.

ASHA’s school Medicaid toolkit is the profession’s translation: free-care limits on mass screenings, EPSDT as the usual benefit theory for evaluation and treatment, NPI-versus-LEA billing as a state-plan choice, parental consent under IDEA/FERPA when you bill.

## What a clinician should not invent

Do not tell a parent that signing an IEP “is Medicaid.” Consent to evaluate is not consent to bill. Do not bill a universal kindergarten screen just because the child has a Medicaid number — ASHA still flags the free-care problem. Do not assume your state requires claiming; some do, some treat it as optional. Do not assume an SLPA who is legal in the education code is a 440.110 qualified provider.

Documentation in the federal school-based materials wants dates, who, where, duration, condition, and a clinical record that can survive a financial-management review. That list should look familiar if you also bill Part B. The audience is different. The need for a sentence is not.

## 2026 note

This brief is here because school-based Medicaid keeps getting sold as new news. The public federal guide is 2023. Your state plan is whatever year your agency last amended it. Open both.
