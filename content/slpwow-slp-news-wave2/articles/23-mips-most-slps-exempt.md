# Most SLPs are still outside MIPS — that is not a quality vacation

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The Quality Payment Program is how Medicare has tried, since MACRA, to move Part B clinicians onto either the Merit-based Incentive Payment System or an Advanced Alternative Payment Model. ASHA’s 2026 booklet, in the QPP section, says the quiet part in one line: **most SLPs remain exempt from MIPS reporting** and have limited ability to participate in Advanced APMs, even though opportunities continue.

That exemption is why the second conversion factor exists. Qualifying APM participants get $33.57. The rest of the profession gets $33.40. If you are exempt from MIPS and not a QP, you are in the second line. You did not “win” a quality holiday. You were left out of the machines that trigger the higher update.

## Why the exemption exists

MIPS was built around physician-style reporting, eligible clinician types, and volume thresholds. Many speech-language pathologists fall below low-volume thresholds, work in settings that bill under a facility number, or are simply not in the eligible-clinician list the way a physician group is. ASHA’s pages on QPP have tracked this for years. The 2026 booklet is a status check, not a new statute.

CMS’s fact sheet, meanwhile, is full of primary-care and behavioral-health add-on codes. Those are real 2026 news for physicians. They are not automatically SLP news. Do not let a QPP webinar shame a school-based or SNF SLP for “not reporting MIPS.”

## What you still owe

Medical necessity. Outcome measures your employer chose. State quality rules. Joint Commission or CARF if you live there. A family who wants to know whether the person can swallow thin liquid. None of that is MIPS. The exemption does not make a thin chart ethical.

If you *are* a MIPS-eligible SLP in a group that reports, read the current year’s measures before you believe last year’s slide. This brief will not list measures; they churn.

## How to answer the administrator

“We are probably MIPS-exempt; confirm against this year’s NPI lookup. We are almost certainly not a qualifying APM participant, so model 2026 with $33.40. We will not pretend exemption equals no outcomes.”

If a health system dumps SLPs into a MIPS registry because “everyone reports,” ask who is the eligible clinician and who is just employed. Reporting the wrong person is not extra credit. It is a mess. If you *are* in an Advanced APM and someone can show you the QP letter, then — and only then — model $33.57. Bring the letter to the budget meeting. Verbal “we’re in an ACO” is not a conversion factor.
