# The 2016 Scope of Practice is a map of the profession, not a license

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The Scope of Practice in Speech-Language Pathology that ASHA still points to is a 2016 Board of Directors document (approved 4 February 2016; a hearing-prevention revision that May). It is official association policy. It is also one of the most abused PDFs in hiring fights. People wave it as if it were a state practice act. The document itself says the opposite.

Near the top of the purpose section: this scope does **not** supersede existing state licensure laws or change how those laws are read. It may be a model when a legislature writes a bill. Model is not statute.

## What the map is for

The SLP, in ASHA’s definition here, is the professional who practices in communication and swallowing across the life span. Communication is allowed to be a large word: speech production, fluency, language, cognition, voice, resonance, hearing. Swallowing includes feeding. The committee’s big structural move in 2016 was to split the work into eight service-delivery domains — collaboration, counseling, prevention and wellness, screening, assessment, treatment, modalities/technology/instrumentation, population and systems — and five professional-practice domains: advocacy, supervision, education, research, administration/leadership.

That grid is why a later certification standard can mention telepractice without rewriting the scope, and why a hospital can argue that counseling a family after a new tracheostomy is inside the profession. It is also why the document keeps repeating a competence warning. No one practices the whole grid. The Code of Ethics already said you stay inside education, training, and experience. The scope repeats it so a payer or a principal cannot pretend that “ASHA says every SLP does FEES.”

## Autonomy, and the payer exception

ASHA calls SLPs autonomous primary providers of speech-language pathology services — not prescribed or supervised by another profession as a condition of being SLPs. Then the same page admits the real world: some settings and some payers require a physician order or an eligibility ritual. Professional judgment, the scope says, decides whether those extra requirements are indicated. That sentence is how you talk to a medical-staff office without pretending Medicare vanished.

Evidence-based practice is defined the usual three-legged way: current research, clinician expertise, client values. The Practice Portal is named as the association’s evidence shelf. Wave 2 has a later brief on Evidence Maps. They are shelves. They are not orders.

## What not to do with this PDF

Do not tell a state board it has been preempted. Do not tell a family that a clinician without a CCC is “not a real SLP” on the basis of this page alone — the scope’s definition of SLP is written around the CCC, while states license people ASHA has never met. Do not treat the 2016 date as proof the profession froze. The document says the practice evolves and tells clinicians to read the literature and take continuing education.

If a job description copies the entire service-delivery list and then assigns one person every line, the job description is the ethical problem, not the scope.
