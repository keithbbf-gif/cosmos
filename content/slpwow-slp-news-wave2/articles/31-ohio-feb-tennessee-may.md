# Ohio opened in February. Tennessee opened in May. The map is a calendar.

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The Commission’s home page, in the banner this desk captured, dated Tennessee’s launch: **28 May 2026**, privileges to practice now issuing. Registration, the same banner said, was open for licensed practitioners in Louisiana, Ohio, Tennessee, and West Virginia. The Commission’s privilege-status FAQ added the same two Midwestern and Southern names as newly able to register.

Ohio’s Speech and Hearing Professionals Board was more precise than a banner. Ohio, it said, was the 14th state to join and would **begin processing applications on 9 February 2026**. Eligibility notes on that page are local: a criminal-background-check step for people first licensed before 18 January 2018; applications through CompactConnect, not through eLicense Ohio. As of February 2026, Ohio licensees were told they could seek privileges only in the states already live — then West Virginia and Louisiana.

The Commission’s July 2026 fee-and-jurisprudence PDF gives a second pair of “accepting” dates that match the story: Louisiana and West Virginia on **28 October 2025**; Ohio on 9 February 2026; Tennessee on 28 May 2026.

## Why a calendar brief exists

Because “the compact launched” is not a year. It is a sequence of Tuesdays. A clinician who applied in November 2025 had two remote states. A clinician who waited until June 2026 had four, if their home state was one of those four. Everyone else still had a statute and a waiting room.

## What the Ohio page teaches everyone else

Do not look for the compact button on your state portal. Look for CompactConnect. Do not assume your original license’s background check satisfies the compact if your board published a cutoff date. Do not assume you can privilege into every enacted state on opening day.

## What this is not

Not a promise that a fifth state will open in a particular month. Not travel-company marketing. Not a fee quote — that is the next brief.

If your board’s page and the Commission banner disagree on a date, believe the board for *your* license and email the Commission. Then tell this magazine’s editor. News desks update calendars. They do not invent them.

Write the four dates on one line if you brief travelers: LA/WV 28 Oct 2025; OH 9 Feb 2026; TN 28 May 2026. That line will age. Until it does, it is the only compact calendar that is not a wish. A clinician whose home license is in a dark enacted state cannot borrow Ohio’s February as if it were national. Ohio opened Ohio.

If you lived through the nursing compact’s long onboarding, this sequence will feel familiar. Compacts launch as statutes and mature as software. The software is late. The statute is still real. Hold both.
