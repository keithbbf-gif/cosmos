# The July 2026 fee sheet is the only honest compact price list

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

Travel blogs like a single number. The Commission published a table. The July 2026 *State Fee and Jurisprudence* PDF is the artifact to open before anyone quotes “about fifty dollars.”

The Commission’s own compact fee in that table is **$50** per privilege. Then come state rows. Among the four jurisdictions that were accepting privileges, the PDF’s accepting dates and extras were:

- **Louisiana** — accepting 28 October 2025; jurisprudence not required; state fee $50 on the row this desk read.
- **West Virginia** — accepting 28 October 2025; WV jurisprudence required; state fee $25.
- **Ohio** — accepting 9 February 2026; jurisprudence not required; state fee $25.
- **Tennessee** — accepting 28 May 2026; TN jurisprudence required; state fee **$150**.

Other enacted states sit on the same PDF as “not yet,” with jurisprudence flags and fees that will matter when they launch — Maryland’s $100, Nebraska’s $80, Washington’s $80, Wyoming’s $120, several TBDs. Do not pay those fees until the accepting column moves. Do not ignore them when you plan a 2027 travel year.

## Two tests, not one

A jurisprudence exam is a state-law quiz. The compact privilege does not waive it where the sheet says it is required. West Virginia and Tennessee wanted it. Ohio and Louisiana did not, on this edition. If you have not taken a state’s quiz in five years, assume you will take it again when the row says so.

## How not to quote this brief in 2027

Fees move. The PDF’s path includes `/2026/07/`. A later upload will have a later month. If your recruiter’s number disagrees with the current PDF, the PDF wins. This magazine will not scrape the whole grid into HTML; the file is the grid.

## What the $50 is not

Not a license renewal in the home state. Not a CCC maintenance fee. Not malpractice insurance. Not a Medicare application. Not a family copay.

If someone says the compact is “free because ASHA wanted access,” show them Tennessee’s $150 line and sit quietly.

Budget a privilege the way you budget a license: Commission fee, state fee, quiz time, print appointment if the home board demands a new FBI set, and the days you will not be able to see patients while the dashboard thinks. A “fifty-dollar compact” quote that ignores Tennessee or a new-print requirement is a recruiting lie. Ask for the PDF page, not the slogan.

When a not-yet state lists TBD, do not prepay a rumor. When it lists a number, do not assume the number includes the Commission’s $50. The table’s columns are separate on purpose.

Keep a screenshot of the PDF’s date with the receipt. When a board later changes a fee, you will want to show which edition you paid. CompactConnect will not remember your hallway conversation with a recruiter.
