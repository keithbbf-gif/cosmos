# Aphasia telepractice reviews are encouraging — and protocol-thin

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The telepractice Evidence Map is a pile of reviews. Two aphasia cards are enough for a news brief. A systematic review of telerehabilitation after stroke (31 studies in the ASHA summary this desk read) found language-outcome improvements in 28. Four of six studies that compared remote to face-to-face reported no significant difference on the measures they used. Multimodal treatments were the cheerful subset. Lexical treatments were mixed-to-positive. That is a literature with a lean, not a product warranty.

A 2023 *AJSLP* scoping review (Weidner, Hall, and colleagues, in the usual citation trail) asked what assessment and intervention protocols have actually been used remotely. Most included papers were intervention. Two looked at assessment. The authors still supported telehealth as an alternate mode and then complained about **how little the protocols vary**. If every study is the same naming task on the same platform, you have not mapped the field. You have mapped a habit.

A 2025 telemedicine review of assessment psychometrics (Teti, Murray, et al., on the same map) is even more map-like: a handful of tools with excellent labels on some validity flavors and holes on others. English-only inclusion is a bias the authors admit.

## How this meets the CMS telehealth clock

Congress extended practitioner authority through 2027. These reviews are why a clinic can say remote aphasia work is not a dare. They are not why a clinic can skip bandwidth checks, caregiver training, or a plan for when the person cannot point to a picture on a laptop. They are not a 92507 modifier. They are not a compact privilege.

## What not to tell a spouse

Do not say “research proves Zoom is the same as the hospital.” Say: several reviews found similar language-test changes when the protocol was simple and the connection held; assessment research is thinner; we will still need an in-person look if the question is swallow, or if the screen fails the person.

Assessment thinness is the part programs skip. A treatment study that shows naming gains does not validate a remote Western Aphasia Battery in your language, on your laptop, with your lighting. The 2025 psychometrics review is a list of holes as much as a list of excellents. If you are going to evaluate remotely, pick a tool that actually has remote data and say so in the report. “We did the usual battery on Zoom” is not a methods section.

Bandwidth notes belong in the chart. A dropped call in the middle of a repetition task is a methods problem, not a personality problem. Write it down so the next clinician does not think the score is a soul.
