# The GPCI floor is a locality story hiding inside a national raise

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

National conversion factors make good headlines. Geographic practice cost indices make payroll. The CY 2026 final rule says CMS is updating GPCIs and malpractice RVUs as the statute requires. ASHA’s SLP booklet adds the sentence rural and small-city clinics actually needed: elimination of the **GPCI floor** will reduce payment in specific localities, with ASHA dating the bite to **31 January 2026** in its November reading. In some towns, that geographic cut can eat the legislative increase.

This desk will not invent a list of losing localities. That list belongs in the CMS GPCI files and in your MAC’s fee lookup. The news is the mechanism.

## What a floor was doing

A GPCI adjusts work, practice expense, and malpractice RVUs for local cost. A statutory floor kept some indices from falling as far as the raw data wanted. Remove the floor and a low-cost (on paper) locality sees a smaller multiplier. The conversion factor can rise in Baltimore and still fall in the county that lost its floor.

ASHA’s political ask — write Congress — is about this class of quiet cut, plus sequestration, plus the efficiency adjustment. You can decline the politics and still owe your board a locality printout.

## How to check without a consultant

CMS publishes locality-adjusted files. ASHA’s outpatient-payment pages explain how to retrieve a rate. Pull 92507 and your two most common evaluation codes for 2025 and 2026 in *your* locality. If the national booklet said +2 percent and your file says minus, you have met the GPCI story.

Do not average three neighboring towns. Do not use a cash-pay “Medicare rate” from a blog. Do not tell a patient that “Washington raised speech therapy” if your remittance shrank.

## What this is not

Not a compact map. Not a Medicaid school rate. Not proof that your rent is low. GPCI is a federal formula about relative cost, not a compliment.

If a recruiter promises “2026 Medicare is up 3.3 percent everywhere,” send them this brief and the CMS fact sheet in the same email.

Rural clinics feel this first. The national story is a raise. The locality file is a shrug. If you are the only outpatient SLP in a floor-losing county, do not let a hospital system in a high-GPCI city tell you that “we all got 3 percent.” Pull both files. Bring both printouts. The conversation gets shorter.

ASHA’s advocacy ask — write Congress — is about this class of quiet cut. You can skip the letter and still owe payroll an honest model. A budget that used the national conversion-factor percent as a revenue forecast is a fiction. Replace it before the first remittance of February.
