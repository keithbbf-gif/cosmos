# ASHA’s generative-AI pages say the model is not the clinician

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

ASHA did not wait for a statute. The association’s public page on generative artificial intelligence for audiology and speech-language pathology clinicians opens with Principle II of the 2023 Code of Ethics: certificate holders have to consider and evaluate the technology they use. Then it says the sentence vendors skip. AI cannot replace the audiologist, the speech-language pathologist, or the assistant. It might support individualized care if someone actually reads the output.

That is the news. Not a product launch. A refusal.

## What the pages actually require

The hub page is cautious about law. It notes limited legislative and regulatory oversight, flags that some states have started to write their own rules, and points readers at federal AI documents from 2025 and 2026 without pretending those documents are a scope of practice. Efficacy claims, ASHA says, need evidence. A chatbot that “writes IEPs” is a marketing sentence until someone shows outcomes.

The ethics-considerations page gets more concrete. Principle III, Rule E is the accuracy duty: clients, patients, and students get accurate information about services. If a tool drafts a treatment note or a family handout, the clinician still owns the words. ASHA’s paraphrase of good practice is unglamorous: know the tool, read the terms and the privacy policy, keep protected health information out of the prompt, then edit. Add the data the model cannot know. Delete the confident error.

The worked example on that page is a family handout about written language disorders, not a progress note. The clinician points the model at ASHA public pages, asks for plain language and a short FAQ, and refuses to treat the first draft as a handout. That is the whole method. It is also why this magazine will not paste ASHA’s sample prompt into a “copy me” box as if it were a protocol.

## Ambient scribes are still a signature problem

ASHA names ambient listening products that draft notes. The ethical move is the same as for a student clinician’s draft: the certificate holder reviews, corrects, and signs. A clinic that lets the scribe post without a human pass has not automated documentation. It has automated a Board of Ethics fact pattern.

None of this is a ban. The hub page says many clinicians are already experimenting. The association promises more articles, symposia, and professional-development hours. Experimentation without a review step is the failure mode.

## What this is not

Not a CMS coverage decision. Medicare has not, in the documents this desk read for wave 2, created an “AI-scribe” CPT that replaces 92507. Not a compact rule. Not permission to put a child’s voice sample into a consumer model. Not a claim that SLPWOW endorses any vendor.

If your administrator says “ASHA says we have to buy a scribe,” open the URL. ASHA said you have to remain the clinician.
