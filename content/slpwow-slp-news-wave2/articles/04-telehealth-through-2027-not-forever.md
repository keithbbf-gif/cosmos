# Congress extended Medicare telehealth for SLPs through 2027 — not forever

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The useful sentence is a date. Under H.R. 7148, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, Medicare telehealth authority for audiologists and speech-language pathologists runs through **31 December 2027**. ASHA’s 2026 news post says the provision was delinked from short government-funding cliffs, so a later appropriations fight is less likely to snap the authority off at midnight. That is a real operational win. It is still a sunset.

ASHA’s own headline keeps the second clause: the association continues to push for permanence. The Public Policy Agenda asked for permanent coverage and payment parity. Congress bought two years. Do not let a vendor webinar turn those two years into “telehealth is solved.”

## Two clocks, one clinic

There is a **statutory clock** and a **fee-schedule clock**.

The statutory clock is who may furnish a telehealth service to a Part B beneficiary. During the public-health emergency, and in the extensions that followed, audiologists and SLPs were written into temporary authority. ASHA’s earlier 2026 alert, written when a January 30 funding deadline still threatened a lapse, is the scar. H.R. 5371 had carried authority only through that January date. H.R. 7148 moved the wall to the last day of 2027.

The fee-schedule clock is which codes sit on the Medicare Telehealth Services List and how they are billed. CMS’s CY 2026 Physician Fee Schedule final rule streamlined how services get onto that list and, in ASHA’s November 2025 reading, finalized permanent *list* treatment for speech-language pathology codes even while Congress had not yet finished the practitioner-authority fight. Those two documents can both be true. A code can be listable and a profession can still need a statute to stay on the call.

CMS later revised the Therapy Services page to say that section 6209 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, extends the ability of physical therapists, occupational therapists, and speech-language pathologists to furnish telehealth services, including telephone assessment and management codes 98966–98968, through 31 December 2027. That CMS sentence is the one to print for a billing meeting. ASHA’s news post is the one to print for a Hill meeting.

## What the extension does not do

It does not make every commercial plan match Medicare. It does not make a compact privilege unnecessary when the patient is in another state — practice is still where the patient is. It does not decide supervision rules for assistants (Medicare still does not cover SLPA services as skilled therapy). It does not bless audio-only for every code. It does not set your locality rate.

It also does not mean ASHA stopped asking. The association’s post recites the work: bipartisan permanence bills, repeated short extensions, nearly 20,000 member messages. That is advocacy history. It is not a coverage determination.

## How to write the staff note

“Medicare Part B telehealth for SLP services is authorized through 31 December 2027 under the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026. Confirm the code is on the current Telehealth Services List and that the patient’s location and modality match CMS and MAC rules. After 2027 the authority lapses unless Congress acts again. Private plans are a separate contract.”

If someone says “ASHA made telehealth permanent,” send them the ASHA URL. The association did not say that. It said the opposite, politely.
