# Late talking is a risk description, not a lifelong label

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

“Late talker” is a waiting-room word. CATALISE phase 1 tried to put numbers under it. Many children with a small expressive vocabulary at 18–24 months catch up without a special program. Research, the panel said, is bad at picking which individual child will not. The children at greater risk of a lasting problem are late talkers who also understand poorly, gesture poorly, or have a family history of language impairment. Even then, prediction for one name on a caseload is unreliable. Unless the problem is already severe, **reassess in about six months**.

That paragraph is why this magazine will not sell a late-talker kit. It is also why “wait and see” as a closed door is malpractice-adjacent, and “panic and enroll” as a reflex is a different kind of harm.

## What later reviews added

The 2024 German-language guideline review in *Deutsches Ärzteblatt* (Neumann and colleagues) treats children in the third year with developmental language delay as at risk for later DLD, and it reports effect sizes for parent training and for language therapy that vary by whether the delay is expressive-only or includes comprehension. It also complains that German intervention research is thin, that parent training is underused, and that therapy often starts too late. Those are health-system sentences from one country. They are not your school district’s eligibility manual. They are evidence that “late talker” is a window, not a brand.

The 2025 *Frontiers in Pediatrics* screening review (next brief) is the other half: tools are weak at age 2, better around 2.5, and DLD as a stable label is more comfortable near 4.

## How to talk to a caregiver

Name what you see: words, comprehension, gesture, play, hearing, languages at home. Name the follow-up date. Do not say “it’s just a late talker” as if that ended the story. Do not say “this is DLD” at 20 months because a blog used the letters.

Hearing testing is not optional because a milestone app was green. Two languages at home are not a delay.

## What this is not

Not a treatment protocol. Not a promise that parent training is always paid. Not CDC’s 75-percent checklist, which is a different instrument with a different job.

If you run a parent-coaching group, say what the 2024 review actually claimed: some parent-training effects are real, especially when the delay is expressive, and the research base in that country is still too small. Do not sell the group as a cure for DLD. Do not refuse a child who needs direct therapy because a guideline somewhere liked parents. CATALISE’s six-month reassessment is a calendar, not a coupon for delay.

Two languages, ear fluid, and a quiet household are not moral failings. They are part of the look. Write them down. Then keep the follow-up date anyway.
