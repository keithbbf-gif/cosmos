# 2026 is the year Medicare grew a second conversion factor

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

Since 1992 the Physician Fee Schedule has multiplied relative value units by one conversion factor and called it a rate. Calendar year 2026 breaks the habit. The CY 2026 final rule (CMS-1832-F), posted 31 October 2025, implements a statutory split: one factor for qualifying alternative-payment-model participants, one for everyone else.

CMS’s fact sheet rounds the finals to **$33.57** (qualifying APM, about +3.77 percent from the prior $32.35) and **$33.40** (non-qualifying, about +3.26 percent). Trade groups that keep more decimals print $33.5675 and $33.4009. ASHA’s SLP booklet uses $33.57 and $33.40 and tells the profession the obvious: most speech-language pathologists are not qualifying APM participants. Use $33.40 before locality.

MACRA froze the annual update at zero through 2025, then promised 0.75 percent for qualifying APM clinicians and 0.25 percent for the rest. A later statute — ASHA names the One Big Beautiful Bill Act — added a one-year **2.50 percent** bump for 2026. CMS also describes a small positive adjustment (about 0.49 percent in the fact sheet) to keep the books aligned with work-RVU changes. Add those pieces and you get two larger-looking conversion factors that still sit on top of sequestration, efficiency adjustments, and geographic floors that may be disappearing.

The political sentence is that Congress “raised rates.” The operational sentence is that you now have to know whether you are a QP. If you do not know, you are almost certainly on $33.40.

## A conversion factor is not a take-home

ASHA’s booklet is useful here because it refuses the press-release mood. CMS’s own impact story for SLPs is a small negative on top of the conversion-factor arithmetic. Sequestration is still two percent. Some localities lose a GPCI floor. Some codes lose 2.5 percent of work RVU to the efficiency adjustment. ASHA’s “could approach 4 percent” warning is advocacy math. CMS’s fact sheet does not print that 4 percent. Both can be on your desk if you label them.

## How to calculate without lying

Payment is still (work RVU × work GPCI + PE RVU × PE GPCI + MP RVU × MP GPCI) × conversion factor, with extra cuts for MPPR and with the KX modifier after the threshold. If your biller multiplies an old 2025 rate by 3.26 percent and calls it 2026, they have skipped every RVU change. Use the CMS locality file or ASHA’s method for looking up locality, then test the codes you actually bill.

Do not publish ASHA’s national table in a blog. Point at it.

## What this is not

Not an APM enrollment guide. Not a reason to tell families that “Medicare paid more so we raised cash prices.” Not a compact issue. Not a school Medicaid rate.

If someone in a Facebook group says “the conversion factor is $33.57,” ask if they are a qualifying APM participant. If the answer is a blank stare, they have the wrong factor.
