# IDDSI is a texture framework — not a Medicare meal ticket

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative gave kitchens, SLPs, and nurses a shared ladder for drinks and foods. Hospitals like a common noun. Researchers like a scale they can write into a methods section. A 2025 *Frontiers in Nutrition* paper validating a Polish Functional Oral Intake Scale used IDDSI’s functional diet scale beside FEES and the Penetration-Aspiration Scale. That is how IDDSI shows up in the literature now: as a comparator, not as a miracle.

This desk will not reprint IDDSI’s level names as a household menu. Thickener recipes and “try honey drink at home” are out of bounds here. The news is institutional. A tray sticker is not an instrumental swallow study. A PAS score is not an IDDSI level. A FOIS score is not a reason to skip the SLP.

## What CMS actually pays attention to

In a SNF, PDPM’s SLP component cares whether the MDS found a swallowing disorder and a mechanically altered diet. Those are assessment-item facts, not an IDDSI audit. You can have a perfect IDDSI kitchen and a blank K0100. You can have a K-section that says “altered” and a tray that is still a guess. The payment flag and the safety system are friends only if someone makes them talk.

Home-health PDGM will not add visits because a level changed. Part B will not pay 92526 because the cardstock is color-coded.

## What the 2025 validation paper is good for

It is good for reminding people that oral-intake scales, FEES, and diet frameworks can move together in a cohort — the Polish group reported a strong inverse correlation between FOIS-PL and PAS, and high inter-rater agreement between a dietitian and an SLP. It is not a U.S. coverage decision. It is not your facility’s policy until your facility says so.

## How to talk in the kitchen

Use the framework your building adopted. Say when you do not know because no one has looked in the throat or at a fluoroscopy screen. Do not let a laminated pyramid replace a person.

Training kitchens on IDDSI is still worth doing. Shared nouns prevent a nurse from saying “nectar” and a kitchen from hearing “honey.” Just keep the noun attached to an assessment date. A level that is six months old after a new stroke is folklore.

This magazine will not publish a household conversion chart. If a family asks what to cook tonight, the answer is: ask the treating SLP who has seen the swallow, and do not thicken from a social video. That is a safety sentence, not a recipe.
