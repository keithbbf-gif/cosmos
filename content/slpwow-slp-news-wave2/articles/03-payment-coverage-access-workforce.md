# Payment, coverage, access, workforce — four PPA verbs that are not one story

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The 2025–2026 Public Policy Agenda is a stack of verbs. Expand and permanently cover telehealth. Protect payment. Keep licensure standards interoperable. Operationalize the compact. A social post collapses them into “ASHA is fighting for you.” The PDF keeps them in separate bullets for a reason. They fail in different buildings.

## Payment is a conversion factor and a threshold

Medicare outpatient speech-language pathology rides the Physician Fee Schedule. Calendar year 2026 introduced two conversion factors and left the KX modifier threshold at $2,480 for physical therapy and speech-language pathology combined. Those numbers live on CMS pages and in ASHA’s November 2025 fee-schedule analysis. The PPA does not print them. It says ASHA will keep working with Medicare, Medicaid, Tricare, and private plans, and will keep writing tools so members can survive the case-level mess.

If your clinic’s problem is a 2026 locality rate or an efficiency adjustment on a diagnostic code, the agenda is the wrong PDF. Open the CMS fact sheet and the ASHA booklet. Then write Congress if you want the statutory update changed. That last step is advocacy. It is not billing.

## Coverage is a benefit category, not a vibe

“Cover telehealth” in the agenda means two different fights. One is Medicare Part B authority for audiologists and SLPs to furnish listed services through a telecommunications system — extended through 2027 by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, not made permanent. The other is payment parity and a full scope on the telehealth list, which ASHA is still asking for. A state Medicaid agency can cover school-based speech under 42 CFR 440.110 and still refuse a telepractice code. Those are different coverage stories. The agenda names the federal one first.

## Access is a person who cannot get in the door

The agenda’s workforce paragraph is the most emotional sentence in the packet: needs growing, members overburdened, communities underserved. That is a description, not a caseload cap. ASHA’s ask on this point is licensure interoperability — remind stakeholders that licenses exist to protect the public, hold the education line, and stand up the compact so a clinician can follow a patient across a state line.

The compact is the access tool the agenda can actually point at. It is also the tool most often misread. Thirty-seven jurisdictions had enacted ASLP-IC language by September 2026. Four were issuing privileges. Operationalize, in the agenda’s verb, still meant “make the rest of the map real.”

## Workforce is not a school posting

A district that cannot hire an SLP is living the agenda’s complaint. The agenda does not set a recommended caseload. ASHA has other documents for workload. This desk will not pretend the PPA replaced them. If you need hiring, look at the compact, at SLPA rules in *your* state, and at whether Medicare still refuses assistant billing (it does). Those are later briefs.

## How to use the four verbs in a staff note

Write four lines, not one:

1. Payment: check the 2026 conversion factor that applies to you (almost always the non-APM $33.40 national figure before locality).
2. Coverage: check whether the visit is Medicare telehealth, Medicaid school-based, or a private plan with its own list.
3. Access: check the Compact Map, not a conference rumor.
4. Workforce: do not treat a shortage headline as permission to skip supervision or to bill an assistant to Medicare.

The PPA is the association’s shopping list. The receipts are elsewhere.
