# The 2026 practice-expense shift is built for offices — and SLP is paid that way anyway

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

CMS’s CY 2026 fact sheet spends its practice-expense section on a demographic change: fewer physicians in independent offices, more employed by hospitals, and an old PE formula that still treated the facility setting as if the doctor kept a full office. The agency is not adopting the AMA’s new practice-cost survey for 2026 — sample size and response problems, it says — but it is changing the allocation. Indirect costs will weigh more heavily in the **non-facility** setting and more lightly in the **facility** setting. Facility PE tied to work RVUs drops to half the non-facility treatment.

Specialties that live in hospitals will feel a PE cut. Specialties that live in offices will feel a PE raise.

## The SLP twist

Speech-language pathology outpatient services under Part B are paid at the **non-facility** rate regardless of setting. ASHA’s booklet repeats the statutory quirk and then draws the conclusion: the 2026 PE redistribution is, on average, a **positive** for SLP payments under the MPFS. You can treat in a hospital outpatient department and still be priced like an office for this fee schedule.

That does not mean the hospital’s own OPPS bill is an SLP office bill. It means the professional SLP line on the PFS uses the non-facility PE. Do not tell a CFO that “CMS just raised hospital SLP.” Tell them the PFS PE formula moved, and SLP already sat on the side of the formula that got heavier.

## What CMS refused to do

The AMA handed over updated Physician Practice Information and Clinician Practice Information survey data. CMS modeled it, published impacts, and walked away for 2026. If those data return in a later rule, PE could move again. This year’s story is the allocation change, not the new survey.

## How to use the fact

If you are modeling 2026, do not apply a hospital-specialty PE cut to your SLP column. If you share a building with facility-heavy physicians, their mood and your remittance may diverge. That is not a math error.

Still run locality files. PE is only one third of an RVU stack, and MPPR still eats PE on the second procedure of the day.

Hospital outpatient departments should hear the other half. Your SLP line on the PFS may look a little better because of non-facility PE. Your building’s OPPS claim is a different book. Do not let a therapy director spend the PE tailwind twice. And do not tell employed physicians that “CMS is helping offices, so we should all move outpatient.” The PE story is a formula. It is not a real-estate strategy.

If the AMA survey data return in a later year, reread this brief before you reuse the 2026 sentence. Allocation and survey adoption are different switches. CMS left one off.
