# Medical necessity is still the sentence the KX is promising

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

CMS’s Therapy Services page, quoting the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018, says the KX modifier is a confirmation that services are medically necessary and that the record can justify them. That is the whole doctrine. The 2026 dollar amount ($2,480 combined for PT and SLP) is an index. The doctrine is older than the index.

ASHA’s Medicare FAQs, written for people who have to put codes on a 1500 form, keep circling the same drain: if it is not documented, a payer can deny it. Dates, who treated, where, how long, the condition, the skilled work. School Medicaid has its own list. Part B has its own. The genre is the same.

A note that says “patient tolerated treatment well” is a weather report. A note that says what was assessed, what was trained, what changed, and why a qualified SLP had to be the one in the room is a Medicare sentence. This magazine will not hand you a phrase bank. Phrase banks become the next audit finding.

Maintenance therapy has a narrower Medicare theory than people wish. Improvement is not always required; skill still is. If you are holding a swallow in place against expected decline, say that, and say what you did that an unskilled person could not. If you cannot say it, you are in social-visit territory.

## Families and the word “cap”

Families still hear “cap.” Tell them the 2018 truth: there is a dollar flag, not a hard stop, and the flag is shared with physical therapy. Tell them a reviewer can still ask why. Do not tell them a blogger’s “magic KX language” will save a hollow chart.

## What this is not

Not a compact brief. Not a research protocol. Not permission to copy last year’s goals forward without a new question. Not legal advice if your MAC has an LCD that names specific documentation elements — open the LCD.

A decent habit: one functional sentence the family would recognize, one skilled action only you could have done, one reason the next visit still needs you. That is not a template. It is a test. If you cannot fill those three without copying last Tuesday, you may be done — or you may be bored. Boredom is not a benefit category. Neither is fear of the KX.

If therapy is ending, write the ending. A chart that just stops is how reviews become worse than they needed to be.

Share the ending with the family in ordinary words: what you were working on, what is safer now, what to watch, whom to call if it slides. That conversation is not a CMS form. It is the part of medical necessity that the person can actually use. Then write that you had it.
