# Practice is where the patient is — the compact says so on the home page

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

The last official sentence on the Commission’s “about” block is the one telepractice programs keep trying to bargain with. Practice of audiology and speech-language pathology occurs in the state where the patient or client is located at the time of the encounter.

If you sit in Louisiana and the child is in a grandparent’s kitchen in a non-issuing state, you need that state’s authorization theory — usually a full license — not a Louisiana home-state glow. If the child is in Tennessee and Tennessee is issuing, a privilege into Tennessee is the compact path. The camera does not drag the child to Baton Rouge.

## Two tele- stories that are not one

Medicare telehealth authority through 2027 is a **payer** story about Part B beneficiaries and listed codes. The compact patient-location rule is a **licensure** story. You can be perfectly legal on a Medicare telehealth claim and illegal on a state-board complaint, or the reverse. ASHA’s permanence campaign does not rewrite the compact sentence.

Employers who say “we’re a compact company, take anyone in a compact-enacted state” are skipping the issuing column *and* the location rule. Enacted-but-not-issuing is not a privilege. A privilege into Ohio does not cover a patient who drove to Pennsylvania for the week.

## Families

If you travel, tell the clinician where you will actually sit. A session from a hotel in a state the clinician cannot enter is not a cute exception. It is unlicensed practice with a pediatric witness.

## What this is not

Not a ban on telepractice. The compact lists telehealth as a reason it exists. It is a ban on pretending geography dissolved. Not a HIPAA lecture, though the same session has one. Not a CMS place-of-service how-to.

School telepractice adds a third geography: the child may be in a district that sits on a state line, or at a grandparent’s after a snow day. The IEP does not move the compact border. If the session’s IP address is in a state you cannot enter, stop and reschedule. That sounds harsh. Unlicensed practice in a special-education file is harsher.

Document the patient’s location the way you already document consent. “Home” is not a state. “Home in parish X, Louisiana” is.

Snowbirds and custody weeks are the adult version of the same problem. Ask where the person will sit next Tuesday. If they do not know, do not guess a privilege. Reschedule until the state has a name.

If two children in a sibling session sit in two states, you need two authorization theories. That is rare and it is not cute. Split the session or get the second privilege. Do not average the zip codes.
