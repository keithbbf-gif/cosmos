# New RTM codes landed on the therapy list — they are not 92507

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

Remote therapeutic monitoring is the 2026 fee-schedule topic most likely to arrive in your inbox as a product demo. CMS’s Therapy Services page is drier. For CY 2026 the agency added **98979, 98984, and 98985** to the list of codes that sometimes or always describe therapy services, and it revised descriptors on **98976 and 98977**. The annual therapy-code list and dispositions are the files that matter. ASHA’s booklet has an RTM heading for the same reason: someone will ask whether an SLP can bill a dashboard.

“Sometimes therapy” means the code is therapy when a therapist furnishes it under a therapy plan of care, and something else when a physician or other qualified practitioner furnishes it under a different benefit theory. It does not mean “optional documentation.” It does not mean “this is speech treatment.” RTM codes describe monitoring and management of respiratory or musculoskeletal systems, or other RTM families as the descriptors read *this year*. Swallowing, language, and cognitive treatment are still 92526, 92507, 97129/97130, or whatever your MAC actually pays — not a renamed sensor.

If a vendor says their app “is 98985 for SLPs,” open the current CPT descriptor and the therapy-list disposition. If the descriptor does not match the clinical work, it is not your code.

## Telehealth and RTM are different pipes

The 2027 telehealth extension is about furnishing listed telehealth services. RTM is a family of remote-monitoring codes with their own time, device, and interactive requirements. Do not assume a compact privilege plus an app equals an RTM claim. Do not assume Medicare Advantage uses the same edits.

ASHA’s November 2025 booklet discusses RTM in the SLP context and then sends readers back to coding rules. This magazine will not invent a how-to. The news is that the therapy list moved, and that “moved onto the list” is not “indicated for your caseload.”

## What to tell a founder who wants a pilot

Ask for the code, the disposition, the device classification, the time documentation, and the plan-of-care theory. Ask whether an SLPA or an aide is touching the data (Medicare still will not call that skilled SLP therapy). Ask whether the patient is actually a Part B beneficiary. Then call the MAC. A news brief is not a coverage determination.

A fair test for an RTM pitch: can the clinician name the CPT descriptor from memory, name the minutes or days the code wants, and name who is looking at the feed? If the answer is “the app does it,” you do not have a code. You have a demo. Swallow sensors and language apps will keep arriving with RTM tattoos. Most of them will fail the descriptor test. That is allowed. What is not allowed is billing hope.

Keep 92507 for treatment. Keep 92526 for swallow treatment. Keep RTM in a folder labeled “maybe, after the MAC letter.”
