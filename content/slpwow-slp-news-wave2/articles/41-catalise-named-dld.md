# CATALISE named developmental language disorder — and dropped the IQ gap

*Educational news brief for SLPWOW. Not legal advice, not billing advice, and not a substitute for reading the primary source or for evaluation by a licensed clinician.*

For a generation the field argued about specific language impairment, language delay, developmental dysphasia, and whether a child with a low-ish nonverbal score “counted.” The CATALISE project — Criteria and Terminology Applied to Language Impairments: Synthesizing the Evidence — tried to end the argument with a Delphi panel instead of another slogan.

Phase 1 (*PLOS One*, 2016) worked on identification. Phase 2 (Bishop and colleagues, 2017, open in the Oxford archive) worked on names. The panel, 57 people across professions and countries, reached at least 78 percent agreement on 19 of 21 statements and folded them into 12 consensus lines.

## The names that stuck

**Language disorder** is the profile that impairs everyday life and carries a poor prognosis. **Developmental language disorder (DLD)** is that profile when it is **not** tied to a known biomedical condition. Risk factors — neurobiological or environmental — do **not** block the DLD label. DLD **may** sit next to other neurodevelopmental diagnoses, ADHD included. DLD does **not** require a gap between verbal and nonverbal ability.

That last sentence is the one that still has not reached every eligibility meeting. A child can have DLD and a nonverbal score that is not conveniently higher. The old SLI clubhouse is not the consensus.

## What CATALISE did not do

It did not write your state’s special-education category. It did not set a standard-score cutoff. It did not diagnose a late talker at 18 months. Phase 1 was explicit: many late talkers catch up; prediction for one child is poor; comprehension problems, poor gesture, and family history raise risk; reassess in about six months unless the problem is already severe.

German-language guidelines later used “DLD” more broadly, including children with comorbidities CATALISE would park under language disorder associated with a condition. If you read a 2024 *Deutsches Ärzteblatt* review, notice the footnote. Same letters, different fence.

## How to use the papers without a dump

Cite the open *PLOS One* article and the phase-2 PDF. Do not paste the 12 statements into a blog as if they were a test protocol. They are consensus sentences. Your assessment still has to be an assessment.

If a colleague says “we don’t say DLD, we say SLI,” ask them which document they are defending. Tradition is allowed. It is not CATALISE.

School eligibility categories in the United States were not rewritten by a Delphi panel in England. A child can meet CATALISE DLD and still fail a district’s “severe discrepancy” habit, or the reverse. Write both sentences in the report if you have to live in both systems. Do not pretend the IEP software knows Bishop et al. 2017.

Researchers who still recruit “SLI with a 15-point gap” should say so. Comparability with older papers is a real reason. Quietly relabeling those samples as DLD is how prevalence slides start to lie.
