# Bibliography — fig pests & diseases pack

Real pages and papers the drafts lean on. No invented trials. Dates are what the page showed when this folder was written (2026-09). Labels and registrations move. **`[VERIFY]`** before anyone treats a sentence as this year’s law.

## Extension and university

- LSU AgCenter. *Louisiana Plant Disease: Fig* (Pub. 1802, 2025 PDF). Cultural management; **no fungicides labeled for figs in Louisiana** in that document; rust cycle; root-knot notes (avoid recent tomato / okra / tobacco ground; organic matter; clean plants).
- LSU AgCenter. Ferrin, Don, and Charles Overstreet. *Fig Rust* (Pub. 3159). *Cerotelium fici*; litter spores; defoliation.
- LSU AgCenter. Ferguson, Mary Helen. “Netting is the Most Reliable Way to Protect Fruit from Birds.” 19 July 2025. Scare tactics habituate; stake netting to the ground.
- LSU AgCenter. “When Picking a Fig Variety, You Have Choices.” Celeste closed eye / cold hardy; LSU Purple foliage notes and cold sensitivity; LSU Gold open eye / cracking.
- LSU AgCenter. “History of the LSU AgCenter Fig Breeding Program.” LSU Gold: moderate resistance to fig leaf rust (relative). LSU Purple: leaf-disease talk in other LSU pages — do not upgrade to immune.
- LSU AgCenter. Pyzner, John R. *Figs for Commercial and Home Production in Louisiana* (Pub. 1529). Variety list; rust / thread blight context.
- University of Arkansas Division of Agriculture (UAEX) Plant Health Clinic. Smith, Sherrie, and Jason Pavel. “Fig Rust.” *Physopella fici* (older name); defoliation in two to three weeks; late flush and cold injury; historic **neutral copper** when first leaves full size, repeat 3–4 weeks.
- UF/IFAS. “Fig Leaves Dropping Early?” Gardening in the Panhandle, 18 Sept 2017. *Cerotelium fici*; once spots are obvious, chemical talk is late; rake; airflow; Florida prune-after-harvest note (state practice — do not export as Arkansas law).
- UF/IFAS. Noling, J. W. *Nematodes of Backyard Deciduous Fruit and Nut Crops in Florida* (ENY-055 / NG044). Root-knot on figs; inspect nursery roots; do not plant galled stock.
- UF older fig pest PDF (PG01000). Mosaic complexity; **no EPA-approved fungicides for edible figs in Florida** in that document; Armillaria / mushroom root rot cultural notes.
- Texas A&M AgriLife. *Texas Plant Disease Handbook* — Fig. Anthracnose, mosaic, rust (*Physopella fici*), leaf blight, limb blight, root-knot, souring, closed-eye table (Celeste, Texas Everbearing, Alma). Older chemical asides (Vapam, Maneb) are **not** to be copied as current homeowner recipes.
- Virginia Cooperative Extension. Burnett, Amy, and Jayesh B. Samtani. *Backyard Figs in Virginia* (SPES-760P, 2026). *Cerotelium fici*; mosaic + *Aceria ficus*; nematodes; rust lookalikes; copper/sulfur talk — still subject to label.
- NC State Extension. Fox, Gene. “My Fig Just Dropped All of the Leaves!” Beaufort County. Rust sanitation; copper mentioned in older pubs with label caution.
- NC State Extension. “Green June Beetle in the Landscape.” *Cotinis nitida*; fruit feeding; “fig-eater” common name in some regions; grubs and organic matter.
- NC State. Ritchie, Dave. “Fig Diseases in North Carolina.” Cold and nematodes as limits; no chemical control for established nematode plants (as cited in the FigRoots nematode draft).
- University of Georgia Extension. Krewer, G. W., and Floyd Hendrix. *Home Garden Figs* (C 945, rev. 2023). Water, fertility, spacing.
- University of Tennessee. *Figs for Tennessee Gardens and Landscapes* (D234-B). Birds, squirrels, raccoons; netting; pick at full ripe for fresh, slightly earlier for preserves.
- Walter Reeves (Georgia). Fig notes: birds the main pest; pie pans rarely work; netting is the sure tool.
- UC IPM. “Fig Mosaic” (home/landscape and agriculture). FMV / mite *Aceria fici* (also spelled *A. ficus*); graft and cutting transmission; not seedborne; no practical residential cure; replace a tree that will not perform.
- UC IPM. “Fig Mite.” Bud scales; virus vector; 20× lens.
- UC IPM. “Sour Rot of Fig” / “Sour Rot.” Yeasts; driedfruit beetle *Carpophilus hemipterus*; vinegar flies; no bactericide/yeasticide; sanitation.
- UC IPM. “Fig Beetle” (*Cotinis texana*). Adult fruit damage; larval organic matter; no chemical control recommended in that guideline.
- Oregon State / PNW Handbook. “Fig (*Ficus carica*)—Fig Mosaic Disease.” *Aceria ficus*; multiple associated viruses; clean stock.
- TAMU / Johnson 2015 figs PDF (as cited on FigRoots rust/nematode drafts). Rust as a major Texas leaf threat; sandy ground and root-knot. **[VERIFY]** PDF URL if you quote page numbers.
