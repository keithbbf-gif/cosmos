8a sits in the kind of map that still gets a leftover tropical rain. Not every year. Often enough that I have a walk I trust when the sun comes back and the figs look like they went through a wash.

The canopy drips for a day after the radar goes quiet. Fruit that was a day from jam is split or swollen or already smelling like a still. Leaves that were only thinking about rust now have a splash pattern. Clay holds a lake. A pot saucer holds a swamp. Mosquitoes write their own calendar. A neighbor wants a storm spray. I want a bucket.

I do not invent a storm spray. I close doors.

[Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already gave you the one-line: tight eye if August stays wet. This page is the week after the sky proved the sentence.

## Fruit doors

Split skins. Open eyes that were already generous. A smell starting. I pick what is ripe enough to finish inside. I take the wrecks off so sap beetles do not file a claim. I do not leave a windfall mattress. UC IPM’s sour-rot talk is sanitation and prompt harvest. No yeasticide. I will not write one because the parish got a band of rain.

Tight-eye names still split if the week was stupid. They just do it less. Celeste and the other closed-eye yard names earn their keep in a wet August. They are not a refund policy. I do not write a variety novel in the first twenty-four hours. I write a kitchen list.

Open-eye names I already knew were a rain risk. LSU Gold’s open eye and cracking talk lives in their variety pages. I do not upgrade that to a curse. I pick earlier if I have to. I finish them on the counter. I have done it. I will not turn it into a law.

Green June beetles will find the ferment. Driedfruit beetles and vinegar flies will carry yeast into the next ostiole. That is not a new animal the storm invented. That is the beetle posts in this series, louder. Morning walk. Bucket. Trash. I do not fog ripe fruit I will eat tonight. I do not fog it because kids and dogs use this grass.

Birds will find splitters. A dark ripe fig with a crack is a billboard. Dawn is still the harvest. A plastic owl did not get smarter in the rain.

If fruit is dropping while it is still firm, and the inside is mined, and it does not match souring or a bird, I do not start a group chat. I photograph it. I bag it. I call a desk. The black-fig-fly page is biosecurity, not a claim that a leftover tropical rain imported California. Do not mash a wet-week drop with a federal name for fun.

## Leaf doors

Rust splash on a wet interior. *Cerotelium fici* — older papers say *Physopella fici* — likes a closet. A week of wet leaves is a closet. I do not copper a dripping canopy. Contact metal on a wet map you can already see is late talk even in a state that still mentions copper. UF/IFAS said once the spots are obvious, chemistry is late. LSU 2025 said Louisiana has no labeled fig fungicide in that document. I will not invent a storm exception. **[VERIFY]** nothing on a dripping Saturday.

I wait until I can walk without a lake. Then I flip leaves so I know what I am raking when they drop. Underside. Thumb. Grit is rust. Paint is mosaic. A webby early-season look is the thread-blight sort, and a leftover rain in May is not the same calendar as a leftover rain in August. Date the leaf.

I do not mow the rust quilt into the root zone. That is burial. LSU said collect and destroy. I bag. I do not compost a rust blanket against the trunk and call it feeding the soil.

A cooked pot that sat in a saucer of rain is a drowned pot. Dump the saucer. Raise the pot. That is not nematodes. That is a dish. The top wilted because the roots sat in a lake. If the wood is thick and the wilt stays after the dish is empty, I look at the collar before I write a novel. Ordinary sour rot of roots is enough. Armillaria is a different funeral. I do not mash them because both can follow wet.

## Root doors

Clay that holds a lake: I do not add more hose. I do not till. I let it breathe. A fig in a puddle is a rot risk. A fig on a mound I already built is why I built the mound. [Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) already talks pots, beds, shade, water. This week is why a flat clay hole was a bad idea in May.

I do not fertilize a tree standing in a lake “to help it recover.” Recovery is air in the pore space, not a nitrogen bump into a late flush if we are already near fall.

Sand that flushed and then baked: the opposite week comes fast. The sun returns. The surface turns to crust. Knotted roots will tell on you at noon. The storm did not invent the galls. It invented the wilt on damaged pipe. Water when the lake is gone. Depth, not a daily sprinkle. The irrigation page owns the hose. This page owns the week the hose has to shut up, then speak again.

I do not bleach-irrigate a hole because I am angry at the sky.

## Insect doors

Mosquitoes are not a fig pest. They are a reason I will not stand in the drip line at dusk waving oil. They are also a reason I dump saucers and any bucket I left out. That is not a fig fungicide. That is how you live in 8a after a rain week.

Green June will find the ferment. So will flies. Sanitation is the meeting I can cancel.

Scale did not wash off. Mites may have drowned on a leaf and then rebuilt on the dry wind that follows. I do not oil a wet canopy. I look later, in the shop and on the pot block, when I can see a bump.

Ants will farm whatever still leaks sugar. I follow them. I do not fog the column as if it were the disease.

## What I will not do

A bleach walk. A hydrogen peroxide “soil drench.” A cinnamon line. Those are how these posts define a fake cure.

A “fungicide just in case.” Just in case is how you write residue on fruit you will eat, drift on a toy, and a use fig may not even be on. Label is law. The rain did not print a new one.

A fertilizer bump to “help them recover” into a late flush if we are already near fall. UAEX already told you a rust strip plus a second spring is a winter bill. A rain week in September is not a reason to feed.

A Facebook ID from a muddy leaf. Photograph it when it dries enough to show the underside. Bag it if the tree is failing. The county photo page is the shot list.

A storm exception for a backyard bait, a fruit-tree cocktail, or a copper personality. The kids-and-dogs page still applies when the grass is wet. Especially then. Kids play in it.

## The next clear morning

Fruit first — it is the perishable. Ripe, almost-ripe I can finish inside, wrecks off the tree and off the ground. Necklace gone. Beetle cafeteria closed.

Water situation second — lake or dust. Dump dishes. Do not hose clay. Do not forget sand when it bakes. Weight a pot before you write a virus.

Leaves third — flip, do not fog. Note rust, paint, web, sunburn. Rake what is already down. Leave a dripping map for the next dry walk if you cannot bag soup.

Notes fourth — which name split, which name held, which pot drowned, which hole ponded. That note is next year’s variety talk and next year’s site talk. It is not a hex. It is how a tight-eye name earns another hole, and how an open-eye name stays a pot if I will not pick twice a day.

Wood fifth, if a limb broke. Clean cut when I can see what I am doing. Do not leave a torn hanging strap as a porch. The saw-and-prune page owns the rest. I do not sculpt in mud.

## What the week actually was

Storms are allowed. They are not a new disease. They are a week that opens every door these posts already named — split, sour, rust splash, drowned roots, beetles on ferment, birds on a crack.

Close the ones with a bucket. Leave the jug in the shed. The sun will come back. The necklace will not, if you picked it.

I have skipped that walk because I was tired and the mosquitoes were a vote. I have also paid for the skip with a week of sap beetles and a rust quilt I mowed into the crown. The clear morning is the program. 8a will send another rain. I want the doors shut before it does.
