The FigRoots magazine pack already split split vs sour. I am not pasting it. I am writing the pest version: **souring has a taxi.**

UC IPM: yeasts and bacteria, a sour odor, water-soaked flesh, rancid drip from the eye. Driedfruit beetles and vinegar flies carry the mess. **No bactericide or yeasticide is recommended.** Pick. Clean up. Let sun hit the ground under the tree.

TAMU’s souring table called Celeste, Alma, and Texas Everbearing resistant, Magnolia and Kadota susceptible. Closed eye versus open eye. LSU says Celeste resists splitting and souring. Gold’s open eye is a wet-week warning. Relative words. Not a spell.

Maneb showed up in an old TAMU handbook as a “help.” I will not copy that onto a hose. Stale chemistry is how blogs get people in trouble.

## What I smell on the dawn walk

A brewery. Not rust. Not mosaic. If you need a lab to tell you a necklace is sour, you are arguing for fun.

Pink jelly at the ostiole. Bubbles. A white scum later. Fruit that hangs like a rag. Sap beetles in the eye if you look. Vinegar flies if the week is loud.

I walk after a two-inch rain and a hot night. That is 8a souring weather. The Gulf leftover does not have to be a named storm. A still wet pocket and a 90° morning will finish the job.

Split fruit can sour second. Split is a skin-and-water story. Sour is a microbe-and-taxi story. They date the same storm often enough that people mash them. Mash them in the trash if you want. Do not mash them in a spray.

A bird peck is a door. A raccoon grab leaves rinds. A green June chews a shoulder. The little nitidulid walks into whatever door is open and brings yeast the yard already had. Name the first mouth. Then take the necklace off so the second shift does not clock in.

## Endosepsis is not your Celeste

California caprifigs and *Blastophaga* and *Fusarium* endosepsis are a different file. Common figs in Arkansas are mule flowers. We do not run a wasp. I will not import orchard panic from a Smyrna chapter into a backyard turkey. If you are actually running caprifigs, you already know you are not the reader of this sentence.

## How the taxi works

UC names *Carpophilus hemipterus*, the driedfruit beetle, and the vinegar flies that ride with them. They walk yeasts into a ripe fig. *Candida*, *Hanseniaspora*, *Pichia*, *Saccharomyces* — you do not need the Latin in the driveway. You need the no. There is no backyard yeasticide I will write. Killing every fly in the county is not a real plan.

An open eye. A split after rain. A peck. A June beetle hole. The ostiole on a ripe common fig is a door. Closed-eye types keep that door smaller. They still sour if you leave them past ripe in a steam-bath 8a week. They sour less often than a wide-open Magnolia-type story. Relative. Not a vaccine.

LSU Gold’s writeups mention a partially open eye and cracking after rain. That is a pick-sooner name, not a spray name.

A bird net with half-inch mesh will not keep a 3-millimeter beetle off. Do not ask one cloth to do every chapter. Frame the net for birds. Pick for yeast.

## The program in wet 8a

Plant closed-eye names for the trees you will not babysit.

Pick the open-eye names like they are milk.

Take the necklace off the same day. They do not stay as “maybe jam.” They are beetle bait. Trash or a hot compost that is **not** under the tree. Last year’s fruit dump is next year’s chafer room. That is a different draft. Same walk.

Do not leave peaches and tomatoes to rot in the same garden as if beetles read crop labels. UC said remove fallen and overripe fruit of any kind.

Do not fog ripe fruit. You are about to eat the skin.

Do not blame a virus. Mosaic paint lives on the leaf. Pink syrup lives in the eye.

Let sun hit the ground under the tree. A wet rust quilt plus a fruit mattress is two nurseries. Rake. Do not till the mess in and call it organic matter.

Water the dirt, not the midnight leaf. A wet room helps more than yeast. It helps rust. It helps the leaf blights TAMU named. Morning water if you have to go overhead.

## Tight eye is not tight enough to skip the walk

I have soured a Celeste by leaving it. Resistance is a smaller door, not a locked vault. Heat and rain and a late plane ticket will still write a necklace.

I have also picked a Gold a day early after a storm and finished it on the counter. Tennessee’s note said full ripe for fresh, a little earlier for preserves. That is ordinary English. It is not a fake cure.

If a name necklaces every August no matter how early you pick, retire the name for this hole. The yeast will still be in the county. The door does not have to be yours.

## What I will not write

I will not write a yeasticide.

I will not copy Maneb or any stale handbook chemistry onto fruit.

I will not write carbaryl or malathion on ripe figs you intend to eat as casual backyard advice.

I will not hang a soda-bottle trap and call the tree saved. Orchard bait traps exist for monitoring. A backyard bottle is a hobby. If it makes you walk the tree, fine. If it replaces the pick, it is a toy.

I will not mash anthracnose’s pink spore mass with sour syrup. Sunken fruit with a powder is a different rot. Smell. Look. If you cannot sort it, bag a fruit for the clinic. Do not invent a weekly fungicide because you learned a third Latin name. LSU Pub. 1802 (2025) said no fungicides labeled for figs in Louisiana in that document. **[VERIFY]** your own state. A contact metal will not turn a fermented necklace back into jam.

## The curse story

The curse story is appealing because it has no homework. Yeast has homework. The taxi has homework. You have a bucket. Use it before you write a hex.

A closed-eye Celeste after a quiet dry week can look like you won. Then a Thursday storm and a Friday you spent in town and the same tree writes a necklace. I have done that. The homework is not a personality. It is a bucket on a hook by the door.

I have stood in that driveway with a neighbor who already bought a bottle that said fruit tree. We smelled the necklace. We picked. We did not spray. The next morning the tree was still a tree. The brewery was in the trash. That is the program. It does not look like one on a shelf.
