Arkansas’s rust note has a sentence people treat as decoration. Defoliation may make the tree put on foliage late, and that growth goes into winter soft. Then cold has an easier job.

Zone **8a** is not a tropical fig joke. Average annual extreme in the teens. We still get nights that settle an argument with a variety that was bred for a milder parish. A rust year that strips in August and a warm October that feels like a gift can stack into a March dieback people will blame on “the virus” or “the beetles.”

The virus did not do that. The beetles did not do that. The tree believed a second spring.

## What I see after a strip

Bare arms in September. A flush of lime-green leaves in a warm spell. People cheer. The tree is “coming back.” The tree is spending sugar it should be putting in wood.

I have wanted that cheer. I have also walked those arms after a January surprise and scraped brown. The rust was the first chapter. The late flush was the second. The freeze wrote the end.

UAEX did not invent that sequence to scare you off figs. They wrote it so you would not treat rust as only a cosmetic.

## What I will not do in October

I will not hit a rust-stripped tree with a nitrogen bump “to help it recover.” Recovery, in this climate, is lignified wood and a quiet top. Lawn fertilizer in October is how you buy a tender tip.

I will not water like July if the dirt is already wet from fall rain. I will not drought-stress a tree that has no leaves, either. Roots still live. Mulch still helps. The hose gets boring and correct.

I will not copper the new baby leaves as a personality. If you even have a legal copper use, the clinic window was May. October copper on a panic flush is how you burn the only green the tree has left.

I will not prune a second time “to shape the new growth.” Leave the leaves it made. They are late. They are still a factory if a freeze holds off. Cutting them for neatness is a hobby.

## What I will do

Rake the first drop so the fungus does not keep splashing a flush you did not want.

Let the tree harden. In our other FigRoots winter pieces we talk about not pushing a fig in September if you still want the wood in March. Rust years need that sentence more, not less.

Watch the forecast like a person who has lost wood. If an arctic shot is real, the in-ground tool trees get whatever wrap or mulch pile I already believe in for 8a — breathable, not a plastic sweat lodge. Pots move. I will not write a full winter-wrap manual here. That file already exists in the cuttings magazine pack. I am only saying: **a rust year is a winter year**.

If the late flush dies, cut the dead after you can see living green in spring. Not in a January mood. Cold-injured wood and limb blight like a wet stub. Clean cuts later. Disinfectant on tools between a canker and a clean tree is ordinary hygiene. I will not name a household bleach ratio as if it were a pesticide label.

## Variety talk without a vaccine

Some LSU releases were bred with leaf disease in the conversation. LSU Gold has a **moderate** rust-resistance note in the breeding history page. Moderate is not a winter waiver. A resistant leaf that still drops in a monstrous year can still flush late if October is foolish.

Celeste holds a lot of 8a yards because the wood and the eye make sense here. It still rusts some years. I do not upgrade any name to immune.

## The bill

Rust is a leaf tax. 8a winter is a wood tax. Pay the leaf tax with a rake and do not volunteer for the wood tax with October feed. If March looks rough, you are not starting a spray. You are counting live buds and deciding which trunks still earn a hole.
