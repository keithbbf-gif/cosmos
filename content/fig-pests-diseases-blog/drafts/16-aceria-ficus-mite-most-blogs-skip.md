UC IPM describes a mite you will not see on a porch. Two pairs of legs up front. Wedge-shaped. Pale. A fraction of a millimeter. *Aceria fici* in their spelling, *Aceria ficus* in others. Same animal in the bud scales and around the eye.

They also say the sentence blogs skip: feeding by **one** infectious mite can move fig mosaic virus to a healthy seedling. The virus is not in the egg. The mite picks it up by eating and keeps it through molts. Cuttings and grafts move the virus without any mite at all.

If you only remember one thing, remember the split. **Mite control is a spread story. It is not a cure story.**

I keep a cheap 20× loupe in the greenhouse on a nail. I do not keep a mosaic jug. The nail is the more honest tool.

## A morning with a lens, not a cart

I do not pretend I census eriophyids for fun. On a day I care — about a month after leaves are out — I pick a young leaf and a piece of bud trash and I go inside where the light is not a lie. UC said May in their calendar, which is California. Our 8a budbreak is its own clock. A warm March can put us in “their May” before the calendar agrees.

I am looking for russet, a faint bronze, a stunted interior flush. I am also looking for ordinary heat and wind. A bronze patch on a west leaf after a dry week can be weather. A stunted interior that never grew up can be a mite hotel, or a bad pot, or both.

If I cannot find a mite and the leaf is a painted oak-leaf, I still have mosaic. The virus does not require a visible mite convention on the day I walked out.

If I find a lot of bud-mite trouble and I am trying to keep a clean seedling clean, that is the only chapter where mite talk earns a product conversation. Product means **label**. I will not write Forbid, or a brand oil, as a FigRoots recipe. SEO notes in our own pillar list mentioned those names. This pack will not upgrade a mention into a spray card. **[VERIFY]** with a county agent if a collection in a greenhouse is getting eaten alive. A backyard Celeste with old mosaic does not become a pesticide project because I bought a lens.

I will not name a miticide as “what we use.” Labels move. Fig as a crop is a paperwork orphan. If fig (or the right group) is not on the label in this state in this year, it is not a fig spray.

## Winter buds are a hotel

PNW’s mosaic chapter said mites overwinter in buds and that cutting out buds in winter can lower numbers. I have cut dead tips. I have not sat in January carving every viable bud off a tree I want fruit from. That is a nursery sanitation idea, not a jam-tree idea.

If I am starting clean seedlings or a rare plant I believe is still clean, I am more religious. I do not park it in the canopy of a painted old turkey if I can help it. Distance is a habit. The mite is small. It can hitchhike on a sleeve, a knife, a cutting you “just set down for a minute.” I still do not spray the county.

A seedling tray on a bench away from the old stools is not a clean room. It is a habit that costs me a walk. I wash the knife. I do not use the same rag on a painted giant and a plant I am trying to keep quiet. I am not performing theater. I am trying not to be the taxi.

If I take budwood off a painted tree on purpose, I have already agreed to the file. I do not then oil the baby and call it clean. That is a lie with extra steps.

## Oils, soaps, and the burn

Horticultural oil on mites is a real tool in a lot of fruit books. Figs you will eat still need fig (or the right group) on the label. Oil in 8a heat is how you cook a leaf. Soap on a painted leaf you already live with is how you feel busy.

I will oil a scale problem in a winter shop on a pot I can move — different pest, different draft — and I will still read the label. I will not oil a July in-ground tree as a mosaic rite.

A 98° afternoon and a “dormant” oil you found in the shed is how you write a cooked canopy and then blame the virus for the brown.

Diatomaceous earth shows up in the Virginia Tech backyard guide as a “natural” mite reducer. DE is dust. Dust on a leaf in our humidity is a mud. Dust in lungs is a person problem. I will not write DE as the mosaic program. If you use it in a greenhouse on a floor, that is your shop. It is not a virus evictor.

Neem is not a virus evictor. A compost tea is not a virus evictor. I will keep saying evictor until the algorithm gets bored.

## What the mite does not explain

It does not explain rust grit. Flip the leaf.

It does not explain a wilted top over wet dirt. Knock a spare plant.

It does not explain a vinegar necklace. Pick the fruit.

It does not explain winter-killed tips after a rust strip and a late flush. That is the October draft.

Spider mites are a different animal — fine pepper on the top in a hot, dry spell. We get those in 8a even in a humid county. They are not *Aceria*. Do not run one program for both because the word mite felt efficient.

## Cuttings move more virus than a mite you cannot see

I tell people this when they want the mite to be the whole story. The cutting they mailed in a baggie is a bigger mover than the eriophyid they have never seen. Grafts too. Seed does not carry it, in the UC telling. That is why a seedling you grew yourself is the only plant I will mentally file as “maybe still clean,” and even then I watch it through a heat week before I get proud.

UC’s fig-mite page is a bud-scale and 20× story. Their mosaic page is a live-with-it or replace-it story. Read both. Do not mash them into a spray card.

A marketplace listing that says virus-free is not a lab file. I will not buy that sentence. I will buy wood from a person who says the mother painted in July and still loaded. That is an honest box.

## What I tell a person who just learned the mite exists

I tell them the mite is why “I never touched that tree” is not a quarantine. I tell them the cutting they mailed is a bigger mover than a mite they cannot see. I tell them a 20× lens is cheaper than a jug and more honest.

I tell them they will not see the mite on a Facebook photo of a yellow leaf. They will see mosaic or rust or sunburn. The mite is a reason to be careful with clean plants. It is not a reason to fog a working tree.

I tell them a greenhouse collection and a yard Celeste are different jobs. One might earn a grown-up conversation with an agent. The other earns a bowl and a rake.

I tell them to mark **[VERIFY]** on any product name they heard on a forum, including names I just refused to turn into a recipe. Labels move. Counties disagree. Fig is often not on the paper.

## The skip

Most blogs skip this chapter because you cannot sell a photo of a mite that looks like dust. They sell a cure instead. I will sell you a lens and a no. The virus in the wood stays. The next clean plant is the plant you do not stick in the old tree’s pocket.

If you came here for a three-step mite rite that clears mosaic, you came to the wrong porch. If you came here to keep a seedling honest, bring a loupe, a little distance, and the patience to watch a July. That is the whole program I will stand on.
