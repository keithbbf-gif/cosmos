The LSU fig program was built in air like ours. That matters. It does not mean a purple fruit is a fungicide.

I like those names. I grow in the same sticky county class they were selected for. I will still rake under an LSU tree. I will still refuse the sentence “I planted Purple so I do not get rust.”

I heard that sentence in a nursery aisle with a wet cardboard pot in my hand. The tag said improved. The clerk said LSU. The man behind me said he does not spray because he planted Purple. I put the pot in the cart anyway. I did not put his sentence in the cart. August would grade both of us.

## What the pages actually said

LSU’s variety writeups have said **LSU Purple** has some resistance to leaf diseases, bears young, and is colder-sensitive — better thought of as a Gulf plant than a northern-Louisiana bet. Other LSU pages talk a heavy main crop and a late crop that can run long.

“Some resistance to leaf diseases” is a pile of foliage troubles, not a rust medal you can hang on a fence. Thread blight lives in this folder as its own early-season chapter. Rust is the late-summer orange one. Mosaic is a virus paint job and is not a leaf *disease* in the breeding sense people want it to be. Do not hear “leaf disease” and cash it in as “my Purple will not paint, rust, blight, or drop.”

The breeding-history page said **LSU Gold** has **moderate** resistance to fig leaf rust and is recommended for the Gulf Coast. Moderate is a breeder’s shrug. It is not a number. It is not a year. Gold’s own writeups also talk a partially open eye and cracking after rain. That is a souring-and-pick sentence. It is not a rust sentence. Do not trade one for the other.

Celeste shows up as the statewide hardy sweet closed-eye workhorse. I have not seen LSU hand Celeste a rust-immune medal. Closed eye is a souring sentence, not a rust sentence. I plant Celeste because the wood and the eye make sense in 8a, not because I think the leaf is a raincoat.

I will not invent a table that ranks 40 internet names. I will not upgrade “some resistance” to “I stopped raking.”

**[VERIFY]** any specific “does not rust” claim against *your* canopy. A dry year will make a liar out of a proud owner. A steam-bath year will make a liar out of a catalog.

## A walk between two trees in the same week

I like a cheap comparison. Same Saturday. Same county air. One LSU name I can put a finger on, one unnamed turkey that has been in a ditch since somebody’s uncle.

I flip ten leaves on each, underside first. Rust pustules are grit. A painted oak-leaf with a clean back is mosaic talking, and Purple is allowed to paint. A yellow map with orange dust is rust talking, and Gold is allowed to rust.

What I hope for from a “resistant” name is not a spotless leaf. I hope the canopy still works the week the fruit is finishing. Fewer early strips. A factory that stays on the job through the necklace week. If the turkey is already a skeleton and the Gold still has a working shirt, that is a reason to keep Gold. It is not a reason to skip the rake.

I have had “resistant” talk and still seen yellow maps in August. That is allowed. Breeding is a pile of years and a pile of scores. Your ditch is one year and one hole.

If a name holds leaves better than the neighbor’s unnamed turkey, good. That is a reason to keep the name. It is not a reason to skip airflow and litter.

## Cold is the other half of an LSU tag

Purple’s cold note matters in 8a because rust and winter stack. A tree that strips, flushes late, and is already the tenderer LSU child is how you write a short trunk. Gold’s rust note does not pay that bill either.

Zone 8a is a winter number — average annual extreme about 10 to 15°F. It is not one climate. A parish closer to the Gulf and a South Arkansas clay hole do not get the same teen night. I plant LSU names because the fruit and the humidity make sense. I still protect wood when the forecast is stupid. I still do not treat Purple as a north-Arkansas houseplant.

I have a pot of Purple I can walk to a wall. I have an in-ground stool I cannot. The tag did not change. The wagon did.

If October grows a second spring on a rust-stripped Purple, I do not feed it. That is the late-flush draft. I mention it here because people shop LSU names as if the pedigree includes a winter waiver. It does not.

## What I tell a person shopping “disease-resistant figs”

I tell them to read the word **relative**. I tell them to ask which disease. Root-knot is not rust. Mosaic is not rust. Souring is an eye. Birds are a color and a net.

I tell them LSU Purple’s foliage notes are not a nematode passport. We will not print “nematode-resistant fig variety” as a fact because a leaf looked better in a breeding row. LSU Pub. 1802 still talks cultural nematode sense: avoid recent tomato, okra, tobacco ground; start with clean plants. A purple fruit does not reset last year’s vegetable sand.

I tell them LSU breeding is one of the few programs that was looking at *our* air. That is worth money. It is not a waiver.

I tell them a big-box tag that says “improved” or “LSU-type” may not be a release. The breeding-history page even warned that names circulate with no connection to the program. **[VERIFY]** the source. Fruit will tell you later. A label in a pot is a starting rumor. The canopy in August is the sentence.

I tell them to smell the dirt in the pot before they argue about rust genes. Galls on a nursery root are a different refund than a painted leaf.

## What resistance looks like in a yard

A tree that holds a working canopy through the week the fruit is finishing. Fewer early strips. Not a spotless leaf.

A tree that still rusts in a monstrous year and still comes back in April if you did not also freeze a late flush.

A tree you still rake under, because Ferrin and Overstreet’s litter spores do not read tags.

I space LSU names like I space any fig I want to keep dry in the morning. Air in the interior. Water on the dirt. No midnight shower on a leaf I am bragging about.

I do not plant the collection in a solid hedge and then complain that the “resistant” one rusted on the inside. Resistance is not a substitute for a saw you were too polite to use.

## Copper, Louisiana, Arkansas, and the shopping cart

People hear LSU and they hear copper in the same breath because old Southern fruit books stacked those words. LSU Pub. 1802 (2025) said **no fungicides currently labeled for figs in Louisiana** and pointed at cultural management. UAEX’s Arkansas rust note still talks historic **neutral copper** when first leaves are full size, then three or four weeks later. UF’s older fig notes said **no EPA-approved fungicides for edible figs in Florida** in that document.

Those three sentences can be true at the same time. I will not flatten them into “spray copper, the LSU tree will be fine everywhere.” **[VERIFY]** the label in your state in the year you are holding the jug. If fig is not on it, it is not a fig spray.

I will not write a weekly calendar as if figs were apples. Once the spots are obvious, UF/IFAS Panhandle already said chemical talk is late. Pedigree does not reopen that window.

## The rake still gets the last word

Plant Purple. Plant Gold. Plant Celeste. Plant what you will actually pick. Then flip a leaf in August. If it dusts your thumb, you have rust, and you have a rake. The pedigree can ride in the truck. It does not get to drive.

I have eaten well off LSU names in a sticky year. I have also bagged their leaves. Both things can be true. Breeding bought me time. It did not buy me a vaccine, and I will not sell you one with their initials on it.
