I pinched a leaf in August and my thumb came away looking like I had been handling a cheap eyeshadow. The neighbor said the tree was dying. The tree was making rust.

Fig rust is a leaf fungus with two hats in the paperwork. Texas and Louisiana mostly say ***Cerotelium fici***. Arkansas’s clinic note still says ***Physopella fici***. Same disease. Small angular yellow-green flecks. They go yellow, then rusty brown. The underside raises little blisters. Those blisters let go of brown or orange spores. Then a tree that can drop a canopy in two or three ugly weeks if the air stays thick.

UAEX wrote that last part. TAMU wrote that rust often gets loud as fruit is trying to finish. LSU wrote that **fruit is not the rust host**. Remember that when somebody wants to spray a vinegar fig for a leaf problem.

We are in zone **8a** with a sticky August. That is rust weather. A dry ridge in the same zone will see less. I will not pretend every yellow leaf on this place is a culture I sent off. I will tell you what the dust on a thumb usually means.

## How it starts when you are not looking

It does not start as a dead tree. It starts as a few spots you blame on heat. You are watering. You are watching fruit. The interior of the canopy is still wet at 10 a.m. after a night rain. The fungus likes that closet.

LSU’s cycle is the one I want in grower English. Spores live on diseased leaves on the ground. Wind and splash put them on new leaves. Infected leaves brown and fall. New leaves come. In a wet Gulf summer that strip-and-replace can happen more than once. The insult is timing. The week you want leaves pulling sugar, the leaves walk off.

I have had years the tree still finished a main crop with a ragged skirt. I have had years the late fruit stalled because the factory left. Same fungus. Different rain.

## Flip the leaf. Rub it.

The upper surface can look almost smooth — a yellow map with a reddish edge. The story is underneath. Blisters. Grit. Dust.

Mosaic does not dust your thumb. Sunburn is a bleach on the face of a leaf that cooked. Spider-mite stipple is fine pepper and webbing if it is bad, and a dry week, and a dusty pot. Sooty mold wipes off as a film. Rust’s underside is a different texture. Once you have felt it, you stop guessing from the porch.

If the tree is stripping and you want a name on paper, the county clinic still beats my driveway. Bag a few leaves. Say when it started. Say whether fruit is involved. **[VERIFY]** their current intake. I am not their clerk.

## What rust is not

It is not a fruit rot. A sour necklace is yeasts and beetles and an open eye. A split fig is water and skin. Rust does not move into the pulp and ferment it. The crop suffers because photosynthesis walked away.

It is not mosaic. Mosaic is a paint job through the blade — oak leaf, rings, a pattern that got louder in heat. No pustules. No orange thumb.

It is not a reason to cut the tree down. Rust will not usually kill a healthy fig. Repeated early strip will weaken wood and, in UAEX’s warning, a late replacement flush can go into winter soft and take cold better than you wanted. That is the 8a twist. We have enough winter to punish a tree that thought October was May.

## What I do when I see the dust

I rake. I bag or I move the leaves out of the canopy drip. I do not compost a rust blanket against the trunk. LSU said do not compost diseased leaves. I believe them.

I open the interior next winter or at the prune I already owed the tree. Morning sun. A breeze. A wet still center is a closet.

I water the dirt. If I have to water overhead, I do it in the morning so leaves can dry. Dusk irrigation on a pot block is how you rent the fungus a night.

I do not fog a canopy of already-orange leaves and call it a cure. UF/IFAS in the Panhandle said once those spots are obvious, you are late for chemistry. Sanitation and next year are the chapter you still own.

Copper talk is a different draft because the states do not agree, and a label is not a blog. LSU 2025: no fungicides labeled for figs in Louisiana. UAEX: historic neutral copper when first leaves are full size. I will not hose-reconcile that here. **[VERIFY]** your own label.

## August thumb

If your thumb is orange, you have a leaf disease with a name. You do not have a dying fig and you do not have a fruit infection. You have a rake, a wet summer, and a tree that would like a breeze. Start there. The aisle can wait.
