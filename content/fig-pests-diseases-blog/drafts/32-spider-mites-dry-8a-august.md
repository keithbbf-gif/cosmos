People hear humidity and think mites cannot live here. Then a south wind and a week without rain and a pot on hot gravel write a bronze leaf. I have walked a pot block in August after five dry days and the leaves looked tired in a way rust is not tired. Fine pepper on the top. Underside with tiny moving dots if you bring a loupe. No orange thumb. No brewery on the fruit.

Two-spotted spider mites like dusty, dry, and still. We can make that room in zone **8a** without moving to Arizona. South Arkansas will hand you a sticky week and a dust-bowl week in the same month. The in-ground turkey in a still wet pocket rusts. The pot on the drive mites. That is why one word — blight — is useless.

They are not *Aceria*. They do not carry mosaic. They do not rust your thumb orange. They stipple. They bronze. They web if you let a greenhouse go.

UC’s fig chapter lists webspinning spider mites as an orchard pest. A backyard pot block is a smaller orchard with worse air.

## The sort on a hot walk

Flip the leaf. A 10× or 20× loupe. Tiny moving dots. Cast skins. Fine webbing. Underside first. I do this in the morning before the leaf is a skillet. Afternoon on gravel will lie to you about color.

Rust: blisters, orange dust, angular map. Fruit is not the host. LSU already said that.

Mosaic: paint, rings, oak-leaf yellow, louder after a 98° week. No grit. No web. No spray-off. UC: no practical residential cure for the virus.

Sunburn: bleach on the hot face, no committee on the underside. A pot you forgot to shade.

Scale soot: black film that smears. Sticky rim. Bumps on wood. Different leak.

If you cannot see a mite and you still want a name, the county can look. I will not diagnose a bronze from one driveway photo.

Bird pecks, mammal grabs, and sap-beetle eyes are fruit stories. A bronze pot leaf in a dry wind is not a mockingbird. Do not hang tape for a mite. Do not fog ripe fruit for a stipple.

## What I do before a product

I rinse the underside with water in the morning. Not a 4 p.m. oil. A hard spray on the underside of a pot I can tip is a real move. A twenty-foot turkey will laugh at your hose if you only tickle the top.

I shade the pot block the way we already shade for July. [Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) already owns pots, beds, shade, water. This page does not rewrite that. It says mites like the dust bowl you made when you skipped the shade cloth.

I stop the dust bowl. A bare gravel sea in a south wind is a mite factory. I am not planting a lawn under pots. I am not proud of a dust devil I made. A little water on the gravel in the morning, not a lake at the pot rim, can cut the dust without drowning roots. Clay saucers that hold a lake are a different lie.

I do not drought-stress a plant for sport. Mites like a plant that already gave up. A heavy pot that still wilts at noon might be nematodes. Knock a tool plant. Do not assume every tired top is a mite.

I keep the bud-mite / mosaic conversation in its own file. *Aceria ficus* lives under bud scales and moves virus in the wood. A soap dunk is not a virus program. Two-spotted mites are a dry-leaf animal. Do not mash the mites because both words have mite in them.

I walk the block after a dry week the same way I walk fruit at dawn in a bird week. Different calendar. Same habit. Look.

## Products I will not write as a recipe

I will not write a miticide brand. I will not write “just use neem weekly.” I will not write dish soap ratios as if they were FIFRA. Horticultural soap and oil have labels. Heat plus oil burns. Fruit you will eat has a crop list. If fig is not on the label in your state, it is not a fig spray.

If a greenhouse collection is a carpet of web, call the agent. **[VERIFY]** a labeled use. A jam turkey in the yard with a little stipple after a dry week often needs water and weather, not a personality.

Predatory mites exist as bags you can buy. They have rules about heat and sprays you already used. I will not pretend I run a biocontrol program on a porch.

I will not write a statewide spray calendar. I will not write “safe for kids and pets” next to a jug.

## 8a is two weathers

The same August can rust an in-ground tree in a still wet pocket and mite a pot on a windy drive. After a tropical leftover we get soup and rust splash. Then a high and a south wind and five days of dust. The mite is a dry-room animal. Give it less room.

A shop in January can mite a plant under a heater as easily as it grows scale. Warm, dry, still. Walk the shop. Rinse. Do not wait for August to learn the stipple.

Night that drops into the 60s after a 98° day does not cancel a dust pad. The pot still sits on a skillet. Shade. Water the dirt. Do not water the midnight leaf on the in-ground tree you are trying not to rust.

## What I will not mash

I will not copper a bronze stipple. Copper talk in this series is a rust-and-label conversation, and LSU 2025 already said Louisiana has no labeled fig fungicide in that document. A metal on a mite is busy and wrong.

I will not blame mosaic for a web.

I will not fog ripe fruit because a pot bronzed. The fruit bucket is birds, beetles, souring, mammals. Name it.

I will not write that humidity makes mites impossible here. I have the bronze leaves. The county has the dry weeks. The gravel remembers.

I have walked that pot block after a church-hour Sunday in August when the gravel could fry an egg and the in-ground Celeste fifty feet away was still wet at the trunk from Saturday’s rain. Same zip code. Two rooms. The Celeste wanted a rake later. The pots wanted a rinse that morning.

A pot I had shoved against a south wall to “catch sun” after a cool snap paid for that idea by Tuesday. Stipple on the hot face. Underside busy. I moved it back under the shade cloth and rinsed. I did not buy a jug because a wall made a skillet.

Keep the rust rake for the wet tree. Keep the dawn bowl for the ripe necklace. Keep the hose for the underside of a dusty pot. Name the room. Name the leaf. The mite is a dry-room animal. Give it less room.
