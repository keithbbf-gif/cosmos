I have thrown away more time worrying about mosaic than mosaic has thrown away fruit. The leaf looks like somebody spilled a map on it. Heat turns the volume up. A person who just learned the word *virus* wants a funeral.

UC IPM is calmer than the internet. Once a residential fig has mosaic, there is no practical control. If the tree is not performing, replace it. If it is performing, you already have your answer.

Most old Southern wood carries this. Cuttings move it. A mite moves it. Seed does not. You cannot bleach it off a blade.

I said that to a neighbor who had already Googled “fig virus cure” and come back with a cart of oils. The tree behind him was painted and loaded. I pointed at the fruit. He pointed at the leaf. We stood there long enough for a mockingbird to take a shoulder off a ripe one. The virus did not do that either.

## What it looks like when it is mosaic

Light green to yellow patches. Oak-leaf outlines. Rings. A mottled quiet version that you only see when you hunt. Leaves may come smaller or twisted. Fruit may show faint patterns. Some cultivars drop fruit and leaves early. TAMU’s handbook listed those. Preising’s 2021 review said the “fig mosaic” story is a pile of viruses and hangers-on, not one tidy villain. I will not pretend I sequenced your tree.

Walk ten leaves, not one. Mosaic is a pattern through the blade. It does not grit your thumb. The new flush often talks louder than last month’s shirt. That is why people date the “infection” to the week they finally looked up.

What it does **not** look like: orange grit on the underside. That is rust. Do not copper a paint job.

What it does not look like: a tree that wilted at noon with wet dirt and beaded roots. That is downstairs. Root-knot does not paint an oak-leaf. It just makes a tired top.

What it does not look like: bleach on the west face of a pot after a 98° afternoon on gravel. That is a cooked leaf. Move the pot. Do not start a virus protocol.

What it does not look like: pink syrup at the eye and a brewery smell. That is souring. Mosaic will try to take the credit if you let it.

I photograph the underside and the top on the same day. I have argued with myself in October without those pictures. I lose.

## Heat is a loudspeaker, not a new infection

8a July will make a quiet mosaic look like an emergency. The wood had it in April. The mite may have added more later. The Tuesday you noticed was weather.

I do not cut a tree down in August because the leaf got theatrical. I wait for the year. Did it grow? Did it finish fruit? Did it come back after winter? Mosaic that stays a paint job is a note. Mosaic on a plant that never makes a canopy is a cull.

A west pot on gravel writes the loudest page. An in-ground stool in afternoon shade writes a quieter one. Same wood. Different speaker. That is why I will not diagnose a collection from one Facebook leaf taken at 3 p.m. on a baked driveway.

Black Mission, in UC’s older agriculture note, takes mosaic harder than some California names. Kadota and Calimyrna were called less affected. That is their orchard, not your unnamed turkey. I will not import a California ranking onto a South Arkansas stool. I will say: **variety matters, and I will not guess yours from a photo.**

PNW’s handbook said high summer temperatures can make symptoms worse. I did not need a handbook to see it. I needed a July. The heat draft in this folder unpacks the week. This page only needs you to stop dating the infection to the heat wave.

## The mite, in one honest chunk

*Aceria ficus* (UC often writes *A. fici*) is an eriophyid you will not admire without a 20× lens. It lives in buds and around the eye. One infectious mite can move virus to a clean seedling in their telling. Eggs are not born with the virus; feeding acquires it. Controlling mites may reduce *spread*. It does not evict the virus already in the wood.

That is the split I will keep repeating until I am tired. Mite talk is a spread story. It is not a cure story. Oils may bother mites. They do not evict a virus. Diatomaceous earth does not evict a virus. Bud-scale carving does not evict a virus. A heat lamp does not evict a virus.

The mite chapter is the next file. I only need you to stop buying a “virus spray.”

## What I do with a painted tree that still works

I leave it. I pick it. I do not propagate it as if I were selling “clean stock.” If I take a cutting for myself, I am taking the paint job. I am allowed to do that for a flavor I like. I am not allowed to write “virus-free” on a box.

I keep the tree watered and I do not stack rust and drought on it for sport. Vigor is not a cure. Vigor is how a painted tree still pays rent.

I do not spray oils all summer as a mosaic program. Oils have mite uses and burn risks and label problems. A residential tree that is already infected does not become uninfected because I shined the leaves.

I rake rust litter so I am not running two factories of ugly at once. I net the fruit I actually want. I do not hold a funeral in July.

I have eaten off painted wood for years. The jar does not ask about the leaf.

## A year is the test, not a Tuesday

April: I look at the first flush. Quiet paint is common. I write it down if I have a new name.

July: I look again after a 98° week. Louder is common. I flip for rust so I do not treat the wrong bucket.

September: I ask whether the tree finished fruit and grew wood. A painted leaf on a working canopy is not a tragedy.

March: I ask whether the wood lived. Dead tips after a rust strip and a late flush are a winter chapter. Mosaic will try to take that too.

If the plant never makes a canopy — year two, year three, a stick with three theatrical leaves — I replace it. UC said that. I say that. I do not owe a weak plant a decade because the leaf is interesting.

I do not plant the replacement in a romantic way next to a mite factory if I can help it. Distance is not a wall. It is a habit. Clean-looking nursery stock is still a rumor until it grows. Symptom-free is not virus-free. I will say that until I am tired.

## The death sentence people want

They want mosaic to explain every failure. Dropped fruit. Small crop. Winter kill. Souring. Wet feet. Birds. It will not. Mosaic is a real disease. It is also a scapegoat.

A tree that wilts at noon with wet dirt is a root story until you knock a plant you can spare. A necklace that smells like a brewery is yeast and a door. A dark ripe fig gone by 7 a.m. is a bird. A rust strip in August is a fungus. Mosaic can sit on all of those trees. It is not the author of all of those weeks.

Do not sell or imply virus-free plants unless a named lab test on *that* plant is in hand. I will not. Most old Southern clones carry mosaic to some degree. That is not a reason to quit figs. It is a reason to quit the funeral.

If your 8a tree paints and still feeds you, you do not have a tragedy. You have a fig. Flip the leaf so you do not rust-treat it, then go pick.

I have stood in that driveway with the neighbor and the cart of oils. We picked a bowl instead. The leaf was still a map. The bowl was still breakfast.
