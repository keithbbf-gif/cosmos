I bagged a child’s first fig on a pot by the porch with an organza party bag and a clothespin. The jay still found the rest of the tree. We ate that one fig over the sink. That is the honest size of this method.

A twenty-foot turkey does not get a hundred organza bags unless you have a different personality than I do. A young tree, a pot, a branch you can reach — those get bags in the loud week if I will not build a frame this year.

Growers have used newspaper and clothespins. Organza party bags. Paper sleeves. The method is older than a blog. The rule is the same as netting: you are trading time for fruit, and you can trap heat or an insect if you forget to look.

## When I bag

When the fig has started to swell and show the week it will soften — not when it is a hard marble, not when it is already glossy and the bird has a reservation.

I walk the small tree at dusk and pick the candidates. In 8a the neck can drop overnight after a wet afternoon and a 98° evening. If I wait until the fruit is a billboard, I am bagging a reservation.

When I can reach it without making the tree a stunt. A bag on a ladder over a ditch is how you write a different injury.

When I only need a bowl, not a bushel. Bags are a kitchen method. Nets are a tree method. Ferguson’s 2025 note still stands for the crop: netting is the reliable tool; scare tape habituates. Bags are what I use when the tree is a person, not a harvest.

Green-skinned fruit hides. I mark the branch with a bit of flagging or I will bag the wrong week and miss the ripe one. Contrast is not immunity. Hungry mockingbirds still read a soft neck through a thin story.

## What I use

Mesh organza that breathes. 8a August inside a plastic zip bag is a steamer. I will not steam a fig to hide it. A sealed plastic bag in our humidity is a science fair and a souring invitation.

Paper can work and can melt in rain. Newspaper on a clothespin is the old trick. It looks like laundry. It works on a small set. After a two-inch rain I replace the paper. Wet paper on fruit is a compress.

I do not seal a wet fig in a sauna. I do not use foil as a solar cooker. I do not use a black plastic grocery bag because it was on the counter.

The mouth of the bag needs to close enough that a mockingbird cannot reach in and still open enough that the fruit is not sitting in a cloud. Clothespin. Twist. A soft tie that will not girdle the neck of the branch.

## What still gets in

A sap beetle can walk into a loose mouth. A bag is not a clean room. If the eye is already open and the week is wet, you can bag a souring. UC said no yeasticide. A party bag is not a yeasticide. Pick. Do not fog the bag.

Ants farm whatever they farm. I am not bagging to solve ants. If the fruit split, ants show up because it is jam. That is a pick, not a scale chapter.

Squirrels chew organza like a snack. Then you have a bag on the ground and a joke. Raccoons treat a bagged branch as wrapping paper. Color of the fruit does not matter to a hand.

Bird pecks through thin cloth if the bag lies on the fig the way a thrown net lies on a canopy. Hold it off if you can. A small hoop of wire in the bag is fuss I have only done for a fruit I refused to lose.

## Daily look

I open or peek. Heat. Moisture. A beetle that clocked in. Fruit that finished and needs to come inside. A bag left a day too long in our humidity is pink syrup and a smell you will not put on toast.

If I cannot look daily, I should not bag. I should pick or I should net or I should pay rent.

Morning is kinder. The bag is cooler. The beetle is slower. The neck tells the truth in that light. Afternoon in a still pocket can cook a paper sleeve against the fruit.

After rain I check sooner. Water in the bag is a closet. Rust already wants a wet room on the leaf. Fruit wants out of a wet sock.

I take pecked or soured fruit out of the bag the same walk. Leaving it in there is a cafeteria with a door you built.

## What I will not claim

I will not claim bags stop all birds. I will not claim they replace a frame on a tree you care about as a crop. I will not sell a branded “fig sock” as a miracle. I will not tell you to bag a collection of 200 pots unless that is actually your idea of a good time.

I will claim a dozen bags on a child’s tree can be the difference between a first fig and a story about a jay.

I will not write poison, glue, or a pellet story because bagging felt like too much work. Migratory birds are not a pesticide label.

I will not write that a green name plus a bag is a force field. Contrast helps. The bag is a small fence. Check it.

I will not mash bird peck, mammal grab, and sap beetle into one “something ate it.” Look at the hole. The bag failed in different ways for each mouth.

## 8a weather on a small tree

Pots on a south gravel pad get hotter than an in-ground turkey in grass. A bag on a pot fig in a dry windy week can still steam if the plastic was a mistake. Mesh and shade. We already shade pots in July for the plant. The bag does not get to cancel that.

A tropical leftover rain week will split open-eye fruit inside a bag if you were late. Tight-eye names buy time. They do not buy a skipped walk. LSU’s Celeste talk is relative resistance to split and sour, not a vault.

I have bagged a fruit, left town one night, and come home to a necklace in organza. The yeast did not need my permission. The taxi walked in. The bag kept the bird off and kept the mess on the branch.

## The honest split

Net the crop trees. Frame. Stake the hem. Check daily for wildlife in a fold. That is Ferguson’s tool, built so it is not a snare.

Bag the sentimental branch. Walk the rest at dawn. Three tools. One week. No CD.

If that still feels like too much work, grow fewer figs. I say that without insult. A bag is a promise to show up. So is a net. So is a bird. Only one of those promises is automatic.
