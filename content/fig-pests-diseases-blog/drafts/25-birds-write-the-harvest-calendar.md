I have stood under a tree at dusk and counted pecks I did not hear happen. The neck had been tight at lunch. At 7:15 the first hole was already dry at the edge. Zone **8a** fig week is also mockingbird week, starling week, jay week, sometimes a waxwing week if the calendar lines up. A ripe fig does not wait for your Saturday. A bird does not care that you work.

Jack already said it on The Fig Jam: after a dead tree, birds are the part of fig season he likes least. That is his harvest mouth. This page is mine. I will not mash the two pens. I will write the calendar they own.

## What I see that is birds, not beetles, not a raccoon

A triangular peck. A hollowed shoulder. Fruit still hanging, ruined. Fruit on the ground with one neat bite and the rest left as a sermon. A dark ripe fig gone while the green ones stay. That last one is contrast, not magic. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already used the green-vs-dark line. I will not rebuild that essay. I will say: the calendar is still a dawn walk even on a green name.

Beetles chew messier and stay for the party. Green June is a clumsy mouth on a ripe shoulder. Sap beetles walk into an open eye and bring a brewery. They do not commute at 6:30 and punch a triangle.

Mammals grab. A squirrel samples unripe and wastes. A raccoon shops at night and takes a lot. A deer browses a short tree. The ground under a mammal night looks like a grocery bag dumped. The ground under a bird morning looks like a few pecks and a commute.

If you only say “something ate my figs,” you are not ready for a product. You are ready for a closer look at the hole.

## A dawn walk in a loud week

I walk at first decent light in the two weeks the necks drop. South Arkansas August can already be wet on your shirt. The grass is loud with dew. Mockingbirds are on the fence acting innocent. I do not need a field guide for every beak. I need a bowl.

I pick what is ripe, not what will be pretty on Friday.

I pick a day early if the flock has arrived and I will finish fruit on the counter. Not a week early. A hard green fig is not a strategy. A fig that has started to drop its neck can finish inside.

I take the pecked ones off so sap beetles do not clock in behind the bird. A peck is a door. Yeast already lives in the yard. The taxi is the little beetle. The bird opened the office.

I look at the high fruit I will not reach and I admit rent. Walter Reeves’s Georgia joke still works: grow the tree tall so the birds can have the high ones. That is also a prune decision. A twenty-foot turkey you will not net is a landlord tree.

I walk again if a two-inch rain hits and then the sun comes back hard. Splitters and souring share that week with birds. The calendar does not pause for weather. It gets louder.

## Scare tape is a week of television

LSU’s Mary Helen Ferguson said the quiet part in July 2025. Pie pans, foil, fake owls, fake snakes — limited time. Birds get used to them. **Netting is the most reliable way** to protect fruit. Scare tactics habituate. I will not decorate that sentence.

I have hung tape. It flashed in a south wind and moved the pecks for a bit. Then it became furniture. The mockingbird sat on the pole. I will not tell you tape never helps a two-day window when a new flock arrives. I will tell you it is not a season.

Commercial toys — air dancers, eye balloons, cannons, lasers, methyl anthranilate — have mixed orchard records. A backyard owl on a stick is a decoration by day ten. Radios and dogs are neighbor stories. I will not write them as a method. A radio that plays all night is how you meet a different neighbor.

CDs on fishing line belong in the same drawer. They move. Then they do not.

## 8a flocks are not theoretical

We have resident mockingbirds that learn a tree like a job. One pair can work a small yard the way a person works a route. We have seasonal crowds. A year the figs hit during a migration is a different tax than a year they hit in a quiet week. I do not forecast birds. I forecast that if the fruit is dark and soft, somebody with feathers already knows.

Starlings show up as a committee. Jays are loud about it. A cedar waxwing week, when it happens, is a strip. You do not negotiate with a strip. You pick what you can and you net what you refuse to share, or you make jam off the low branches and stop writing a war.

Heat makes the neck drop faster. A 98° afternoon after a wet night will finish fruit you thought had two days. The bird already read the gloss. Green fruit hides better in the canopy. It is not a force field. Hungry mockingbirds still read a soft neck. That is the next draft. This one is the clock.

## What I will not do

Poison. Glue. A pellet gun story on a migratory bird. That is not a garden tip. That is a legal and a decent-person line. Migratory birds are not a pesticide label.

I will not write a fake hawk as a personality.

I will not blame mosaic for a hollowed fruit. Mosaic paint lives on the leaf. A peck lives on the shoulder.

I will not fog ripe fruit “because birds.” Residue on a fig you will eat tonight is a different kind of stupid.

I will not promise a green-skinned name is bird-proof. Contrast helps. It is not immunity.

I will not leave a sour necklace because “the birds already wrecked it.” That necklace is sap-beetle bait. Take it off.

## The calendar I actually keep

Dawn in the loud two weeks. Bowl. Not a Saturday plan.

Pecked fruit off the tree the same walk.

If I will net, I want the frame up *before* the first dark ripe fig is a billboard. Netting that goes up after the flock has punched in is a hat on a meeting that already started. Ferguson said stake or weight every edge so birds do not walk under. Frame is ideal. Check it. That is the next page.

If I will not net, I pay rent and I pick what I can reach. I do not write a complaint as if the birds were new. They were not new. They published the schedule.

University of Tennessee’s fig note said the ordinary English version: birds, squirrels, raccoons; netting; pick at full ripe for fresh, a little earlier for preserves. I will stand on that without inventing a percentage.

## Landlord math

A tree I love and will not share gets a frame. A tree I planted because I had a hole gets a dawn walk and a jam pot. A pot on the porch can come inside at dusk if the week is a strip. I do not move two hundred pots. I move the two that are finishing.

If you will not net and you will not walk at dawn, you are a landlord. Charge rent in jam off the low branches and stop writing a war. The birds already published the schedule. You can read it at 6:30.
