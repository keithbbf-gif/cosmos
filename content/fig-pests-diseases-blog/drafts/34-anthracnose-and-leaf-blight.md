I have rusted more trees than I have cultured anthracnose. I still want the names so you do not copper a pink fruit rot or ignore a webby blight because the thumb was not orange. Rust is the loud 8a leaf. It is not the only fungus that can sit on a fig.

TAMU’s handbook still earns a look for the names people mash.

**Anthracnose** (*Glomerella* / *Colletotrichum*): fruit with a sunken discolored spot that grows a pink spore mass; immature fruit dried on the tree; leaves with a dark brown margin; drop. Sanitation. Get diseased fruit and leaves off the place.

**Leaf blight** (their *Pellicularia kolerga*): watersoaked yellow patches, upper surface going silvery, underside light brown with a thin web, then brown and shrivel. Sanitation is the only recommendation they wrote.

I am not a clinic. I am a driveway with a wet week and a neighbor who already bought a bottle.

## How I sort them in a driveway

Rust: angular map, underside pustules, orange dust, fruit not the host. LSU said that last part. UAEX said a tree can strip in two or three weeks. Thumb stain.

Anthracnose on fruit: a spot, a sink, pink crumbs. Not brewery syrup. Souring smells like a still. Anthracnose looks like a bruise that grew a powder. I smell first. I look second.

Leaf blight: a web, a silvering, a collapse that is not a rust blister. Flip. The underside is the tell TAMU wrote.

Mosaic: paint, no powder, no web. Louder after heat. No spray-off.

Sooty mold: black smear after a leak. Ants. Bumps. Wipe. Do not copper sugar.

Spider-mite bronze: dry pot, stipple, maybe web of a different kind. Dust week, not a tropical leftover.

If I cannot sort it and the tree is failing, I bag a leaf and a fruit for the clinic. Dry paper towel. Note about rain and whether fruit is involved. I do not invent a weekly fungicide because I learned a third Latin name.

Bird pecks, mammal grabs, and sap-beetle eyes still belong in their own photos. A peck is a triangle. A raccoon leaves a pile. A sap beetle walks into an eye and brings yeast. A pink spore mass on a sunken spot is not a peck. Do not hang netting for anthracnose. Do not fog ripe fruit because you learned *Colletotrichum*.

## What I do

Pick the spotted fruit. Do not leave mummies. TAMU said sanitation is extremely important. I believe a handbook when it is boring.

Rake. Same tarp as rust. Do not compost the mystery leaves against the trunk. Do not till them in. LSU’s rust talk said do not compost diseased leaves. I will not build a fruit-and-leaf mattress under the drip line and call it feeding the tree. That mattress is also a chafer room.

Open the canopy when the prune is due. Wet still rooms are fungus rooms. I do not butcher a fig in August to “let it breathe” and steal a crop that is two weeks from jam. I take the dead wood. I wait for the prune I already owed the variety.

Do not water the midnight leaf. Morning if overhead. Dirt, not a shower on a rusting canopy.

Do not write a rescue fog. LSU 2025 already said Louisiana has no labeled fig fungicide in that document. Other states may have historic copper talk aimed at rust, not at every Latin you found tonight. **[VERIFY]**. A contact metal will not turn a pink fruit crater back into jam. Once the fruit is a crater, you are late for chemistry the same way UF/IFAS said you are late when rust spots are obvious.

Take the sour necklace too. Yeast is a different bucket. Same walk. Same trash. Not under the tree.

## Virginia Tech mentioned anthracnose too

Their backyard guide (SPES-760P) talked dark lesions, fruit drop, sanitation, and “commercially available fungicides” as preventative. That last clause is the one I will not upgrade. Commercial and labeled and fig and your state have to be the same object. I will keep the sanitation and leave the catalog.

I will not flatten Arkansas, Louisiana, Florida, and Texas into one hose recipe. UAEX’s rust note still talks historic neutral copper timing. LSU Pub. 1802 said no labeled fig fungicide in Louisiana in 2025. UF’s older fig pest note said no EPA-approved fungicides for edible figs in Florida in that document. Those sentences can be true at the same time. **[VERIFY]** the label in your hand this year.

## 8a wet weeks stack names

A tropical rain leftover can give you split, sour, rust splash, and a fruit spot in the same necklace. Heat after the rain finishes the neck. Birds punch holes. Green June shows up. Sap beetles keep office hours. Mammals shop at night.

Name the worst door you can close today. Usually that door is “pick and rake.” The Latin can wait in a bag for a weekday desk.

I walk at dawn after those weeks. Bowl. Spotted fruit off. Necklace off. Pecked fruit off so the taxi does not get a second door. Look at the ground for a grab. Look under ten leaves for rust grit versus a web. Do not copper a smear. Do not spray a brewery.

Green fruit is not a force field against a fruit-rot fungus. Contrast is a bird chapter. A sunken spot does not care what color you planted.

Thread blight is a different early-season web some Southern notes mention. If the canopy looks like it was glued with a pale thread, photograph it and ask the clinic. I will not mash every web into *Pellicularia* from a porch. The blight word is how we got into this folder.

## What I will not write

I will not invent a weekly copper / sulfur / chlorothalonil calendar as if figs were apples.

I will not copy Maneb or Vapam asides from an old handbook onto a living tree.

I will not say neem is a fruit-rot vaccine.

I will not compost a diseased blanket against the trunk.

I will not blame mosaic for a pink crater.

I will not write a percentage of control.

I would rather a grower rake the right litter than mispronounce *Colletotrichum* over a jug. The blight word is how we got into this folder. Use the smaller words. Spot. Web. Dust. Syrup. Peck. Grab. Then do the boring work.

I have come back from a wet Saturday with a tarp of leaves and a bucket of spotted fruit and nothing that looked like a purchase. The tree was lighter. The ground could take sun. That is the win I will stand on.

The tree will still be there in the morning if you picked and raked. The bottle can wait until a person with a key and a label says it even belongs in this state. Most weeks it will not. The rake will.
