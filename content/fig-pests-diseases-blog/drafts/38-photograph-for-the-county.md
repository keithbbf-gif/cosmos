I will look at a photo. I will not bless a jug from a photo. The county clinic is the grown-up version of both. They still want a sample. The pictures are how you do not waste their Monday.

A neighbor texts me a yellow leaf on a kitchen table and wants a product name before supper. I have been that neighbor in my own head. I have also mailed a bag and gotten a name that saved me a second bottle. The difference is not talent. The difference is five frames, a few words, and a zip bag that did not cook in a truck.

**[VERIFY]** your clinic’s current intake — Arkansas’s plant health clinic, LSU, TAMU, UF, whoever you actually use. Hours change. Addresses change. What they will accept in summer heat changes. This is the shot list I wish came with the sad text message. It is not a substitute for their form.

I am not a clinic. I will say that again. A cropped Instagram story is not an ID. A good photo is a courtesy. A good bag is a diagnosis.

## Five frames

**1. The whole tree.** North side if you can, so the light does not bleach the canopy into a blob. Include the ground. A lake, a mulch volcano, a rust quilt, grass to the trunk, a pot in a saucer — those are diagnosis. How much canopy is gone matters. One yellow leaf on a full tree is not the same story as a skirt of drop and a skinny top.

Step back. If you only shoot the pretty side, you hid the dead stool. If you only shoot the dead stool, you hid the live ones. I want both.

**2. The leaf, top.** Several leaves, not the prettiest. New flush and old flush. Mosaic likes to talk on new wood. Rust likes older summer leaves first and then gets greedy. Sunburn talks on the side that faces the west wall. One hero leaf is how Facebook writes a novel.

**3. The leaf, underside, in sun.** If you only send the top, you hid the rust. Pustules live there. A webby blight lives there. Sooty mold is a film. I tilt the leaf toward light so grit shows. Shade photos of undersides look like nothing. That is not the fungus being shy. That is the camera.

**4. The fruit, if fruit exists.** Eye. Drip. Peck. Pink powder. Split. A coin for scale. Souring smells like a brewery — you cannot photograph a smell, so write it. A sunken spot with a pink crumb is not brewery syrup. Anthracnose and souring are different photos. Birds leave a peck. Beetles chew a shoulder. A firm fruit on the ground with a mined inside is a different email than a vinegar necklace. Do not mash them.

**5. The root or collar only if you already had a reason to look.** A tool plant. A pot-up. A corpse. Do not rip a rare name for content. Galls, if they are there, are not subtle once you have seen them. A creamy white skin under bark at the soil line is the Armillaria draft. A brown mushy pot root is a dish. Photograph what you already exposed. Do not invent a surgery.

A ruler or a coin in one frame. “Small spots” means nothing. I have called a rust pustule small and a scale bump small and meant two different animals.

## Words that belong in the email

When it started. Not “a while.” A week, a month, last August and again this May.

Rain that week. A tropical leftover. A dry wind. Irrigation you added after you forgot them for ten days.

Whether fruit is involved. Leaf only is one bucket. Fruit only is another. Both is a week, not one Latin name.

Age of the tree. First year in the hole is not a twenty-year Celeste.

Pot or ground. Mix you bought or dirt you inherited. Last year’s tomatoes, okra, tobacco. LSU already said that ground is a root-knot conversation.

Whether you already sprayed, and what. Clinics hate guessing under a film. Oil, soap, a “fruit tree” jug, a copper you cannot name — say it. Date it.

Variety if you believe the tag. County. 8a is not a site. Your sand or clay is a site. Shade from a metal building is a site. A pot block on gravel is a site.

Do not write “blight.” Write dust, paint, syrup, wilt, beads, web, peck, split. Those words sort buckets. Blight is how this folder got too many wrong bottles.

If the drop is unusual — fruit still firm, inside mined, not a bird, not a vinegar necklace — write that without decorating it. Photograph it. Bag it. That is clinic territory, and if a desk wants a federal desk, they will say so. I will not let a group thread name a federal pest for fun. The black-fig-fly draft is the biosecurity version. It is not a Facebook caption.

## What I do not want

A filtered Instagram. Warm-and-cozy yellow is not rust grit.

A single yellow leaf on a table with a wedding ring for scale and no underside.

A video that never flips the blade and never shows the ground.

A demand for a product name in the first sentence.

A photo of the bottle you already bought, as if the label were the diagnosis.

A close-up so tight I cannot tell fig from pecan.

If you already sprayed, say so. If you already pruned the evidence off, say so. If the tree is a pot you just brought home from a marketplace, say so. Source is part of biosecurity. We already said that about nematodes. Soil moves more than stories.

## The bag still wins

Pictures get you in line. A few leaves in a zip bag with a dry paper towel, plus a fruit if fruit is the story, get you a name.

Heat in a mailbox cooks a sample. Follow their shipping note. I will not invent a cooler protocol as if I were a courier. **[VERIFY]** how your clinic wants it this month. Some want fresh. Some want a form first. Some will not take a rotting necklace in August without a warning.

I do not stuff a wet leaf in a sealed bag and leave it on a dashboard. That is soup. Soup cultures itself. The clinic then IDs the soup.

Roots and soil for a nematode sample are a different bag and a different draft. Do not mail a rare plant’s whole root ball because a leaf was yellow. Do not mail a corpse collar in a grocery sack without asking. Ask. Then follow what they said.

I like a loupe in the yard. Twenty times on a bud scale is how you see a mite the blogs skip. I like a clinic on the week the tree is stripping and I am about to do something expensive. Facebook will still offer cinnamon. Send the bag anyway.

## 8a honesty in the caption

Write that it is humid. Write that it has been 98°. Write that the tree rust-stripped last year and flushed in October. Write that a freeze took the tips in March. Write that the hole used to be okra. Write that the pot sat in a saucer through a rain week.

Those sentences change which bucket a stranger reaches for. They are not extra. They are the weather the fungus already used. They are the dirt the nematodes already used. They are the reason a California orchard PDF and a Louisiana 2025 disease note can disagree about copper and both be documenting their own rules.

I will not flatten your caption into “fig blight, what spray.” I will not flatten it from here either. If I cannot put the scene in a bucket, I will say I cannot. That is the first draft in this pack. The name comes before the bottle.

## What a good photo still cannot do

It cannot bless a copper schedule. UAEX historic timing and LSU 2025 cultural-only are both real sentences. A picture of a yellow leaf does not pick the winner. **[VERIFY]** your label and your desk.

It cannot declare a tree doomed. Mosaic can look loud and still feed you. Rust can strip a tree that will leaf in April if you do not also freeze the late flush. Nematodes can sit under a tree that looks “fine” until a dry week.

It cannot tell me what is in the pot mix. It cannot smell souring for you if you refuse to type the word.

It cannot replace a root look when the story is downstairs. It cannot replace a collar scrape when the tree died standing.

A good photo is a courtesy. A good bag is a diagnosis. Keep both. Keep the jug in the shed until somebody who is not me names the bucket.

If you want a shorter rule for the phone: whole tree, both sides, fruit, ground, coin, then the bag. If you skip the bag, you asked for a rumor. I will not upgrade a rumor to a recipe.
