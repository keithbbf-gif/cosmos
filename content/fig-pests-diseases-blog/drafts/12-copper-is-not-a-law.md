I am going to put three official sentences on the same table and not pick a favorite.

LSU AgCenter Pub. 1802, 2025: fig diseases in Louisiana are managed with culture and variety **because no fungicides are currently labeled for use on figs in Louisiana**.

UAEX Plant Health Clinic, fig rust note (Smith and Pavel): control depends on timing; **neutral copper** when the first leaves are full size in May or early June, again in three to four weeks.

UF/IFAS older edible-fig notes: **no EPA-approved fungicides for use on edible figs in Florida** in that document.

A blog that flattens those into “just spray copper, it is organic” is lying to somebody. Maybe you. Maybe a Louisiana neighbor who took Arkansas advice across a state line.

I grow in **8a South Arkansas**. I still will not write your jug. I will write how I read those sentences.

I had those three PDFs on the kitchen table one wet May morning. First leaves were finally full size. The interior of the tree was still holding last night’s rain at 9 a.m. That is rust weather getting dressed. It is also the only chapter copper talk belongs to — if fig is on the label in your hand. I drank the coffee. I did not invent a fourth sentence that made the three agree.

## What copper is, and what it is not

Copper is a contact, preventative metal. It sits on a leaf and makes life harder for spores that land. It does not travel through a tree and chase rust out of a yellow map. It can burn a leaf if you mix like a cook instead of like a label. NC State’s Gene Fox, in the Beaufort note, said phytotoxicity is real, and late afternoon may be kinder. That is still not a recipe. That is a warning.

OMRI on a bottle means a certifier said the *product* can be used in organic systems when the *use* matches the label. It does not mean fig is on the label. It does not mean more is better. It does not mean safe for kids and pets if you leave a tank on the tailgate. We will not write that last pairing. “Organic” is not a synonym for legal or effective.

I will not name a brand. Brands change crop lists.

Virginia Tech’s backyard fig guide mentions copper and sulfur talk. It is still subject to the label. I will not upgrade a mention into a summer fog.

## Timing, if you even have a legal use

UAEX’s timing is early. First flush hardened. Repeat once. That is the opposite of a weekly summer fog.

TAMU’s handbook added a fruit clause I respect: do not spray when fruit is a quarter inch if you do not want ugly residue; resume after harvest if you are still in a program. Harvest in 8a is often the rust-loud week. That clause alone should kill the “spray what I see” habit.

If your label does not list fig — or a group that honestly includes fig — you do not have a program. You have a leftover jug from the tomatoes.

**[VERIFY]** the label in your hand this year. Not a screenshot from 2019. Not this paragraph. Not a Facebook crop list. Dates move. Registrations move. A 2025 Louisiana PDF and an older Florida PDF and an Arkansas clinic note can all be documenting their own rules at the same time.

I will not write a statewide copper calendar. Figs are not apples. Anyone who sells you a two-week summer for figs is selling you a habit from another crop.

## Why states disagree

Registration is not a fungus biology class. It is a paperwork and residue and market class. Figs are a minor crop. Companies do not always spend the money to put fig on a label. Extension then tells the truth they are allowed to tell. Louisiana’s 2025 PDF told a hard truth: cultural only. Arkansas’s clinic still had a historic copper sentence. Florida’s older fig PDF said no EPA-approved fungicide for edible figs.

I will not referee a department of agriculture from a driveway. I will not tell a Louisiana reader to “just do what Arkansas said.” I will not tell an Arkansas reader that copper is required. Plenty of trees here live with rust and a rake.

LSU’s variety pages will talk relative rust talk on names like LSU Gold — moderate resistance, not immune. That is foliage genetics, not a pesticide. I will not upgrade it.

## What I actually do

I rake. I open the canopy when the prune is due. I water the dirt. I accept some rust in a steam-bath August.

I do not copper a mosaic leaf. I do not copper a sunburn. I do not copper sour fruit.

I do not copper a tree that is already dropping. UF/IFAS already said that chapter is late.

If a year is so ugly I consider a labeled product, I read the label like it could get me in trouble, because it can. I stay off bloom if bees are the question. I stay off fruit I am about to eat. I do not mix “a little extra.”

Most years I do not get that far. The tree is still there in April.

I walked that same tree in August. Thumb orange. Map loud. Fruit on the wood. I bagged litter. I did not go back to the kitchen table for a fourth sentence. The three official ones had already told me September is not a rescue.

## What I will not write even if the aisle asks

I will not name a copper salt as “what we use.” I will not give you a rate. I will not tell you it is fine everywhere because it is “organic.” I will not write a Vapam story in the same breath. I will not put “safe for kids and pets” next to a tank. I will not tell you to fog ripe fruit because beetles showed up. I will not flatten LSU 2025, UAEX historic timing, and UF’s older no into one hose.

If fig is not on the label, the rust program is a rake. If you do not know whether fig is on the label, you do not have a program yet. Ask the county. Read the paper in your hand. **[VERIFY]**.

8a will still give you a sticky August. The fungus does not care which PDF you liked. Your hose is not a referee between Baton Rouge and Fayetteville.

## A farm-store crop list

I have stood in a farm store with a jug that said fruit tree in friendly type. Fig was not on the crop list. Tomato was. The clerk was kind. Kind is not a label. I put it back.

That is the whole legal education I will give you. Read the crop. Read the state. Read the year. Ask the county if the paper in your hand still matches what they will say out loud. **[VERIFY]**. I will not do that reading for a parish I do not farm.

May on this place can still hold a night rain in the canopy at breakfast. That is the weather UAEX’s historic timing was aimed at — first leaves full size, not a panic after church in September. August is a different room. Steam. Fruit. A thumb that is already orange. I do not take the May sentence into August and call it loyalty to Arkansas.

## The sentence for the shed

**Copper is a legal object, not a personality.** If fig is not on the label in your state, the rust program is a rake. If fig is on the label, the rust program is still a rake, plus a narrow early window you might use.

Anyone who sells you a summer-long copper calendar for figs is selling you a habit from apples. We do not grow apples in this folder.
