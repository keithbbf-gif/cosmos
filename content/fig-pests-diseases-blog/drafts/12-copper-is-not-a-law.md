I am going to put three official sentences on the same table and not pick a favorite.

LSU AgCenter Pub. 1802, 2025: fig diseases in Louisiana are managed with culture and variety **because no fungicides are currently labeled for use on figs in Louisiana**.

UAEX Plant Health Clinic, fig rust note: control depends on timing; **neutral copper** when the first leaves are full size in May or early June, again in three to four weeks.

UF/IFAS older edible-fig notes: **no EPA-approved fungicides for use on edible figs in Florida** in that document.

A blog that flattens those into “just spray copper, it is organic” is lying to somebody. Maybe you. Maybe a Louisiana neighbor who took Arkansas advice across a state line.

I grow in **8a South Arkansas**. I still will not write your jug. I will write how I read those sentences.

## What copper is, and what it is not

Copper is a contact, preventative metal. It sits on a leaf and makes life harder for spores that land. It does not travel through a tree and chase rust out of a yellow map. It can burn a leaf if you mix like a cook instead of like a label. NC State’s Beaufort note said phytotoxicity is real, and late afternoon may be kinder. That is still not a recipe. That is a warning.

OMRI on a bottle means a certifier said the *product* can be used in organic systems when the *use* matches the label. It does not mean fig is on the label. It does not mean more is better. It does not mean safe for kids and pets if you leave a tank on the tailgate.

I will not name a brand. Brands change crop lists.

## Timing, if you even have a legal use

UAEX’s timing is early. First flush hardened. Repeat once. That is the opposite of a weekly summer fog.

TAMU’s handbook added a fruit clause I respect: do not spray when fruit is a quarter inch if you do not want ugly residue; resume after harvest if you are still in a program. Harvest in 8a is often the rust-loud week. That clause alone should kill the “spray what I see” habit.

If your label does not list fig — or a group that honestly includes fig — you do not have a program. You have a leftover jug from the tomatoes.

**[VERIFY]** the label in your hand this year. Not a screenshot from 2019. Not this paragraph.

## Why states disagree

Registration is not a fungus biology class. It is a paperwork and residue and market class. Figs are a minor crop. Companies do not always spend the money to put fig on a label. Extension then tells the truth they are allowed to tell. Louisiana’s 2025 PDF told a hard truth: cultural only. Arkansas’s clinic still had a historic copper sentence. Florida’s older fig PDF said no EPA-approved fungicide for edible figs.

I will not referee a department of agriculture from a driveway. I will not tell a Louisiana reader to “just do what Arkansas said.” I will not tell an Arkansas reader that copper is required. Plenty of trees here live with rust and a rake.

## What I actually do

I rake. I open the canopy when the prune is due. I water the dirt. I accept some rust in a steam-bath August.

I do not copper a mosaic leaf. I do not copper a sunburn. I do not copper sour fruit.

I do not copper a tree that is already dropping. UF/IFAS already said that chapter is late.

If a year is so ugly I consider a labeled product, I read the label like it could get me in trouble, because it can. I stay off bloom if bees are the question. I stay off fruit I am about to eat. I do not mix “a little extra.”

Most years I do not get that far. The tree is still there in April.

## The sentence for the shed

**Copper is a legal object, not a personality.** If fig is not on the label in your state, the rust program is a rake. If fig is on the label, the rust program is still a rake, plus a narrow early window you might use.

Anyone who sells you a summer-long copper calendar for figs is selling you a habit from apples. We do not grow apples in this folder.
