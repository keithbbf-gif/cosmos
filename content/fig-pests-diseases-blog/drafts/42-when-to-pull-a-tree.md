I keep trees too long. That is a personality. It is also how a hole stays occupied by a plant that does not pay rent. This page is the fire rule I want when I am being romantic.

A fig in **8a** can look tired for a dozen reasons this pack already named. Rust strips a canopy and the tree still leafs in April. Mosaic paints a leaf after a 98° week and the tree still finishes fruit. Birds write a harvest you refused to net. A beetle week takes a necklace you refused to pick. Winter kills tips and the stools push. None of those is automatically a funeral.

Occupying is not earning. A live trunk in a sun patch you wanted for jam is still a decision.

## Not a reason

A painted leaf in July. Mosaic on a tree that still finishes fruit. UC IPM is blunt: there is no practical residential cure; live with it or replace a tree that will not perform. The second clause is about performance, not about paint. I have eaten off mosaic wood for years. I will not pull a working Celeste because a group thread learned the word virus.

Rust in a steam-bath year on a tree that leafs in April. LSU’s cycle is leaves and litter. UAEX’s late-flush warning is a winter chapter. A rake and a refusal to feed October are the program. A yellow skirt is not a death certificate.

A beetle week. Green June on a ripe shoulder is a bucket. Sap beetles in a sour eye are sanitation. They are not a reason to take the tree unless you have already decided you will never pick.

Birds on a dark name you refuse to net. That is a you problem. LSU’s Mary Helen Ferguson said netting is the reliable tool. Scare tape habituates. I will not pull a tree to satisfy a mockingbird, and I will not blame the variety for a frame I would not build. Green fruit is contrast, not a force field. The bird drafts already said that.

One bad winter on a multi-stool bush that is already pushing. Virginia Tech said Southeast figs often live as bushes because trunks get hurt. Cut the dead when you can see green. Keep the stools. Do not hold a funeral in January.

A neighbor who learned the word nematode. Galls on an old tool tree that still makes jam are a living-with chapter. Water. Mulch. Do not put the next baby in that spoonful. Do not solarize a twenty-year plant because a forum said eradicate.

A late freeze after budbreak that fried the first leaves. Wait. Then saw. The wood chapter is not a pull chapter.

One split week after a leftover tropical rain. Tight-eye names still split if the week was stupid. Write a kitchen list. Do not write a refund.

## A reason

White fungal skin at the collar and a summer crash. The hole is retired. UF said remove the tree and the roots, and do not replant that spoonful. I believe them. I will not write a drench. I will not put a rare name in the grave. That is a site funeral, not a variety funeral.

A young rare name on galled sand that stall-yellowed two seasons. Lift it. Do not “toughen” it. UF said young plants take the hit hardest. NC State’s honest line, as we use it, is that established infested figs do not get a homeowner chemical reset. A baby is not established. A baby in last year’s okra sand was a donation. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said pots if nematodes. This is the divorce when you ignored that sentence.

Three years of no crop on a name you planted to eat, after weather you can name, after you actually picked on time. The name is a tourist. I have kept tourists. They steal sun. They steal a hole. They steal the morning I owe a tree that works.

Scale crust you will not clean on a shop plant that is also a mite carpet. Trash is sanitation. Two hundred pots is not a reason to keep a farm in January. The shop-scale line already exists. Fire the plant on a Tuesday.

A tree that is a raccoon ladder against a fence you will not change, and you are tired of lying about jam. Wildlife is not a pesticide label. If you will not net, will not pick, and will not move the tree, the honest pull is you admitting the site.

A pot that drowned twice in a saucer and cooked the roots, and you keep the corpse because the tag was expensive. The tag is not the plant.

A hole that ponds every rain week and you will not mound and will not tile and will not move. Wet feet will fake every bucket. The tree is not the stubborn one.

## How I pull

I take the label first. I have thrown away names with the wood. A piece of tape on a stick in a drawer is cheaper than a year of “what was that.”

I look at the collar and the roots if the story downstairs would change the next planting. Tool plants and corpses. Not a healthy rare pot for curiosity. White skin: retire the hole. Beads on feeders: retire the hole for figs, or admit it is a tool-tree hole only. Brown mush and a lake: fix the water or pick another zip code on the same property.

I do not plant the replacement in the same spoonful if the story was nematodes or Armillaria or a lake. I will plant a pot thirty feet away the same afternoon. I will not plant an in-ground eater in a grave I just opened and call it hope.

I do not burn the county. I do not need a ceremony. I need the hole back or I need to admit the hole is done.

Cuttings off a working flavor are allowed before the funeral if the wood is the flavor and not the grave. Mosaic comes along. We already said that. Galls do not, if you wash the dirt off the stool and start in clean mix. Armillaria is leftover root, not a cutting you washed. Do not stick that cutting back in the same grave.

I stage wood I do not want. I do not leave a standing corpse as a totem. I do not chip a cankered or collar-killed tree into the next fig’s mulch the same day.

I tell myself the bucket out loud. If I cannot name it, I am not pulling because I learned a word. I am pulling because I am tired. Tired is allowed. Tired should not pretend to be diagnosis.

## Pots make this kinder

A collection can fire a plant on a Tuesday. In-ground is a marriage. That is why rare names stay in mix when the yard is a rumor. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said pots. [An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said do not root in mystery dirt. This is the divorce clause.

A pot I will not water is still a pull. Neglect is not a nematode. A pot I will not dump after a rain week is a dish funeral. I have held those too long too.

If the only in-ground holes I trust are two tool trees that feed us, the collection stays in pots. That is not a lesser orchard. That is how this place stays honest.

## 8a time

Give a plant a summer and a winter before you write a novel. A first-year fig can look stupid in August and ordinary in June of year two. Do not give it a decade of a hole you wanted for a tree that feeds you.

A rust year plus a late flush plus a January surprise can make year three look like failure when it was a weather stack. Count live stools in April. Then decide.

A mosaic year that got loud in heat is not a new infection. Do not pull in July because the leaf is theater.

Motion is the point. A dead name on a tag in a drawer is cheaper than a live name that steals a sun patch.

I would pot the next rare name. I would leave a living tool tree that still pays rent, galls and paint and all. I would not put anything in an Armillaria spoonful. I would not fog a tree I am about to fire “to give it one more chance.” The chance was cultural. The bottle is not a stay of execution.

Pulling a tree is not a failure if you learned the bucket. It is a failure if you plant the same bucket again and call it hope.

If you want a shorter rule: paint and rust and birds are usually not the funeral. A white collar, a galled baby, a tourist name, a shop farm, a hole you will not fix — those are. Name it. Then move. 8a will grow another fig. It will not grow another July if you spend it on a plant you already know is done.
