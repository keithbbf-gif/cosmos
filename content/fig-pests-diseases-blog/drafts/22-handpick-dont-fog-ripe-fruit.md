I have watched a person oil up a backpack sprayer while the figs were already soft. That is not IPM. That is a residue you will argue with at the table.

UC’s fig-beetle guideline did not recommend chemicals. Their sour-rot guideline did not recommend a yeasticide. NC State’s green June note is a turf-and-ornamental chapter, not a permission slip to carbaryl a jam crop. I will not name a fruit-tree cocktail. I will not write PHI math for a product I am not putting on this site.

The honest tools in a backyard 8a week are ugly: your hands, a bucket, a morning alarm, a trash run.

He had the tank mixed before I finished saying the fruit was ripe. Green June beetles (*Cotinis nitida*) were loud in the canopy. He wanted them gone by supper. I wanted supper. Those are not the same job.

## Morning is a method

Green June beetles and a lot of chafers are slower when it is cool. I walk then anyway because the fruit is cooler and the birds have already clocked in. A beetle in the hand goes in soapy water. I do not make a speech about it.

Japanese beetles — if your county has *Popillia japonica* — go in the same bucket. Different insect. Same hands. Confirm the white tufts before you write the name. Do not hang a lure bag over the tree you are about to pick. That is a later draft and a bad neighbor move.

A twenty-foot turkey laughs at a bucket. Then the method is a ladder you will actually use, or a net, or a decision that the top third is bird rent. I have paid bird rent. I have also lowered a tree over years so I could pick it. That is a prune chapter. It is also beetle control.

I take two containers. Keepers in one. Vinegar and beetle-ruined fruit in the other. They do not share a bowl and they do not stay under the tree.

I walk the ground. Windfall is a second cafeteria. UC’s sour-rot sanitation is for any overripe fruit, not just figs. A peach dump is a beetle farm.

## What a fog pretends to solve

It pretends the tree is an apple orchard with a spray card and a processor. You are a person who will eat a fig unwashed in the driveway. Even if you wash, the ostiole holds what it holds.

It pretends you can miss the bees, the neighbor’s garden, the kid who grabs a low fruit. “Safe if you follow the label” is a real sentence for a labeled use. It is not a dare to invent a use. If fig (or the right group) is not on the label in this state in this year, it is not a fig spray. **[VERIFY]**. I will not flatten LSU, UAEX, and UF into one fruit-tree jug. LSU Pub. 1802 (2025) already said no fungicides labeled for figs in Louisiana. Insecticide labels are their own paperwork. I will not write you a cocktail to paper over that.

It pretends the next beetle is not flying in from the compost. Sanitation is the population. The fog is a mood.

It pretends mosaic or rust is involved because the leaf is ugly. A painted leaf did not chew that shoulder. Orange grit did not ferment that eye. Put the leaf chapters down.

It pretends “organic” means you can fog ripe fruit you will eat tonight. Organic is not a synonym for safe, legal, or empty of residue.

## What I will do instead of a mood

Pick ripe. Pick almost-ripe if birds and beetles are winning and you will finish them on the counter — an Oregon MG said that about squirrels; I will not turn it into a law, but I have done it.

Remove the vinegar ones.

Do not run a rotting-fruit pile under the canopy.

Net the tree you care about, on a frame, staked, checked. LSU’s Mary Helen Ferguson said netting is the reliable bird tool. Scare tape habituates. Mesh that stops a June beetle is not sap-beetle cloth. I still like a frame. Netting that pools on fruit traps wildlife.

Handpick what you can reach without becoming a story.

If a commercial planting needs a labeled insecticide, that is a licensed conversation with an agent and a label that says fig. This folder is not that farm. **[VERIFY]** if you are that farm. Do not copy a peach schedule.

I would pot a rare name I cannot reach. I would not fog a rare name I plan to eat off of.

## A small tree and a tall one

A pot I can walk around is a ten-minute morning. I can see shoulders. I can hear the loud fliers. I can take the necklace off before it paints the leaf.

A tall turkey is a different contract. I have watched people buy a longer wand instead of a shorter tree. The wand still puts residue on fruit. The shorter tree takes years and a saw. I have paid both kinds of time. I like the saw better when I am honest.

Bird rent on the top third is a sentence I will say out loud. Jack already said birds are the part of fig season he likes least after a dead tree. I will not mash our pens. I will say the top I cannot pick is not a reason to fog the bottom I will eat.

## Kids, dogs, and the tailgate jug

I have a separate draft for the backyard fog. The short version belongs here: if the fruit is ripe, the animal you are protecting it from is not the only animal on the property. A jug that says “fruit trees” is still a poison if the crop is not on the list or the timing is a lie.

I will not write “safe for kids and pets” next to any pesticide talk. That sentence is how people stop reading labels.

I would rather lose a necklace than write a clever mix.

A dog that invents desserts does not care that you “only hit the upper canopy.” Gravity still works. So does a low fig.

## The Southwestern cousin and the trap bag

If your cousin in a dry fig belt is reading UC on *Cotinis mutabilis* / *C. texana*, they were told no chemical controls in that guideline. Do not import their flood talk onto a South Arkansas clay hole, and do not import a Japanese-beetle trap onto a county that does not have Japanese beetles. Write the insect you hold.

I will not sell carbaryl, malathion, or a fruit-tree cocktail on ripe figs you intend to eat as casual backyard advice. That is the guardrail. I will stand on it when the backpack is already wet.

## The table test

Would I eat this fig tonight. If yes, the beetle gets a bucket or the fruit gets picked early. The sprayer stays in the shed. If that feels weak, grow fewer names and pick the ones you have. A collection you cannot harvest is a beetle farm with labels.

I have eaten a fig in the driveway with juice on my wrist. That fig is why I will not write the fog. The beetle can have the bucket. I want the fruit.
