LSU’s rust cycle is not a mystery. The fungus spends the off season on the leaves you left. Wind and rain put it back to work. You can talk copper until the truck rusts. If you leave a wet rust quilt under the tree, you already voted.

Ferrin and Overstreet (LSU AgCenter Pub. 3159) and the 2025 fig disease note (Pub. 1802) both keep the cultural sentence: collect and destroy diseased leaves. Do not compost them into the next crop of spots. UAEX’s clinic note is the other half of the same honesty: a canopy can go in two or three weeks, and a late flush can walk into an 8a winter soft.

I like a rake. I like a tarp. I like a bag I will not open in the compost against the trunk. That is the program. It is not glamorous. It works in 8a because our trees actually drop those leaves, and they drop them early when rust is loud.

## A Thursday after the storm

I walked the drip the morning after a two-inch rain. The driveway was already steaming. Under the fig the dirt was a wet plaster of yellow-brown leaves. You could smell the mash. That is not the pine-straw mulch I meant to keep. That is a spore bed with a splash zone.

I pulled a tarp. I raked onto it. I bagged. I did not till the quilt in and call it organic matter. I did not leave a ring “for the worms.” The worms I am thinking about in this draft are not the ones you want.

The neighbor’s ditch still had leaves. I did not sterilize the county. I stopped my own splash.

## What I pick up, and what I leave

I pick up the fig leaves under *that* tree. I do not need to sterilize the county. I need to stop a splash zone.

I do not till rust leaves into the root zone and call it organic matter. That is burial, not sanitation. LSU said do not compost diseased leaves. I will compost a lot of things. I will not build next year’s inoculum on purpose.

Grass clippings from a clean lawn can still be mulch if I keep them off the trunk. Rusty fig leaves are not grass clippings.

Fruit mummies and sour necklaces are a **beetle** job. I pick those too. They are not rust. They still do not get to sit in the shade as a cafeteria. One walk can serve two buckets. UC IPM’s sour-rot pages want sanitation and a prompt pick, not a yeasticide. I can do that with the same hands that hold the rake.

## When I rake

After the first heavy drop. After a storm that flattened a yellow skirt. In fall when the rest comes down — rust-stripped trees finish early; a clean tree may hold into a proper frost.

I do not obsess over every leaf in the neighbor’s ditch. I obsess over the drip line I control.

If I have pots on gravel, I still pull leaves out of the saucers and the gravel. A pot block can hold a rust humidistat. 8a August will fog a tight pot row overnight. Leaves in a saucer stay wet until noon. That is a closet you built with plastic.

I rake when the dirt is workable. I do not grind a wet clay smear into paste and call it done. I wait a day if I have to, then I come back. The spores will wait. They are patient. I can be too.

Winter sanitation is the same job with a coat on. A tree that stripped in August still has a quilt if you never bagged it. January is not too late to pick up what you left. It is late for that year’s canopy. It is on time for the next one.

## Air is the other half of the rake

A rake without a hole in the canopy is a man cleaning a closet he will not open. Prune so morning sun hits the interior. John R. Pyzner’s LSU fig production note (Pub. 1529) and the Johnson / Louisiana talk we have used on FigRoots wanted space and a center that is not a wet room. I want that too.

I do not butcher a fig in August to “let it breathe” and steal a crop that is two weeks from jam. I take the dead wood. I wait for the prune I already owed the variety. Breba people and main-crop people argue the calendar. **[VERIFY]** your own wood. The rust draft is not a license to chainsaw a July tree.

Water on the dirt. Morning if overhead. That is sanitation you do with a hose. UF/IFAS Panhandle said the chemical chapter is late once spots are obvious. The hose chapter is not late. It is just earlier in the day than people like.

## What I will not add to the rake

A lime dust “to sweeten the soil and kill fungus.” A sulfur bomb. A bleach walk. Those are how you write a different problem.

Chickens under a fig will eat a lot of things and scratch a lot of dirt. They are not a rust certification. They will also eat fruit. I like chickens. I do not list them as a fungicide.

I will not write a weekly copper summer and call the rake optional. I will not compost the blanket against the trunk and call it feeding the soil. I will not tell you a dry September eradicated anything. The leaves left. The litter you left is still a vote.

I will not sell you a personality. The aisle wants a program that looks like a jug. The program is a tarp and a decision about compost.

## The shop and the pot row

I still pull leaves out of a warm shop in January. A rust-stripped tree in the yard finished early. A potted name under plastic held a few leaves that went dirty and sat in the saucer. Same fungus. Smaller room. I dump the saucer on the knotted side of the yard, not on the clean bench. I do not want that water walking to a seedling tray.

After a humid night in August the pot row looks like it slept in a cloud. Gravel holds the quilt. If I skip that walk because the in-ground tree got the tarp, I left a second starting lineup in plastic.

Pyzner’s spacing talk and the ordinary 8a habit agree on one homely thing: you should be able to walk under the tree and see morning light in the middle. If I cannot, the rake is doing half a job.

## Next May

If you are in a state where a label still talks fig and copper, the clinic notes want you *before* the map is loud. UAEX: first leaves full size, then three or four weeks. That only makes sense if you also raked. Copper on a spore bed you left in place is a personality.

If you are in Louisiana as Pub. 1802 described it in 2025, you do not have a labeled fig fungicide in that document. You have a rake. That is not a lesser program. That is the program they wrote down.

**[VERIFY]** both sentences before you treat them as this year’s law. Labels move. PDFs get revised. The rake does not need a revision to stay true.

I can live with a tree that rusts some years. I cannot live with a grower who will not pick up the leaves and still wants a miracle. The rake is the miracle. It just does not look like one on a shelf.
