I have watched a person spray a sooty fig for rust because the leaf was black and they were tired. The black smeared on a thumb. Rust does not smear like that. Sugar grew a fungus that eats sugar. The leak was a bump or an aphid. The ants were employees.

This is a short bucket that saves a jug. I am still going to walk it, because the driveway conversation is never as short as the bucket.

## Two ant meetings

**Honeydew meeting.** Ants on wood and leaves, not necessarily on ripe fruit. Sticky. Soot. Scale, mealybug, aphid, whitefly. The ants may fight you for the bump. They are not the bump.

**Jam meeting.** Ants on ripe figs because the fruit split or a bird opened a door. That is harvest. Pick. The ant is not a disease. A necklace will pull every sweet tooth in the county, six legs or two.

Do not mash the meetings. An ant on a soft fig in July is not proof of scale. A commuting column in January in a shop is not proof the fruit is ready.

I walk both meetings the same way I sort a peck from a grab from a sap-beetle eye. Look at the sugar. Name it.

A triangular peck and ants on that shoulder: bird opened the door, jam meeting. A pile of rinds at dawn and ants: raccoon shopped, jam meeting. Pink syrup at the eye and ants: souring, yeast, taxi, still a pick. Sticky wood in a warm shop and ants: leak, bump, honeydew meeting.

Green fruit is not a force field against ants. Soft necks still drip. Contrast is a bird-eye chapter.

## Follow the line

I follow the line. Trunk. Branch. Underside. Pot rim. In a 70-degree shop in January the line is easy. On an in-ground turkey in August the line can hide in grass you should not have piled as a mattress.

I scrape or pick the leak I can reach. Toothbrush on a pot. Fingernail on a twig I can spare. The scale page is the bump chapter. This page is the commute.

I wipe soot if I need to see the leaf. I do not copper the soot. Sooty mold is a sugar fungus. It is not *Cerotelium*. LSU Pub. 1802 (2025) said no fungicides labeled for figs in Louisiana in that document. Other states have historic copper talk aimed at rust, not at a smear. **[VERIFY]**. A contact metal will not evict a bump.

I do not start a yard-wide ant war as fig IPM. Baits are a house and a pet conversation. They can reduce farmhands. They will not replace a toothbrush on a scale crust.

I keep food off the drip line. A pet bowl under a fig is a raccoon chapter and an ant chapter. Last August’s fruit dump is a chafer nursery and an ant cafeteria. Compost, not there.

## What I will not do

I will not write a trunk insecticide paint as a backyard dare.

I will not flood a nest in the root zone and then wonder why the fig sulked. Clay that already holds a lake after a two-inch rain does not need a hose down a hole. Sand that never holds a drink does not need me to collapse a cavity next to a feeder root.

I will not blame mosaic for a sticky leaf. Paint does not leak sugar. The virus stays in the wood. UC said live with it or replace a tree that will not perform. An ant column is not a reason to cut a mosaic tree down.

I will not call sooty mold “black rust.” Words matter. Rust is orange grit. Soot is a film.

I will not fog ripe fruit because ants found jam. Residue on a fig you will eat tonight is still a bad trade. Pick the splitters. Take the necklace off. UC said no yeasticide. An ant spray on ripe fruit is not a souring program.

I will not write poison for wildlife because ants and raccoons shared a bowl. No peanut stories.

I will not hang scare tape for ants. Ferguson said tape habituates for birds. Ants do not watch television.

## 8a shops and 8a yards

Warm winter pots leak more. Outdoor wood with a real cold snap — 8a still means winter can bite — often resets the pretty insects better than a heater room. If your ants-and-soot story is a shop story, the fix is the shop and the bump. Light. Not a jungle. A look in January.

If it is a July fruit story, the fix is a pick. Dawn. Bowl. Pecked fruit off so the jam meeting does not become a beetle meeting.

The same week can hold both. A pot on the bench with a sticky rim. An in-ground Gold with an open eye after rain. Ants at both. Two names. Two jobs.

After a tropical leftover the ground is wet and every sweet thing is loud. Ants get busier. So do sap beetles. So do birds. Walk. Do not buy a bottle that says “ant and blight” and call it a fig program.

## The leak is the job

Aphids on a soft flush show up some springs and then a hot week cooks them. I still look. A blast of water in the morning on a pot I can handle is a move. I do not write a soap ratio as law.

Mealybugs in a shop are a white leak. Same commute. Same rule. Scrape. Isolate a crusted pot from a clean cutting.

Whitefly is less my 8a fig story than a greenhouse story. If you have it, you will see the puff. Still a leak. Still not rust.

The ants are honest. They go where the sugar is. Find the sugar. Name it. Honeydew or jam. Bump or split or peck. The leaf disease aisle can stay closed.

I have followed a line from a porch post to a pot I forgot on the bench in February. The commute ended at a rim that stuck to my finger. January in a closed shop can feel like June for a bump. I scraped. I washed the leaf. I did not mail the county a sooty leaf and ask for a rust ID.

In July I have followed a different line to a split Gold after a two-inch rain. Same six legs. Different sugar. I picked. I did not copper the drip.

A pecan year will pull ants to the other side of the yard. A quiet fruit week will send them back to the shop rim you forgot. I do not forecast ants. I forecast whether I left sugar on wood or on a necklace. The commute writes the map. I just read it.

I would follow a line before I bought a jug. I would wipe a smear before I named a virus. I would pick a necklace before I declared a curse. The ant is a pointer. It is not the disease.
