If your 8a county does not have Japanese beetles, skip this page and go pick figs. If it does, somebody will sell you a yellow bag and a lure and a feeling of doing something.

The lure is floral scent plus a sex attractant. It works. That is the problem. It works on beetles that were going to the neighbor’s roses, and then it works on the ones that miss the bag and sit on your fruit.

I will not pretend I ran a university trap trial. I will tell you the extension habit I will not fight: **do not hang the trap next to the crop you care about.** Some agents say put it a long way downwind or skip it on a small lot. **[VERIFY]** your own county’s current sentence. I will give you mine: I will not buy the bag as fig control.

A neighbor hung one on the fence that shares my Celeste. He was proud of the bag. The bag was busy. So was the fig. I asked him to move it. He moved it toward his roses. That was the first honest use I had seen.

## ID before you shop

*Popillia japonica*: smaller than a green June. Copper wing covers. Metallic green head and thorax. White tufts along the abdomen. They skeletonize leaves. They chew fruit. They come in committees.

A green June beetle (*Cotinis nitida*) is a loud velvet walnut, bronze-yellow margins, the animal I actually meet most weeks in this humid 8a East / South yard. The Southwestern figeater (*C. mutabilis*, also handled as *C. texana* in UC’s fig chapter) is a cousin with a horn and a different dirt story. A Japanese beetle is a shiny dime with a plan. If you cannot see the tufts, you do not get to write the name.

Range is still moving. A Facebook map is not your driveway. If you have not held one, do not hang a lure “just in case.” You are mailing an invitation.

Skeletonized leaves are a clue, not a verdict. Heat, rust strip, and a hungry June beetle can all make an ugly shirt. Photograph the insect, not just the lace.

I will not write one beetle for the whole country. West of the Japanese beetle line you may only have June beetles and sap beetles. East and north of a spreading front you may have all three. **[VERIFY]** the county, not the catalog.

## A walk the week the committee arrives

Morning. They are slower. So am I.

I hold one and I look for tufts. If I have *Popillia*, I pick fruit and I pick beetles into soapy water. Same bucket as the green Junes. Different insect, same hands.

I do not oil a backpack. Ripe figs and insecticides are a food argument. UC’s fig-beetle guideline (written for the Southwestern cousin) did not recommend chemicals. I will not invent a Japanese-beetle fog for fruit I will eat tonight.

I check the fence for a yellow bag I did not hang. If I already own one and I will not throw it away, it goes far from the figs and far from the neighbor I like. I do not add a second lure because the first one “is working.” Working is the problem.

I net the tree I care about, on a frame, staked, checked. Birds plus Japanese beetles is a real week. One cloth, two jobs, still not sap-beetle cloth. Netting that pools on fruit traps wildlife. LSU’s Ferguson piece is a bird sermon. I will borrow the frame and still pick.

I walk windfall. Sanitation helps more than a bag. A fermenting necklace is a meeting even without a lure.

## What I do if I have them

Handpick in the morning into soapy water.

Protect fruit with a frame and net if the committee is a crowd.

Keep the tree from looking like the only green buffet on a brown lawn if you can. That is not always possible. I will not tell you to plant a trap crop I did not run.

Milky spore is a grub story for *Japanese* beetle larvae in some soils over years. It is not a July fruit rescue. It does not impress green June the same way people hope. I will not sell it as a fig orchard program.

Lawn grub insecticides are a turf argument with water and kids and labels. They are not a fig spray. I will not write them onto a drip line.

I will not write carbaryl or malathion onto ripe figs you intend to eat as casual backyard advice. If a commercial planting thinks it needs a labeled insecticide, that is an agent and a label that says fig. **[VERIFY]** if you are that farm.

## The bag as theater

I have seen the bag fill and the grower smile. I have also seen the fig next to the bag look like a meeting. The smile is the product. The meeting is the cost.

The lure does not know your property line. It knows roses, grapes, linden, and a ripe fig. A small lot cannot put “far away” far enough. That is why some agents shrug and say skip it. I shrug with them.

If you already own a trap and you will not throw it away, hang it far from the figs and far from the neighbor you like. Check local advice. Do not add a second lure because the first one “is working.”

Do not hang it “just to see.” Seeing is how you host.

I will not hang one over the jam tree to “draw them off the fruit.” That sentence is how the fruit becomes the lobby.

## What the bag does not do

It does not replace a morning pick.

It does not keep sap beetles out of an open eye. Different insect. Different draft.

It does not cure mosaic, rust, or a wilted top. People will still try to make one purchase do four jobs.

It does not make a green June go away. *Cotinis nitida* is not shopping from that lure. If your only beetle is a loud velvet walnut, you bought the wrong theater.

It does not impress a Southwestern figeater story onto a South Arkansas stool. Read UC if you live in that dirt. Do not run their flood talk on a clay hole, and do not hang their cousin’s bag either.

## 8a is not one beetle county

West of the Japanese beetle line you may only have June beetles and sap beetles. East and north of a spreading front you may have all three. Write the insect you hold. Buy the tool that matches. A lure bag is a tool for a person who wants to count beetles in a field. It is a poor tool for a jam tree by the porch.

I would rather miss a few June beetles with my hands than host a Japanese beetle convention over the Celeste. The bag will still be on sale. That is not a recommendation.

If your county does not have them, leave the bag on the shelf and go pick. If it does, pick anyway. The lure will still be trying to sell you a feeling. I will sell you a bucket and a no.
