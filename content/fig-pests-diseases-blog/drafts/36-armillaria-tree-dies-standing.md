Most tired figs in this pack are rust, nematodes, wet feet, or a winter. Once in a while a tree dies like it was switched off in the heat. Leaves hang. The fruit stops. You water. You wait a day. The tree does not negotiate.

I have seen that look blamed on “the virus,” on beetles, on a fertilizer the neighbor swears by. I have also seen it blamed on root-knot because this folder talks about galls a lot. Galls are a years-long tired. This is a different funeral.

UF’s older fig notes call that mushroom root rot — *Armillaria tabescens* in their wording. Peel bark at the soil line. A creamy white fungal skin. Mushrooms may not show before the tree is gone. All trunks may not die the same year. Dead trees must be totally removed — roots and soil. Do not replant. The fungus lives in leftover root.

I have not made this the default diagnosis. It is the diagnosis I want you to *check* before you write a nematode novel and plant a rare name in the corpse hole.

## How it sorts from the other root stories

I scrape the collar before I invent a story. I do it on a tree that already failed, or on a stool I am already taking out. I do not peel a working rare name for a blog photo.

**Nematodes.** Beads on feeders. A slow tired top. Years, not a Thursday. You can live with a tool tree. You do not put the next baby in that spoonful. NC State’s fig-disease talk, as we have used it on FigRoots, is that established plants do not get a homeowner chemical reset. That is a living-with chapter. It is not a summer crash with white skin.

**Wet feet.** A lake after a two-inch rain. Clay that smells sour. Roots brown and mushy, not webbed in a white sheet. A pot without a hole, or a saucer you forgot. Dump the dish. Ordinary root rot from water is ugly. It is still not Armillaria until the collar says so.

**Cold.** Brown wood from the tips. A line. Spring that tells the truth. Limb blight and a frost stub share a zip code with each other, not with a collar fungus. The saw draft owns that walk.

**Planted too deep.** A girdling root collar, mulch volcano, trunk buried to the first scaffold. The tree can wilt in heat like it was switched off. Scrape anyway. Look for the flare that should be at grade and is not. I have killed a fig with kindness and a shovel deeper than the nursery line. That funeral is my fault. It is not a mushroom drench.

**Armillaria.** A summer crash. A white skin at the collar under the bark. A site that already killed a tree, often an oak, an old fruit tree, a stump you ground and called gone. Mushrooms at the base *may* show. UF said they may not show before the tree is gone. Absence of a toadstool is not a clean bill.

Cotton root rot shows up in TAMU’s handbook as a pointer to another chapter. Texas has that conversation. I will not fake an 8a-Arkansas field ID of *Phymatotrichum* from a porch. If you are on that soil, ask the desk that knows it. **[VERIFY]**.

If I cannot sort it and the tree is a corpse, I still treat the hole as guilty until a weekday desk says otherwise. I do not need a Latin name to refuse the same spoonful.

## What I do if the collar is white

I do not spray the canopy. I do not fertilize a corpse.

I take the tree out. As much root as I can stand. UF said dead trees must be totally removed — roots and soil. I am not a crew with a backhoe. I get what I can. I do not grind the stump and call it clean if the roots still web the hole. The leftover *is* the inoculum.

I do not plant a fig in that spoonful. UF said avoid replanting. I believe them. A pot collection can move thirty feet and live. An in-ground replacement in the same grave is a donation.

I do not “solarize for a weekend” and declare the site reset. I do not bleach the hole. I do not pour a drench I saw under a different crop name and invent a fig rate.

If the plant was a multi-stool bush, UF’s note that all trunks may not die the same year is the sentence I keep. A live stool next to a dead one is not proof the site is fine.

I take the label off before I forget the name. If the wood was the flavor and the grave was the fungus, I do not stick a cutting back in that dirt. Mosaic rides cuttings. Armillaria rides leftover root. Those are different taxis.

## What I will not do

I will not write a fungicide drench as a homeowner Armillaria fix. UF said no EPA-approved fungicides for edible figs in that Florida document. Even where a product exists for other plants, this site will not invent a fig rate. LSU Pub. 1802 in 2025 said no fungicides labeled for figs in Louisiana. There is nothing here to mix.

I will not blame mosaic. A painted leaf in July on a tree that still holds fruit is a virus we already live with. That is not a white skin at the soil line.

I will not tell you every sudden wilt is Armillaria. A girdling collar will wilt a tree. A lake will wilt a tree. A pot that cooked in a saucer will wilt a tree. Look. Dig. Do not only theorize.

I will not tell you to plant a “resistant fig” in the grave as a cleanser. I will not keep the dead trunk as a totem. Get it off the place.

## Site memory

Old oak stumps. Old fruit trees. A yard that has killed things at the collar and then grown a circle of toadstools after rain. That is a memory in the wood underground.

Figs are not a cleansing crop. If I am planting in-ground on a place with that memory, I ask more questions than I like. Where did the last tree fail. Did anyone peel the bark. Did you grind and leave the roots.

Pots are allowed to be the answer. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said pots if nematodes. [An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already said do not root in mystery local soil. This page adds a third reason the hole can be a rumor: leftover root that still feeds a fungus.

I will not tuck a mail-order name into a stump circle because the sun is good there. If I have to use a yard with a bad memory, I pick a different zip code on the same property. Thirty feet is not a ritual. I still will not call that a guarantee. I will call it less stupid.

## The pot that is not this funeral

A collection in dishes after a tropical rain can rot roots and wilt a top in a day. That is a drowned pot. Dump the saucer. Ordinary sour rot of roots is enough. I do not write Armillaria on every overwatered three-gallon. Shop plants that fail in a warm room in January are usually scale, mites, or a hose habit. Do not mash the calendar.

## 8a honesty

We have clay holes that hold a lake and sand pockets that never hold a drink, sometimes in the same county. We also have old home places with a stump in every third backyard.

That mix is why I want the collar check on a summer death, and why I will not make Armillaria the story I tell about every tired fig in August. Rust will strip a canopy and leave a live tree. Mosaic will paint a leaf and leave a live tree. Root-knot will nibble a decade. This one takes the standing tree and the hole.

A funeral that is this fungus is a site funeral, not a variety funeral. The next name can live. It does not have to live in the same grave.

If you want a bottle, there is not one I will write. If you want a move, take the tree, take the roots you can stand, retire the spoonful, and put the next fig where the ground has not already written this sentence.
