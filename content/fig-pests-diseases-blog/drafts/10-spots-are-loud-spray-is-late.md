The Panhandle extension piece I keep coming back to is not kind, which is why I trust it. *Fig Leaves Dropping Early?* — UF/IFAS, 18 September 2017. Warm humid weather. *Cerotelium fici*. Then the line growers skip: once you see those yellowish-green to reddish-brown spots, **it is too late to provide any control**.

People hate that sentence. It makes a jug feel stupid. The jug was already stupid if the leaf is a map.

I stood in a driveway in early September with a man who had just bought the jug. The canopy behind him was already a yellow-brown quilt. Fruit was sizing. He wanted a rescue. I asked him when he first saw flecks. He thought June. He had been watching the figs, not the undersides. The schedule was written in June. September was a receipt.

Rust chemistry, where it exists at all, is a **preventative** conversation. UAEX’s clinic note (Smith and Pavel) talks copper when the first leaves reach full size, then again three or four weeks later. TAMU’s handbook said start when first leaves are grown, and do not spray when fruit is a quarter inch if you care about residue on the crop. That is spring-into-early-summer talk. It is not a September rescue after a steam-bath August in 8a.

## What “late” means on a leaf

A fungus like rust colonizes, then shows. The yellow you see is the office already open. A contact spray on that leaf does not turn the office back into a green factory. You may feel better. The leaf is still in the drop queue.

I have watched people coat a half-stripped tree and then blame the product when the rest of the leaves fall on schedule. The schedule was written in July.

If you want chemistry in the states where a label still pretends to include this crop — **[VERIFY]**, because LSU AgCenter Pub. 1802 (2025) says Louisiana has none for figs — you wanted that decision when the first flush hardened, not when the neighbor noticed.

UF’s older edible-fig notes said no EPA-approved fungicides for edible figs in Florida in that document. Arkansas’s clinic still had a historic copper sentence. Those can sit on the same table. I will not flatten them into a hose recipe for a tree that is already dropping.

## What still works in August

Rake. The spores on the litter are next year’s starting lineup and this year’s splash if the rain keeps coming. LSU (Ferrin and Overstreet, Pub. 3159, and Pub. 1802): collect and destroy. Do not compost them into the drip line.

I did this on a Saturday after I had watered the pot row at dark the night before. Leaves stuck to wet gravel. The saucers held a tea-colored puddle. That is not organic matter you meant to feed. That is inoculum you housed in plastic. Tarp. Bags. Out of the drip.

Open the tree next chance you have a prune that does not steal a crop you still want. Air. Morning sun on the interior. NC State’s Gene Fox put sanitation in grower English for a tree that had already dropped. I will not upgrade that into a chainsaw in July.

Stop watering the midnight leaf. Morning, on the dirt. 8a August nights do not dry a canopy. They sit on it. Dusk irrigation on a pot block is a gift to the fungus you are angry at.

Keep the tree from stacking drought on top of a missing canopy. A rust-stripped fig still has roots. Those roots still sit in 8a heat. Mulch. Water. Do not “punish” a tree that already lost its leaves.

Do not spray ripe or nearly ripe fruit to satisfy a leaf grudge. TAMU already warned about residue while fruit is small. I will add the obvious: you are about to eat that skin.

Pick the sour ones so beetles do not join the week. That is a different bucket. It still belongs to August.

## The late flush problem

UAEX said a tree that replaces a canopy late can go into winter tender and take cold. That is an 8a sentence. We still see teens. A fig that thinks October is a second spring will pay in March.

I do not force a late fertilizer bump to “grow the leaves back.” I let the tree shut up. I rake. I protect wood I still want if an arctic blast is on the map — that is a winter draft, not a rust spray.

I have watched a tree push a pretty October skirt after an August strip. It looked like hope. It looked like a target when the temperature forgot its manners in March.

If a tree rust-stripped three years running and also sits on galled sand, you have a vigor pile-up. Change the room for the next name. Do not buy a fourth jug.

## The week people still own

Late July, before the map is loud, I still walk undersides. That is not a spray week unless a label already put you in an early program. It is a rake-and-air week if you missed the first flecks. I have pinched a leaf in that window and gotten only a little stain, not the full cheap-eyeshadow thumb. The office was opening. I still did not fog fruit that was already more than a rumor on the wood.

TAMU’s residue clause sits in that same calendar. Quarter-inch fruit. A crop you intend to eat. I will not write a rescue that coats it because I am angry at a leaf.

A pot block at dusk is the other late I still own. I used to water at dark because the day was a skillet. The leaves stayed wet until the next sticky morning. I moved the hose to breakfast. The fungus did not send a thank-you. The closet got smaller.

## Why I am repeating the no

Because the algorithm will show you a rescue. Copper every two weeks. Milk. Baking soda. Compost tea as a fungicide. Neem as a vaccine. Virginia Tech mentioned neem as a “more natural” aside. I will not upgrade an aside into an August fog.

Milk and soda are how you write a mess on a leaf that was already leaving. Compost tea is a religion. I will not join it on a rust canopy.

I will not write a statewide copper calendar. Labels move. States disagree. **[VERIFY]** the jug in your hand, this year, this state, this crop list. If fig is not on it, you do not have a late program. You never had an early one either.

I will not name a brand. I will not tell a Louisiana reader to do what an Arkansas clinic note said in a different year. I will not tell you the spots “cleared” because a dry week arrived. The leaves left. That is not a cure.

## What I will do instead of the jug

Walk the whole tree, not the prettiest leaf. Flip ten. If the thumb is orange and the map is loud, I bag litter. I water dirt. I pick fruit that is ready so souring does not stack. I write a note for May: think about this when the first leaves harden, and only after a label that actually says fig.

If the thumb is clean, I stay in the lookalike list. Mosaic, sunburn, mites. A jug for the wrong animal is still late. It is also wrong.

The honest August job is ugly and cheap. Leaves in a bag. Dirt watered. Fruit picked. A note on the calendar: **next year, think about this in May**.

If the spots are loud, you are not late to panic. You are late to chemistry. You are on time for a rake.
