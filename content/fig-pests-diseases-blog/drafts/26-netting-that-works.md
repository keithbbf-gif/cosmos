I threw a cheap net over a young turkey one July and called it done. By the second morning a fig on the edge had a peck through the cloth. By the third I had a fold that held a lizard I did not mean to keep. I took the net off and built a frame. That is the whole sermon in one week.

Ferguson’s 2025 LSU note is the sentence I will not decorate. Scare tactics fade. **Bird netting is the most reliable way** to protect figs, blueberries, grapes. Frame is ideal. Net can rest on the plant if you accept losses on the outer fruit. Stake or weight every edge so birds do not walk under.

Walter Reeves said the same in Georgia: pie pans rarely work; cover the plant; take the net off after fruiting.

Figs4fun’s old FAQ, grower not university, still said the safety part I will repeat: loose plastic netting is a death trap for birds, lizards, snakes. Frame it. Keep it taut. Do not leave folds. Check it.

I am not selling a brand of mesh. I am selling a shape.

## The walk that tells you the net is a hat

Walk the skirt. If you can lift an edge with two fingers and see daylight a mockingbird could walk through, the net is a hat. Walk the canopy. If ripe fruit presses the cloth, a bill can still punch. Walk the folds. If there is a pocket, something living will try it.

I do this daily in the loud two weeks. Not because I enjoy a chore. Because a bird inside a cinched net is my problem. A snake in a fold is my problem. I would rather lose fruit than write a clever snare.

8a heat makes the cloth sag. A two-inch rain pools water in a belly. A south wind wraps mesh around a sucker and tears a door. The net you built on a calm Saturday is not the net you have on Thursday. Check it.

## The shape that works

A frame — PVC, electrical conduit, wood, whatever you will actually build — that holds mesh **off** the fruit. Birds peck through cloth that lies on a fig. That is not a theory. That is a hole I have seen.

I like hoop or box frames I can walk into. A zipper or a clothespin seam so the dawn pick does not become a wrestling match. If the net makes you skip the walk, the birds still win. They do not have zippers. You do.

Mesh small enough for the smallest fruit-eater you have. Half-inch stops most birds. It will not stop a sap beetle. Do not ask one cloth to do every chapter. Green June beetles are clumsy; some mesh slows them. Driedfruit beetles walk through bird mesh like it is a rumor.

Straight down to the ground. Weighted. Bricks, pins, a board, soil on the hem. If you cinch only at the trunk, a bird walks in at the skirt and a raccoon tests the same door at night. Ferguson said stake it. Do that.

Leave a little slack for weather, not a hammock. Taut enough that a snake does not get a sock. Loose enough that a 98° afternoon does not wire-cut a leaf.

## The shape that fails

A throw blanket over a twenty-foot turkey. You lost the top. You trapped a snake. You tore the cloth on a sucker. You cannot pick without cursing. That is not a net. That is a story.

CDs on fishing line. Tape. An owl. A radio. A week of hope. Ferguson already wrote the habituation sentence. I hung tape. It became furniture.

Net cinched at the trunk like a skirt on a dancer. Birds walk under. So do cats. So do the animals you did not want in a bag.

Net left on after the crop because you were tired. Then a late rust flush grows through the mesh and you have a tree in a bag in October. Take it off. Ferguson photographed nets that come down when the fruit is done. Do that.

A tight plastic tarp. That is not a bird net. That is a sweat lodge. Use mesh. Let air move. Rust already wants a wet room in our sticky August. Do not build her one.

## Wildlife you did not mean to catch

I will say this in the ugly font. Check daily. A bird inside a cinched net is your problem. Cut it out if you have to. Do not leave it because you were going to “look later.”

A snake in a fold is your problem. Gloves. Patience. Do not invent a cruelty.

If lizards are a thing, some growers run a low plastic skirt so reptiles do not climb into mesh — that is a figs4fun trick, not an LSU law. **[VERIFY]** whether it makes sense on your dirt. A skirt that pools water against a trunk is a different problem.

Migratory birds are not a pesticide label. I will not write glue. I will not write poison. I will not write a pellet story as a garden tip. Netting is a barrier. If the barrier becomes a trap, you failed the barrier.

## Squirrels chew

Netting that stops a mockingbird may not stop a squirrel. They cut. They get stuck. A metal skirt on a trunk can stop a climb if the tree is isolated — an Oregon master gardener wrote that. Isolated is the word. A fig against a fence is a squirrel highway. I will not promise a collar on a tree that leans on a shed.

I have cinched net and found a hole the next morning. I have also had weeks squirrels ignored a tree because a pecan was louder. I do not forecast squirrels. I forecast whether the tree is a ladder.

Bagging individual fruit is the small-tree version. See the bagging page. A twenty-foot turkey does not get a hundred organza bags unless you have a different personality than I do.

Raccoons test a loose skirt at night. Deer do not care about mesh on a short tree they can browse through if the frame is a suggestion. Mammals grab. Birds peck. Sap beetles walk. Name the mouth before you buy more cloth.

## 8a weather on a frame

Our August is sticky and then suddenly a dry south wind. Mesh holds humidity against a leaf if you built a tent. Morning sun through an open interior still matters. Do not drape a rust closet.

After a tropical leftover rain week the ground is soup. Weights sink. Pins pull. Walk the hem. Reset it before the fruit you saved becomes a raccoon door.

A late rust flush in September will try to grow through anything you left on. Take the net down when the crop is done. The tree needs winter air and you need to rake. A bagged canopy in October is how you write a cold-stub chapter you did not want.

Pots can wear a tomato cage and a sleeve of mesh. That is a porch method. Stake the sleeve. Check it. A pot net that blows into the gravel is a snare on the ground.

## After you build it once

The second year is faster. You already know which tree you refuse to share. You already know the hoop lengths. The year you do not build it is the year you write a complaint about birds as if they were new. They were not new. The calendar was published. You skipped the frame.

I still pay rent on trees I will not net. I net the ones I refuse to share. That split is allowed. Pretending CDs are a frame is not.

If you will not check daily, do not cinch a bag around a living yard. Pick, or pay rent, or build the shape you will actually maintain. The reliable tool Ferguson named is still a tool. A tool you abandon in a fold is a hazard.
