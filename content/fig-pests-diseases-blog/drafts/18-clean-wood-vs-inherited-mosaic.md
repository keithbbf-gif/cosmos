UC IPM said obtain plants from a quality supplier and do not propagate from mosaic trees. That is the clean-stock sermon. I believe the sermon. I also live in a country where most fig wood is a cutting of a cutting of a yard tree.

Those two facts have to sit together or you will either lie in a listing or refuse to grow figs.

I open boxes on the tailgate. I have opened pretty ones and dead ones. I have never opened a lab file. That is the country I am writing from.

## What “clean” can mean

**Tested.** A lab looked for named viruses on *that* plant. Rare. Expensive. Almost never what a marketplace title means. Preising’s 2021 review made mosaic even less of a single-test fairy tale — a pile of viruses and hangers-on. A test for one name is not a halo for the whole pile.

**Symptom-free this Tuesday.** A quiet May leaf. Useful as a first look. Useless as a lifetime stamp. Heat will talk. A 98° week in 8a will write a map on a plant you swore was clean at the sale.

**From a tree that always painted.** Honest. You are buying the flavor and the file.

**“Virus-free guaranteed.”** A claim I will not print on FigRoots and I will not buy. Symptom-free is not virus-free. I will walk past that title even if the photo is pretty.

I will buy wood from people who talk like the third line. I will watch wood from people who talk like the second. I will walk past the fourth.

A nursery that says “we do not see symptoms in the house” is talking about their house. Your July is not their house.

## Inheriting a yard

Somebody’s grandmother had a fig. It is painted in July and loaded in July. You take stools. You now own mosaic, a flavor, and a story. That is a good inheritance if the tree works. Do not launder it into a clean-collection origin story.

I have a stool like that. I will not name it into a catalog. I will pick it. I will give a neighbor a stick if they ask and I will tell them the leaf paints. If they want a funeral they can have it at their house.

If you are building a tray of rare names, do not stick those grandmother stools in the same tub of mix and call the tray a quarantine. Knives. Hands. Mites. You know this. You still do it when the weather is nice.

I wash. I keep grandmother wood in its own corner until I see what I see. I do not need a clean room. I need a habit.

The mite (*Aceria ficus* / *A. fici*) can move virus to a plant you believed was still clean. One infectious mite, in the UC telling. Cuttings move it without a mite. Distance is not a wall. It is a habit. I still will not spray the county to protect a rumor.

## Mail-order pretty pots

A nursery plant with perfect leaves in April is a sales photo. Look at the roots for galls while you are there — that is the nematode chapter. Look at the leaves again in your own 8a heat. If it paints and still grows, you have a fig. If it paints and sits still, you have a decision.

I open the box the day it lands. I photograph the label, the wood, the roots if they are loose. I water. I do not dunk it in a “virus dip.” There is no honest dip.

I do not drive a plant back across a state for mosaic paint. I do drive one back for a necklace of galls, or a scale crust, or a lie about the name. Mosaic is too common to treat as a refund event unless the seller promised clean-tested wood. If they promised that, they owe you a file, not a feeling.

A big-box pot that was expensive is still a rumor. Price is not a lab.

[An Introduction to Figs](https://figroots.com/an-introduction-to-figs/) already told you not to root in mystery yard dirt. This page adds: do not root a rare name in the same shared tub as a grandmother stool and then call the rare name clean because the leaf was quiet in May.

## What I take cuttings from

Flavor I want. A tree that earns. I assume the paint job comes along.

I do not take cuttings from a plant that never grew up and tell myself the babies will be different. The virus is in the wood. The weak growth may be virus plus a bad root plus a bad hole. I do not need to clone the whole pile.

Jack’s cutting specs live on FigRoots already — length, nodes, lignified wood. This page is not that. This page is: **the wood has a medical history even when the leaf is quiet.**

I take wood in the season we already teach, not in a July panic because the leaf got loud. Loud July is heat on a file you already own. Quiet January wood is still that file.

I label the stick with the name I believe and the tree I took it from. If I later decide the name was a story, the paint job is still mine.

## A corner of the yard that is not a quarantine theater

I keep new arrivals on a bench or a pot row I can walk without brushing the old canopy. I do not need plastic walls. I need to stop using the painted giant as a shade tree for the babies.

I do not share a pruning saw from a cankered limb to a clean-looking rare name without wiping. That is ordinary hygiene. I will not name a bleach ratio as a pesticide label.

I watch through a summer and a winter. One painted leaf in July is not a cull. A plant that cannot make a canopy is a cull. A plant that paints and loads is an inheritance I am allowed to keep.

If I add a seedling I grew from seed, I treat it as the only “maybe still clean” plant in the conversation, and I still watch it. Seed is not a mosaic mover in the UC telling. My hands still are.

## What I will not do for a stranger

I will not certify your box. I will not write “our figs are virus-free.” I will not tell you a big-box plant is clean because it was expensive.

I will not launder a yard stool into a marketplace title.

I will not tell you to throw away a working painted tree because you want a clean collection. You can have both stories on one place if you keep them from sharing a tub and a lie.

I will tell you to watch a plant through a summer and a winter in 8a. One painted leaf in July is not a cull. A plant that cannot make a canopy is a cull. The inheritance is allowed. The lie is not.

If you want clean-tested wood, pay a lab and a nursery that will show you the paper on *that* plant. If you want figs in South Arkansas, you will almost certainly inherit paint. Eat anyway.
