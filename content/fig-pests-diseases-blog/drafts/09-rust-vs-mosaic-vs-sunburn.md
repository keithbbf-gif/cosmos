Virginia Tech’s backyard fig guide (Burnett and Samtani, SPES-760P, 2026) said the quiet part in a list: rust shares a yellow look with sunburn, leaf spot, sooty mold, and mosaic. People still take one picture from the driveway and buy a “fruit tree” jug.

I want four names you can use in 8a without a lab. If you cannot pick one, you mail the county. You do not spray a sampler pack.

I did this walk with a neighbor at 4 p.m. on a west pot wall. The plastic was hot enough you would not leave a hand on it. He had one yellow leaf in his fingers and a bottle in the truck. We flipped ten leaves, not one. Two had grit. Three had oak-leaf paint and a clean thumb. Two were bleached on the face that took the afternoon. One had fine pepper and a little web in the dusty interior. He put the bottle back. That is the whole draft.

## Rust

Angular flecks. Yellow to rusty brown. Underside blistered. Thumb stained. Then drop. Thick air. Old leaves first, then greed.

Fruit is not the host. LSU Pub. 1802 and Pub. 3159 both keep that sentence. The week of harvest is when you notice because the factory is leaving.

UAEX’s clinic note (Smith and Pavel) is the 8a version of the drop: a canopy can go in two or three weeks. TAMU said rust often gets loud while fruit is trying to finish.

Sanitation. Air. Morning water. Chemistry only if your **current** label and your state still say fig, and only as a preventative chapter — UAEX’s historic copper timing is early summer, not a rescue in September. LSU 2025 says Louisiana does not have a labeled fig fungicide in that document. Those facts can sit together. **[VERIFY]** before you buy a jug.

I rake. I do not compost the quilt against the trunk. I do not copper a map that is already orange and call it a cure. UF/IFAS Panhandle, 2017: once the spots are obvious, you are late.

## Mosaic

A paint job. Oak-leaf. Rings. A patch of yellow that is not angular grit. The leaf may cup or come out smaller. Heat makes it louder. A 98° week in South Arkansas will convince a person the tree “caught” something last Tuesday. The wood has had it.

Cuttings and an eriophyid mite named *Aceria ficus* (UC IPM also writes *A. fici*) move it. Seed does not. UC IPM’s fig mosaic pages are blunt: graft and cutting transmission, not seedborne, no practical residential cure. Oregon State / PNW Handbook says the same family of facts and names more than one associated virus. Preising and others, 2021, in *Plant Disease*, called the whole mess a complex, not one tidy virus story. I will not flatten that into a kitchen protocol.

No pustules. No orange thumb. No spray that evicts it. UC IPM: live with it or replace a tree that will not perform. I have eaten off mosaic trees for years. I have also culled a plant that never grew up. Those are different trees.

I will not write a heat-lamp “therapy.” I will not sell virus-free from a symptom-free picture. I will not tell you oil, neem, or bud-scale carving cleared the virus. Mite pressure can be reduced. The virus in the wood stays.

Old Southern clones carry some degree of this more often than listings want to admit. That is not a reason to cut a tree that still loads fruit.

## Sunburn and heat bleach

West wall. Black pot on black plastic. A leaf that cooked. The yellow is a scald, often between veins or on the face that took the afternoon. The underside is not a rust factory. The tree may drop that leaf. It is not going to strip the whole canopy in a rust pattern unless rust is also invited.

8a July will do this on a pot you forgot to shuffle. [Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) already talks shade and water. I am only saying: bleach is not blight. Move the pot. Do not copper a sunburn.

A trunk scald on a newly opened west face is a different scar. Paint and wraps have their own arguments. I will not smuggle them into a leaf ID.

I walked that west wall after a 98° Tuesday. The leaf felt thin and crisp on the cooked face. No grit. No oak-leaf paint. The man still wanted a fungicide because yellow is yellow. Yellow is not a product category.

## Spider mites

Hot, dry, dusty. We get those weeks even in a humid county — a south wind, a greenhouse, a shop. Fine stipple on the top of the leaf. Sometimes a bronze cast. Sometimes webbing if you let it go. A loupe helps. Twenty power is not a personality. It is a tool. UC IPM uses that kind of lens talk on the fig mite; I will steal the habit for the dusty-leaf mite too.

Mite stipple does not dust orange. It does not paint an oak-leaf virus pattern. It can drop leaves. People then say rust.

Horticultural oil and soap are the usual homeowner talk on ornamental mites. Figs you intend to eat have a label problem again. I will not write a mix-and-spray as if fig were a houseplant. **[VERIFY]** the label. A rinse, a shade, and killing the dust bowl under a pot block will do more than a panic dunk.

The fig bud mite that carries mosaic is a different beast — eriophyid, in buds, not the two-spotted spider mite on the underside of a dusty leaf. Do not treat them as one animal because both are “mites.”

## Sooty mold, while we are here

Black film. Wipes. It is sugar from scale, aphids, whitefly, mealybug. Ugly. Not rust. Not a fungus you beat with a rust program. You beat the leak, then you wash what you can stand to wash.

## Fruit, so you do not steal this list

A necklace that smells like a brewery is souring. UC IPM: yeasts, driedfruit beetles, vinegar flies. No bactericide they recommend. Rust did not ferment that fig. Mosaic did not either. Leave this list. Pick the fruit.

## A two-minute sort

Flip ten leaves.

Dust and blisters: rust.

Paint and rings, no grit: mosaic.

Bleached face on the hot side: weather.

Pepper and maybe web, dry week: spider mites.

Black film that smears: sooty mold.

Fruit vinegar: leave this list.

If two of these are true at once — and they can be — treat the one you can move this week. Rake rust. Water a cooked pot. Leave mosaic. Do not stack products.

I would rather a grower use four homely names than one fancy blight. Blight is how you buy the wrong bottle.

## What I will not do from one yellow leaf

I will not bless a copper schedule. I will not name a miticide. I will not declare a tree doomed. I will not write a virus cure. I will not tell you a sampler pack is diagnosis.

If the ten-leaf walk still leaves you unsure, mail the county a leaf. Write when it started. Write whether fruit is involved. **[VERIFY]** their intake. A clinic envelope is cheaper than a year of the wrong jug.
