This is a walk, not a spray card. If you came for copper every two weeks, the copper draft already told you no. If you came for a mosaic protocol, that draft told you no. This page is when I look, and what I refuse to mash.

South Arkansas **8a**. Sticky August. A freezer that will still surprise a fig that rust-flushed in October. Clay in one hole and sand in the next. Your parish is allowed to be two weeks off. **[VERIFY]** your own frost dates. I am talking from this yard.

I put names on months so I stop buying the wrong bottle in the wrong week. I do not put rates on months. LSU Pub. 1802 in 2025 said no fungicides labeled for figs in Louisiana. UAEX’s rust note still talks historic copper when first leaves are full size. Those sentences can sit on the same wall. They do not become a hose calendar.

## January–February

Outdoor wood: dead tips, leftover mummies, a rust quilt I stepped over in November. I do not sculpt until I see live buds. The saw draft owns the cut. This month owns the look.

Pots that wintered warm: scale, spider mites in a dry room, a saucer that grew a swamp. I do not oil a heat-stressed leaf because I am bored. I do not carry yard gravel into the shop “for weight.”

Shop plants I am tempted to buy get the same bump look I give my own. Mosaic is quiet now. Leave it. Do not write “clean” on a tag.

## March

Buds move. A late freeze after budbreak is the cruel one. I look at what pushed. I cut dead when I can tell. I keep live stools. I do not fertilize a rust hangover into a tender flush.

This is when last October’s second spring tells the truth. UAEX warned that rust defoliation can push soft wood into winter. Brown tips in March are often that bill, not a new blight I need to fog.

If a limb wilts from a point, I look for a mummy, a wound, a canker. LSU’s Botrytis limb blight starts with frost-hurt fruit. I cut below. I do not copper the stub.

## April

First leaves harden. Nursery plants I am about to put in a hole get a root look. Galls. Bumps. UF said do not plant galled stock.

I still rake last year’s litter if I missed it. LSU: spores on fallen leaves.

If you are in a state with a historic copper sentence and a label that still says fig, this is the chapter UAEX meant — first leaves full size — not August. I still do not invent a jug. **[VERIFY]** the paper in your hand this year. If you are in Louisiana as Pub. 1802 described it, you have a rake.

Thread blight, in the LSU calendar sense, is an early foliage fungus. If the canopy is ugly in April or May with a webby underside and no orange grit, I do not write rust as the default. I date the leaf. I rake it either way. That sort is the last draft in this pack. It is not a copper card.

## May

Leaves full size. Rust preventative talk belongs here *if* it belongs at all. Once spots are loud, UF/IFAS already said chemical talk is late. May is the look for the first flecks, not a personality.

Mite lens on clean seedlings if I am trying to keep them clean. Ant columns on shop escapees: I follow the ants to the bump. I do not spray the ant as if it invented the sugar. I open the canopy if the prune is due. I do not butcher a tree that is two weeks from a breba I actually want.

## June

Breba on some names. Birds learn. First souring if I leave fruit. Japanese beetles in counties that have them — not the same animal as a Green June, not the same animal as a Southwestern figeater. I pick. I do not fog ripe fruit I will eat tonight.

Tight-eye names still earn their keep if the month stays wet. Open-eye names start the split conversation. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already owns that one-line. I write which name split in a note. I do not write a refund the first week.

I water dirt. Morning if I go overhead. Dusk overhead is a fungus lease.

## July

Main crop loud. Green June beetles loud. Birds loud. Heat turns mosaic into theater. That paint is not a new infection. The heat draft already said so. I do not spray a virus. I do not cut a working tree because a leaf looks oak-leaf yellow after a 98° week.

Rust may start its map in a wet year. I flip leaves. Underside. Thumb. If I see grit, I am in the rust bucket. Fruit is not the rust host. I do not spray a vinegar fig for a leaf fungus.

I pick at dawn. I do not diagnose nematodes from a porch wilt without a root. Noon fold on sand with a heavy pot is a plumbing story. Knock a tool plant, not a rare name.

I do not copper a paint job. I do not hang a plastic owl and call it a bird program. LSU’s Mary Helen Ferguson said netting is the reliable tool. Scare tape is a week of entertainment.

## August

Steam. Rust grit on a thumb. Necklaces. Sap beetles. Afternoon wilt on sand. This is the month people buy bottles. This is the month I buy bags for leaves and time for a rake.

Once the spots are obvious, I am late for chemistry even in a state that still talks copper. UF/IFAS said that quiet part. I rake. I do not compost a rust blanket against the trunk.

Fruit trouble is a different walk the same morning. Sour, split, Green June, birds. I pick. I take the wrecks off. A tropical leftover rain week gets its own draft. Close doors. Do not invent a storm spray. Kids and dogs live in this grass. I will not fog the yard because a leaf is ugly.

## September

Late fruit on some LSU names. Rust-stripped trees may flush. I do not feed that flush. I do not cheer a second spring. UAEX already told you why. Soft wood is a March bill. Birds still work the dark figs. Green fruit is contrast, not a force field. I keep raking. Lawn food on a fig in September is how you buy a tender tip.

## October

Harden. Rake the rest. Pots start the shuffle. Scale look before I carry a bump into the shop. I do not wrap a late flush like a gift. Plastic sweats. A rust year is a winter year. I do not copper baby leaves in a panic. If you even have a legal copper use, the clinic window was May. Notes: which name held leaves longer. Relative. Not immune. I will not upgrade LSU Gold’s moderate rust note into a vaccine.

## November–December

Sanitation I skipped. Mulch off the trunk. Leftover mummies come off. Shop door stays a bump look. I do not wrap a tree in plastic. I do not write a dormant-spray personality unless a label and a reason exist. Most rust programs that even mention copper wanted May.

## What the walk is actually for

I am sorting buckets by month so I stop mashing them.

Early canopy trouble is not automatically rust. Late orange grit is. Heat-loud paint is mosaic we already own. June and July fruit is beetles and birds and yeast. A summer crash with white skin at the collar is a different funeral. A noon wilt on wet dirt is not a virus.

Thread blight versus rust is a calendar sort. That is why this page exists next to the last draft. It is not why a copper card exists.

## What never gets a month

Eradicate. A statewide fungicide calendar. A bird owl. A nematode spice. A mosaic tea. A backyard bait for a federal fly we have not claimed in this county. A fog because kids are in the yard and the leaf is yellow.

Those can stay off the wall.

Put this page on the shed if you want. Put a jug under it only if a label and a weekday desk said so. The walk is the program. 8a will keep you in practice.

I have skipped months. I have also bought a bottle in August for a problem that wanted a rake in November. The calendar is how I stop repeating that. It is not how I look busy with chemistry.
