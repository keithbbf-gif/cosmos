I walked out at first light after a loud fig night and the ground looked like a grocery bag had been dumped. Not a triangular peck. Not a green June sitting on a shoulder. Hands had been here. Half-eaten ripe fruit, a broken pot rim, a trail toward the woods. Zone **8a** woods-edge yards get shoppers. Birds commute. Mammals shop.

I will not write a poison. I will not write a live-trap sitcom as if it were a fig SOP. I will write what I have actually used and what extension already said in ordinary English. University of Tennessee named birds, squirrels, and raccoons in the same breath as netting and a timely pick. That is the size of the honest toolbox.

## Name the mouth before you buy a gadget

**Bird.** Triangular peck. Hollowed shoulder. Fruit often still hanging. Dark ripe gone, green still there some weeks. Contrast, not immunity. Dawn commute.

**Sap beetle.** Little bodies in the eye. Pink syrup. Brewery smell. They did not grab the fig. They walked into a door a split, a peck, or a late pick left open.

**Green June.** Clumsy big mouth on a ripe shoulder. Day shift. Messy.

**Squirrel.** Samples unripe. Wastes. Green fruit on the ground with one bite. Chews netting. Day, and also whenever they feel like it.

**Raccoon.** Takes ripe. Takes a lot. Night. Hands. A pile. A pot moved. Dog food under the tree was an invitation.

**Deer.** Browse on a short tree. Tips and leaves first on a young fig. Fruit if they can reach. Dawn and dusk. A fence they can shrug is not a fence.

If you only say “wildlife got them,” you will hang tape for a raccoon and fog fruit for a squirrel. Name the hole.

## Squirrels

They sample unripe. They waste. They cut netting. An Oregon master gardener told a homeowner to net, cinch, check daily, and if needed add a **two-foot metal collar** about six feet up on an isolated trunk. Isolated is the job. A fig that touches a fence, a shed, or another canopy is a bridge. The collar becomes jewelry.

I have cinched net and found a hole the next morning. I have also had weeks squirrels ignored a tree because a pecan was louder. I do not forecast squirrels. I forecast whether the tree is a ladder.

Hot pepper sprays are a mood in rain. 8a rain laughs. A two-inch Gulf leftover washes the sermon off the fruit. I will not sell a weekly capsaicin religion.

A pot can move away from the highway. An in-ground turkey against a fence cannot. Prune the bridge if you can do it without butchering the crop week. I wait for the prune I already owed the tree. I do not chainsaw a July fig to spite a squirrel and steal jam.

They will chew organza bags. They will get stuck in loose mesh. Check daily. A squirrel in a fold is your problem the same way a bird in a cinched net is your problem. Frame the net. Do not write a snare and call it IPM.

## Raccoons

They take ripe. They take a lot. They are hands. I have seen a finishing pot on gravel at dusk and a clean saucer at dawn. The fruit was not pecked. It was gone.

A dog that actually patrols at night is a tool some people have and some people only talk about. A porch pot can come inside at dusk. An in-ground tree cannot.

Net to the ground, weighted, checked. Ferguson’s stake-the-edge sentence was about birds. It also closes a raccoon door if the hem is real. A raccoon will test a loose skirt. Electric poultry net is a farm tool I will not prescribe from a blog for a suburban lot. If you already run it for chickens, you already know the rules.

Do not leave dog food under the fig. Do not leave the sour necklace. You invited a meeting. UC’s sanitation line for sour rot — pick, clean up, do not leave overripe fruit of any kind — also lowers the night grocery.

I will not write a poison peanut. I will not write a glue board as a raccoon plan. I will not write a shooting story on a city lot.

## Deer

A short fig is salad. A tall fig is a different story unless they have learned to stand. I have watched a young in-ground name stay a bush because something browsed every flush. That is not mosaic. That is a mouth.

Repellents rotate and wash off. 8a rain and a week of 90s will erase a soap bar on a string. I will not invent your HOA. A fence that is actually tall enough is the only sentence I will stand on.

If deer pressure is the reason a young tree never gets a canopy, the tree is in the wrong room. A wire cage for the first two years is ordinary orchard practice. It looks ugly. It works better than a radio.

Green fruit is not a force field here either. Deer are not taking a color exam. They are browsing what they can reach.

## Dogs and the fantasy of a truce

A dog can keep a raccoon honest and also eat the figs. I have known both dogs. I will not write a breed. I will not write that a dog replaces a frame.

Chickens scratch and eat fruit. Same warning I wrote under the compost pile. Livestock is not a mammal program unless you already fence for livestock.

## Pots as a privilege

A collection in pots can move. That is a FigRoots habit we already have for winter and July sun. It is also a raccoon habit. I do not move 200 pots at dusk. I move the two that are finishing fruit on the porch. The rest pay tax or get a frame.

Pots on a south gravel pad in a dry August week have their own mite story. Moving them at dusk for a raccoon does not cancel water. It is one job. Do not mash it with a jug.

## What I will not write

Poison peanuts. Glue. A shooting story on a city lot. Those belong in a different kind of file.

I will not write that birds and mammals are one pest. The peck and the grab need different photos. If you only net the top and leave the skirt open, you built a bird hat and a raccoon door.

I will not write a fake owl as a raccoon tool. Scare tape habituates for birds. A plastic coyote habituates faster for a mammal that lives here.

I will not fog ripe fruit because something with fur shopped. Residue on a fig you will eat tonight is still a bad trade.

I will not blame a virus for a pile of rinds.

## 8a woods-edge weather

We live where the woods were first. A wet night after a hot day makes fruit loud. Soft necks. Smell. The raccoon calendar and the bird calendar can share a week. Walk at dawn for pecks. Walk the ground for grabs. Take the necklace so sap beetles do not file a third shift.

After a tropical rain leftover the fence line is a highway of wet grass and tracks. Reset the net hem. Weights sink in clay soup. Sand does not hold a pin. Check it.

Mammals are not a reason to fog a tree. They are a reason to decide which names are inside the fence you actually have. The woods were here first. The fig is the new shop. Build a door or admit the sale.
