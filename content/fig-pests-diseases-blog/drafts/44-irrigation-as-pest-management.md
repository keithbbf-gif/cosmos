I have blamed insects for a hose habit. I have also blamed a hose for insects. Both can be true. This page is the water chapter of the pest pack.

Figs want a drink in **8a** heat. Virginia Tech and UGA talk an inch to an inch and a half a week as a starting rhyme, more in sand, less in clay. I will not turn that into a law. I will not invent a timer schedule and call it IPM. I will turn it into doors.

The air here is already thick. A dusk overhead run is how you add a closet on purpose. The sand is already hungry. A daily sprinkle is how you grow a mat in the hottest occupied inch. The clay already holds a lake. A hose after a two-inch rain is how you write rot on top of weather.

[Outdoor](https://figroots.com/outdoor/) already talks pots, beds, shade, water. The pest posts unpack the other half of that hose.

## Rust door: the wet leaf at night

Overhead at dusk is a closet. Leaves sit wet. *Cerotelium fici* likes that. Morning overhead at least has a drying day. Dirt-level drip or a hose on the soil is better. Pot blocks on timers at 9 p.m. are how a collection rusts together.

I still run overhead when I am lazy. I am not proud of it. I move those runs to morning when I remember I wrote this sentence.

Once spots are loud, UF/IFAS said chemical talk is late. A wet August map is a rake, not a reason to add another night-wet leaf. LSU’s cycle starts on litter you left and splash you keep giving it. Water on the dirt is sanitation you do with a hose. Water on the midnight leaf is a vote for the fungus.

I do not mist a canopy “to cool it” at dark and then complain about rust grit on a thumb. Cooling is shade and a morning drink and a mulch that is not a volcano.

I do not copper a dripping leaf because I feel guilty about the timer. Copper is a legal object, not a personality. UAEX’s historic timing is early, on hardened first leaves, if fig is even on your label. LSU 2025 said Louisiana has no labeled fig fungicide in that document. The hose is still legal. **[VERIFY]** the jug. Do not **[VERIFY]** the dirt.

## Nematode door: the noon wilt with a heavy pot

Galls break plumbing. The medium can be wet and the top still folds. If you answer every wilt with more water, you drown a clay pot and still do not fix sand galls. Lift. Weight. Look at a tool plant’s roots at pot-up. Deep rare drinks on sand. No lake on clay.

Shallow sipping grows a mat in the hottest occupied inch. That is a nematode-friendly habit. LSU wanted moisture around the roots and a plant in good health — not a daily tease. Slow water. Less often. Actual depth.

I do not diagnose nematodes from a porch wilt. I knock a plant I can spare, or I wait for pot-up. Beads on feeders are not subtle once you have seen them. A rare name does not get ripped for curiosity.

Last year’s tomato or okra sand is not a fig hole. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already said pots. The hose will not reset that ground. NC State’s honest line, as we use it, is no homeowner chemical control for an established infested fig. Water is how you live with a tool tree. Water is not a drench recipe.

I mulch two to four inches and I keep it off the trunk. Bare sand in August is a skillet. Mulch is not a nematicide. It is a calmer room so I do not have to sprinkle every afternoon.

## Rot door: the saucer

A pot in a dish after a tropical rain is a swamp. Ordinary sour rot of roots is enough to kill a plant. Dump the dish. Raise the pot. Do not call it drought because the top wilted in a lake.

Armillaria is a different funeral — white skin at the collar, a summer crash, leftover roots in a hole, UF said remove the tree and do not replant. I do not mash a drowned three-gallon into that Latin. I still look at a thick collar if the wilt stays after the dish is empty.

Clay in-ground after a rain week: I do not add more hose. I do not till. I let it breathe. A fig on a mound I already built is why I built the mound. A fig in a puddle is a rot risk I voted for at planting.

I do not bleach-irrigate a hole. I do not hydrogen-peroxide a pot and call it a reset.

## Mite door: the dust and the drought

A dry windy pot block grows spider mites. Fine pepper on the top of a leaf in a hot, dry spell — we get those in 8a even in a humid county. Water and a little shade shrink that room. A plant you forgot for ten days and then drowned is two problems stacked.

You can also overwater a shop plant in winter and grow fungus. Season is part of the hose. January is not July. A warm room and a weekly swamp is how you write rot and fungus gnats and a sad stick. A warm room and bone-dry mix is how you write mites. Weight the pot. Do not water the calendar.

*Aceria* in the bud is a different mite and a different page. A hose will not evict a virus. Oils may bother mites. They do not evict Fig mosaic virus. Do not invent a mist protocol.

## Beetle door: the fruit you watered into a split

A drought then a two-inch rain plus a heavy hand on the hose will split skins. Split is a beetle door. Split is a yeast door. Tight-eye names still split if the week was stupid. They just do it less.

I do not chase a perfect moisture curve. I do try not to invent a flood after I forgot the plants for ten days. Even water through a dry spell is boring and correct. A guilt flood on a Friday is how you write a necklace of wrecks and a Saturday of sap beetles.

I do not fog those wrecks. I pick them. The handpick-beetles and kids-and-dogs pages already refused the plume. The hose is allowed to be the mistake. The backpack does not get to “fix” it.

Birds will find a splitter. So will you. Dawn walk. The hose does not replace the bucket.

## What I will not do with water

I will not flood to drown chafer larvae on 8a clay because UC wrote a Fresno sentence. Their fig-beetle note is a different animal and a different dirt. I will not import a California irrigation stunt into Arkansas clay and call it grub control.

I will not bleach-irrigate nematodes. I will not cinnamon-drench. I will not molasses a hole.

I will not foliar-feed my way out of galls. A tired top on knotted roots is an uptake story. A soil test talks fertilizer. A hose talks depth. Neither is a worm killer.

I will not mist mosaic off a leaf.

I will not run a dusk sprinkler as a “cool down” and then buy copper in August.

I will not leave a timer on vacation settings through a tropical rain week. The after-rain page said dump the saucer. The timer does not read radar unless you do.

## The boring setting

Morning. Dirt. Depth that matches the hole. Dishes empty. A wilt I investigate before I answer.

That is pest management. It does not look like a product. It looks like a person who owns a hose and a clock.

On sand I water slower and deeper than my impatience wants. On clay I wait. In pots I lift. In the shop I remember the month. After rust I do not feed a second spring. After a storm I do not add a lake.

8a will keep you honest. The air is already thick. Do not add a night-wet leaf on purpose. The sand is already hungry. Do not add a lake on purpose. The hose is allowed to be the smartest tool in this series. It is also allowed to be the dumbest. Pick which day it is.

I have written rust on a pot block with a 9 p.m. habit. I have written rot with a saucer I forgot. I have written a noon wilt on galled sand by sipping the surface. I have also kept a tool tree alive in a hot July with a slow drink and mulch off the trunk. Same hose. Different door. Name the door before you turn the valve.
