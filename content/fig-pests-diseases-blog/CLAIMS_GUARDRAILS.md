# Claims guardrails — fig pests and diseases (FigRoots)

Internal. Not a blog post. Not a spray schedule. Not a product label.

This pack is grower copy for [figroots.com](https://figroots.com) about nematodes, rust, mosaic, beetles, birds, and the lookalikes that get blamed for all five. Default yard: **USDA 8a, humid South / South Arkansas**. Drafts stay `status: draft`. Nothing here is cleared to Publish.

If a sentence would be a fake cure on a Facebook reel, it is a fake cure under a FigRoots title.

## What this brand will not say

### Viruses and mosaic

- There is no backyard spray, oil, tea, heat lamp, or “immune booster” that **cures** fig mosaic disease or Fig mosaic virus (FMV) once the wood is infected.
- Do not sell or imply **virus-free** plants unless a named lab test on *that* plant is in hand. Symptom-free is not virus-free. Most old Southern clones carry mosaic to some degree.
- Do not claim diatomaceous earth, neem, Forbid, horticultural oil, or bud-scale carving **clears the virus**. Mite pressure can be reduced; the virus in the tree stays.
- Do not write a home “heat-therapy protocol” as if a kitchen is a tissue-culture lab.
- UC IPM: once infected, there is no practical control on a residential tree except live with it or replace a tree that will not perform. Say that.

### Nematodes

- Root-knot nematodes (*Meloidogyne* spp.) are **not eradicated** from a backyard hole by cinnamon, molasses, dish soap, garlic, castor oil, or a weekend drench.
- Do not copy stale handbook **fumigant recipes** (including older TAMU Vapam talk) onto a living tree or a pot. Pre-plant fumigation is a licensed, dated, site-specific act — not a blog how-to.
- Do not claim marigolds, mustard, or a bag of “beneficial nematodes” from a big-box aisle will reset fig ground. Cover-crop literature is mixed and was not written as a fig-row miracle.
- Do not treat a seedling-trial neem nano-emulsion paper as a backyard control program.
- NC State / extension consensus we will stand on: **no chemical control for an established infested fig** that a homeowner should run from this site. Pots, clean stock, site choice, vigor, and not moving dirty soil are the honest tools.
- “Nematode-resistant fig variety” is a phrase we do not print as a fact unless a named trial said so. LSU Purple’s foliage notes are not a nematode passport.

### Rust and other fungi

- Once the leaf is yellow-orange and dropping, you are **not curing** that leaf. UF/IFAS Panhandle said the quiet part: after the spots are obvious, chemical talk is late.
- Do not invent a weekly copper / sulfur / chlorothalonil calendar as if figs were apples. **Label is law, and the label is state-specific.**
  - LSU AgCenter Pub. 1802 (2025): **no fungicides currently labeled for figs in Louisiana**; cultural only.
  - UAEX (Arkansas clinic note): historic **neutral copper** timing when first leaves are full size, then 3–4 weeks later.
  - UF/IFAS older fig notes: **no EPA-approved fungicides for edible figs in Florida** in that document.
- Those three sentences can be true at the same time. Do not flatten them into “spray copper, it is fine everywhere.”
- Do not spray a labeled product onto fruit that is already sizing if the source you are citing said residue will ruin the crop (TAMU rust note). Do not spray anything onto ripe fruit “because beetles.”
- Neem oil is not a rust vaccine. Virginia Tech mentioned it; we will not upgrade that clause into a guarantee.
- Do not compost a rust blanket against the trunk and call it “feeding the soil.”
- Fruit is **not** the rust host (LSU). Souring is a different bucket. Do not spray a vinegar fig for a leaf fungus.

### Beetles, flies, souring

- No bactericide or “yeasticide” is recommended by UC IPM for sour rot. Prompt harvest and sanitation are the program.
- Do not recommend carbaryl, malathion, or a fruit-tree cocktail on **ripe figs you intend to eat** as casual backyard advice.
- Japanese beetle **traps attract more beetles**. Do not sell them as fig control.
- Milky spore is a slow, incomplete, regional grub story. It is not a fig-orchard fix.
- Green June beetle (*Cotinis nitida*, East / South) is not the same animal as the Southwestern figeater (*C. mutabilis* / *C. texana*). Do not write one beetle for the whole country.
- Fig endosepsis is a **caprifig / Blastophaga** problem. Common figs in Arkansas are not waiting on a wasp. Do not import California orchard panic into a Celeste row.
- Driedfruit beetles and vinegar flies **carry** souring yeasts. Killing every fly in the county is not a real plan.

### Birds and other wildlife

- Scare tape, pie pans, plastic owls, radios, and CDs **habituate**. LSU AgCenter (Ferguson, 2025): netting is the reliable tool.
- Do not write poison, glue, or illegal take. Migratory birds are not a pesticide label.
- Do not promise a green-skinned fig is bird-proof. Contrast helps. Hungry mockingbirds still read a soft neck.
- Netting that pools on fruit **traps wildlife**. Frame it, stake it, check it. That is a safety claim we will make.

### Products, brands, and biosecurity

- Do not name a miticide, nematicide, or copper salt as “what we use / what you should buy” unless the **current** label lists fig (or the crop group) in **that** state. Prefer: “ask the county office; read the label; if fig is not on it, it is not a fig spray.”
- Do not claim Black fig fly (*Silba adipata*) is established in Arkansas. Write the biosecurity version: do not import mystery California pots; watch unusual drop; APHIS / county — not a Facebook ID.
- “Organic” is not a synonym for safe, legal, or effective.
- No medical claims about eating figs, latex, or “antiviral fruit.”

## Allowed shapes (still need a source or a `[VERIFY]`)

- What it **looks like** in 8a: orange pustules under the leaf; knotted roots; oak-leaf mosaic that gets louder in heat; green June beetles on a ripe necklace; pecked shoulders at dusk.
- What extension actually wrote, with the document named and dated when we have it.
- Cultural moves that change pressure: rake, airflow, morning water, closed-eye fruit, pots on clean mix, prompt pick, framed netting, do not plant the collection in last year’s okra sand.
- “I would leave a living tool tree and keep it watered. I would not put a rare name in that hole.”
- “This is mosaic we already live with. It is not a reason to cut the tree down.”
- “I do not know from your phone photo. Mail the county clinic a leaf.”

## Process

1. If the only evidence is a marketplace listing or an unsourced OurFigs thread, mark **`[VERIFY]`** or cut the sentence.
2. Resistance is **relative**. “Holds leaves longer some years” is not “immune.”
3. Zone 8a is a winter number (avg. annual extreme about 10–15°F). It is not one climate. East Texas sand, Arkansas clay, and a coastal Carolina yard do not get the same rust week. Say which yard you are talking from: **South Arkansas, 8a, sticky August.**
4. Do not outrun the other FigRoots drafts. [Pick The Right Fig](https://figroots.com/2025/07/02/pick-the-right-fig-for-you/) already owns the one-line version. This pack unpacks pests. It does not contradict pots-for-nematodes, tight-eye-for-rain, or green-vs-dark birds.
5. Empty seats: we are not a plant pathologist and we do not invent a lab partner to launder a claim.

## Ban-list sentences (kill the draft)

- “This essential oil blend eliminates fig rust.”
- “Cure your mosaic in three sprays.”
- “Eradicate nematodes naturally.”
- “Virus-free guaranteed.”
- “Just use Vapam / bleach / hydrogen peroxide in the hole.”
- “Copper every two weeks all summer.”
- “A plastic owl will keep the birds off.”
- “Safe for kids and pets” next to any pesticide talk.
- “Clinically proven.”
- “In today’s rapidly evolving garden…” and the rest of `STYLE_GUIDE.md`.
