# Stated goal

Write a public, educational history of the chips that made modern
machine learning possible — mainly GPUs, the CUDA era, and Google's
TPUs — in a human voice.

The piece should leave a reader able to answer four questions without
looking anything up:

1. Why was a *graphics* chip the right shape for neural nets?
2. What did CUDA change that a faster card alone could not?
3. Why did Google build a different chip instead of only buying more
   GPUs?
4. After 2017, why did the story stop being "one card on a desk" and
   become memory, wires, and buildings?

Acceptance:

- The history is public. Press releases, papers, company blogs, museum
  notes, conference talks. If a number is vendor-peak marketing, say
  so.
- A smart non-specialist can follow it. A specialist should not wince
  at the chronology.
- It sounds like a person. Contractions. Scenes. The occasional "look."
  No landscape-of-AI throat clearing.
- It stays educational. It does not recruit, sell, or file anything.
- It names the software that sat on the silicon. A chip without a
  language is a paperweight.

The live artifact is the final essay in this folder. Everything else
is how it got there.
