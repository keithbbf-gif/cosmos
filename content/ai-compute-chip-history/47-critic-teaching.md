# Critic — teaching

Does a smart non-specialist leave able to answer the four
questions in `01-stated-goal.md`?

## 1. Why was a graphics chip the right shape?

Yes. Loop, tax on surprise, game frames, volume. The cousin
sentence is enough. Do not add a matrix diagram in prose.

## 2. What did CUDA change that a faster card could not?

Yes: door, C, stack, vanishing into cuDNN and Python, OpenCL
as the other door. A reader can retell this.

Gap: we never say, in one plain line, that AlexNet's *source*
is CUDA. Draft 05 had the museum. Bring back half a sentence.
It makes "door" concrete.

## 3. Why did Google build a different chip?

Yes: facilities math, known loop, subtract surprise, disk bay.
The hunger is the best teacher in the piece.

Gap: a skimmer might think TPU replaced GPUs at Google. One
clause: they still used CPUs and GPUs for other work; v1 was
inference. Jouppi says this. Steal it.

## 4. Why did the story become memory, wires, buildings?

Yes. 34 → 600 GB/s. H200 as a memory rename. Rack as unit.
Megawatts. Train/serve split.

Gap: utilization is taught by implication. Steal one sentence
from draft 23: peak is the sound if it never waits. Put it
near the K80 comparison or near the close, not both.

## What a specialist would wince at

- Not much chronology.
- Maybe the triad feeling like a TED third act. It is
  load-bearing for "not a directory." Keep it short.

## What we still do not teach, on purpose

A buyer's guide. A full TPU v3–v5 tour. Cerebras-vs-Groq.
How to write a CUDA kernel. Good omissions.

## Verdict

The four questions are answerable. Add three sentences
(AlexNet source, TPU not a GPU ban, peak vs wait). Then stop
teaching.
