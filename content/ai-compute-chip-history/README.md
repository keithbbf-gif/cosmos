# How a graphics chip learned to think

Public educational history of AI compute: GPUs, CUDA, TPUs, and the
racks those chips grew into. Written for a curious adult who is willing
to sit with a story, not a spec sheet.

This folder is **staged writing**, not a dump. Forty-plus drafts sit
here on purpose. Early files gather facts and argue about shape. Middle
files write the essay in pieces, then as a whole. Later files cut,
teach harder, and try to sound like a person. The finished piece is
`50-essay-final.md`.

If you only have time for one file, read that.

## How the stages work

| Stage | Files | What they are for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | `01`–`02` | What this is, who it is for, what it will not do |
| Research | `03`–`13` | Public history, checked against primary sources |
| Reference | `14`–`16` | Timeline, glossary, source list |
| Architecture | `17`–`20` | Two outlines, a consensus, a voice charter |
| Section drafts | `21`–`31` | The essay, written one movement at a time |
| Full drafts | `32`–`44` | Whole-essay revisions with a named job each time |
| Critics | `45`–`47` | Accuracy, voice, teaching |
| After critics | `48`–`50` | Repair, read-aloud, final |

A later draft is supposed to be *better*, not longer. If a pass added
words without adding sense, it failed.

## What this is not

Not a company history commissioned by anyone who sells these chips.
Not a legal document. Not a product comparison for buyers. Not a
secret. Every date and number here is the kind of thing a company
already put on a blog, a paper, or a press release.

## Voice, in one line

Talk like you are at a kitchen table with someone smart who did not
live through this, and you refuse to impress them with jargon.
