**Is a GPU just a graphics card?**
It started as one. The useful ones in this story are still
cousins of that card. The ones in racks have dropped the
monitor and kept the grid.

**Why didn't CPUs just get better at this?**
They did, a little. Vector units, more cores. They still carry
the generalist tax. The volume money was also not in "slightly
better at matrix multiply." It was in games, then in the habit
that games paid for.

**Could OpenCL have been the door?**
In another history, yes. In this one, the desk that won 2012
already spoke CUDA, and the libraries followed the desk.

**Is Google's chip "better"?**
At the job they measured in 2017, on their inference, it was
faster and thriftier than the CPU and GPU they compared it
to, and still memory-bound. "Better" without a job is a
keynote.

**Why do people still rent NVIDIA if TPUs exist?**
Because Tuesdays exist. Because not everyone lives in that
house compiler. Because the vanishing act is a place.

**Did transformers need a new chip?**
The first public one trained on P100s. Later ones ate racks.
The architecture did not wait for a generation. The generation
chased the architecture.

**What should I remember if I remember three dates?**
1999: the noun and the market. 2006: the door. 2012: the desk.
2017 is a fourth if you have room: the matrix gets a chair in
both houses.
