# Draft 22 — better ending

Job: write five last pages, keep one.

## E1 — the store (current)

If you take one thing to the store: ask what the chip is waiting
on. The answer is the real history.

*Keep the question. It is the only closer that is also a tool.*

## E2 — 2006 and 2012 in the room

I keep coming back to 2006 and 2012. Not because the present is
less interesting. Because you can still see them. CUDA is still
why a good chip can feel late. Two 580s are still the picture of
price meeting shape. The multipliers got cheaper until the
argument moved to water.

*Good as a penultimate. Not the last line. It reminisces.*

## E3 — train and serve walk apart

For a while one card faked both jobs. Training is a chorus.
Serving is a memory that has to answer before you feel the gap.
2026 is the year both big houses said that out loud. The loop did
not split. The afternoon did.

*True. A little newsy. Use one sentence in the body, not the
bow.*

## E4 — no winner

Do not ask who won. The GPU had a body and grew a language. The
TPU had a loop and grew a pantry. The rest is groceries and
counties. You are allowed to find that less romantic than a
keynote. It is also more useful.

*A bit editorial. Steal "body / language / pantry," drop the
scold.*

## E5 — the work did not change its mind (too fond of itself)

The work did not change its mind. It is still a lot of the same
sum.

*I have written this sentence too many times. Once in the whole
essay, or none. If it stays, it stays early, as a promise, not as
a last chord.*

## Decision

Penultimate: 2006 and 2012 still visible; pantry and language.
Last line: the store question, slightly quieter.

Draft of the actual close:

> You can still see 2006 and 2012 from here. A language that
> turned a graphics chip into a career. Two small memories that
> beat a building, then became a building. The GPU grew a matrix
> unit and kept its Tuesday. The TPU grew a pantry and kept its
> loop. The groceries got expensive.
>
> If you rent one of these things, or you only read about them:
> ask what it is waiting on. That answer is the history.

Yes.
