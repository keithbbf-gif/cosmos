# Draft 27 — no heroes

Job: strip the piece of implied statues. Buck, Jouppi, Krizhevsky
stay as people who did a named thing. No destiny. No genius
lighting.

---

CUDA is easy to tell as a hero story. A researcher walks into a
company, a door opens, the world changes. The true shape is
smaller and more crowded. Brook had a group. The 8800 had an
architecture team. The press release had a compiler to sell.
Buck's useful public sentence is a taste decision: C, not a new
religion. That is a committee that listened, or a company that
wanted customers who already existed. Either way, not a lone
lamp.

AlexNet is easy to tell as three names and a miracle. The paper
is three names and a memory limit. They cite a field. They split
a net because 3 GB said so. They used a language a lot of other
people were already using. The hinge is real. The statue is
optional.

The TPU is easy to tell as Google invents the future. The 2017
paper is a crowd. Jouppi is the name on the door of the
document. The useful move is subtraction and a disk bay, and the
useful confession is the skinny pipe. Other groups inside the
same company were sketching accelerators; the systolic one
shipped. That is how houses work.

2017 is easy to tell as NVIDIA "responds." Tensor Cores and TPU
v2 and the Transformer land on one calendar because the loop
had become obvious, not because a rivalry needed a television
beat.

The final essay should keep every proper name that earns a
picture and refuse the rest of the marble. If a sentence works
without a surname, try it.
