# Outline B — tell it as three arguments

The dangerous outline. A reader who likes "here is why" will be
happy. Risk: dates float and a specialist gets itchy.

1. **Argument 1: the work already had a shape.**
   Matrix multiply. Why CPUs pay a tax. Why a shader pipeline does
   not. Walk a pixel and a neuron until they look related. Then,
   and only then, say 1999 and 2006.
2. **Argument 2: a language is worth more than a multiplier.**
   Brook as almost. CUDA as C. cuDNN as the BLAS. Frameworks as
   the last mile. OpenCL as the virtuous other door. This is why
   a faster non-CUDA chip can still lose.
3. **Argument 3: once the loop is known, you can carve it in
   stone — and then you have to feed it.**
   TPU v1 as subtraction. The DDR3 punchline. Tensor Cores as
   NVIDIA carving the same loop *without* leaving CUDA. HBM,
   racks, power. 8t vs 8i and Rubin as "we finally admitted
   train and serve are different jobs."

Open with 2012 anyway — it is the best scene — but treat time as
evidence, not as the spine.

Strength: a reader leaves with a model of the industry, not a
list. Weakness: TPU generations will get squeezed; AMD may get
one paragraph and feel it.
