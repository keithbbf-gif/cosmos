# Draft 21 — better opening

Job: try three cold opens. Pick one. Do not keep a museum
entrance.

## Open A — the paper's shrug (current favorite)

In 2012, the paper that made computer vision change its mind
spends a few sentences on the hardware and shrugs. Two GTX 580s.
Three gigabytes each. Five or six days. The net was too big for
one card. Things will get better, the authors say, when the cards
get faster. They were not selling a future. They were explaining
a wait.

The wait became the industry.

## Open B — the building first (rejected)

Google used sixteen thousand CPU cores to find a cat. That is the
sentence people remember. The sentence that mattered more was
quieter, and it happened on a desk: two gaming GPUs, a week, a
contest. I am not opening with the cat. The cat is a magazine.
The desk is the fork in the road.

*Why reject:* clever, a little smug, and it makes the cat
experiment sound cheap. It wasn't.

## Open C — the sum, no date (rejected for now)

Multiply. Add. Repeat. That is most of a neural net and most of a
frame of a video game. This is a history of people noticing that,
then spending twenty years trying to keep the multipliers from
going hungry.

*Why reject as opener:* true, and better as the second breath.
Without 2012 it sounds like a textbook.

## Open D — a disk bay (save for the TPU section)

They slid the first TPU into a slot meant for a hard drive. Forty
watts, give or take how you count. A marching grid of multipliers
and a memory pipe like a straw. This is not the opening. It is
the middle's best object.

## Decision

Use A, then immediately the building as the second paragraph, not
as a trick. Draft 13 already does a version of this. Final should
start closer to A: the shrug first, the cards second, the
building third. Less "embarrass the field." The paper is enough.
