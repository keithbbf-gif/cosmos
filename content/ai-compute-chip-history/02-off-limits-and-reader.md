# Off-limits, and who this is for

## Reader

Someone who has used a chatbot, or trained a small model on a rented
GPU, or just watched the news and thought: *why is everyone talking
about chips?* They do not need a CS degree. They will tolerate a
systolic array if you walk it across the page like a parade.

They will not tolerate a brochure.

## Off-limits

- Any private system, house operating system, or internal mesh. This
  is public chip history. Period.
- Legal strategy, filings, claim charts, inventor stories dressed as
  education. Counsel does not live here.
- Undisclosed roadmaps. If it is only a rumor, it stays out or it is
  labeled as a rumor and then dropped.
- A buyer's guide. "Should you rent B200 or wait for Rubin" is a
  different essay, and a worse one.
- A graveyard tour of every startup accelerator. Name a pattern, pick
  two or three examples that teach it, move on.
- Hero worship. Jensen Huang is in this story because he is in this
  story. So is Norm Jouppi. So is a graduate student with two gaming
  cards. Nobody gets a statue.
- Fake precision. "About 40 watts" is honest when the paper and the
  blog disagree on the label. "Exactly 39.7" is a costume.

## Tone I will not use

- "In the rapidly evolving landscape…"
- "It is important to note that…"
- "Revolutionized the industry overnight."
- Vendor first person: "we deliver unparalleled…"
- Textbook passive: "It can be seen that matrix multiplication is…"

If a sentence could be read by a keynote backdrop, cut it.
