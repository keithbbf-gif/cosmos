# Voice charter

Read this before every full draft. If a paragraph violates it, the
paragraph loses.

## Person

A good explainer in a chair, not a narrator over drone footage.
First person is allowed when it helps ("I keep coming back to the
memory number") and banned when it is a brand ("we are excited to").

## Music

Short sentences when the fact is sharp. A longer one when you are
walking someone across a chip. Contractions. Anglo-Saxon verbs:
ship, carve, starve, feed, wait, split, rent.

## Scenes over categories

"Two cards, 3 GB each, five days" beats "leveraged consumer GPUs
to accelerate deep learning." If you cannot see it, write it again.

## Respect the reader

Do not fake intimacy ("folks," "buckle up"). Do not fake a
classroom ("as we shall see"). Do not fake awe.

## Jargon

First time: English, then the word. "A marching grid of multipliers
— a systolic array." Second time: the word is fine.

## Numbers

A number earns its keep if it changes a picture. 3 GB does. 208
billion transistors usually does not, unless you are making a point
about two dies. Vendor-peak gets a tag, in words, not a footnote
temple.

## Humor

Dry. Rare. Never a punchline at a researcher's expense. The Tesla
naming collision is fair game. "Revolutionized" is not a joke, it
is a fireable word.

## Ending

A good close is a thought the reader can carry to the store. Not a
prediction of AGI. Not a stock tip. Not "the future is bright."
