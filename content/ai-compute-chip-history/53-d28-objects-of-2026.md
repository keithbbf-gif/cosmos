# Draft 28 — three objects, not a catalog

Job: give the present a body without SKUs. One object each.

## Object 1: a rack with a name

Blackwell is two dies sold as one GPU, then — in the systems
people actually argue about — a rack whose name includes a
number of GPUs sharing a fabric. The object is not the
transistor count. The object is a plumber and a contract. When
a programmer says "a GPU" and a buyer says "an NVL72," they
are pointing at different decades that happen to share a year.

## Object 2: a TPU that admits serving is the job

Ironwood, 2025, is a seventh-generation TPU pitched as
inference-first: more HBM, fatter wires, pods you measure in
megawatts. The object is a long conversation that has to fit
somewhere. 8i, a year later, makes the admission a product
line. Training still exists. It got its own sibling.

## Object 3: a platform launch that is not yet a desk

Rubin, January 2026: six chips announced together, partner
boxes promised for the back half of the year. The object is a
slide that is allowed to be a slide. As of September 2026 you
do not write as if the world's rented default changed last
Tuesday. You write that the named successor exists, and that
the installed generation is still the one the invoices know.

## How this sits in the essay

Three sentences, maybe four. If the present needs a section,
it is "Groceries," not "The generations of 2024–2026." The
objects teach the same move as 2015's disk bay: look at the
package, not the adjective.
