# Butcher-block countertops — history, care, island tops

Staged drafts. Not published. Not a catalog.

This folder is a working guide for **wood tops** on **Bradley islands**:
what a butcher block actually is, how the kitchen stole the name, how
you keep an oiled top alive, and how a top sits on a box built in
Warren, Arkansas. The writing is meant to be read out loud to a
homeowner who is about to put real money in the middle of a kitchen.

If you want the short path: read `drafts/47-sequence-job-to-care-card.md`,
then the spec sheet in `drafts/46-spec-sheet-for-a-wood-top.md`.
Everyone else should start at `01` and walk the stages.

## Voice

Human. A person who has oiled a thirsty maple top in January and
refused a sink in walnut for a client who will not wipe a rim.
Numbers come from the USDA Forest Products Laboratory *Wood
Handbook*, from shop practice, and from the care habits commercial
block makers have published for a century. Guidelines are not a
warranty. Local humidity and the household win.

We do not invent SKUs, lead times, or warranty language. Named
Bradley islands (Moro, Caney Creek, Cossatot, Shiloh Creek, and the
rest) are **catalog memory** — river names the shop put on boxes —
not a live order book. Confirm the current dealer sheet before you
quote a client.

No COSMOS. No process talk. If a sentence sounds like a brochure,
it is wrong.

## How to use a draft

1. Read the frontmatter. If the topic is not your fight, skip it.
2. The first paragraph is the claim. If you disagree, write in the
   margin. Do not “improve” the sentence into marketing.
3. Thickness and overhang numbers are shop numbers unless a handbook
   is named. Do not treat a shop number like a stamp on a permit.
4. Food-safety talk is habit plus a clean top. It is not a lab
   certificate you can tape to a residential island.

## Provenance

Researched against:

- USDA Forest Products Laboratory *Wood Handbook* (FPL-GTR-190 /
  FPL-GTR-282) for hardness, shrinkage, and moisture movement.
  Paraphrased. Not copied.
- Public trade history of laminated maple blocks (shop cookies to
  glue-ups; the Midwest commercial shops; Williams-Sonoma and the
  1970s residential counter). Company lore is labeled as lore.
- Dean O. Cliver’s early-1990s wood-versus-plastic board work, and
  the later habit of overselling it. Cleaning still matters.
- NSF/ANSI 2 as a **commercial listing** story, not a house halo.
- Bradley Brand Furniture catalog memory: Warren, Arkansas; river-
  named islands; walnut/ash and gum two-tones; drop leaves; oiled
  cutting boards sold beside the islands. Inactive SKU sheets are
  history, not inventory.

Date-stamp a reprint before you hand it to a client. Wood does not
care about last year’s PDF.
