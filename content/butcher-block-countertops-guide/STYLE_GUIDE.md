# Style Guide

This series talks to people who will live with a wood island top, or
who will sell one and then get the phone call in February. Bradley
Brand builds islands in Warren. The series teaches the top. It does
not sell a line.

## Voice

Write as a person who has scraped a gum-wood glue-up and watched a
client leave a wet bag of ice on walnut overnight. Prefer shop
words, dates, species, and habits over general claims. A sentence
may be short. A sentence may run if it is carrying a fact.

Speak to the reader as a peer. Do not coach. Do not cheerlead. Do
not close with a moral.

First person is allowed when it is a shop observation. It is not a
diary.

## Banned phrasing

Do not use: delve, landscape (metaphorical), robust, leverage,
unlock, cutting-edge, game-changer, “In today’s,” “It’s important
to note,” Moreover, “Whether you’re,” “In conclusion,” “At the end
of the day,” “rich tapestry,” “journey,” “elevate,” “empower,”
“seamless,” “holistic,” “unpack,” “nuanced” as filler, “look no
further,” “in this comprehensive guide,” “let’s dive.”

Do not open with a dictionary definition. Do not close with a recap
list of “key takeaways.” If a list is a working table (Janka
numbers, oil schedule, fastener types), keep it. Decorative bullet
stacks are out.

No COSMOS. No AI. No process talk about how the draft was written.

## Facts

Mechanical numbers (Janka, shrinkage) come first from the USDA
Forest Products Laboratory *Wood Handbook*. Shop numbers (how far a
1.5-inch maple top can fly before it wants a bracket) stay labeled
as shop numbers.

Do not invent a live SKU, a lead time, a warranty, or a royal
commission. Historical Bradley island names are catalog memory.
If a story is too good and the paper is thin, say so.

Food-safe is a maintained surface, not a label. NSF listings are
commercial. Do not tell a homeowner their island is “NSF certified”
because the wood is maple.

## Brand

Bradley Brand Furniture. Warren, Arkansas. Hardwood islands and
boards. American-made claim is the shop’s; LLC paperwork is later.
Do not flatten those two dates into one founding myth. Do not write
as if every river-named island is still on a truck.
