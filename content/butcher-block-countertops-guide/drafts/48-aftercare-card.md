and the next owner, will thank the person who did.

The card is one page. It is not this series. It is the
residue of the series that can survive a move.

**What it is.** Species, grain, thickness, finish name,
mill, date, sink or no sink, seating fly if any.

**Oil contract.** Wash, dry, oil when pale. Pharmacy
mineral oil or the named cream. Never cooking oil.
Never the garage can. Year one drinks. Winter is the
exam.

**Film contract.** Wipe. Use a board. A scratch is a
finisher, not a rag.

**Heat.** Trivet. The iron is hotter than the story.

**Water.** No soak. No dishwasher. Dry the rim tonight
if a sink lives here. Steam from the dishwasher door
is still water.

**Marks.** Lemon and beet are chemistry. Sand with the
grain, oil a halo. Do not belt-sand the kitchen.

**Movement.** Hairlines in January are often weather.
Do not add screws. Call the mill if a strip actually
splits.

**Kids.** Marker and homework are why some lids are
filmed. If this one is oiled, it will look used. That
was the deal.

**The box.** Soft-close wants a hand. The furniture
back will take jeans. Lock the casters before you chop
if this island rolls.

**The word.** This lid may have been sold as butcher
block. The contract above is the thing that matters.
Cut only if the finish allows it.

I like a date and a name at the bottom. Houses change
hands. The island should arrive with its biography.
Maple is allowed to go amber. Walnut is allowed to
dent. Gum is allowed to look like gum. The farm table
that never left is allowed to look used. That is how
you know the room is a kitchen, and that is how a
Bradley top earns the floor it occupies.

If the card is missing, this draft is the card. Print
it. Tape it. Do not trust the drawer of manuals. The
trash pull is where Thursday lives.
