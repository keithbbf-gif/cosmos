The first butcher block was not laminated. It was a slice of a
tree, fibers standing up, three legs under it if you were lucky.

That is the object the word belonged to. A cookie of trunk,
end grain to the cleaver, heavy enough that it did not walk
when a shoulder of beef hit it. Butchers scraped it. They
salted it. They oiled it when it went pale. When the face
went hollow they sent it out to be turned down, or they lived
with the dish until the block was too thin to trust. Nobody
asked the block to host a birthday. Nobody asked it to match
the cabinets.

Lamination came later, when shops wanted size without a tree
that wide, and when a cracked cookie became a liability instead
of a badge. Strip on strip, or block on block, glued under
real pressure, maple taking the job because it glued clean and
did not perfume the meat. The tool stayed a tool. The joints
just got smaller.

Three facts transferred into the house, and the rest of the
romance did not.

One: thickness is structure. A shop block is two, three, four
inches because knives and bones are going to live there. A
1.5-inch residential edge-grain top is a good lid. It is not
a meat-market block. Do not specify a thin panel and a
stone-style seating fly and then act surprised when it bounces.

Two: oil is maintenance, not décor. The butcher did not
polyurethane the cookie. When you put a film on a “block,”
you have made a table that photographs like work.

Three: sanitation is a habit. Scrape. Wash. Dry. Oil. The
wood is not magic and the plastic is not a priest. A neglected
block with chicken water in the hollow is a neglected tool.

What did not transfer: sitting at the work. If you want stools
at a wood island, you are mixing jobs. Mix them on purpose.
Keep the hottest pans on a pad. Keep the standing water off
the oil. Tell the client they will treat this lid the way they
treat a cast-iron pan. If that sentence makes them tired, they
want quartz, and I will help them buy it without a speech
about authenticity.

Bradley can wear a shop-minded top. We order it like a tool
when the household is a tool household. We order furniture
when they are not. We do not let the word do the choosing.
