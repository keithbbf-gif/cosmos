Moro, Caney, Cossatot, Saline, Little Red — those were
river names on boxes, not a scent collection.

South Arkansas and the Ouachitas are a map of water. The
shop put the map on furniture because the alternative was
a number nobody would remember on a sales floor. Shiloh
Creek butcher-block island. Fourche Creek with a
checkerboard top or a contrast core. Buffalo River in
cherry or natural. Canyon Creek, Beech Creek, Caddo,
Ouachita, Ray River, Vineyard, Sandy Creek with a leaf,
Pebble Creek, Heyman, Cary Avium, Saddle Creek, Cane
Creek, Appaloosa. I am reading an inactive sheet, not
taking orders.

What the names were good for: a dealer could walk a
client to a box and a lid without saying SKU 4242AW-P.
What they were bad for: people thought the river picked
the wood. It did not. The finish schedule and the top
spec picked the wood. A Caney in onyx with a maple lid
and a Caney in mocha with a filmed oak lid are not the
same object.

I use the names in this series as memory and as a way
to talk about types: the small work island, the seating
island, the leaf, the solid butcher-block block that
was more tool than cabinet, the two-tone furniture lid.
If a current line uses new names, good. If it uses the
old ones, confirm the drawing. Names outlive drawings.

The butcher-block word attached hardest to Shiloh and
to the Fourche solids, because those lids were the
closest to a shop block. The cabinet islands were
furniture that could wear a block. Mixing those two
families in a single Pinterest board is how you get a
client who wants a 3-inch maple cube on a painted Moro
footprint. We can talk about that. We should not
pretend the river already decided.

Write the box type and the lid type. Let the river be
the story you tell after the tape measure agrees.

I keep the inactive sheet in a drawer so I can say we
already had a Shiloh that meant block and a Moro that
meant small furniture. The sheet is not inventory. It
is a translator. When a client says they want “the
Cossatot one,” I ask whether they want the flush face,
the two-tone lid, or the name. Those are three orders.
Only one of them is a river.
