Warren sits in Bradley County because the timber was here, not
because a marketing meeting liked the sound.

The county name is older than the furniture LLC. The lumber
towns of south Arkansas — Warren, Crossett, the mills that
ate pine and the hardwoods that stood in the bottoms — are
why a shop in this place knows gum, oak, ash, and the tight
local wood the old catalogs called Arkansas mahogany. I will
not pretend that name is a botanist’s name. It is a shop
name for a strong, relatively light hardwood with a fine
grain, closer to maple in texture than to the tropical trees
people picture when they hear mahogany. We engraved it. We
oiled it. We sold bread boards in it next to walnut-and-ash.

The furniture claim “since 1903” is the shop’s lineage talk.
The LLC paperwork is 2008. Those two dates can both be true
without being the same story. I will not flatten them. What
I will say is that the islands we built — Moro, Caney Creek,
Little Red, Buffalo, Cossatot, Ouachita, Saline, Fourche,
Shiloh Creek — were named like a map of water, and a lot of
them wore hardwood lids from the same woods the county already
knew.

Northern maple still wins a cutting contract. We have used
it. We have also used what freight and the yard made sane:
sycamore for boards that take a knife and a laser, red oak
when the floor was already oak, walnut when the client wanted
chocolate, heart gum next to walnut on a custom leaf. The
Midwest shop and the Arkansas shop are not rivals in this
series. They are two answers to the same question: what
hardwood will take glue, take a finish, and take a life in
a kitchen.

An island top from Warren is not a Boos block and should not
be sold as one. It is a furniture lid that can be specified
toward the tool or toward the table. The timber history is
why we are allowed to have an opinion. It is not a halo. A
bad glue-up from Arkansas is still a bad glue-up. A good
maple top from Illinois is still a good top. Buy the contract,
not the county.

When I walk a dealer through a Bradley wood top, I start with
the woods this shop actually handled, then I say which of
them I will oil and which I will film. That is the Arkansas
part of the history. The rest is just pride, and pride does
not keep a rim from staining.
