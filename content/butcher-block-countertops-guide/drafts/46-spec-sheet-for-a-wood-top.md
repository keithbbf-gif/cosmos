A spec sheet for a wood island top is six lines if you
are honest and two pages if you are scared.

I use six, plus the extras when they are real.

1. Species. Hard maple. Black walnut. White oak. Ash.
   Heart gum. Not “wood.” Not “butcher block.” Binomial
   on the first mention if the mill is fussy; shop name
   after.
2. Grain. Edge, end, or face. Strip width if edge.
   Checker size if end.
3. Thickness. A number. Built-up edge called out if the
   field is thinner than the nose.
4. Finish. Food-grade mineral oil, named cream, named
   hardwax oil, or named film. Recoat window if it is
   not mineral oil.
5. Fasteners. Z-clips, figure-eights, buttons, centerline
   screws. Underside finished yes or no.
6. Wet work. No sink. Sink with rim detail. Hybrid seam
   on rail. Dishwasher under fly yes or no.

Extras when they apply: seating fly and support; leaf
hinge and support; portable casters and locks; two-tone
pair and which wood takes the engraving; destination
climate; acclimation days; weight range for the box.

Refuse lines I write on the same page: no cooking oil;
no dishwasher; no “butcher block” as a spec; no NSF
claim on a residential lid we did not list; no live
SKU from a dead sheet.

The box gets its own lines: rails, mid-rail, finish of
the furniture back, seating height. This sheet is the
lid. Mixing lid and box on one muddy paragraph is how
a painted Moro gets quoted with a 3-inch end-grain
cube and nobody notices until the truck.

I date the sheet. I name the mill. I name the dealer.
I put a copy in the trash pull with the care card.
Houses change hands. The next owner should not have to
guess whether they may cut.

If a designer sends a rendering instead of this sheet,
I send the rendering back with these numbered lines
blank. Pretty lids are easy. Specified lids last.

One line I add when the dealer is new to wood: “cut
allowed — yes or no.” It feels childish until the
first client takes a knife to a filmed walnut and
sends a photograph. The child line would have saved
the week. Put it next to the finish. Let it be ugly
on the page. Ugly specs stay. Pretty ones get
paraphrased.
