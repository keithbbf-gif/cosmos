Wood will fly farther than stone before it needs steel,
and people use that fact to get greedy.

A stone slab is heavy and brittle. Shops put steel in a
long fly because the stone will snap and because the
weight wants a rail. Wood is lighter and it bends. A
1.5-inch maple edge-grain panel will take a breakfast
overhang that would make a 2-centimeter quartz nervous,
and then someone asks for 15 inches of stools and a
place to cross the legs.

Shop numbers, again labeled as shop:

- 8 to 10 inches on 1.5-inch edge-grain maple: I sleep.
- 10 to 12 on a thicker panel or with a hidden steel
  or a corbel: I sleep with one eye open until I see
  the hardware.
- 15 inches of unsupported wood: I do not sell it as
  seating. I sell it as a drawing that will bounce.

The bounce is the thing. A guest parks an elbow. The
field drums. The household decides wood feels cheap.
The wood is fine. The fly was vain.

Grain direction matters. Strips running with the length
of the island make little beams toward the stool. Strips
running the short way make the fly a set of short
cantilevers. I write the strip direction on the drawing
when seating is involved.

Bradley seating islands — the ones that were always meant
to take a stool — need the fly in the spec, not as a
field measurement after the box is built. A box designed
for a flush lid will not suddenly grow a comfortable
overhang. We add a rail, or we change the box, or we
tell the stools to live at a table.

Comfort numbers still come from the planning guidelines:
about 24 inches of width per person, enough height under
the lid for knees, enough aisle behind the stool that a
person can pass. Those are not wood numbers. They are
people numbers. Wood does not get to skip them because
it is friendly.

A filmed walnut fly can look thicker than it is. An oiled
maple fly will show the first dent on the nose, where
guests park a belt buckle. I ease that edge on purpose
and I do not make it sharp so a photograph can look
“modern.” The nose is where the lid meets the world. If
the fly is honest, the nose can be kind.
