A countertop that can take a knife is a different object from a
countertop that can take a photograph.

That sentence is why this folder exists. We have sold wood tops that
were tools, and wood tops that were furniture, and we have watched
the same two words — butcher block — get stapled to both. The
photograph does not show the lemon ring that will not sand out in
one Saturday. It does not show the screw that split the panel in
January because someone fastened a living top like a slab of
quartz. It does not show the client who wanted to “just wipe it”
and then called in March because the maple had gone pale and
thirsty under a forced-air vent.

Bradley islands are boxes. The top is a second contract. This
guide is the conversation we should have before anyone orders
“the wood one” off a mood board.

You will find numbers. Most of them come from the Forest Products
Laboratory handbook — hardness, shrinkage, the way maple moves
when a house goes from August in Warren to January in Denver —
and from shop practice that is older than any of our catalogs.
Where I give a shop number, I will say it is a shop number. Do
not treat a shop number like a stamp on a permit.

You will also find refusals. Some households should not own an
oiled top. Saying that early is cheaper than saying it after the
top is on a truck.

Read this like a person talking, because that is what it is. If a
sentence sounds like a brochure, it is wrong. Mark it.

The three fights this guide is built to win:

1. History, so you stop treating butcher block as a 2014 Pinterest
   texture and start treating it as a shop tool that learned to
   host.
2. Care, so the finish contract is chosen on purpose — oil you
   will keep, or a film you will not cut on.
3. Island tops for Bradley boxes: grain, thickness, overhang,
   the sink, the leaf, the fasteners, the card you tape inside
   the trash pull.

If you only have twenty minutes, skip to the sequence draft and
the spec sheet. If you have an afternoon, start here and keep
walking. The top will still be in the middle of the room in ten
years. It should earn the knives, or admit it never wanted them.
