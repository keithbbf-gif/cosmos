Hand a dealer a top spec that a mill can build, not a
mood.

Dealers live in filters. Butcher block is a filter.
Wood is a filter. Natural is a filter. None of those
builds a lid. I give them a six-line block they can
paste into a PO and a paragraph they can say on the
floor without sounding like a catalog.

The six lines: species (hard maple, not maple), grain
(edge, end, or face), thickness (a number), finish
(oil name or film name), fastener type, wet work (sink,
no sink, hybrid). If seating is involved, a seventh
line for fly and support. If a leaf is involved, an
eighth for hinge and support. That is enough. A two-
page finish schedule is how a PO gets skimmed and a
top gets guessed.

The floor paragraph: this is a wood lid on a furniture
box. If you cut on it, you oil it. If you host on it
and will not oil, we film it or we change material.
It will change color. It will take a mark. It can be
repaired if we stay in the oil contract. It is not
quartz. It is not a Boos commercial block unless we
actually bought one. It is a Bradley island top.

I do not give dealers a live SKU from a 2017 inactive
sheet. I do not give them a warranty sentence I cannot
stand behind. I do not give them “NSF maple” as a
hangtag. I do give them this series’ care card and the
sequence draft.

If the dealer needs a photograph, we photograph the
sample that has lived in a kitchen, not only the one
that left the sander. If they need a price, we price
the lid separately from the box so a later stone swap
does not invent a discount argument.

A good dealer will stand in the aisle with a tape and
use the refuse line. A dealer who only sends links
will sell the filter. I write for the first one. The
second one will not read this anyway.
