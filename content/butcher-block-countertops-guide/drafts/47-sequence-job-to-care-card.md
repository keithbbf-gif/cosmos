Choose the job, then the grain, then the species, then
the finish, then the fasteners, then the care card.

That is the whole method. People start with a photograph
of walnut stripes and then try to invent a life that
fits the photograph. I start with Wednesday.

Job. Cut, host, or both. If both, can the field split.
If the room cannot take an island, refuse the island
before you refuse the wood.

Grain. Edge for most residential work. End if they earn
it. Face if it is a table.

Species. Hard maple for oil and a knife. Walnut or
cherry for furniture and a story. Oak if the floors
already voted. Ash and gum when the pair or the yard
makes sense. Sample in the room.

Finish. Oil if they will cut and will keep the habit.
Film if they will host and will not. Hardwax oil if
they are the sort of household that already owns a
specific can and will buy it again. Do not stack
contracts.

Fasteners. Let it move. Finish the underside. Tell the
Bradley box the weight. Plan the fly. Plan the leaf.
Plan the seam.

Card. Oil name, pale test, three refusals, trivet,
lemon, winter, who to call. Tape it in the trash pull
on delivery day.

History sits under the method so you do not get fooled
by the word. Care sits in the method so the finish is
a habit. The island top sits on a Warren box so the
lid has furniture under it. If you skip a step, the
word butcher block will fill the hole, and the word
is how we got the filmed oak miles and the thirsty
pale islands and the screws in round holes.

Twenty minutes is enough to walk this sequence with a
dealer and a tape. An afternoon is enough to read the
rest of the folder. Ten years is how long the lid will
be in the middle of the room. Start with Wednesday.
End with the card. Do not start with the filter.
