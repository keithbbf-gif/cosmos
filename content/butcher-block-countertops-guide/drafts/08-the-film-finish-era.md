The 1980s gave America miles of oak “butcher block” sealed in
amber plastic, and then wondered why nobody cut on it.

That is not a joke about taste. It is a finish contract that
got sold as a material. Builders liked a glue-up that looked
busy under a warm varnish. It hid seams. It matched the oak
cabinets. It wiped like a table. The listing still said
butcher block because the strips showed. Homeowners put hot
crock-pots on it and were told to use a board, which they
did, which meant the expensive glue-up was doing the job of
a $20 plastic slab from the drawer.

I am not against film. Film is how I finish a walnut hosting
island that will see wine and homework. I am against the era’s
habit of calling a filmed oak run a cutting surface. The
knife scars the jacket. Water that sits in a scratch goes
black. Repair is a refinisher, not a rag of mineral oil.
People then decide wood is fussy, when the fussy thing was
the lie.

Conversion varnish and the better waterbornes of the last
twenty years are nicer jackets than the amber urethane of
the mall-kitchen years. They still are jackets. You do not
cut on a jacket. You do not “refresh” a jacket with salad
oil. You wipe it, you use a board, and you call a finisher
when the skin breaks.

The oil tradition never left the shops that still sold
tools. It looked old-fashioned next to the plastic oak.
Then the Food Network years put maple boards back on
television, and a new generation learned to oil, and a
newer generation learned to buy a filmed “block” from a
box store and feel confused.

Bradley lived through both. We sold oiled boards in gum
and walnut-ash next to islands that sometimes wore film
because the retailer wanted a no-maintenance story. This
guide will not pretend those two finishes are one heritage.
They are two contracts that shared a thumbnail.

If a client points at an 1988 oak island and says they want
that, I ask whether they want the color, the glue lines, or
the wipe-and-forget. Color we can talk about. Glue lines
are edge grain. Wipe-and-forget is film, or stone. I will
not rebuild the lie and call it restoration.
