# SLPWOW Claims Guardrails

**Status:** canon for every free resource in this pack.
**Audience:** writers, designers, reviewers, and anyone who ships a PDF under the SLPWOW free line.
**Binding rule:** if a sentence would not survive this file, it does not ship. Rewrite the sentence. Do not add a footnote to paper over it.

This file is the claims authority for `content/slpwow-free-resources-curriculum/`. Age bands, format briefs, domain briefs, and the clinician PDF style guide all defer here. A pretty layout does not override a forbidden claim.

## 1. What these materials are

SLPWOW free resources are **original practice supports** for licensed speech-language pathologists, clinical fellows under supervision, and student clinicians under a licensed supervisor.

They are:

- session-ready paper tools (worksheets, printouts, word lists, report *shells*)
- observational data boxes the clinician fills
- caregiver carryover *ideas* the clinician selects and adapts
- design-target age bands so a writer knows who the page is for

They are **not**:

- evaluations, screeners, or diagnostic instruments
- treatment protocols that promise an outcome
- medical advice
- a substitute for a licensed clinician’s judgment
- an endorsement by ASHA, a school district, a state board, or any test publisher

If a user could reasonably treat a page as a test, the page is wrong. Add a clinician-facing “not an instrument” line and remove scoring language.

## 2. Absolute prohibitions (do not write these)

Do not diagnose. Do not imply a diagnosis. Do not let a worksheet “find” a disorder.

| Forbidden move | Why it fails | Rewrite toward |
|---|---|---|
| “This child has [disorder].” | Diagnosis. | Leave the page blank for the clinician’s own words, or use “the clinician’s working targets.” |
| “Use this to diagnose / confirm / rule out …” | Diagnosis by product. | “Use only after the clinician has already selected a practice target.” |
| “Screen for,” “screener,” “identifies delay,” “detects disorder.” | Screening is a clinical act with a standard of care. A free PDF is not that act. | “Practice items. Not a screen.” |
| Standard scores, percentiles, stanines, age-equivalents, “severity ratings” printed as product output. | Those belong to standardized instruments the clinician already owns. | Tallies, plus/minus, independent/cued, clinician-chosen scale with no published norms. |
| “Age equivalent: 3;2” or any number that looks like a test result. | Fake psychometrics. | Chronological age as a *header field the clinician writes*, never a computed result. |
| “Treats,” “cures,” “fixes,” “eliminates,” “remediates [named disorder].” | Outcome claim. | “Practice,” “rehearse,” “try,” “support.” |
| “Guaranteed progress,” “will improve,” “results in 6 weeks.” | Outcome claim. | “Outcomes vary. The clinician decides whether and how to use this page.” |
| “Evidence-based,” “research-proven,” “clinically proven,” “ASHA-approved.” | We do not attach those badges to a free PDF unless Keith has a citation we own and a reviewer has checked it. Default is **no badge**. | Name a *technique class* in plain English (“imitation,” “focused stimulation”) without a proof stamp. |
| “Meets IEP / Common Core / state standard X.” | Compliance claim we cannot stand behind for every reader. | “A clinician may *choose* to align a target; this page does not certify alignment.” |
| Medical directives for swallow, voice pathology, airway, or hearing as treatment. | Out of scope for a free paper pack; risk of harm. | Educational hygiene *ideas* at most, plus “ask the treating clinician / physician.” |
| “This proves eligibility.” | Legal/educational determination. | No eligibility language. |

**Names of disorders** may appear in clinician notes as *examples of settings where a clinician already works* (“a caseload that includes children with a diagnosed language disorder”). They must never appear as something the PDF assigns to a person.

## 3. Required voice (use these)

Prefer verbs that describe **use**, not **verdict**:

- practice, rehearse, try, notice, mark, tally, describe, share, adapt
- clinician-selected target
- observed in this session
- cue provided / cue not provided
- independently / with model / with choice

Prefer nouns that stay humble:

- practice page, carryover idea, word list, observation box, session snapshot
- design age band (never “developmental age”)

Required stance on every product:

1. A licensed clinician (or supervised trainee) **chooses** whether the page fits.
2. The page does **not** tell the family what the person “has.”
3. Numbers on the page are **session tallies**, not scores.
4. Home use is **optional practice**, not assigned treatment, unless the treating clinician says otherwise.

## 4. Canonical footer (every free PDF)

Print this block on **page 1** (or the only page) in a readable size, not 6-pt gray:

> **SLPWOW free practice page — not an evaluation.** These materials do not diagnose, screen, or determine eligibility. They do not replace a licensed speech-language pathologist. Any boxes you fill are session notes the clinician chose to keep. Outcomes vary. © SLPWOW. Original work. Do not upload another publisher’s worksheet into this file.

If a page is a **report template**, append:

> **Not a diagnostic report.** This is a blank shell. Write only what you observed or what the record already states. Do not invent test scores. Do not paste protected health information into shared or public folders.

If a page touches **fluency or voice**, append the domain line from §8.

Do not replace this footer with a logo-only lockup. The words must remain.

## 5. Age-band claims

Age bands in this curriculum are **design targets** for vocabulary, picture load, font size, and page density. They are **not**:

- developmental norms
- age-of-acquisition tables presented as science
- cutoffs for who “should” produce a sound
- evidence that a child is delayed if they cannot complete the page

Allowed:

- “Designed for early-elementary sessions (about kindergarten through grade 2).”
- “Pictures assume a preschool attention span. The clinician may use the same words with an older student.”

Forbidden:

- “By age 5 all children will …”
- “If the child misses 4 items, refer for evaluation.” (That is a screen.)
- “Typical age of mastery: …” as a product claim. If a writer needs a developmental reminder, put it in an **internal** clinician brief, cite a public professional source the team actually read, and **do not print mastery ages on the family-facing or student-facing page.**

See `AGE_BANDS.md`.

## 6. Data boxes vs. tests

A data box is allowed when it is clearly a **tally of what happened on this page, this day**.

Allowed column headers:

- item
- response (check / dash / NR)
- cue (none / model / choice / other)
- notes

Forbidden column headers:

- standard score
- percentile
- severity
- “pass/fail” as a product judgment
- “disordered / typical”

A clinician may write a clinical opinion in a **notes** field. The printed product must not pre-print the opinion.

Do not ship answer keys that say “correct production of /r/.” Ship “clinician judgment of the target the clinician named.”

## 7. Report templates — extra fence

Report templates in the free line are **shells**: headings, blank lines, and reminder prompts.

They must not:

- contain canned diagnostic paragraphs (“The results indicate a moderate …”)
- contain fake scores that look like a real test
- name a commercial test and then supply a score table (that invites copying a publisher’s protocol)
- tell the writer to “conclude the student qualifies”
- include real names, real dates of birth, real school IDs, or any sample that could be mistaken for a living person

Sample text, if any, uses clearly fictional markers: `Alex Rivera (fictional)`, `DOB: 0000-00-00`, `School: Example Elementary`.

HIPAA / FERPA reminder (print on the template cover):

> Store completed copies only where your employer or practice already allows. This PDF is not a medical-record system.

See `briefs/report-templates.md`.

## 8. Domain-specific fences

### Speech sound and pattern practice

- Target is “the sound or pattern the clinician already chose.”
- Do not print “lisps mean …,” “if they cannot say /r/ by grade 2 …,” or tongue-placement medical claims.
- Cue notes may say “watch the clinician’s model.” They may not prescribe oral-motor exercise programs.

### Language (words, sentences, stories)

- Items are practice prompts, not language-sample norms.
- Do not print MLU cutoffs, “below average vocabulary,” or “autism-style language.”
- Narrative pages collect *what the student said*, not a disorder label.

### Phonological awareness / early print

- These are sound-and-print *games and marks*, not reading-disability screens.
- Do not claim to diagnose dyslexia or “reading readiness.”

### Social communication

- Scripts are optional practice, not a personality test.
- Do not imply that a quiet student, an autistic student, or a multilingual student is “deficient” for using a different style.
- Offer more than one way to respond (words, gesture, AAC, drawing).

### Fluency-friendly pages

Append:

> **Fluency note:** This page does not treat or cure stuttering. Do not coach a student to “just slow down” as a printed instruction. The treating clinician chooses any fluency strategy.

Forbidden on the student page: “stop stuttering,” “smooth speech goals” as a product title, prize charts for fluency counts that shame.

### Voice and hearing

- Free pack default: **out of medical scope.**
- A one-page “kind voice habits” printout may list hydration and shout-breaks as *general education*, never as treatment for nodules, reflux, or hearing loss.
- No decibel claims. No “this replaces an ENT visit.”

### AAC partner pages

- The board is a **partner support**, not an assessment of cognition.
- Do not write “low tech means low language.”
- Include a blank cell so the clinician adds the person’s own words.

### Feeding / swallow

- **Out of the free PDF pack.** Do not add chew-and-swallow worksheets.

## 9. Audience and download context

Free PDFs are **clinician-facing first**. A caregiver may see a page because the clinician handed it over. That does not make the PDF a direct-to-consumer therapy product.

Do not write:

- “Parents: diagnose at home.”
- “Skip the evaluation — start here.”
- “Share this group to see if your child has a delay.”

Do write:

- “Clinician: strike any item that does not fit. Send home only what you chose.”

## 10. Originality and copyright (non-negotiable)

This line ships **original SLPWOW work only**.

Do not:

- retype, paraphrase-close, or redraw a worksheet you found on the web, Teachers Pay Teachers, Boom, Super Duper, Linguisystems, a Facebook SLP group, or a colleague’s paid pack
- lift a publisher’s word list, game board, or distinctive character set
- use trademarked characters, school mascots you do not own, or celebrity likenesses
- paste ASHA, CDC, or test-manual tables into the PDF

You may:

- use ordinary English words (language itself is not a publisher’s list)
- describe linguistic categories (CVC, initial cluster, past-tense regular) in your own sentences
- commission or draw original pictures
- use fonts and stock art with a license that allows free redistribution of the PDF

If you cannot say **who wrote the sentence and when**, delete the sentence.

A “inspired by a session I ran” note is fine. A “recreated the popular ___ packet” note is a refuse.

See `PREPUBLISH.md` for the originality sign-off.

## 11. Inclusive and accurate language

- Use person-first or identity-first language the way the treating clinician and family already do. Default printed voice: **neutral and specific** (“the student,” “the child,” “the client”) without disorder-first headlines.
- Multilingual homes: say “home language” not “they don’t have language.”
- Do not use baby-talk in clinician copy. Do not mock misarticulations in titles (“wittle wabbit” as a product name).
- Avoid food as the only reinforcer art (allergies, feeding trauma).
- Avoid “boys vs girls speech” stereotypes.

## 12. Reviewer refuse list (fast scan)

A reviewer **refuses** the PDF if any of these are true:

1. Footer missing or rewritten into a slogan.
2. Any diagnose / screen / percentile / age-equivalent language.
3. Any outcome guarantee.
4. Any copied worksheet text or recognizable clone of a named commercial page.
5. Real-looking PHI in a sample.
6. Pass/fail printed as the product’s judgment.
7. Eligibility or IEP-qualification language.
8. Medical treatment instructions for swallow, voice pathology, or hearing.
9. “Evidence-based” badge without an owned, checked citation.
10. Age band presented as a developmental cutoff.

The refuse is the correct outcome. Fix the page. Do not argue the footer into 6-point type.

## 13. Claims the product *may* make

These are allowed because they are about **us**, not about the client:

- “Free to print for your own clinical sessions.”
- “Original SLPWOW page.”
- “Designed for [age band] sessions; clinician adapts.”
- “Black-and-white printer friendly.”
- “One-page session tool.”
- “Leave fields blank if they do not apply.”

These remain **disallowed** even if they are true of a future paid line until legal/content lead signs a separate claims sheet: insurance billing codes, CEU credit, “used in 10,000 clinics,” outcome percentages.

## 14. When someone asks for a diagnosis page — refuse

If a request is “make a worksheet that tells me if it’s apraxia / autism / disorder of …”:

**Refuse.** Point them to a licensed evaluation. Offer instead: a practice page for a target the clinician already named, or a blank observation snapshot.

That refuse is part of the product, not a failure of the product.
