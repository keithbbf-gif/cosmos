# Curriculum brief — Worksheets (`WS`)

**Format code:** `WS`
**Wave:** free clinician PDFs
**Read first:** `../CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md` · `../AGE_BANDS.md` · `../FORMAT_SCOPE.md` · `../STYLE_CLINICIAN_PDF.md`

A worksheet is a **trial sheet**. The clinician already has a target. The page gives the student something to point to, mark, or say, and gives the clinician a place to tally what happened **today**.

If the page decides who “has” a disorder, it is not a worksheet. It is a refuse.

## 1. Job, in one sentence

The student works a short, original set of items; the clinician records responses and cues; nobody leaves with a score that looks like a test.

## 2. In scope / out of scope

**In**

- picture-supported word or sentence tries
- mark-the-picture choice (2–3 choices, not a stealth screen)
- repeat-after-me lists with a data strip
- short “say it in your sentence” frames (`LE`+)
- optional self-rating of *task ease* (`LE`+)

**Out**

- cut-score probes
- “which sound is missing? that means you have …”
- 20-page seasonal packets
- handwriting curriculum
- reading-comprehension tests wearing an SLP logo
- clones of a famous publisher page (even with new clip art)

## 3. Age-band cuts (worksheet-specific)

| Band | Student items | Student action | Writer watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EI` | 1 | point, take, vocalize; adult marks | If you drew 8 boxes, you left the band. |
| `PK` | 4–6 | point / mark / say after model | No independent paragraph. |
| `EE` | 8–12 | say, mark, optional trace of **the target word** | Do not turn it into a decoding test. |
| `LE` | 10–16 | say + optional written word or short frame | Humor OK; mockery not. |
| `AD` | 8–12 text items | say, rephrase, refuse | Looks like a study sheet. |
| `AX` | 8–12 | same, adult register | No cartoon prize bar. |
| `MX` | ≤ 8 | clinician strikes | Print the strike instruction. |

## 4. Page inventory (what the writer actually delivers)

1. **Header** — SKU, practice job (≤ 8 words), band feel.
2. **Clinician box** (3 lines max)
   - Target the clinician names (blank line).
   - How to run: model → student try → mark.
   - “Strike items that do not fit. Not a test.”
3. **Student field** — original items. Number them.
4. **Data strip** — one row per item, or a compact tally if `EI`/`PK`.
5. **Send-home line** — `Send home: yes / no` (clinician circles). Default visual weight is *low*. The page is clinic-first.
6. **Footer** — canonical claims block.

Optional back:

- cue key (`none / model / choice / other`)
- two lines for “what I might try next session”
- three-word caregiver optional-try (or point them to a `PO`)

## 5. Item writing rules (original)

Write items from **ordinary English** and the session job. Do not import a published list.

**Speech-sound / pattern pages**

- One target the clinician fills in. You may *suggest* a position (`final`) without claiming mastery age.
- Prefer picturable, everyday nouns and verbs. Avoid tongue-twister jokes that shame.
- Minimal pairs are linguistic facts. Choose **your own** small set. Do not recreate a trademarked pair deck’s sequence, characters, or teaching script.

**Language pages**

- One job: name, describe, combine, retell a *short original* picture event.
- Do not print MLU targets or “below average.”
- Multilingual: allow a response in the home language; the clinician decides what to count.

**Awareness / print pages**

- Clap, sort, match first sound — as a **game of marks**, not a dyslexia screen.
- If the student must read a sentence to play, you left `PK`/`EE` or you changed domain.

**Social / fluency pages**

- Prompts are optional. Print “skip any.”
- No prize for “smooth speech.”
- Fluency footer line from the guardrails is required on `FLU` SKUs.

### Worked example (original items — not a publisher list)

These eight words are an **example of tone and picturability** for an `EE` speech-sound page whose clinician-named target happens to be a final voiceless stop. They are ordinary English, compiled here, not copied from a commercial deck:

`bike · book · cup · kite · sock · duck · boat · cake`

Clinician header (print):

> Target I already chose: ________  Position I care about today: ________

Do **not** print “final /k/ mastery age 4;0.”

### Worked example — sentence frame (`LE` `SYN`)

Original frames (the student may refuse or change them):

1. I put the _____ on the table.
2. We looked for the _____ by the door.
3. She handed me a _____.

The blank is a **word the clinician supplies from their own list**, not a hidden test item.

## 6. Data — what “good” looks like

Allowed marks: `+` `−` `NR` and a cue tick.

Write in the clinician box:

> `+` means I judged the try as meeting **the target I named**. That is my judgment today, not a standard score.

Forbidden: a printed key that maps 7/10 to “mild” or “refer.”

## 8. Production notes for design

- Item pictures: one idea per cell. Number outside the art.
- Leave a **blank item cell** on `MX` and unknown-caseload pages so the clinician can write a word.
- Photocopy test: print, copy once, fill with a ballpoint. If the table dies, thicken it.
- No “score” circle in the header, even empty. Empty circles get filled with percentages.

## 9. Claims and originality sign-off (worksheet)

Writer initials on the ticket:

- [ ] I did not retype a page I saw on the web or a marketplace.
- [ ] I did not paste a publisher word list.
- [ ] Title names a job, not a disorder.
- [ ] Footer is the canonical block.
- [ ] No referral rule, percentile, or age-equivalent.
- [ ] Fluency/voice extra line present if domain needs it.

Reviewer may refuse on any unchecked box. That is success of the process.

## 10. What we will not “just add”

| Ask | Answer |
|---|---|
| Answer key with IPA and severity | No severity. IPA on the **clinician** back is optional. |
| QR to a YouTube “speech delay test” | No. |
| 100-trial massed sheet for home | Out of wave. A `PO` with three optional tries is the home object. |
| “Make it like Boom” | Paper first. |
