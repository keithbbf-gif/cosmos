# Domain brief — Speech-sound and pattern practice (`ART`, `PHN`)

**Domains:** `ART` (clinician-named sound) · `PHN` (clinician-named pattern)
**Formats that may carry them:** `WS` · `PO` · `WL`
**Authority:** `../CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md` §8. Do not print mastery ages.

This domain is **practice of a target the clinician already chose**. The page does not discover a disorder, name a lisp as a punchline, or run an oral-motor program.

## 1. What we are making

Pages that let a person **try words or pictures** while a clinician listens for a sound or a pattern they named before the session.

We are not making:

- an articulation test
- a phonological-process inventory with percentages that “mean” a diagnosis
- a tongue-thrust protocol
- a clone of a famous card deck

## 2. Language on the page

**Header job examples (allowed):**

- Speech-sound practice — you name the sound
- Picture sort — you name the pattern
- Words that can show a final consonant

**Header job examples (forbidden):**

- Apraxia cards
- Lisp fixer
- /r/ delayed? find out

Clinician blank, always:

```
Target I already chose: ____________________
What I am listening for today: ______________
```

The PDF may suggest a *position* (initial / medial / final) as a checkbox the clinician uses. It may not say “children master this at age ___.”

## 3. `ART` vs `PHN`

| | `ART` | `PHN` |
|---|---|---|
| Writer’s job | Pictures or words; clinician writes the sound | Two piles or two colors; clinician writes the pattern |
| Student job | Say or mark | Sort, then say if the clinician wants |
| Trap | Becoming a 50-word reading list | Printing process names as diagnoses (“fronting = disordered”) |

Pattern names (`fronting`, `cluster reduction`) may appear **only** on the clinician header as optional words the clinician might write. Prefer: “Pattern I already named: ______.”

## 4. Session flow we design for

1. Clinician names the target (not the PDF).
2. Student sees pictures or a short list.
3. Clinician models if they choose to.
4. Student tries.
5. Clinician marks `+` `−` `NR` and a cue.
6. Nobody computes a severity.

Cue card companion: `SLPWOW-FR-PO-ART-EE-01` (wait / model / choice).

## 5. Stimulus (original)

Quarry words from `../briefs/word-lists.md` Seeds A–B, or write new ordinary English. Rules:

- Picturable.
- Not a joke at the student’s expense.
- Not a trademarked character.
- Not a 40-item dump on `PK`.

`PK` `PHN` sort: **four to six** pictures, two piles, pile labels are blank or generic (`this pile` / `that pile`) until the clinician writes.

`EE` `ART`: **eight to twelve** pictures, numbered, data strip aligned.

## 6. What we do with “errors”

The data strip records the clinician’s judgment of **today’s named target**. It does not print:

- phonetic transcription keys that assign a disorder
- “stimulable / not stimulable = prognosis”
- a mouth diagram the family will read as disease

A back-page clinician line is allowed:

```
I might listen for something else next time: ________
```

That is planning, not a second diagnosis.

## 7. Age-band notes

- `EI`: one object, adult marks. No “say 10.”
- `PK`: sort or four pictures.
- `EE`: default worksheet.
- `LE`+: fewer baby pictures; words may stand without art if the student can use them.
- `AD`/`AX`: list + blank self-record of *practice tries*, not “how disordered am I.”

## 8. First-wave SKUs (this domain)

- `SLPWOW-FR-WS-ART-EE-01`
- `SLPWOW-FR-WS-PHN-PK-01`
- `SLPWOW-FR-WL-ART-MX-01`
- `SLPWOW-FR-WL-ART-MX-02`
- `SLPWOW-FR-PO-ART-EE-01`

## 9. Refuse list (domain)

- Oral-motor exercise sheet as a “sound fixer”
- “Signs of childhood apraxia of speech” poster
- Any page that tells a parent their child has a lisp
- Massed home 100-trial calendar
- Recreated commercial minimal-pair characters
