# Domain brief — Language practice (`VOC`, `SYN`, `NAR`)

**Domains:** `VOC` (words) · `SYN` (sentences / morphology practice) · `NAR` (telling)
**Formats:** `WS` · `WL` · `PO` (caregiver optional-try only) · never a stealth language sample with norms
**Authority:** `../CLAIMS_GUARDRAILS.md`. No MLU cutoffs. No “below average vocabulary.”

These pages **rehearse** words, frames, and short tellings the clinician already wants to hear. They do not measure a language disorder.

## 1. Three jobs, kept separate

| Code | Student-facing job | Clinician-facing job |
|---|---|---|
| `VOC` | Name, describe, or use a word | Tally whether the try met **the word job I named** |
| `SYN` | Finish or change a sentence frame | Tally the frame job (not “Brown’s stage”) |
| `NAR` | Tell a short original picture event | Write what they told; do not score story grammar with a cutoff |

Do not stack all three on one `EE` face. A `MIX` page is strikeable and still **one** primary job in the title.

## 2. Language we will not print

- “Receptive / expressive delay”
- Autism-as-language-style headlines
- “Low MLU”
- Grade-level vocabulary badges
- “ELL = disordered” (never)
- Eligibility present-levels essays

Allowed clinician line:

```
Language I am hoping to hear today (my words): ________
Home language(s) I should leave room for: ________
```

A response in a home language is a **response**. The clinician decides what they were listening for. The PDF does not mark it wrong.

## 3. `VOC` — words

**Worksheet feel (`EE`):** one row of pictures. Three optional adult prompts, printed as a *menu*:

1. Name it.
2. Tell something about it.
3. Use it in a sentence you choose.

The clinician circles which prompt they used. They do not have to use all three.

**List feel:** Seed C (mime verbs) and Seed D (hallway nouns) in `../briefs/word-lists.md`.

**Trap:** a page of obscure nouns to look “academic.” Everyday words first.

## 4. `SYN` — frames

Print **frames**, not a grammar textbook.

Original frames (may ship on `SLPWOW-FR-WS-SYN-LE-01` / `SLPWOW-FR-WL-SYN-LE-01`):

- I put the _____ on the table.
- We looked for the _____ by the door.
- She handed me a _____.
- Yesterday I _____ the door. *(only if the clinician cares about past; otherwise strike)*

Rules:

- The blank is filled by a word the clinician or student supplies.
- Do not print “correct auxiliary or you fail.”
- Do not number “Stage 3” on the student page.

`PK` does not get a syntax worksheet. `PK` gets talking around pictures (`VOC` or `NAR`).

## 5. `NAR` — telling

**Three-box original event** (`SLPWOW-FR-WS-NAR-EE-01`):

We draw **our own** simple sequence (example assignment to art, not a copyrighted comic):

1. A backpack on a hook.
2. The same backpack on a floor.
3. A person picking it up.

Student job: tell what happened. Gesture, device, or speech all count as telling.

Clinician box:

```
I heard / saw this telling: ______________________________
I am not scoring this against a published story rubric.
```

Prompt words for the clinician (not a cutoff list): Seed F — `who · where · what happened · then · problem · fix · end · feeling`.

Do not print “missing ‘problem’ = disordered narrative.”

`AD` narrative pages use teen-plausible events (missed bus, group project) without soap-opera trauma.

## 6. Caregiver printout

If language goes home, it goes as `PO` optional-tries, not a language sample script:

> Listen once. Repeat their word. Add one short word if you want. Do not correct every sentence at dinner.

No “narrate everything they do for 20 minutes or they will not catch up.”

## 8. Refuse list (domain)

- Recreated story-grammar scoresheets from a named curriculum
- Vocabulary “age lists” copied from a web table
- “Autism social script” titled as a language diagnosis
- Cloze tests that are actually reading tests on an `EE` speech page
