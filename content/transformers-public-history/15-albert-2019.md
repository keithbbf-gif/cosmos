# ALBERT: factorized embeddings and cross-layer sharing (26 September 2019)

**Status:** staged draft. Not a publication.
**Lane:** public transformer architecture history.
**First public appearance:** 26 September 2019 (`arxiv-v1`).
**Primary source:** Lan, Chen, Goodman, Gimpel, Sharma, Soricut, *ALBERT: A Lite BERT for
Self-supervised Learning of Language Representations*, arXiv:1909.11942.

## The claim

ALBERT attacks **parameter count**, not sequence length. Two public devices:

1. **Factorized embedding parameterization.** The WordPiece embedding matrix is \(V \times E\)
   with \(E \ll H\), then projected to hidden size \(H\). Vocabulary no longer dominates the
   parameter table when \(H\) grows.
2. **Cross-layer parameter sharing.** All encoder layers share one set of weights (or share
   attention and FFN separately, in ablations). Depth still stacks, but the unique parameter
   tensor does not grow linearly with \(L\).

They also replace NSP with **sentence-order prediction** (SOP): same two segments, correct vs
swapped order. That is an objective tweak, closer to RoBERTa's NSP skepticism than to a new
block.

## What the artifact specified

ALBERT-xxlarge configurations in the paper reach BERT-Large-beating GLUE/SQuAD numbers with
far fewer unique parameters and more compute per step (because the hidden size is large even
when layers are shared). Sharing does **not** make a step cheaper in FLOPs the way a smaller
depth would; it makes the *parameter memory* smaller. That distinction is the architecture
lesson.

ICLR 2020 is the venue. The public date is 26 September 2019.

## What it displaced

The lazy identity "more layers ⇒ more parameters ⇒ better." After ALBERT, a paper must say
whether it scaled **depth, width, unique parameters, or FLOPs**. Mixture-of-experts will later
scale parameters faster than FLOPs per token (the opposite direction). ALBERT scaled FLOPs
and width while holding unique parameters down.

## Immediate lineage

Universal Transformer (Dehghani et al., 2018) had already publicized recurrence-over-depth.
ALBERT is the BERT-era production-simple sharing paper. Later "looped transformers" and
parameter-sharing deep-equilibrium work are cousins. Do not call them ALBERT.

Embedding factorization reappears whenever vocabularies get huge (multilingual, byte, or
code). The move is 2019-public.

## What this draft does not claim

It does not claim layer sharing is free in quality. The paper's own ablations show sharing
hurts if you do not compensate with width. It does not claim 2023 LLMs secretly share all
layers; open LLaMA-class models generally do not.
