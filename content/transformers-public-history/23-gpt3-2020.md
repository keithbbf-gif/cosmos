# GPT-3: few-shot in-context learning at 175B (28 May 2020)

**Status:** staged draft. Not a publication.
**Lane:** public transformer architecture history.
**First public appearance:** 28 May 2020 (`arxiv-v1`).
**Primary source:** Brown, Mann, Ryder, Subbiah, Kaplan, Dhariwal, Neelakantan, Shyam, Sastry,
Askell, Agarwal, Herbert-Voss, Krueger, Henighan, Child, Ramesh, Ziegler, Wu, Winter, Hesse,
Chen, Sigler, Litwin, Gray, Chess, Clark, Berner, McCandlish, Radford, Sutskever, Amodei,
*Language Models are Few-Shot Learners*, arXiv:2005.14165.

## The claim

GPT-3 is a **decoder-only** Transformer scaled to **175 billion** parameters, trained as a
language model, and evaluated as a **few-shot** learner: the "weights" of a new task are
examples sitting in the context window, not a fine-tune run. The architecture is not a new
attention primitive. The public event is that this topology, at this width and depth, plus
this prompting protocol, does things 1.5B GPT-2 did not.

NeurIPS 2020 is the venue. The public date is 28 May 2020.

## What the artifact specified

The 175B model (Table 2.1 in the paper): \(n_{\mathrm{layers}} = 96\),
\(d_{\mathrm{model}} = 12288\), \(d_{\mathrm{head}} = 128\), 96 heads, context 2048 tokens,
alternating dense and locally banded sparse attention (they cite Sparse Transformer). Smaller
models in the family are published as a scaling series. Training compute and data-mix
descriptions are in the paper and are author-reported.

Few-shot, one-shot, and zero-shot are defined as *inference protocols*, not as training
objectives. That definition is why later instruction-tuning papers can say they are changing
the protocol's reliability rather than inventing in-context learning.

Sparse / dense alternating layers mean GPT-3 is **not** a pure 2017 dense stack. Secondary
sources that drop this are wrong. It is still not a MoE.

## What it displaced

The practical need, for many NLP tasks, to fine-tune a BERT-class encoder to get a
headline-worthy number. It also displaced 1–10B as "the large model." The closed-weight
pattern (paper + API, no full checkpoint) becomes the commercial default after this.

## Immediate lineage

InstructGPT (blog 27 January 2022; arXiv 4 March 2022) aligns a GPT-3-class model with
human preferences. ChatGPT (30 November 2022 blog) is a product event on this line; it is
not an architecture paper. PaLM, Chinchilla, and LLaMA are the public-science scale
responses. OPT and BLOOM are the 2022 open-weight dense responses.

## What this draft does not claim

It does not claim the 175B figure was independently counted by this series; it is the paper's
number. It does not reconstruct unpublished later GPT blocks from this table. It does not
treat "emergent abilities" (a later paper) as part of the 2020 specification.
