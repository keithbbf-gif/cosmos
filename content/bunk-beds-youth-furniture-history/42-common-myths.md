# Common myths

I am going to knock these down in the voice I would use at a kitchen table. Sources live in the rest of the series. This page is the index of bunkum.

## "If it has a rail, it's safe."

Which rail. How long. How high above **this** mattress. How does it come off. What is under it. The 1990s ASTM-conforming deaths had rails.

## "Adult bunks don't need the kids' rules."

Part 1213 is the adult bunks' kids' geometry. Same openings. Different certificate. The cousin still visits.

## "It's a loft, not a bunk."

Tape the foundation underside. Over 30 inches is a bunk in the U.S. Europe already named high beds in the title.

## "Under 6 is just a suggestion."

It is a required instruction. The death file is toddlers. Suggestions do not get 16 CFR numbers.

## "The bottom bunk is a crib."

No. Cribs are 1219/1220 after 2011. A lower bunk is a bed with end openings and a ladder nearby.

## "Thicker mattress is plusher, so better."

Thicker mattress is a shorter fence. Maximum thickness is a compliance number.

## "We'll keep it off the wall."

Beds walk. The rule assumes the wall and demands a continuous rail.

## "Institutional means tougher, so better for kids."

Institutional is a manufacture-purpose exemption with an under-6 clawback. Tougher steel can still have a slot.

## "It meets ASTM, so CPSC is covered."

1999 happened because that sentence was false as a **system**. Ask for 16 CFR 1213 or 1513 **and** the F1427 year.

## "CPSC approved this SKU."

CPSC writes rules. Makers certify. "Approved" is a sales adjective.

## "Our kids survived it."

So did most kids. The file is about the ones who did not. Survival is not a test.

## "A strap on the dresser means it passes STURDY."

Straps are F3096 plus Anchor It. Passing is 60 pounds on an open drawer on a carpet block, for in-scope units after 2023-09-01. Old dressers need the strap **because** they may not pass.

## "Drop-side is fine if we immobilize it."

CPSC said looking cannot tell, and traditional drop-sides do not meet the 2011 crib standards. Immobilizer is not 16 CFR 1219.

## "Japanese / floor-style means we can skip the rail."

A futon on a loft is a mattress on a bunk. Floor tradition stacks time, not children.

## "Camp bunks don't count."

Children sleep in them. Culture is not a CFR exemption. Under-6 facilities definitely do not get "institution."

## "One more kid on the top is fine, they're small."

The instruction is one person. Load and horseplay are how falls and hangings share a night.

## "We took the ladder off so the little one can't climb."

See the footnote about the child confined on the upper bunk. You may be locking someone **up**.

## "Lead paint is a toy problem."

Children's bunks have coating and content limits. Rails get mouthed. CPSIA rode with the bunk.

## "Built-in millwork doesn't need a standard."

Then you are the standard. The child is unchanged.

## "This series is telling me never to buy a bunk."

This series is telling you to **see** one. Plenty of households use complying bunks with kids who are old enough, rails that finish, mattresses that fit, and rooms that have been walked for cords. The myth is that the object is automatically kind. Nothing high is automatically kind.
