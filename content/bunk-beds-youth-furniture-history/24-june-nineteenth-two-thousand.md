# June 19, 2000

Dates are how you keep a rule from becoming a legend.

- **January 22, 1998** — ANPR. 63 FR 3280. 2–1 vote.
- **January 22, 1999** — extension of time. 64 FR 3456.
- **March 3, 1999** — NPR. 64 FR 10245. Parts 1213 and 1513 proposed. 2–0–1.
- **March 24, 1999** — oral-comment notice. 64 FR 14158.
- **July 9, 1999** — extra neck-entrapment proposal. 64 FR 37051. 2–1.
- **December 22, 1999** — final rule published. 64 FR 71888. 2–1.
- **June 19, 2000** — effective. Applies to bunks **manufactured in the United States or imported on or after** that date.

Six months from publication to effective is the industry's time to retool, relabel, and decide whether a decorative heart stays. It is also how you get a last run of old geometry. I do not have a shipment count for that window. I have the calendar.

## What "effective" does and does not do

It does **not** vacuum every older bunk out of American bedrooms. Those beds stayed. They are in attics and on Facebook Marketplace now. The rule is a **manufacture/import** line, not a possession ban.

It does **not** mean a May 2000 bed is "certified safe" because it is close. Close is not a clause.

It **does** mean a retailer ordering for autumn 2000 should have been asking for the new geometry. If they were not, that is a 25-year-old compliance story, not a mystery.

## How to read a date stamp

The permanent label wants **month and year of manufacture**. That stamp is the runtime-binding of this whole series. Not the catalog year. Not the "collection" name. The month and year.

- On or after 06/2000: the bunk rule **applies** as a legal duty for that unit (unless institutional-only, etc.).
- Before: the bunk rule does not apply **as that duty**. ASTM of the day might. Common sense does. A recall might. Your child still has a neck.

CPSIA (2008) adds another calendar for children's products: testing, tracking, chemistry. A 2003 children's bunk can be "post-bunk-rule" and "pre-CPSIA extras." Two dates, two stacks. Draft 25.

## Why I want this date memorized

Because people say "modern bunks are safe" the way they say "modern cars have airbags." Modern is a fog. **June 19, 2000** is a switch.

Also because used bunks lie. A frame can be 1994 steel with a 2016 mattress and a 2022 quilt. The quilt does not re-manufacture the frame. Look at the stamp on the **wood or metal**, not at the bedding.

If there is no stamp, you have no date. No date is not "probably fine." No date is an unknown.

## Other dates you will need later

- **June 28, 2011** — new crib standards in force at retail (draft 32).
- **December 28, 2012** — child care and public accommodation cribs.
- **October 20, 2011** — toddler bed rule effective (draft 33).
- **December 29, 2022** — STURDY signed (draft 35).
- **September 1, 2023** — CSU rule (F2057-23 via 16 CFR 1261) applies to units manufactured after that date.

Youth furniture is a **calendar subject**. If you hate dates, you will be sold a story. If you like dates, you can still be sold a story, but you will notice.

## A small ceremony

If you work with kids' rooms — parent, buyer, facilities, social worker — write **2000-06-19** on the inside of your notebook cover next to **crib 2011-06-28** and **dresser 2023-09-01**. That is a whole professional education in 3 inches of ink.

The rest is commentary. I am writing the commentary anyway. The dates are the spine.
