# What I would ask a salesperson

Not to be a jerk. To see if anyone in the building has read a file.

If they can answer most of these, stay. If they pivot to thread-count and dinosaur decals, you are in a catalog, not a store.

## The bunk

1. Is this intended for children or adult use — and which certificate do you issue, CPC or GCC?
2. Please show the certificate. What 16 CFR part does it cite? 1213 or 1513?
3. What ASTM F1427 **year** is on the test report?
4. What is the **maximum mattress thickness**, and is it on the frame or only in a PDF?
5. Which side is the continuous rail, and where does the ladder go?
6. Show me the wall-side ends. I want to see the 0.22-inch story, not a 4-inch story.
7. Month and year of manufacture of **this unit**, not the floor sample.
8. Any open recalls on this model?
9. If I convert it to two twins or to a loft-only, what hardware and what instructions come in the box?
10. If I am in the EU/UK, do you have EN 747-1/2:**2024** — or are you waving an old edition?

You will not get ten complete answers in a big-box aisle. You might get four and a card for the vendor. Four plus a card is a start. Zero plus a smirk is a walk.

## The dresser next to it

1. Date of manufacture after September 1, 2023?
2. Does it meet 16 CFR 1261 / F2057-23?
3. Can you talk about the 60-pound carpet test without looking at a manager?
4. Where is the F3096 device, and what wall fastener do you recommend for a stud?
5. Height, weight, enclosed volume — does it even in-scope?

If they say "it comes with a strap, so you're good," draft 36.

## The crib (if the same ticket)

1. 16 CFR 1219 or 1220?
2. Manufacture date after the 2011 line — and not a drop-side.
3. CPC?
4. If it converts to a toddler bed, is the kit **in this box**, and is that life under 1217?

## How to hear the answers

"I'll have to check" is honorable.  
"They all meet all standards" is a tell.  
"CPSC approved" is a tell.  
"It's solid wood" is not an answer.  
"Our kids have one at home" is draft 42.

Write down the model **before** you leave. Certificates that arrive by email for a different model are a sport.

## Online listings

Same questions in a message. If the listing already names 16 CFR 1513 and a thickness and a date, they have done some homework. If the listing says "safe for toddlers" on an upper bunk, they have done the opposite of homework.

Photos: zoom the ends. Zoom the label. A seller who will not photograph a label is a seller who does not have one.

## The educational sentence

A salesperson is a probe for the **store**. Most-adverse orientation. If they fail, you have learned about the opening you were about to put a child in — the opening between you and a serious answer.
