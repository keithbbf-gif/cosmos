# Why ASTM F1427 was not enough

ASTM F1427 is not the villain. **Reliance** on a voluntary book with uneven compliance was the villain.

The 1999 findings under the CPSA, the ones a Commission has to make to issue a standard, include the ugly pair:

- Compliance with the voluntary standard is **not likely** to eliminate or adequately reduce the risk; **or**
- It is **unlikely** there will be **substantial compliance** with that voluntary standard.

They checked those boxes in public. Appendix to part 1213. 15 U.S.C. § 2058(f)(3).

## What F1427-96 already had

The preamble lists it fairly.

- Guardrails both sides, except up to **15 inches at each end**.
- Rail upper edge at least **5 inches** above the maximum specified mattress.
- Rails attached so removal takes an intentional release or sequential forces.
- Openings around the upper bunk small enough to stop a tapered block 3.5 by 6.2 inches.
- Lower-end openings within 9 inches of the lower sleeping surface: either stop that block or pass a 9-inch sphere.
- Labels and instructions.

That is a real standard. It is the parent of the federal geometry. If you mock ASTM as "just a sticker," you are not reading.

## What it did not hold

**Both ends open.** Fifteen inches at each end is how you get the wall-slot deaths in conforming beds.

**Neck in large lower-end openings.** The curved-member death. The mid-1999 extra proposal.

**Compliance as a social fact.** The Commission opened the ANPR (63 FR 3280, January 22, 1998) because deaths continued **and** because they had indications the volunteer book was not being followed enough. A standard that exists on paper while factories ship gaps is a story, not a control.

I do not have the 1998 compliance survey in this folder. I have the Commission's published judgment. That is enough to explain the rule. It is not enough to quantify "X percent of SKUs failed." I will not invent X.

## Voluntary versus mandatory is a political technology

Voluntary means: the industry writes, the good actors comply, the bad actors compete on price, and CPSC can nag, recall, or eventually preempt. Mandatory means: the same geometry (or a stricter one) is **the law** for every importer on the effective date, with certificates, customs, and civil penalties in the background.

Keith-voice: a voluntary standard is a **hope with a test method**. A mandatory standard is a **floor with a prosecutor**. COSMOS people will recognize the move. A check that cannot fail is not a check. A check that never ran is indistinguishable from a pass. ASTM that nobody follows is a green log.

## F1427 still exists

CPSC's current guidance still points at ASTM F1427 as an industry consensus standard with **other** design and performance requirements. The federal rule did not eat the whole book. It ate the entrapment core and federalized it.

So a serious maker today should be able to say: **16 CFR 1213 or 1513**, *and* **ASTM F1427-YY**, with a year. One citation is a red flag. Two citations is a conversation.

I did not open the current F1427 PDF. I will not recite 2024 clause numbers I have not seen. If you are buying for a school or a camp, pay for the current book or hire a lab that already owns it.

## The educational moral

When someone says "it meets ASTM," ask:

1. Which designation and year?
2. Does it also meet 16 CFR 1213 or 1513?
3. Who tested it, and is that lab CPSC-accepted if it is a children's bunk?
4. Is there a CPC or GCC you can **see**?

If they cannot do four, you do not have ASTM. You have a word.

The 1999 Commission already ran this experiment at national scale. The volunteer word was not enough. That is why you are reading a CFR part instead of a pamphlet.
