# Dressers and the tip-over decade

The bunk kills in openings. The dresser kills by becoming a wall that moves.

A clothing storage unit is a lever. Drawers are steps. The top is a prize (a toy, a remote, a parent phone). A toddler climbs. The mass that was stable with drawers closed is unstable with drawers open, especially on carpet, especially if the back legs sink in. The unit rotates. The child goes under it or with it.

I am not going to recite a death count I did not pull from a CPSC table in this pass. CPSC and pediatric groups have published tip-over numbers for years; they move as the files update. What I will stand on is the **legal scar**: Congress named an Act **STURDY** — Stop Tip-overs of Unstable, Risky Dressers on Youth — and signed it **December 29, 2022**. You do not get a name like that from a vibe.

## Why the dresser sat in the youth room unregulated

Because it was "adult furniture." Because matching suites put a tall chest next to a crib and called it a nursery. Because anchoring was a pamphlet (Anchor It) while the product could still be sold as a free-standing rectangle that failed a child's weight on an open drawer.

Voluntary ASTM F2057 existed. Like F1427, existence was not the same as a floor. The IKEA MALM recalls and other public cases taught a generation of parents the word *tip-over*. I will not reconstruct every recall SKU here. The public memory is enough to explain why STURDY had a constituency.

## What changed, in outline

CPSC first issued its own CSU rule (November 2022) with a May 24, 2023 effective date. Then STURDY told the Commission to adopt a qualifying voluntary standard or write a new one. On April 19, 2023, CPSC determined **ASTM F2057-23** met STURDY's tests. Direct final rule, 88 FR 28403, May 4, 2023. **16 CFR part 1261** now incorporates F2057-23. Applies to clothing storage units **manufactured after September 1, 2023**. The November 2022 rule was stayed and superseded.

That timeline is messy. Messy is honest. The floor that exists now is **F2057-23 as federal law** for in-scope units after that manufacture date.

## Scope, from CPSC's guidance

Free-standing, typical of bedroom clothing storage: chests, chests of drawers, armoires, chifferobes, bureaus, door chests, dressers.

F2057-23's public gates, as CPSC and AHFA describe them: at least **27 inches** tall, at least **30 pounds**, at least **3.2 cubic feet** of enclosed storage. All three.

Out of scope examples CPSC lists: bookcases, entertainment furniture, office, dining, jewelry armoires, underbed drawers, occasional/accent not for bedroom, laundry sorters, **built-ins**, clothing storage chests as in ASTM F2598.

Fabric dressers with drawers can be in if they meet the gates. "It's fabric" is not a waiver.

## The youth-room punch

The dresser is often older than the child. It is the object that **does not get replaced** when you buy a new bunk. So the room can have a 2024 bunk and a 2008 chest. The new rule does not re-manufacture the chest. Anchoring and retirement still matter. Draft 36.

## The educational sentence

A matching finish is not a matching standard. The bunk's book and the dresser's book are different decades and different physics. If you only modernized the bed, you modernized the wrong half of the photograph.
