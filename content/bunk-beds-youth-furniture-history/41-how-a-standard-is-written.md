# How a standard is written

A standard is not a revelation. It is a committee, a death file, a vote, and a test tool.

I want this page in a youth-furniture series because people treat ASTM and CPSC as weather. Weather just happens. Standards are **made**, which means they can be made badly, captured, late, or — sometimes — honest.

## The volunteer path

ASTM (and ISO, and CEN) gather manufacturers, test labs, advocates, sometimes government. They argue dimensions. They publish a number with a year. Good actors use it. Bad actors price against it. The book can be excellent and still lose as a social fact. F1427-96 is the exhibit.

Revision years matter. F2057-23 is not F2057-17. F1821-26 is not F1821-09. A certificate that hides the year is hiding the argument.

## The mandatory path in the U.S.

CPSC can:

- **Incorporate** a volunteer book (toddler beds, changing products, now CSUs).
- **Write** its own performance text (bunks 1213/1513).
- **Ban** a geometry by making compliance impossible (traditional drop-side cribs).
- Get **pushed by Congress** (CPSIA § 104; STURDY).

The bunk proceeding is a textbook of the write-your-own path: ANPR because deaths plus shaky compliance → NPR → extra proposal when the neck deaths demanded it → final rule with CPSA findings → six-month effective date.

The CSU proceeding is a textbook of the congressional path: campaign and volunteer book → CPSC's own rule → statute → adopt the new ASTM edition → supersede.

Neither path is "more scientific." They are **different machines for the same kind of scar**.

## What has to be true for a rule to exist

Under the CPSA findings (the bunk appendix): unreasonable risk; public interest; volunteer book won't do it or won't be followed; benefits vs costs; least burdensome adequate requirement.

Those sentences can be fought. Gall fought them. Fighting is not a bug. A 2–1 rule is still a rule. A 3–0 crib rule is still a rule. Unanimity is not the test of whether children died.

## Capture and the other failure

Committees can be slow. They can under-weight the advocate and over-weight the tooling cost. They can also be the only place the **wedge gets a number** instead of a sermon. I hold both facts.

If you only trust government, you will miss F1427's useful extras. If you only trust industry, you will miss 1999. This series is a both-houses education.

## International copies

EN and ISO are not CPSC. They share injury physics and diverge on scope, access, and institutional philosophy (draft 31). Copy-paste regulation is how you get a 600 mm trigger and a 30-inch trigger in the same shipping container and a factory that understands neither.

## How a civilian participates without becoming a lobbyist

- Read the *Federal Register* when your object is named. The bunk 1999 text is readable if you are stubborn.
- When a comment period is open, comment with **incidents and measurements**, not vibes.
- When a store lies about a standard, ask for the year. That is a tiny market signal.
- Do not treat a hangtag as participation.

## The educational sentence

Someone **chose** 15 inches, then chose to close one side of it. Someone **chose** 60 pounds and a carpet block. Someone **chose** June 19 and June 28 and September 1. Those choices are the opposite of natural. Natural is the gap. The standard is the argument we put in the gap's way.
