# Hotels, camps, and who the rule covers

People think safety rules are for "products in stores." Then they put their four-year-old in a bunk room that is **architecture**.

## Hotels, motels, Airbnbs

If the operator bought a **consumer bunk** and put it in a guest room, they bought a consumer bunk. 16 CFR 1213 or 1513 attached at manufacture/import. The operator's job is not to un-make the rail for the photo.

If the operator built a bunk **into the wall**, they may have become a manufacturer in the only sense that matters to a child. Custom millwork does not get a romantic exemption. It gets a gap, a cutout, and often **no label and no mattress spec**. I have seen beautiful timber bunk rooms with heart cutouts at toddler-head height. Beautiful is not a probe.

Crib precedent: lodging had an explicit **use** deadline in 2012 for complying cribs. Bunks never got that same public-accommodation use-date in the 1999 rule. Do not hear that as "hotels are free." Hear it as: the bunk duty attached to the **product**, and lodging is a place products live. State fire and lodging codes may add more. I am not your innkeeper's counsel.

Airbnb is a hotel that pretends it is a cousin. The child does not care. If you host, you chose to be in the lodging business. Under-6 on the top is still the instruction. A pack-and-play for the toddler is still the better object.

## Camps

Drafts 11 and 27. Procurement in dozens. Staff turnover. Rails unscrewed so trunks fit. The cultural script.

A camp director who wants to be educational rather than folkloric:

- Inventory date stamps.
- Retire pre-2000 frames from younger cabins first.
- Restore continuous rails on wall-side beds.
- Specify mattress thickness in the supply bid.
- Train counselors that "under 6" is not relevant if your camp starts at 7 — **hanging and horseplay still are**.
- Do not buy "institutional" as a personality.

I cannot write your accreditation body's bunk page. I can tell you folklore is not accreditation.

## Schools and daycare

Under-6 facilities are **not** institutions under the bunk parts. A preschool bunk is a consumer bunk problem. Many preschools should not have bunks at all. Cots exist. The existence of a rule is not a recommendation to install the product.

Schools with overnight trips: you just became a camp for a weekend.

## Universities

Draft 12. Policy PDFs plus product law. If you specify lofts, specify the book. If students bring retail lofts, you have a mixed fleet. Mixed fleets fail in the gap between departments.

## Who the rule covers, said in one breath

**Makers and importers** of non-institutional-only bunks on or after June 19, 2000.  
**Children's** extra stack if intended for 12 and under.  
**Retailers** who sell those products (and, for cribs, a sharper used-sale line).  
**Operators** who put the products in rooms — duty of care, state law, the product's own remaining instructions.  
**Parents and hosts** — not as manufacturers, as the last people who can still put the long rail to the wall.

If you are in two of those roles, you do not get to pick the lighter one.

## The educational sentence

A bunk room with a design budget is still a product room. If you cannot name the standard the millwork met, you did not buy a feature. You built a question and rented it to strangers.
