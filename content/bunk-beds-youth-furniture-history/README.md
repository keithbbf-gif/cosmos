# Bunk beds and youth furniture — history plus the safety house

This folder is a **staged educational series**. It is not a catalog, not a buying guide, and not a lawyer. It is one person — Keith BBF — walking the history of stacked sleeping furniture and the safety standards that got written after children died in it.

Forty-five drafts. Read them in order if you want the argument. Skip around if you already know which scar you came for.

## What this is

Youth furniture looks friendly. That is the trick. A bunk bed is a height machine in a child's room. A dresser is a lever with drawers for handles. A crib is a cage we trust with an infant who cannot speak. Every one of those objects has a paper trail: deaths, near-misses, a voluntary standard that did not hold, then a mandatory rule with a date on it.

I am not selling beds. I am teaching the paper trail.

A claim in this series is bound to a source. If I do not have the artifact, I say so. Invented death counts and "studies show" with no citation are the failure class this house was built to refuse.

## What this is not

- Not legal advice. Not medical advice. Not a product endorsement.
- Not a how-to for defeating a probe, a wedge block, or a lock.
- Not a complete world survey. The spine is United States CPSC law, with EN 747 and ISO 9098 as the other houses, not as decoration.
- Not a promise that a compliant product cannot hurt anyone. Compliance is a floor. Rooms are messier than floors.

If you need a ruling for a specific product, read the current Code of Federal Regulations and the current ASTM text, or ask counsel. Standards move. This series is a map of how they got there.

## Voice

Human. Mine. I will not write "in today's fast-paced world." I will not "delve." I will not pretend a Federal Register table is a vibe.

When CPSC counted **91** bunk-bed deaths of children under 15 from January 1990 through August 9, 1999, I will say **91**, and I will say what they warned in the same breath: those reports are a minimum, not a census. That warning is in the table footnote. Most summaries drop it. I will not.

## Reading the numbers

Three numbers do most of the work in the bunk half of this series:

1. **30 inches.** Underside of any foundation over 30 inches from the floor is a bunk bed under 16 CFR 1213.2 / 1513.2.
2. **June 19, 2000.** Effective date of the federal bunk rules. Beds made or imported on or after that date.
3. **Under 6.** The instruction that must travel with the bed: do not allow children under 6 years of age to use the upper bunk.

The dresser half of the series has its own three:

1. **December 29, 2022.** STURDY signed (15 U.S.C. § 2056f).
2. **September 1, 2023.** 16 CFR part 1261 applies to clothing storage units manufactured after that date.
3. **60 pounds.** The child-weight apparatus on the carpeted stability test in ASTM F2057-23.

If those six are the only facts you keep, you already know more than the average aisle.
