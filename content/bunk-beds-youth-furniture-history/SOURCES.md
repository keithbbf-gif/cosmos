# Sources

Primary first. Commentary last. If a draft asserts a number, the number lives here.

## United States — bunk beds

- CPSC, *Safety Standard for Bunk Beds; Final Rule*, 64 Fed. Reg. 71888 (Dec. 22, 1999). Effective June 19, 2000. Death and injury tables are in section B. [govinfo HTML](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-12-22/html/99-32676.htm)
- 16 CFR part 1213, Safety Standard for Entrapment Hazards in Bunk Beds (adult / not-intended-for-children). Definitions at § 1213.2. Requirements at § 1213.3. Tests at § 1213.4. Marking at § 1213.5. Instructions at § 1213.6.
- 16 CFR part 1513, Safety Standard for Bunk Beds (children's). Substantively identical entrapment geometry. Paired FHSA path.
- 16 CFR 1500.18(a)(18), hazardous-substance designation that part 1513 exempts a complying children's bunk from.
- CPSC, *Bunk Beds Business Guidance*. Definition (foundation underside more than 30 inches), guardrail summary, CPSIA extras, CPC vs GCC. [cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Bunk-Beds)
- ASTM F1427, *Standard Consumer Safety Specification for Bunk Beds* (voluntary; 1996 edition discussed in the 1999 final rule; later editions exist). Read-only ASTM library for current text.

## United States — cribs, toddler beds, changing products

- CPSC press release, June 28, 2011, "Safer Cribs for Babies Available Starting Today." 11 million+ crib recalls since 2007; at least 32 drop-side-related infant deaths since 2000; child-care / public-accommodation deadline December 28, 2012. [cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2011/Safer-Cribs-for-Babies-Available-Starting-TodayRetailers-required-to-sell-only-cribs-that-meet-CPSCs-new-crib-standards)
- CPSC OnSafety, June 14, 2011, "The New Crib Standard: Questions and Answers." [onsafety.cpsc.gov](https://onsafety.cpsc.gov/blog/2011/06/14/the-new-crib-standard-questions-and-answers)
- 16 CFR part 1219 (full-size cribs); 16 CFR part 1220 (non-full-size cribs). ASTM F1169 family, incorporated by reference as updated.
- 16 CFR part 1217, Safety Standard for Toddler Beds. Original effective date October 20, 2011 (76 FR 22019 / 22028). Later ASTM F1821 revisions incorporated under CPSIA § 104.
- CPSC, *Baby Changing Products Business Guidance*; 16 CFR part 1235; ASTM F2388. [cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Baby-Changing-Products)

## United States — clothing storage / tip-over

- STURDY Act, 15 U.S.C. § 2056f. Signed December 29, 2022.
- CPSC direct final rule adopting ASTM F2057-23, 88 Fed. Reg. 28403 (May 4, 2023). Effective September 1, 2023. Supersedes the November 2022 CSU rule that had been set for May 24, 2023.
- 16 CFR part 1261, Safety Standard for Clothing Storage Units.
- ASTM F2057-23, *Standard Safety Specification for Clothing Storage Units*.
- ASTM F3096, anti-tip device performance (packaged with covered CSUs).
- CPSC, *Clothing Storage Units* business guidance. Scope examples and out-of-scope list. [cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Clothing-Storage-Units)
- CPSC news release on adopting F2057-23. [cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Adopts-Final-Consumer-Product-Safety-Standard-to-Prevent-Tip-overs-of-Dressers-and-Other-Clothing-Storage-Units)
- CPSC Anchor It campaign (consumer anchoring guidance; not a substitute for F2057).

## Europe and ISO

- EN 747-1:2024, *Furniture — Bunk beds and high beds — Part 1: Safety, strength and durability requirements*.
- EN 747-2:2024, test methods.
- ISO 9098-1:2023, *Bunk beds and high beds — Safety requirements and tests — Part 1* (2nd edition, May 2023). Scope: internal length greater than 1 400 mm; maximum bed-base width 1 200 mm; upper surface of a bed base 600 mm or more above the floor. Special-purpose (prison, military, fire brigade) excluded. [iso.org](https://www.iso.org/standard/80976.html)
- SATRA summary of EN 747-1:2024 marking and under-six warning. [satra.com](https://www.satra.com/spotlight/article.php?id=329)

## History and culture (secondary)

- *Etymonline*, "bunk (n.1)": 1758 ship's sleeping-berth; *bunk-bed* by 1869; probable shortening of *bunker* "seat." [etymonline.com](https://www.etymonline.com/word/bunk)
- OED, *bunk* n.1: mid-1700s; origin listed as unknown in the unrevised entry. Use as a check on folk etymology, not as a full citation here (paywall).
- Penelope Green, bunk-room reporting, *New York Times* (Sept. 2, 2021) and related *Seattle Times* reprint. Natalie Larson (Williamsburg bedding historian): early-19th-century prisons, railcars, barracks; virtually no residential/hospitality bunk evidence before the 20th century.
- Dwell, "When Did Bunk Beds Become Childhood Status Symbols?" Mid-century TV (*Brady Bunch*, *Diff'rent Strokes*); Jason Reid, *Get Out of My Room!: A History of Teen Bedrooms in America*.
- Jason Reid, *Get Out of My Room!* (University of Chicago Press) — teen-bedroom history; use for culture, not for CPSC geometry.

## Cross-cutting U.S. product law

- Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act of 2008 (CPSIA), Pub. L. 110-314. Children's product testing, tracking labels, lead and phthalate limits. Section 104 durable infant/toddler product program (cribs, toddler beds, changing products).
- 16 CFR part 1303, lead-in-paint.
- 16 CFR 1500.48 / 1500.49, sharp points and edges on children's products.

## How I used these
