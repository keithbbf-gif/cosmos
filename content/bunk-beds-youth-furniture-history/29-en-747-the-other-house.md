# EN 747: the other house

If the U.S. bunk rule is a 1999 entrapment statute, EN 747 is a **furniture standard** that kept getting revised until 2024.

I have not purchased EN 747-1:2024 or EN 747-2:2024. I have the public scope lines (BSI, NEN, DIN table of contents) and a SATRA testing-house summary. That is enough to map the house. It is not enough to certify a bed. If you place product in the UK or EU, you need the text or a lab that owns it.

## What the 2024 scope says

EN 747-1 specifies safety, strength, and durability for bunk beds and high beds, **domestic and non-domestic**.

It applies when:

- internal length **greater than 1 400 mm**
- maximum bed-base width **1 200 mm**
- upper surface of a bed base **600 mm or more** above the floor

It does **not** apply to special-purpose beds including prisons, the military, and fire brigades.

If the bunk includes a table or storage, other European furniture standards may also apply. That sentence is how a stair-chest and a desk-loft drag extra books into the file.

## The table of contents is a personality test

DIN's public TOC for EN 747-1:2024-07 lists:

- Construction; accessible holes, gaps, openings
- Bed bases
- Safety barriers around upper beds
- Means of access (this is a first-class citizen)
- Shear and squeeze points
- Strength of access, frame, fastenings
- Stability
- Fastening of the higher bed to the lower bed
- Marking, instructions, purchase information
- Annex A, rationales

Compare to 16 CFR 1213: rails, ends, wedge, neck, labels. The U.S. mandatory text is a **hazard standard**. EN 747 is a **product standard** that includes hazard geometry **and** durability, stability, and the climb.

Neither is "stricter" in a single adjective. They are different shapes of attention.

## Marking, from SATRA's public summary

Beds should be clearly and permanently marked with:

- name or trademark of manufacturer or importer
- **maximum mattress thickness**
- for bases 600 mm up: **"This bed is not suitable for children under six years"** or a specified pictogram, visible

Permanent thickness on the product is the habit I already stole for draft 17. The under-six line matching the U.S. instruction is the developmental consensus crossing an ocean.

## High beds in the title

"Bunk beds and high beds." Europe refused to let lofts rename themselves out of the room. Six hundred millimeters to the **top** of the base is about 23.6 inches — a **lower** trigger than the U.S. 30-inch **underside** rule, and a different datum.

A bed can be in EN 747 and out of 16 CFR 1213, or the reverse, depending on construction. Do not convert and declare victory. Measure in the system you are selling into.

## Non-domestic

Hotels, hostels, staff housing, maybe the university. The standard wants those rooms **in** unless they are the special-purpose trio. That is a different institutional philosophy than the U.S. "manufactured only for" exemption. Draft 31.

## 2024 is a year, not a forever

EN 747 has had earlier lives. If a 2012 certificate is all a seller waves, you are looking at an old edition. I will not narrate every inter-edition change I have not read. I will say: **ask for 747-1:2024 / 747-2:2024** if you are in that market now, and let a lab explain any transition.

## The educational sentence

EN 747 is the house that says: height, holes, **and** the climb, for home and for lodging, with a permanent under-six mark. If your whole education is 16 CFR, you are half-traveled.
