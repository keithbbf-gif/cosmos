# The institutional exemption

16 CFR 1213.1(b) / 1513.1(b): the standard applies to all bunks except those **manufactured only for institutional use**, made or imported on or after June 19, 2000. Then: **facilities intended for use by children under age 6 are not considered to be institutions.**

Every word in that sentence is a fence. People lean on the word *institution* like it was a sofa.

## "Manufactured only for"

Not "eventually used in." Not "also sold to a college." **Only for.**

A factory that runs the same frame for Walmart and for a state prison bid is not making an institutional-only bed. A factory that builds to a Department of Corrections drawing, never catalogs it, never sells it to a parent, is in a different conversation.

I cannot audit your SKU from this chair. I can tell you the adverb **only** is doing the work, and adverbs in safety rules are where lawyers go to live.

## What is an institution, besides the clawback

The CFR bunk parts do not hand you a tidy list. ISO 9098 and EN 747 do, in the negative: they do not apply to special-purpose beds including prison, military, and fire brigades.

That trio is the archetype. Custody. Command. A firehouse bunk room. Supervised adults, procurement officers, in-house maintenance.

A **day-care nap room** is the opposite archetype, which is why the under-6 sentence exists. You do not get to call a preschool a prison.

## Gray buildings

**Summer camp.** Children, often over six. Seasonal staff. Beds bought in bulk. Is the bed manufactured only for institutions? Usually no — it is a catalog camp bunk. Even if a maker stamped "institutional," a camp for eight-year-olds is not the under-6 clawback, and I am still not going to bless a rusted 1988 frame. Draft 11.

**College.** Adults, sometimes. Housing department. Could be institutional-only if the bid is truly closed. Often is retail "college loft" with a purchase order. Draft 12.

**Hospital / psych / group home.** This is where I stop guessing. Licensing, CMS, state fire, and product law can stack. If you run one of these, you need counsel and a facilities file, not an essay.

**Hotel bunk room.** Paying guests, including families with toddlers. EN 747's non-domestic scope wants this kind of room *in*. The U.S. institutional exemption wants a high bar before a bed is *out*. A hotel buying retail bunks is a retailer-to-business sale of a consumer product. Act like it.

## Why the exemption exists at all

Barracks and cells have different users, different supervision, different sometimes-welded architecture. Forcing every brig bed through a children's warning label can be theater. The danger is the **export** of that theater into the children's market: "it's institutional, so it's tougher, so it's finer for kids." Tougher steel and a 15-inch wall gap can live in the same object.

The under-6 clawback is the Commission saying: we see that export coming.

## How a buyer should use this page

If anyone offers you an "institutional bunk" for a child's room, a family Airbnb, or a preschool, ask:

1. Was it manufactured **only** for institutional use — and can they show the bid spec?
2. Is the facility for children under six?
3. Does the frame still have openings you can see?
4. Why not a retail bunk with a CPC or GCC?

If the answer to 4 is "cheaper," you have the whole plot.

## The educational sentence

Institution is a **manufacture-purpose** word with a **small-child** tripwire, not a compliment and not a toughness rating. When in doubt, you are in the consumer rule. Doubt is the usual state.
