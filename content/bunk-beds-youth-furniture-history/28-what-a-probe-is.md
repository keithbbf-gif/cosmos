# What a probe is

A probe is a stand-in for a child who cannot attend the lab.

That is the whole philosophy. We do not put toddlers in openings on purpose. We put **shapes that represent body parts** into openings, with specified forces, times, and orientations, and we let pass/fail be a physical event.

The bunk rule uses at least three stand-ins:

1. The **wedge block** (Figure 1) — torso metaphor, tapered, "most adverse" orientation.
2. The **9-inch rigid sphere** — head-can-follow metaphor.
3. The **neck template** (Figure 2, A and B) — head-then-neck metaphor.

CPSC's guidance mentions a specially designed probe that simulates a child's head and neck and then **refuses to summarize** the motion. I am following that refusal past the purpose-level description in draft 15. The CFR is the procedure. This page is the **idea**.

## Why "most adverse"

A child does not approach a hole on the designer's favorite axis. They twist. The test is required to twist first. If you only ever photograph the pretty axis, you are doing marketing.

Most-adverse is also how you stop a factory from "passing" by careful aim. The lab is instructed to be a little cruel. Cruelty in the lab is mercy in the bedroom.

## Why a force and a minute

The 33 lbf for 60 seconds under the rail is not a poke. Soft goods and slightly flexible wood can give, then recover. A child who is stuck is not a poke either. They are a load over time. The minute is the honesty.

I will not translate 33 pounds into "about as hard as." People will try it with their hands and call it science.

## Why we undress the bed, then dress it again

Gaps hide in the dressed state and appear in the undressed state, or the reverse. The rule uses both where it has to. A test program that only ever uses the catalog mattress is a catalog.

## What a probe is not

It is not a child's body. Children vary. A pass is a **reduction of a known pattern**, not an immunity.

It is not something you need to 3D-print to be a good parent. Literacy is knowing that a heart-shaped cutout is a claim that should have met a shape. You do not need to own the shape.

It is not a toy. I have watched (in other product categories) people turn probes into content. That content slides toward how to beat the test. This series will not.

## Other youth-furniture probes

Cribs, toddler beds, play yards — ASTM and CPSC texts are full of probes: torso, head, neck, finger. Dressers use **weights and a carpet block** instead of a body-part silhouette, because the hazard is a tip, not a hole. Different physics, same idea: **simulate the child without using the child.**

Once you see it, you see it everywhere. That is the education.

## The COSMOS rhyme, if you want it

A probe is runtime binding for a furniture claim. "Safe openings" is an intention. The block either goes through or it does not. The report quotes the event, not the brochure.

I am not going to force this whole series into OS metaphors. I will allow this one. It is the same scar: a green log that never ran the flag.

If a maker cannot tell you **which probe, which clause, which lab**, they have a green log. You can walk away from a green log. You should.
