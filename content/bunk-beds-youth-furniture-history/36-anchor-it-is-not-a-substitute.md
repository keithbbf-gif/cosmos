# Anchor It is not a substitute

CPSC's Anchor It campaign is good advice. It is also how people talk themselves out of caring whether the dresser **passes**.

Anchoring is a **household control**. F2057-23 is a **product control**. They stack. They do not trade.

## Why the campaign exists

Because most of the dressers in American bedrooms were built before September 1, 2023, and because even a passing new dresser meets a child who is not a 60-pound test weight on a good day — two kids, a foot on the drawer, a carpet thicker than 0.43 inches, a unit walked six inches off the wall so the strap is slack.

The campaign's personality is: **assume the object can come down, attach it to the building.** Walls have studs. Drywall anchors have ratings. Televisions have their own straps. It is boring and it works when someone actually drives the screw.

## Why it is not enough alone

A strap that is in the junk drawer is not a strap.  
A strap into drywall fluff is a decoration.  
A rental where you "aren't allowed to make holes" is a **policy versus physics** fight. Physics does not care about the lease. (There are landlord-friendly kits; I am not your hardware aisle.)  
A strap on a unit that already fails with drawers open is a last line, not a design.

The old industry move — ship a cheap strap, fail a basic stability test, call it "anchoring included" — is exactly the move STURDY was written to end. F3096 still wants a **competent** device in the box. The stability tests still want the **bare unit** to survive the simulations.

If you let anchoring replace the test, you recreate 2010.

## Bunks and straps

People ask whether to strap a bunk to the wall. The bunk rule's answer to the wall is a **continuous rail**, not a furniture strap. A bunk can still rack, walk, or tip if it is a cheap tower on carpet. ASTM F1427 and EN 747 talk stability in ways 16 CFR 1213 barely does. A tall loft used as a jungle gym is a stability object.

I will not write a homemade bunk-anchoring spec. I will say: the wall is already in this story as a **gap**, and it can also be in this story as a **hold**. Do not solve the gap by pulling the bed six inches off the wall and then forgetting a strap. That is how you buy both problems.

## Cribs

Do not "anchor" a crib like a dresser if you mean something that changes the crib's geometry or adds cords. Cribs have their own hardware. Extra straps in an infant sleep space are how you invent hanging. Different object, different fear.

## How I do the room, as a person

New in-scope dresser: look for a 1261 story, install the F3096 device into a **stud**, still run the "would I let a toddler pull every drawer" thought.

Old dresser: strap or retire. Especially if it is tall, light, and already in a child's room. Retirement is allowed. Donation of an unanchorable cheap chest is how the next family gets the problem.

TV on the dresser: now you have a two-stage missile. Anchor both or change the furniture.

## The educational sentence

Anchor It is a campaign because the installed base is huge. STURDY is a law because campaigns do not redesign SKUs. Use both. Quote both. Do not let a store clerk trade you one for the other.
