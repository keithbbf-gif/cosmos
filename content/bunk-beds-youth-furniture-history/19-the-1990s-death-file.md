# The 1990s death file

I am going to put the table in English and then refuse to let it become a round number you repeat without the footnote.

**Source:** CPSC data files, January 1990 through August 9, 1999, as printed in 64 Fed. Reg. 71888, section B.

## The 91

Ninety-one reported bunk-bed deaths of children under fifteen.

| Year | Total | Entrapment | Hanging | Falls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1990 | 7 | 5 | 2 | — |
| 1991 | 15 | 10 | 2 | 3 |
| 1992 | 4 | 3 | 1 | — |
| 1993 | 19 | 10 | 7 | 2 |
| 1994 | 10 | 6 | 3 | 1 |
| 1995 | 12 | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| 1996 | 12 | 11 | 1 | — |
| 1997 | 8 | 6 | 2 | — |
| 1998 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 1999 (partial) | 1 | — | 1 | — |
| **Total** | **91** | **57** | **25** | **9** |

1997–1999 death-certificate files were **not complete** when they published. The late years looking "better" is partly an unfinished inbox. The Commission said so under the table. If you ever see a chart that ends in 1999 and celebrates a drop, ask whether they kept the footnote.

They also said these 91 are **neither a complete count nor a probability sample**. They are a **minimum** and a set of circumstances. Capture-recapture math estimated about **ten entrapment deaths a year** in the United States since 1990. Ten a year is the number I use when someone wants the "real" rate. I use it with the word *estimated* attached.

## Age is the plot

Under 1: 18 deaths, 16 of them entrapment.  
Age 1: 20 deaths, 19 entrapment.  
Age 2: 16 deaths, 13 entrapment.  
Age 3: 8 deaths, 7 entrapment.  
Age 4: 4 deaths, 1 entrapment.  
Age 5: 1 death, hanging.  
Then the hanging column grows.

Over 96 percent of entrapment deaths were age three or younger. All but one entrapment death was younger than five. About 76 percent of hanging deaths were age six or older. Falls split between little kids and kids nine and up.

This is why "under 6" is not a superstition. Entrapment is a **toddler** geometry. Hanging is often an **older child plus a ligature** geometry. One bed, two ages, two mechanisms. A single slogan cannot cover both. The instruction sheet tries anyway, and then adds horseplay and bedding because the hanging file demanded it.

## Where on the bed

122 entrapment incidents, fatal plus near-miss:

- Top bunk: 77 (39 fatal). Guardrail 51. Bed/wall 11. End structure 12.
- Bottom bunk: 27 (12 fatal). End structure 14. Bed/wall 6.
- Ladder: 7 (2 fatal).
- Unknown bunk: 11.

Near-misses were usually a caregiver pulling a child out. Minor injuries or none. The Commission kept them because the **potential** was the same as the deaths. That is a grown-up way to read a file. "Nobody died this time" is not a design review.

## What ASTM would have saved, and what it would not

Of 57 entrapment deaths, CPSC concluded **39** could have been prevented if the beds had conformed to the then-current ASTM standard, and **42** by the Commission's own rules. Three deaths happened in ASTM-conforming beds: two wall-gap slips through the allowed end opening, one neck in a curved lower-end member.

Those three sentences are the entire case for a **mandatory** rule that is **stricter than the volunteer book**. Not "ASTM is fake." ASTM prevented a hypothetical 39. The leftover deaths were still unacceptable, and compliance with the volunteer book was not dependable enough to bet children on.

## Injuries, 1998

About **34,300** bunk-related injuries to children under 15 treated in U.S. emergency rooms in 1998 (NEISS estimate). 41 percent younger than five. Mostly falls. A few limb entrapments. One two-year-old found hanging in a sheet, admitted with a head injury.

Deaths ≠ injuries. If you only read the death table you will over-weight entrapment and under-weight the everyday fall. If you only read the ER estimate you will think rails and ladders are the whole job. Read both.

## How I want this file used

Not as a scare poster. Not as "91" tattooed on a slide with a stock photo. As a **reason the clauses have the shapes they have**.

Wall gap → continuous rail.  
In-between openings → wedge plus sphere.  
Curved end → neck template.  
Toddler on top → under-6 instruction.  
Older kid plus belt → hanging warning, no extra ligatures.  
Uneven ASTM compliance → federal floor.

The rest of Stage 3 is those arrows, one at a time.

If a newer CPSC report has a later count, use the later count and cite it. Do not update this table from memory in 2030 and keep my name on it. The artifact is the 1999 *Federal Register*. I quoted it. That is the job.
