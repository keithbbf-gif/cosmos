# How to read this series

Start with the object in the room, not the slogan on the box.

A bunk bed is two sleeping surfaces sharing one footprint. That sentence is true and almost useless. The useful sentence is the legal one: in the United States, a bunk bed is a bed whose **foundation underside** sits **more than 30 inches** off the floor. A loft with no bed underneath can still be a bunk. A cute twin-over-twin that sits low enough might not be. Height is the gate, not marketing.

I learned to read furniture the way I learned to read systems. Existence is not identity. A folder named `safe` is not a safe folder. A rail that *looks* continuous is not continuous if the gap at the end is wide enough for a toddler to slide through and wedge against a wall. The 1999 Commission spent pages on that exact gap. Two children died in beds that already met the voluntary ASTM rail rules of the day. The mandatory rule closed the wall-side hole.

So: when I say "the rule," I mean a dated text you can fetch. When I say "the story," I mean the cultural path from ship berth to Brady Bunch set to the aisle at a big-box store. Do not mix them. The story explains why the object exists. The rule explains what the object is allowed to be.

## Three reader mistakes I am going to block early

**Mistake one: treating "ASTM" as a gold star.** ASTM F1427 is a real consensus standard and it still exists. It was not enough in the 1990s. CPSC said so, in public, with death counts. A hangtag that says ASTM is a starting claim. Ask which year, which clauses, and whether the **mandatory** federal parts were also tested.

**Mistake two: treating "adult bunk" as a children's exemption.** Part 1213 exists because adults' bunks get used by children anyway. The entrapment provisions are substantively identical to part 1513. The paperwork differs (GCC versus CPC). The geometry does not get a pass because the catalog said "college" or "military-style."

**Mistake three: treating the under-6 warning as folklore.** It is an instruction the manufacturer must ship with the bed. It is not a superstition and it is not a complete shield. Most of the 1990s entrapment deaths were age three and under. The warning matches the file. It does not make the lower bunk a crib.

## How I handle history

Stacked sleeping is older than the consumer product. Ships, barracks, railcars, prisons, camps. A bedding historian named Natalie Larson, quoted in a 2021 *Seattle Times* / *New York Times* line of reporting, said she found almost no evidence of bunks as ordinary residential or hotel furniture before the twentieth century. Homes preferred low, movable beds. Inns stuffed strangers onto shared mattresses.

I will not invent a medieval inventor. I will not baptize Iroquois longhouse platforms as "the first bunk bed" just because a furniture blog did. Platforms that stack people are a family of solutions. The **consumer bunk** — boxed, warranted, sold for a child's room — is a mid-century retail object riding a baby boom and a small bedroom.

If a date is etymology (1758 for "bunk" as a ship's berth; 1869 for "bunk-bed"), I will mark it as word-history, not as a patent.

## How I handle standards

A standard is a **decision with a test**. The test is the part that matters. "Guardrail required" is a slogan. "Upper edge at least 5 inches above the thickest mattress the maker allows, and no wedge-block passage under the rail with the mattress off" is a decision.

I will describe tests at the level a parent, a buyer, or a student needs: what is being simulated, why, and which citation holds the procedure. I will not write a lab cookbook. The neck-entrapment template is specified in 16 CFR 1213.4(c)(3). If you need to run it, open the CFR. If you need to understand why it exists, stay here.

## How I handle youth furniture that is not a bunk

The room is a system. Cribs (16 CFR 1219 / 1220, the 2011 line), toddler beds (16 CFR 1217), changing products (16 CFR 1235), clothing storage units (16 CFR 1261). A child who ages out of a crib does not age out of gravity. The dresser kills on a different schedule than the bunk. I will not pretend one rule covers the room.

## Tone

I talk the way I write the rest of this tree. Short when the fact is short. Long when the Commission was long. I will name the vote when the vote was 2–1. I will name the Commissioner who dissented. Disagreement is part of how a rule gets born.

If a paragraph sounds like a store, I failed. If a paragraph sounds like a statute dumped into English without a human in the room, I also failed. The job is the middle: a person who read the file, telling you what is in it.
