# Secondhand bunks and the garage sale

This is where the dates go to die.

A rule that attaches to **manufacture and import** leaves a 25-year trail of legal-to-own, ugly-to-use frames. Garage sales, Buy Nothing, campus curb, church rummage, the "free" listing with a photo taken at night. No label. One rail. A mattress from a different twin. A story: "our kids survived it."

Survival is not a test method. The 1990s file is full of kids who did not.

## What I will not do

I will not tell you it is illegal to **own** a 1998 bunk. I am not your state attorney general, and the federal bunk rule is not written as a household possession ban.

I will not tell you every pre-2000 bunk is a death. Some were built like bridges. Some of those bridges still have the 15-inch wall doors ASTM allowed.

I will not tell you a 2018 bunk from a stranger is fine. Rails walk off. People drill extra holes. A "2018" date can be a mattress tag.

## A used-bunk filter that is still educational, not a ban

Walk away — or treat as lumber — if:

- No manufacture date and no trustworthy maker story.
- Date before June 2000 **and** you need it for children, especially with a wall.
- A rail missing, or a rail that lifts off in one motion.
- Wall-side ends open like mouths.
- Decorative openings in the lower end that look like a torso-sized oval.
- Seller says "we never used the rails" or "the ladder is somewhere."
- It is a drop-side **crib** being sold as a bed. Wrong object. Draft 32.
- Price too good plus fresh paint (chemistry, and what did they cover).

Consider, if you are stubborn and the bed is post-2000, complete, labeled:

- Run the ten-minute ritual in draft 26.
- Search recalls.
- Buy the **specified** mattress size and thickness, new if you can.
- Put the long rail to the wall.
- Under 6 stays down.
- Do not accept missing instructions as a vibe. Email the maker.

Stubborn is allowed. Romantic is not.

## Cribs and dressers on the same table

Used crib: after June 28, 2011, **selling** a non-complying crib is the problem CPSC wrote into commerce. A church rummage crib from 2004 is not a bargain. It is a liability with a mobile attached.

Used dresser: pre-2023 is most of them. Anchor or don't take it. Especially the tall light ones.

Used changer: look for 1235 / F2388 era, straps that still exist, a pad that is the one the table was sold with.

The garage sale is a **time machine**. You can buy 1996, 2008, and 2022 in one afternoon and call it a nursery.

## Culture of "waste not"

I believe in not wasting. I also believe in not **re-circulating a known geometry**. There is a difference between a solid wood twin headboard and a bunk end with a pinch oval. One is material. One is a pattern we already paid for.

If you want to be green, retire the bad geometry to a reuse that is not a child's sleep — a storage loft in a workshop with no kids, a garden trellis, art. I am half joking about the trellis. I am not joking about retirement.

## The educational sentence

The secondhand market is the long memory of every effective date. If you only shop used, you must be **better** at labels than a person who shops new. New at least owes you a certificate in theory. Used owes you whatever the last kid did not break.
