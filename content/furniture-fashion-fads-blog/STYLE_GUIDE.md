# Style Guide

This pack is a magazine series, not a catalog, not a brand manifesto, and not a trend report. It is written for a reader who has bought a dining table, painted a room the wrong color, or inherited a dresser they cannot quite throw away.

Bradley Brand Furniture (bbfur) is **soft context only**. The shop’s useful contrast is hardwood that outlasts a catalog cycle versus the demo-room mistake that looks dated before the financing does. Argue for lasting craft. Do not sneer at the people who bought the fad. Do not invent company history, catalog claims, or customer quotes. If a Bradley fact is not in a dated source, mark `[CITE NEEDED]` or leave it out.

## Voice

Write as a person who has stood in High Point showrooms, thrift stores, and kitchens with oak islands that still take a beating. Prefer the specific object over the category. Prefer a decade and a designer over a mood.

- Sentences of mixed length. One short one after a long one is fine. Three short ones in a row is a tic.
- First person is allowed when the observation is earned (“I have seen more avocado refrigerators than I have seen avocado sofas that anyone still wanted”). Do not invent a personal life.
- Address the reader as a person in a room, not as a demographic.
- Humor is dry. No punchlines that require a drum.
- Opinions are allowed. They must sit next to a named object, a named year, or a named shop.

## Hard bans (AI speak)

Do not use: delve, landscape (as metaphor), robust, leverage, unlock, cutting-edge, game-changer, “In today’s,” “It’s important to note,” stacked “Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally,” “Whether you’re,” “In conclusion,” “At the end of the day,” “Let’s dive in,” tapestry, beacon, nestled, plethora, myriad, underscore (as verb), “elevate your,” “curated lifestyle,” “journey,” “empower,” “seamless,” “holistic,” “synergy,” “paradigm,” “next-level.”

Do not open with weather, a rhetorical question stack, or a paragraph that could sit on any blog about any product. Do not close with a recap of the recap.

“Curated” may appear only when mocking or describing a retail claim. “Wellness” and “biophilic” appear in the essay that is about those words as marketing.

## Facts

- Name decades, designers, retailers, woods, finishes, and hardware.
- Cite a source in the text or in a footnote-style parenthetical. If the fact is real but the page is not in hand, write `[CITE NEEDED]`.
- No invented percentages, “studies show,” or influencer quotes.
- No fake customer testimonials.
- If two sources disagree on a date (Memphis debut Sept. 18 vs. 19, 1981), say so or pick one and mark the other.
- Brand claims from product listings (Bradley Lumber 1902; no particleboard) are labeled as the company’s own copy until a primary record is attached.

## What ages / what dates

Every essay should leave the reader able to answer, about one class of object: what still works in a room, what already looks like a year, and what was never trying to last. That is the series question. Not “is fashion bad.” Fashion is how rooms get born. The question is what you are willing to replace in five years, and what you are not.

## Structure of a draft

1. YAML front matter: `status: draft`, `voice_check: human`, title, slug, dek, series, word_count.
2. A room or object in the first paragraph. No throat-clearing.
3. History with names and dates.
4. Retail: who sold it, in what form (catalog, showroom, television, feed).
5. Status: what the piece was meant to say about the owner.
6. Hangover: what the next decade did to it.
7. A craft note — what a hardwood shop would have done, or refused to do — without a sales close.
8. Photo cues in italics at natural breaks, keyed to `PHOTO_CAPTIONS.md`.

Target length: 1,200–2,000 words. If a piece wants 2,100, cut the last summary. If it is under 1,200, it is missing a retailer, a material, or a hangover.

## Photos

Period rooms, ads, catalog pages. Prefer public-domain or museum collections (Library of Congress, Smithsonian, Cooper Hewitt, Met, Indianapolis Museum of Art / Newfields for Miller House, Herman Miller archives where licensed). Note rights on every caption. Bradley Brand catalog pages and old sales PPT decks are an **optional own-source later** — do not pretend they are in the file now.
