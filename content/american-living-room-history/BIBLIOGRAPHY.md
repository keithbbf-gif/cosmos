# Bibliography

Working list for the series. Object records beat general surveys. If a draft cites a fact not on this list, the draft should name the source in its own Sources section.

## Rooms and the parlor-to-living-room change

- Clifford Edward Clark, Jr., *The American Family Home, 1800–1960* (University of North Carolina Press, 1986). Hall-and-parlor mixed use; later parlor and living-room arguments.
- Andrew Jackson Downing, *The Architecture of Country Houses* (1850). Center table as “emblem of the family circle.”
- Lillian Hart Tyron, “Abolishing the Parlor,” in *Speaking of Home: Being Essays of a Contented Woman* (Houghton Mifflin, 1915).
- Henry L. Wilson, *The Bungalow Book* (1910). Living room as heart, not face.
- *House Beautiful* (1898), on the over-decorated drawing room as a show-room.
- Godey’s Lady’s Book, parlor as “the face of the house,” as quoted in later parlor historiography.
- Oscar P. Fitzgerald, *Three Centuries of American Furniture* (1982; Gramercy reprint 1985). Seven-piece parlor set.
- Rancho Los Cerritos, “Status, Style, and Culture: Interpretation of the 1870s Parlor and the 1931 Living Room” (2018) — use for the set and Downing; do not follow its 1910/1918 stitch.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, Rococo Revival Parlor (Whittemore House woodwork, Astoria, ca. 1850; Belter-attributed seating).
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, Parlor from the William C. Williams House, Richmond, 1810–11 (68.137), Gallery 728.

## Early houses and seating

- Abbott Lowell Cummings, *The Framed Houses of Massachusetts Bay, 1625–1725* (Harvard / Belknap, 1979).
- Mark Girouard, *Life in the English Country House*.
- Ronald L. Hurst and Jonathan Prown, *Southern Furniture 1680–1830* (Colonial Williamsburg / Chipstone).
- Nancy Goyne Evans, *American Windsor Chairs* (Hudson Hills / Winterthur).
- Morrison H. Heckscher, *American Furniture in The Metropolitan Museum of Art, I. Late Colonial Period*.
- Benno M. Forman and Winterthur joined-furniture literature; Robert F. Trent on “Pilgrim Century” names.

## Federal, Empire, factory parlor

- Charles F. Montgomery, *American Furniture: The Federal Period* (Winterthur / Viking, 1966).
- Peter M. Kenny and Michael K. Brown, *Duncan Phyfe: Master Cabinetmaker in New York* (Metropolitan Museum of Art, 2011).
- *The London Chair-Makers’ and Cabinet-Makers’ Book of Prices* (1802; 1808 supplement) — curule seating.
- Met published parlor objects: sofa 60.4.1 (Pearsall); sofa 42.16; window seat 66.221.9 (Foot suite).
- Easy chairs: Met 63.114; 1993.31 (Hepplewhite saddle-cheek / commode function on the label).
- Belter: U.S. Patent 19,405 (23 February 1858); Met suite 57.130; slipper chair 51.79.9.
- Charles Eastlake, *Hints on Household Taste* (1868; American editions).

## Craftsman, ranch, midcentury

- Gustav Stickley, *The Craftsman*; David Cathers on Stickley numbering.
- Frank Lloyd Wright house literature for Robie / Prairie hearths — inventories only as published.
- Indianapolis Museum of Art / Newfields, Miller House and Garden NHL documentation (Saarinen, Girard, Kiley; 1953–57).
- Herman Miller / Knoll studio histories: Eames Lounge (1956); Saarinen Womb (1948).
- USDA Forest Products Laboratory, *Wood Handbook*, FPL-GTR-190 and later revisions.
