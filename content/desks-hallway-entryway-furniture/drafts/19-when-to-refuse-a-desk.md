Some rooms should not get a desk.

I will refuse a desk in a bedroom that already lost its walking space to a bed and a dresser. I will refuse a desk in a hall that is doing hall work — the mail will eat the writing, the wet will eat the finish, and you will stand up every time the door opens. I will refuse a desk in front of the only good window if the monitor will close the window like a shutter. Light is not furniture. Do not murder it for a hutch.

A dining table can be a desk three nights a week. That is a schedule, not a room. If the schedule becomes daily, you do not have a dining room. You have an office with a gravy stain. Either protect the table with a pad and admit the dual use, or move the work. I will not sell you a 60-inch executive pedestal to put on the carpet beside the table "just for now." Just for now is how dining rooms die.

Closets can take a desk if you are honest about heat, light, and door swing. A closet office is a real form. A closet stuffed with a 54-inch laminate L is a storage unit you sit in. Measure the opening. Leave the door. Ventilate. If you cannot, the closet should stay a closet.

I refuse standing desks as punishment. If a doctor asked for one, fine. If a catalog asked for one, sit down and think. I refuse "girl desks" and "youth desks" as names. Height changes. The job does not. Build the height. Do not shrink the seriousness.

Refusal is part of design. BBF collections will still show a desk for every room because that is what collections do. This folder sits beside them so someone can say no. No is a measurement.

I have said no to a sunroom that wanted to be an office in August. Heat and glare will write a worse review than I will. I have said no to a landing at the top of stairs. A chair with casters on a landing is a stunt. If the only leftover rectangle in the house is a stunt, the house does not have an office yet. It has a wish. Furnish the wish later.
