Sequence: walk in, put something down, sit, hang, leave.

That is the hall. If you also work at a desk in this house, add: sit, light, stay, hide the work, walk back through the hall. The two sequences should not share a surface. I have said that enough times that I can hear myself. I will say it again because houses keep trying to make one table do both.

Walk the hall sequence with an empty bag, then with a full one, then with a child, then with a guest who does not know your hooks. Each pass will delete an object or move a hook. That is the work. Collections can arrive after the sequence is true. If they arrive before, you will cut legs and return a bench.

Desk sequence, shorter because the desk drafts already did the math:

1. Refuse the room that cannot take it.
2. Height for the body. Twenty-nine inches as a camp, not a shrine.
3. Depth for the screen and the page.
4. Kneehole that is a hole.
5. Drawers that match the mess.
6. Light from the side the hand is not.
7. A back if the room can see it.

If you only have one afternoon, do the hall tape and the desk sawhorse. Those two mockups will save more money than a sale code. BBF collections will still be there on Monday. History will still be there. The door swing will still be there.

I write this as a close because folders like this get used out of order. That is fine. Standalone is the point. When you want the order, this is the order. Walk in. Do not decorate first. Decorating first is how we got lemons in a bowl on a console that blocks the storm door.

Keith will cut this. If a sentence got pretty, cut it again. The sequence is the artifact.

If you are recording, walk the sequence on camera with a bag and a wet umbrella. Do not point at a styled set. Point at the arc on the floor. The channel is a shop. Shops demonstrate. Adjacent collections can take the b-roll after the tape has spoken. That order is the whole argument of this folder.
