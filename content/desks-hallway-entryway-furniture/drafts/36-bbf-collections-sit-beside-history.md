BBF collections can sit next to this history. They should not replace it.

A collection is a set of forms that can be bought in the same finish, the same hardware language, the same height story. That is a real gift. A house can match a desk to a bookcase, a hall tree to a bench, without hiring a shop to invent a language. Matching is not a sin. Matching without a job is a rental.

This folder exists so a collection page does not have to carry the century. The page can show the object. The draft can say whether the name is earned. Secretary means a flap. Partner means two faces. Hall tree means a seat you can use. Pier table means a parlor pier, which you probably do not have. If the page uses the word anyway, the draft next to it should flinch in public.

I will not invent SKUs, stains, or lead times. I will not pretend a ready-to-assemble cam lock is a mortise. I will also not pretend a cam lock cannot hold a coat. Plenty of good halls run on honest RTA. The lie is the historical costume, not the particle core.

How to sit beside a collection without becoming it:

- Use the job words first: writing surface, kneehole, hook, drip, bench.
- Use the historical words only when the form can survive the essay.
- Photograph the back, the well, the underside of the seat.
- Say what the piece refuses. A 16-inch console refuses to be a desk. A 20-inch "kneehole" refuses knees.
- Leave a path to a handmade or antique piece if that is the better door. Collections are one door. They are not the house.

The BBF channel has other folders — handmade retail history, finishing chemistry, millwork — that teach adjacent truths. This one teaches desks and halls. Together they are a shop talking. Apart they are content. I would rather be a shop.

If a collection changes a finish name next season, these drafts should not break. They do not carry the stain chip. They carry the kneehole and the drip pan. That is the point of adjacency. The page can move. The job cannot. When the page moves and forgets the job, bring the draft back to the top of the pile and make the page flinch again.
