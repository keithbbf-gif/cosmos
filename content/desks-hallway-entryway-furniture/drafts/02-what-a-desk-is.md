A desk is a horizontal surface that will forgive you for leaving work on it overnight.

That is the whole definition. The drawers, the kneehole, the return, the hutch — those are arguments about what kind of mess you keep and whether other people are allowed to see it. If the top will not take a day's paper, a laptop, and a cup that will leave a ring, you do not have a desk. You have a console that wandered into the wrong room.

People get romantic about desks because writing used to be a public act. A merchant's counting house, a minister's study, a clerk on a high stool — the furniture announced that someone in the building had business that outlasted the afternoon. We still want that announcement. We also want a place to hide the charger and the tax envelope. Those two wants fight. History is the record of that fight.

A writing table is a desk that admitted it was a table. A secretary is a desk that admitted it was a cabinet. A partner desk is a desk that admitted two people were going to sit in each other's air. A roll-top is a desk that admitted the work was ugly when you left it. None of those admissions is a style. They are jobs. If you buy the silhouette and skip the job, you will own a photograph.

I will not call a makeup table a desk because it has a drawer. I will not call a dining table a desk because you paid bills on it in 2020. I will call a kitchen island a desk only if you are willing to say the kitchen is the office, which some houses honestly are. Names keep us honest.

Height is the first honesty test. Most seated American writing furniture lands near twenty-nine or thirty inches to the top. That number is a shop habit, not a commandment. A tall person in a thick chair will hate it. A short person at a 42-inch standing ledge will hate it for a different reason. Measure the body, then pick the form. History will wait.

Depth is the second test. Eighteen inches will hold a notebook and a lamp. Twenty-four will hold a keyboard if you are willing to live with the mouse hanging off the side. Thirty will hold a blotter the way a 1910 office expected. Past thirty-six you are buying a conference table and calling it intimacy.

BBF collections will offer rectangles that pass or fail those tests in a given room. That is fine. The collection is not the definition. The overnight surface is.
