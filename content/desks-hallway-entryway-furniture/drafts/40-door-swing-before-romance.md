Measure the door swing before you buy the romance.

Every pretty entry I have regretted had a door in it. Front doors swing in. Storm doors swing in or fight you. Closet doors swing into the bench. Bedroom doors swing into the "office nook" that is really a hall. I put painter's tape on the floor and I make the client walk the arc with a grocery bag. If they clip the tape, the furniture is already guilty.

Inswing depth is a number. A 36-inch door wants more than 36 inches of clear floor if you also have a body and a bag. Add a bench at 16 inches and you have built a puzzle. Sometimes the answer is a wall-mounted rail and no bench. Sometimes the answer is a bench on the hinge wall, tight, where the door's last inches miss the knees. Sometimes the answer is a slider you do not have. I will not invent a slider. I will invent a refusal.

Desks get this wrong too. A desk in an alcove with a door is a bruise waiting. Leave the door's arc or change the door. Do not change the person's shins.

Hardware height on the door will catch a hall tree's side hook. I have seen it. The hook wins once. The door wins forever. Keep side hooks inboard of the swing.

Romance is the photograph of a wreath and a console. I like a wreath. I like a console that the door can pass. If you can only have one, take the pass. The wreath can live on the door that already moves.

Outswing doors, where you have them, change the math and not the habit. People still buy as if every door eats the room. Measure anyway. A rare outswing can gift you a bench you thought you could not have. Do not assume. Tape.

This is staging in the traffic sense, and it is why these drafts are staged in the editorial sense: we are not publishing a vignette. We are publishing a way to not hate Tuesday.
