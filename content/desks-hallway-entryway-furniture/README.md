# Desks, hallway, and entryway furniture — BBF-adjacent drafts

Staged drafts. Not published. Not a collection page. Not a brochure.

This folder is history and design for **desks** and for **hallway / entryway furniture**, written to sit **next to** BBF collections — not to replace them. A collection page can show a desk, a hall tree, a console, a bench. These drafts teach the century that produced those forms, and the measurements that keep them from failing in a real house.

If you want the short path: read `drafts/36-bbf-collections-sit-beside-history.md`, then `drafts/43-sequence-walk-in-to-leave.md`. Everyone else should start at `01` and walk the stages.

## Voice

Human. Shop-floor first person. A person who has built, repaired, and refused desks and hall pieces, talking to a homeowner or a dealer who is about to spend real money or real floor space.

We do not invent SKUs, lead times, or warranty language. We do not call a shoe bench a Victorian hall stand. We do not call a pedestal desk a partner desk. Public history (Wooton 1874, hall stands ~1840, pier tables in parlors) is paraphrased, not copied. Shop numbers are shop numbers. Guidelines are not law.

BBF collections are the adjacent door: ready forms a person can buy after they understand the job. This folder is the job.

## How to use a draft

1. Read the frontmatter. If the topic is not your fight, skip it.
2. The first paragraph is the claim. If you disagree, write in the margin. Do not “improve” the sentence into marketing.
3. Measurements are finished-floor and clear passage unless noted.
4. Speak it. These are written to be said, not scanned.
