# Liftgate is a machine, not a promise

A liftgate is a shelf that goes up and down on the back of a truck.

That is the whole definition. Everything else people stuff into the word
is a different purchase.

## Why it exists

Trailers and many city units sit at dock height. Houses do not have
docks. Without a gate, the carton would have to jump. People get hurt.
Pieces get dropped. The gate is how freight meets a driveway.

It is also how a 1,500-pound crate becomes a driveway problem the
household cannot finish. The gate got it to the ground. The ground is
not the kitchen.

## What it is not

- Inside delivery
- A helper
- Stairs
- White glove
- A guarantee that this truck has a gate (you booked a gate; dispatch
  still has to put the freight on a gated truck)
- A guarantee that the gate will work on a slope, in snow, or on a
  street the truck cannot sit straight on

If the driveway is a hill, the driver may refuse the gate for safety.
That refusal is not laziness. It is how liftgates crush people and
doors. Have a backup: a different truck, a different day, or a
threshold the household can live with.

## Booking

Residential plus liftgate is the ordinary pair for a house without a
dock. Booking residential without a gate is how you get a tractor that
cannot put the freight down, then a redelivery, then a fee.

Booking a gate to a place that has a dock is how you pay for a machine
nobody used. Docks do not need gates. Houses do. Retail back rooms
sometimes need them. Ask.

Weight matters. Some gates and some drivers have limits. A hospitality
crate may need a different tool (forks, a smaller truck, a spot with a
dock).

## The BBF / marketplace memory

Those Wayfair-style BOLs that checked “Liftgate Service Required at
Delivery” were not being fancy. They were trying to keep an island from
being the last piece on a dock-height truck in a subdivision. The fee
was real. The checkmark was the only reason the fee was not a surprise
— when someone actually read the BOL.

If our factory-direct quote forgets the gate and the address is a
house, we donated the surprise.

## The truck that “has a gate” and the freight that is on the other truck

Booking a gate is a dispatch problem. The freight has to be on a
unit that actually has one. If the city run is a dock-only pup, your
gate lives on a different door. That is how a “liftgate prepaid”
still becomes “they couldn’t get it off.”

When a customer calls that story in, do not argue about whether
houses have docks. Trace: was the accessorial on the tender, and
did the delivering unit have the machine? If we booked it and they
sent the wrong truck, that is their redelivery. If we never booked
it, that is our quote.

## How to say it to a customer

“The truck has a lift so the piece can come down to your driveway. It
does not carry it through the house unless we booked that too.”

If they need it in the kitchen, climb the ladder in
[21-delivery-ladder.md](21-delivery-ladder.md). Do not lean on the word
liftgate to do the climb.
