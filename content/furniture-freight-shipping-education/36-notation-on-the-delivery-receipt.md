# The notation on the delivery receipt

Five words on a phone screen are worth more than a two-page email
on Monday.

The delivery receipt (POD, driver handheld, paper with a carbon, the
thing they ask you to sign) is the carrier’s memory of the stop. If
it says clear, their memory is that the freight looked fine. Your
later memory will be treated as a sequel.

## What to write

Specific, visible, boring:

- “Crushed left carton corner, possible top damage”
- “Wet carton, bottom 6 inches”
- “Crate slat broken, right side”
- “1 of 2 pieces only — base missing”
- “Pad torn, front right corner raw”

Not:

- “Damaged”
- “Looks bad”
- “Driver was rude”
- “Will inspect later”
- nothing, plus a smile

“Damaged” is a shrug. A clerk can shrug back. A location on the
piece plus the skin condition is a fact.

If the handheld only has a damage dropdown, use it *and* put words
in the note field. If there is no note field, photograph the screen
and the piece together.

## When to write it

Before the signature. The signature is the period. People sign and
then try to add a sentence. Some drivers will reopen. Many will not.
Treat the first sign as the last.

If the driver is in a hurry, that is not your emergency. The island
is your emergency.

## “Subject to inspection”

Households love this phrase. Some clerks treat it as meaningless.
It is better than silence and worse than a real exception. If you
can see a wound, name the wound. If you cannot, open the carton
while they wait if the job allows, or name the skin (“carton
punctured, contents unknown”) so the file is not clean.

## Driver pushback

They will say: “That’s just the box.” They will say: “My boss will
kill me.” They will say: “Nobody else writes novels.”

Answer: “The box is how your network handles my furniture. I’m
writing what I see.” Stay calm. Do not write a novel about the
driver. Write the wood.

If they refuse to let you note, do not sign, photograph the refusal
if it is safe, and call the number on the BOL. A signature you were
bullied into is still a signature. Avoid it.

## BBF on company truck

We still write a receipt. We still want the exception if we scuffed
a door. Honesty at the stop is cheaper than a review in a month.

## Words that hold vs words that melt

Hold: “Puncture 2 in. on carton right face, aligned with rail.”
Melt: “Box dinged, maybe ok.”

Hold: “Wet to 8 in., mold smell, refused.”
Melt: “Looks rained on??”

Hold: “Only 1 pc on truck, BOL says 2, short base.”
Melt: “Where’s the rest?”

If English is not the signer’s first language, a photo of the
wound plus the word “damage” plus a finger in the frame is still
better than a clean sign. Help them. Do not write the novel for
them in a way that invents a wound you cannot see.

## After

Photograph the signed screen or the paper. Send it to the office
the same hour. A notation that lives only on the customer’s phone
will go into a beach bag.
