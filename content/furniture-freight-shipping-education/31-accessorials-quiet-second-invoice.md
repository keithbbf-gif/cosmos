# Accessorials — the quiet second invoice

The linehaul is the number people remember. Accessorials are the
number that arrives later and makes us look like we baited them.

An accessorial is extra work: a gate, a house, a wait, a second
attempt, a flight, a notify, a weekend, a limited-access church. The
carrier did the work (or says they did). The invoice names it.

## The ones furniture lives with

- Residential
- Liftgate
- Inside / threshold / room of choice
- Notify / appointment
- Stairs / extra labor / two-person
- Limited access
- Overlength / extreme length
- Redelivery
- Detention / wait time
- Reconsignment / storage
- Sort and segregate (retailer docks)
- Hazardous (rare for us; still do not hide a finish-related
  flammable if we ever ship one)

You do not need to memorize prices. You need to memorize *which of
these the address implies*.

## Implied vs booked

If the address is a house, residential is implied even when we
forget to book it. The fee will still come. Booking it is how we
put it in the first quote.

If the address is a house and we forget the gate, we may pay a
redelivery *and* the gate.

If the driver waits at a retailer because we had no appointment,
detention is implied by the wait, not by our mood.

## The ones that look like insults

Redelivery. Storage. Attempt. Customers hear punishment. Explain
them as second jobs: the truck came, the job could not finish, the
truck has to come again. If the miss was our bad number, we should
eat it. If the miss was a screened call, they should.

## Marketplace pre-checks

Those old Wayfair BOLs that pre-checked liftgate, residential,
inside, stairs, notify were an attempt to move accessorials into
the first truth. When the program does that, do not uncheck them
to make a rate look pretty. You are unchecking a porch.

## A stack that would have saved an argument

Illustration only — your tariffs will print different dollars:

- linehaul, 102 lb, high class: the number the salesperson
  remembered
- residential
- liftgate
- notify
- *not* included: inside, stairs, redelivery, detention

The remembered number was never the job. The job was a house.
If we had printed the stack, the customer could have chosen
threshold vs “my nephew will help at the curb.” What they cannot
choose is a surprise.

When a second invoice arrives, answer with the stack, not with
shame. If we omitted a knowable line, we eat it and fix the
template. If they asked for a house and we said house, they own
the house lines.

## How BBF quotes without looking slick

A freight number should be a little stack:

- linehaul
- accessorials we already know
- a written “if the site needs X, X is extra”

If we need one number, we bake the likely stack and say we baked
it. A single pretty number with no stack is how we fund arguments.
