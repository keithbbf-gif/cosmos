# Blanket wrap, said plainly

Blanket wrap is not a cozy word for “we were gentle.” It is a way of using
a truck.

The piece is not a carton in a wall of cartons. It is furniture, wrapped in
moving pads, strapped into a trailer that was built (or at least equipped)
to haul furniture. People also say pad wrap. Same idea. If a salesperson
says “we’ll blanket wrap it” and the carrier that shows up is a commodity
LTL city unit with no pads, you did not buy blanket wrap. You bought a
sentence.

## What you are buying

- Pads between finish and strap, finish and floor, finish and the next
  piece.
- A crew that knows how to wrap a case so the pad stays.
- Space. Honest blanket wrap does not stack a pallet of something else on
  a dining table.
- Often air-ride, because a leaf-spring surprise is a crack looking for a
  glue joint.
- Fewer terminal handoffs than general LTL. Sometimes none.

You are usually not buying, unless you wrote it:

- white glove assembly
- debris haul-away
- a two-person carry up a walk-up
- insurance at replacement value
- a guaranteed Thursday

Those are other products. They can ride with blanket wrap. They are not
included in the pads.

## What it is good for

Finished case goods. An island with a top you care about. Hospitality
product that will be seen in a corridor, not hidden in a warehouse.
Anything whose carton would cost as much as the ride, or would still lose
to a forklift.

BBF’s hardwood is exactly the citizen this mode was invented for — when
the dollars and the calendar can stand it.

## What it is bad for

- A driver who uses one thin pad and a ratchet like a punishment.
- A shop that sends a greasy, dusty piece and expects the pad to be a
  bath.
- A destination that still needs a 53-foot truck on a lane that wants a
  straight truck.
- A buyer who heard “blanket wrap” and thought “white glove into the
  bedroom, hardware in a labeled bag, cartons gone.”

Dirty pads transfer grit. Grit under a pad is sandpaper. If the specialized
carrier’s pads look like they moved a garage, say so before they wrap your
top. We can supply clean shop blankets for the first layer. We should.

## Blanket wrap vs “write blanket wrap on the LTL BOL”

A note on a commodity bill of lading is a wish. The dock may pad it. The
dock may not. The next terminal will not inherit your wish. If the piece
must be pad-wrapped, book a pad-wrap carrier or crate for the network you
actually booked.

## A sentence that is allowed and a sentence that is not

Allowed: “This island is going on a furniture van, pads and straps,
not a general LTL terminal if we can book the van this week.”

Not allowed: “We’ll blanket wrap it so it shows up like white
glove.”

The first is a mode. The second is two products taped together.
If the customer needs the second, sell the van *and* the rung.
If the van is not available this week, say crate-and-LTL or wait.
Do not write blanket wrap on a commodity pickup as a consolation.

## Cost, said without romance

You pay for the unused air above the piece and for a human who is slower
than a forklift. Compare that to crating labor, crate freight (crates add
cube), and the probability of a refinish. If the specialized number is
higher and the crate-plus-LTL number is lower, still ask which one arrives
as furniture.
