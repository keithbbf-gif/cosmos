# What the driver is and is not allowed to do

A driver is a person with a clock, a back, a tariff, and sometimes a
helper. They are not the salesperson who said “they’ll take care of
it.”

If BBF sells past the driver’s job, the porch becomes a fight between
two innocents.

## What they are there to do

- Show up with the freight that matches the PRO
- Get it off the truck in the way that was booked (dock, gate, carry)
- Get a signature
- Keep themselves and the piece from becoming a hospital story

On a furniture / white-glove crew, add: wrap, place, maybe unpack,
maybe light assembly, maybe haul debris — *if the paper says so*.

## What they will often refuse

- Shoes off in a house (some will, some will not; do not sell “they’ll
  wear booties” unless the program says booties)
- Moving the customer’s existing sofa to make a path
- Installing into a wall, leveling a floor, cutting a crate in a
  finished room with no protection
- Taking a cash tip as a substitute for an accessorial (and we should
  not teach customers to try)
- Entering a site that looks unsafe: ice, dogs, a ladder as a stair
- Waiting while someone runs to the bank
- Signing a customer’s extra waiver that their dispatcher has not seen

Union and contract drivers have brighter lines. Final-mile two-person
crews have more room and still have lines. LTL city drivers have the
brightest lines of all: freight off the truck, paper signed, gone.

## Helpers

A helper is a second body you paid for, or a rider the driver brought.
A customer’s teenager is not a helper the carrier owns. If the piece
needs two trained people, book two trained people. Do not build a plan
that requires a stranger’s kid.

## Our own driver

Company truck is not a loophole in physics. Our person may still
refuse a walk-up that needs two. We should back them. A wrecked
shoulder is not a customer-service win. If we want white glove, we
send a crew, not a hero.

## How to brief a customer

“The driver will do what we booked. They will not rearrange your
house. Please have a path. Please have the person who can sign and
inspect. Please do not ask them to ‘just take it upstairs’ if we did
not buy stairs.”

If the customer needs more, we change the booking or we meet them
with our own crew.

## “Can you just…”

The most expensive three words on a porch. Just take it upstairs.
Just wait twenty minutes. Just put it on the wet lawn under a tarp.
Just sign for my neighbor. Just cut the crate in the dining room.

Each *just* is a different job or a different risk. The polite
answer is: “That’s not on the ticket. We can reschedule with the
right service, or I can take it to the booked stop.” If BBF’s
customer is the one asking, we should have sold the ticket that
included the *just*, or we should say no before the truck is
there.

## When a driver is wrong

Sometimes they are: they dump at the curb when inside was paid; they
refuse a gate that would have been safe; they are rude. Get names,
times, photos, the PRO. Call the carrier the same day. Do not scream
at the person and then forget the file. The file is how the next
driver does better.
