# Obvious damage vs concealed damage

Claims are two different sports. People lose because they play the
second sport with the first sport’s habits.

## Obvious (visible, noted, “exception”)

The carton is crushed. The crate is stove in. The pad is torn and
the corner is raw. The piece is wet. A fork hole has a view.

The consignee can see it while the truck is there. The job is to
write what they see on the delivery receipt, photograph it against
the truck if they can, and decide: refuse, or accept with the
exception.

This is the claim a cargo clerk expects. The paper matches the
wound.

## Concealed

The carton looks like a carton. The signature is clean. Two hours
or two days later, the island comes out with a cracked rail or a
finish scar that was hiding.

Now the carrier gets to wonder whether the living room did it. The
consignee has to show a wound that looks like transit, keep the
packaging, ask for inspection, and move on a short clock. See
[37-the-clocks-48-72-five-carmack.md](37-the-clocks-48-72-five-carmack.md).

A clean POD is not a legal execution. It is a hill. Origin photos
and prompt notice are how you climb.

## The gray

A carton with a soft corner and a “looks fine” signature. A crate
with a broken slat and no note. These are obvious problems that
people treat as concealed because they were tired. Teach: if the
skin is wounded, the contents may be wounded. Open or notate. Do
not be polite at the skin.

## Packaging creates concealment

A good carton is supposed to hide the piece from the network. It
also hides the piece from the driver. That is the deal. The cure
is not a worse carton. The cure is a fast, lighted open and a
habit of notating the skin.

Pad wrap conceals less. A scarred pad or a printed strap line is
often visible. That is one reason specialized exists.

## Shortage is not concealment

A missing piece is short, not concealed damage. Count at the truck.
A “we’ll find the bag of hardware in the carton later” is how
hardware becomes a new order.

## A pair of islands, same week

Island A: carton corner stove in, customer writes “crushed left
carton, top unknown,” photos the truck, opens, finds a mashed
corner that matches. That file is a freight claim with a spine.

Island B: carton pretty, clean sign, opened Sunday, rail cracked,
carton already in the alley, email to us on Wednesday that says
“shipping damaged it.” That file is a concealed-damage climb with
missing packaging and a late flare. We still help. We do not
pretend it is Island A.

Teach the pair. People remember stories.

## How BBF should talk

“If you can see it, write it before you sign. If you cannot see it,
open it as soon as you have daylight, keep the box, and call the
number we gave you the same day you find it.”

Two sports. Two instructions.
