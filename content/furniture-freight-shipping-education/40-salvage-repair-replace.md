# Salvage, repair, and when replacement is the honest path

Not every wound wants a new island. Not every wound will accept a
wipe with stain.

The claim’s job is to make the customer whole enough, at a cost
someone will actually pay. That someone may be the carrier, BBF,
a retailer, or a mix. Pretending it is always a remake is how
shops work for free.

## Repair

A corner, a scratch that is not through the story of the piece,
a foot, a ding a good finisher can lose. Repair is the grown-up
path when the bones are sound.

Get a written estimate from someone who has finished this
species before. A generic “furniture repair $200” on a custom
oil top is fiction.

If BBF repairs, we photograph before and after. We decide
whether the piece is still firsts or is now a conversation. We
do not silently downgrade a hospitality job.

## Replace

The crack runs a rail. The top is a map of impact. The case is
racked. The finish is a heat disaster we cannot stand behind.
The project date cannot eat a repair cycle.

Replacement is honest here. It is also two freights and a shop
slot. Price that into the demand. Do not replace and then forget
to claim the second ride.

If the customer wants a remake because they are angry and the
wound is a thumbnail, we can still be kind without being foolish.
Offer repair first. Put the offer in the file.

## Salvage

The carrier (or the insurer) may want the wrecked piece if they
pay. That is salvage. Do not throw the island away because it
hurts to look at. Do not let a household take it to the curb.
Ask, in the claim, who owns the carcass if they pay X.

If they pay a diminished-value number and leave the piece, say
so. If they pay replacement and want pickup, schedule it so it
does not sit in a dining room like a ghost.

A shop that destroys salvage before the clerk answers has
destroyed the reason they would write a check.

## Mitigation

The law-flavored word for: do not make it worse. Keep it dry.
Do not let a wet carton sit on a finish. Do not let a household
“try to sand it” unless we asked them to. A bad DIY is a new
claim against the living room.

## A number line we can say out loud

Repair $280 and keep the piece in the house.
Replace $1,100 plus two freights, salvage to the carrier if they
pay.
Diminished value $150 and a frank conversation that the corner
will always be a corner.

Write the three numbers before the customer writes their own
story. If we only offer replace, we train every scratch to become
a remake. If we only offer repair, we will try to glue a racked
case and call it character.

## Released value again

If the tariff will only pay a scrap, BBF still has a reputation
decision: remake at our cost, repair at our cost, or walk the
customer through a hard number. That decision is ownership, not
freight. Make it on purpose. See
[32-released-value-and-insurance.md](32-released-value-and-insurance.md).
