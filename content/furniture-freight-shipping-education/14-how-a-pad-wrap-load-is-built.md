# How a pad-wrap load is built

If you have never watched a furniture load get built, “blanket wrap” will
sound like a hug. It is a structure.

This is the picture BBF should have in its head so we can tell a good crew
from a fast one.

## Floor first

The trailer floor is clean enough that a pad will not pick up gravel. If
it is not, say so. A stone in a pad under an island foot is a dent with a
delay.

Air-ride helps. It does not excuse a dirty floor.

## The piece gets a skin

Clean pads, enough of them that wood does not see strap. Corners get extra,
or they get cardboard/foam corner guards *under* the pad so the strap does
not print a line through quilt. Glass and stone get their own rules — see
[16-corners-glass-stone-bad-travelers.md](16-corners-glass-stone-bad-travelers.md).

Wrap so the pad will not walk. A pad that slides is a rag. Stretch film
over a pad can keep it honest on a long ride; film on bare finish can mark
or sweat. Know which you are doing.

Hardware bags stay on the piece or in a marked pouch taped where a stranger
will look. A bag in a pad fold is how a hospitality job loses forty knobs.

## It gets a place, not a pile

The crew sets the piece against the wall, into logistics posts, or into a
decked slot so the next stop’s sofa cannot lean into your top. Decking is
the furniture van’s version of a second floor: something rides above
without sitting *on* you.

No-stack means no-stack. If you see a carton climb your island because
“it will ride,” you are watching a commodity load in a nicer truck.

## Straps are tools

Straps keep the structure from becoming a landslide. They are not a way to
make a loose piece become a smaller piece. Over-tightening prints. Under-
tightening lets the piece walk and bang the post.

A good driver tugs, waits, tugs again after the first miles in their head.
A bad driver ratchets until the wood complains.

If BBF is loading our own truck, same rule. Our ratchet is not kinder
because it has our name on the handle.

## Mix is a design problem

A day’s furniture van is a puzzle: tall case goods, upholstered, maybe a
mattress, maybe our island. Mix is fine when the crew builds lanes and
pads the kiss points. Mix is how you get the sofa problem when they do
not — [18-mixed-freight-the-sofa-problem.md](18-mixed-freight-the-sofa-problem.md).

Ask what else is on the truck if the finish is a one-off. You will not
always get a vote. You always get to decide whether this is the carrier
for the next one-off.

## Time

A real pad-wrap pickup is slower than an LTL “here’s a pallet.” If the
driver has six minutes and six pieces, they will skip the structure. Pay
for the time or do not book the mode.

Drop-trailer / leave-the-van is how some shops load without a driver
tapping a boot. If we ever have that luxury, we load to our standard and
photograph the completed wall before the tractor hooks.

## Photos that match the structure

Origin photos should include the wrapped piece *in the truck*, not only
the pretty island in the shop. A claim argument that “they didn’t pad it”
dies faster when you have the pad in the frame.
