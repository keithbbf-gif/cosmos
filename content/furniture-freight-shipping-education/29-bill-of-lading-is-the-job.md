# The bill of lading is the job

If it is not on the BOL, it is a rumor.

The bill of lading is the contract the carrier took when they took the
wood. It is also the script the destination driver was given. Pretty
emails do not ride on the truck.

## What has to be true

- Shipper name and origin that a stranger can find
- Consignee name and a deliverable address (unit, hours, phone)
- Piece count that matches the dock
- Weight you will stand on
- A description that is furniture, not “misc”
- Who pays (see [30-prepaid-collect-third-party.md](30-prepaid-collect-third-party.md))
- The accessorials you already know
- Any special instruction that is actually special (“call 30 min out,”
  “inside first floor,” “do not stack”) knowing some will be ignored
  and still writing them

If the marketplace printed the BOL, our job is to read it like it
might be wrong — because it might.

## Piece count is a moral number

Two cartons called one “set” will short at a terminal. One island
called two pieces will over. A top and a base that we think of as
one SKU are two handling units if they do not nest as one.

Count what a forklift would count.

## Signatures

The driver’s signature at origin is their acceptance of apparent
condition and count. Our signature (or our person’s) is that we
tendered that count. If we are short a shelf and everyone is in a
hurry, the BOL should say short a shelf. A clean BOL on a short
tender is a lie we will tell later to ourselves.

## A filled-in picture (not a form to photocopy)

Shipper: Bradley Brand Furniture, 501 Pennington St, Warren AR
71671, 870-226-9200.

Consignee: name, street, unit, city, ZIP, a phone that a human
answers.

Qty: 1 handling unit. Weight: 102 lb. Description: hardwood
kitchen island, mocha, 34x28x40 carton, NMFC/class if you have
them. Freight prepaid (or the term that is true).

Accessorials: residential, liftgate, notify.

Special: call 30 min out; do not stack.

That is a job. “Furniture / 1 / 100 / COD / ship ASAP” is a
future tracing call.

## The BOL is not a marketing page

Do not write a novel in special instructions. Drivers have a minute.
“Residential, liftgate, notify, inside 1st floor, apt 2B, gate 1234”
is a novel that earns its lines. A paragraph about the customer’s
taste does not.

Do not put a different ship-to in the email than on the BOL and
hope dispatch reads Gmail. The truck reads the BOL.

## After pickup

A copy in the order folder the same day. The PRO written on it. If
the carrier emails a billed copy that changed class or weight, that
copy is the next argument. Keep both.

## Claims live here

A claim without a BOL is a shrug. The cargo clerk will ask for it
first. If we cannot find it, we are asking them to believe a feeling.
