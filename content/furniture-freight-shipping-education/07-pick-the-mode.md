# Parcel, LTL, FTL, dedicated — pick with your hands

Mode is not a personality. It is a match between the piece, the address, and
the number of times you can stand to see a stranger’s forklift.

BBF has shipped boards in parcel, islands in LTL, and loads on a company
truck. The mistakes happen when the mode is chosen by habit (“we always
freight it”) or by a portal checkbox.

## Parcel

Use it when the handling unit is a carton the sortation network will
accept: weight, length, girth, and packaging that can take a chute. Cutting
boards, small accessories, replacement parts. If you have to ask “will they
take it?” the answer is probably freight.

Parcel wins on price and on the customer’s familiar tracking. Parcel loses
when a finished edge meets a conveyor. Do not “try parcel” on a piece you
cannot remake this month.

## LTL (less-than-truckload)

You are buying a slice of a trailer and a network of terminals. Good for
one island, a small set, a retailer dock that knows freight. You will share
space with freight that does not love your finish unless you crate or
carton like you mean it.

LTL wins when cube is modest, the consignee has a dock or will pay
accessorials honestly, and transit can be a range. LTL loses when the piece
is loose, high-gloss, and heading to a third-floor walk-up sold as
“shipping included.”

## FTL (full truckload)

You buy the whole trailer. One origin, one destination (or a planned
multi-stop). No terminal ballet if you booked it that way. Makes sense for
a hospitality floor, a store reset, a cluster of houses on one street, a
healthcare wing. Does not make sense for a single island unless the island
is a pretext and the real load is the rest of the truck.

FTL is not automatically gentle. A dry van with no pads and a driver in a
hurry is still a dry van. Specify air-ride, pads, no-stack, or a furniture
van if the finish needs it. The word “truckload” only means you bought the
box.

## Dedicated / exclusive use

Cousins of FTL: you pay to keep other people’s freight off your piece, or
to keep a team on your job. Exclusive use on LTL means they do not load
other freight on that trailer — you are buying air again. Dedicated can
mean a truck and driver on your calendar for a project.

Use it when the claim risk is more expensive than the empty cubes. A custom
finish for a hotel corridor is a candidate. A stock bread board is not.

## Specialized furniture / blanket wrap

A mode that looks like LTL or truckload on an invoice and behaves like a
moving company on the dock. Pads, straps, often air-ride, fewer forklift
kisses. Read Stage 3 before you say yes because you liked the brochure.

## Company truck

## A way to choose without a meeting

Ask four questions in order:

1. Will a parcel network accept this carton without a fight? If yes, parcel.
2. Is this a project’s worth of cube going to one site or a tight cluster?
   If yes, price FTL / dedicated / company truck against a pile of LTL.
3. Is the finish or the geometry too honest for a terminal? If yes,
   specialized or crate-for-LTL. Do not do “loose LTL and hope.”
4. If none of those, LTL with the accessorials named and the carton built
   for forklifts.

If two answers compete, pick the one that protects the finish. You can
explain a higher freight number. You cannot un-scar a top.
