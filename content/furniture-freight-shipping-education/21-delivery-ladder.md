# The delivery ladder

“They’ll deliver it” is not a rung. It is a fog.

If BBF cannot point to a rung, we sold a feeling. Feelings do not carry
islands.

## Curb / tailgate / “to the truck”

The freight comes off the vehicle (sometimes only to the tailgate). The
consignee owns the rest: the walk, the stairs, the door, the unpack. This
is the cheapest honest last mile. It is also the one households hear as
an insult if they thought they bought a move.

Use it for docks, for handy customers, for pieces that a two-person
household can actually live with. Do not use it for a 36-inch island and
a customer who told you they live alone.

## Liftgate (not a rung — a machine)

A hydraulic plate from trailer height to ground. It can serve curb or
threshold. It does not climb a stoop. See
[22-liftgate-is-a-machine.md](22-liftgate-is-a-machine.md). Put it on the
ladder conversation because customers mash it together with “inside.”

## Threshold / first dry place

Across the first door that keeps rain off, or into a garage, or into a
lobby. No stairs unless the paper says stairs. No unpack. No room tour.

This is the honest “they brought it in” for a lot of freight programs.
It is not white glove. If the first dry place is a garage with a step
the driver will not do, the paper will say garage.

## Inside delivery

A paid walk past the threshold, often still first floor, often still
wrapped. Some carriers treat inside and threshold as cousins. Some treat
inside as a long carry with a fee that wakes up after the fact. Ask how
many feet. Ask how many people. Ask about stairs.

## Room of choice

To the room named on the order. Still often wrapped. Still often no
assembly. Stairs may be one flight, or extra, or refused. Elevators may
be required and may be too small. Measure the piece against the house
before you sell the room.

Room of choice is where a lot of “white glove” quotes quietly live. If
you do not also buy unpack and debris, you sold a placement, not a
reveal.

## White glove

The top rung people steal for marketing. In a furniture program that
means what it says, white glove is:

- to the named room
- unpack
- light assembly if the paper says so
- packaging hauled away if the paper says so
- usually two people
- usually an appointment

If any of those is missing, say which. “White glove minus assembly” is
allowed if you write it. “White glove” as a vibe is how a one-driver
LTL attempt becomes a one-star story.

Marketplace BOLs sometimes check a white-glove-shaped pile of
accessorials (inside, stairs, notify) without sending a two-person crew
with a tool bag. Believe the crew, not the poetry.

## A kitchen that was sold as white glove and booked as liftgate

The customer planned a Saturday reveal. The truck had a gate. The
island sat on the driveway. The driver was one person. The stairs
were four, plus a turn. The email from sales said “white glove.”
The BOL said residential + liftgate.

Everyone in that story can be telling the truth about *their*
paper. The customer is still the one with an island in the weather.
BBF’s job is to make the three papers match before pickup, not to
apologize in a more sincere font after.

If we discover the mismatch after the PRO exists, we call the
carrier and try to upgrade while the freight is still movable. We
do not wait for the driveway photo.

## How BBF should sell the ladder

Pick the rung on the quote. Repeat the rung on the confirmation. Repeat
the rung when the PRO goes out. If the customer upgrades, upgrade the
booking, not only the email.

If we do not know the house, we do not know the rung. Ask: dock or
house? stairs? elevator? will someone be there who can take a 100-pound
carton from a tailgate? The last question saves more marriages than it
offends.
