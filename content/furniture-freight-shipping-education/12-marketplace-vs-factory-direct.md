# Marketplace routing vs factory-direct freight

BBF has lived both lives: a factory that books its own truck, and a vendor
that prints a portal’s bill of lading and waves at whoever shows up.

They look similar on the dock. They are not similar in the file.

## Factory-direct

We pick the mode. We pick (or at least see) the accessorials. We send the
customer a sentence we can defend. When it breaks, the claim conversation
runs through people who have our phone number.

The risk is our quoting. If we under-describe the house, we eat the
accessorial. If we over-promise white glove, we eat the relationship.

## Marketplace / retailer routing

The retailer already sold “delivery.” Their system prints a BOL that may
check residential, liftgate, inside, stairs, notify — sometimes all of
them, sometimes the wrong ones. A carrier the portal loves arrives. The
PRO lives in their world. The customer calls the retailer. The retailer
opens a case. The case becomes a deduction.

Old BBF paper already knew this shape: Wayfair BOLs that told the driver
the house needed a liftgate and a flight; Overstock labels that wanted
small parcel on a piece that did not want a chute; partner SKU sheets
that argued with the dock.

You do not win by arguing with a driver about a portal’s childhood. You
win by shipping what the BOL says you shipped, photographing it, and
keeping the portal’s own paperwork.

## What the dock must still do

Portal freight is not a trance. Count the pieces. Match the SKU. Do not
let a driver take two islands on a BOL that says one. Do not let a BOL
that says “1 of 1” cover a top and a base you consider two pieces unless
the program says they nest.

If the BOL is missing a liftgate and the ship-to is a house, you will not
fix the retailer’s rating engine at 6 a.m. You *can* note it, photograph
the BOL, and tell the office so the first angry email is not a surprise.

## Who the customer thinks you are

On factory-direct, you are the shipper they chose. On marketplace, you are
a brand name inside a larger machine. They will still email the factory
when the porch goes wrong, because the carton says Bradley. Have a script:

“This order moved on the retailer’s freight program. We will send the
origin photos and the BOL we shipped against. The claim and the delivery
promise sit with the program. We will not disappear.”

“We will not disappear” is the human part. “The claim sits with the
program” is the true part. Both are required. One alone is either cold or
a promise we cannot cash.

## The driver who works for a logo we did not pick

On factory-direct we can say no to a truck that looks like a rolling
garage. On marketplace we often cannot. We can still refuse to load
the *wrong piece* or a piece that is not finished. We can still
photograph. We can still call the portal if the BOL is a different
SKU.

What we cannot do is reinvent their last mile on our dock and then
be shocked when their last mile arrives.

## Chargebacks are the afterlife

A marketplace can take the money after you thought you were paid. Damaged
on arrival, late, missing hardware, a customer who kept the piece and
still opened a case. The educational point is not bitterness. It is
file-keeping: origin photos, carton standards, ship-scan dates, and a
refusal to ship on a BOL you know is the wrong piece.

See [41-retailer-chargebacks.md](41-retailer-chargebacks.md).
