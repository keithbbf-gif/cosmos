# Labels, marks, and the piece that arrives at the wrong house

Freight does not read our minds. It reads a label, a mark, and a piece
count.

When those three disagree, an island goes to the wrong porch, or to a
terminal cage, or into someone else’s living room where it becomes “ours
now” because it looks expensive.

## One name, many numbers

A shipment can wear:

- BBF order number
- customer PO
- retailer/marketplace id
- PRO (after pickup)
- a stencil from 2013 that still says a SKU we retired

Pick a master mark and put it on two faces of the handling unit. The
BOL description should use the same words a stranger would use at 5 a.m.
“Island, mocha, 1 pc, order 4xxx” beats “furniture” and beats a poem.

If the piece is 1 of 2, say 1 of 2 on both pieces. Sets that travel
apart are how bases and tops divorce.

## Labels that fall off

A label is not a finish. Rain, stretch film, and a forklift rub will
undress it. Use a pouch or a second label under film. Write the mark on
the carton with a pen that does not smear. For pad wrap, a hangtag that
can survive a strap, not a sticky note on a quilt.

Old BBF shipping labels already knew this life: shipper block, ship-to
block, weight, dims, a barcode someone else assigned. The barcode is
useless if it is on the piece that stayed in Warren.

## The wrong house

Misdelivery is an OS&D story with a doorbell. The POD may show a
signature that is not your customer. The photo may show a porch you do
not recognize.

Call the same day. Ask for a redelivery or a pickup from the wrong
address. Do not wait for the customer to “see if it shows up.” While you
wait, the wrong house is living with your island.

If the driver delivered to “the house with the dog” because the number
was worn off the mailbox, that is still the carrier’s stop, and it is
also why we print addresses like we mean them. Apartment, unit, gate
code, “use the alley” — if it is not on the BOL, it does not exist.

## Marks that lie

A reused carton with a previous ship-to still peeking through. A
marketplace label on a factory-direct piece. A “copy 2 of 2” from a
reprint that the driver thinks is a second piece.

Cover old marks. One visible address.

## Two islands, one Friday

A mocha and a black, both “island,” both 1 of 1, both leaving the
same door. If the marks are sloppy, the mocha goes to the black’s
house. Both customers will be sure we shipped the wrong finish.
One of them is right. The file is a photo of the label on each
carton *and* a photo of each finished face.

Color words on the carton help humans. SKU numbers help portals.
Use both. Do not trust “they’ll know it when they see it.” A
terminal will not see it.

## Shorts that are really label problems

A terminal has your island and cannot match it to a PRO because the
label died. It becomes overfreight in a cage. Your tracing call says
short. The piece is sitting under someone else’s fluorescent lights,
waiting for a human to care.

This is why the description on the BOL should be unique enough that a
clerk can walk a cage and say “that’s the Warren island.”
