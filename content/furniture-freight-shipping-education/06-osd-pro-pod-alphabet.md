# OS&D, PRO, POD — the alphabet you actually need

Freight loves initials. Most of them are noise. Four of them will save a
week: PRO, POD, OS&D, and BOL (which is three letters and the whole job).

Learn these so you can talk to a tracing clerk without sounding like you
shipped a feeling.

## PRO

The PRO is the carrier’s tracking number for that shipment. It is not the
customer’s PO. It is not BBF’s invoice. It is not the marketplace order id.
When a clerk asks “what’s the PRO?” and you read them a Wayfair number, you
have started a new call.

Get the PRO the day of pickup. Write it on the order, the customer email,
and the photo folder. If pickup happened and nobody has a PRO by the next
morning, call. Freight that is “out there somewhere” is how islands become
ghosts.

Different carriers format PROs differently. Some look like a long integer.
Some look like a barcode under another name. The habit is the same: one
external id that the trucking company will recognize at 7 a.m.

## BOL

Bill of lading. The contract the dock signed. Piece count, description,
weight, who pays, special instructions. The PRO is born from this paper
(or this EDI). If the BOL is wrong, the PRO is a well-tracked mistake.

Never tell a customer “I’ll send you the tracking” if you have not seen the
BOL that matches the piece that left. Two islands, one sloppy BOL, one PRO
— that is how the wrong living room gets a Caney and the right living room
gets a story.

## POD

Proof of delivery. The carrier’s record that someone signed, when, and
sometimes a photo of a porch. POD is not a quality inspection. It is a
time stamp plus a name.

Ask for the POD the day the customer says it did not come, and the day they
say it came broken. The first use is tracing. The second use is the
signature and the exception line. A POD that says “clear” is the mountain
a concealed-damage claim has to climb.

If the driver used a phone, there may be a geo-stamp and a blurry picture
of a carton. Keep it. A blurry picture of a crushed corner is still a
picture of a crushed corner.

## OS&D

Over, short, and damage. The dock’s exception language.

- **Over:** more pieces showed up than the BOL. Do not celebrate. It may be
  someone else’s freight riding your PRO, or a split that will go missing
  later.
- **Short:** fewer pieces than the BOL. Start the file the same day. A
  “they’ll find it” that lasts a week is how shorts become denials.
- **Damage:** visible harm at a terminal or at delivery. Photos, piece
  count, and a request for inspection if the rules you booked under want
  one.

OS&D is not a vibe. It is a report. If a terminal marks OS&D and nobody at
BBF hears it, we will learn on the porch.

## The letters you can ignore until you need them

SCAC (the carrier’s four-letter code). NMFC (the classification book).
EDI (the machines talking). FAK (freight-all-kinds, a contract cheat that
BBF probably does not have). You will meet them. Do not lead with them.

## How to talk to a tracing desk

Have this in front of you before you dial:

1. PRO
2. Pickup date and origin ZIP (71671)
3. Destination ZIP
4. Piece count and weight
5. Shipper name as it appears on the BOL
6. What you want: location, POD, OS&D notes, appointment

Do not start with the story of the customer’s anniversary. The clerk cannot
rate a marriage. They can rate a PRO.
