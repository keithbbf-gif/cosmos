# Freight is not parcel

A cutting board can ride in a brown box. A kitchen island cannot, not honestly.

That is the whole lesson, and BBF has paid for it every time a customer heard
“shipping” and pictured a driver in shorts with a handheld and a smile. Parcel
is a network built to throw identical cartons through machines. Freight is a
network built to move space. Furniture lives in the second network even when
the piece only weighs as much as a stubborn dog.

## Two networks, two promises

Parcel (UPS Ground, FedEx Ground, USPS, the marketplace “small parcel”
checkbox) assumes:

- a carton a person can lift
- a label the sortation belt can read
- a delivery that can be attempted and left, often without a signature
- a claim process that thinks in ounces and retail SKUs

Freight assumes:

- a handling unit that may need a dock, a pallet jack, or a liftgate
- a bill of lading instead of a 4×6 label as the contract
- a driver who is paid to move freight, not to assemble your life
- a claim process that starts with the delivery receipt you signed at the truck

When BBF puts an island on LTL because the cube and the finish demand it, and
then the website copy still sounds like a parcel promise, the customer is not
wrong to be angry. We taught them the wrong animal.

## What the customer thinks they bought

They think they bought a date, a tracking number that updates like Amazon, and
a person who will bring the piece through the door. What they often bought is:

- a pickup window at origin, not a day-certain on their porch
- a PRO number that goes quiet in a terminal
- a truck that may be 53 feet long on a street that is not
- a delivery that ends at the curb unless somebody paid for more

None of that is a scam. It is the product called freight. The scam is using
parcel language on a freight job.

## The weight lie

People weigh a piece and say, “It’s only a hundred pounds.” The island on the
old Wayfair-style worksheets sat around that number. Parcel still may refuse
it because of length, girth, or “not a carton.” Freight still may charge it
like a small refrigerator because of class and cube. Weight is one input.
Shape is another. Finish is a third. Who has a dock is a fourth.

A bread board is a parcel citizen. A 36-inch island with a finished top is
not, even if a clerk once typed “UPS SMALL PARCEL” on a partner sheet because
a portal wanted a box. If the piece cannot survive a chute, it is freight.
Argue with the portal after you protect the wood.

## Hands

Parcel is touched by belts, trucks, and a last driver. Freight LTL is touched
by origin dock, linehaul, one or more terminals, destination city driver, and
sometimes a third-party final-mile van. Every extra hand is a chance to put
a strap mark on a corner. That is why specialized furniture trucks exist, and
why “we shipped it” is not an answer when the finish is bruised.

If you need one picture: parcel is a letter. Freight is a sofa. You can mail
a letter. You can move a sofa. You cannot mail a sofa and then be shocked
that the mailbox chewed it.

## What BBF should say out loud

Say “freight” when it is freight. Say “the carrier will call to set a
delivery” when that is the truth. Say “to the truck” or “to the threshold”
or “into the room” — pick one, write it, sell only that. If the customer
needs parcel comfort, they need a smaller piece or a local pickup.

Do not say “it ships like everything else.” Everything else is not an island
leaving Warren.

email on each. If the freight email could be swapped onto the parcel order
without sounding wrong, the freight email is a lie. Rewrite it before the
truck is booked.
