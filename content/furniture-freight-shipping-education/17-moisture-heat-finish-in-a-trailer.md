# Moisture, heat, and finish in a trailer

A trailer is weather with walls.

In August it is an oven. In January it is a barn. In a rain week it is a
sweaty glass. Hardwood that left Warren in shop equilibrium will meet a
climate that did not sign our finishing schedule.

This is not an excuse for a cracked top. It is a reason we pack and
promise like woodworkers, not like people who ship plastic totes.

## Heat

Dark finishes and dark trailers. A carton in the sun on a dock. A van
that sat. Film on a finish that wanted to breathe. Softening, imprint from
a pad, a blush in some film finishes, a sticky oil that then grabs dust.

If we ship a freshly oiled piece, we are shipping a wet rag that looks
like furniture. Give oil time, or say the first week in the house is part
of the finish, not a defect. Do not let a customer rub a hot, soft top
with a moving pad and then call it a carrier scratch — sometimes it is,
sometimes it is us.

## Cold

Glue and some finishes get brittle. A drop that would have been a ding in
June is a chip in January. Drivers still have to work. Receivers still
open cartons in a driveway. Tell northern deliveries to let a piece sit
in the house before they judge a finish or run a cloth over it. Cold
wood plus household heat is a movement story. Photograph, then wait a
day before anyone sands a “defect” that is still waking up.

## Sweat and rain

A wet carton is a claim and a mold story. Plastic on the outside can
save a rain crossing and make a greenhouse. If you bag, vent or use
methods the shop has already tested. A soaked blanket on an oil finish
is a woodworker’s horror movie.

Terminal floors are damp. Pallet the carton. Do not store finished goods
on a wet trailer floor “for an hour.”

## What is a freight problem vs a wood problem

Freight: a pad printed through heat; a wet carton; a scrape with a
direction and a corresponding carton crush.

Wood: a hairline at a glue joint that opened after a week of AC; a
seasonal check in a wide top; a finish blush that matches a known
product issue.

The porch does not care about our categories. The file must. If we treat
every movement as a carrier crime, we will lose the claims that *are*
carrier crimes. If we treat every movement as “wood does that,” we will
train customers to think we do not stand behind a truck.

## What BBF can actually control

- Do not ship unstable moisture. If the shop knows a top is still wet,
  it is not ready, even if the portal date is lonely.
- Shade and time on the dock. A black carton in Arkansas sun is a choice.
- Clean, dry pads.
- A sentence in the customer packet: let it sit, inspect in daylight,
  do not park it in a garage that behaves like a trailer.

We cannot climate-control every LTL trailer. We can stop pretending
trailers are showrooms.
