# Quoting without a fantasy transit

A date certain is a product. A smile is not.

Furniture freight is a chain of rooms. Rooms have weather, cube,
and appointments. If we quote a single Thursday because the
estimator’s default said 3, we are writing fan fiction.

## Start the clock at the right nail

Transit begins at pickup, not at “we entered the order,” not at
“the finish is almost dry,” not at “the customer paid.” If
production will take ten days, say ten days *plus* freight.

Marketplace ship-by dates are portal religion. They are not
physics. If the portal wants Tuesday and the top needs Friday, the
honest fight is with the portal before we tender a wet island.

## Ranges

“Five to eight working days after pickup to the destination
terminal, then an appointment.” That sentence is ugly and usable.

“You’ll have it Friday.” That sentence is pretty and expensive.

Add days for rural, for interline, for weather season, for the
week of a holiday the terminals still pretend to staff.

## Guaranteed products

Some carriers sell a guarantee. You pay. They pay a fee if they
miss — often a freight refund, not a hotel bill, not a wedding.
If we did not buy it, we do not say guaranteed. If we did buy it,
we still do not promise the customer’s whole project, only what
the guarantee pays.

## The appointment tail

A piece can be “there” and not deliverable. Destination terminal
Friday, first appointment Tuesday, nobody home, redelivery
Thursday. The customer experienced two weeks. The linehaul was
three days. Both facts can be true. Quote the experience, or at
least name the tail.

## A worked lane (illustration, not a tariff)

Say an island is ready Wednesday. Pickup Thursday. Commodity LTL
from 71671 toward a house outside Boston.

A rating engine may print “4.” That 4 is working days to a
*terminal*, in a week with no weather and no interline. The
household hears “next Wednesday on my porch.”

A sentence that matches the life of the shipment:

“Ready Wed. Pickup Thu–Fri. Five to eight working days to the
area, then they call to set a delivery window. First porch date
is often the following week, not the fourth morning. This is not
a guaranteed service.”

If that sentence loses the sale, the sale wanted parcel. Do not
win it with a Thursday.

Holiday weeks: add days before you are asked. A terminal that is
“open” on a Friday before a Monday holiday is still a terminal
that already sent the city drivers home.

## How BBF should write it

1. Ready date (our dock)
2. Pickup window
3. Transit range to terminal / area
4. Appointment / notify language
5. “Not a guaranteed service unless named”

If a designer needs a hard install morning, we need a mode that
can buy that morning (dedicated, company truck, a final-mile crew
with a calendar) or we need to say no.

Write the same five lines on marketplace jobs even when the
portal owns the truck. The customer will still ask us. “The
retailer owns transit; their site will show the PRO; we do not
control the appointment” is a transit quote. “Should be there
soon” is not.
