# Refused delivery, reconsignment, and storage

Sometimes the right move on a porch is to say no.

A refusal feels like a scene. It is also a document. If we teach
customers only how to be polite, we will teach them to sign clean and
cry later.

## Refusal

The consignee will not take the freight: crushed carton, wet, wrong
piece, late enough that the project is dead, a COD they will not pay.
They should write the reason on the delivery receipt and keep photos.

A refusal is not a tantrum if the piece is not the piece. It is a
tantrum if the carton is perfect and they just changed their mind —
that becomes a return, a reconsignment, and a bill.

BBF should coach: if the harm is obvious and ugly, refuse or accept
with a hard exception. If the harm is a maybe, accept with a note and
open it immediately. A blanket “never refuse, it makes them mad” is
how we eat wrecked tops. A blanket “always refuse” is how we pay
storage on a scratch that could have been a repair.

## Reconsignment

Change the ship-to after the freight is already moving. New address,
new appointment, sometimes a new carrier. It is a paid rewrite of the
job. It is not a favor.

If a designer “just wants it sent to the job site instead of the
warehouse,” that is reconsignment. Do it on purpose. Do not do it as
a text to a driver.

## Storage

Freight that cannot deliver sits. Terminals charge. Specialized
warehouses charge. After a clock, the carrier may consider it
abandoned and start a path you will not like.

Nobody home, a site not ready, a refusal without a next instruction —
these are how storage is born. The instruction “hold until I call”
must include who pays the hold.

## Return to origin

The piece comes home. Freight both ways, plus whatever the ride did
the second time. Sometimes this is the honest end of a wrong order.
Sometimes it is a failure of notify. Price it before you promise
“we’ll just bring it back.”

## A porch decision, said in order

1. Is it the right piece and the right count? If no, refuse or
   accept the right pieces only and short the rest on paper.
2. Is the skin wrecked (crush, wet, puncture, torn pad, raw
   corner)? If yes, photograph, write the exception, then choose:
   refuse if it is not usable as furniture; accept-with-note if a
   repair will do and you want it in the house tonight.
3. Is the skin fine? Sign, then open in daylight the same day.
   Do not refuse a perfect carton because you are angry at the
   wait. That anger is a return, not a damage claim.
4. After any refusal: tell BBF and the carrier *where the freight
   should go next* (hold, return, reconsign). A refusal with no
   next instruction is how storage is born.

## Project sites

Healthcare and hospitality love a receiving window that closes. If
the wing is not ready, the truck becomes storage in a city you do
not live in. Book the receiving, not only the ship date. A
“ship when ready” on a wing that is still drywall is a storage
purchase we forgot to price.
