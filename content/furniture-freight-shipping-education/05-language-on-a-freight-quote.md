# The language on a freight quote

A freight quote is a sentence with prices attached. If the sentence is
sloppy, the price is a teaser.

BBF has seen the teaser version: a number that looks like “shipping,” a
customer who hears “to my kitchen,” and a second invoice that names
liftgate, residential, limited access, and a redelivery because nobody was
home. The second invoice is not a surprise to the carrier. It is a surprise
to the person we taught badly.

## Words that change the job

**Shipping / delivery / freight.** Not synonyms. Freight is the linehaul.
Delivery is the last act. Shipping is the fog people hide in. Use the word
for the work.

**Dock to dock.** Two businesses with forklifts. No liftgate. No porch. If
the consignee is a house, this word is a lie.

**Residential.** A house or apartment. Different truck, different attempt
rules, different fee. Saying “we can ship to your home” without this word
is how the fee arrives as a betrayal.

**Liftgate.** A machine on the back of a truck. Not a helper. Not inside.

**Inside / threshold / room of choice / white glove.** A ladder, not a mood.
See [21-delivery-ladder.md](21-delivery-ladder.md). If the quote says
“delivery” and the salesperson said “they’ll bring it in,” the quote and
the mouth are in a fight. The quote will win in accounting.

**Notify / appointment.** Someone will call. The freight will wait if no one
answers. That wait can become storage. Write the phone number that a driver
can actually use.

**Estimated / proforma / prepaid and add.** The number will move when the
scale, the class, or the accessorials move. If you sell an estimate as a
cap, you just wrote a guarantee the carrier did not sign.

**Guaranteed.** A product you buy. Not a feeling about Thursday. If we did
not buy a guaranteed service, do not say guarantee.

**Curbside.** The truck’s neighborhood, not the dining room.

## The quiet adjectives

“Standard,” “regular,” “normal,” “basic.” These words mean *whatever this
carrier does when you do not pay extra*. On one carrier that is dock only.
On a furniture van it may include a pad and a helper. Do not import a
furniture-van “standard” onto an LTL quote.

“White glove” on a marketplace BOL is sometimes a checklist (inside, stairs,
notify) and sometimes a two-person crew with assembly. If you did not read
the program’s definition, you do not have white glove. You have a phrase.

## Numbers that are not the quote

Transit days are not the quote. They are a weather report. Put them in a
range and name the start: from pickup, not from order; working days, not
calendar wishes; destination appointment extra.

Fuel is not the quote. It is a surcharge that moves.

Reweigh and reclass are not the quote. They are what happens when our
worksheet was optimistic.

## A quote BBF can stand on

Write five lines, even on a short email:

1. Mode (LTL / specialized blanket wrap / company truck / parcel).
2. Where the job ends (dock / curb / threshold / room / white glove as
   defined).
3. Accessorials already in the number, and the ones that will add if the
   site is a house, a flight, a long carry.
4. Transit as a range from pickup.
5. The sentence: this is an estimate until the carrier rates the actual
   pieces, weight, and services performed.

If a customer needs a hard cap, get a hard quote from the carrier for that
address and those services, then add the margin for reweigh. Do not cap a
fog.
