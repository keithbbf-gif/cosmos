# Furniture specialized vs general commodity LTL

There are carriers who will take your island because they take everything,
and there are carriers who take your island because that is the work.

Both will send a truck. They are not the same product.

## General commodity LTL

This is the national and regional networks people mean when they say
“freight”: terminals, city units, linehaul sleepers, a rating engine, a
claims department that sees more auto parts than tabletops.

What they are good at:

- density of coverage
- a PRO you can trace
- a price that looks like a price
- docks that already know how to receive a pallet

What they are not built for:

- a loose finished piece
- a driver-helper pair in a living room
- a promise that nothing will be stacked on your carton
- a culture that treats a corner as sacred

You can still use them. Carton or crate like the piece will be forklifted,
set down, and forklifted again. Buy the accessorials that match the house.
Tell the truth about the last mile. Do not ask a commodity network to
pretend it is a moving company.

## Specialized / furniture / blanket-wrap

These outfits run padded equipment, logistics posts, decking, straps, and
drivers who have moved case goods before. Some are household-goods cousins.
Some are final-mile furniture vans. Some are project carriers for hotels
and healthcare.

What they are good at:

- unboxed or lightly wrapped product
- no-stack space
- fewer terminal handoffs
- a delivery that can look like a move

What they are not:

- cheap
- everywhere tomorrow
- magically white glove unless you bought white glove
- immune to a bad strap or a wet trailer

“Blanket wrap” on a commodity LTL tender is often a note a dock will ignore.
Blanket wrap as a *carrier type* is a van that showed up with pads. Do not
confuse the note with the van.

## How the docks feel different

On commodity LTL, your island is a handling unit in a wall of handling
units. Speed is the virtue. A pallet jack does not ask about sheen.

On a furniture van, your island is a piece. The driver (or the two-person
crew) wraps, sets, straps, and thinks about the next stop’s sofa rubbing
your edge. That thought is what you are buying.

If BBF’s finish is oil and honest wood, a strap over a thin pad will still
leave a memory. Specialized is not a force field. It is a better set of
habits.

## Price is a poor narrator

A specialized quote that is twice LTL can still be cheaper than one remake
and one angry designer. A specialized quote that is twice LTL and then
delivers curb-only is a waste. Compare *end of job*, not *linehaul*.

Ask:

- How many times will this piece be unloaded before the porch?
- Will anything sit on it?
- Is the truck air-ride?
- Is delivery dock, threshold, or room?
- Who pads it — us, or the crew?

If the specialized carrier cannot answer those, they are a commodity
carrier with nicer marketing.

## When BBF should still use commodity LTL

- The piece is boxed for forklifts and the consignee is a retailer dock.
- The dollar risk is small and the carton is honest.
- The specialized carrier cannot hit the calendar and the customer prefers
  a later safe dock to a romantic van that does not exist this week.

When BBF should not: high-gloss, glass, stone, antiques, a one-off finish,
or any piece whose remake would wreck a project date.
