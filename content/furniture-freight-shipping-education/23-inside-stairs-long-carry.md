# Inside delivery, stairs, and the long carry

The driveway is not the room. The fee for the distance between them is
where furniture freight gets a reputation for bait.

BBF should know the distance before we smile on the phone.

## Inside

Paid movement past the first door. Sometimes a few feet. Sometimes a
path the driver did not agree to in the terminal. Carriers write limits:
first room, first floor, fifty feet, two people. If we do not have the
limit, we have a story.

Inside does not mean they will unpack. It does not mean they will wait
while someone moves a sofa. If the path is blocked, the job becomes
threshold plus an argument.

## Stairs

A flight is a different job than a hallway. Two flights is a different
job than one. A spiral is a refusal waiting to happen. Outdoor stoops
count even when the customer says “it’s only three steps.”

Marketplace BOLs that checked “Additional Flight of Stair” were trying
to buy the extra work in advance. If the house has the flight and the
BOL does not, the driver may stop at the bottom, or the invoice may
grow, or both.

Ask: how many steps from truck to the room we sold? If the customer
does not know, they should walk it with a tape before we book room of
choice.

## Long carry

The truck cannot sit at the door. A city street, a gate, a HOA, a mud
season lane, a building that makes you use the loading zone a block
away. Some tariffs start charging after 50 or 100 feet. Some drivers
just say no.

Rural Arkansas and rural anywhere else share this: the pin on the map
is not the walk. If BBF ships to a place we would not take a 28-foot
truck, say so up front. Company truck or a smaller final-mile van may
be the real mode.

## Safety is not a customer-service failure

A driver can refuse a carry that needs two people when they are one
person. They can refuse a weight that their tariff or their back will
not do. They can refuse ice. We can be angry about the booking (we
under-described the house) without being angry at a person who does not
want to become a claim.

If we sold a one-driver LTL inside on a 100-pound island up a walk-up,
we sold a cartoon.

## Measure like the piece has to turn

An island carton that is 40 inches in one dimension will not love
a 32-inch stair turn. Room of choice is not only labor. It is
geometry. If we have the dims, we can ask the customer to measure
the tightest door and the tightest turn. If we skip that, we are
selling a carry that becomes a curb when the landing is too small.

Hospitality corridors and healthcare soiled-utility routes have the
same problem with nicer names. Ask the installer for the path, not
the lobby photo.

## What to put on the order

- Flights (and whether they are inside or stoop)
- Elevator? size?
- Distance from legal parking to door, if it is not obvious
- Two-person required? (if the piece or the path says so)
- Photos of the approach if the job is picky
- Tightest door / turn if the piece is honest about its size

This is not extra. This is the job.
