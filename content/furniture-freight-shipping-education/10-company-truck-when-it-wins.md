# Company truck — when BBF wheels are the right tool

There is a reason the old order forms said “Ship Via: Company Truck” like
it was a carrier. For a radius we can actually serve, it is the only mode
where the person who built the edge might be the person who sets it down.

That is power. It is also how a shop becomes an unpaid moving company.

## What company truck is

Our vehicle, our time, our pads if we brought them, our conversation if
something is tight in a stair. No PRO unless we invent one for ourselves.
No Carmack filing against a stranger — if we break it, we own it in the
ordinary way a maker owns a drop.

The customer experiences it as “they brought it.” That sentence is worth
money. Do not spend it on a job that ruins Tuesday’s production.

## When it wins

- The drive is a day, not a tour.
- The piece is awkward for LTL (finish, glass, a top that should not be
  boxed into a terminal) and the dollar risk is real.
- The site is a store, a house we have been to, a healthcare floor that
  will not accept a 53-foot surprise.
- We need the relationship more than we need the trailer cube.
- We are already going that way with a load that fills the bed honestly.

Company truck also wins when the alternative is customer-own pickup by a
friend with a pickup and a blanket from a guest room. That friend is a
claim with a first name.

## When it loses

- We are covering for a freight quote we were afraid to give.
- The “quick run” eats a finisher’s day.
- There is no helper and the piece needs two people. Heroes get hernias
  and the shop gets a workers’ comp story.
- We have no photos, no release, and a customer who will remember a nick
  we did not make.
- The radius creeps. Warren to the next town is a truck. Warren to a
  coast is a fantasy that will be sold as loyalty.

If the honest LTL or specialized number is ugly, show the ugly number.
Do not donate a truck because the silence felt kind.

## How to run it like a mode, not a favor

Write a mini-BOL anyway: pieces, time out, time back, who rode, fuel,
and where it was placed. Photograph on the truck and on the floor.
Get a signature that says where the job ended (garage, kitchen, dock).
If you assembled, write that you assembled. If you did not, write that
you did not.

Charge it. A line called “company delivery” that is zero trains everyone
to ask for it. A line that is real lets you say no.

## A radius card

Write the towns we will run without a meeting. Write the towns that
need a meeting. Write the states that are freight, period. Put the
card on the wall so a kind yes at 4:45 p.m. has to walk past it.

If the card keeps growing, we are a carrier now. Price it like one
or stop growing it.

## Insurance and the quiet risk

A common carrier has a cargo product, however stingy. A shop truck has
whatever is on the business policy. Ask, once, out loud, whether cargo
on our wheels is actually covered for a finished piece going to a
customer’s house. If nobody knows, that is not a small unknown.

## Monday

List every company-truck run in the last quarter. Mark each as *should
have been freight*, *right call*, or *favor*. If favors win, the mode is
sick. Then read [26-what-the-driver-may-do.md](26-what-the-driver-may-do.md)
and apply it to our own people too — our driver is still not a two-person
white-glove crew unless we sent two people.
