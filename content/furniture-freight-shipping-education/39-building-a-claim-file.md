# Building a claim file a cargo clerk cannot shrug off

A clerk is not paid to imagine your island. They are paid to match
a checklist. Give them a checklist that is full.

This is the file BBF should be able to assemble even when we are
only helping a customer who owns the claim.

## The spine

1. **Demand letter** — PRO, origin, destination, pickup and
   delivery dates, piece, a sentence that the carrier is liable
   for the loss, and a **specific dollar amount**.
2. **BOL**
3. **Delivery receipt / POD** (the exception line, or the clean
   one you have to climb)
4. **Invoice / value proof** — what the piece sold for, or the
   remake cost, depending on who is claiming
5. **Origin photos**
6. **Destination photos** (piece, wound, packaging, label)
7. **Inspection request** and any inspection report
8. **Repair estimate** or remake quote if that is the demand
9. **Freight bill** (sometimes they want to see they were paid)

Part 370 language is picky about identifying the shipment,
asserting liability, and claiming a specified amount. Skip the
dollar and you have a complaint, not a claim.

## The dollar

Pick one theory and show the math:

- repair (labor + materials + refinish)
- replacement (piece + inbound freight + outbound freight you
  will eat)
- depreciated value if that is the honest remaining use (rare
  on new case goods, common on arguments)

Do not demand a number you cannot support. Do not demand a number
that ignores salvage if they will ask. See
[40-salvage-repair-replace.md](40-salvage-repair-replace.md).

Remember released value. The demand can be the real loss. The
check may be the tariff. Both belong in our own notes so we do
not promise the demand.

## Timeline in the letter

A short chronology: tendered sound (photo date), delivered
(POD date), exception (words), discovered (if concealed),
notice sent (date), inspection (date). Clerks like time in a
line. They do not like a memoir.

## What not to send

- A rant about the industry
- Twenty identical photos
- A threat of a review on page one
- A demand that they “make the customer happy” with no math

Be a person in the last paragraph: we will repair, we will
wait on salvage instructions, we will not dispose of the piece
until they say. Human, not soft.

## Who files

If the contract says the buyer files, we still build the pack
and hand it to them. If BBF files, we file on the letterhead
the carrier knows. If a marketplace portal wants their form,
we fill their form *and* keep our pack. Portals lose PDFs.

## After it is in

Write the claim number on the order. Watch their acknowledge
clock. If they go quiet, a polite nudge with the claim number
is a file, not a feeling. If they deny, the denial letter is
what starts Clock C’s lawsuit floor — and what we read twice
before we tell a customer it is over.
