# Unpack, assemble, debris — write it or do not offer it

The reveal is a different job than the ride.

A customer who has waited three weeks wants to see wood, not a carton
in the garage. That want is human. It is also how “white glove” gets
stolen as a synonym for kindness. Kindness does not remove a crate.

## Unpack

Opening the carton or taking the pads off at the site. This is when
concealed damage becomes visible. If the crew unpacks, they should
stay for the walk-around. If they unpack and run, they have turned a
visible problem into a concealed one.

If the customer unpacks later, teach them to keep the carton until the
inspection clock they are actually under has been respected. See Stage
6.

## Light assembly

Feet, handles, a shelf, joining a two-piece island that was split for
the ride. “Light” is the word everyone abuses. A hospitality wall of
case goods is not light. A bed with fifty bolts is not light. A level,
a square, a hidden fastener system — not light.

If BBF’s piece is supposed to be one piece off the truck, do not
create an assembly job by shipping it in two without a plan. If it
must ship in two, write who joins it: crew, installer, customer,
us on a company run.

Do not let a marketplace “assembly available” checkbox become our
promise if the crew that arrives has no tools and no time.

## Debris

Cartons, pallets, stretch, crate lumber. Haul-away is a separate line
almost everywhere. If nobody bought it, the wood stays. Households
hate this. Tell them anyway. A crate in a small apartment is a piece
of furniture they did not order.

Some cities will not let a driver leave a pallet on the curb. Some
will. Do not use the city’s mood as a plan.

## Installation is not debris

Anchoring to a wall, plumbing an island, electrical, scribing to an
out-of-square kitchen — that is a trade. Freight crews are not that
trade unless the contract is a project install. BBF’s healthcare /
hospitality history already knew the difference: FF&E install vs a
truck. Keep the difference.

If a customer needs an island placed under a pendant and leveled to a
tile floor, that is a carpenter or our own install visit, not a
liftgate.

## What “light assembly” can honestly include for BBF

- Leveling feet that were removed or wound up for the ride
- A shelf on pins
- Handles from a bag taped to the back
- Joining a two-piece island *if* we designed the joint for a
  crew with a hex key and twenty minutes

What it cannot honestly include:

- Scribing to a tile floor
- Plumbing or electrical
- Anchoring to a stud
- Building a hospitality run of twenty cases to a laser line
- “Make it look like the catalog photo” when the room is out of
  square

If the customer needs the second list, we are selling an install
visit or naming their installer. We are not selling a nicer
synonym for freight.

## The sentence to put on paper

“Includes: [rung], [unpack Y/N], [assembly: list], [debris Y/N].
Does not include: plumbing, electrical, wall anchor, packing of
customer’s old furniture.”

If a line is blank, it is a no.
