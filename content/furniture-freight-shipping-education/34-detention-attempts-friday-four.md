# Detention, attempts, and the Friday 4 p.m. delivery

Time is a commodity the truck sells by the minute once the minute
is no longer theirs.

Detention, attempts, and the late-day drop are how a “simple
delivery” becomes a story we tell for years.

## Detention

The truck is at the site, ready to work, and the site is not.
Receiving is at lunch. The installer is not there. The path is
blocked. The warehouse needs an hour to find a dock.

Tariffs give a free time, then a clock. Project sites and retailer
docks are where BBF meets this. Households meet a cousin: the
driver will not wait while you run to school pickup.

If we book a hospitality receiving window, we book it like a
flight. Late is money.

## Attempts

The driver tried. Nobody home, nobody authorized, a gate, a dog,
a “come back tomorrow” from a person who is not the shipper. Each
try is a stop. Stops are priced.

A photo of a closed door is a cold little document. Keep it. It
ends the “they never came” conversation so we can have the “who
will be there Tuesday” conversation.

## Friday 4 p.m.

Freight loves to arrive when humans are done being useful. A
destination city driver has a list. Your island is the last stop
because the cube worked out that way. The customer is trying to
leave for the weekend. The driver is trying to close the week.

This is how clean signatures happen: everyone wants to go. This is
how refusals happen: the carton looks tired and nobody has light.
This is how “just leave it in the garage” happens: a sentence that
skips the walk-around.

Coach customers who can: if the window is late, have lights, a
helper, and permission to reschedule without shame. A Monday
morning inspection is better than a Friday dusk shrug — unless
the carton is obviously wrecked, in which case use the light you
have and write.

We should not request Friday-late on purpose for a finish-sensitive
piece. If the carrier offers Thursday 10 a.m., take it.

## A week that looks like bad luck and is actually a booking

Monday: pickup. Wednesday: destination terminal. Thursday: notify
call to a number that rolls to voicemail. Friday 3:40 p.m.:
attempt, photo of a storm door, nobody home. The household was
at a ball game. Monday: storage starts whispering. Tuesday:
redelivery fee. Wednesday: they sign at dusk and skip the
walk-around. Thursday: they open the carton and call us crying.

Nothing in that week required a villain. It required a notify
number that worked and a refusal to treat Friday-late as a gift.

## What BBF can do

- Appointments that are not the last slot
- Notify numbers that answer
- A script that says rescheduling is cheaper than a bad signature
- On company truck, do not copy the Friday-late habit just because
  we can
- On project docks, a named receiving person and a window that
  is not “whenever”
