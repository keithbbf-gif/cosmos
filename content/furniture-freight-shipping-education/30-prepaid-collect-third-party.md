# Prepaid, collect, third-party

Who the carrier bills is not who smiles on the porch. Mixing those
up is how freight sits in a terminal while two offices argue about
a $40 fee.

## Prepaid

Shipper pays. For BBF factory-direct, that is often us, then we
proforma or prepaid-and-add to the customer. We have an account. We
have a relationship. When the invoice is wrong, we can fight it.

Prepaid does not mean we own the in-transit risk. It means we own
the phone call about money. See
[04-who-owns-it-when-the-truck-leaves.md](04-who-owns-it-when-the-truck-leaves.md).

## Collect

Consignee pays before or at delivery. If they are not set up, the
freight waits. Households are bad collect citizens. They do not have
carrier accounts. They will not wire a terminal at 4 p.m.

Collect to a retailer dock can work if the retailer asked for it.
Collect to a house is how a Saturday gets ruined.

## Third-party

Someone who is neither shipper nor consignee pays: a retailer, a
3PL, a designer’s account, a marketplace program. The BOL must name
them correctly. A wrong third-party account is a rejected bill and
a delayed truck.

Old BBF labels already lived here: “BILLING: 3RD PARTY” and a
reference the portal understood. The dock’s job is to print the
label the program generated, not to invent a billing party because
the driver asked for a shortcut.

## COD

Cash (or certified funds) on delivery. Rare, messy, and a way to
turn a driver into a cashier. If we still have a reason to use it,
write the exact amount and the form. If we do not, do not.

## When money holds the freight

Carriers will not deliver a collect or third-party shipment that
cannot bill. They will not pick up if our prepaid account is in
bad standing. That is not a warehouse slight. That is credit.

If a customer is waiting and the hold is money, say money. Do not
say “it’s on the truck” if it is on a clerk’s desk.

## A mix-up BBF has already met

A retailer wants third-party. We tender prepaid on our account
because the dock was in a hurry. Now we have a freight bill we
were not supposed to eat, and their portal has a shipment they
did not rate. Or the reverse: we tender third-party on a dead
account and the pickup never happens while the customer watches
a ship date.

The fix is not a smarter driver. The fix is a field on the order
that the dock can see from the door: PREPAID / COLLECT / 3RD
PARTY + account name. If the field is empty, the piece is not
ready, even if the finish is.

## How to explain it

“The carrier has to send the freight bill to someone they trust.
On this order that someone is [us / you / the retailer]. If that
bill fails, the piece sits.”

Short. True. If the customer wants to “just pay the driver cash,”
that is COD or a fantasy. Name it. Do not nod.
