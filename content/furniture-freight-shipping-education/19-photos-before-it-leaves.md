# Photos before it leaves

A claim file without origin photos is a story. A claim file with origin
photos is a comparison.

BBF does not need a studio. It needs a habit that survives a busy
Thursday.

## What to shoot

For every freight piece, not only the fancy ones:

1. The piece naked in shop light: front, back, both sides, top.
2. Any existing mark you are shipping anyway (and the customer already
   accepted, if that is the deal).
3. The label / stencil / hangtag with the order id readable.
4. The closed carton or the wrapped piece, two faces.
5. On the truck or at the tailgate if you can do it without becoming a
   documentary crew.
6. The signed BOL or dock release.

A phone is enough. Blurry is better than nothing; in-focus is better
than blurry. Do not use filters. Do not crop out the crushed corner you
hope nobody sees. If we are shipping a known scar, the photo is how we
stay honest.

## When to shoot

After final inspection, before the driver owns the clock. If you shoot
yesterday and someone bumps it tonight, you photographed a different
island.

If production wants “we always shoot in finishing,” fine — add a dock
shot. Finishing light is pretty. Dock light is the truth of the handoff.

## How to file

Same place every time: order number as the folder name, date in the
filename, a short word (`origin-top`, `origin-carton`, `bol`). A dump
called `camera` is how photos go missing the week you need them.

Marketplace orders get the portal id *and* our id in the filename. When
the deduction lands in six weeks, you will not remember which island.

## What photos do not do

They do not prove the carrier is a villain. They prove the piece did not
leave Warren with *that* wound, or they prove it did, which is a
different kind of mercy.

They do not replace a delivery notation. A beautiful origin file plus a
clean POD is still a concealed-damage climb.

They do not replace packaging. A photo of a perfect top inside a costume
carton is a photo of a future argument.

## A set that actually works in a dispute

Imagine the porch photo: a mashed front-right corner. Your origin
set needs a sharp front-right corner from Warren, in a frame that
includes enough of the piece that nobody can say it was a different
island. A beauty shot from the showroom angle that hides the corner
is not a set.

If finishing already photographed the top for color, keep that too.
Color arguments and impact arguments are different files.

Name the files so a stranger can order them: `10422-origin-front.jpg`,
`10422-origin-top.jpg`, `10422-carton-closed.jpg`. A camera roll
called `IMG_8841` is how a week disappears.

## Talking to customers about photos

You may send origin photos when a porch panic starts. It calms the ones
who needed to see that we are not shrugging. It also sets a baseline:
“This is how it left. Please send your daylight photos of the wound and
the carton.”

Do not send twenty shop-glamour shots as a substitute for a claim path.
