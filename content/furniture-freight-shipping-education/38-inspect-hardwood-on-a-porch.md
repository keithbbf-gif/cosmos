# How to inspect a hardwood piece on a porch

You do not need a lab. You need daylight, a slow walk, and the
willingness to annoy a driver for four minutes.

This is the script BBF can send to a household, a store, or an
installer. Steal it. Put it in a confirmation.

## Before they cut the tape

1. Count pieces against the BOL.
2. Walk the carton or crate: corners, fork paths, wet, crush,
   puncture, old labels.
3. Photograph the skin next to the truck if you can.
4. If the skin is wounded, write it before anyone signs.
5. If the job is pad wrap, look at the pads and the first
   uncovered corner before the crew leaves.

Do not sign in the house with the piece still on the truck.

## When they open

Daylight. Not a yellow porch bulb on a Friday at 6. If they must
open late, they still open — and they re-check in morning light
before they argue about finish.

Keep every bit of packaging in the room until the inspection
request is done. The carton is evidence. The crate slat with the
fork kiss is evidence. A clean living room is how evidence goes
to the curb.

## The walk

- Top: look across the grain in raking light (stand so the light
  skims). Dents and pad prints show as craters and shines.
- Edges and corners: fingers find what eyes forgive.
- Legs / feet / plinth: the first thing a floor or a tailgate
  hits.
- Back and underside: racking, splits, hardware that tore out.
- Doors and drawers: do they still meet, or did the case go
  parallelogram.
- Finish: a scratch has a direction; a heat print looks like a
  pad quilt; a blush looks like a cloud; a check in the wood
  follows grain.

If they do not know the words, they can still say: “line here,
soft spot here, corner mashed.” Words plus a ruler in the photo
beat poetry.

## What is probably the truck

A crush that matches the carton. A puncture. A fresh raw corner
with dirt in it. A linear rub at sofa height. A clean break with
impact, not a long seasonal check.

## What might be wood or house

A hairline that opens after a week of heat. A finish issue that
matches a known batch. A dent under a vase they already set down.
Be honest. A claim file that cries wolf on every pore will lose
the fork hole too.

Cold pieces: let them sit before anyone judges a finish or starts
a repair. See
[17-moisture-heat-finish-in-a-trailer.md](17-moisture-heat-finish-in-a-trailer.md).

## Hardware

Open the bag now. Count. A missing pull is a short, not a later
mood.

## Company they should keep

Photos: whole piece, then the wound with a coin or tape for
scale, then the matching carton wound. A photo of their face
does not help. A photo of the PRO on the label does.
