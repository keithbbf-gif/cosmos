# Customer-own pickup and the dock release

When they send their own truck, we are not the freight department. We are
the last people who will see the piece while it is still ours to protect.

That should make the dock slower, not faster. The instinct is the opposite:
their driver is in a hurry, our people want the door clear, and everybody
treats the handoff like a favor. Favors do not photograph themselves.

## What “customer-own” means

The buyer (or their designer, their installer, their brother-in-law, their
3PL) books the wheels. BBF’s job is to have the piece ready, marked, and
sound, and to record the moment it leaves. The fee to stage it for pickup
— if we charge one — is not freight. It is dock time.

If they asked us to “just throw it on their guy’s truck,” the answer is:
we will load what is safe to load, we will not strap a finished top under
a ladder, and we will not sign their story later.

## The walk-around is the product

Do this even when the driver sighs:

1. Piece count against the order.
2. Visible walk of every finished face, with shop lights, not hope.
3. Photos: four sides, top, any existing mark, the label, the truck
   tailgate with the piece on it if you can do it without being a pest.
4. A paper (or a phone note that gets printed) that says: picked up by
   [name / company], date, time, piece count, “received in apparent good
   order except as noted,” signature.

If they refuse to sign, the piece does not get on the truck. That sentence
will feel rude once. It will feel wise the month a cracked stretcher shows
up in Colorado.

## Their BOL is not automatically ours

They may hand you a bill of lading that already lies: wrong piece count,
“used household goods,” a value that would not buy the finish. You can
refuse a lie. You can add an exception. You cannot pretend you did not see
it.

If they want “freight collect” to a house with no liftgate on a 53-foot
trailer, that is their booking. Mention the obvious (“that truck will not
like this street”) and write that you mentioned it. Then get the signature.

## Friends with pickups

This is customer-own wearing jeans. Same release. Same photos. Add: we do
not supply a legal cargo policy for a personal truck. If they want us to
care about the ride, they should hire a carrier. If they want to save the
money, they own the ride.

Do not lend pads we need for a real job unless we treat it as a loan with
a name.

## Sales tax and the pickup story

Pickup location can change tax stories. BBF is not going to practice tax
law on the dock. Flag it for the office when the “ship to” and the “pick
up at” are different states or when a reseller is suddenly a household
with a trailer. The dock’s job is the wood and the signature. The office’s
job is the rest.

## After they leave

File the photos under the order the same day. If the customer emails two
weeks later that “your guys loaded it wrong,” you will be glad you have a
picture of their guy’s strap. If you do not have the picture, you will
write a nicer email than you can afford.

## Monday

of exception space. A photo prompt. A signature. Use it on the next
customer-own pickup even if the driver is a regular. Regulars still wreck
