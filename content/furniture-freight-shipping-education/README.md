# Furniture freight & shipping education (BBF drafts)

Staged drafts for **Bradley Brand Furniture** — the Warren shop that builds hardwood
and then has to get it across a country that treats a kitchen island like a pallet
until it isn’t. These are education, not a rate book.

**Status:** staged drafts. Not published copy. Not a carrier tariff. Not legal
advice. Not a promise that every furniture van or LTL clerk uses the same words.

**Voice:** a human at the freight desk talking to the rest of BBF — shop,
office, and anyone who has to explain a porch delivery to a customer without
lying. If a sentence could sit on a marketplace FAQ, rewrite it.

**Count:** 42 drafts in six stages. Read in order the first time. After that,
jump by the problem in front of you.

## How to use this

1. A new hire reads Stage 1 before they quote a ship date.
2. Before you book a mode, read Stage 2 and the wrap draft that matches the
   piece.
3. Before you tell a customer “white glove,” read Stage 4. The phrase is not a
   feeling.
4. When something shows up marked or broken, start at Stage 6. Do not invent a
   clock.

Each draft has YAML frontmatter: `draft`, `slug`, `stage`, `status`, `topics`,
`related`. `status: staged-draft` means ready for BBF edit, not live web.

Cross-links use repo-relative filenames. Keep them when you retitle.

## What these drafts are not

- A substitute for the carrier’s rules tariff or the actual BOL in the file.
- A promise that marketplace portals (Wayfair, Overstock, and the rest BBF has
  lived with) use the same words as a furniture specialized carrier.
- Legal counsel. Carmack and 49 C.F.R. Part 370 are named so BBF knows the
  neighborhood. A real dispute goes to counsel and the paper in the folder.
- Atlanta “Bradley” trade-show copy. Different company. This pack is Warren.

## Monday rule

If two drafts disagree, the **paper on that shipment** wins: the BOL, the
delivery receipt, the carrier guide you booked against. Then fix the draft.
