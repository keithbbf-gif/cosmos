In 2009, the National Institute for Health and Clinical Excellence issued Clinical Guideline 90 on depression in adults. Commentaries in the *British Journal of Psychiatry* and elsewhere noted that the guideline recommended MBCT for people who were currently well but had experienced three or more episodes. I am reporting a 2009 committee document, later updated and re-sited as NICE's naming and numbering moved. I am not telling you to enroll in a class. I am not telling an Arkansas clinician to obey a British PDF.

Guidelines are historical objects. They have chairs, evidence tables, conflicts of interest, and revision dates. They are not oracles. They are not "the science." They are a group's bet, at a time, for a health system.

NICE CG90 matters to this pack because it is the most famous *institutional* sentence MBCT received in the 2000s. ACT's institutional sentences are patchier and more country-dependent: Division 12 lists that change, VA and other system endorsements that come and go, insurance language that is not a finding. I will not freeze a 2026 list on a page that will be read in 2028. I will say: if you meet "recommended by" on a workshop flyer, ask *which body, which year, which population, which document*. Then read the document's limits.

The United States does not have NICE. It has a mess of professional associations, payers, and state boards. A WOW Therapies page that imported a 2009 British MBCT sentence onto a speech-language site would be a category error and a service implication. Do not do that.

APA, ACA, ASHA, state counseling boards — different jobs. This pack is not a billing guide. Educational history may mention that *someone* named a treatment in a guideline. It may not turn the mention into a "we follow NICE" badge.

Updates: NICE's depression guidance has been reviewed and restructured since 2009 (CG90 lived alongside later updates; numbering and websites move). A later editor should re-open the live NICE page before any public import and mark the year. This draft's last verification is 2026-09-14 and still treats CG90 2009 as the founding public object of the MBCT-in-the-NHS story.

Other countries wrote other documents. I have not surveyed Australia, Canada, or the Nordic systems here. Thinness is honest. A global "guidelines say" is a lie.

For ACT, the honest institutional sentence is smaller than fans want and larger than enemies want. There are trials. There are reviews of mixed quality. There are systems that have used ACT in specific clinics (primary care, VA specialty shops). There is not, in this pack's mouth, a blanket "approved for your diagnosis."

The sister pack already banned schema `MedicalTherapy` and `treats` / `indication` on history posts. Same ban. `Article` is enough. A guideline citation in an educational essay is a citation. It is not structured medical markup.

If you are in care, your clinician's license and your system's formulary matter more than this folder. If you are a student, learn to read a guideline's inclusion table. If you are a designer, do not put a crest next to a hexagon.

Committees are made of people who argue. The argument is the point. A PDF that looks calm was not calm in the room. History can admit that without pretending to have been in the room.

CG23, the earlier NICE depression guideline (2004, revised 2007), is the document CG90 partially updated. I name it so 2009 does not look like the first British sentence about depression care. MBCT's appearance is a *change* in a lineage of PDFs, not a burning bush.

This series does not publish treatment protocols, and it does not publish treatment recommendations. A 2009 sentence stays in 2009.
