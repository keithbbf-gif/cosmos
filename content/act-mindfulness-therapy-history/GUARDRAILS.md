# Claims guardrails — ACT / mindfulness therapy history

These drafts are **educational psychotherapy history** for WOW Therapies. They are
not labels, not session manuals, and not a back door into a service page.

A reviewer should be able to fail a draft by quoting a sentence. If the only
defense is "the reader will know what we meant," the sentence is wrong.

## What a draft may do

- Name people, places, languages, hospitals, societies, and documents.
- Describe what a school **claimed**, who paid for it, who dissented, and what
  later researchers revised.
- Treat a randomized trial as a **historical event**: year, journal, sample,
  comparison, what the authors reported. That is not a homework assignment.
- Quote or paraphrase **guidelines as committees**. NICE CG90 (2009) naming
  MBCT for a defined group of recovered patients is a fact about a guideline.
  It is not this site telling a reader to enroll.
- Mark dating disputes in public. "ACT began in 1999" and "the first protocol
  was written in 1981 under another name" cannot both be flattened into a
  brand origin story.
- Admit Buddhist and other contemplative debts without pretending a county
  clinic is a sangha, and without pretending a Pali text is a 1979 syllabus.
- Name controversy: evidence quality, cultural borrowing, corporate
  mindfulness, acceptance taught as a muzzle.
- Close with a short "why a contemporary clinician still reads this" that does
  **not** become a protocol.

## What a draft may not do

- Any instruction a reader could follow as a session: breathing counts, body
  scans, thought records, values card sorts, defusion scripts, exposure
  hierarchies, "try this at home," "ask yourself these ten questions."
- "Gold-standard treatment," "clinically proven to," "studies prove ACT works
  better than CBT" without a named trial, a dated review, **and** a sentence
  about limits.
- Imply that WOW Therapies diagnoses, treats, or specializes in a DSM/ICD
  condition **in these history posts**.
- Imply that reading an essay is therapy, counseling, meditation training, or
  a substitute for a licensed clinician.
- PHI, composite patients, or "a client once told me" stories.
- Guarantees of outcome. Crisis protocols. Safety-planning worksheets.
- Collapse satipaṭṭhāna, zazen, vipassanā, Morita's *arugamama*, or Benson's
  relaxation response into "ancient ACT."
- "Freud discovered the unconscious." (Wrong pack, and still false.)
- Invent quotations, trial counts, or workshop attendance.
- Living-person gossip or unsourced health claims. Public books, lectures,
  and interviews only.
- Schema `MedicalTherapy`, `Drug`, or `treats` / `indication`. `Article` only.
- Superlatives about the practice ("best therapist in Arkansas").
- Disease keywords paired with a booking CTA on these URLs.

## Voice

Write like a historically literate magazine writer who has sat in enough
consulting rooms to know that a theory is not a person. Warm without being
pastoral. Precise without being a textbook. First person is allowed when it is
a readerly "I" ("I keep a pencil in the margin"), not a testimonial "I"
("ACT saved me").

If a source is thin, say the trail is thin. If two reviews disagree on a year,
put both years on the table.

## How a draft proves it stayed inside the fence

Every draft carries YAML with `claims_posture: educational-therapy-history`
and `status: draft`. Every draft opens with the educational note and ends with
a **Claims box** a skimming reader can find without finishing the essay. The
linter in `tools/lint_claims.py` is a tripwire, not a conscience.

## If asked for both a history and a how-to

Print the history. Refuse the how-to in one sentence: this series does not
publish treatment protocols.
