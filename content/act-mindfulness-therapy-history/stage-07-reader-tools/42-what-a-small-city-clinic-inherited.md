**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

WOW Therapies is a Southeast Arkansas practice brand. The live homepage, when last fetched, spoke of occupational, physical, and speech services: early childhood, school age, adult neuro, swallowing, voice. It did not speak of ACT. It did not speak of an eight-week MBCT class. Heritage education is allowed to be wider than the appointment book. Heritage education is not allowed to fake the appointment book.

What did a small-city clinic in this country inherit from the story this pack told?

A **vocabulary**. Patients and parents already say "mindfulness." Teachers already say it. Apps already say it. The word arrives before the Guilford book. A clinician who has never opened Hayes 1999 still has to decide what to do when a caregiver asks if mindfulness would help a child's stutter or an adult's aphasia. The honest answer is usually: that is a different job than this session, and I will not sell you a noun.

A **suspicion of scripts**. If this pack worked, staff will hear a DIY session line as a fence, not as a content idea. Speech homework is a real thing, licensed and consented. A defusion metaphor printed on a blog is not speech homework.

A **respect for staffing**. Linehan's team, MBCT's trained group leader, ACBS's trainer lists, a primary-care behavioral-health job — all of them say that a weekend is not a service line. Small clinics already know this about dysphagia and childhood apraxia. They should know it about third-wave words too.

A **habit of dates**. 1979, 1991, 1999, 2000, 2004, 2005, 2008, 2009, 2013. Dates keep a staff meeting from floating.

A **sister calendar**. If a reader wants Freud or Rogers or Van Riper, there are other folders. This one does not have to become an encyclopedia to be useful.

What the clinic did *not* inherit, unless a later human decision says so:

A new service.
A new billing code.
A new specialty claim.
A lotus logo.
A hexagon.
A waiting-room prescription to sit still.

Southeast Arkansas is not Worcester and not Reno. Distance is not backwardness. It is a chance not to import the yogurt years wholesale. You can teach the history of a noun without staffing the noun. You can refuse a fad without sneering at the 2000 table.

I live in a different kind of room — a writing room — and I am not pretending to know the waiting room in Monticello or Pine Bluff. I am saying what the *files* allow a brand to say. If the files ever sit next to a booking button, someone has failed the import notes.

The 15-second clock that runs other parts of this repository is not a content strategy. Do not dump forty-five URLs in a week. The sister INDEX already said a human pace is 10–14 days a piece. This pack can be slower. History is not a product launch.

If a parent is in crisis, the sentence is still the short one: local emergency services. Not a breathing app. Not this essay.

The inheritance I most want to leave the brand is a tone. Warm enough to be a small-city clinic that answers its own phone. Cold enough not to sell a method it does not staff. That tone is the style guide's desk. It is also, if I have done this right, the sound of these drafts.

You can put a series hub page on staging that lists the slugs and the fence. You can leave production alone. Motion, in COSMOS's other sense, is not the same as publishing. This folder is motion toward a review, not toward a sitemap.

If a reporter calls and asks whether the practice "does mindfulness now," the answer is the homepage plus this fence: we published educational history; we did not silently add a service. A reporter who wants a vibe will not like that sentence. Write it anyway.
