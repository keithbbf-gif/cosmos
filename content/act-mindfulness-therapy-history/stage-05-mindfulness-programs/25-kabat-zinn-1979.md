**Educational note.** This is history for a general reader. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not a substitute for care with a licensed clinician.

In September 1979, Jon Kabat-Zinn started what was first called the Stress Reduction and Relaxation Program at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center. It was later renamed the Stress Reduction Clinic, he wrote, to sound like other clinical services in the Department of Medicine. He is living as of last verification. This essay uses his 2011 *Contemporary Buddhism* article, later institutional pages, and the 1990 book — public objects. No health speculation.

He has described a ten-second "vision" on about day ten of a two-week IMS retreat in spring 1979: a hospital model, a long scientific tail, livelihoods. He also wrote that he seldom mentioned it because it was weird. I print the 2011 telling as a founder's memory, not as a miracle that licenses a method.

The first referrals, in that same essay, came from three physicians he names: Tom Winters, Bob Burney, and John J. Monahan. Named referrers are better history than "the hospital embraced mindfulness." A new service lives or dies on who will write the slip. Early MBSR was a referral object for people the rest of the hospital was tired of — chronic pain, the census that does not get well on time.

Kabat-Zinn was not, in 1979, founding a psychotherapy school. He was founding a medical-behavioral class: eight weeks, in his telling, with sitting, yoga, and a secular vocabulary that could survive a medicine department. Psychotherapy schools later borrowed the vocabulary. The borrowing is Stage 5's later essays. The 1979 object is a basement (institutional memory still says basement) and a hypothesis that attention training might sit beside standard care.

UMass pages now speak in the voice of a proud descendant: hundreds of thousands served, "we know unequivocally," prevention of stress-related illness. That is institutional advertising in 2020s English. A history pack can note the pride and still prefer the 2011 essay's more careful weirdness — one foot on a roller skate, he said, the other on a banana peel, because medical-school politics.

The Center for Mindfulness in Medicine, Health Care, and Society is dated to 1995 on Kabat-Zinn's own site. In 2019, some training programs moved into the UMass Memorial Health system, the health system's page says. Institutions migrate. The 1979 clinic is not the 2019 org chart. Students who say "I trained at UMass" may mean several different shops.

Why this is not ACT: different building, different parentage, different census. Hayes's 1981 protocol nickname lived in a behavior-analysis lab. Kabat-Zinn's 1979 class lived in a Department of Medicine. They later shared English. Shared English is not a merger.

Why this is not "the West discovering meditation": essay 05 (*Attention Is Older Than a Clinic*) already refused that. Kabat-Zinn's originality was a *hospital object* that could be copied without a conversion. Copying without conversion is also what critics later named as the loss.

This page will not describe a class session. It will not tell you to lie down. It will say: September 1979, Worcester, a renamed clinic, three early referrers, a retreat-room memory written down in 2011. That is enough to keep later "mindfulness-based" manuals from pretending they have no address.

Portrait: living. Type only. No TED stills. No basement reconstruction. If the hospital has a historical photo with a clean license, a later editor may add it to PORTRAIT_SOURCES. Until then, type.

He has also described, in that 2011 essay, years of sitting and wandering before the ten seconds — a koan about livelihood, not a sudden conversion. I mention the wandering so the vision does not look like a comic-book origin. Origins are usually slower than the scene that gets filmed.

If WOW Therapies ever mentions MBSR on a public page, the honest version is this address and this year, plus a sentence that the practice is not, by writing history, offering the class.
