Plenum published Robert J. Kohlenberg and Mavis Tsai's *Functional Analytic Psychotherapy* in 1991 — the same year Linehan's trial appeared and the same year an ABA talk put "acceptance and commitment therapy" in a title. FAP is a radical-behavioral therapy about the live relationship in the room: clinically relevant behaviors, reinforcement in session, a closeness that behaviorists were not supposed to want. Hayes's 2004 guest list and Öst's 2008 guest list both invited it. Öst noted there were no FAP RCTs in his set. A neighbor can be important and still thin on the kind of trial a reviewer wants.

I will not teach FAP's in-session coding. Intimacy-as-method is exactly the sort of thing a blog should not amateur.

Paul Gilbert's compassion-focused therapy grew in the 2000s from evolutionary and social-rank models of shame, with a 2010 Routledge book as a convenient public object among many papers. Kristin Neff's 2003 *Self and Identity* article on self-compassion, and later work with Christopher Germer on mindful self-compassion programs, built a parallel boom: scales, workshops, a vocabulary that workplaces liked even more than "acceptance."

These rooms are neighbors, not mergers.

FAP shares ACT's behavior-analytic hallway and often shares trainers. It does not share a hexaflex. CFT shares a concern with how people relate to their own pain; it does not share RFT. MSC shares a cushion and a secular class form with MBSR; it is not MBCT's relapse census. A headline that says "compassion-based therapy" has already lost the addresses.

The compassion boom matters to this pack because it is what happened when "acceptance" started to sound cold. Markets prefer kindness nouns. Kindness nouns then get the McMindfulness treatment: a poster, an app, a manager's all-hands. Purser and Loy (2013) were writing into that weather. Gilbert and Neff are not responsible for every poster. Founders never are. They still have to watch the posters.

I will not adjudicate whether self-compassion "works." There is a literature. There is also a greeting-card problem. This page's job is to keep the 1991 FAP book, the 2003 Neff paper, and the 2010 Gilbert book from being swallowed by ACT's brand, or swallowing it.

Living people: public books and articles only.

For WOW Therapies: "we take a compassionate approach" is ordinary clinical English and should stay ordinary. Do not upgrade it to a named school unless you have the training and the consent language. Compassion as a virtue is not CFT. Warmth is not MSC. A kind speech-language pathologist is not doing FAP.

The sister pack's humanistic essay already owns Rogers's warmth as a historical object. This page will not restage it. I mention Rogers only to say: the compassion boom sometimes reinvented a person-centered temperature and called it new because the citation was evolutionary psychology or a Buddhist-derived noun. Reinvention is allowed. Amnesia is sloppy.

Öst's empty FAP cell is a useful cold shower. Neighboring rooms can be rich in theory and poor in the trial design a 2008 reviewer demanded. That does not make them worthless. It makes them neighbors you should not advertise as "evidence-based" on a service page without doing the homework this educational series will not do for you.

IBCT (Andrew Christensen and Neil Jacobson) and McCullough's CBASP appear in Öst's 2008 guest list as third-wave cousins with almost no RCT volume in his set. I name them so ACT does not look like the only neighbor. I will not give them essays. Thin guest-list presence is a fact about 2008, not a verdict on a marriage or a chronic-depression model.

If you want a map, draw four houses: ACT, DBT, MBCT, and then a street of cousins (FAP, CFT, MSC, IBCT, CBASP — the last two appear in Öst's list). Do not draw a mall called Mindfulness.
