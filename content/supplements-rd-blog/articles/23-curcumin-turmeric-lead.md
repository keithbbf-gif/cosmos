Turmeric sold through the 2020s as an anti-everything powder. Two facts traveled slower than the ads: curcumin's oral bioavailability is poor unless you change the formula, and **economic adulteration with lead chromate** is a documented way to make ground turmeric look expensive.

## Lead is not a rumor

CDC and state health departments have published case-level lead poisonings tied to turmeric (including imported powders used as food *and* capsules). `[VERIFY]` the specific MMWR or state notice you quote — several exist across 2017–2024, including US household investigations. FDA has used import alerts on turmeric lots with high lead. ABC's Botanical Adulterants program has a turmeric bulletin for a reason: color and weight are cheap to fake with chrome yellow.

If your COA is a curcumin % HPLC and a "heavy metals: conforms" with no ICP-MS numbers and no LOQ, you bought a color. Finished-product lead **per daily serving**, not just ppm in the powder, is the row that matters for Prop 65 and for children.

## Curcumin the molecule

Curcuminoids are a fraction of *Curcuma longa* rhizome. Isolated 95% curcumin extracts are not "a teaspoon of kitchen turmeric." Doses in human trials are often grams of extract, not 50 mg fairy dust. Outcomes (joint comfort, "inflammation" markers) are mixed, often industry-linked, often short. Inflammatory-marker changes are not a disease treatment. Do not put rheumatoid x-rays on the PDP.

## Piperine and "enhanced absorption"

Black-pepper extract (piperine, Bioperine-class) raises curcumin appearance in plasma in small studies. It also **inhibits drug-metabolizing enzymes**. That is a feature for the curcumin graph and a bug for a buyer on a narrow-therapeutic-index drug. A "better absorbed" claim that hides the interaction is the 2020s version of a footnote nobody reads.

Phytosomes, nanoparticles, "theracurmin" — each is a **different article** with its own PK paper, or it is a word. Do not transfer one branded extract's RCT onto a generic 95% from a broker (same lesson as ashwagandha extracts, piece 07).

## Kitchen spice vs SKU

Food turmeric in a curry is a dietary pattern. A 1,500 mg curcuminoid capsule with piperine is a concentrated product. Labels that show a spice photo and a drug-sized dose are doing a bait. Residual solvents on extracts, pesticides on rhizomes, and species mix-ups (*Curcuma* vs cheaper cousins) belong on the incoming ID.

## What changed since 2020 (box)

Demand stayed high. Lead stories kept landing because the incentive (color + price) did not die. A 2026 turmeric SKU that I would ship has ICP-MS metals, a curcuminoid profile (not a single number), no piperine unless the interaction warning is in 12-point type, and no "replaces your NSAID" joke.
