# Style guide — supplements R&D pack

Voice is the product. A clinician-curious operator or a white-label founder should finish a piece and trust the sourcing. If it sounds like a wellness SEO mill, it fails even when the citations are real.

`voice_check: human` in front matter means the draft was written against this file, not that a model is claiming to be a person.

## Who is speaking

A careful operator who reads primary sources: FDA letters, FTC guidance, RCTs, Cochrane, NAMS, ISSN, USP/NSF program pages. Not a brand mascot. Not a medical-advice bot. Not a LinkedIn thought-leader.

Future science/QA partner has a clean seat. Do not invent a lab, a chief scientist, or a corporate parent. No public Unilever ties. ElitElixirs / white-label context may sit in internal notes, not as on-page hero copy.

## Hard bans (fail the draft)

Do not use:

- "In today's rapidly evolving…"
- "It's important to note"
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" (as metaphor for a market or field)
- "robust"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "In conclusion"
- "Moreover" / "Furthermore" stacks (one is already too many)
- "Whether you're a … or a …"
- empty dualities ("not just X, but Y"; "both an art and a science")
- throat-clearing first paragraphs that delay the first fact
- symmetrical list-filler (three parallel "empower / enhance / elevate" items)
- fake confidence on weak evidence

Also drop the cousins: "navigate," "tapestry," "plethora," "utilize," "harness," "elevate your," "the future of," "at the forefront," "a holistic approach," "empower," "in this article we will explore."

## Do this instead

1. Open on a dated event, a paper, a letter number, or a number a reader can check.
2. Mix sentence length. Short after a long one. Do not write every sentence as subject–verb–object with a trailing clause.
3. Name the trial. VITAL, STRENGTH, REDUCE-IT, COVID A to Z, CORONAVIT, AGA 2020. Do not say "a recent study" if you have the name.
4. Cite or mark `[CITE NEEDED]` / `[VERIFY]`. No invented n, p-values, or market-share figures.
5. Say when evidence is thin. "One 40-person trial" is a fact. "Emerging science suggests" is a dodge.
6. Prefer structure/function, quality, bioavailability, testing, supply chain, and labeling education over disease stories.
7. Keep the DSHEA disclaimer on every draft. Educational only. Not medical advice. Supplements are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent disease.
8. Stop when the piece has said the thing. Target 1,000–1,800 words. Cut the last recap paragraph if it only restates H2s.

## Claims posture

- Efficacy and safety sentences need a PMID, DOI, FDA/FTC/USP URL, or a named review — or a flag.
- Market size numbers are usually trade-press. Label them as such.
- Do not convert a nutrient deficiency finding into a "this product treats COVID / menopause / aging" claim.
- CBD, NMN, NAC: report the agency position and the date. Do not litigate it in marketing voice.

## Headings

H2s should be specific ("CORONAVIT did not cut COVID incidence") not thematic ("The immune conversation"). "What changed since 2020" boxes are allowed when they hold dates, not slogans.

## Voice check before `voice_check: human`

Read the draft out loud. If a sentence could sit under any other supplement brand without changing a noun, rewrite it. If two adjacent paragraphs start with the same syntactic shape, break one. If you cannot point to a source for a number, flag it or delete it.
