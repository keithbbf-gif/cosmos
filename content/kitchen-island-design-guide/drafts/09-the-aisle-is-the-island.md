People think they are buying an island. They are buying the air
around it.

NKBA wants a work aisle at least 42 inches wide for one cook and
48 inches for more than one, measured between counter frontages,
tall cabinets, and appliances. Walkways that are just walking
should be 36. Those are not decorative numbers. They are the
difference between cooking and shuffling.

I have watched a competent cook go quiet in a 38-inch aisle.
Nothing is “wrong.” The refrigerator door and the oven door just
cannot be friends. The island is blamed because it is the new
thing. The aisle was the thing we spent.

When I size a Bradley island I start with leftover floor, not
with a dream rectangle. Measure wall to wall. Subtract the
counter depth on each side (24 inches of box plus the top
overhang, usually about 25.5 finished). Subtract 42 or 48 for
each work aisle you actually need. What remains is the island
you may have. If that remainder is 22 inches, you do not have
an island. You have a cart, or you have a peninsula, or you
have a conversation about knocking a wall down.

A 3-by-5 island with 42 inches all around wants about 120 square
feet of kitchen. Fine Homebuilding did that napkin math years
ago and it still holds. Clients hear “small island” and picture
a modest object. They do not picture 10 feet by 12 feet of
committed floor.

Do not steal from the work aisle to buy seating behind the
stools. That theft shows up every Thanksgiving. Someone is
carving. Someone is trying to get a platter to the table. The
stools become a fence.

If the room can only give you 42 inches on the cook side and
32 behind the stools, the stools lose. Seating can live at a
table. The cook cannot cook in the hallway.

Write the aisle dimensions on the plan in inches, not just the
island. I want to see 48, 42, 36, 44 — the real ones — before
I care what door style we picked. The island is whatever is
left after the aisles are honest.
