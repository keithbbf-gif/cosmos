A lot of island drawings are still living
in 2020. The 2023 National Electrical
Code changed the story, and your town may
or may not have adopted it.

I am not your electrician. I am the person
who will not spec a side outlet like it is
still 2016. Confirm the edition with the
AHJ. Then talk like this:

Through 2020, islands and peninsulas were
expected to have receptacles serving the
top, and the count was tied to the size
of the surface. Designers put outlets on
the side of the box, a few inches under
the stone, because it was easy and the
slab stayed clean.

2023, section 210.52(C)(2), said something
ruder and more useful. A receptacle
serving the island or peninsula top is
no longer required. If you do not put
one in, you must leave provisions to add
one later. If you do put one in, you
must locate it per 210.52(C)(3).

210.52(C)(3) is the part that kills the
old side outlet as a counter-serving
device. The allowed places, in plain
language:

1. On or above the work surface, not
   more than 20 inches above it.
2. In the countertop, using an assembly
   listed for countertops.
3. In a work surface, using an assembly
   listed for work surfaces or
   countertops.

Below the top, on the cabinet side, is
not a legal way to serve the top
anymore under this edition. There is
also language keeping you from playing
games 24 inches down and calling it
service. USB-only devices without a
receptacle can still live lower. That
is a charging story, not a toaster
story.

Why the change? CMP-2 looked at injury
data. Cords pulled off the side of an
island by a kid or a passing hip were
not a myth. Optional outlets plus
tighter locations is the code’s answer.
CMP-2 tied that to hospital-visit counts
in the thousands [VERIFY count and source
against NFPA/CMP-2 records for the adopted
NEC edition]. It is a safety answer that
happens to annoy a lot of pretty elevations.

Your kitchen may still be under 2020
or a local amendment that keeps the
old count. Do not guess. The rest of
these electrical drafts assume you
will ask, and that if you are on 2023
you will not hide a “counter” outlet
on the show side of a Bradley box
just because the last job did.
