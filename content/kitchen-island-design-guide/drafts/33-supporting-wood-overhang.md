Wood warns you. It flexes. People read
that flex as “rustic” for about a week.

A 1.5-inch edge-grain top can fly 10 to
12 inches and feel all right if the
species is maple and the diner is not
doing chin-ups. Fifteen inches wants
help. End grain, being thicker, can
look like it does not need help and
still twist if the glue-up is a panel
without a stout edge.

Help looks like:

- Steel brackets, same as stone, set
  back from the knee.
- A thicker edge lamination that turns
  the fly into a torsion bar.
- Legs. Honest ones. A table is allowed
  to have legs. An island is allowed to
  remember it was a table.
- A second tier or a dropped panel that
  carries the fly back to the box.

What I do not like: a pair of skinny
corbels under walnut, 6 inches in from
each end, on an 8-foot span. The middle
becomes a diving board. Three or four
supports, or a continuous angle.

Fasteners still have to let the top
move. A steel bracket screwed into
slotted holes in the top’s underside
is a good hybrid. A bracket glued and
screwed rigid across a 27-inch-wide
panel is how you get a split that
starts at the bracket.

If the wood is oiled and the bracket is
steel, think about black marks and about
a finish on the steel that will not rust
onto maple. These are small thoughts. They
are cheaper than a refinish.

Bradley furniture ends can hide a steel
channel behind a panel. That is my
favorite support because the seating
side stays clean and the knees stay
unbruised. It has to be in the box
order. A channel is not a weekend
add-on if the drawers already run
full-depth.

Bounce test: sit on the overhang in the
shop if you can, or press it like you
mean it after install. If you would not
let a guest do that, add a bracket. Do
not wait for the guest.

Seasonal check: a wood fly that was
stiff in January can feel livelier in
August if the house went humid. That
is movement, not a failed glue-up.
If it feels lively in both seasons,
we underbuilt. Add the channel. Do
not tell the household to “just not
lean.” People lean. That is what a
seating overhang is for.
