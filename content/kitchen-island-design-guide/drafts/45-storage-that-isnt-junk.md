Islands become junk because we leave
the inside unnamed.

Name the bays before we order.

- Knives and boards, on the prep
  side, not behind a diner’s knees.
- Trash and recycle, near the sink
  if the sink is here, or near the
  prep if the sink is on the wall.
  Two cans. NKBA wants two
  somewhere in the kitchen. The
  island is a good somewhere.
- Mixer or bowls, a deep drawer
  that is actually deep, not a
  4-inch utensil tray wearing a
  costume.
- Serving platters, vertical, on
  the seating side if you want
  guests to help.
- A chase bay for power, even if
  it is only 6 inches. Future you
  will send a thank-you.

I fight the single wide drawer that
runs the whole length. Full-access
already makes drawers wide. An
8-foot drawer is a lake of spatulas.
Split. A 30 and a 30 and a trash
pull will beat a monument.

Doors on an island should earn the
swing. A door into a 42-inch aisle
is a door that will be hated. Prefer
drawers on the cook side. Prefer
drawers or a clean panel on the
seating side so knees do not meet
knobs. If we use doors, I want them
on an end, swinging away from the
walk.

Open shelves on the seating face
are friendly and dusty. I allow a
short run for the bowls you actually
use. I do not allow a 7-foot
styling stage. The stylist does not
live here.

Charging drawer: see the USB draft.
One. Not three. Labeled.

Kids’ station: if the island is
homework, give it a bay that is
not the knife bay. Paper and
pencils can live under the
seating fly. Knives cannot.

I walk the named bays with the
household using the stuff they
already own. If the stand mixer
does not fit the “mixer drawer”
on the drawing, the drawing is
wrong. Bradley will cut a taller
box. They will not invent a
shorter mixer.

Unnamed storage becomes a drawer
of batteries, takeout menus, and
a warranty for a toaster you
threw away. Name it or lose it.
The island is too expensive to
be a dumpster with a pretty top.
