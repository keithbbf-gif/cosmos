The pop-up used to be a gadget. Under
2023 it is one of the few legal ways
to put power on the island without
building a backsplash that is 6 inches
tall.

Use an assembly listed for countertops
or work surfaces. “Listed” is the word
that keeps you out of a fight. A pretty
import with no listing is a pretty
no. The cutout is a hole in stone or
wood that has to be sealed and, in
wood, treated like a sink. Water and
pop-ups are a marriage you should
discuss. I do not put a pop-up on the
low side of a wood top that already
cups toward a sink.

People hate the look until they use
one for a week. Then they hate the
look less than they hated the cord
to the wall. Put it toward the cook
side, out of the primary landing,
not in the pastry stretch. A mixer
wants power near where the mixer
stands. A pop-up in the seating
overhang is how you serve a guest a
cord in the soup.

On or above, within 20 inches, is the
other legal family. A two-tier island
can carry a receptacle on the face of
the upper tier, feeding the 36-inch
worktop, if we keep it in that 20-
inch band. A little raised splash on
the far side of a cook island can do
the same. Both are design, not
afterthoughts.

The side outlet that served the
counter is, on a 2023 job, dead for
that job. You may still have
receptacles on the box for other
reasons — a vacuum, a hidden charger
that is not claiming to serve the
top — and you may still get an
inspector who wants to talk about
it. Do not freelance. Do not tell a
client the old outlets are “probably
fine.”

USB-only, no 125-volt slots, can live
lower in some readings. That is
handy for a homework face. It is not
a toaster. I label it in my head as
charging, and I still want GFCI
thinking on anything that is a
receptacle.

Bradley finished backs make side
outlets tempting because the
furniture panel is a beautiful place
to cut a hole. Resist the habit.
Cut the hole for a listed pop-up
in the top, or build a legal above-
counter location, or leave the
provision and a clean panel. Pretty
is allowed. Pretty that pulls a
kettle onto a child is not.
