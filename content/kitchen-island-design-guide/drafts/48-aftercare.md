The island is not finished when
the crew leaves. It is finished
when the household knows what
Thursday looks like.

**Oiled wood.** Wipe it like a
table. Dry it if it gets wet.
Oil it when it looks thirsty —
often in the first months, then
less. Use the oil we wrote on
the spec, not a salad dressing
and not a polyurethane “refresh.”
Cut on it if that was the
contract. Sand a scar with the
grain and oil again. If a sink
lives in the wood, that rim is
a nightly wipe, not a weekly
intention.

**Filmed wood.** Wipe. Use a
board. Do not take a knife to
the jacket. A scratch is a
refinish conversation, not a
rag conversation.

**Quartz.** Wipe. Trivet. No
mystery cleaners, no oil soap
that leaves a ghost. Seams get
a look now and then. If a
pop-up lives in the slab, keep
the gasket clean so you are
not washing crumbs into a
listed device.

**Granite.** Wipe. Trivet if
you are not performing. Water-
drop test once in a while. Seal
on the calendar the fabricator
gave you, not on a memory.

**Marble.** Enjoy the etch or
stop buying lemons for the
island. Boards, boards, boards.
A honed top will hide more and
still tell the truth.

**Soapstone.** Oil when it looks
uneven. Sand a scratch. Do not
treat a chip as a moral failure.
It is a soft stone. You knew.

**The box.** Soft-close hinges
get a little dust. Full-
extension slides want to be
closed with a hand, not a hip,
if you want them to stay
soft. Wipe the furniture back.
People lean there with jeans.

**The seats.** Felt or a floor
you can live with. Do not drag
stools like you are moving a
fridge. If a rug must live
behind them, keep it flat and
out of the travel.

**The power.** Know where the
GFCI lives. Test it when you
change clocks. If a pop-up
sticks, stop forcing it. If
the future provision is a
blank cover, do not bury it
under a breadbox.

**The aisle.** The tape we put
down was not a suggestion. If
a new stool or a new bin
spends the 48, the bin leaves.
The island cannot defend
itself.

I like a one-page care card
taped inside the chase bay or
the trash pull, where a future
owner will find it. Species,
finish, stone name, fabricator,
electrician’s edition note,
the refuse line. Houses change
hands. The island should arrive
with its biography.

Live with it. Let maple go a
little amber. Let soapstone go
dark. Let quartz stay stubborn.
The farm table that never left
is allowed to look used. That
is how you know the room is a
kitchen.
