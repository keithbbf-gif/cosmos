# Sources — outdoor and garden furniture history and materials

Paraphrased. Not copied. Date-stamp a reprint before you hand a number to a client or say a date on camera.

## Old-world sitting

- Roman peristyle houses (Pompeii, Herculaneum): colonnaded garden courts; outdoor dining and stone exedrae. Woods largely lost; bronzes often melted; marble and masonry survive. Museum holdings and site publications, not a catalog of "Roman patio sets."
- Islamic courtyard gardens (Andalusi, Maghrebi, Persian lineages): shade, water, hard seating, often built-in. Thermal job first. Do not reduce to lantern styling.
- Chinese garden seats (zuodun / drum stools): ceramic, stone, sometimes wood under cover; pierced drum form; Ming–Qing examples widely published. Dual use as seat and stand. Freeze plus trapped water is a shop warning, not a period text.
- Italian villa gardens (Renaissance onward): benches and exedrae as stations for prospect. Stone as leave-it material. Loggia as the roofed sit.

## European garden

- French formal gardens: axes, bosquets, orangeries. Seats as blocking. Painted movable chairs under glass or on terrace; not a license to leave conservatory wicker on an open deck.
- English landscape garden (Brown and the picturesque quarrel): benches as viewing stops. Rustic branch furniture as a performed wildness. Later park bench as civic object.
- Coalbrookdale Company, Ironbridge, Shropshire: coke-smelted iron lineage (Darby); garden seats mid-19th century; Great Exhibition 1851 as a public amplifier. Christopher Dresser among designers for industrial ornament. Cast iron: heavy, brittle relative to bar, historically painted. See Ironbridge Gorge Museum publications; foundry catalogs.
- Victorian display gardens: rustic, cast, ceramic, tea furniture; staff and put-away rituals. Conservatory / sunroom as glazed indoor climate with plants.

## American porch and patio

- American front porch as a 19th–early-20th-century social room (roof, draft, street). Screened porch later. Air conditioning and the ranch backyard split porch furniture from patio furniture.
- Thomas Lee, Westport, New York, 1903: plank lawn chair. Harry C. Bunnell, U.S. patent 794,777 (11 July 1905), "Chair" (Westport chair). Later popular name: Adirondack. Eleven-board lore is shop talk around the early examples — confirm a specific chair before counting slats on camera.
- Gustav Stickley / American Craftsman: indoor oak honesty; porch settles as room furniture under a roof. "Mission outdoor" as a stain name on aluminum is a later theft.
- Cyrus Wakefield; Heywood Brothers; Heywood-Wakefield: rattan and wicker porch industry, Massachusetts and beyond. Catalog furniture, paint, seasonal storage.
- Marshall B. Lloyd, Menominee, Michigan, 1917: woven paper-on-wire fabric (Lloyd Loom). American rights associated with Heywood-Wakefield; British/European production continues the name. Paper drinks; regularity is the visual tell.
- Postwar aluminum patio: Brown Jordan founded 1945 (Robert Brown, Hubert Jordan, California). Tropitone mid-1950s. Woodard (Owosso, Michigan) as a 20th-century iron/mesh name that also had to live with aluminum. Strap, sling, stack. Gauge separates the named lines from department-store cousins.

## Woods

- Teak (*Tectona grandis*): natural oils, silica (tool dulling, dust caution). Ship-deck use. English garden makers (Barlow Tyrie from 1920; Lister among bench makers) as garden borrowers of a naval tree. Bare teak silvers; "teak oil" cans are often not the tree. Plantation vs older stock is a grade conversation; legality/FSC are papers, not a sit.
- Western red cedar (*Thuja plicata*): thujaplicins in heartwood. Soft, dentable. Smell is not a grade. Sapwood vs heartwood matters.
- Bald cypress (*Taxodium distichum*): Southern boat and tank reputation; outdoor carpentry cousin to cedar. Eastern red cedar is a juniper — do not mash the names.
- Ipe (Handroanthus / Tabebuia group, "Brazilian walnut"): very high Janka, silica, decking fame. Hardness is not a hinge. Pre-drill. Watch wide glue-ups. Name/legality skepticism on magic prices.
- Oak: white oak heartwood tyloses vs red oak open vessels. Barrel and boat history. Tannin + iron = black ink stains. Indoor quartersawn flake is a sawing choice, not a raincoat.
- Pressure-treated: CCA residential phaseout EPA (end of 2003 / 2004). Successor copper systems (ACQ, copper azole, etc.) harder on some fasteners — hot-dip galvanized or stainless rated for the treatment. Wet-from-the-cylinder stock warps; not furniture-grade by default.

## Metals, films, woven, other

- Cast iron vs wrought iron vs mild steel: wrought as historic low-carbon slag-bearing iron, now scarce at patio prices; most "wrought" is bent welded mild steel. Cast = poured, heavy, ornament, snap risk, paint historically.
- Aluminum: no red rust coin; fatigue; heat; cast vs tube; stacking ovaling cheap tube.
- Powder coat: electrostatic powder, bake. Prep, mils, chalk, chips at edges and stack faces. Film, not metal.
- Rattan: climbing palm; poles and peel (cane). Thirsty. Porch/indoor.
- Wicker: method of weaving, any fiber.
- Resin / HDPE "all-weather wicker": polymer skin on (ideally) aluminum. UV chalk and fade. Say polymer.
- Sling / mesh: drain as the job; corner abrasion; textile UV calendars (Phifer and others are industry names — verify hours on a current card, do not invent them).
- Stone and concrete: leave-it; thermal mass; freeze; stone span; concrete cover over reinforcement; salt.

## Shop practice (not code)

- Three climates: roofed porch, open patio/deck, open lawn. "Outdoor" is a department.
- Typical indoor dining height (~29–30 inches) does not match a deep Adirondack sit. Do not pair them.
- Fasteners: stainless family (304 common; 316 near salt — still not a sermon); galvanic pairs; no interior drywall screws.
- Winter: leave-it vs bring-in; covers as greenhouses; storage path.
- Local building, accessibility, and structural rules win over a furniture essay. A foundry bench on a balcony is an engineer, not a style name.
