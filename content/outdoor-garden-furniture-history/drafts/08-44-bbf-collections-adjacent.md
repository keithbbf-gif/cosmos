A neighbor is not a costume.

This shop builds indoor furniture. Tables, casegoods, chairs that live in rooms with ceilings. The work leaves Ferdinand on our trucks and often meets a client through a designer showroom. That is the business. This folder is not a back-door garden catalog. It is a set of educational scripts so the words around sitting outside get their jobs back — and so a collection page that sits next to this history cannot lie about climate.

Adjacent, in the way we have used it on this channel, means the duties transfer. A joint should not be a cistern. A finish is a system. A stolen word is how a room gets the wrong object. A photograph is not a specification. We already fight those fights indoors. Outdoor is the louder exam. Teaching the exam does not mean we poured a fern-backed bench in the back bay.

What a collection page may do:

Show a bench, a dining form, a chair, and let this series teach the century that produced the form.
Use porch as a word only when the roof is real.
Refuse teak, wrought, all-weather, and patio set as decorations on an indoor piece.
Send a reader to a draft when they want the weather version of the duty.

What a collection page may not do:

Invent a garden SKU, a lead time, or a warranty this shop does not print.
Caption an indoor dining table on a slab as proof the table is a patio table.
Call a stain teak.
Call a powder-coat-brown aluminum chair Mission.
Call a resin lounge Heywood.

I have watched indoor work get hurt by outdoor staging. A beautiful film, a west sun, a sprinkler, a weekend. The repair is possible and the story is stupid. If we stage near glass or a porch, we say so. If we do not say so, the staging is a lie. This series exists so the lie has less room.

Refusal is part of adjacency. Some rooms should not get a desk. Some slabs should not get a sectional. Some clients should not get teak if what they want is a brown that never changes. Saying no is cheaper than a March argument. Collections can still show the object. The essay can still say no. That pairing is the point.

Keith can still walk a patio with a designer and talk species, film, and refusal. That walk is not a product launch. It is the same walk we do in a dining room when the window is wrong. Adjacent is the walk. The carton is someone else's aisle unless we decide, in public, to build an outdoor object and put our name on the ticket. Until that ticket exists, the name on the door stays indoor.

If you only have twenty minutes in this folder, read the stolen-words draft, the three-climates draft, and the read-the-patio draft. If you have an afternoon, start at the weather problem and keep walking. The garden will still be there in ten years. The object you put in it should earn the weather it occupies.

I have been in showrooms where a beautiful indoor table was staged with a garden view behind glass, and a shopper thought they could buy the view. They can buy the table. They cannot buy the glass climate unless they have the glass. Our job, on this channel, is to keep the glass and the slab from sharing a caption. Shared captions are how good indoor work gets a bad outdoor year and then a quiet complaint that never names the climate. Name the climate. Keep the neighbor neighborly.

I will not close with a sale. I will close with a boundary and an offer of sense. We will build you the indoor piece as well as we can. We will tell you the truth about the outdoor cousin. The truth is the adjacent product. The rest is rain.
