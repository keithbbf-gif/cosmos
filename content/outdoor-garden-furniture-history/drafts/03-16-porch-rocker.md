A chair that moves is doing a job a still chair cannot fake with a cushion.

The American porch rocker is a work chair for heat. You rock and you make a little air. You rock and the conversation has a tempo. You rock and a baby goes down, or a stubborn adult pretends they are not waiting for someone on the walk. The form is older than the porch — Windsor cousins, Boston rockers, bentwood — but the porch made it civic. A still parlor chair on a porch looks like it got evicted. A rocker looks hired.

Runners are the honesty test. They have to be long enough not to dump you and not so long they trip the path. They have to be hard enough to take grit on a painted floor. They will wear the paint. They will be worn by the paint. Sand is a cutting compound. I have seen pretty maple runners go oval in a season because the porch was a construction site and nobody swept. If you want a show wood runner, sweep. If you will not sweep, paint the runner and admit it is a shoe.

Splay and back angle are comfort, not style names. A porch sit is longer than a dining sit. The American rocker often has a seat that is not trying to look slim in a catalog. Good. Slim is how you get a person perched for a photograph and gone in ten minutes. I would rather see a slightly homely rocker that holds a body than a "porch collection" dining chair with rockers glued on as an afterthought. Those afterthoughts lever joints they were not drawn for.

Wood. Painted hardwoods. Sometimes oak. Sometimes cheaper pines that need the paint. Outdoor-adjacent, not patio-native. The roof is still doing the work. If you put a heirloom rocker on an open deck because it "looks cottage," you are using a moving chair as a wet sculpture. The runners will take water and then take freeze. The crest will take sun. The chair will rock less and complain more.

I am not going to give you a brand genealogy as if one factory invented restlessness. I am going to tell you the shop failures I know. Loose stretchers from seasonal movement. Runners that delaminate if they were laminated cheaply. Arms that get wet hands all summer and then get a finish the indoor dining table would have survived. Design the wear patches. The front of the arm is a handle.

This shop builds indoor chairs that do not rock unless the room asked. Adjacent means I still know when a chair is a machine. A rocker is a machine. A patio dining chair is a different machine. Do not order one drawing for both. BBF collections can show a still chair. This essay sits beside it so nobody bolts a runner on in a caption.

A porch that is also a walkway will punish a long runner. I have seen people clip the same rocker every time they carry groceries because the chair was placed for the photograph from the street, not for the body with the bags. Place the machine for the work. The street can still see you. It does not need the runner in the path.

If you make furniture, watch a person get out of the rocker. The arm and the runner have to allow a stand on a narrow porch without a lunge into the rail. If you buy furniture, sit and rock and then walk past it with a grocery bag. If you clip the bag, the runner is too long for the room. If you are a designer, do not line four rockers on a five-foot porch and call it hospitality. Call it an obstacle course with nostalgia.

The next American plank is even more famous and even more lied about. Westport, 1903. Eleven boards. Not a lifestyle. A chair.
