A die can imitate a vine. The sun will still find the polymer.

All-weather wicker — HDPE or a cousin, extruded, sometimes with a texture printed into the strand — is one of the more honest modern patio inventions if you let it be plastic. A powder-coated aluminum frame. A weave that does not mildew like cane. A chair you can leave if the frame drains and the feet are sane. That is a real assignment. I would rather see that on an Indiana slab than a historic rattan set "just this season" that becomes a compostable sculpture.

The dishonesty is the memory. The strand is trying to look like rattan. The marketing is trying to sound like a porch. Then the client expects the patina of a vine and gets the patina of a polymer: fade, chalk, a strand that flattens, a color that goes dusty on the south face and stays richer on the north. People call that a defect. It is ultraviolet doing work. Better resins take longer. Worse resins take a year. Price is a clue, not a proof.

Heat. A dark weave on a tube can soften if the formulation is cheap and the August is not. I have seen weaves that took a set from a heavy pot. I have seen weaves that got brittle in a cold snap after they had already chalked. Plastic has a temperature graph. Wood has a different graph. Do not mix the expectations.

The frame is still the structure. If the aluminum is thin, the weave is jewelry on a noodle. If the weave hides a steel frame that rusts, you have bought a rust surprise inside a plastic basket. Flip the chair. Look for rust bleed at the feet. Look for a frame that is actually aluminum.

Repair is ugly. You do not reweave resin the way you reweave cane in a dining chair. You replace a section badly or you replace the chair. Design for that economy. A household that wants heirloom weaving wants a different fiber and a roof.

Color names are a trap. "Driftwood" on a polymer is a pigment load. Some pigments take ultraviolet better. I will not recite a pigment lecture. I will say: ask for a strand left in sun, not only a binder of chips in a conference room. The conference room is kind. The slab is not.

This shop will not sell you a resin lounge as a Fritz piece. Adjacent means I can say when a plastic skin is the right climate choice. Indoor collections should not steal the texture as if it were a craft veneer. If we ever show a woven indoor panel, it is cane or a named craft, not a patio extrusion.

Strands flatten at the sit and at the wrap over a sharp frame edge. A sharp frame edge is a design error. Radius the rail that the polymer turns over. Indoor cane already taught us not to cut the fiber on a hard corner. The die-made strand deserves the same courtesy. If the rail is a knife, the weather will finish the cut.

If you make furniture, specify the strand material and the UV package, not only the color name. If you buy furniture, rub the south-facing strand of a floor sample. Chalk on your fingers is a preview. If you are a designer, write polymer and aluminum if that is what you mean. Save wicker for when you mean the method and you are ready to name the fiber.

Lloyd Loom is the twentieth-century plot twist: paper on a wire, a loom, a chair that looked like weave and was a factory idea. It still gets left in the rain by accident.
