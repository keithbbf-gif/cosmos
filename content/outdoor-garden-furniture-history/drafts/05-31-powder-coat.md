The color is a crust.

Powder coat got popular because it is tougher than a lot of wet paint in the places factories care about: even-ish coverage, a bake, fewer solvent conversations, a finish you can ship. Outdoor furniture leaned on it. I will use it as a word of respect when the prep was real — clean metal, good pretreatment, enough mils, a cure that happened. I will use it as a word of suspicion when the chair is a year old and the coat is already mapping every weld like a topographic survey.

Chalking is ultraviolet eating the binder. The color goes dusty. Dark colors show it. You can sometimes clean a chalk and live with it. You cannot wipe a chip back on. A chip on steel is a rust seed. A chip on aluminum is an oxidation seed and a snag. Edges chip first. So do feet. So do stacking faces. Design the abuse or specify a film that was meant for it.

Prep is the religion. Oil left on a tube becomes a fish-eye. A weld left dirty becomes a rust bloom under a skin that looked perfect on day one. I have more trust in a slightly imperfect wet-paint job that a person maintains than in a perfect powder coat on dirty metal. The bake does not forgive a lie. It entombs it.

Touch-up. You will not perfectly match a baked powder with a rattle can. You can protect a chip. Say that to the client before the first truck. Indoor, we already have this conversation about film finishes. Outdoor, the sun and the stacking make it louder. Adjacent knowledge: a finish is a system, not a color chip.

Texture hides sins and dirt. Smooth high-gloss shows everything and can be a better wipe. Neither is a climate. I like a texture on a garden table if the household will not wipe pollen every morning. I like a smooth rail if someone is going to rest an arm in July and I do not want grit.

Salt and pool chemistry are accelerants. A coat that is fine in a backyard in Ferdinand can look tired faster near a chlorinated splash or a coastal fog. I will not invent a lab hour. I will tell you to tell the finisher the site. Site is part of the spec. "Outdoor" is not a site. A pool deck is a site. A screened porch is a site.

This shop's indoor films are not powder coat. I will not pretend our conversion varnish is a patio answer. BBF collections should not caption an indoor sheen as powder-coat tough. Different systems. Different exams. The essay exists so the word film stays attached to the coat.

I have watched people try to "refresh" a chalked powder with furniture wax. Wax is a dirt magnet outdoors and a lie about what died. If the coat chalked, you are in clean-and-live or recoat-in-a-booth territory. A booth is a metal shop. A rag is not a booth. Adjacent indoor knowledge: we already refuse miracle rags on a failed dining film. The patio does not get a miracle rag either.

If you make furniture, specify mils and prep like you specify a species. If you buy furniture, look at feet and the underside of arms. If you are a designer, do not pick a color only from a screen. Pick it from a metal coupon left in the actual sun for a week if the job is large. The screen is a liar. The sun is not.
