The party went around back, and the roof did not always follow.

A ranch house with a sliding door onto a slab is a different social machine than a street porch. Privacy from the street. A grill. Kids in the yard. The neighbor is a fence, not a sidewalk conversation. After the war, and after air conditioning, that machine won a lot of American evenings. The furniture had to change. Wicker attics could not keep up with a room that was now the size of a living room and open to the sky.

Patio is a Spanish floor word that Americans used for a concrete idea. Deck is a lumber idea that wanted to be a patio and a dock at once. Both are unroofed or half-roofed. Both get called outdoor living rooms by people who would not leave a sofa in a driveway. I have been in those rooms. They can be wonderful in September. They are a materials exam in February and in a Midwestern July storm.

The living-room ask is the problem. Deep seats. Sectionals. Rugs. Coffee tables at indoor heights. Televisions under a cover that looks like a barbecue shroud. Each of those objects has an indoor cousin that already struggles with crumbs and sun in a sunroom. On a patio they need a different construction or a ritual that most households will skip after year two. I would rather see two good chairs and a table that drains than a sectional that becomes a wet loaf.

Rugs outdoors are a special foolishness I will name. They hold water against a deck. They mildew. They trip a person carrying a platter. If you need a rug to make the slab feel like a room, you may need a roof more than you need a rug. I have pulled more than one sodden outdoor rug off boards that were trying to rot in the dark. The rug was the fashion. The boards were the furniture of the house.

The front porch did not die everywhere. It died as a default. You can still sit on one. You should, if you have one. The furniture history just split. Porch objects and patio objects are not interchangeable. Retail mashed them because the aisle was one aisle. One aisle is why Adirondacks ended up at dining tables and why wicker ended up under snow.

Decks add fasteners and ventilation. A chair on a deck can stain the boards. A metal foot can make a rust coin. A covered grill can still dump grease on a teak slat. The patio is a workshop. Design for splash from the hose, grease from the cooking, and a sliding door that is trying to be a wall.

This shop's indoor dining tables sometimes get photographed near a window that looks at a patio. That is adjacent, not a specification. I will build you the indoor table. I will not pretend it is the patio table unless we change the brief — species, film, or refusal. BBF collections should not launder a slab into a dining room with a caption. The essay is here so the caption has to work.

If you make furniture, ask whether the door is a slider. Sliders want a clear swing of furniture that is not a swing — a path. If you buy furniture, furnish the patio for the three months you will actually use the deep seats, and furnish the rest of the year for what you will leave. If you are a designer, do not copy the living-room plan onto the slab and change the fabric code. Change the object list.

Aluminum is waiting. Aircraft habits, California names, a chair you can stack. That is the metal the patio deserved and then overbought.
