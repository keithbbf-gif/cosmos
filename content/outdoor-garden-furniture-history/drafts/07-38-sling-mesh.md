Water should fall through you and keep going.

A sling seat — a textile in a track or laced to a frame — is one of the smarter patio ideas of the last seventy years. Mesh metal seats — Woodard cousins, expanded metal, wire — are an older and a parallel idea. Both refuse the slat tray. After rain you can sit sooner. After a spill you are not sponging a cushion. That is hospitality that does not require a speech.

Tension is the sit. Too loose and you are in a bag. Too tight and the rail takes a load it was not drawn for, or the fabric tears at a corner. The corners are where I look. Abrasion against a metal eyelet. A track that cut the edge. A cheap sling is a rectangle that was never patterned for the frame. A good sling looks like it was born there.

Ultraviolet. Outdoor textiles have hour ratings I am not going to invent on camera. Phifer and others made names in this world. The names are not the point. The point is: the fabric is a sacrificial skin with a calendar. The frame can outlast it. Design for a reskin or admit you bought a disposable chair. I respect both if they are labeled.

Mesh metal gets hot and can print a pattern on a bare thigh. It can rust if it is the wrong metal or a tired coat. It can be superb in a wind because it does not sail like a solid. Balconies like mesh. Cushions on mesh are optional, not the seat. If you need the cushion to sit, you have a different object.

Dining on a sling is a different brief than lounging on a sling. A dining sling can be firmer and still drain. A lounge sling can be a hammock if you let it. Do not buy one tension for both and then complain that salad and sunset disagree. They always disagreed. The Adirondack already taught that. The sling just made the lesson textile.

Indoor, we do not sling dining chairs as a house habit. Adjacent is the idea of a seat as a replaceable skin — cane, leather, a slip. The patio just makes the skin more honest about rain. BBF collections should not call a cane seat a sling. Different tracks. Different centuries.

I like a sling because it tells the truth about water. I dislike a sling that was specified so the stylist could skip the cushion conversation and then the client adds a cushion anyway and we are back to foam on a tray that was designed not to be a tray. If you add the cushion, you add the ritual. The sling cannot save you from a textile you piled on top of it. The drain is still there. The loaf is now on the drain.

If you make furniture, pattern the sling. If you buy furniture, sit, twist, look at the corners. If you are a designer, specify whether the evening is bare-leg or clothed, because mesh plus July is a review you can predict.

A sling that has already bagged will bag more. You cannot tension a tired textile back to new without a new textile. Budget the reskin when you budget the frame, or buy a cheaper frame and admit the calendar. I respect a cheap honest calendar. I do not respect a luxury frame price on a sling that cannot be replaced.

Cushions are the last fake furniture. They are textile and foam. They are not the chair. Treating them as the chair is how patios become landfills with zippers.
