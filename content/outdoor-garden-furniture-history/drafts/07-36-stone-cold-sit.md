If you cannot leave it, you bought the wrong mineral.

Stone is the oldest outdoor sit that still shows up to the meeting. Marble, granite, limestone, sandstone, a slab on blocks. The virtue is the ritual you do not do. No attic. No oil can. No stacking. The vice is the sit. Stone takes the temperature of the week. April in Indiana is a dare. August is a griddle if the bench is in the sun and dark. People who grew up with wooden porches are surprised by this every time, as if physics were a vendor error.

Span. A thin slab on widely set legs is a break in waiting. Historic slabs that last are thick enough or supported enough. Cultured products — concrete wearing a stone face, or a wafer of stone on a steel frame — have their own calendars. I will not call a wafer a villa bench. I will call it a tabletop trying to be geology.

Freeze. Any stone that can hold water in a crack can lose a lip in a winter. Limestones and some sandstones are more conversational with freeze than a tight granite. I am not your geologist. I am the person who tells you not to let a basin sit in a carved seat over January. Drain. Or take the bowl in. The Chinese drum stool already taught that.

Stain. Leaves, iron, oil from a grill, berries. Porous stones blot. Dense stones blot less and still take rust from a cheap table next door. If the household wants a white marble garden seat under an oak, they want a staining hobby. Say it.

Weight and pad. Stone on a lawn sinks. Stone on a roof is an engineer. Stone on a wood deck can print a dent and a moisture trap. Put it on something that can take it. This is not fussy. This is how you do not buy a second bench to replace the porch board.

Arms on a stone bench are architecture. They are also a place a person will put a sweating glass. Marble will take a ring if the stone is open enough. Granite may shrug. Do not promise a kitchen-counter performance on a garden mineral you have not named. Name the stone or skip the promise.

This shop does not quarry. Adjacent means placement and honesty, the same as a heavy indoor case on an old floor. BBF collections do not get a marble garden caption on a wood bench. If we show stone, it is a top we actually set, indoors, with a pad and a climate.

I have asked clients to sit on a step in April before they commit to a stone bench for morning coffee. The ones who last five minutes can have the geology. The ones who last thirty seconds want wood under a roof and they should not be talked out of it by a villa photograph. A villa had a different morning and often a different staff. Your morning is the brief. The photograph is a suggestion that has to pass the sit.

If you make furniture, do not fake stone in foam and paint for outdoor use and then act shocked. If you buy furniture, sit in the real weather before you pay for geology. If you are a designer, write the sit temperature into the concept like a grown-up, or add a roof, or add wood.

Foam-and-paint stone is a parade float. Rain finds the seam. Then you have a soggy interior and a crust. That is not geology. That is a prop that stayed after the party. Props are allowed. They are not benches.

Concrete tried to be the democratic stone of the twentieth-century garden. Sometimes it was. Sometimes it was a spall.
