A board that shrugs off a dent will still lose a screw argument.

Ipe is the patio's favorite flex. Janka numbers that make oak look polite. Color like a dark honey that will still silver if you leave it. Silica again, worse for tools than teak on a bad day. Decking made it famous in the United States. Furniture followed because if it can be a walking surface it can be a seat — which is true until you try to cut a clean mortise or drive a fastener without a plan.

Hardness is a tax. It taxes bits, planes, saws, and the person who has to lift the table off the truck. It taxes the joint if you designed a pine joint and swapped the species on a whim. Dense woods can be brittle in ways softwoods are not. They can split if you do not pre-drill. They can starve a glue line if you think like interior maple and do not respect how little they want to drink.

People treat ipe like plastic that grew. It is a tree. It moves less than some, more than a rumor. It weathers. It can check on a wide unrelieved top the way any dark dense slab can check. Oil rituals exist here too, with the same sticky failures. I would rather see ipe left to silver on a well-detailed bench than oiled into a dust magnet.

Legality and names. Ipe is a group, not a pet. Papers matter. I will not recite a customs lecture I am not qualified to close. I will say: if the price is magic, the name may be magic too. Adjacent indoor work has already taught this shop to be suspicious of bargain exotics. The patio does not get a discount on suspicion.

Fasteners. Stainless or a system rated for the density and the weather. Hidden deck clips are a deck language. Furniture wants joints. If your ipe "furniture" is a bunch of deck boards screwed to a frame, you have built a sitting deck. Fine, if you say so. Not fine if you invoiced a table.

Dust again. Silica and dense tropicals are not a weekend-without-a-mask hobby. If you cut ipe, treat the dust as a shop problem, not as perfume. I say that because homeowners now buy decking and then decide to "just make a bench" with a circular saw on the driveway. The driveway is not a shop. The lungs are still lungs.

This shop is not an ipe patio factory. We know dense tropicals from indoor work. We know when a client is buying a number to feel safe. Safety is detailing and fasteners and a realistic top width. The Janka score does not attend the punch list. BBF collections should not use ipe as a macho caption on an indoor walnut piece. Different trees. Different rooms.

Color movement: ipe can go from brown to silver like teak, and households who bought it for the brown will call the silver a defect. Write the future color on the ticket. If they want brown forever, they want a ritual or a different object. If they want a deck-hard sit they can leave, they can have silver. I will not let a Janka number hide that conversation. Hardness does not lock a hue.

If you make furniture, pre-drill as if you mean to keep the board. If you buy furniture, lift the end. Density is a feel. If it feels like pine and says ipe, believe the feel. If you are a designer, do not specify a six-board-wide glue-up like an indoor slab and then act shocked at a check. Relieve it, breadboard it with a method that can take weather, or make slats.

Oak is the European and American outdoor wood that people think they already know from dining rooms. Outdoors it is a different oak, or it is a short story.
