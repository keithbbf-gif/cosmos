# Custom furniture production — market landscape (online / DTC + Southeast Arkansas)

**Date researched:** 14 September 2026  
**Scope:** National online / made-to-order furniture and cabinetry, plus the local production landscape around Monticello and Southeast Arkansas (SE AR).  
**Purpose:** Opportunity-focused brief for a small custom shop or online maker. Isolated from COSMOS product code.

**Not legal, tax, or investment advice.** Licensing notes cite public Arkansas contractor rules only; confirm with the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board and counsel before operating.

---

## How to read this brief

| Confidence | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **High** | Government series, first-party company sites, dated local/state press |
| **Medium** | Industry surveys, BBB profiles, recent social posts with a named place |
| **Low / soft** | Paid market-research landing pages, scraped business directories, undated aggregator “revenue” fields |

**Rules used here**

- Shop names appear only when a source was found. Directory-only names are labeled **unverified / possibly stale**. Directory employee counts and revenues are **not** treated as facts.
- “Custom furniture” market-size estimates from commercial research houses **disagree by a factor of ~3–4**. Prefer Census manufacturing shipments, BLS employment, and Harvard remodeling series over those estimates.
- Local new-construction demand in Drew County is tiny on official permit counts. Remodel, replacement, commercial, and shipped goods matter more than “new subdivision kitchens.”

**Radius used for “local”:** roughly 75 miles / 90 minutes of Monticello — Drew, Bradley, Ashley, Lincoln, Desha, Chicot, Cleveland, and Jefferson counties, plus El Dorado (Union) and Monroe / West Monroe, Louisiana as the nearest larger millwork cluster.

## 1. Executive takeaways

1. **The national furniture market is large and slow-growing; “custom” is a fuzzy slice.** Census seasonally adjusted furniture-and-related-products shipments ran about **$7.4–$7.8 billion per month** in 2024–mid-2026. Separate “U.S. custom furniture” figures of $8–$35 billion in 2024 are **not reconcilable** and should not be used as a TAM without a definition.
2. **Kitchen and bath cabinets are the most credible local money**, not Instagram dining tables. Cabinets are commonly cited as ~**29–40%** of a kitchen remodel. Houzz’s 2025 study (fielded on 2024 projects) still shows major kitchen work holding at high medians even as overall renovation spend cooled.
3. **Monticello already has a factory-custom *commercial* furniture maker (Akin) and a national woodworking *content* brand (731 Woodworks).** It does **not** have a clearly documented, web-visible residential custom-furniture showroom. Several cabinet-shop names exist only in directories.
4. **Drew County’s housing engine is remodel + jobs, not permits.** Official private housing permits: **13 (2024) → 4 (2025)**. Median household income is **$42,824** (ACS 2020–2024). A full custom kitchen is a stretch purchase for a typical household; contractor-grade semi-custom, vanities, built-ins, and commercial casework fit the income and job mix better.
5. **Industrial investment is real and nearby** (Weyerhaeuser TimberStrand ~$500 million; Georgia-Pacific Crossett $90 million; UAM Forest Health Research Center). That can lift commercial interiors and some housing — it also competes for trades labor.
6. **National online-only custom sofas are a poor SE AR startup bet.** Wayfair Custom Upholstery, Interior Define, BenchMade Modern, Joybird, and modular DTC (Burrow, Floyd, Cozey, Lovesac) own that channel. Freight and upholstery labor are the killers.
7. **Best-fit hypotheses (ranked below):** (1) contractor-allied cabinets / vanities / built-ins; (2) shipping-aware small or knock-down wood goods sold online; (3) commercial casework around institutions and new plants; (4) mid-ticket solid-wood pieces with *regional* delivery, not coast-to-coast white-glove.

## 2. Category map

Think in **who specifies**, **how unique the piece is**, and **how it ships**.

| Category | What it is | Typical buyer | Spec / uniqueness | Ship / install | SE AR note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ready-made / stock** | Factory SKUs, big-box and regional retailers | Homeowners, rental flippers | Low | Parcel, LTL, or store truck | Beer’s Hometown and Queen City already occupy this |
| **Semi-custom / factory-custom** | Catalog silhouette + size/fabric/finish options | Homeowners, designers | Medium | LTL + white-glove common | National DTC sofas; Wayfair / Ferguson cabinets |
| **Artisan one-off** | Unique design, often hardwood | Affluent homeowners, gifts | High | Local delivery or freight quote | Thin public presence in Monticello |
| **Cabinet shop (residential)** | Kitchens, baths, laundry, closets | Homeowners + GCs | High fit, repeatable boxes | Install on site | Names exist; few modern websites |
| **Architectural millwork / casework** | Trim, reception, nurse stations, offices | GCs, institutions | Spec-driven | Jobsite install, often bonded | Nabholz-class shops are in Central AR, not SE AR |
| **Hospitality / senior-living furniture** | Durability + cleanability + FF&E packages | Operators, designers, GSA | Spec + custom | Truckload / project freight | **Akin Complete Furniture is local** |
| **Outdoor / porch / camp** | Weathered hardwood, poly lumber | Homeowners, camps, lake houses | Medium | Often local; freight if KD | Climate and lake/hunt culture fit; no verified specialist found |
| **Upholstery / reupholstery** | New or recovered seating | Homeowners, hospitality | High skill | Local; bulky | Statewide upholsterer count is small (BLS) |
| **CNC / kit / RTA** | Nesting plywood, IKEA-box + custom doors | DIY, small GCs | Configurable | **Parcel / UPS** — the shipping win | Semihandmade model; no local IKEA |
| **Component shop** | Doors, drawer boxes, slabs | Other shops, GCs | Repeatable | Freight-friendly stacks | Possible B2B if CNC is bought |

**Custom vs ready-made is a spectrum**, not two markets:

- **Stock:** Home Depot / Lowe’s / IKEA / Ashley — weeks, not months.
- **Semi-custom:** Width increments, door styles, stain colors (cabinet brands; Wayfair “Dwelling” cabinet ladder).
- **Made-to-order DTC:** Fixed frames, many SKUs of fabric (Joybird, Interior Define).
- **True custom:** Any dimension, mixed materials, site measure, shop drawings.
- **Factory-custom contract:** Akin-style — custom *within* a hospitality catalog and compliance stack.

**Artisan vs factory-custom**

| | Artisan / bench | Factory-custom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit economics | High margin, low volume | Lower margin, scheduled batches |
| Bottleneck | Finisher + joiner skill | CNC, finishing line, QC |
| Sales | Referral, Instagram, Etsy | Reps, Houzz, designer accounts |
| Failure mode | Owner is the shop | Overhead if demand dips |

A SE AR startup that tries to be both a YouTube brand and a full kitchen shop usually fails at cash and lead time. Pick a **primary production system** (cabinet boxes vs solid-wood casegoods vs upholstery vs CNC kits).

## 3. Online / national DTC landscape

### 3.1 Positioning clusters (notable names — not exhaustive)

**Mass marketplace / omnichannel**

- **Wayfair** — huge assortment plus **Wayfair Custom Upholstery** (sofas, sectionals, chairs, ottomans in house fabrics) and a **designer-assisted custom cabinet** path under the Dwelling / Kindwood ladder (value → full-custom / RTA). Quality is supplier-dependent; this is the price-and-selection gravity well.
- **Build.com → Ferguson Home** (announced February 2025, *Kitchen & Bath Design News*, 19 March 2025) — contractor-grade plumbing, lighting, and **stock / built-in cabinetry** (e.g. Maplevilles, Sunny Wood, Design House), not handmade furniture. Relevant as the *channel contractors already use*, not as a peer custom shop.

**Design-forward ready-made (Article-adjacent)**

- **Article** — Vancouver DTC, curated modern/Scandinavian SKUs, in-house last-mile (ADT) cited as a differentiator (Cylindo brand roundup). Adjacent to custom only in *aesthetic*; not a true custom shop.
- **Castlery**, **AllModern / Joss & Main** (Wayfair family) — same “good design, finite SKUs” lane.

**Configurable / made-to-order upholstery (the “custom sofa” internet)**

- **Interior Define** (2014; acquired by **Havenly** in 2023 after post-pandemic fulfillment distress) — fabrics, sizes, legs; studios + virtual design; lead times often cited in the **6–14 week** range (comparison sites; confirm on current PDP).
- **BenchMade Modern** — U.S. production (LA / Dallas commonly cited); deeper customization; higher price (reviewers cite **$3.5k+** sofas); shorter lead times sometimes claimed (**2–6 weeks**).
- **Joybird** — mid-century, many fabrics; **acquired by La-Z-Boy (2018)** — factory-custom with a DTC face.
- **Inside Weather** — high configuration count, made-to-order / low inventory (Cylindo).

**Modular / ship-in-boxes (the freight workaround)**

- **Burrow**, **Floyd**, **Cozey**, **Lovesac Sactional**, **Albany Park** — tool-free or boxed modules. This is the winning *operations* pattern for national DTC, not the winning *craft* pattern.

**Artisan marketplaces**

- **Etsy** — handmade / vintage / supplies. Official help: for **very heavy items such as furniture**, tell buyers shipping varies, quote, then use a **custom listing**. Transaction fee **6.5% of order including shipping**; listing and payments fees extra ([Etsy Help — fees](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014483627-What-are-the-Fees-and-Taxes-for-Selling-on-Etsy); [shipping](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014115187-How-to-Set-Up-Shipping-Information)).
- **Chairish**, **1stDibs** — higher design-buyer fees (third-party guides often cite ~20–25%; **verify current seller terms**). Better for vintage/unique than production runs.
- **Facebook Marketplace / Craigslist** — local, low fee, price-sensitive; good for remnants and pickup-only slabs.

**Regional makers who ship**

- National “made in USA hardwood” brands (example first-party terms: Adams Furniture **50% deposit**, ~**6–8 week** ship, 1-year defect warranty; Swiss Valley Furniture **50% custom deposit**, **8–14 week** build). These are the *terms* a SE AR shipper will be compared against, not local competitors.
- **Semihandmade** — U.S.-built doors/fronts for **IKEA Sektion / Akurum** (and their own BOXI boxes). Proof that **shipping doors and fronts** beats shipping assembled kitchens.

**Luxury / showroom-custom (context only)**

- Restoration Hardware, Room & Board, West Elm, Design Within Reach, The Citizenry — high photography, long lead times, white-glove. A Monticello shop will not out-brand them; it can out-*fit* a specific room if the customer can visit.

### 3.2 Pricing / positioning patterns

| Lane | Sofa / seating (informal buyer-guide bands) | Cabinets (installed, widely republished ranges) | Promise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget marketplace | Under ~$1,000 | Stock ~$100–$300 / linear ft | Fast, replaceable |
| Mid DTC | ~$1,200–$2,500 | Semi-custom ~$150–$650 / lf | Style + some options |
| Custom / USA bench | ~$3,000–$6,000+ | Custom ~$500–$1,200+ / lf | Fit, wood, longevity |

Cabinet dollar ranges: Kitchen Cabinet Kings guide (updated for 2025 planning; cites Houzz kitchen-trend medians). **Treat per-linear-foot bands as planning ranges, not quotes.** Rural SE AR install labor is usually cheaper than coastal metros; **material and hardware are national**.

**Positioning that actually converts online**

1. **Configurator + swatches** (Interior Define model) — reduces return fear.
2. **Photographed in real rooms**, not only white sweep.
3. **Lead time honesty** — 8–16 weeks is normal; “2 weeks” is stock.
4. **Shipping as a product** — free threshold freight, or boxed modules, or local pickup. Hidden $400 freight at checkout kills conversion.
5. **Deposit + non-refundable custom** — 50% / 50% is the shop standard (multiple first-party terms).

### 3.3 Fulfillment and shipping constraints

Furniture is in the **big-and-bulky** last-mile economy, not parcel e-commerce.

- Armstrong & Associates (with National Home Delivery Association), 2025 study: U.S. 3PL **big-and-bulky last-mile** market **$10.15 billion in 2024**; **furniture 30.2%** of commodity mix (~**$3.06 billion**); appliances 40.6%. CAGR **11.4% (2017–2024)**; estimated **7.2% (2024–2026)**. Growth slowed in 2024.
- Practitioner freight bands (AtoShip, Kashew, International Van Lines 2026 guide — **not tariffs**):
  - Single-item **LTL:** often **$200–$800**
  - Cross-country sofa: often **$300–$700**
  - **White-glove** (room of choice, unpack, sometimes assemble): **$400–$3,500+**
  - Accessorials (liftgate, residential, appointment, limited access) routinely add **$50–$200+** each
- **Rural SE AR is “limited access”** on many carrier tariffs. Outbound to a Brooklyn walk-up is expensive *and* high-claim. Inbound white-glove *to* Monticello is scarce compared with metros.
- **Parcel path** (under common 70 lb / 108 in limits): stools, benches in parts, cutting boards, small tables, cabinet *doors*, drawer boxes. This is how a SE AR maker competes nationally without a 3PL.
- **Damage and claims** dominate furniture DTC reviews. Blanket-wrap and white-glove exist because carton + LTL destroys finishes.

**Implication:** Design the *SKU* around the *lane*. A 9-foot walnut table is a local/regional product. A nested plywood desk or a pair of doors is a national product.

## 4. Market size, growth, and drivers

### 4.1 Prefer these official / academic series

**U.S. furniture *manufacturing* shipments (Census M3, FRED `A37SVS`)**  
Seasonally adjusted monthly value of shipments, furniture and related products:

| Period | Monthly range (SA, $ millions) | Rough annual sum of monthly SA values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2024 | 7,403–7,490 | ~**$89.3 billion** |
| 2025 | 7,489–7,734 | ~**$90.8 billion** |
| 2026 YTD (Jan–Jul) | 7,617–7,789 | run-rate still ~**$7.6–$7.8 billion / month** |

Summing seasonally adjusted months is an **approximation**, not a Census “annual total” publication. Directionally: shipments are **flat-to-up slightly** after the pandemic boom, not a hockey stick.

**U.S. furniture *retail / consumption* market (commercial syntheses — use as context only)**

- **Grand View Research:** U.S. furniture market **$180.55 billion (2025)** → **$292.26 billion (2033)**, CAGR **6.4% (2026–2033)**; residential **60.21%** of 2025. Cites ~**1.47 million** U.S. housing starts in 2024.
- **Mordor Intelligence:** **$193.60 billion (2025)** → **$232.61 billion (2030)**, CAGR **3.74%**. Home furniture **63.1%**. Notes post-pandemic demand tied again to **housing turnover**, not emergency home-office buys.

Those two “total market” numbers differ by ~$13 billion in the same year. **Soft.** They also include mattresses, imports, and retail markup that Census shipments do not.

**“Custom / customized furniture” — flag as soft**

| Source | Claim | Why it’s soft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DataHorizzon | U.S. *custom home furniture* **$8.2B (2024)** → **$13.7B (2033)**, CAGR **5.9%** | Paid report landing page; definition unclear; their own table also labels “North America” $8.2B |
| Deep Market Insights | U.S. *customized furniture* **$12.57B (2025)** → **$33.3B (2034)**, CAGR **11.46%** | Same problem; sofa/seating called largest segment |

**Do not average these.** They are different products wearing the same adjective.

**Remodeling (the demand that actually feeds cabinet shops)**

- Harvard JCHS **Leading Indicator of Remodeling Activity:** after two years of decline, owner-occupied improvement + repair spending projected **+1.2% in 2025**; remodeling market size projection raised to **$509 billion in 2025** (AHS rebasing; JCHS, 2025 LIRA write-up).
- Earlier 2024 LIRA: ~**$466 billion** annual rate through mid-2025.
- JCHS *Improving America’s Housing 2025:* broader remodeling (owners + rental) **above $600 billion** through 2025; 2019→2022 jump **$404B → $611B**. Labor shortages called out as a constraint.
- **Houzz**, “5 Home Renovation Spending Trends to Know About in 2025,” 22 April 2025 (2025 U.S. Houzz & Home Study, **>22,000** homeowners, **2024** projects):
  - Median renovation spend **$20,000** (2024) vs **$24,000** (2023)
  - Kitchen remodel median **$22,000** (2024)
  - Major kitchen ≥200 sq ft median **$55,000** (third year flat)
  - Major kitchen <200 sq ft median **$35,000** (**+9%** YoY)
  - 24% of renovating homeowners did a kitchen; baby boomers **59%** of renovators
  - Living-room / bedroom / home-office spend **fell** (home office median **−50%**)

**Kitchen cabinet share:** NKBA allocation widely republished as cabinets **~29%** of a kitchen budget (then labor, appliances, counters). WSJ Buy Side (2025 compilation) cites cabinets **30–40%**. Journal of Light Construction 2025 figures (via WSJ): minor kitchen ~**$28,458**; upscale major ~**$164,104**.

### 4.2 Materials cost

- BLS / FRED **PPI hardwood lumber (`WPU0812`)**, index 1982=100: **~260** in early 2024 → **~284–288** by mid-2026 (e.g. Jun 2026 **288.168**). Hardwood inputs have **crept up**, not repeated the 2021–22 spike.
- Trade commentary (TimberInsider 2025 review — **trade desk, not a futures tape**): Q1 2025 Appalachian **red oak** 4/4 S&B ~**$1,240–$1,380/MBF**; **walnut** ~**$2,840–$3,120/MBF**. Claims **8–12%** higher walnut/cherry kitchen quotes vs early 2024. Use as color, then **get mill quotes**.
- Softwood (the local Interfor product) is **not** furniture-grade hardwood. Local pine is an advantage for outdoor, painted built-ins, and crates — not for walnut tables.

## 7. Operations realities

### 7.1 Shop equipment (capability, not a shopping list)

| Stage | Typical kit | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakdown | SawStop-class table saw, bandsaw, jointer, planer | Hardwood furniture; dust and fingers |
| Cabinets | Nested CNC *or* very good panel saw + confirm; edgebander; 32 mm line-bore | CNC is how El Dorado/Nabholz advertise speed |
| Solid wood | Domino/mortiser, clamps, wide-belt or drum sander | Bottleneck is **sanding and finish**, not cut |
| Finish | Separate booth or tent; HVLP; humidity control | Claims and rework live here |
| Upholstery | Frame jig, sewing, foam storage | Different trade; AR has **170** upholsterers statewide (May 2023 OEWS) |
| Outbound | Pallet jack, box truck, foam-in-place | Rural last-mile is **you** |

**Do not buy a CNC first** unless the plan is doors/boxes/RTA. A used CNC without a finisher is a way to make scrap faster.

Capex must be **quoted from current dealers**. No equipment prices are invented here.

### 7.2 Lead times, deposits, warranty (industry practice, not law)

From multiple first-party shop terms (Adams, Taylor Forrest, Swiss Valley, Oak & Loom, ENWA/Mililab):

- **Deposit:** **50% to start** is the modal custom term; 30% appears on some catalog orders. Zero deposit is treated as a red flag by makers themselves.
- **Balance:** before ship or at delivery.
- **Lead time:** **6–8 weeks** (optimistic catalog) to **8–14 or 16–18 weeks** (true custom + freight). Always “estimate, not guarantee.”
- **Warranty:** **1 year** workmanship/finish is common; some frames **lifetime / 5-year structural**. Wood movement, humidity, and misuse excluded. Customer often pays **return freight**.
- **Cancellation:** 48–72 hour window, then deposit **non-refundable** once lumber is bought.

Local GCs will want **shop drawings, a date, and a punch list** more than a pretty warranty PDF.

### 7.3 Freight vs local delivery

- Beer’s already runs **regional delivery** and is adding a truck (April 2026 local press). A custom shop can **piggyback** or stay inside a **60-mile install radius**.
- **White-glove 3PLs** optimize metro density. Monticello is the wrong node for that network.
- **Design for two people and a ramp**, or for **knock-down**.

### 7.4 Labor

**National**

- O*NET / BLS projections: cabinetmakers and bench carpenters **86,000** employed (2024), median wage **$22.44 / $46,680** (2025), employment **projected to decline** through 2034, still **~8,100** openings/year from replacement.
- BLS industry page NAICS 337: furniture manufacturing employment **~328,000** (Jul 2026 preliminary); **56,210** cabinetmakers *inside* that industry (2025 occupational table).
- Eagle Woodworking / *Woodworking Network* survey of **500** tradespeople (2026 write-up): carpenters/woodworkers named the **most shortage-exposed** trade (**47%** multi-select; **19.6%** single choice). Quality, not just schedule, is what respondents said slips.
- ABC construction worker-gap figures are widely repeated (~**439,000** additional workers in 2025). Useful as **direction**, not a SE AR headcount.

**Arkansas (official OEWS, May 2023)**

| Occupation | Employment | Median hourly | Mean annual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cabinetmakers and bench carpenters | **930** | **$17.58** | **$41,110** |
| Furniture finishers | 160 | $17.27 | $35,990 |
| Upholsterers | 170 | $18.20 | $38,590 |

Location quotient for cabinetmakers in that release was **1.26** (slightly *above* U.S. concentration). A 2025 aggregator page claiming **$60,650** mean pay in Arkansas **conflicts** with the 2023 OEWS file and is **not used**.

**SE AR specific:** UAM’s published technical programs are **industrial / manufacturing / welding / HVAC**, not cabinetmaking. There is **no obvious local bench-carpenter pipeline** on the catalog pages reviewed. Weyerhaeuser and GP will bid against you for **reliable industrial workers**. Plan on **owner-operator + one helper**, or raiding a retiring directory shop.

### 7.5 Licensing (orientation only)

Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing / Residential Contractors Committee (public applications and A.C.A. § 17-25-513):

- **Home improvement license** for specialty work on a **single-family** residence when the project is **more than $2,000** (labor + materials). Classification list includes **“Carpentry, Framing, Millwork, Cabinets”** and **“Kitchen, Bathroom Renovations.”**
- **Limited** home-improvement license: residential projects **under $50,000**.
- **Commercial contractor** license when a non-single-family project is **$50,000+**.
- Exemptions exist (including subcontractor to a licensed contractor, and jobs **≤ $2,000**). **Do not structure work as a string of $1,999 tickets** — the statute addresses that.

Selling a table for pickup with no install is a different fact pattern than installing a kitchen. **Get a determination; this paragraph is not advice.**

## 8. Competitive gaps and ranked opportunity hypotheses

Rank is **for a small shop based in SE AR**, not for a well-funded coastal DTC brand. Scores are judgment on **local fit × defensibility × capital intensity**, not financial forecasts.

### Rank 1 — Contractor-allied cabinets, vanities, and built-ins (hybrid local)

**Thesis:** New houses are rare (4 permits). Kitchens and baths still get replaced. National spend on those rooms held up in 2024 (Houzz). Stock cabinets are a click away (Ferguson Home, Home Depot, Wayfair). **Fit, scribe, install, and showing up** are what a local shop sells. Directory-only cabinet names suggest **aging or invisible** incumbents — a succession or a service gap, not a proven empty market.

**Why SE AR:** GCs in Drew/Bradley/Ashley/Lincoln cannot wait on Cabot or Conway for a $12k laundry + vanity. El Dorado is the documented quality alternative.

**Risks:** Median income; one GC can be 40% of revenue; finish quality under humidity; license/insurance; Weyerhaeuser wage pressure.

**First test:** Three GC interviews + two jobsite ride-alongs. If they already have a shop that hits dates, **do not** enter as the fifth cabinet shop — enter as **doors/boxes/closets**.

### Rank 2 — Shipping-aware SKUs + custom-listing hardwood (online, from SE AR)

**Thesis:** 731 proved the **world will watch woodworking from Monticello**. The open slot is **physical goods that survive UPS/FedEx**, or Etsy custom listings with **quoted freight** on $1,500+ pieces — not another sofa brand.

**Examples:** knock-down benches, wall shelves, dog kennels (731 historically built one), door/drawer fronts, cutting boards, plan + hardware kits.

**Why SE AR:** Lower shop rent; hardwood story; 80% broadband for running ads/Etsy; outbound ground from a South-central node is acceptable for **small cartons**.

**Risks:** Etsy fees on shipping; 731 already owns “Monticello woodworker” search; commodity cutting boards are a race to the bottom; returns.

### Rank 3 — Commercial / institutional casework (B2B)

**Thesis:** UAM construction, clinic remodels, mill offices, and senior-living refreshes need **plastic-laminate and hardwood casework**, not farmhouse tables. Akin owns **loose furniture** for hospitality/senior living. A small shop can take **built-in** work Akin may not want, or subcontract.

**Why SE AR:** Healthcare receipts and a university in a town of 17k; Weyerhaeuser offices coming; Crossett employment base.

**Risks:** Akin relationship (competitor or customer?); **$50k commercial license**; bonding; payment cycles; Nabholz-class GCs bringing their own millwork.

### Rank 4 — Mid-ticket solid-wood tables and built-ins with regional delivery (hybrid)

**Thesis:** Ouachita Antique Woods and North Delta already sell the **live-edge** story from Monroe. A Monticello shop can differentiate with **painted pine + hardwood tops**, Cane Creek / lake houses, and **delivery inside the Beer’s radius** (Arkansas + north LA).

**Risks:** Freight if you get greedy nationally; seasonal; taste concentration (farmhouse saturates Etsy); finishing defects in humid summers.

### Rank 5 — Outdoor / camp / porch (niche product line)

**Thesis:** 731’s origin story was **patio chairs that sold overnight** (their about page). SE AR has heat, hunting camps, and porches. Big-box outdoor is plastic and aluminum.

**Risks:** UV and rot warranties; seasonal cash flow; Chinese poly-lumber imports.

### Rank 6 — Reupholstery and furniture repair (local service)

**Thesis:** No verified Monticello upholstery shop in this pass; boomer + senior-living stock; cheaper than a new Joybird sofa. Statewide upholsterer employment is **tiny**.

**Risks:** Skill rarity; dust/finish compliance; low willingness to pay until a sofa is sentimental.

### Rank 7 — CNC component supply to other shops (B2B, capital-heavy)

**Thesis:** Eagle Woodworking’s own shortage essay is an ad for **buying doors and dovetail drawers** instead of making them on a stretched crew. If Pine Bluff / El Dorado shops are real and busy, a nested CNC cell in Monticello could sell **components**.

**Risks:** Capex; you need **their** trust; they may already buy from large door plants.

### Rank 8 — National online-only custom upholstery (do not prioritize)

**Thesis:** The category is real (Wayfair Custom, Interior Define, BenchMade, Joybird). **Starting it in SE AR without a sewing floor, a 3PL, and a claims desk is how shops die.** Upholstery labor is scarce; white-glove does not density-route here.

**Risks:** All of the above plus Havenly/Interior Define’s documented post-pandemic cancellation scar (customers remember).

## 9. Startup / expansion checklist (sketch)

**Not a business plan. Sequence, not spend.**

1. **Pick one production system** (cabinets / KD wood goods / commercial casework). Write the SKU and the install radius on one page.
2. **Visit before you tool** — every name in §5.1–5.3, plus two Monroe shops and Beer’s. Photograph storefronts; note whether lights are on.
3. **License and insurance path** — Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board; general liability; if installing, workers’ comp; if spraying finish, environmental rules. Counsel.
4. **Cost model v0** — materials (get oak/plywood quotes from a real yard, not Instagram), hours, finish, delivery, **credit-card + Etsy fees**, warranty reserve, idle time. Target **50% deposit**.
5. **Minimum shop** — dust collection and a finish space before a CNC. OSHA/finishing discipline; L&P’s 2005 record is a reminder that furniture plants get inspected.
6. **Identity** — Google Business, Facebook, a phone that a GC will text. Houzz if chasing remodel photos.
7. **Three reference jobs** — friends-and-family priced at **true cost** to get photos, not “free forever.”
8. **GC house-account one-pager** — lead times, species, box construction (ply vs particle), hinge brand, what voids warranty.
9. **Shipping rules** — parcel SKUs vs “quote freight” vs “Monticello pickup.” Never advertise free national sofa shipping.
10. **Labor** — apprentice from welding/industrial programs; or buy a retiring shop’s **phone book**, not just its table saw.
11. **Do not** open a second location, a full upholstery line, and a YouTube network in year one.
12. **Cash** — custom furniture eats deposits and then waits. Model **12 weeks** of materials + payroll.

## 10. Next validation steps

Do these in order. Desk research cannot replace a drive down Gabbert and Hyatt.

### A. Shop visits (week 1–2)

- Walk **702 N Gabbert**, **1210 N Hyatt**, **605 Scogin**, **417 W McCloy**, **113 Commerce**, **749 Walmart Access**, **253 Hwy 425 N**, **190 Wilson Mill**, **211 Old Troy**.

- Monroe: OA Woods, M&M, Bailey’s.
- Score: open Y/N, showroom Y/N, CNC visible, lead time quoted, deposit %, species on the floor.

### B. Contractor interviews (n ≥ 8)

Script (30 minutes):

1. Where did the last kitchen cabinets come from?
2. What failed — date, scribe, finish, hardware, communication?
3. What would you pay for a **4-week** vanity + linen vs **12-week** custom kitchen?
4. Do you need a **house account** or only bid-by-bid?
5. Who installs — you or the shop?
6. Any **UAM / clinic / mill office** work in the next 18 months?

Talk to GCs in Monticello, Warren, Star City, Crossett, and one Pine Bluff remodeler.

### C. Competitive teardowns

- Order **swatches** from Interior Define or Wayfair Custom (cost of education).
- Price a **10×10 stock kitchen** at Home Depot / Ferguson Home vs a **phone quote** from El Dorado Cabinets.
- Etsy: search `custom dining table Arkansas` and `shaker vanity` — record ASP, ship policy, review complaints (freight/damage).
- Sit in a **Beer’s** Ashley sofa and write the price. That is your homeowner’s reference point.
- If a directory cabinet shop is open, ask to see a **box construction** (ply vs particle, dovetail vs staple).

### D. Cost model (spreadsheet)

Build three SKUs:

1. 36" vanity, painted maple, local install  
2. 72" dining table, oak, pickup  
3. Pair of shaker doors, parcel  

For each: materials, hours, finish, packaging, delivery, fees, warranty 2%, **cash days**. Kill any SKU that needs **white-glove** to clear 30% gross after freight.

### E. Demand probes (cheap)

- Facebook post: “Taking 3 kitchen measure-and-quotes this month.” Count responses, not likes.
- One **lunch** with Monticello Economic Development (Nita McDaniel is named on the Weyerhaeuser release) about **plant office / training-room** casework — a conversation, not a claimed contract.
- UAM facilities / housing — student-furniture and office standards (Akin may already be on campus; **ask**).

### F. Stop rules

- If two competent cabinet shops are **open, booked, and liked by GCs**, do not clone them — supply them (Rank 7) or go Rank 2/5.
- If homeowners only want **$799 sofas**, you are in Beer’s market; stay out or wholesale a bench to Beer’s.
- If a CNC quote exceeds **12 months of conservative gross profit** on the door/box plan, wait.
