# Nutritional supplements market landscape and opportunity brief

**Prepared:** 14 September 2026
**Scope:** Opportunity-focused landscape of the **dietary / nutritional supplements** category (vitamins, minerals, herbals, specialty, sports/protein, personalized, adjacent formats).
**Audience:** Strategy / product / GTM, not clinical or legal counsel.
**Prior task:** A planned isolated Cursor TypeScript SDK sample was **abandoned** in favor of this brief.

> **Not medical advice. Not legal advice.** Figures below are cited from published sources. Syndicated market-research houses use different category definitions and often do not publish full methodologies on free pages. Where sources conflict, both numbers are shown and the gap is flagged.

## Executive take

The U.S. dietary-supplement industry is a **high-70s-billion-dollar, mid-single-digit-growth** market (Nutrition Business Journal / New Hope Network), sitting inside a **~USD 210 billion** global dietary-supplement industry (Grand View Research, 2025). Penetration is already high: about **three in four U.S. adults** take supplements (CRN/Ipsos 2024). That means growth is less about converting non-users and more about **trading up, specializing, and capturing new need-states**.

The commercially interesting motion is **not** another undifferentiated multivitamin. It is:

1. **Need-state stacks** that already have pull — GLP-1 companion nutrition, midlife women’s health, muscle/longevity, gut 2.0, sleep beyond melatonin.
2. **Format and channel innovation** — food-like powders/liquids/gummies, Amazon + TikTok Shop, practitioner digital fulfillment, subscription as a retention tool rather than a brand.
3. **Trust as a product** — third-party testing, Amazon 2026 cGMP/listing rules, Consumer Reports heavy-metal scrutiny, FTC-grade substantiation.

The category is **structurally fragmented** (no single U.S. brand dominates), easy to enter via private-label contract manufacturing, and hard to **defend**. White space is therefore less “nobody makes X” and more “nobody owns a credible, claim-safe, retention-positive version of X for a specific buyer.”

### 1.4 Usage and spend (U.S. consumers)

Council for Responsible Nutrition **2024 Consumer Survey** (Ipsos; fielded 7–12 Aug 2024; n = 3,194 U.S. adults; press release 29 Oct 2024):

- **75%** of U.S. adults use dietary supplements (in line with the prior year).
- **91%** of users say supplements are essential to maintaining their health.
- Median monthly spend: **USD 50** in 2024 (USD 48 in 2023).
- Users who buy from **healthcare professionals**: median **USD 100** / month.
- **69%** of users say a **personalized regimen** is important when choosing products.
- **27%** of non-users cite **cost** as a barrier; **41%** cite lack of perceived need.
- Ingredient usage among users: magnesium **23%** (from 19% in 2023); ashwagandha **8%** (from 2% in 2020); melatonin **16%** (from 10% in 2020); prebiotics **7%** (from 5%).

Grand View’s U.S. page also cites the **2023** CRN/Ipsos wave (**74%** use; **55%** “regular users”). Do not treat 74% and 75% as a trend without the full time series.

## 3. Competitive structure

### 3.1 Fragmented, not oligopolistic (U.S.)

AlixPartners (“Stacked: The new shape of the supplement industry,” March 2026) states that **no company has more than a 5% share** in U.S. VMS and that **private label can out-share the largest branded SKUs**. That matches the lived shelf: many brands, low loyalty except among CRN’s “regular users” (71% say they are brand-loyal — a survey attitude, not Nielsen share).

### 3.2 Incumbent archetypes

**Mass / pharmacy VMS (trust + distribution)**

- **Haleon** (Centrum, Caltrate, Emergen-C): FY25 VMS revenue **£1.685 billion**, **+1.9% organic**, **−0.6% reported** (Haleon FY25 results, 25 Feb 2026). Growth **outside the U.S.**; North America hit by a **softer multivitamin category and distribution losses**. Haleon is building a **cross-category GLP-1 support** platform and pushing **Centrum Daily Kits** (life-stage personalization) in APAC.
- **Nestlé Health Science:** announced **1 Sep 2026** sale of **mainstream** VMS (Nature’s Bounty, Osteo Bi-Flex, Ester-C, Gard, Nuun, Puritan’s Pride, Sisu) **plus U.S. private-label manufacturing** to **Yellow Wood Partners for USD 1 billion**. The package did **USD 1.2 billion sales in 2025**. Nestlé **keeps premium/science brands Solgar and Pure Encapsulations**. Signal: **mainstream pills are a PE / operator game; premium practitioner-ish brands stay strategic.**
- **Reckitt** (Schiff, MegaRed, Move Free), **Pharmavite** (Nature Made, MegaFood), **P&G** (New Chapter), **NOW Foods**, **Church & Dwight** (VitaFusion / L’il Critters — strategic review / exit process reported through 2025) sit in the same “known brand, mass door” layer.

**Direct selling / MLM (still large, slower channel)**

- **Amway:** **USD 7.4 billion** company sales in 2024 (12 Mar 2025). Nutrition **64%** of sales, nutrition **+2%**. Nutrilite/Nutriway claimed as **#1 global vitamin/dietary-supplement brand by retail value share** (GlobalData claim on Amway’s release). 2025 company sales were reported lower in local press (~USD 7.3 billion); use the **2024 official release** until an official 2025 letter is checked.
- **Herbalife:** net sales **USD 5.0375 billion in 2025** vs **USD 4.9931 billion in 2024** (FY25 release / 10-K tables). Nutrition + sports + weight, not a pure VMS comp.

**Specialty retail**

- **GNC** (Harbin Pharmaceutical Group since the 2020 bankruptcy exit) and **The Vitamin Shoppe** (sold to Kingswood / Performance Investment Partners, May 2025 — secondary trade reporting) remain discovery and attached-service doors (Vitamin Shoppe’s Whole Health Rx GLP-1 support is an example in L.E.K., Nov 2025).

**Practitioner / “professional” brands**

- Metagenics, Designs for Health, Thorne, Pure Encapsulations (Nestlé), Fullscript-style fulfillment. Higher price, higher trust, slower creative. L.E.K. (Nov 2025) cites practitioner-channel growth around **~7% annually**, outpacing the total market.

**DTC / digitally native**

- **Ritual:** Forbes (30 Jul 2025) reported **USD 250 million “sales” / “retail value”** and a **300-store Ulta** launch. Treat **retail value ≠ audited net revenue**.
- **Persona** (Nestlé), **Care/of** (Bayer; U.S. DTC **wound down**), **Hiya** (kids; acquired by USANA Dec 2024 — L.E.K.), **OLLY** (Unilever, 2019 — category-defining gummy), **MaryRuth’s** (L.E.K.: **> USD 70 million on TikTok Shop**), Bloom, AG1 (Athletic Greens) as “foundational nutrition” / influencer packs.
- Contract-manufactured **Amazon/TikTok private-label** brands: thousands of SKUs, 30–60 day trend cycles (trade press; quality of those claims is marketing-grade).

### 3.3 Amazon and marketplace dynamics

L.E.K. / Stackline (TTM ending Q3 2025): U.S. supplement-like categories on **Amazon ~USD 19.9 billion**, **~21% YoY**, vs **SPINS measured retail ~USD 24.8 billion, ~6% YoY**. Amazon is already a **peer of measured brick retail**, not a side door.

*Nutraceuticals World* / NIQ (16 Mar 2026):

- **TikTok Shop ~USD 1 billion** of U.S. supplements in 2025; **3%** of supplement sales in the year to Jan 2026, **+71% YoY**.
- Vitamins and supplements were **13% of all TikTok Shop sales** (top product type on the platform).
- NIQ: only **37%** of supplement brands felt “well-prepared” for social-commerce response.

**Private label:** retail store brands are strong in mass VMS (AlixPartners). Amazon-**owned** labels are a small share of **total Amazon GMV** (~**2%** of units, Numerator early 2025 — **marketplace-wide, not supplement-specific**). The real Amazon competitive set is **third-party sellers + retail private label + brand.com**, not “Amazon Basics vitamins.”

**2026 Amazon U.S. policy (trade reporting, Mar 2026):** third-party **cGMP / TIC** documentation for dietary supplements and tighter alignment of listings to the Supplement Facts panel, with AI enforcement. This **raises the floor** and will **delist thin private-label** operators — a moat for brands that already have NSF/USP/Informed-Sport-style paper and a real manufacturer.

### 3.4 M&A climate

L.E.K. (Nov 2025): post-COVID strategic M&A **slowed**; PE more active; “forever brands” need **repeat/subscription, omnichannel velocity, and science**. 2025–2026 headlines: Nestlé portfolio split, Church & Dwight vitamin review, Danone / Kate Farms and Akkermansia Company, USANA / Hiya, Kimberly-Clark / Kenvue (consumer-health adjacency). **Capital is available for brands with proof, not for another untested gummy.**

### 4.1 Subscription

Not a separate NBJ channel. It is a **pricing and retention layer** on e-comm / DTC / practitioner reorder. CRN: users already spend every month. The economic problem is **CAC vs 3–6 month churn**, which is why **Care/of** could be shut and **Ritual** is adding **Ulta** — subscription-only personalization has struggled to stay independent.

### 4.2 Journey, not a single door

New Hope (14 Jul 2026): most people shop **at least three** of the major channels. AlixPartners: after a **provider** recommend, **~9%** switch the repurchase to Amazon; after **DTC**, **~20%** look to Amazon. **Discovery** (TikTok, clinician, AI search) and **replenishment** (Amazon Subscribe & Save, brand.com) are splitting.

NBJ 2026 consumer survey (*Nutraceuticals World*, 16 Mar 2026): important information sources — **internet 55%**, **healthcare practitioners 55%** (vs **23% in 2024** — a very large jump; treat as survey, possible wording change), friends/family 43%, social/influencers 31%, **AI platforms 16%**. Certifications of interest: **Made in USA 48%** (ahead of USDA Organic 35% for the first time), Non-GMO 26%, gluten-free 18%, **USP 17%**.

## 5. Regulatory and claim constraints (U.S., high level)

This section is a **map of constraints that change product and marketing design**. It is **not** a compliance program and **not** legal advice. EU Novel Foods, ASEAN, China Blue Hat, Canada NHP, and Prop 65 are additional regimes for any export plan.

### 5.1 DSHEA / FDA (labeling, identity, safety — postmarket)

Under the **Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994**, FDA **does not pre-approve** supplements or most claims (FDA Q&A, current agency page).

Implications that actually shape a brief:

1. **Easy to launch, hard to stay clean.** The firm — not FDA — is initially responsible for safety, identity, purity, and labeling. FDA inspects, samples, and writes warning letters **after** the fact (21 CFR Part 111 CGMP).
2. **Structure/function vs disease.** Allowed examples (FDA): “calcium builds strong bones,” “fiber maintains bowel regularity.” **Disease claims** (diagnose / treat / cure / prevent) turn the product into an **unapproved drug**. The required DSHEA disclaimer does **not** legalize a disease claim.
3. **Notification:** structure/function (and related) claims need **FDA notification within 30 days of first marketing** and **prior substantiation** that the claim is truthful and not misleading (FDA structure/function page).
4. **New dietary ingredients (NDIs):** ingredients **not marketed in the U.S. before 15 Oct 1994** generally need a **75-day premarket NDI notification** with a reasonable-expectation-of-safety file, unless they are present in the food supply in an unchemically-altered food form (FDA Q&A). Novel “longevity” molecules are where startups get surprised.
5. **Serious adverse event reporting** and facility registration are mandatory.
6. **No FDA serving-size cap** — dose is a manufacturer decision bounded by **safety and labeling truth**, not by a monograph like OTC drugs. That is why “clinical dose” vs “fairy-dust blend” is a **positioning choice with liability attached**.

Recent enforcement texture (examples, not a survey): FDA warning letters in 2025–2026 for **unsafe NDIs** (e.g., 7-OH kratom alkaloids, Jun 2025) and **Part 111 failures** (missing identity/purity/strength/composition and heavy-metal specs — Nutratech Jun 2026; Hearthy Foods collagen Feb 2026).

### 5.2 FTC (advertising — including social, influencers, Amazon A+ )

FDA owns **labels**; FTC owns **ads**. FTC *Health Products Compliance Guidance* (issued **20 Dec 2022**, replacing the 1998 supplement advertising guide) applies to **all health products**, not just supplements.

Design constraints:

- Claims need **competent and reliable scientific evidence** **before** the ad runs. FTC generally looks for **high-quality randomized, controlled human trials** that match the **product, dose, population, and implied claim**.
- The **DSHEA disclaimer does not save a deceptive ad**. FTC does **not** require the disclaimer in advertising and does **not** treat FDA notification as approval.
- **Implied claims** count (weight-loss before/after, “natures Ozempic,” GLP-1 “mimic,” disease innuendo in TikTok audio).
- Endorsements, typicality, and “clinically proven” language are frequent tripwires.

**Product-strategy translation:** a TikTok-native sleep or GLP-1 gummy that “works in the creative” can be **unlaunchable** if the file is a rodent study and a blend at 5% of the studied dose. **Claim architecture should be designed before formulation**, not after the influencer brief.

### 5.3 Marketplace and state overlays

- **Amazon 2026 U.S. supplement policy:** third-party CGMP evidence + listing/SFP alignment (Vitafoods Insights / trade explainers, Mar 2026). Treat Amazon as a **private regulator**.
- **California Prop 65** and retailer quality manuals (Costco, Target) often bind **heavy metals** more tightly than federal DSHEA.
- **NSF, USP, Informed-Sport, Informed-Choice, UL** are voluntary but increasingly **commercial licenses** (NBJ: USP interest 17%; Made in USA now #1 cert mention).

## 6. Consumer trends (2023–2026)

### 6.1 From “clean” to “show me the study” — without dropping clean

McKinsey Future of Wellness **2024** (5,000+ consumers, CN/UK/US): roughly **half** of UK and US consumers named **clinical effectiveness** a top purchase factor vs about **20%** for natural/clean — a reversal from earlier waves. Strongest in OTC and vitamins/supplements.

NBJ 2026: **Made in USA** overtook **USDA Organic**; certification interest “more than doubled” year-on-year in several badges. Clean is **table stakes in natural**; **proof** is what prices a premium in mass and practitioner.

### 6.2 GLP-1 adjacency (the largest new demand shock)

- Gallup web survey of **5,065 U.S. adults (28 May–5 Jun 2026)**, cited by *NutraIngredients* (25 Aug 2026): **11% of U.S. adults** currently taking GLP-1s for **weight loss**.
- SPINS (Apr 2025 Expo): classic **weight-management formulas −11.9%**, energy −7.6%; **companion** demand in protein, greens, gut; **berberine** as a “competitor” SKU — with an explicit warning **not** to claim “nature’s Ozempic.”
- Nestlé Health Science launched a **2024 GLP-1 nutrition platform** (muscle, gut, micros, hydration, hair/skin, rebound) across Garden of Life, Boost, Nature’s Bounty, Nuun, Vital Proteins (*NutraIngredients*, 25 Aug 2026).
- L.E.K. (Nov 2025): Vitamin Shoppe Whole Health Rx kits; SuperGut-type prebiotic lines.
- BCG via Statista (survey Apr 2026, published Jul/Aug 2026): among **active GLP-1 users**, **increased** spend vs prior — protein **+24%**, fiber **+22%**, collagen **+20%**, multivitamins **+18%**, electrolytes **+18%**, magnesium **+16%**, hair/skin/nails **+16%**, probiotics **+15%**, creatine **+12%**. (This is **change in spend among users**, not category CAGR.)
- Clinical literature is **emerging, not settled**. A 2025–2026 narrative review (PMC12685510) discusses protein **1.2–2.0 g/kg/d**, creatine, HMB, fiber/probiotics, and multivitamins as **considerations** — useful for **hypothesis**, not for claim copy.
- SPINS’s Dicker (*NutraIngredients*, Aug 2026): long-run shoppers buy **benefits** (satiety, muscle, gut, metabolic adequacy), not the letters “GLP-1.” Johns Hopkins’ Kantha Shelke: much of the market is **relabeling**; **protein/muscle** has the best rationale; **collagen for “Ozempic face”** is ahead of the evidence; **sensory/format** (small serve, nutrient density) is the real R&D problem.

### 6.3 Women’s health, midlife, “systems” longevity

- L.E.K. (Nov 2025): **73% of women do not treat menopause symptoms**; **80%** experience hormonal imbalance in their lives; fertility challenges in **13%** of women 15–49 (L.E.K. citations in-report).
- Mintel via *NutraIngredients* (22 Jun 2026): women’s longevity framed as **muscle + ovarian + gut systems**, not anti-aging serums; **proteinification** and **creatineification** of women’s wellness.
- NBJ: menopause **+8.6%** (2024); women’s general health still only **3.8%** of condition dollars — **under-labeled vs under-served**.

### 6.4 Sports → everyday muscle / healthspan

- Sports nutrition **+8% / +8.4%** (2025 / 2024 NBJ).
- SPINS (Apr 2025): **active nutrition** fastest supplement category **four years running**; hydration **+28.9% to ~USD 1.7 billion**; creatine **+17.2%**; magnesium **+33.5%**; ashwagandha **+26.5%**. Creatine is expanding **off young men in gyms** into gummies, RTDs, older adults, women.
- McKinsey 2025: functional nutrition purchases are common; **energy, gut, immunity, muscle/bone/joint** are the most sought benefits; GLP-1 ecosystem boosts **protein-fortified food, gut comfort, muscle programs**.

### 6.5 Gut 2.0

- McKinsey 2024: **>80%** in CN/UK/US say gut health is important; **>50%** will prioritize it more in 2–3 years; **~1/3 of US and UK** (half of China) want **more products**.
- L.E.K.: SPINS probiotics + digestive aids **~10%** of supplement sales.
- Innovation moving to **pre/syn/postbiotics**, strain stories (**Akkermansia** — Danone bought The Akkermansia Company, Jul 2025), and **fiber** for GLP-1 GI side effects.

### 6.6 Sleep, stress, mind

- Sleep is McKinsey’s **#2 wellness priority** and the **largest unmet-need** pocket (2024).
- CRN: melatonin use **16%** of users — **mature, crowded, dose/safety scrutiny**.
- SPINS: calm/mood **+24.3%** (2024 Expo); ashwagandha, L-theanine; alcohol **moderation** as a demand pool.
- Opportunity is **non-melatonin or combination sleep** (magnesium, theanine, circadian) with **honest claims**, plus **wearable-tied** routines (NBJ: **52%** use a wearable; **50%** say wearable data influenced a supplement purchase — Apr 2025).

### 6.7 Formats

U.S. 2025 (NBJ / *Nutraceuticals World*): **gummies 24.6%**, powders **18.7% (+11%)**, liquids **12.7% (+10%)**, softgels 12.5%, capsules 10.3%, tablets **7%**, chewables **+9.5%**. Food-like > “medicine-like.” Channel fit: **RTD/RTM win in stores; capsules/liquids/softgels win on Amazon** (SPINS/Dicker, Apr 2025).

### 6.8 Discovery stack: social + clinician + AI

TikTok for trial, clinicians for permission, Amazon for refill, AI (16% already “important”) for comparison. Brands that only buy Meta CACs will lose the **recommendation layer**.

Workstreams are ordered for a **go / no-go in weeks, not a multi-year science program**. None of this is a clinical protocol.

### 8.1 Data (buy or extract before building SKUs)

4. **Amazon category health:** share of search, review-velocity, and **suppressed listings** after the 2026 TIC rule (seller forums + a broker).
5. **Prop 65 / CR-style metal panel** on any protein or botanicals shortlist — **lab, not brochure**.

### 8.2 Customer and channel interviews (n ≈ 25–40, structured)

| Segment | What to learn |
| --- | --- |
| Current GLP-1 users (injectable and oral if available) | Actual diet volume, protein grams, GI symptoms, what they already bought, what they **stopped**, trust in “GLP-1 support” vs “high-protein snack” |
| Women 40–60, supplement users | What they take vs what they wish existed; clinician vs TikTok; creatine stigma; sleep vs vasomotor vs muscle priority |
| Fullscript / clinic RDs and obesity MDs | Protocol gaps, brand-switch triggers, Amazon leakage, required certs |
| Natural-channel buyers vs Costco buyers | Price ceiling for tested protein; Made-in-USA vs organic |
| Churned quiz-pack subscribers | Why Care/of-class products failed them (price, pills-in-packets, no felt effect) |

### 8.3 IP and formulation

- **Freedom-to-operate** on strain IP (Akkermansia and named probiotics), branded creatine/HMB, and any “GLP-1 companion” trademarks (crowded).
- **NDI screen** on every non-vitamin botanical extract and “longevity” molecule.
- **Dose math:** if the RCT used 5 g creatine and the gummy delivers 1 g, the product is a **format toy** unless you can show a different endpoint.
- **Finished-product** vs **ingredient-borrowed** claims (FTC cares about **this** product).
- Contract manufacturer: Part 111, identity testing, **metal specs on incoming cocoa/plant protein**, allergen controls, and whether they will **put their name on the COA**.
- Packaging: small serve, travel, GLP-1 nausea (smell/flavor), and club-size economics.

### 8.4 Claim and creative legal review (counsel, not this brief)

- Structure/function deck **before** influencer scripts.
- Ban-list: drug names, “replaces / mimics Wegovy,” disease, typicality.
- Amazon A+ and TikTok text as **labeling/advertising**, not “just content.”

### 8.5 Pilot design (only after 8.1–8.4)

- **One** hero need-state (rank 1, 2, or 3), **one** primary channel, 90-day subscribe-and-save test with **refund and adverse-event** process.
- Success metrics: repeat at day 60, CAC payback, claim-complaint rate, COA fail rate — not vanity GMV.

## 9. Working conclusions

1. **The U.S. market is large, used, and only growing mid-single digits.** Winning is **share of a need-state**, not “category growth.”
2. **Vitamins are the ocean; sports, specialty, and midlife/GLP-1 need-states are the current.**
3. **Channels are converging on e-comm #1 by ~2028**, but **mass already mainstreamed** sports and meal replacements, and **practitioners punch above their 9%** on trust and dollars per user.
4. **Regulation is the strategy filter.** DSHEA makes launch cheap; **FTC + Amazon + metals testing** make scaling expensive for sloppy claims and sloppy powder.
5. **Best hypotheses to pressure-test first:** formulated GLP-1 companion nutrition; women’s midlife muscle/sleep/bone; and **provably cleaner protein**. Personalization and longevity molecules are real themes but **worse first bets** on CAC, IP, and evidence.
