# Web Development B2B Market Landscape — Online / National and Southeast Arkansas (Monticello)

**Research date:** 14 September 2026  
**Scope:** B2B web services sold to other businesses — national/online models and the local market around Monticello / Drew County and a reasonable SE Arkansas radius.  
**Purpose:** Surface concrete opportunities for a small B2B web practice based in Monticello / SE AR.  
**Isolation:** This brief lives in top-level `research/` and is not COSMOS product documentation.

**Not legal, tax, or investment advice.** Figures from commercial market-research houses are **soft** unless they come from a primary filing, Census, BLS, or a named survey with a method. Local firm lists are limited to names that could be verified on a first-party site, chamber listing, LinkedIn company page, or official directory. SEO “city pages” from out-of-state agencies are labeled as such and are **not** treated as local shops.

---

## 0. Opportunity snapshot (read this first)

The national B2B web market is **large, fragmented, and being split in two**. Brochure and template sites are being compressed by Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, Hostinger, Durable, and other AI-assisted builders sold directly to SMBs. Custom work, local search, bookings, payments, accessibility, and “someone I can call” remain hard to productize. Promethean’s 2025 agency survey (published May 2025) counted **50,000+ digital agencies in the US and Canada**, **88% under 50 FTEs**, and a modest drop in shops offering web development (**75% → 69%**) and web design (**73% → 67%**) from 2023 to 2025 — consistent with commoditization, not disappearance.

Southeast Arkansas is the opposite of a saturated agency market. In the seven counties the Arkansas Small Business and Technology Development Center (ASBTDC) at UAM serves (Ashley, Bradley, Chicot, Cleveland, Desha, Drew, Lincoln), Census QuickFacts show about **1,686 employer establishments** and **5,428 nonemployer establishments** in 2023. Drew County itself had **423 employer** and **1,209 nonemployer** establishments (2023) and about **17,054** residents (1 July 2025 estimate). Verified **in-town web agencies are scarce**. The clearest Monticello-named web shop is EvoDev Designs / Evolutionary Development and Design; the clearest SE AR creative shop that lists website design is Crossbuck Creative in Pine Bluff. Computer-repair / IT shops exist locally but do not present as productized web agencies. Out-of-state firms flood Google with “Monticello web design” location pages.

**Highest-conviction local play:** a **productized “found + booked + paid” package** (mobile site + Google Business Profile + reviews + click-to-call / booking / payments) sold face-to-face to main-street SMBs, contractors, and clinics, delivered with AI-assisted production, and converted into a **$150–$400/month care retainer**. Compete on **trust, speed, and local accountability**, not on beating Fiverr’s $300 gig or Little Rock’s $15k custom build.

**Do not treat this as a TAM number.** There is no published SE AR web-services market size. Any dollar estimate below is a **scenario**, not a measurement.

## 2. Online / national B2B landscape

### 2.1 Agency models

Promethean Research’s *2025 Digital Agency Industry Report* (PDF dated May 2025; surveys 2015–2025, 1,062 agencies in the core sample plus an observational set of 50,302 US/Canada shops) describes a **crowded, maturing** industry:

- **50,000+** digital agencies in the US and Canada; **179,000+** worldwide.
- Average shop is tiny; **88%** have fewer than 50 FTEs; most have fewer than 10.
- Surveyed agencies grew **~12%** annually over five years, but **2024 average growth was 5%**.
- **84%** now identify as specialists.
- **95%** sell projects, **91%** sell retainers, **88%** sell both. Fast growers did **24% more projects** and served **16% fewer retainer clients** than average — retainers stabilize cash, projects grow the top line.
- Average **net margin ~16%** historically; **14% in 2024**. Smaller studios historically earn higher net margins than 50+ FTE shops.
- In the latest survey, about **36%** of agencies billed **$175–$199/hour** and **32%** billed **$200–$249/hour**. Those are **surveyed agency list rates**, not what a Monticello HVAC shop will pay.
- From 2023–2025, share of agencies offering web development fell **75% → 69%** and web design **73% → 67%**. Promethean attributes this to in-housing, commoditization, and site builders.

**Implication:** the winning small shop is not “full-service digital agency.” It is a **narrow offer** with a **repeatable delivery system** and a **local or vertical wedge**.

### 2.2 Marketplace (Upwork / Fiverr agency tiers)

Marketplaces set the **price floor** that local owners Google first.

- **Upwork** (own “Web Designers cost” page, retrieved 2026): typical historical hourly band **$15–$30**; one-page **$15–$40+/hr**; CMS **$20–$50+/hr**; custom **$45–$75+/hr**. Upwork’s 2026 “cost to build a website” article quotes **$300–$5,000** for many site types, corporate **$1,500–$4,000+**, e-commerce **$1,000–$5,000+**, maintenance **$50–$200/month**.
- **Fiverr** (2025 guide): **$20–$100/hour**, **$500–$10,000** per project; DIY **$0–$300**; freelance **$500–$5,000+**; agency **$500–$10,000+**.
- Fees: Upwork’s own 2026 comparison says freelancer service fees **0–15%** and an Agency Plus plan; Fiverr takes a **20%** seller commission and buyers pay about **5.5%**. Third-party 2026 write-ups (Hire in South, Sonary) describe Fiverr Pro as the vetted tier and Upwork Expert-Vetted / Top Rated as the quality badges. Treat fee details as **platform-changeable**.

**Agency-tier pattern:** productized gigs (5-page WordPress, Shopify setup) at the bottom; milestone / hourly teams in the middle; “Pro” / “Expert-Vetted” as a trust badge that still does not replace a local phone number.

### 2.3 Productized services

Productized shops sell a **SKU**, not a discovery workshop. Common SKUs online:

- “Launch site in 14 days” brochure packages
- Landing-page retainers / “unlimited” design (one active track)
- Webflow / WordPress care plans
- Migration packages (Wix → WordPress, WordPress → Webflow)

Public examples (positioning, not recommended vendors): DVNC lists a design+build subscription **from $7,000/month** (3-month minimum). Digital Design Studio lists retainers **$7,500–$12,500/month**. Amply’s 2026 Webflow guide puts most B2B agency retainers at **$2,000–$7,500+/month**, with **$3,000–$5,000** as a typical active-site band. Those prices are **national B2B / startup**, not SE AR main street.

Forge (productized vs retainer, 2026) is useful conceptually: a **productized retainer** = recurring fee for a **defined monthly outcome** (e.g. “two content updates + uptime + GBP posts”), which is the right shape for a Monticello care plan.

### 2.4 SaaS-adjacent builders sold to SMBs

These are the **substitute goods** for a $2,000 agency brochure site.

| Vendor | What they sell B2B | 2025/2026 commercial signal | Local implication |

| Band | Who occupies it | What they sell | Typical published range | Source class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DIY / AI builder | Owner + SaaS | Live site this weekend | $0–$50/month platform | Vendor pricing pages |

**Positioning pattern that repeats:** (1) **speed + price** (builders, Fiverr), (2) **outcomes + retainer** (SEO/CRO shops), (3) **trust + proximity** (local boutique), (4) **platform lock-in** (Shopify/Wix partners). A Monticello practice should occupy (3) and borrow production tricks from (1).

Clutch’s 2025 *State of Digital Marketing / Web Design* piece contrasts DIY builder plans (**$16–$159/month**) with an **~$7,139 average monthly agency cost** on Clutch reviews. That agency figure is **review-biased toward larger projects**. Do not quote it as “what websites cost in Arkansas.”

### 3.4 SMB digitization and AI-site disruption

**Digitization is incomplete, especially locally.** Census ACS 2020–2024: Drew County households **90.5%** computer, **80.5%** broadband — so **about one in five households still lack a broadband subscription**. Neighboring counties are similar or worse (Lincoln broadband **75.0%**; Desha **78.0%**; Bradley **79.7%**). A mobile-first site that works on mid-range Androids over LTE matters more here than a WebGL hero.

**AI builders steal the first $500–$2,000 project**, not the relationship. Clutch (2025) and agency blogs all make the same point: builders win on **time-to-first-publish**; agencies still win on **SEO continuity, migrations, accessibility, integrations, and accountability**.

**AI coding assistants change delivery cost, not willingness to pay — unless you pass speed through as price.** Published effects are mixed:

- Peng et al. (GitHub / ACM, widely cited): Copilot users completed a lab task **~55% faster** (n=95, recruited partly via Upwork — **limited generalizability**).
- GitHub + Accenture (13 May 2024): high adoption; earlier GitHub lab claim “up to **55%** faster.”
- Cui et al. / related enterprise field experiments: on the order of **~26%** more pull-request throughput.
- Google-internal RCT (arXiv 2410.12944): roughly **~21%** faster after controls; **not statistically significant** at p<0.05 in the best-fit model.
- Jellyfish (146k Jira tickets): **~20%** less coding time, **~26%** faster PR review — observational.
- Zoominfo (arXiv 2501.13282): developers reported **~20%** median time savings; **33%** suggestion acceptance.
- NAV IT longitudinal study (2025): **no significant commit-output increase** after Copilot adoption.

**Working assumption for a 1–2 person shop:** AI tools can cut **production** time on brochure sites **roughly 15–30%** if the operator already knows the stack; they do **not** cut sales, photography, content chasing, or GBP work. Pass some of that into **faster turnaround and a lower entry SKU**, not into a race to $299.

## 4. SE Arkansas / Monticello local landscape

### 4.1 Place and buyers

Monticello is Drew County’s seat and the commercial/education hub of a timber-and-Delta economy.

- **City:** Census QuickFacts, 1 Jul 2025 estimate **8,269** (down **2.3%** from 2020 base); 2020 Census **8,442**. 2022 retail sales **$290.3M**; health care & social assistance receipts **$97.4M**. Poverty **30.1%** (city); per capita income **$25,624** (2020–2024).
- **County:** population **17,054** (1 Jul 2025); median household income **$42,824** (2020–2024, 2024 dollars); per capita **$28,973**; QuickFacts poverty **17.8%** vs ACS S1701 5-year **22.4% (2024)** — **two official poverty figures; use both and do not flatten**. Broadband **80.5%**.
- **UAM** is the regional university and home of the state’s School of Forest Resources; ASBTDC at UAM is a **one-person regional SBDC** covering seven SE AR counties (SBA success-story page, 2023 award; UAM news 2025 still lists the same seven counties).
- **Hospital:** Baptist Health Medical Center–Drew County — Baptist Health began managing Oct 2023 and completed acquisition Dec 2023; 60-bed facility serving a claimed **~200,000** residents in SE AR (Monticello EDC).
- **Timber / industry:** Interfor Monticello mill (211 Old Troy Road); Maxwell Hardwood / flooring south of town; Hood Packaging; War Eagle / SeaArk boat legacy; Drew Foam. **Weyerhaeuser** announced **18 Nov 2024** an estimated **$500M** TimberStrand plant near Monticello and Warren and **~200** jobs (Arkansas EDC / Gov. Sanders). That is a **supplier and housing** demand pulse, not automatic website spend.
- **City comprehensive plan (2022)** describes downtown struggle, commercial shift to the **US-425 corridor**, loss of Burlington Mills, and health care overtaking manufacturing as the largest employment sector. Attracting business was the top 2021 survey concern.

## 7. Go-to-market for a small B2B web practice in Monticello / SE AR

**Positioning line (draft):** *Websites and Google that make the phone ring — built here, updated here, no disappearing freelancer.*

**Do not lead with “AI.”** Owners hear “I will be replaced” or “cheap template.” Use AI **behind** the 14-day clock.

### 7.1 Offer (suggested starting SKUs — **to be price-tested**, not market facts)

| SKU | Includes | Suggested test price | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Launch** | 6 pages, mobile, GBP cleanup, click-to-call, 1 form, 1 photo session, 14-day target | **$1,800–$3,500** | Sits above Durable/Wix DIY, below Little Rock custom |
| **Shop** | Shopify or Square Online, ≤50 products, payments, pickup/delivery copy | **$2,500–$6,000** + platform | Matches Upwork e-comm band without marketplace fees |
| **Care** | Hosting or platform admin, WP updates, backup, uptime, 60 min edits/mo, monthly GBP post | **$150–$400/mo** | Recurring; cheaper than a part-time employee |
| **Rescue** | Rebuild of an embarrassing or hacked site | Launch + $500–$1,000 | Urgency |

These prices are **hypotheses** informed by Upwork/Fiverr floors and national boutique blogs, **not** a SE AR survey.

### 7.2 Channels (in order)

1. **ASBTDC at UAM** — offer a free 45-minute “Google + website checkup” at their next workshop; become the implementation partner they can hand people to. Seven-county mandate matches the TAM.
2. **Monticello–Drew County Chamber** (335 E. Gaines; montdrewchamber.com; 870-367-6741) — membership + after-hours + directory. Their own stack is a Joomla-era chamber site — a quiet signal that **local institutional web is dated**.
3. **In-person teardowns** on US-425 and downtown: 20 shops, photograph the Google result on your phone, show the competitor who outranks them. This is the Close.
4. **Referral triad:** one CPA, one insurance agent, one printer / sign shop. They see every new LLC.
5. **Own GBP + LSA** for “website designer Monticello AR” so you don’t lose the people who search the category you just taught them.
6. **Crossbuck-style content** only after 5 local case studies: short phone-camera before/after reels, not a brand film.
7. **Do not** buy 50 city SEO pages. That is what Ohio/PA/Dallas shops do; it is why locals ignore the SERP.

### 7.3 Geography

- **Core:** Drew, Bradley, Lincoln, Cleveland (drive-to in a morning).
- **Secondary:** Ashley (Crossett/Hamburg), Desha (Dumas/McGehee), Chicot (Lake Village) via ASBTDC.
- **Optional:** Jefferson / Pine Bluff — more firms (**1,199** employer establishments) but Crossbuck + Little Rock gravity.
- **Not first:** statewide remote or Upwork as the brand. Use marketplaces for **overflow labor**, not customer acquisition, until local proof exists.

### 7.4 Sales motion

- One-page PDF, three SKUs, **50% to start / 50% at launch**, Care billed monthly card.
- Content delay clause: if photos/copy are late, the calendar slips, not the invoice.
- Guarantee that is **operational**, not legal: “GBP complete, site under 3s on a phone, click-to-call works — or we work free until it does.” Do **not** guarantee rankings or ADA/HIPAA.

## 8. Ops — scoping, contracts, hosting, retainers, AI margins

**Not legal advice. Have an Arkansas attorney review the first contract.**

### 8.1 Scoping

- **Intake:** 12-question form (services, service area, hours, 3 competitors, existing GBP, Facebook URL, must-have pages, booking/payments, who sends photos).
- **SOW = SKU + exclusions.** Exclusions: logo from scratch, custom app, copy for 20 services, ADA legal certification, print, drone, ongoing ads.
- **Change order** for anything not on the SKU. This is how Promethean-described **fixed-fee** work keeps its margin.
- **Timebox** Launch at **20–35 production hours** including revisions. If AI does not get you there, the SKU is underpriced.

### 8.2 Contracts (checklist, not a template)

- Parties, SKU, timeline, payment, late-content clause
- Client supplies license-clean photos or pays the photo session
- **IP:** client owns the site after final payment; you retain a portfolio license
- **Platform accounts** in the **client’s** name (Google, Shopify, Square, domain). This is a trust feature.
- Hosting/Care auto-renew, 30-day cancel
- Limitation of liability; no ranking / revenue promises
- For clinics: **no** “HIPAA compliant website” language; BAA only if you actually touch ePHI (you usually should **not**); strip marketing pixels from clinical forms
- Accessibility: “we follow WCAG 2.2 AA practices” ≠ “certified compliant”

### 8.3 Hosting and stack (keep it boring)

| Client type | Default stack | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brochure / contractor | Managed WordPress (Cloudways, WP Engine, or Flywheel-class) **or** Squarespace if they will self-edit | WP is the care-plan machine; Squarespace reduces ticket volume |
| Retail / makers | Shopify or Square Online | Payments and tax apps already exist |
| Clinic | Squarespace or WP **without** dark-pattern chat pixels; booking via a vendor they already use | Reduce tracker surface |
| One-off landing | Webflow or WP — only if you already operate it | Don’t add a third CMS in year one |

Avoid “$5 cPanel + 40 plugins” unless Care pricing includes the incident you will get at 10 p.m.

### 8.4 Maintenance retainers

National blogs (WP Super Help, The Web Factory) commonly cite **$200–$2,000/month** care or **10–20% of build cost per year**. Local SE AR will cluster at the **bottom**.

**Minimum viable Care:**

- Core/plugin/theme updates (WP) or platform monitoring
- Off-site backups + quarterly restore test
- Uptime ping
- SSL / broken form check
- 60 minutes of content or GBP work
- Annual “does this still match your hours and prices?” call

**Do not** include ads management in Care. That is a different SKU and a different liability.

### 8.5 AI productivity and margins

Promethean: average agency **net 14% (2024)**; studios can run higher. AI is the only realistic way a Monticello solo can hit **50%+ gross** on a $2,500 Launch without living on the project.

**Use AI for:** first-draft service copy, image compression, schema, alt text drafts, WP theme tweaks, Shopify section code, QA checklists.  
**Do not use AI for:** final legal/medical claims, unique local photography, GBP category strategy without looking at the map pack, or “set and forget” accessibility overlays.

**Margin math (illustrative only):**

- Launch at $2,500, 25 hours fully loaded at $40 opportunity cost = $1,000 labor → **60% gross** before photos/software.
- If AI saves 6 hours, either **keep 60% in 19 hours** (higher effective hourly) or **drop price to $2,000** to win the Facebook-only owner. Prefer **keeping price and cutting cycle time** so you can run more projects — Promethean’s fast growers were **project-heavy**.
- Care at $200/month × 25 clients = $5,000/month before hosting. That is the **floor that makes the practice a business**. 25 Care clients is a **year-2** number, not a week-2 number.

Software stack cost (Cursor/Copilot, hosting, Adobe or Affinity, Google Workspace) should stay **well under one Care client**.

## 9. Next validation steps

Do these before printing a rate card or registering a second LLC.

### 9.1 Local interviews (n=15, two weeks)

Script (30 minutes, record notes, not a sales pitch until the end):

1. How do new customers find you today?
2. Do you have a website, Facebook only, or both? Show me on your phone.
3. Have you claimed Google? Who last changed the hours?
4. What did you last pay for anything “online”?
5. If a local person could launch in two weeks for $X, would you do it? Test **$1,500 / $2,500 / $4,000**.
6. What would make you say no? (Trust, money, “nephew does it,” Wix already)

**Quota:** 5 contractors, 3 retailers/restaurants, 3 clinics/professionals, 2 civic/church/school staff, 2 B2B/ag or industrial. Use Chamber directory + drive the 425 corridor. Offer a one-page teardown as the thank-you.

### 9.2 Competitive teardowns (n=25 sites)

For each: load on a mid-range phone over cellular; note TTFB/LCP if easy; GBP completeness; click-to-call; HTTPS; last copyright year; platform (W3Techs or builtwith-by-eye); contact path.

**Must include:** 10 Monticello businesses, 5 Warren/Crossett, 5 Pine Bluff, Crossbuck’s own site, EvoDev (if live), Flypaper El Dorado page, 2 Studio 29/EZMarketing landing pages (to show owners the out-of-state pattern).

**Mystery-shop** (email only, same RFP) Crossbuck, Flypaper, one Little Rock boutique, one Fiverr Pro: price, timeline, whether they will drive to Monticello. **Do not invent their replies — log them.**

### 9.3 Pricing tests

- Three Chamber/ASBTDC conversations with **live SKUs**.
- One **spec site** for a fictional Drew County HVAC and one for a clinic — used as the leave-behind.
- Track: conversations → paid Launch → Care conversion at day 45. **Kill SKUs that do not convert after 10 honest pitches.**

### 9.4 Institutional

- 30 minutes with **Callie Becks / current ASBTDC-at-UAM director** (UAM news lists Becks in 2025; SBA 2023 story named Aimee Weaver — **confirm the sitting director**).
- Chamber ambassador breakfast.
- UAM School of Computer Information Systems: internship, not cheap labor as the brand.

### 9.5 Kill criteria

Stop or reposition if, after 15 interviews:

- Fewer than ~5 owners describe a **broken or missing** site/GBP as a real problem, **or**
- Nobody will discuss a number above **$1,000**, **or**
- A local incumbent is already productized at that price with Care and good reputation.

Then the play is **partner/sub to Crossbuck or Flypaper**, or a **narrow vertical** (e.g. only contractors), not a general Monticello agency.
