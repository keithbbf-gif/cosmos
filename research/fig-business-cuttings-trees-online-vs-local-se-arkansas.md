## Executive summary

Figs (*Ficus carica*, common type) fit **Southeast Arkansas’s climate** unusually well. University of Arkansas Cooperative Extension and the *Arkansas Democrat-Gazette* (Janet B. Carson, 14 August 2026) describe common figs as easily grown in **central and southern Arkansas**, with Celeste and Brown Turkey / Texas Everbearing as the locally familiar cultivars. Monticello sits in **USDA Plant Hardiness Zone 8b** on the 2023 map (third-party ZIP compilations; confirm on the official USDA map). That is a **mother-stock advantage** versus cold-climate fig farms that cut plants to the ground each winter.

The **commercial fruit** fig industry is not the opportunity here. AgMRC (revised April 2024) states California accounts for nearly **98%** of U.S. fig production. UAEX FSA-47 (Oct 2017) counted only **12 Arkansas farms** reporting figs in the **2012** Census of Agriculture. This brief could not extract a clean 2022 Arkansas farm-count/acreage pair from the Census PDF. The addressable business for a SE AR operator is **plants** (cuttings and trees), not dried-fig commodity fruit.

The **online plant market** is real, seasonal, and two-tiered:

1. **Backyard / big-box / catalog trees** — Celeste, Brown Turkey, Chicago Hardy, LSU Purple/Gold — typically **~$35–$70** for a small potted or mail-order tree; some 3-gallon garden-center SKUs around **$45–$50**.
2. **Collector cuttings** — hundreds of named clones, winter dormant sales, frequent sell-outs. Public list prices cluster around **$6–$15** per unrooted cutting at specialty shops; auction and “rare” listings run **higher and noisier**. Figaholics (Golden State Orchards, CA) reported **500–800 orders in the first hour** of its annual sale and a 30 Jan–8 Mar 2026 window; next sale expected **January 2027**.

**Local SE AR nursery structure (verified vs gap):** the official Arkansas Department of Agriculture nursery-licensing directories (fetched 14 Sep 2026) list **Fuller Lawn & Garden LLC** (Monticello) as a **nursery dealer**, and two **White Hall** nurserymen (Azalea Hill Gardens & Nursery; Native Sun Nursery). **No Drew County / Monticello fig specialist** appears on those lists. The only Arkansas-licensed **fig-named** grower found is **The American Fig Company** in **northwest Arkansas** (Canehill listing; news places the farm near Morrow / Farmington), which already sells cuttings and rooted fruit plants online and was largely **sold out** when checked.

**Highest-ranked hybrid hypothesis:** use SE AR’s long season to build **in-ground mother plants** of Gulf-South-adapted cultivars (Celeste, LSU releases, closed-eye types), sell **dormant unrooted cuttings by mail in winter** and **potted trees by local pickup / farmers-market / B2B in spring**, with education (variety ID, humidity/souring, hardiness) as the trust product. Main risks: Plant Board licensing, destination quarantines (especially California / Hawaii / soil rules), mislabeling, summer heat shipping, and a thin local population (~8,300 in Monticello; ~17,000 in Drew County).

## 1. Product map

Figs are sold as **propagation material** and as **finished plants**. Adjacent SKUs (other fruit cuttings/trees) appear on the same specialty sites because the customer overlap is high.

### 1.1 Core fig SKUs

| Product | What the buyer gets | Typical buyer | Seasonality (observed) | Shipping / ops notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unrooted hardwood cutting** | Dormant stick, often 6–12 in, ≥3 nodes | Collectors, hobby propagators, some landscapers starting cheap | Peak **Nov–Mar**; several shops run a **single annual drop** | Light, cheap to ship; no soil; buyer takes rooting risk. Figaholics specifies ~3/8–1 in diameter, 7–12 in, treated against mold, **no arrival/rooting guarantee** |
| **Unrooted green / semi-hardwood cutting** | Non-dormant wood | Same, plus impulse Etsy buyers | Anytime growth is available | Stores poorly; Lazy Dog Farm (26 Dec 2023) warns green wood is a poor winter-storage SKU |
| **Rooted cutting / liner** | Small plant in a 2–4 in or tree pot | Collectors who do not want to root; northern growers wanting a head start | **Apr–Jun** ship windows common | Needs heat/cold pack discipline; more loss in transit |
| **Potted tree (1–3+ gal)** | Finished plant, often own-root | Backyard growers, garden centers, landscapers | Spring retail peak; some ship year-round with heat holds | Heavy, expensive freight; Just Fruits and Exotics (FL) states they **never ship bare-root** and refuse CA/HI/AK |
| **Bare-root tree** | Dormant dug plant | Catalog buyers, some Southern growers | **Feb–Apr** (Peaceful Heritage, KY, states Feb–Apr bare-root, May–Oct potted) | Lower freight than pots; more destination-soil-rule friendly |
| **Rare / named collector clone** | Same physical products; the SKU is the **name** | OurFigs / FigBid / Facebook-group collectors | Sells out in hours–days on opening | **Identity risk** is the product risk (UAEX: name confusion is common) |
| **Bundles / starter packs** | Mixed cuttings | Beginners | Same as cuttings | American Fig Co. listed a “3 Cold Hardy Fig Package” at **$55** and a “Fig Lover’s Starter Bundle” at **$165** (sold out when fetched) |
| **Fresh fruit** (not a nursery SKU) | Perishable figs | Local retail / farm stand | **August** in Arkansas (Carson 2026) | TAMU: fruit must ripen on the tree and is quite perishable; modest commercial fruit ventures need a marketing plan. **Out of scope** as a primary nursery P&L |

### 1.2 Fig-adjacent products (relevant, not required)

Specialty fig nurseries already bundle other edible woody plants:

- **Trees of Joy** (Bethlehem, PA) — figs plus jujube, mulberry, pomegranate, pawpaw, kiwi (site fetched 14 Sep 2026).
- **The American Fig Company** (NW Arkansas) — figs plus elderberry, passion fruit, pawpaw, goji; free e-books on cold-climate figs and propagation (site fetched 14 Sep 2026).
- **Just Fruits and Exotics** — large fig list plus other fruitscapes; jujube and persimmon on the homepage.
- **Flomaton Famous** (Flomaton, AL) — “fig trees and specialty fruit plants,” Square store, YouTube / Famous Figs community.

For a SE AR hybrid, **pomegranate, mulberry, and muscadine** are climate-plausible adjacents. This brief did **not** price those SKUs systematically. Treat them as a later add-on after fig mother stock is proven.

### 1.3 What is *not* a product in Arkansas

UAEX (Sherri Sanders) and LSU AgCenter: only **common-type** figs (parthenocarpic; no wasp required) are appropriate. **Smyrna** types need caprification; **San Pedro** types need a wasp for the main crop. Figaholics explicitly warns buyers without fig wasps to avoid those classes. Do not treat Calimyrna / Bursa-type SKUs as backyard SE AR plants.

### 2.3 Shipping seasons (pattern, not a rule)

Observed from first-party pages:

- **Dormant unrooted cuttings:** November–March (Kremp, Cold Hardy Figs, American Fig Co. news, TAMU hardwood-cutting guidance). Figaholics ran late January–early March 2026. Dingdong’s next drop is **1 January 2027**. Off the Beaten Path: **late November / early December**.
- **Rooted liners / potted trees:** April–June common (Cold Hardy Figs: trees from April; Kremp rooted: late April–June). Just Fruits lets the buyer pick a Monday ship date. One Green World noted heat/wildfire delays when fetched.
- **Heat holds:** national mail-order nurseries routinely pause live-plant shipping in high heat. A SE AR summer ship of potted trees to the Upper Midwest is a **loss-rate problem**, not a merchandising problem.
- **International:** Figaholics no longer ships outside the U.S. Etsy restricts cross-border live plants/cuttings for several countries. USDA phytosanitary certificates apply to **export**, not automatically to every interstate box.

### 2.4 Cold-climate vs Southern demand

Two different jobs-to-be-done:

| Segment | What they optimize | Cultivars they hunt | What SE AR can sell them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold-climate collectors (zones 5–7)** | Early ripening, dieback recovery, pot culture, Chicago-type hardiness | Chicago Hardy, Ronde de Bordeaux, Florea, Green Michurinska, many “unknowns” from northern yards | Cuttings and small rooted plants **in spring**; hardiness education. Competing with PA/KY/NWA growers who already brand “cold hardy” |
| **Southern / Gulf backyard (zones 8–9)** | Closed eye (rain/souring), fig rust, heat, in-ground size | Celeste, Brown Turkey / Texas Everbearing, LSU Purple, Gold, Tiger, Champagne, O’Rourke / Improved Celeste, Scott’s Black, Hunt | **Finished trees** and locally proven cuttings. | **National catalog buyer** | One reliable tree, warranty, easy checkout | Chicago Hardy, Brown Turkey, Celeste | Hard to beat Stark / Trees.com / Depot on CAC; compete on **variety + advice**, not on a 1-quart Chicago Hardy |

Figaholics (California, long season) is the **flavor/collector** pole. Cold Hardy Figs and American Fig Company are the **zone 5–6 survival** pole. A Monticello nursery sits on the **Gulf-South performance** pole — closer to LSU’s breeding target than to Pennsylvania.

## 3. Cultivar / variety dynamics

### 3.1 Extension “what actually grows here”

**Arkansas (UAEX, Sherri Sanders; Carson / Democrat-Gazette 14 Aug 2026 and the 2016 UAEX reprint):**

- **Celeste** — most cold-hardy of the commonly sold AR figs; small brown fruit; **tight/closed eye** (less souring / dried-fruit beetle); ripens before Brown Turkey; **does not rebound quickly** from winter kill or hard late-winter pruning (pruning Celeste hard can erase that year’s crop).
- **Brown Turkey / Texas Everbearing** — slightly less hardy; **rebounds faster** after freeze or pruning; longer bearing window.
- Common figs fruit on **current-season wood**, so freeze-to-ground trees can still crop.
- **Breba** (overwintered crop) rarely persists in Arkansas.
- **Name confusion is expected.** UAEX: 700+ listed cultivars worldwide; only about **50** in the U.S. nursery trade; same clone under several names.
- Winter injury is worse in **northern Arkansas**; southern Arkansas is the easy belt.

**Louisiana / Gulf South (LSU AgCenter, 1 Jun 2015 news; LSU release PDFs):**

- Celeste is the most popular backyard fig in Louisiana; Brown Turkey more popular elsewhere in the Southeast.
- LSU program (O’Rourke 1950s; Johnson-era releases): **LSU Purple** (1991), **LSU Gold** (1995), **Tiger, Champagne, O’Rourke** (2007). Later mentions include **Scott’s Black**.
- **O’Rourke** was circulated as “Improved Celeste” before official release (LSU Southern Garden Q&A, 2015).
- Breeding goal: fruit size, cold-hardiness, **disease resistance** for the humid Gulf South (fig rust / Cercospora complex called out in HortScience release notes).
- Closed or partially closed eyes matter in rainy Augusts (UAEX souring note; TAMU dried-fruit beetle / open-eye warning).

**Texas (TAMU HORT-PU-025, orig. Nov 2013):** Alma, Celeste, Texas Everbearing as standards; LSU Purple among trial suggestions; fig rust is the greatest disease threat; no conventional fungicide labeled for rust in TAMU’s write-up (copper organics used at onset).

### 3.2 Collector demand (what sells out)

OurFigs “Figaholics 2025/2026 Sellouts” (posted 31 Jan 2026) lists varieties that sold out in the **first 24 hours** two years running, including Belleclare Sport, Beltrana, Black Portuguese (Belleclare), Cardinello, Castel Trosino, Craven’s Craving, De La Gloria, Dels Ermitans, GM 175 Gozo Gem, Moro di Caneva. Newer 2025/26 first-day sellouts included Coll de Dama Gegantina and several “(New)” names.

**Interpretation (medium confidence):** collector demand concentrates on **flavor-famous or newly released clones** (Black Madeira / Craven’s Craving identity cluster, Coll de Dama / Col de Dame types, Italian heirlooms), not on Celeste. Figaholics itself says it generally will not sell a variety until they have **picked fruit** to validate the name — that is the trust standard collectors want.

### 3.3 Two segments, two catalogs

| | **Backyard / SE AR local** | **Collector / national mail-order** |
|---|---|---|
| Catalog size | 6–12 proven SKUs | 50–400 names |
| Purchase driver | “Will it fruit here and not sour?” | “Is this the real Smith / CdD / BM?” |
| Price tolerance | Sensitive (see §6 and Monticello income) | High for rare names; low for commodity clones |
| Failure mode | Dead first winter / sour fruit / rust | Mislabel, mosaic, weak cutting, mold in transit |
| Education that sells | Hardiness, eye type, LSU vs Celeste vs BT | Strain notes, fruit videos, forum reputation |

**Do not** build a local nursery SKU mix from Figaholics sellout lists. Build it from UAEX/LSU/TAMU, then offer a **small** collector add-on once mother plants fruit and are photographed.

### 4.1 Climate / zone fit

| Fact | Source | Confidence |

**Business implication:** Monticello can grow **large in-ground mother plants** and take **heavy dormant cutting harvests** without the Zone 6 “cut to the ground and mulch” system American Fig Company describes for northwest Arkansas. The tradeoff is **humidity**: rust, fruit souring, and splitting in wet Augusts. Closed-eye LSU/Celeste types are the local product, not open-eye California dessert figs.

## 5. Operations (propagation, media, winter, shipping rules, lead times, loss)

### 5.1 Propagation (what published sources actually say)

**Hardwood dormant cuttings (standard commercial method)**  
TAMU: 6–10 in, ~½–1 in diameter; optional 10–14 day callus in a warm humid bag; then commercial potting media. Softwood needs **mist**.

**Figaholics spec (what a top seller ships):** 3/8–1 in (rarely thinner), 7–12 in, ≥3 nodes; mold treatment; **buyer owns rooting outcome**.

**Lazy Dog Farm (26 Dec 2023, grower blog — not a trial report):** prefers current-year (“new”) wood; ≥¼–½ in; beginners ≥8 in and 3–4 nodes; green cuttings root fast but do not store; take wood after full dormancy if you are not a commercial greenhouse. **Beginner success “around 50%”; their own commercial lots “high 80s or 90th percentile”; never 100%.** Start twice as many as needed.

**Peer-reviewed ranges (do not treat as your nursery’s rate):**

- Aljane & Nahdi, *Int. J. Fruit Sci.* / PMC4897291: field hardwood cuttings of Tunisian cultivars, **root emergence 10–90%** depending on cultivar and cutting type.
- Romano et al., *Plants* 14(2):160 (2025), cv. Dottato, heated bench + mist, no hormone: **50–86.8%** rooting in 30 days; two-year wood and **20 cm** cuttings beat tip/short wood. Media after rooting: perlite vs soil/peat/sand.

**SE AR calendar (inferred from climate + sources, not a local trial):**

| Window | Activity |
|---|---|
| After first hard freeze / leaf drop (often Nov) | Take dormant cuttings; grade; label |
| Nov–Feb | Ship unrooted; or store in cooler (Figaholics: walk-in cooler) |
| Feb–Mar | Stick remaining cuttings (bottom heat ~70–72 °F is a common grower practice; Cold Hardy Figs describes Promix FPX in 2 in pots on **72 °F sand beds**) |
| Apr–Jun | Pot up; sell rooted / 1 gal; last spring plant-out |
| Jun–Oct | Grow-on 3 gal; **do not** mail pots in extreme heat without a protocol |
| Aug | Fruit on mother plants; photography for next year’s SKU pages (Figaholics model) |
| By 15 Aug | Arkansas **nursery inspection application** deadline (2 CAR § 50-303) |

### 5.2 Media and facilities (descriptive, not a recipe)

Published / first-party media mentions:

- TAMU: commercial potting media after callus.
- Cold Hardy Figs: Promix FPX, heated sand, full sun in early spring.
- Romano et al.: perlite on basal-heated benches (23±2 °C), then perlite or 1:1:1 soil/peat/sand.
- Just Fruits: ships **in the pot it was grown in**.

A Monticello operation can root **outdoors or in an unheated hoop** more months of the year than a zone 5 farm. That is a **capex advantage**. Humidity and summer heat raise **fungus / soft-cutting** risk — budget a covered bench, not a mister over green wood in July until you have data.

### 5.3 Overwintering

- **In-ground mothers, zone 8b:** usually no structure. Carson: mulch roots; young trees more tender; early/late freezes worse than mid-winter dormancy. TAMU: water before a hard freeze if the fall was dry; hay mounds / stuffed cages for young plants.
- **Potted inventory:** the liability. Pots freeze faster than ground. Need a **heel-in bed, unheated shed, or packed hoop**. This is the inventory-risk difference vs cuttings (which live in a cooler).
- **NW AR contrast:** American Fig Company cuts to the ground and mulches — a method you should **not** copy in Monticello if the goal is large cutting volume.

### 5.4 Phytosanitary / shipping (high level — not legal advice)

**Arkansas (grower or dealer inside the state)**

- Selling nursery stock in person requires a **nurseryman, dealer, agent, or landscape-contractor license** (2 CAR § 50-302(d); 2 CAR § 50-303).
- Nurseries inspected at least annually; **apply by 15 August** (2 CAR § 50-303).
- Stock sold or transported **within or into** Arkansas must carry a **certificate of inspection or dealer’s certificate** on the outer container (2 CAR § 50-302(a)).
- Out-of-state shippers into Arkansas must file origin inspection documentation and attach origin certificates (2 CAR § 50-302(b)); no fee for those out-of-state shippers under (c).
- Dealer application portal (AccessGov) states Level 1/2/3 fees **$150 / $300 / $450** plus **$0.90/tag**, renewal window **15 Aug–31 Oct**. Older Circular 11 PDFs also mention a **$10** license fee plus inspection tiers — **fee schedules may have been restated; use the current portal, not this brief.**
- License year on older Circular 11: **1 Nov–31 Oct**. Confirm.

**Interstate / mail**

- National Plant Board publishes **state law summaries** (Arkansas summary last marked **April 2022** on the NPB index — **stale; verify**). Destination rules differ. California’s 2026 NPB summary: official paper certificates for quarantine compliance; **no QR-only**; Japanese beetle / soil rules are a common reason mail-order nurseries **refuse CA** or ship **bare-root / soil-free only**.
- Just Fruits and Trees.com / Tree Center **do not ship** to various combinations of AK, AZ, CA, HI, OR, PR. Pattern: **soil + quarantine states are expensive to serve**. Unrooted dormant cuttings are easier than potted trees.
- USPS Publication 52 §56: plants are mailable domestically **only if** federal/state prohibitions do not make the shipment unlawful. Publication 14 restates USDA nursery-stock authorities. **Mailable ≠ legal for that destination.**
- Etsy plant policy: buyers and sellers must follow applicable law; live plants/cuttings/soil have extra labeling; **cross-border** live plants are restricted for several countries.
- Figaholics / collector forums discuss fig mosaic as ubiquitous; Figaholics **discloses FMV** and sells anyway. That is a **reputation choice**, not a regulatory clearance.

**International export** needs APHIS phytosanitary certificates (XPM). Out of scope for a U.S. hybrid launch.

### 5.5 Lead times

| Output | Lead time (order-of-magnitude from sources + horticulture, not a SE AR trial) |
|---|---|
| Unrooted cutting from existing mother | Weeks (dormancy) — you already have the wood |
| Sellable rooted liner | ~3–6 months after stick (Lazy Dog: green wood “weeks” to bud; hardwood is slower) |
| 1 gal / small tree | **One growing season** |
| 3 gal garden-center tree | **Often two seasons** |
| New rare SKU you must fruit-verify first (Figaholics standard) | **2–4 years** to a photographed crop |

Figaholics: first-hour orders can wait **2–4+ weeks** to ship because of volume, not biology.

### 5.6 Loss rates (ranges only — your trial will differ)

| Stage | Published / first-party range | Use in planning |
|---|---|---|
| Rooting, beginner | ~50% (Lazy Dog) | Stick 2× intended sales |
| Rooting, practiced / good cultivar | high 80s–90s (Lazy Dog); 50–87% Dottato trial; 10–90% Tunisia trial | Never quote 100%; Figaholics does not guarantee rooting |
| Transit mold / dessication (cuttings) | Not quantified; Figaholics treats and still disclaims | Winter Priority Mail; avoid weekend holds in heat |
| Transit death (potted) | Not quantified here | Spring ship; heat holds; local pickup preferred |
| Summer unsold 3 gal | **Inventory risk** — no public figure | Price down at Market in the Park rather than overwinter pots cheaply |

## 7. Online vs local nursery comparison

| Dimension | Online (cuttings / small plants) | Local nursery / pickup (potted trees) |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross margin on goods** | High on **owned** wood; low if reselling bought cuttings | Medium; pots and time dominate |
| **Freight** | Cuttings: Figaholics-like **$12–$26** covers many sticks. Trees: freight can kill | Near zero if pickup; Market in the Park stall time is the cost |
| **CAC** | Paid search and marketplace ads are expensive relative to a $10 SKU. Specialty shops rely on **OurFigs / YouTube / annual-sale FOMO** (Figaholics 500–800 orders/hour is demand concentration, not a CAC formula) | Chamber, Master Gardener sale, UAM, Facebook, word of mouth. Low cash CAC; **slow** |
| **Trust** | Weak until reviews + fruit photos + licenses. Mislabel is the industry scar (UAEX). Figaholics invests in videos of **340+** fruited varieties | High if the tree is in fruit in August and the grower is at Market in the Park |
| **Seasonality** | Sharp winter spike; summer is content/mother-care | Spring plant sales; August fruit as marketing |
| **Inventory risk** | Cooler of sticks; if unsold, **root them** | Pots occupy space all summer; freeze in winter |
| **Regulatory load** | Same Plant Board license + **every destination’s** rules | Mostly Arkansas certificate + local sales |
| **Returns / guarantees** | Figaholics: **no** rooting guarantee. Fig 4 All: arrival window. Big catalogs: 1-year live guarantees (Trees.com “Alive & Thrive”) — that is a **cost** | Face-to-face replacement is cheaper than shipping a dead 3 gal twice |
| **Competition** | National, sophisticated, already branded | Sparse **specialty** competition in Drew County (see §4.3) |
| **Fit to SE AR income** | National ASP can be high | Must match local willingness to pay |

**Hybrid logic:** use **local** for trust, fruit proof, and heavy pots; use **online winter cuttings** for SKU breadth and cash without freight. That is the same split Figaholics (cuttings only) and Just Fruits (pots only) each chose one side of — a Monticello operator can hold **both** because land/season allow mothers *and* a Saturday market.

## 8. Ranked opportunity hypotheses (SE AR + online hybrid)

Rank is **strategic fit**, not a financial ranking. All assume legal licensing is obtained.

### 1. Local pickup trees + winter mail-order cuttings (core hybrid) — **strongest fit**

**Offer:** 8–12 Gulf-South cultivars as 1–3 gal in spring (Celeste, Brown Turkey/Texas Everbearing, LSU Purple, Gold, Tiger, Champagne, O’Rourke, Scott’s Black, Hunt, maybe Chicago Hardy for pots). In winter, sell **unrooted cuttings** of the same plus a short collector list, US-only, with published ship brackets like Figaholics.

**Rationale:** Zone 8b mothers; LSU genetics match humidity; Fuller is a dealer not a fig grower; American Fig Co. is 5+ hours away and optimized for cold; Market in the Park has **no vendor fee** (page text); Master Gardener sale is a one-day demand pulse.

**Risks:** Must actually license and pass inspection; summer pot inventory; cutting mislabel; sold-out culture trains buyers to **one day a year** (cash-flow lumpiness).

### 2. Education-led brand: variety ID + humidity / hardiness guidance — **strong differentiator**

**Offer:** Care cards, fruit photos from **this** farm, “closed eye vs open eye,” Celeste-vs-O’Rourke naming, rust sanitation. YouTube/Facebook in the Flomaton Famous / Figaholics pattern, but aimed at **Arkansas–Louisiana–Mississippi–east Texas**.

**Rationale:** UAEX already says names are confused. Collectors pay for **identity**. Backyard buyers fail on souring and freeze, then blame the seller.

**Risks:** Content is unpaid CAC until the list converts; wrong ID destroys the brand.

### 3. B2B liners to landscapers and garden-center dealers — **medium, worth a quote round**

**Offer:** 50-count Celeste / LSU Purple liners in spring to Fuller-type dealers, El Dorado/Camden/Magnolia dealers, and landscape contractors on the Plant Board list.

**Rationale:** Dealers need a certified grower. White Hall nurseries are azalea/native, not fruit. Big-box Brown Turkey at $45–$50 leaves room for a **better cultivar story** at similar retail.

**Risks:** Wholesale price pressure; you become Flowerwood’s tiny rival; payment terms; you must hit **size specs**. No public wholesale fig liner price was found — **quote before building**.

### 4. Farmers-market + Master Gardener as the only local storefront — **low capex test**

**Offer:** No retail greenhouse year one. One Market in the Park Saturday + April MG sale + UAM/Extension workshop table.

**Rationale:** Tests willingness to pay without rent. Matches cottage-style market rules (homegrown).

**Risks:** Volume too small to justify mothers; weather; market copy may be outdated; still need a **license** if selling nursery stock.

### 5. Fig-adjacent cuttings (pomegranate, mulberry, muscadine) — **later**

**Rationale:** Trees of Joy / American Fig Co. already do this; same packing line.

**Risks:** Dilutes fig identity; muscadine and grape have their own pest/permit issues. Validate fig first.

### 6. Subscription / “cuttings club” — **weak until reputation exists**

**Rationale:** Smooths Figaholics-style lumpiness.

**Risks:** You promise SKUs you may lose to freeze or mislabel; subscriptions amplify refunds; CAC to fill a club is high. Revisit in year 3.

### 7. Rare-only collector play (FigBid / 400-name catalog) — **crowded**

**Rationale:** High ASP on FigBid ($80+ rooted Smith, etc.).

**Risks:** Figaholics, Trees of Joy, OTBP, Dingdong’s, and hundreds of hobby sellers already own this. Identity wars. SE AR humidity may **fail** open-eye dessert clones, producing bad reviews. Use as a **sidecar**, not the business.

### 8. Fresh-fig fruit stand — **not the nursery business**

TAMU: perishable, limited commercial success in Texas. Carson: August harvest. Possible **add-on** at Market in the Park to advertise trees. Do not fund the nursery from fruit.

## 10. Next validation steps

Do these **in order**. Cheap tests first. No step is “the business is approved.”

### A. Regulatory and identity (week 1–2)

1. Call **Arkansas Department of Agriculture Plant Industries** (directory page contact: 1 Natural Resources Dr., Little Rock; 501-225-1598) and ask — as a prospective **nurseryman** — inspection timing, current fees, whether **unrooted fig cuttings** are nursery stock (almost certainly yes), and certificate format for outbound USPS.
2. Read the current **Arkansas NPB summary** (the index still said April 2022) and destination summaries for any state you intend to ship (CA, AZ, HI, OR first).
3. Decide **ship / no-ship** list before building a cart (copy Just Fruits’ CA/HI/AK stance if certificates are not worth it).

### B. Local demand checks (week 2–4, in person)

4. Walk **Fuller Lawn & Garden**, Monticello Walmart garden, and any open Pine Bluff/White Hall yard. Photograph **actual fig SKUs and prices**. Do not rely on this brief for inventory.
5. Ask Drew County Extension and Drew County Master Gardeners what people request, and whether the **April 2027** plant sale takes vendor plants.
6. Confirm **Market in the Park 2026/27** dates and plant-vendor rules with the Chamber (870-367-6741).
7. Informal interviews: 15 backyard fig owners (church, UAM, market). Ask cultivar name (expect “the old fig”), winter kill, and whether they would **pay** for a labeled LSU tree vs a free cutting from a neighbor.

### C. Competitor teardowns (desktop + one order)

8. **Mystery-shop one cutting order** in the next dormant season from each of: an Arkansas seller (American Fig Co.), a flat-price shop (Dingdong’s or OTBP), and Figaholics if the 2027 sale is open. Score: packing, nodes, mold, label accuracy, days to ship.
9. Archive **Figaholics / OTBP / AFC** price pages **during a live sale** (this brief could not capture Figaholics SKU prices off-season).
10. Tear down FigBid sold comps for **Celeste, LSU Purple, Chicago Hardy, Black Madeira** over one winter (spreadsheet; n will be small).
11. Watch Flomaton Famous / Figaholics YouTube for **positioning**, not agronomy gospel.

### D. Propagation trials (one season, before scaling)

12. Stick **≥20 cuttings each** of Celeste, Brown Turkey, one LSU, one “hard” collector (e.g. Black Madeira — Figaholics notes it is harder to root). Record media, bottom heat Y/N, % rooted at 30/60 days. Compare to Lazy Dog / Romano ranges.
13. Overwinter a **tray of pots** vs in-ground stools. Count losses.
14. Do **not** sell a clone until it fruits on **your** plant if you are making a true-to-type claim.

### E. Channel tests (year 1, small)

15. One **Market in the Park** Saturday with 20 labeled plants and a cutting waitlist QR. Record ask-price vs sold-price.
16. One **B2B conversation** with Fuller and one El Dorado/Camden dealer: liner spec, price, volume. If both say no, drop B2B for a year.
17. A **simple own-site** winter cutting list (PayPal/Square) before Etsy. Revisit Etsy only if you accept ~10%+ fees and plant-policy labeling (verify live fee schedule).

### F. Kill criteria (write these down now)

- Cannot obtain / keep a Plant Board certificate.  
- Rooting **<40%** after one tuned trial on easy cultivars.  
- Local pickup demand is only “free cuttings from my grandma.”  
- Mystery-shopped identity error rate on **your** labels is non-zero.
