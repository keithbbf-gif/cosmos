# Southeast / South-Central Arkansas: Standalone Websites vs Facebook, Pricing, Supply, and a 3-Year Planning P&L

**Study date:** 20 September 2026  
**Geography:** Drew County / Monticello core, plus Ashley, Bradley, Chicot, Cleveland, Desha, and Lincoln counties. Pine Bluff (Jefferson) and El Dorado (Union) appear only as competitive-supply context.  
**Purpose:** Diligence brief for deciding whether and how to sell productized websites and “Facebook-upgrade” packages in this market.  
**Status of numbers:** Market counts are from U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts / SBA Advocacy state profiles as cited. Local presence classes come from a **convenience web sample** (n = 78 classified entities), not a census of all establishments. Agency and platform prices are **public-page or directory** figures, labeled verified vs third-party. The P&L is an **illustrative planning model**, not a forecast, guarantee, or investment advice.

**Not legal, tax, accounting, or investment advice.**

## 1. Market definition & geography

### 1.1 Study area

**Primary trade area (this brief’s “SE / South-Central AR”):** the seven counties already treated as one rural SBDC region by the University of Arkansas at Monticello ASBTDC office — **Ashley, Bradley, Chicot, Cleveland, Desha, Drew, and Lincoln**. The SBA’s 2023 Excellence and Innovation write-up of that office states it is a one-person center serving exactly those seven counties ([SBA success story, UAM ASBTDC](https://www.sba.gov/success-story/2023-arkansas-small-business-development-center-excellence-innovation-center-award)).

**Hub:** Monticello (Drew County), county seat and home of UAM. City population estimate 8,269 (1 July 2025) ([Census QuickFacts: Monticello city, AR](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/monticellocityarkansas/PST045225)).

### 1.3 Employer vs nonemployer shape

Seven-county **employer establishments (2023): 1,686**. **Nonemployers: 5,428**. Nonemployers outnumber employers about **3.2 to 1**. That is the structural fact for a web-services pitch:

- Most “businesses” are sole props / side gigs / farms / trades with no staff.
- Website willingness-to-pay concentrates in the **employer** set (and the more serious nonemployers that already buy insurance, a truck, or a shop).
- A product that needs a $5,000 custom build is a **few-dozen-per-year** market here, not a few-hundred.

SBA Office of Advocacy **Arkansas 2025/2026 state profile**: 292,728 small businesses (99.3% of Arkansas businesses); 501,512 small-business employees (46.1% of state employment). Industry mix is statewide, not SE-AR specific ([SBA Advocacy Arkansas 2025 PDF](https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Arkansas_2025-State-Profile.pdf); [Arkansas 2026 PDF](https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Arkansas.pdf)). The 2024 profile (older vintage) had 280,772 small businesses ([SBA Advocacy Arkansas 2024](https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Arkansas.pdf)).

ASBTDC statewide 2025 scorecard (updated January 2026): $66.2M capital infusion, 188 business starts, 2,576 Arkansans assisted ([ASBTDC Results](https://asbtdc.org/results/)). The UAM office is one consultant covering seven counties — useful as a **referral partner**, not a demand database (they do not publish county-level client counts).

### 1.4 Income constraints that shape willingness to pay

1. **Household median ~30% below the Arkansas median** in Drew, Ashley, Bradley, Chicot, Desha. Owners who “pay themselves last” will treat a $3,000–$8,000 website as a capital project, not a utility.
2. **Poverty 18–28%** in most of the study counties vs 15.5% statewide. Discretionary marketing spend is thin outside medical, legal, banking, dealerships, and a handful of destination restaurants.
3. **Broadband at 80.5% of Drew households** (vs 86.7% statewide) means some customers and some owners still live partly offline. Facebook-on-phone is the path of least resistance.
4. **Population is falling** (Chicot −10.8%, Desha −12.4% since 2020). The customer base is not growing; share-of-wallet and out-of-area traffic (UAM, hunting/fishing, highway 425/65) matter more than “more locals next year.”
5. **SAIPE vs ACS gap** ($55k vs $43k in Drew) is a reminder not to price as if the county were “middle-income Arkansas.” Many households that lift SAIPE still do not own the businesses that would buy a site.

**Practical WTP ceiling (planning hypothesis, not a measured local survey):** for an independent restaurant, salon, HVAC shop, or farm-supply store in this geography, a **$400–$1,500 setup + $45–$129/month** package is in the same conversation as a radio spot, a Monticello Live banner, or a part-time bookkeeper. A **$4,800–$30,000** Little Rock agency quote is a different market (hospitals, banks, multi-location medical). That hypothesis is tested against public package pages in §4 and should be price-tested in §10.

## 2. Digital presence audit method

### 2.1 What was sampled

**Design:** convenience, multi-category, web-visible sample of **named businesses and churches** that a resident or visitor would actually try to find. Not a random draw from a licensing file.

**Frames used (20 September 2026):**

| Frame | How used | Bias |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google web search for category + town (“restaurants Monticello AR website”, HVAC, dentist, hotel, farm, attorney) | Primary discovery | Over-represents businesses that already rank — i.e. those with websites |
| Explore Monticello dining list | Town tourism page | Franchise-heavy; incomplete independent set ([Explore Monticello dining](https://exploremonticello.com/activities/dining/)) |
| Monticello Live “Local Websites” / sponsor rail | Persistent local advertisers | Over-represents firms that already pay for local media ([example rail](https://www.monticellolive.com/drew-farm-supply-70/)) |
| Chamber / civic mentions | Monticello-Drew Chamber site (thin public directory) | Chamber site lists a few new members, not a full roster ([montdrewchamber.com](http://www.montdrewchamber.com/mdc/)) |
| Official org sites (hospitals, UAM, churches) | Direct fetch | High website rate by nature |
| Facebook / aggregator pages (Restaurantji, RestaurantJump, Sluurpy, Glonstruct) | Used when search returned `facebook.com` as the “website” | Better at finding Facebook-only restaurants/trades |

**Frames not used (and why that matters):**

- **No Google Maps / GBP scrape** of every pin in the seven counties. That would be the gold-standard sampling frame. This brief used search + named lists instead.
- **No Facebook Graph / page census.**
- **No ADEQ, contractor licensing, or ABC restaurant permit dump.** Those lists exist and should be used in the next validation pass (§10).
- **No in-person Main Street walk** of Monticello, Warren, Crossett, McGehee, or Star City.
- **No owner interviews.** “Why Facebook-only” reasons in §3.4 are inferred from (a) national surveys and (b) observable local patterns — they are **not** quoted from Drew County owners.

### 2.2 Presence classes

Each entity was placed in one class from public evidence:

| Class | Definition used here |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Website + Facebook** | Own domain (or institution site) **and** a confirmed Facebook Page URL or an on-site Facebook link |
| **B. Website only** | Own domain found; no Facebook Page confirmed in the time budget (may still have one) |
| **C. Facebook / social-only** | Search and directories list Facebook (or Instagram) as the website; no independent domain found |
| **D. GBP / directory / none** | Visible on Google / Restaurantji / Chamber-of-commerce.com / Monticello Live ads, but no first-party site and no Facebook URL confirmed |

**Classification rules:**

- National/franchise booking sites (IHG, Hilton, Wyndham, State Farm agent locators, Farmers agent locators) count as **website** — the business has a durable URL a customer can land on — but they are **not** a local-web-studio sale. Tagged “corporate/franchise stack.”
- Third-party menu farms (`hey-restaurants.com`, `menu-page.com`) are **not** websites.
- `yourwebsitespace.com` and similar builder subdomains **are** websites (DIY/low-end).
- HTTP checks from this environment failed for some live sites (TLS/SNI). Content previously retrieved (e.g. Ray’s at `http://rayshamburgers.com`) was accepted. `evodevdesigns.com` did not resolve here — flagged **possibly inactive**.

### 2.3 Sample size

**n = 78** classified entities (see the roster in §3.2).

Approximate mix (some entities sit on a category boundary):

| Category | n |
| --- | --- |
| Restaurants / food | 18 |
| Auto / diesel / tire | 6 |
| Medical / dental / vet / pharmacy / hospital | 12 |
| Churches | 5 |
| Contractors / trades / pest / tree | 8 |

### 2.4 Limitations (read before the percentages)

1. **Search bias inflates Class A/B.** Businesses with websites are easier to find on the open web. Facebook-only shops are systematically under-counted unless a directory happens to print `facebook.com` as the website.
2. **n = 78 is small** relative to 1,686 employer establishments. Treat percentages as **directional**, ±10–15 points, not as a county census.
3. **Facebook confirmation is incomplete.** Many Class B entities almost certainly have a Page. True “website + Facebook” is higher than Class A as coded.
4. **Churches and hospitals are not the commercial TAM** but they show that “serious local institutions” already buy websites.
5. **No ADEQ/licensing universe** means we cannot say “X% of licensed HVAC firms have sites.”
6. **Monticello Live advertisers are not a random sample** — they already spend on marketing.
7. **Geo-doorway SEO farms** (Studio 29, EZMarketing, Marketing Local SEO) pollute search for “web design Monticello” and are **not** local shops.

**Honest bottom line on method:** this is a **diligence reconnaissance**, good enough to see the split by type and to size a planning model. It is **not** a statistically representative audit. The next $0 step is a GBP scrape of Monticello + Warren + Crossett + McGehee + Star City (§10).

## 3. Websites vs Facebook — findings

### 3.1 Headline from the sample (with the bias stated)

Of **78** classified entities:

| Class | Count | Share of sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Website + Facebook (confirmed both) | 14 | 18% |
| B. Website only (Facebook not confirmed) | 36 | 46% |
| **A+B combined (has a first-party or corporate URL)** | **50** | **64%** |
| C. Facebook / social-only | 14 | 18% |
| D. GBP / directory / none confirmed | 14 | 18% |
| **C+D combined (no independent commercial website)** | **28** | **36%** |

**How to read this:** 64% “have a website” is an **upper bound** for *visible, searchable* businesses. National SMB surveys (below) say **~33–40% of small businesses have no dedicated website**, and **Facebook is more common than a site**. In a rural county, the true no-website share among *all* employer + serious nonemployer firms is likely **closer to 45–60%**, because the long tail never appears in a Google-web sample.

**Class C is the sellable core** for a Facebook-upgrade product: restaurants, independent trades, some salons, some farm-adjacent retail.

**Class A/B medical, legal, bank, dealer, hospital, church** are mostly **not** first-time website buyers. They are rebuild / SEO / care / booking prospects — if at all.

### 3.5 Why Facebook-only vs website

#### National / U.S. surveys (cite these; they are not SE-AR measurements)

**Small Business Majority, digital-transformation research (surveys Oct 2022–Apr 2024; report 2024):**

- About **one-third** of small businesses **do not have a website**.
- **41%** say they use **social media instead of a website**.
- **48%** cite **lack of time/resources** as a major reason they have no site.
- **36%** say they **don’t know how** to create or run a site.
- **36%** say **cost to build and maintain is prohibitive**.
- Facebook is the top social platform for SMBs (**~70%**), Instagram **~58%**.
- **~8%** have **neither** a site nor social.

Sources: [SBM press release](https://smallbusinessmajority.org/press-release/new-research-report-half-smallest-businesses-don-t-utilize-ai-one-third-do-not-have-business-website); [SBM PDF](https://smallbusinessmajority.org/sites/default/files/research-reports/small-businesses-face-obstacles-opportunities-growing-online-presence.pdf); companion pandemic-era PDF with the same 33%/41%/48%/36% battery ([SBM digital transformation PDF](https://smallbusinessmajority.org/sites/default/files/research-reports/digital-transformation-small-businesses-face-obstacles-opportunities-in-wake-of-the-pandemic.pdf)).

**BrightLocal SMB Marketing Report 2025:** only **40%** of surveyed SMBs say they have a **dedicated business website**, vs **50% on Facebook** and **44% on Instagram**. 89% claim to “invest in organic SEO” — a knowledge-gap finding, not proof of websites ([BrightLocal SMB Marketing 2025](https://brightlocal.com/research/smb-marketing-2025/)).

**BrightLocal Local Business Discovery & Trust Report 2023:** most trusted sources for local-business information are **Google (66%)**, **Google Maps (45%)**, and the **business’s own website (36%)**. Facebook is joint-fourth with Yelp on “most trusted,” yet **43%** of consumers find Facebook information untrustworthy. **30%** use Facebook weekly to find local-business information ([BrightLocal Discovery & Trust 2023](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-business-discovery-trust-report/)).

**BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2024 / 2025:** Google remains the dominant review site (81% in 2024, down from 87% in 2023). Facebook remains in the top three review platforms but usage has drifted down since 2022. 2024 sample: 1,141 U.S. consumers ([LCRS 2024](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2024/); [LCRS 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)).

**IONOS SMB survey 2024 (U.S. + Europe, n ≈ 4,800):** U.S. SMBs report **website presence 49%** (up from 44% in 2023). Top barriers: **cost 52%**, inflation 50%, **lack of time 47%** ([IONOS newsroom](https://www.ionos.com/newsroom/news/ionos-survey-reveals-challenges-smbs-are-facing-in-2024/)).

**U.S. Chamber / C_TEC 2024 technology survey (n = 1,100 SMBs):** social accounts are a default platform even for “low adoption” firms ([Chamber report PDF](https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/Impact-of-Technology-on-Small-Business-Report-2024.pdf)).

These surveys are **national**. They are the right citation for *why the split exists in general*. They are **not** a Drew County poll.

**The pitch that fits this geography:** do not sell “you’re unprofessional without a website.” Sell **owned hours/menu that Google can index**, **click-to-call that works when Facebook is down or a tourist has no FB account**, and **a URL insurance directories and GBP demand**. Keep Facebook. Add a site that makes Facebook and GBP point at something the owner controls.

## 7. Business model options for an entrant

| Model | How it makes money | Fits this geography? | Main failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Productized packages + monthly care (recommended primary)** | 3 SKUs; setup + $45–$149/mo; AI-assisted build; in-person close | **Yes.** Matches WTP, MixedMakerShop comparables, and the Facebook-upgrade job | Under-pricing care; scope creep; churn when “the site is done” |
| **B. Custom agency** | $5k–$30k projects | **No** as primary. Maybe 1–2/year (clinic, credit union, manufacturer) | Pipeline of 1,686 employers cannot feed a studio |
| **C. Retainer-led only (no cheap setup)** | $249/mo WaaS like SEOteric | **Risky.** 12-month $2,988 is a real commitment vs $0 Facebook. Sellable to dentists, not to Chef’s | High no-decision rate |
| **D. DIY coaching / “I’ll set up your Wix”** | $300–$600 one-time | Race to the bottom vs YouTube and N Six $59/mo | No moat |
| **E. Ads-only / Crossbuck lane** | Video + Meta/Google ads | Different business; capital-intensive creative | Not the website question |

### Recommended primary model

**Productized “Found / Booked / Paid” local presence**, sold in person in Drew + one adjacent county at a time, with **monthly care as the real product**.

**Why this geography:**

- Income forbids $7,500 average projects.
- Facebook-only restaurants and trades need a **small** owned surface, not a redesign philosophy.
- A.J. Huffman and Crossbuck do not publish a ladder — a printed three-price sheet is differentiation.
- Population decline means you win by **density** (every HVAC truck in Drew/Bradley/Ashley), not by waiting for growth.
- AI cuts build time so $400–$1,200 setup can still contribute after a drive to Warren.

**Secondary (opportunistic):** one custom rebuild per quarter at $3k–$8k (dental, law, multi-location vet). Do not staff for it.

**Do not lead with** “we’ll replace Facebook.” Lead with “keep Facebook; stop losing the Google stranger and the traveler without the app.”

**What is in each SKU (delivery promise for costing):**

### 8.2 Volume, churn, seasonality (base)

| Driver | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Price change | — | Setup +3% (use $850 blended); monthly +5% ($99 blended) | Setup $875; monthly $104 | Modest; still under SEOteric |

**Ending-book math (base):**

- Y1: 20 new × (1 − 0.22 × 0.5) ≈ 20 − 2.2 ≈ **17.8 → round 16** after a conservative first-year falloff (model uses **16**).
- Y2: 16 × (1 − 0.18) + 28 × (1 − 0.18 × 0.5) = 13.1 + 25.5 = **38.6 → 36** (extra haircut for no-shows).
- Y3: 36 × (1 − 0.16) + 32 × (1 − 0.16 × 0.5) = 30.2 + 29.4 = **59.6 → 57**.

57 retainers after three years is **~8% of the 720 no-site midpoint** — inside the base attach case if some clients are rebuilds. It is **ambitious but not fantasy** for a full-time closer who lives there. It is **fantasy** as a side hustle.

## 9. Risks, competition, kill criteria

### 9.1 Risks

| Risk | Why it is real here | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **$0 Facebook incumbent** | Proven daily tool for food and trades | Do not fight Facebook; wrap it |
| **A.J. Huffman + Monticello Live** | Town trust + ad inventory; can add websites at “buddy rates” | Partner (refer print; you do GBP/web) or differentiate on Google/GBP, not on vinyl |
| **DIY $17–$59/mo (Wix, N Six, Hostinger)** | Good enough for a one-pager | Sell care + photos + being local |
| **Population / demand shrink** | Chicot/Desha already −10% this decade | Density strategy; highway and UAM demand |
| **Owner time** | You *are* COGS | Cap new logos/month; waitlist |
| **Churn after launch** | “The site is done” | Contract: 6-month care minimum; cancel = export + paid offboarding |
| **Payment / collections** | Rural B2B slow pay | ACH monthly; no custom work without 50% |
| **Reputation** | One bad church/restaurant build is town-wide | Over-deliver on hours/phone accuracy |
| **Platform risk** | Google/Meta change discovery | Own the domain and email |
| **Key-person** | Solo | Written playbooks; contractor bench |
| **Legal/tax** | Out of scope | Arkansas contractor/advertising rules; sales tax on digital — **ask a CPA** |
| **Doorway-site confusion** | Owners already burned by national “Monticello web design” spam | Show a local face and a local number |

### 9.2 Competition summary

- **Incumbent attention:** Facebook, Google Business Profile, Monticello Live, radio, word of mouth. These win daily even when a website exists.
- **Incumbent makers (human, in or next to the trade area):** A.J. Huffman Graphic Designs (Monticello; unpublished prices; print + Monticello Live); Crossbuck Creative (Pine Bluff; quote-only; video-led); Astonished Man Design (El Dorado; custom/hosting; unpublished prices); EvoDev Designs (Monticello listings; **domain unresolved 20 Sep 2026**).
- **Incumbent makers (remote / published price):** MixedMakerShop (Hot Springs; $400 / $900–$1,800 / $45–$149); SEOteric WaaS ($249/mo, 12-month); N Six Studios AI sites ($59/mo); Junious Labs managed-site claim ($100/mo).
- **Incumbent DIY:** Facebook $0; Wix from $17.77/mo annual; Shopify from $29/mo annual; WordPress.com from $4/mo annual; Hostinger intro ~$3/mo (renewal much higher).
- **Incumbent marketplace:** Fiverr/Upwork $400–$5,000 brochure sites.
- **Noise, not rivals:** Studio 29, EZMarketing, Marketing Local SEO geo-doorway pages that rank for “web design Monticello.”
- **Adjacent, not web studios:** Mingo Computer (IT); South Arkansas Business Solutions (Pine Bluff office equipment); ONE11 Southern Graphics (print/embroidery).

**Competitive verdict:** the seven-county web-supply bench is **thin and unpublished**. The real fight is **Facebook + “I already know everybody”**, not another Monticello agency. A published three-rung package plus a local phone number is a vacant position. Huffman is the relationship risk; Fiverr/Wix are the price risk; Crossbuck is a different category.

### 9.3 Kill criteria (write these down before the first sale)

Stop, pause, or shrink to a side project if **any two** of the following are true. These are **operating rules**, not moral failures.

1. **90-day paid-pilot miss:** fewer than **3 paying logos** (setup collected) in the first 90 days of active selling (chamber + 20 in-person visits + ASBTDC intro).
2. **Y1 book miss:** fewer than **8 paying retainers** on the 12-month anniversary of first sale.
3. **Unit economics:** blended **CAC** (ads + travel + your time at $40/hr) **> 50% of first-year revenue** per client (~$1,485 in the base model) for two consecutive quarters.
4. **Care load:** more than **12 hours/month of unpaid “quick edits”** per 10 retainers — the product is mis-scoped.
5. **Price collapse:** you cannot hold **≥ $79/mo** blended without including ads management (a different, worse business).
6. **Incumbent foreclosure:** Huffman or another local offers a **published** $400 + $49/mo package and takes the chamber/ASBTDC rail — compete only if you have a clear GBP/review wedge; otherwise partner or exit.
7. **Personal runway:** owner draw cannot stay at the planned $18k Y1 without harming household solvency — the base case is **not** a sole-income plan in year 1.
8. **Ethics/reputation:** one public dispute over “you promised we’d be #1 on Google” — kill **SEO-guarantee language** immediately; if it already shipped, pause sales and rewrite the contract.

**Do not kill** because a restaurant prefers Facebook for daily specials. That is the market. Kill if they will not pay **anything** for an owned URL + GBP after a clear demo.
